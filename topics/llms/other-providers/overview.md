# Other notable providers

⏱ 10 min read · +3h 41m resources

Per-model pages to follow; this page explains the long tail that matters. One section per provider; promotion to its own page happens if a line becomes central.

### How to read this page

None of these providers is trying to top a leaderboard, and reading them as failed frontier labs misses the point. Each optimises a different constraint: parameters per unit of data quality (Microsoft), distribution and billing (Amazon), auditability and on-prem deployment (Cohere, IBM), memory per token during serving (NVIDIA, Liquid, AI21), or simply being the only artefact of its kind (Prime Intellect, SmolLM3). The question for each is not "how good is it" but "what does it make cheap or possible that a frontier API does not".

### Microsoft: Phi (and MAI)

Phi is the flagship result of one hypothesis: most of a pretraining corpus is noise, and a small model trained on a small amount of extremely high-signal text can match a much larger model trained on the raw web. "Textbook quality" is the label, and concretely it means two things: web data filtered aggressively by a classifier that scores documents for reasoning and pedagogical value rather than fluency, and a corpus mostly **synthetic**, exercises, worked solutions and explanations generated by a larger model and deliberately structured so each example demonstrates a reasoning step rather than merely stating a fact. Phi-1 and Phi-2 proved the effect at 1B to 3B scale; **Phi-4 (14B, Dec 2024)** reached 84.8% on MMLU and beat considerably larger models on maths, with **Phi-4-mini (3.8B)** and Phi-4-reasoning variants following.

The cost matters before choosing it. A model whose training distribution is synthesised from benchmark-shaped tasks is strongest exactly on benchmark-shaped tasks; Phi models are consistently weaker on long-tail world knowledge, on messy real inputs, and on anything phrased unlike a textbook exercise, and the synthetic generator having itself seen the evaluations makes contamination hard to rule out. Used as intended it remains the default dense family for local and edge deployment in 2026, MIT-licensed, which is as permissive as licensing gets. Separately, Microsoft's **MAI** team trains in-house frontier models (the MAI-1 line) to reduce dependence on OpenAI, while Azure continues to serve everyone's models regardless; the strategy is to own the distribution layer and treat the model layer as replaceable.

### Amazon: Nova

Nova is Amazon's closed in-house family, available only through Bedrock. The first generation (Dec 2024) was a straight capability ladder, **Micro, Lite, Pro and Premier**, differing in size, latency and price so an application can route cheap turns to Micro and hard ones to Pro without changing vendor. Two siblings are more interesting. **Nova Sonic** is speech-to-speech: audio in, audio out, with no intermediate transcribe-then-generate-then-synthesise pipeline, which removes the latency of three sequential models and preserves prosody, interruption and tone that a text bottleneck discards. **Nova Act** is trained to drive a browser, emitting UI actions (click, type, scroll) against a page rather than prose about it. The **Nova 2** generation is current, Premier retired in favour of Nova 2 Lite.

Nova itself is competent mid-tier and priced to undercut. Amazon's real position is elsewhere: **Bedrock** is the aggregator, one API, one bill, one IAM policy and one VPC boundary across models from Anthropic, Meta, Mistral, Cohere and others, which is what large enterprises actually buy; and **Trainium**, Amazon's in-house training accelerator, is the compute behind Project Rainier for Anthropic, making Amazon a supplier of an alternative to Nvidia rather than merely a customer of one.

### Cohere: Command, Aya, North

Cohere sells to enterprises that will not send their data to a public API, and every part of the lineup follows. **Command R and R+** were the first models explicitly post-trained for **grounded generation with citations**: given retrieved documents in context, the model answers from those documents and emits inline references to the specific passages it used, so a reviewer can check each claim against its source and an unsupported sentence becomes visible. That is a materially different objective from ordinary instruction tuning, and it made them the default RAG models for a period. **Command A (Mar 2025)** is 111B and **dense** rather than mixture-of-experts, which sounds backwards until you notice the deployment target: a dense 111B fits in two GPUs with predictable memory, whereas an MoE of equivalent quality needs every expert resident and a router that complicates multi-GPU serving. Sizing a model to a customer's two-card server is the product decision.

**Aya**, from Cohere Labs, is the open multilingual research line (Aya 23, Aya Expanse, Aya Vision), notable for building instruction data across languages through a large annotator community rather than by machine-translating English datasets, which is what usually leaves "multilingual" models fluent but culturally hollow. **Tiny Aya (Feb 2026, 3.35B, 70+ languages)** pushes that to phone-capable sizes. **North** is the secure agent platform: agents that run inside the customer's environment against internal systems. **North Mini Code (Jun 2026, 30B total with 3B active MoE)** is Cohere's first open agentic coding model. **North Small Translate** (Sep 2026) applies the same instinct to a single task: a 218B total and 25B active MoE built only for translation, 128 experts with 8 per token, 4096-token sliding-window attention and 16K context, reporting 83.6 on WMT26 across 50 languages against DeepL NextGen's 81.37 and Google Translate's 68.20 on Cohere's own evaluation with GPT-5.6-Sol as judge. Free on the Cohere API to rate limits, non-commercial for self-hosting, with a 4-bit checkpoint that runs on one B200 or two H100s. A dedicated 218B translation model is a reminder that single-task specialists at mixture-of-experts scale are still economically viable against generalists. [Cohere blog](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/north-small-translate-1.0) (8 min)

### NVIDIA: Nemotron

NVIDIA ships open models as demand generation for GPUs: anything that makes it easier to build with LLMs sells more of them, so the models, datasets and recipes are given away. The **Nemotron 3 Nano / Super / Ultra** line is current, and the Nano models are the technically distinctive part because they are **Mamba-2 hybrids** rather than transformers.

A state space model (SSM) processes a sequence as a linear recurrence carrying a fixed-size hidden state, so per-token compute and memory are constant regardless of context length; the historical problems were that a sequential recurrence is far slower on a GPU than a parallel matmul, and that a fixed state cannot selectively remember. **Mamba-2** answers both: the recurrence is *selective*, its parameters computed from the current input so the block chooses what to keep and what to discard, and it is formulated so training executes as a structured matrix multiplication rather than a step-by-step scan, which puts it back on the tensor cores. **Hybrid** means mostly SSM blocks with a small number of attention layers interleaved, because a compressed state is bad at exact recall and a few attention layers restore it, the same argument MiniMax reached from the other direction.

The number that matters operationally: KV cache per token falls from hundreds of KiB in a comparable transformer to a few KiB, because only the attention layers cache anything at all. For high-throughput agentic serving, where thousands of long-context sequences must be resident at once and memory rather than FLOPs is the limit, that changes deployment economics far more than a couple of benchmark points would. NVIDIA also publishes open post-training datasets and **reward models** (the HelpSteer line; a reward model scores candidate responses so that RL can optimise against it, and open ones are rare because they encode a lab's preference data), plus **Llama-Nemotron** distills, Llama bases repackaged with NVIDIA's post-training. See the Mamba paper for the underlying method.

### IBM: Granite

Granite 4 (2025-2026) is a family of small hybrid Mamba/transformer enterprise models from 3B to 32B under Apache 2.0, on the same architectural logic as Nemotron Nano: a constant-size recurrent state for most layers means long documents can be processed on modest hardware without a KV cache that dominates memory. What IBM adds is not capability but the compliance surface around it: an ISO-certified governance process for how the models were built, published training-data provenance, and indemnification, which is what actually unblocks a bank or a hospital. Function-calling quality per parameter is strong, which matters because the enterprise use case is structured tool invocation rather than open-ended chat: chosen for the paperwork as much as for the model.

### Liquid AI: LFM

Liquid AI builds deliberately non-transformer architectures for on-device deployment. LFM (Liquid Foundation Model) blocks are short-range gated convolutions combined with a small number of attention layers, rather than a uniform stack of attention blocks, so most of the network mixes information locally at fixed cost and memory does not grow with context as a full KV cache does. **LFM2 and LFM2.5** target phones and laptops, where the binding constraints are RAM and battery rather than throughput, and where quality per FLOP and a small resident footprint decide whether a feature can run locally at all. It is the most credible part of the broadening post-transformer fringe, alongside the SSM hybrids above and the text-diffusion experiments.

### Tencent: Hunyuan

Tencent published competitive open weights across text and multimodal for two years without being in the frontier conversation; the **Hy4 preview** (Aug 2026) put it there. 770B total / 49B active mixture of experts, a context window above 1M tokens, two reasoning levels with high on by default and a `no_think` switch to turn it off, and roughly 1.56TB of weights on Hugging Face, with vLLM support landing in v0.29.0.

Two things are worth carrying. The sparsity ratio, about 16x, is conservative next to Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's 21x, so Tencent bought its place in the band with size rather than with an aggressive active-parameter bet, which also means it is expensive to serve for its tier. And it is positioned on real-world productivity rather than benchmark maxima: an internal blind evaluation with 163 experts across 203 engineering tasks scored Hy4 at 2.99/4.00 against GLM-5.3 at 2.92 and Kimi K3 at 2.94, inside the noise, but the right shape of claim for a model sold on coding, office work and scientific research rather than on leaderboard position.

The detail that connects it to a larger argument: Tencent says the model took part in the automated optimisation of its own training methods, with autonomous infrastructure work producing a 31.8% throughput gain. That is the same claim shape as Anthropic's recursive-self-improvement report and Zhipu's inference-stack post, three labs within a month, and all three are self-reported by the party that benefits. [Tencent](https://www.tencent.com/tencent-releases-and-open-sources-tencent-hy4-preview/) (8 min), [Simon Willison's hands-on](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/29/hy4/) (10 min)

### Other names worth recognising

- **Baidu ERNIE, ByteDance Seed/Doubao, Ant Ling/Ring** and the **Yi** line: the rest of China's open and closed mix, with Tencent Hunyuan and StepFun now carrying their own entries. Seed-OSS ships genuinely competitive open weights across text and multimodal; ERNIE is Baidu's long-running in-house line tied to its search and cloud products; Doubao is the consumer assistant that dominates Chinese usage by volume, which makes ByteDance's data position the one to watch; Ant's Ling and Ring lines and the Yi bilingual models are second-tier in visibility but ship regularly.
- **Arcee AI Trinity Large (400B, 2026)**: a rare US startup attempt at open-weight frontier scale, from a company otherwise known for model merging and small-model distillation. Interesting as evidence about whether a small team can still reach that scale, more than for the model itself.
- **Prime Intellect INTELLECT line**: proofs of concept for **decentralised training**, a single model trained across clusters in different places connected by ordinary internet links rather than a datacentre fabric. Gradient synchronisation over such links is hopelessly slow, so this requires low-communication algorithms that let workers run many local steps and synchronise infrequently. The models are not competitive on quality; the point is that frontier-scale training need not be geographically centralised, which matters strategically far more than the checkpoints do.
- **TII Falcon**: the Abu Dhabi institute that was one of the earliest credible open-weights providers (Falcon 40B and 180B, alongside the RefinedWeb corpus, which showed heavily filtered web data could replace curated corpora). The current **Falcon-H** models are hybrid SSMs, the same architectural camp as Nemotron Nano and Granite 4.
- **Institute of Foundation Models (Abu Dhabi): K2 Horizon**: the second lab, alongside Ai2, publishing *fully* open models rather than merely open weights, which puts two such labs on two continents with different funders where there was one. **K2 Horizon** (Sep 2026) is six Apache 2.0 models at 0.9B, 3.7B, 7B, 32B, a 36B sparse variant and 375B, released with weights, training code, training data and methodology, the combination that makes contamination auditing, data ablations and training-dynamics research possible at all. The fleet shares two architectural ideas: **diffusion distillation**, generating a block of tokens in parallel rather than one at a time for a claimed 3x speedup, and a **mixture-of-value-attention** design aimed at reasoning. IFM claims state of the art at 0.9B, 3.7B and 7B and open-weight competitiveness at 375B, which is a vendor claim until independently rerun. On Hugging Face with vLLM and SGLang support, plus API access through Compass, Cerebras and Nebius. Abu Dhabi now funds two distinct open-model lines, this one and TII's above. [IFM](https://ifm.ai/k2/press-release/) (8 min)
- **HuggingFace SmolLM3**: a small model whose value is documentation rather than capability. Every stage of the recipe, the data mixture and the ablations are published, making it the reference for how a competent 3B-class model is actually built. It is also a usable reference for **NoPE (no positional encoding)** applied to a fraction of layers, where those layers get no explicit position signal at all and rely on the causal mask alone to convey order, a choice associated with better generalisation beyond the trained context length.
- **Xiaomi MiMo**: a transparency precedent rather than a model. With **MiMo 2.6** (Sep 2026) Xiaomi published a **live post-training dashboard**, streaming reward and evaluation metrics from a run while it was still going, the first time an active frontier-adjacent post-training run has been observable from outside as it happens. No model was released alongside it, so the artifact is the dashboard.
- **StepFun**: a multimodal-first Chinese lab that has shipped steadily at second-tier visibility, and since **Step 5 Preview** (Sep 2026) an entrant in the frontier band at 600B total and 27B active, with a 1M-token context and text, image and video input. 27B is the smallest active-parameter count yet shipped in that band, though the 22x ratio behind it is ordinary next to DeepSeek V4 Pro's 33x. The pricing argument that follows from it is on [Topic: llms](../summary.md).
- **Prism ML**: a compression house rather than a model lab. Its **Bonsai 2 27B** is a ternary rebuild of Qwen3.8 27B at 1.76 effective bits per weight, and it is the current reference point for how far weight compression goes before quality breaks.
- **Shanghai AI Laboratory**: **Atria Dawn Preview** is a 744B agentic MoE built as a post-training layer over an existing open base rather than trained from scratch, which is a release shape worth recognising when it recurs.
- **OpenBMB MiniCPM5-2B** (Sep 2026): a dense 2.5B Apache-2.0 on-device model averaging 53.9 across 34 benchmarks, and a useful current reference point for what the small end of the dense ladder buys.
- **inclusionAI Ling 3.0 Flash VL** (Sep 2026): a flash-tier vision-language model, free on OpenRouter at launch.
- **Nous Research Hermes**: post-training specialists who take open bases and produce instruct and reasoning variants with their own data and recipes. They publish datasets and methodology, so the Hermes line is a usable control for "how much of this model's behaviour came from post-training".
- **Reka, AI21, Upstage Solar**: niche but recurrent in evaluations. Reka builds natively multimodal models (text, image, audio and video in one encoder path) from a small team. AI21's **Jamba** combines Mamba layers, attention layers and mixture-of-experts in a single architecture, one of the first to ship very long context at a memory footprint a single node could hold. Upstage's **Solar** popularised **depth up-scaling**: duplicate a slice of an existing base model's layers to build a deeper network, then continue pretraining so the seams heal, producing a larger model without training one from scratch.
