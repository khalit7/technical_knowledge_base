# Other notable providers

Per-model pages to follow; this page explains the long tail that matters. One section per provider; promotion to its own page happens if a line becomes central.

### How to read this page

None of these providers is trying to top a leaderboard, and reading them as failed frontier labs misses the point. Each is optimising a different constraint: parameters per unit of data quality (Microsoft), distribution and billing (Amazon), auditability and on-prem deployment (Cohere, IBM), memory per token during serving (NVIDIA, Liquid, AI21), or simply being the only artefact of its kind (Prime Intellect, SmolLM3). The question to ask of each is not "how good is it" but "what does it make cheap or possible that a frontier API does not".

### Microsoft: Phi (and MAI)

Phi is the flagship result of one hypothesis: that most of a pretraining corpus is noise, and that a small model trained on a small amount of extremely high-signal text can match a much larger model trained on the raw web. "Textbook quality" is the label, and concretely it means two things. Web data is filtered aggressively by a classifier that scores documents for reasoning and pedagogical value rather than for fluency, and the bulk of the corpus is then **synthetic**: exercises, worked solutions and explanations generated by a larger model, deliberately structured so that each example demonstrates a reasoning step rather than merely stating a fact. Phi-1 and Phi-2 proved the effect at 1B to 3B scale, and **Phi-4 (14B, Dec 2024)** reached 84.8% on MMLU and beat considerably larger models on maths, with **Phi-4-mini (3.8B)** and Phi-4-reasoning variants following.

The cost is the part to keep in mind before choosing it. A model whose training distribution is synthesised from benchmark-shaped tasks is strongest exactly on benchmark-shaped tasks; Phi models are consistently weaker on long-tail world knowledge, on messy real inputs, and on anything phrased unlike a textbook exercise, and the synthetic generator having itself seen the evaluations makes contamination hard to rule out. Used as intended, though, it remains the default dense family for local and edge deployment in 2026, and the weights are MIT-licensed, which is as permissive as licensing gets. Separately, Microsoft's **MAI** team trains in-house frontier models (the MAI-1 line) to reduce dependence on OpenAI, while Azure continues to serve everyone's models regardless; the strategy is to own the distribution layer and treat the model layer as replaceable.

### Amazon: Nova

Nova is Amazon's closed in-house family, available only through Bedrock. The first generation (Dec 2024) was a straight capability ladder: **Micro, Lite, Pro and Premier**, differing in size, latency and price so that an application can route cheap turns to Micro and hard ones to Pro without changing vendor. Two siblings are more interesting than the ladder. **Nova Sonic** is speech-to-speech: audio in, audio out, with no intermediate transcribe-then-generate-then-synthesise pipeline, which removes the latency of three sequential models and preserves prosody, interruption and tone that a text bottleneck discards. **Nova Act** is trained to drive a browser, meaning it emits UI actions (click, type, scroll) against a page rather than prose about the page. The **Nova 2** generation is current, with Premier retired in favour of Nova 2 Lite.

Nova itself is competent mid-tier and priced to undercut. Amazon's real position in this market is elsewhere: **Bedrock** is the aggregator, giving one API, one bill, one IAM policy and one VPC boundary across models from Anthropic, Meta, Mistral, Cohere and others, which is what large enterprises actually buy; and **Trainium**, Amazon's in-house training accelerator, is the compute behind Project Rainier for Anthropic, making Amazon a supplier of an alternative to Nvidia rather than merely a customer of one. Judge Amazon as an aggregator and a chip vendor, not as a frontier lab.

### Cohere: Command, Aya, North

Cohere sells to enterprises that will not send their data to a public API, and every part of the lineup follows from that. **Command R and R+** were the first models explicitly post-trained for **grounded generation with citations**: given a set of retrieved documents in context, the model is trained to answer from those documents and to emit inline references to the specific passages it used, so a reviewer can check each claim against its source and an unsupported sentence becomes visible. That is a materially different objective from ordinary instruction tuning, and it is what made them the default RAG models for a period. **Command A (Mar 2025)** is 111B and **dense** rather than mixture-of-experts, which sounds like a step backwards until you notice the deployment target: a dense 111B fits in two GPUs with predictable memory, whereas an MoE of equivalent quality needs every expert resident and a router that complicates multi-GPU serving. Sizing a model to a customer's two-card server is the product decision.

**Aya**, from Cohere Labs, is the open multilingual research line (Aya 23, Aya Expanse, Aya Vision), notable for building instruction data across languages through a large annotator community rather than by machine-translating English datasets, which is what usually leaves "multilingual" models fluent but culturally hollow. **Tiny Aya (Feb 2026, 3.35B, 70+ languages)** pushes that to phone-capable sizes. **North** is the secure agent platform, meaning agents that run inside the customer's environment against internal systems; **North Mini Code (Jun 2026, 30B total with 3B active MoE)** is Cohere's first open agentic coding model. **North Small Translate** (Sep 2026) is the sharpest illustration of the same instinct applied to a single task: a 218B total and 25B active MoE built only for translation, 128 experts with 8 per token, 4096-token sliding-window attention and 16K context, reporting 83.6 on WMT26 across 50 languages against DeepL NextGen's 81.37 and Google Translate's 68.20 on Cohere's own evaluation with GPT-5.6-Sol as judge. It is free on the Cohere API to rate limits and non-commercial for self-hosting, with a 4-bit checkpoint that runs on one B200 or two H100s. A dedicated 218B translation model is a reminder that single-task specialists at mixture-of-experts scale are still economically viable against generalists. [Cohere blog](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/north-small-translate-1.0) (8 min)

The whole bet is that data sovereignty and on-prem deployment beat benchmark position for the buyer they target.

### NVIDIA: Nemotron

NVIDIA ships open models as demand generation for GPUs: anything that makes it easier to build with LLMs sells more of them, so the models, datasets and recipes are given away. The **Nemotron 3 Nano / Super / Ultra** line is the current lineup, and the Nano models are the technically distinctive part because they are **Mamba-2 hybrids** rather than transformers.

A state space model (SSM) processes a sequence as a linear recurrence carrying a fixed-size hidden state, so per-token compute and memory are constant regardless of how long the context is; the historical problem was that a sequential recurrence is far slower on a GPU than a parallel matmul, and that a fixed state cannot selectively remember. **Mamba-2** answers both: the recurrence is *selective*, with its parameters computed from the current input so the block can choose what to keep and what to discard, and it is formulated so that training can be executed as a structured matrix multiplication rather than a step-by-step scan, which puts it back on the tensor cores. **Hybrid** means the stack is mostly SSM blocks with a small number of attention layers interleaved, because a compressed state is bad at exact recall and a few attention layers restore it, which is the same argument MiniMax reached from the other direction.

The number that matters operationally: KV cache per token falls from hundreds of KiB in a comparable transformer to a few KiB, because only the attention layers cache anything at all. For high-throughput agentic serving, where you want thousands of long-context sequences resident at once and are limited by memory rather than FLOPs, that changes the economics of a deployment far more than a couple of benchmark points would. NVIDIA also publishes open post-training datasets and **reward models** (the HelpSteer line; a reward model scores candidate responses so that RL can optimise against it, and open ones are rare because they encode a lab's preference data), plus **Llama-Nemotron** distills, which are Llama bases repackaged with NVIDIA's post-training. See the Mamba paper for the underlying method.

### IBM: Granite

Granite 4 (2025-2026) is a family of small hybrid Mamba/transformer enterprise models from 3B to 32B under Apache 2.0, using the same architectural logic as Nemotron Nano: constant-size recurrent state for most layers means long documents can be processed on modest hardware without a KV cache that dominates memory. What IBM adds is not capability but the compliance surface around it: an ISO-certified governance process for how the models were built, published training-data provenance, and indemnification, which is what actually unblocks a bank or a hospital. Function-calling quality per parameter is strong, which matters because the enterprise use case is structured tool invocation rather than open-ended chat. This is the boring-but-deployable option, and it is chosen for the paperwork as much as for the model.

### Liquid AI: LFM

Liquid AI builds deliberately non-transformer architectures for on-device deployment. The LFM (Liquid Foundation Model) blocks are built from short-range gated convolutions combined with a small number of attention layers, rather than from a uniform stack of attention blocks, so most of the network mixes information locally at fixed cost and memory does not grow with context in the way a full KV cache does. **LFM2 and LFM2.5** target phones and laptops, where the binding constraints are RAM and battery rather than throughput, and where quality per FLOP and a small resident footprint decide whether a feature can run locally at all. Treat the line as the most credible part of the broadening post-transformer fringe, alongside the SSM hybrids above and the text-diffusion experiments.

### Other names worth recognising

- **Tencent Hunyuan, Baidu ERNIE, ByteDance Seed/Doubao, Ant Ling/Ring, StepFun** and [01.AI](http://01.ai/): the rest of China's open and closed mix. Hunyuan and Seed-OSS ship genuinely competitive open weights across text and multimodal; ERNIE is Baidu's long-running in-house line tied to its search and cloud products; Doubao is the consumer assistant that dominates Chinese usage by volume, which makes ByteDance's data position the one to watch; Ant's Ling and Ring lines and StepFun's multimodal models are second-tier in visibility but ship regularly.
- **Arcee AI Trinity Large (400B, 2026)**: a rare US startup attempt at open-weight frontier scale, from a company otherwise known for model merging and small-model distillation. Interesting as evidence about whether a small team can still reach that scale, more than for the model itself.
- **Prime Intellect INTELLECT line**: proofs of concept for **decentralised training**, meaning a single model trained across clusters in different places connected by ordinary internet links rather than a datacentre fabric. Because gradient synchronisation over such links is hopelessly slow, this requires low-communication algorithms that let workers run many local steps and synchronise infrequently. The models are not competitive on quality; the point is demonstrating that frontier-scale training need not be geographically centralised, which matters strategically far more than the checkpoints do.
- **TII Falcon**: the Abu Dhabi institute that was one of the earliest credible open-weights providers (Falcon 40B and 180B, alongside the RefinedWeb corpus, which showed heavily filtered web data could replace curated corpora). The current **Falcon-H** models are hybrid SSMs, putting it in the same architectural camp as Nemotron Nano and Granite 4.
- **HuggingFace SmolLM3**: a small model whose value is documentation rather than capability. Every stage of the recipe, the data mixture and the ablations are published, which makes it the reference for how a competent 3B-class model is actually built. It is also a usable reference for **NoPE (no positional encoding)** applied to a fraction of layers, where those layers are given no explicit position signal at all and rely on the causal mask alone to convey order, a choice associated with better generalisation beyond the trained context length.
- **Xiaomi MiMo**: worth knowing for a transparency precedent rather than for a model. With **MiMo 2.6** (Sep 2026) Xiaomi published a **live post-training dashboard**, streaming reward and evaluation metrics from a run while it was still going, which is the first time an active frontier-adjacent post-training run has been observable from outside as it happens. No model was released alongside it, so the artifact is the dashboard.
- **StepFun**: the **Step 5 Preview** line is covered in the sparsity discussion on [Topic: llms](../summary.md), since its 600B total and 27B active configuration is the most aggressive sparsity ratio yet shipped in the frontier band.
- **Prism ML**: a compression house rather than a model lab. Its **Bonsai 2 27B** is a ternary rebuild of Qwen3.8 27B at 1.76 effective bits per weight, and it is the current reference point for how far weight compression goes before quality breaks.
- **Shanghai AI Laboratory**: **Atria Dawn Preview** is a 744B agentic MoE built as a post-training layer over an existing open base rather than trained from scratch, which is a release shape worth recognising when it recurs.
- **OpenBMB MiniCPM5-2B** (Sep 2026): a dense 2.5B Apache-2.0 on-device model averaging 53.9 across 34 benchmarks, and a useful current reference point for what the small end of the dense ladder buys.
- **inclusionAI Ling 3.0 Flash VL** (Sep 2026): a flash-tier vision-language model, free on OpenRouter at launch.
- **Nous Research Hermes**: post-training specialists who take open bases and produce instruct and reasoning variants with their own data and recipes. Worth knowing because they publish datasets and methodology, so the Hermes line is a usable control for "how much of this model's behaviour came from post-training".
- **Reka, AI21, Upstage Solar**: niche but recurrent in evaluations. Reka builds natively multimodal models (text, image, audio and video in one encoder path) from a small team. AI21's **Jamba** is a hybrid that combines Mamba layers, attention layers and mixture-of-experts in a single architecture, one of the first to ship very long context at a memory footprint a single node could hold. Upstage's **Solar** popularised **depth up-scaling**: take an existing base model, duplicate a slice of its layers to build a deeper network, then continue pretraining so the seams heal, which produces a larger model without training one from scratch.
