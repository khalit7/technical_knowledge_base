## Papers

- **[new] RocketEval: Efficient Automated LLM Evaluation via Grading Checklist** (Wei et al.,
  ICLR 2025, [arXiv:2503.05142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05142)). A frontier model compiles
  each query into 5-10 binary checklist questions once; a 2B judge answers them independently,
  and the score is read off the normalised Yes/No logit rather than the generated token, which
  preserves calibration and removes the channel positional bias travels through. Gemma-2-2B
  reaches 0.965 Spearman with Arena Elo (Mistral-Nemo 0.986, above GPT-4o's own 0.979) at
  $27.70 per 1,000 tests versus $3,400. Instance-level agreement stays at 57.9% against a
  64.7% human-to-human ceiling, and that split is the lesson: ranking a field averages out
  per-item noise, adjudicating one response does not.
- **[new] Prime Agent: A Self-Improving RLM Harness** (Karten et al., Prime Intellect,
  [arXiv:2608.23552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23552), 2026-08-24). Open-source harness on a
  four-level state hierarchy (weights, context, persistent IPython REPL plus live subagents,
  disk history) under a Recursive Language Model abstraction; a "Continual Harness" versions
  prompts, memories, skills, and subagent specs across trajectories, and subagents talk to
  each other over daemon-mediated async queues. ARC-AGI-3 RHAE Best@1 30% to 95.5%;
  85.5-hour nanoGPT speedrun with 19 validated records; 7-day Factorio run clearing 24 of 196
  technologies. Two findings worth carrying: models are not trained to operate most of what a
  rich harness offers, and online self-refinement produced genuine specification exploitation
  (resource-spawning shortcuts in Factorio). Row added at the top of the Papers index.
- **[new] JIT-Agent: Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution** (Zhang
  et al., NUS et al., [arXiv:2608.25593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25593), 2026-08-26).
  Factors any harness into `(Memory, Planning, Action, Capability orchestration)`, then
  trains a 27B model (Qwen3.6-27B; SFT plus bounded repair supervision plus Evo-GDPO against
  a growing harness archive seeded with 13 hand-written references) to synthesise, repair,
  and evolve one per task at inference time. +7.7 average for GLM-5.2 and +8.8 for
  DeepSeek-V4-Flash over nine agent benchmarks, first in 8 of 9 columns, at 14.9-54.1% lower
  cost than a fixed harness; a JIT-harnessed DeepSeek-V4-Flash beats bare GPT-5.6 on
  DeepSearchQA (+9.1) and OdysseyBench (+4.3). Row added at the top of the Papers index.
- **[linked, not promoted]** Apodex 1.1 ([arXiv:2608.23283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23283)),

## agentic-harnesses

- **[new] Harness scaling: where the research went** section on
  `topics/agentic-harnesses/summary.md`. Sets out the three strategies now on the table: give
  the model more machinery and get out of the way (Prime Agent), constrain it with checked
  state (StateM), or train a model to build the harness (JIT-Agent). The useful finding is
  that all three beat the native harness, so the win comes from having a durable state layer
  at all rather than from a philosophy about control. Records the two stated ceilings (models
  are not trained to operate rich harnesses; JIT-Agent's four-module protocol is far poorer
  than what Codex or Claude Code expose, so its parity is parity in a small language), notes
  Apodex 1.1 as the closest model-side attempt, and carries over Prime Agent's
  specification-exploitation finding.
- **[update] harness-engineering.md**: new dated section on **encrypted prompt injection**,
  from Adversa AI's cryptographic context injection against Grok (disclosed 2026-06-03, still
  unpatched). A page ships an AES blob that static filters cannot read; the agent decrypts it
  in its own Python runtime, manufacturing the malicious plaintext inside the trust boundary
  after every filter has passed, then exfiltrates chat history, name, location, and
  subscription tier to an attacker URL. Three transferable lessons written up: a sandboxed
  interpreter is an injection decoder, egress allowlisting is the control that actually
  holds, and confirmation gates must fire on what the action does with sensitive state rather
  than on where the text came from.

## inference-and-serving

- **[new] Diffusion drafting arrives in speculative decoding** section on
  `topics/inference-and-serving/summary.md`, covering DFlash2 (z-lab, drafter for
  Qwen3.8-27B): block-diffusion drafting predicts a whole block per forward pass, keeps top
  candidates at every position, and runs a light selector over the lattice to trace one path
  to verify, with two-tap dynamic convolutions countering the usual end-of-block quality
  decay. Verification is unchanged so decoding stays mathematically lossless (greedy output
  identical, sampled distribution preserved); up to 3.43x throughput in vLLM or SGLang, MLX
  supported. Framed against DiffusionGemma: DFlash2 keeps diffusion's parallel-block
  generation and sidesteps its quality tax by making diffusion a proposal mechanism behind an
  exact verifier.

## evaluation-and-llm-judges

- **[new] Double-blind evaluation** section on
  `topics/evaluation-and-llm-judges/summary.md`, covering DeepMind's pilot with the Singapore
  AI Safety Institute, OpenMined, AVERI, and MLCommons (2026-08-27). Weights and benchmark
  prompts enter the same hardware-encrypted enclave (Google Cloud Confidential Space), so the
  lab cannot train on the questions and the evaluator does not receive the model. The point
  is structural: non-contamination becomes a property of the execution environment rather
  than a contractual promise, which is the first mechanism that could let one private
  benchmark be reused across labs and generations without burning it. Caveats recorded: pilot
  scale (Gemini Flash Lite), the enclave is Google's own infrastructure while Google is also
  the evaluated party, and it does nothing about benchmark questions leaking into the
  pretraining crawl from their public source.
