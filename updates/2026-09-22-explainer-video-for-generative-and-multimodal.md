# 2026-09-22: explainer video for generative-and-multimodal

A single addition: the topic overview video for [Topic: generative-and-multimodal](../topics/generative-and-multimodal/summary.md), plus the one thing the page gained while it was being made.

**[new] A narrated explainer video is attached to **[Topic: generative-and-multimodal](../topics/generative-and-multimodal/summary.md)**.** Six minutes forty-one seconds, 1080p30, one narrator with a second voice carrying the viewer's assumption once. It is a topic overview, so it names the whole board before explaining any of it, and the board is the page's own taxonomy rather than a product line: the generative families (autoregressive, diffusion and flow matching, the VAE, the GAN, distillation), the modalities (image, video, audio and speech, 3D and interactive worlds, time series), and the understanding side (vision encoders, vision-language models, omni any-to-any, document parsing). The map is parked as the home frame and every later beat lights the part of it being discussed.

The organising question is the one the page's own second heading sets up, How families map to modalities, asked as something a viewer can carry: which of these landed on a single default architecture, and which did not, and the ones that did, did they all converge on the same family? Then the tour, one modality at a time. Image and video as a single recipe: you do not denoise pixels, you denoise a compressed latent inside a variational autoencoder, with a diffusion transformer over patches of that latent and a rectified-flow objective, and FLUX.2, Stable Diffusion 3.5 and Qwen-Image-2.1 are all three of them that recipe. Video is the same thing with time compressed as well as space, a causal 3D autoencoder whose latents span a block of frames rather than one image, Veo 3.1, Kling 3, Seedance 2 and Wan, and the last line of the beat is the payoff the map set up one beat earlier: two of the five families no longer ship as generators because they became components, the VAE the compressor under all of this and the GAN a loss term that stops these decoders looking blurry. Condition that same generator on actions and you have a world model, which is Genie 3. Then audio, which converged too and onto the other family entirely: a neural audio codec with residual vector quantisation, EnCodec then Mimi, turns a waveform into a few parallel streams of discrete tokens at a low frame rate, and once it is tokens generation is next-token prediction, so the whole language model stack transfers unchanged and speech synthesis and realtime voice are autoregressive. Then text, the case where the alternative is real and lost anyway, drawn as a grid read across: how each one works, what that buys, and what it comes to. One token at a time against a fully masked sequence unmasked many positions per forward pass; exact conditioning against parallelism; the reasoning frontier against a speedplay, with Mercury, the open LLaDA line and Gemini Diffusion named as the ones shipping. Then the input side, where the year's real change happened, as a stack whose bottom layer replaces the three above it: a vision encoder, a projector into the token space the backbone already understands, the language model backbone, or skip all three and fuse the modalities early inside one model trained that way. Then the numbers, late and with who measured them: Qwen-Image-2.1 at 60.28, seventh overall and first among open weights, on Qwen's own benchmark, with the version-numbering trap beside it (3.0 is a separate closed line that shipped first, in July, with no weights; 2.1 is the open line that came after, so the digit marks the product family and not the date) and the research licence under it, non-commercial only, three licences now running across one image line while the language models have not moved. It closes on what the map is actually for, which is not memorising model names: a new image model is almost certainly that recipe and a new voice model is almost certainly a codec with a language model over its tokens, so the useful question about any release is which of those pieces it changed, and on the input side the question is already settled, because native multimodality became the floor rather than the feature and nobody announces it any more. What moves now is the licence, not the architecture.

**The finding worth carrying forward is that a beat's delivered length is not predictable from its word count, and the reserve depends on it. **The nine beats came back between 113 and 147 words a minute, a spread of thirty per cent, and the slowest was the map: a list of short names read deliberately, so 109 words bought 55 seconds rather than the 43 the estimate assumed. A columns panel has exactly three reveals and spread puts the third at beat length minus reserve, so that extra twelve seconds pushed the third column five and a half seconds past the line naming it. Raising the reserve is the only lever there, because the reveal count is fixed by the map and moving the words cannot help: the arithmetic says the last turn can hold only about reserve-times-rate words, which at 4.5 seconds and 113 words a minute is eight, and naming four things takes more than eight. At 6.0, the cap check_timing allows, the lead is about four seconds, which is where the rest of the episode sits. The general form: set reserves from the rendered durations, never from the estimate, and expect the inventory beat to be the one that moves.

**Pace, and the lever that works. **The image-and-video beat came back at 170 words a minute by check_timing's count, over the gallop threshold, and it is the densest beat in the episode. Its commas became full stops and not one word was cut, which is what the method says to do, and it reads at 147. The cost is that a slower read of the same material is a longer beat: it went from 59.7 seconds to 74.5, and that fifteen seconds is the whole difference between the first render at 6:28 and this one at 6:43.
