**[new] A narrated explainer video is attached to **[Topic: math](../topics/math/summary.md)**.** Six minutes fifty-seven seconds, 1080p30, one narrator with a second voice asking the viewer's question twice. It is a topic overview, so it names the whole board before explaining any of it, and this page is the clean case of the second kind of overview: a mental model rather than a comparison. Nothing on it competes with anything else, so the organising question cannot be what separates them. It is the page's own, the one its central section is named after: what does each area let you say about a model that you could not otherwise say? The map is the four areas with their contents, parked as the home frame, and every later beat lights the column it belongs to.

The tour is one area per beat and then the thing only visible with all four in view. Linear algebra as the notation everything is written in: rank, and the observation that fine-tuning updates have low intrinsic rank being exactly what LoRA exploits; the SVD, through Eckart-Young, turning truncation into arithmetic; the condition number as the one number that predicts a long narrow valley; and layout conventions as why a gradient written in a paper arrives transposed in your code. Probability as the move that turns a loss from an arbitrary choice into a consequence, drawn as a grid: assume Gaussian and out comes mean squared error, Bernoulli and binary cross entropy, categorical and softmax cross entropy, Poisson and the Poisson loss, with proper scoring rules as the reason calibration is a meaningful thing to ask about at all. The statistics half gets a short beat of its own and carries the only figure on the page, a one point gain on a five hundred example evaluation, with the one over root n shrinkage that decides whether it is real. Calculus as the mechanics: backpropagation never materialising a Jacobian and only ever asking each operation for a vector-Jacobian product, a backward pass costing roughly twice a forward one, the activations rather than the floating point operations being the binding constraint, and second order arriving as a connection rather than a new fact, because the Hessian is the condition number from two beats earlier and the Fisher information. Information theory as the measurement layer: cross entropy read as a code length, KL as the gap so the loss floors at the data's own entropy rather than at zero, and perplexity being tokenizer-bound where bits-per-byte is not. Then the convergence, which is the most valuable half minute in it: cross entropy is one object wearing four hats, a maximum-likelihood estimator to probability, a code length to information theory, the thing whose gradient collapses to p minus y to calculus, and InfoNCE to contrastive learning. It closes on the map as a diagnosis rather than a syllabus.
