# 2026-09-22: explainer video for ml-fundamentals

A single addition: the topic overview video for [Topic: ml-fundamentals](../topics/ml-fundamentals/summary.md), plus the one gloss the page gained while it was being made.

**[new] A narrated explainer video is attached to **[Topic: ml-fundamentals](../topics/ml-fundamentals/summary.md)**.** Six minutes forty-nine seconds, 1080p30, one narrator with a second voice asking the viewer's question twice. It is a topic overview, so it names the whole board before explaining any of it, and this is the second of the two kinds: a mental model rather than a comparison, because nothing on this page competes with anything else on it. Ten pages of building blocks, almost all of it revision, which is exactly the problem, because read as ten pages it is a syllabus and a syllabus is a thing you can already read for yourself. So the organising question is the one the page's own Map of the space section sets up and then answers in passing: what do the ten say together that none of them says alone?

The map is the page's own taxonomy, in three columns: training components (losses, activations, regularisation, optimisers and their schedules, normalisation and initialisation), evaluation and paradigms (metrics, classical ML, contrastive and self-supervised learning), and history and practice (sequence models, debugging training). It is parked as the home frame and every later beat lights the part of it being discussed. Then four threads, each running across several of the ten pages, which between them touch nine of the ten.

Cross entropy is one object in three places: the objective on the losses page, where label smoothing, class weights and focal loss are that one loss with its terms reweighted rather than four different losses; the same object read as a measurement on the metrics page, as the entropy, cross-entropy and KL trio; and InfoNCE on the contrastive page, an N-way classification over one positive against the rest of the batch, which is why batch size matters there and nowhere else in this topic. Gating is one construction in two eras, which is what makes the sequence-models page not history: open the LSTM gate and it is two projections of the same input with one of them gating the other, and SwiGLU, the transformer's default feed-forward block, is that same construction, so the slope is learned per neuron. Attention came off the same old page, invented to remove the fixed-vector bottleneck in sequence-to-sequence models. The transformer is what is left of that lineage.

Vanishing gradients is one problem seen from four directions, and it is drawn as a grid: saturation on activations, the wrong scale on initialisation, placement on normalisation (those two pages chasing one invariant between them, roughly constant activation and gradient variance across every layer, at step zero and forever after), and a product of Jacobians on sequence models, which is what the additively updated cell state fixes. Debugging training is then the index back through all four rather than a subject of its own. And weight decay belongs to two pages at once, a regulariser by intent and a property of the optimiser by implementation. It closes on what the map is for, which is not a reading order: read as four threads the subject is a good deal smaller than it looks, and where two of these pages disagree about which of them owns a thing, that disagreement is the interesting part rather than a filing error.

The page called weight decay "a regulariser by intent and an optimiser property by implementation, which is exactly why AdamW exists", and its optimiser row lists decoupled weight decay among the things the deep dive covers. What it never said is what the decoupling is from. A reader can follow the link; a listener cannot, and without it the sentence names a thing rather than explaining it. #### Production notes

**The thread axis came from the page rather than from the cleared cut, though that cut had found it too**. The page's Map of the space is not ten summaries; it is a set of observations that each run across several bands, and it closes by saying so outright: "One thread runs through four of these bands at once." Three things from the cleared cut are not kept. It ran eleven beats and about 1,150 words, past the seven-minute wall where the encode ladder gives up 1080p. Its gating and metrics beats were compare panels, two reveals each over forty-second lines, which is the still-frame defect twice in one episode. And not one of its beats carried a reserve.

**The finding worth carrying forward is that splitting a beat's turns is a pace lever that costs almost nothing**. gating and close came back at 163 and 162 words a minute, which check_timing passes and a listener does not. The punctuation lever would have cost length this episode could not afford, because it was already on the last of the three 1080p rungs. Redistributing the identical words across more turns instead, six into nine on gating and six into eleven on close, brought them to 153 and 155 with not one word changed. render.py builds a beat as a conversation with one item per turn, so more turns means more prosodic boundaries. This is the second episode in this batch to find it independently; the jax-and-tpu entry records the same lever. One calibration note that runs the other way from what the method currently says: the method has the renderer's words-per-minute figure reading four to five low against check_timing. On this episode it read high, by five to six, reporting 158, 159 and 161 on takes check_timing measured at 153, 153 and 155. Whichever way it leans, check_timing is the gate that fails the episode, so it is the one to read.

**Reading the transcripts caught the one defect every gate passed, and it inverted a claim the page makes**. gating scored a character error of 0.065, inside the 0.07 floor, with no invented-word burst and a no-speech probability of 0.02. The only vector is what is left of that lineage", against a script reading "So the transformer is what is left of that lineage". The model reused "fixed vector bottleneck" and "one vector" from the sentence before it and produced the exact opposite of what the page says attention did. The cause is the documented one, a distinctive phrase repeated close by, and the fix was to use the word "vector" once in the beat rather than three times.

**The silent preview caught a layout defect the audit cannot see, and it is a vocabulary limit worth writing down**. The weight-decay beat was a three-step flow. panel_flow's pill is a fixed 3.4 units wide with a one-unit arrow in every gap, and three of them do not fit the roughly 7.8 units left beside a parked map, so fit scaled the step labels to about a third of body size and drew no visible arrows. The layout audit reported nothing, correctly, because nothing overlapped and nothing left the frame. It is a points panel now, which also took the beat from three reveals to five. The method says three flow steps is the ceiling beside a parked map; on this evidence the ceiling is two, or the beat wants a different panel.

**Ordering the panel items is a lead fix that has nothing to do with the reserve**. Four beats named an item in an earlier segment than the one its reveal lands in, which is a lead of eight to twenty seconds that no reserve can touch, because a reserve moves the tail and not a reveal. The close said "not a reading order" in its head segment while that line was drawn as reveal two; the question beat said "one question" and "read" a segment early; the cross-entropy beat said "reweighted" and "batch" a segment before the lines carrying them; and the gating beat's last item said "transformer", a word spoken two segments earlier, so the item was reworded to "what is left of the lineage". check_leads now reports every reveal as named at or after the moment it is drawn.
