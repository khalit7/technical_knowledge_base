# 2026-09-22: explainer video for hardware

A single addition: the topic overview video for [Topic: hardware](../topics/hardware/summary.md), plus the one thing the page gained while it was being made.

**[new] A narrated explainer video is attached to **[Topic: hardware](../topics/hardware/summary.md)**.** Six minutes fifty-two seconds, 1080p30, one narrator with a second voice carrying the viewer's assumption once. It is a topic overview, so it names the whole board before explaining any of it, and the board here is not a list of accelerators. This page carries enough part numbers to make a catalogue, and a catalogue read aloud is not an episode. The three columns are the three scales a hardware claim can be made at: per chip (Nvidia from the A100 to Rubin, AMD from the MI300X to the MI400, Google's TPUs up to Ironwood, Groq and Cerebras, Huawei's Ascend 960), the scale-up domain (NVLink and NVSwitch, AMD's Helios rack, Huawei's UnifiedBus, the Cornelis fabric, the TPU ICI torus), and power and supply (DRAM prices, transformer lead times, grid queues, energisation). The map is parked as the home frame and every later beat lights the part of it being discussed.

The organising question is the one the page's own structure sets up and never states in one place: when somebody tells you one of these is faster, faster at what scale? The unit of comparison has moved right twice in about two years and most quoted numbers have not caught up. Then the tour, one scale at a time. Per chip, where three properties still decide what you can run at all, and where the thing missing from that list is peak arithmetic: memory, not arithmetic, is the binding constraint, and the two specialists are that claim taken to its extremes, Groq removing high-bandwidth memory entirely for about 230 MB of on-chip SRAM and latency deterministic to the cycle, Cerebras declining to cut the wafer up at all so that for a model that fits the network between chips stops existing, and Positron's $875M raised before the chip taped out to build around commodity memory instead. Then the rack, where NVLink's 1.8 TB/s inside a domain against 400 to 800 gigabits a network card outside it is roughly a twentieth of the bandwidth, so the domain boundary rather than the chip decides your parallelism, and that tier is contested three ways: AMD answering like for like with 31 TB of HBM4 a rack, Cornelis raising $205M for an open fabric, and Huawei simply making the domain bigger, to a claimed million linked processors. Then the one independent measurement, drawn as a grid read across, because the page is unusually careful about which of its own numbers to quote: the claim, how it was measured, and how to read it. 7x per megawatt on pre-release software against the 3x Nvidia claimed for itself; 1.4x to 3x per total cost of ownership at 60 to 100 tokens a second, which is the one to use; and 67x per dollar, an extreme operating point and an outlier. Then the third scale, where the topic stops being about chips at all and becomes whether the building they sit in can be switched on: roughly 15GW scheduled for 2027 that may sit dark against an estimated 66GW of American demand, transformers on 48 to 60 month lead times, interconnection queues backlogged, the figure itself originating with an Elon Musk post and so directional rather than audited, and the bottlenecks under it independently documented. That is where the $149.9B against $105.3B of modelled profit per utility gigawatt belongs, because it is a grid measurement and not a rack one. It closes on the question turned into a habit: per chip is what fits in memory, per rack is what you can split, per megawatt is what it pays, and the last thing to ask of any of them is who measured it.

#### Production notes

**The inventory axis came from the cut of this episode cleared when the method changed, which found it independently, and it is the page's own**: the page says outright that both vendors have moved the unit of comparison from the card to the rack, it gives a whole heading to rack-scale competition and another to the grid. A tour of Nvidia against AMD against Google against the ASICs is what the page's taxonomy diagram looks like, and it is the wrong axis. Four things from that cut are not kept. It ran ten beats and 1,222 words, about eight and a half minutes, one rung below the length at which the render fails outright. Not one of its beats carried a reserve, and its rack beat said "look at those two rows together" while spread had drawn two of five. Three of its beats were two-reveal panels over forty-second lines, a compare, a stat and a claim, which is the still-frame defect the timing check exists to catch, shipping three times in one episode. And its caveat beat narrated the rules: "the page says plainly it is an extreme operating point and should not be quoted, so I am not going to." The behaviour is already visible in not quoting it.

**A 3,110-word page with four deep dives under it does not fit in one episode, and a good deal was cut rather than compressed; the script's docstring records what**. The obvious second episode is the supply and grid half on its own, which is one heading of the page and gets one beat here: DRAM at 500%, Samsung doubling HBM4 output, transformer lead times, interconnection queues, H.R. 9340 passing 417 to 3, the Nvidia and Google grid-stress framework, and SemiAnalysis's 2.3GW of restriction-delayed capacity against 300-plus local moratoria. Five dated items in one cluster and no room inside a beat that already has to explain energisation. Also cut: Nvidia's Q2 FY27 print and the $12.93B Hugging Face acquisition, both large stories that do no work on a spine about measurement scale; the Cerebras CS-4, because the wafer-scale argument lands on WSE-3's own numbers and a second system in the same breath is a product roundup; AWS Trainium and Inferentia, whose vertical-integration argument is a different one from the memory argument and wants its own beat; Arm's Neoverse CSS N4 and the A20 Pro, which are a fourth scale below the chip; Huawei's full Ascend 960 schedule and the Atlas SuperPoDs; and high-bandwidth flash. The performance-math bullet was deliberately not squeezed in: it is the arithmetic the whole episode rests on and it is a deep dive of its own.

**The finding worth carrying forward is that a panel's reveals are spread evenly in time, so the narration has to be written in segments of even length, and no reserve can repair it afterwards**. The first render was clean on every check and three of its beats were wrong in a way none of them sees. Rack, grid and close each had one segment roughly twice the length of its neighbours, because the meaty part of the argument naturally wants more words, and the reveals therefore fell seven to thirteen seconds behind the words that named them. A reserve moves the tail and not one reveal, so the only fix is structural: all three beats were rewritten into segments of about thirty words each for a six-reveal beat and re-recorded. Every landing is now within four seconds of its words. The reserves themselves are capped by the still frame rather than by taste, since check_timing fails a frame held over six seconds and still lands about 0.4 above the reserve, so 5.5 is the practical ceiling and the ideal values the arithmetic wanted (5.9, 6.6, 6.7) are all above it. The parked map is the exception: it spends the two-second settle and the 0.7-second morph out of the front of its reserve, so 7.5 measures as 5.17 of still frame.

**Two text-to-speech defects passed the character gate and were caught by reading the transcripts as text, and neither is a class the gates are built for**. A single real word swapped for another: "the domain boundary picks your parallelism" came back as "the domain boundary picture parallelism" at a character error of 0.019, which is not a sentence; "decides your parallelism" reads correctly. And a figure read as a different figure, which is worse because the right one was on screen underneath it: "four hundred to eight hundred gigabits a network card" arrived as "700 to 800" at a character error of 0.052. Written "between four hundred and eight hundred" it came back correct. The same take turned "A M D's Helios answers" into "A Form-D's helios amperes", which is the existing rule about not opening a sentence on the fragile name: "Take A M D first. Helios answers like for like" reads correctly. One thing that looks like a defect and is not: "There, NVLink gives..." transcribes as "Their NVLink gives...", and since they are homophones no reroll can change it.

**On length**. This lands at 6:52 and 4.47 MiB, the third and last 1080p rung of the rate-factor ladder, and that is closer to the seven-minute wall than is comfortable. It is entirely the pace fix: the rack beat came back at 174 words a minute, over the gallop threshold, and turning its commas into full stops brought it to 156 without a word being cut, at a cost of sixteen seconds. That is the trade the method asks for and it is the right one, but the arithmetic is worth stating plainly for the next person: on a page this dense, the pace lever and the seven-minute wall pull directly against each other, and the way to buy the time back is to drop a beat rather than to put the commas back. Overall the episode runs at 151 words a minute.

**The video was not watched**. Nothing here can play a file, so the proxies were used and are worth naming: a frame pulled per beat and read by eye, including the completed state of every panel and the three-column table whose header sits over the right columns; every transcript read as text against its line, which is what caught both voice defects above and the wrong figure in particular; check_references, whose four hits were all ordinary English rather than pointers, with the two real pointers in the script ("whole board first" and "read it across") checked by hand against the reveal arithmetic; and the per-beat words-per-minute table that check_timing prints, which is what found the 174. Pace, whether the three-scale frame lands, and whether the take is worth hearing are not covered by any of that.
