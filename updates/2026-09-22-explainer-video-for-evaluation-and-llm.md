It closes on the attribution rule turned into habits that cost nothing, and on the newest version of the problem, that a stated refusal is not a refusal once the harm lives in the tool calls.

#### Production notes

The inventory axis came from the cut of this episode that was cleared when the method changed, which found it independently, and it is the one thing worth keeping from it: not a list of tools but what you can run, what runs it, and what inside it can be lying. A tour of lm-eval-harness against Inspect against promptfoo would have been a procurement video, and this page is not about procurement. MOLE survives as the page states it, as a claim about what gets graded, with no figure attached.

A dense page does not fit in one episode and a good deal was cut rather than compressed; the script's docstring records what. The omission that hurts most is guardrails, one of the page's six branches and the obvious second episode from it: pre-call, during-call and post-call rails, NeMo Guardrails and the guard-model landscape are a runtime-safety subject rather than a measurement one, and the map's three columns have no place for it without pretending a rail is an eval. Also cut: production eval engineering as a subject, with golden sets, promotion paths and offline-against-online metrics surviving only as habits in the take and the statistics not surviving at all, because a paired test explained quickly is a paired test explained wrongly; the full failure-mode catalogue, since six bias names in sequence is the undifferentiated-list failure; and the double-blind pilot's other contamination path, the benchmark's own questions leaking into the pretraining crawl from their public source, which needs its own thirty seconds and would have been the beat's third caveat.

The finding worth carrying forward is that a panel's reveal count is a constraint on how the narration is written, not just on how long the beat runs. spread puts reveal n of n at exactly beat length minus reserve, and check_timing caps the reserve at six seconds, so whatever is said after the last item is named has to fit in about fifteen words. Draft one put forty-three words after the map's third column was named, and drew that column thirteen seconds late; the harness beat named HELM and promptfoo twelve and sixteen seconds before their rows existed. No check sees this: the layout audit is clean, the timing check is clean, every still frame is under the limit, and the narrator is simply pointing at things that are not there. The fix is structural. A panel with n reveals wants its narration written as n roughly equal segments, with the last item named inside the reserve, and two panels here gained a row so that a closing claim had a reveal of its own to land on rather than having to sit in the tail. Every landing is now within about four seconds of the words.

Nine text-to-speech defects passed the character gate and were caught by reading the transcripts as text, which is worth setting out because the classes are not the ones the gate is built for. Two were whole hallucinated sentences: 'Every harness scores in one of two ways' came back as 'All of them suck. They all score in one of two ways', and a four-word fragment, 'each side puts in one thing', grew 'What a routine.' after it. Three were insertions into the same list of three short noun phrases, 'The model. The judge. The gold labels.', which took 'the readers', then 'the joke', then a bare 'you', on three different seeds; a list of bare noun phrases is a gap the model fills, and each item has a clause now. Two were inverted sentences: 'becomes a property of the machine' came back as 'becomes a promise. The property of the machine', which says the opposite and tripped on the repeated word, and 'Not picking a harness. For remembering that' was delivered as one clause meaning something else. Two were single real words swapped for other single real words, 'One bias' for 'Position bias' and 'forward the same way every time' for 'scored the same way every time', which is the class neither the error rate nor the burst detector can see. Names were their own problem: lighteval came back as 'LittleMul' and then, worse, as the same string the model used for lm-eval-harness, so two different harnesses were named identically until it became Hugging Face's Light Eval; HELM lost its first syllable and arrived as 'LM' until it was spelled as the word Helm; and RocketEval was mangled on four seeds running until it stopped being the first thing in its sentence.

This episode chose the alignment. A cut to six minutes is a cut of a beat, not a trim of every beat.
