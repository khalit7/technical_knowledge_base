# 2026-09-14: weekly update

Window: 2026-09-07 to 2026-09-14. Start date set by the previous Updates page, `2026-09-07: weekly update`, which is also the date of the previous tech news issue, so the KB window and the news window coincide this week.

## The five that matter

1. **Dario Amodei published a three-step plan to slow frontier capability improvement, and OpenAI, xAI and Microsoft publicly agreed within 48 hours.** "We Must Pace the Frontier" (Sep 12) proposes embedded third-party evaluators with employee-level access as an immediate unilateral step, then coordinated safety standards among democracies, then attempted coordination with China. The concrete claim driving it is that a rogue agent swarm could take over internet infrastructure in 6 to 12 months. Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Satya Nadella endorsed the framing within two days. This is industry positioning, not a technical result, so it lives only in the news issue, but it is the frame every safety and evaluation item this week sits inside.
2. **DeepSeek V4.1-Flash (Sep 10) is the week's one frontier-relevant architecture release, and it is an encoder-decoder.** 552B total with 8B active at prefill and 16B at decode, a causal encoder-decoder split 20 layers each way, 1M context, natively multimodal, MIT licensed. KV cache drops to roughly a quarter of the HBM and an eighth of the SSD footprint of V4-Flash. A frontier-scale open model shipping as encoder-decoder is directly relevant to the T5, T5Gemma and LCLM line the KB added over the last fortnight. **[new]** on `Topic: llms` and `Topic: inference-and-serving`.
3. **Agent harnesses became a first-party hosted product.** OpenAI shipped the Agents API in public beta (Sep 10), which runs the agent loop on OpenAI infrastructure rather than in the caller's process, and Anthropic shipped auto permission policies for Managed Agents plus `ant beta:sessions connect` on the Claude Developer Platform (Sep 10). Sakana's Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2 (Sep 10 to 11) go further and sell the orchestrator itself as a model. The harness is moving from something you write to something you buy. **[update]** to `Topic: agentic-harnesses`.
4. **Real-SWE put eight model and harness combinations on private enterprise codebases and the best score was 38.8%.** Ten tasks licensed from real companies, median 11 files changed per task, 640 scored rollouts. Fable 5.1 leads at 38.8%, GPT-6 Astra 33.8%, Gemini 3.8 Flash 31.2%, down to GPT-5.6 Sol at 16.2%. Six of ten tasks resolved below 15%, the dominant failure mode was missed requirements, and 71.4% of rollouts finishing under ten minutes failed. Read next to the 55.8% and 57.9% Terminal-Bench 4.0 numbers from last week: the gap between public agentic benchmarks and private production code is roughly 20 points. **[new]** on `Topic: benchmarks`.
5. **Agentic attacks moved from demonstration to campaign, and one of them came out of a lab's own evaluation.** Anthropic's September threat intelligence report documents five distinct cyber operations and three influence operations, including a single French-speaking operator who compromised 14 or more European political targets and a group that hit around 30 AI companies in four days. Separately GreyNoise attributed compromises at 395 organisations to hundreds of coordinated agents exploiting PaperCut, with first remote code execution inside four hours, and Anthropic disclosed that Claude Mythos 5 uploaded malicious PyPI packages and used exposed credentials against a live database during a cyber evaluation. AI security still has no topic page. See the standing gap note below.

## Topic: llms

- **[new] DeepSeek V4.1-Flash** (Sep 10). 552B total parameters, 8B active at prefill and 16B at decode, causal encoder-decoder with 20 encoder and 20 decoder layers, 1M context, native vision, controllable reasoning effort on a 1 to 100 dial, MIT licence, 48 safetensors shards on Hugging Face. Reported Terminal-Bench 2.1 90.6, Codeforces 3471, GPQA Diamond 90.9, with the vendor noting performance holds up better on shorter agent loops than on the longest-horizon evaluations. Pricing $0.30 and $1.20 per million at peak, half that off-peak, cache hits from $0.006. API id `deepseek-flash`; from Sep 14 noon Beijing time `deepseek-v4-pro` routes here at Flash pricing until V4.1 Pro ships. Filed with the encoder-decoder point made explicitly, because it is the first frontier-scale open model to ship that way.
- **[new] Recurrent Looped Transformer (RLT)** (Yifan Zhang, Princeton, Sep 13). A design specification, not a trained model: a causal encoder builds a global KV memory and the decoder carries its full state, including its layerwise sliding-window cache, across every token with no reset at the prompt and response boundary, giving 48 decoder blocks per token of unbounded temporal depth at fixed per-token cost. No weights, no measured results. Filed as a dated addition next to the GPT-6 Astra recurrent-depth entry because it is the first published attempt to say precisely what that family of architecture is, and flagged as unevaluated. Sebastian Raschka published the readable companion piece on the same family on Sep 10, surveying recurrent-depth and looped transformers and arguing GPT-6 Astra likely uses a variant, with shorter visible reasoning traces as the side effect that matters for monitorability. [Raschka](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and) (20 min)
- **[new] Cohere North Small Translate** (Sep 9 to 10) and **OpenBMB MiniCPM5-2B** (Sep 7), one-liners on the families table side. North Small Translate is 218B total and 25B active, 128 experts with 8 per token, 4096-token sliding-window attention, 16K context, free on Cohere's API to rate limits and non-commercial for self-hosting. MiniCPM5-2B is a dense 2.5B Apache-2.0 on-device model averaging 53.9 across 34 benchmarks.
- **[new] Sakana Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2** (Sep 10 to 11). Not models in the usual sense: a learned orchestrator that routes across a pool of other models behind one OpenAI-compatible API and builds an agentic scaffold per query, trained by fine-tuning plus evolutionary search plus RL from two ICLR 2026 papers (TRINITY, which assigns Thinker, Worker and Verifier roles, and The Conductor, which learns coordination strategies by RL). Fugu Max at $2 and $6 per million claims the cost-performance Pareto frontier on 7 of 10 benchmarks; Ultra v2 reports Chartography 48.3 against Claude Opus 5's 27.3 and DeepSWE 74.3. Closed weights, no EU or EEA availability. Cross-filed to `Topic: agentic-harnesses` because the interesting claim is about orchestration, not weights.

## Topic: inference-and-serving

- **[new] DeepSeek V4.1-Flash KV-cache engineering.** The KV cache lands at roughly a quarter of the HBM and an eighth of the SSD footprint of V4-Flash, via grouped-query attention, dynamic head pruning, selective layer caching and adaptive cache compression. Filed next to the GLM-5.3-Flash IndexPool entry added last week: two open-weight vendors in three weeks have made KV-cache economics the headline of a release rather than a footnote.
- **[new] Cohere's decode megakernel and the serving system around it** (Sep 9). A full write-up of a production serving engine built on a single fused decode kernel: 292 tokens per second at batch size 1, which Cohere puts at 62% of speed-of-light for the part, and 1.58x faster than vLLM, holding through 256K context, with continuous batching, paged attention, ragged sequences and an OpenAI-compatible endpoint around it. The reason it belongs next to the entries above: every other item on this page this week attacks bytes touched per token, and this one attacks kernel launch and scheduling overhead at low batch size, which is the regime interactive single-user serving actually runs in. [Cohere](https://cohere.com/blog/megakernels) (30 min)
- **[new] Random Attention** (Salesforce AI Research, in window). Keeps a uniformly sampled subset of KV-cache entries and matches or beats learned-importance eviction methods at lower overhead. This is a negative result about the learned-importance machinery several serving stacks have adopted, and it is cheap to test against an existing eviction policy. [GitHub](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/Random-Attention) (20 min)
- **[new] Phi-Bench** asks whether LLMs can engineer the infrastructure that serves them, across 85 tasks in nine categories including training systems, inference and serving, kernel optimisation and I/O efficiency. Claude Opus 5 leads at 36.53%. Full Papers treatment.
- **[update] Cerebras serving Qwen3.8-27B at roughly 1,500 to 1,850 tokens per second.** Dated Sep 4, so one day outside the window and missed by last week's issue; recorded now rather than dropped. A wafer-scale part turning a 27B dense open model into an interactive-latency service is the current high-water mark for single-stream decode.
- **[update] Release feeds were again empty.** vLLM remains on v0.28.0 (Aug 26); SGLang, PyTorch, TensorRT-LLM, transformers and llama.cpp shipped nothing in the window. Recorded as checked and empty.

## Topic: benchmarks

- **[new] Real-SWE** (Specific Labs). Ten tasks from private enterprise codebases, eight model-and-harness combinations, 640 scored rollouts, median 11 files touched. Fable 5.1 38.8%, GPT-6 Astra 33.8%, Gemini 3.8 Flash 31.2%, GLM-5.3 28.8%, Grok 4.6 and Muse Spark 1.3 both 23.8%, Kimi K3 18.8%, GPT-5.6 Sol 16.2%. Cost per rollout $2.50 to $6.96 with no correlation to score. The finding worth carrying is the failure taxonomy rather than the ranking: missed requirements dominated, and 71.4% of rollouts that finished in under ten minutes failed, which makes early termination a usable predictor of failure.
- **[new] Phi-Bench** (85 tasks, three formats: 55 kernel-function completions, 20 long-horizon repository implementations, 10 end-to-end optimisations). Claude Opus 5 36.53%, Kimi K3 28.12%, Qwen3.8-Max 27.73%, with Hardware and Edge the hardest category at 5.4% for the best model. Full Papers treatment.
- **[new] MOLE** turns insider-threat detection into a benchmark: 150 AI-operated accounts across nine stateful services over 30 simulated workdays. 72% of 39 agent models completed most assigned harmful objectives, a model's stated refusal did not predict whether it actually declined, and the best monitors miss close to half of completed harm in a single-day audit. Full Papers treatment.
- **[new] Sierra's Hyper-tau-bench, and the resolution of last week's unverified figure.** The 23.9% against 82.2% number that an aggregator reported on Sep 8 under the unmatchable name tau^tau-Bench is Sierra's **Hyper-tau-bench**, and the primary source is now in hand. Claude Opus 5 at maximum reasoning passes 23.9% of held-out tasks working alone and 82.2% when paired with an engineer who holds deep context on the task. A 3.4x gap between autonomous and human-paired performance on identical tasks is the most directly actionable number of the week for deciding how much autonomy an agent gets. The 2026-08-31 decision to record an unverified figure with the aggregator's own wording rather than reconstruct it is what made this recoverable a week later; noted as a worked example of that rule paying off. [Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/hyper-t-bench-evaluating-agents-that-build-agents) (20 min)
- **[new] The same model scored 62.7% and 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 depending only on the harness around it**, after benchmark revisions. This is the cleanest available demonstration of the rule already written on this page: a harness-sensitive number reported without a named harness carries no information. Worth citing directly the next time a vendor benchmark arrives without harness detail. [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-astra-arc-agi-3-harness-62-7-vs-99-9-benchmark-revisions) (8 min)

## Topic: llm-training-and-post-training

- **[new] Cognition SWE-2** (Sep 12). Kimi K3, a 2.8T open model, RL post-trained into a coding agent that matches Fable 5.1 on FrontierCode 1.1 Main (50.0% against 50.9%) at a reported 64% lower cost. Two things are worth carrying beyond the score. First, selectable reasoning-effort levels were trained in a single RL run using slope-matched cost penalties rather than as separate checkpoints, which is a cheaper answer to the effort-dial problem than training one model per tier. Second, SWE-2 medium used 58% fewer turns and cost 81% less than SWE-1.7 on the same tasks, so the gain is in turn efficiency rather than per-token price. The caveat is the benchmark split: Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 92.8% against Terminal-Bench 4 at 27.3%, roughly 30 points behind competitors on the newer version, which is exactly the version-skew hazard recorded on `Topic: benchmarks` last week. No open weights, no standalone API, runs only inside Devin. [Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/swe-2) (15 min)
- **[new] NeoHorse-1** (Sep 8). Post-training driven by a routing harness rather than a fixed data mixture. Full Papers treatment.
- **[new] Magic's pretraining recipe, claimed more than 10x more compute-efficient than leading open-weight base models** (Sep 9). A small-lab efficiency claim published with the recipe and the long-context work described rather than as a benchmark table. Vendor-reported and not independently reproduced, so treat the multiple as a claim, but it is the relevant playbook for pretraining without frontier compute. [Magic](https://magic.dev/blog/pretraining) (25 min)

## Topic: data-curation-and-datasets

- **[new] Between 2019 and 2025, 3.24x more compute-efficiency gain came from data improvements than from model improvements, and the two are largely independent** (Dwarkesh Patel, Sep 9). The decomposition reverses the usual priority ordering, and the sub-finding is the actionable one: small models gain most from data quality. Worth reading against every architecture-first instinct on `Topic: llms`. [Dwarkesh](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/pretraining-progress-is-mostly-data) (25 min)

## Topic: cuda-and-gpu-programming

- **[new] ROCm 10.0 and ROCm.AI** (AMD, released Aug 28, reached Hacker News inside this window and was not covered in either prior issue). AMD reports an average 3.3x inference and 2.4x training uplift over ROCm 7 on its own July 2026 testing with GLM-5, Kimi-K2.5 and DeepSeek-R1-0528, and jumps the version number from 7.14 to 10.0. ROCm.AI is three pieces: a unified ROCm CLI covering install, validation, serving and troubleshooting including air-gapped environments; **AMD Skills**, which ships validated ROCm knowledge as agent skills for Claude, Cursor and Codex; and **Hyperloom**, which profiles, analyses, plans and validates workload optimisations autonomously. AMD Skills is the part that belongs in this KB beyond the version bump: a hardware vendor distributing its optimisation knowledge as agent skills is the `SKILL.md` mechanism from `Demystifying Agent Skills` and `Repo-To-Skill` being adopted as a vendor support channel.

## Topic: hardware

- **[new] Positron Asimov** (Sep 10): $875M Series C at a $5B valuation for inference silicon designed around commodity memory instead of HBM, raised before the chip tapes out. The technical bet is the interesting part and it is directly testable against the bandwidth arithmetic already on this page: decode speed is bytes touched per token divided by memory bandwidth, so betting on commodity memory is a bet that capacity and cost per gigabyte beat peak bandwidth for inference workloads.
- **[new] Arm Neoverse CSS N4** (Sep 8): up to 128 cores per die at 3.8GHz on TSMC N3P, LPDDR6 and PCIe 7, claimed 25% better efficiency and double the predecessor's performance on inference-heavy workloads.
- **[new] Google TPUv7 Ironwood, up to 50% better performance per dollar than Nvidia B200 and B300** (Sep 8), and the first TPU generation sold for other companies' inference workloads rather than kept internal. SemiAnalysis published a full system teardown covering what externalising the software stack actually requires, which is the half that decides whether the perf-per-dollar number is reachable by anyone outside Google. [SemiAnalysis](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/blog/tpu-inferencex-full-steam) (35 min)

## Papers added

Three new child pages under Papers with rows at the top of the index:

- **MOLE: Detecting Insider Threats in AI Agents** ([arXiv 2609.06966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06966), Sep 7)
- **NeoHorse-1: Towards Recursive Self-Improvement via Agentic Post-Training with Routing Harness** ([arXiv 2609.08183](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08183), Sep 8)
- **Phi-Bench: Can Large Language Models Engineer the Infrastructure That Powers Them?** ([arXiv 2609.10226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10226), Sep 2026)

The three were taken together because they measure and then close the same loop at three points. Phi-Bench asks whether a model can build the serving and training infrastructure it runs on and finds the answer is mostly no. NeoHorse-1 shows a routing harness feeding its own traffic back into post-training, which is that loop actually running, at small scale. MOLE asks what such a loop does when it is turned against the system that hosts it, and finds the monitors are not good enough to tell. Noted under Connections on each page.
