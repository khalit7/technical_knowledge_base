It is a topic overview, and the second of the two kinds: a mental model rather than a comparison, because nothing on this page competes with anything else on it. The axis is the page's own opening line, the stack in the order a tensor meets it, because the diagram below it is otherwise a wall of proper nouns. The board is four columns of that stack: core, which is ATen, the dispatcher, autograd and the device backends; compilation, which is TorchDynamo, AOTAutograd, TorchInductor and torch.export; distributed, which is c10d, DTensor, FSDP2 and pipelining; and performance, which is SDPA backends, FlexAttention, torchao and the profiler. The training frameworks and the deployment edge are the two bands deliberately left off it, because they are what the episode argues about.

The organising question is not what all those names are. The page says twice, in two paragraphs that never meet, that the dispatcher is the extension point every backend, custom op and tensor subclass hooks into, and that FSDP2, tensor parallel, pipelining and distributed checkpointing are all expressed in terms of DTensor, which is precisely why they compose. That is one idea stated at two levels: two narrow interfaces, and almost everything else on the board is a user of one of them rather than a thing in its own right. So the tour takes the two interfaces first, then the compiler and the performance layer as things expressed in them, and then a beat that tests the claim rather than decorating it. Three things on the page are dead or dying and the useful question about each is why. FSDP1 is deprecated as of 2.11 in favour of fully_shard, and it did not die of neglect: DTensor arrived underneath and expressed it better. torchtune is discontinued, and it was never an interface anything was built on. torchforge, its intended successor, is paused, and both arrows point at torchtitan. The take is that the interfaces are the bottom and the wrappers are the churn, that the cadence is about one minor release a quarter, and that the habit which keeps this map true is rechecking the top of it and never the bottom.

Delivered at 6:54.4 and 4.27 MiB, on the third and last 1080p rung, at 144 words a minute overall. The page's blurb rounds that to a 7-minute explainer, which is the uploader's convention.

**check_leads found eleven reveals named before they were drawn, in a draft that read perfectly well, and the fix is arithmetic rather than taste.** It is worth writing the arithmetic down, because it is not obvious and every author seems to rediscover it. With n equal segments and a reserve of r, the last reveal lands about (W/n) x 0.4 minus r seconds after its own segment begins, so each item has to be named a few words into its segment and further in for each later one; the offset for reveal k is (k-1)/(n-1) x (L - 2.5r) words, where L is the segment length in words. Every panel item here is also a phrase the narration says verbatim, because the check matches a reveal's label as a contiguous squashed run, so an item worded differently from the line is not checked at all, which looks exactly like a pass. Running it with --estimate before the voice existed caught all eleven on the CPU, and the measured run afterwards was clean.
