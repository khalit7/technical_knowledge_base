**[new] A narrated explainer video is attached to **[Topic: rl](../topics/rl/summary.md)**.** Seven minutes eleven seconds, 1080p30, one narrator with a second voice asking the viewer's question three times. It is a topic overview, and nominally the second of the two kinds: a mental model rather than a comparison, because nothing on this page competes with anything else on it. But the page does tell one story and the episode follows that rather than inventing one. The board is three columns, left to right in the order the field arrived. The classical core, which is Markov decision processes and the Bellman equations, dynamic programming, Monte Carlo and TD, and SARSA and Q-learning. Deep RL, which is DQN, REINFORCE, A2C and A3C, TRPO and PPO, SAC, and AlphaZero and MuZero. And RL for LLMs, which is RLHF, GRPO and RLVR.

The organising question is what actually changes when the environment is a conversation and the reward is a verifier, and the page answers it in one sentence the episode turns into its spine: token generation is a Markov decision process with a deterministic transition, a vocabulary-sized action space and a single reward at the end. So the transition distribution, which is the expensive unknown in every classical setting and the thing the whole model-based line exists to cope with, becomes free; and the reward, which classically arrives as you go, becomes the expensive part, either a learned model of human preference or a verifier somebody had to build. The tour walks the board left to right and says at each step which piece survives, and most of it does. The lineage at the end is read as two deletions rather than three products: RLHF is the full machinery, GRPO deletes the critic and samples the baseline from a group instead, RLVR deletes the reward model and puts a programmatic verifier in its place. The take is that the recursion still holds and an advantage is still an advantage, that what moved is where the difficulty sits, and that this is why the frontier recipe this page links to spends its length on the loop around the algorithm rather than on the algorithm.
