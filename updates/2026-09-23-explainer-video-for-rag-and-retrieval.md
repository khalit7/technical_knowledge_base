So the question beat asks whether you build the pipeline or hand retrieval to the model, and the tour answers it in the page's own order. The pipeline first, because that is the half with measurements. Chunking moves quality more than the embedding model does, because a chunk that has lost its document context cannot be matched by a query that assumes that context. BM25 catches the exact identifiers and rare names dense vectors miss, dense vectors catch the paraphrases keyword matching misses, and the two lists are fused on rank rather than on score by reciprocal rank fusion, because the score scales are not comparable. Then a cross-encoder reranks between fifty and a hundred and fifty candidates in between thirty and three hundred milliseconds, which the page calls the highest-leverage single addition to a stack that works.

Then the page's biggest claim, which is that at the top of the market the corpus and not the model is what is being sold. Astra for Law is one model paired with a proprietary legal index of more than 230 million URLs, claimed to cover more than 99.9% of published US precedential case law, and on the Vals AI Legal Research Bench it is correct on 54% of questions against 38.7% for the same weights with ordinary web search. Fifteen points, drawn as two bars sharing a baseline, all of it the retrieval layer, because generic web search is an index too and just a bad one for the job. The other side of the board is read as a ladder of who decides what to retrieve: naive RAG retrieves once with the raw query, advanced RAG wraps that in a fixed graph you designed, agentic RAG hands retrieval to the model as a tool it calls until it judges the context sufficient, and at the far end a million-token window absorbs the small-corpus case where an index was always pure overhead. The newest result on the page moves the argument sideways: for a long-horizon agent the open variable is when to retrieve rather than what, and a second model deciding at each step whether to remind an acting agent lifted Terminal-Bench 2.0 from 37.6% to 45.9% for Claude Sonnet 4.5 with no retraining of the worker, because reminding selectively beats reminding at every step. The take is that neither architecture decides it. The corpus does, and the shape of the question does, and a retrieval-sensitive number reported without a named index carries no information at all, which includes the vendor's own figures the episode quotes.
