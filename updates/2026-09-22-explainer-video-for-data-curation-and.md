#### Production notes

The inventory axis came from the cut of this episode that was cleared when the method changed, and it is the one thing worth keeping from it: three stages of one process, not three subjects. The page's own map has four bullets and its diagram has five branches, and following either produces a tour of a tree rather than a walk along a pipeline, with mixing stranded beside corpora as though it were a different kind of thing.

An 1,837-word page does not fit in six minutes and a good deal was cut rather than compressed; the script's docstring records what. Column one lost its own beat, which is the decision a reader of the page will query first: its content is a list of names, and its one argument, that what separates the generations is a better quality classifier rather than more raw data, is a claim about column two. So the lineage is named in full during the map beat and the argument was handed to the filtering beat, where the classifier actually lives. The omission that hurts most is the CMR scaling law and with it the whole continued-pretraining thread, because how much replay is the question a practitioner actually arrives with; the page hands CPT mechanics to fine-tuning-and-adaptation, so the episode stops at the general mixing idea. Also cut: the tooling table, datatrove against the Dolma toolkit against NeMo Curator, which is a thing to look up rather than a thing to watch and survives as one clause; decontamination and PII scrubbing; self-instruct, LIMA and UltraFeedback as named datasets; the 150-350M recommendation, which is the single most actionable line on the page and reads far better than it plays; and the FineWeb2, FinePDFs, Dolma 1-3 and Nemotron-CC v1 to v2.1 version history. The second episode sitting in the leftovers is the pipeline itself as a deep dive, one mechanism followed down, with FineWeb's and DCLM's per-stage ablations as the evidence.

Three defects were found by the checks and the frames rather than by watching. **A reserve cannot fix a still frame; it can only make one.** The take was a three-layer stack and held motionless for 17.7 seconds, which check_timing fails at six. The arithmetic is that spread waits a full gap after the last reveal as well, so a beat's motionless tail is about its length divided by its number of reveals, plus the reserve. Raising the reserve makes it worse. The take is now six points over a shorter line and holds for five seconds. The same arithmetic says a parked map cannot use its reserve to pull reveals forward, because on a parked beat the reserve is the still frame, so when the map beat named its third column nine seconds before the column was drawn the fix was to move B's turn between columns two and three: move the words, not the reserve. **A seed swallowed an acronym expansion whole.** Two takes in a row said "DCLM argues for aggressive selection" and dropped "DataComp for Language Models" entirely, and the character gate passed both at 0.043 because a five-word deletion in a 600-character beat is under the floor. Reading the transcript as text is what caught it. Split into "Data Comp" with a "which is" in front, it survives. A third take had said "Omissions" where the script said "And", at a character error of 0.017, which is the single-word substitution class that neither gate sees. **A line break broke a word.** The 3.24x card's caption was split over two lines to stop it crowding the frame edge, and manim rendered the second line as "th  an from models", a gap inside the word itself. Nothing reports that: no overlap, nothing off frame, type well above the legibility floor. It was found by looking at the frame, which is what frames are for now that the layout audit catches collisions.

On length, which is a deviation worth stating. The skill says plan at about 150 words a minute and aim to land under six. The script is 942 words and lands at 6:37 and 4.23 MiB, so it delivers at 1080p on the third and last of the rate-factor ladder's 1080p rungs. The first draft ran to 1,172 words and about eight and a quarter minutes, which is past where the encode gives up 1080p and near where the render fails outright; the beat that bought most of the cut was column one's. The one beat that reads fast is the environments beat at 164 words a minute, one under the limit, and it was left alone rather than rewritten into a reroll with an unknown pace.
