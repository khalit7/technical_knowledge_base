#### Production notes

**One number explains all four frozen frames, and the script had written down the wrong one.** The published file carried a paragraph saying that spread waits a full gap after the last reveal, so a beat's motionless tail is its length divided by its reveals plus the reserve, and that a reserve cannot fix a still frame but only make one. That is no longer true. The tail is the reserve and nothing else: reserve plus 0.35 on an ordinary beat and reserve minus 2.35 on a parked one, both measured to within a twentieth of a second on this render. Four reserves here had been set to 11, 16, 21 and 22 to buy leads back, and every one of them bought an equal number of seconds of frozen frame. The unchanged script was re-rendered first, as the method says to, and the timing file on disk turned out to be nearly right: the four stills were already recorded honestly and only the map beat's had been written by the older tool, reading 5.13 where the truth was 3.13.

**Two panels could not be repaired and had to change kind.** filtering and environments were compare panels on beats of 55.6 and 37.7 seconds. A compare has exactly two reveals, one per side, so the second lands at the beat's length minus its reserve however the reserve is set, and they wanted 11.8 and 12.4 against an ordinary ceiling of 5.5. There is no row to add on a compare. A table is worse here than it looks, and the reason is worth having written down: both beats run one side and then the other, while a table reveals a row at a time across both columns, so every row is named in the first half and drawn in the second, which is a larger lead than the one being fixed. Both are lists now, of eight and seven reveals. Losing the side-by-side is a real loss, and each beat buys back what it was for with one row: filtering gains a row reading DCLM against Nemotron-CC, drawn two seconds before the line says both sides are on the screen, and environments gains a heading naming both routes at the top of the beat, eleven seconds before the line says both are on the screen.

**The cheap fixes covered three more beats, and each one gave a sentence something to land on.** mixing kept its stack and gained a fourth layer, because ten seconds of narration followed long context with no reveal under it. synthetic gained a row for the model-collapse answer, which is the last twelve seconds of the beat, and lost its heading: a focus beat spends 3.75 seconds lighting the parked map before it draws anything, so that heading was already three seconds behind the words it paraphrased while pushing every real row one place later. question is the case where neither lever exists. A stat has two reveals and the second is the note, so the note can only paraphrase the last thing said in the beat, and it was carrying the caveat, spoken twenty seconds earlier and therefore drawn 17.8 seconds after the words that name it. Re-pointing it is the only move a stat has. The caveat leaves the frame and stays in the narration, where it is said twice; the caption cannot take it without running to a second line, which broke inside a word the last time this panel was given one.

**The map is the one beat the visuals could not reach, and the only re-render this cut paid for.** A columns panel reveals once per column, the map is three columns by construction, and forty-two of the beat's 123 words came after column three was first named: nineteen seconds of tail against a parked ceiling of 8.4, so no reserve within reach could close a 14.3 second lead. Splitting a column would have put four headings on screen under a narrator saying column three, which is a worse defect than the one being fixed. So the words moved. B's line and A's answer went up ahead of column two, the Common Crawl summary came out of the closing turn and became the bridge into column three, and the newest thing here moved from the end of the column-three list to the clause that opens it. Twenty-two words now follow the first naming, which fits. Two words were dropped and none were added. Only the tail count matters, which is the part that is easy to get wrong: adding words in front of a column moves its naming and its drawing by the same amount and buys nothing at all. The take came back on the third seed at 131 words a minute and a character error of 0.003, and its transcript was read against the line: nothing invented, nothing dropped.

The worst still frame in the episode is now 5.88 seconds and the worst lead 2.2.
