# 2026-09-22: explainer video for llms

A single addition: the topic overview video for [Topic: llms](../topics/llms/summary.md), plus the one thing the page gained while it was being made.

**[new] A narrated explainer video is attached to **[Topic: llms](../topics/llms/summary.md)**.** Five minutes fifty-eight seconds, 1080p30, one narrator with a second voice asking the viewer's question twice. It is a topic overview, so it names the whole board before explaining any of it, and the board is the page's own taxonomy: what you are allowed to do with the weights. Closed frontier (OpenAI with GPT-6 Astra, Anthropic with Fable 5.1, Google with Gemini 3.8, SpaceXAI with Grok 4.6, Meta with Muse Spark 1.3), open weights (DeepSeek V4.1-Flash, Qwen 3.8 Max at 2.4T, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, MiniMax and Mistral, Tencent and StepFun), and fully open and small (OLMo 3.1, K2 Horizon, Gemma 4 and gpt-oss). The map is parked as the home frame and every later beat lights the part of it being discussed.

The organising question is the one the page's six-sentence state of play sets up and never asks: if they have stopped separating on architecture, what is left? The answer the episode gives is two things, and every lab on the page falls into one of them. First, where the thinking goes, which is the page's own framing of recurrent depth as a third way to spend a test-time budget, so OpenAI's hidden router, Anthropic's caller-set token budget, Gemini Deep Think's parallel branches and GPT-6 Astra's activations looping back through its own layers are four answers to one question rather than four labs taking turns, and the beat closes on the consequence rather than a fifth lab: no monitor can read the last of them, and OpenAI says that use is limited so far. Second, what a model costs to serve, which is where the open-weight labs compete hardest: DeepSeek changing the shape of the stack to an encoder-decoder and saying plainly that the reason is the key-value cache rather than quality, Zhipu averaging every four lookup vectors before it selects and landing at nine cents a task against about two dollars, Qwen making the selection hardware-friendly by choosing blocks rather than single tokens, and MiniMax going all in on linear attention and then partly back. Then the correction that matters most, drawn as a grid: there is no single strongest open-weight model any more, because Kimi K3 leads the aggregate indices, GLM-5.3 leads private enterprise code and cost per index point, and DeepSeek V4 Pro leads SWE-bench verified, so the reply to "which open model is strongest" is two words, on what. Then where the competition actually landed, late and with the numbers: not total parameter count but how many you switch on per token, with Step 5 Preview's 27B active out of 600B as the smallest absolute count in the frontier band, V4 Pro's 33x as the ratio that ranks differently and is not the cheaper one, and Bonsai 2's 1.76 bits a weight coming at the same cost from the other side, keeping 98% of the score. The two compose: fewer parameters running, and each one smaller. It closes on what the map is for, which is not picking a model, because any ranking on the page is stale before you finish reading it. It is for predicting what a lab does next, because the bets move far more slowly than the scores. What would redraw it is not a benchmark result: it is somebody following DeepSeek into the encoder-decoder, or the model ceasing to be the unit at all, which is what a learned orchestrator selling one endpoint over a pool of other models would do.

**The finding worth carrying forward is that a lowercase compound is as dangerous at the end of a segment as at the start, and the gates cannot see what it does there**. The map beat ended on "G P T O S S" and the take repeated that phrase three times, at a character error of 0.029 and a no-speech probability of 0.29. Both numbers pass. The existing rule says not to OPEN a sentence with a fragile name; this is the same rule at the other end, and the line now reads "G P T O S S. And Gemma four." Three more defects of the same family, all found by reading and none by a gate. "Whole board first" arrived as "Hole. Board first.", which is a different word, so the beat opens "Start with the whole board" instead. "StepFun took that furthest" arrived as "Step 5 took that furthest" immediately before a sentence about Step 5 Preview, collapsing the lab and the model into one name; the lab is not load-bearing there and is gone. And "Meta, with Muse Spark one point three" arrived as "Meta with NewSpark 1.3" on two consecutive seeds, with the correct name on screen underneath it, which is the worst shape there is. Rerolling did not fix it, exactly as the method says about a name that fails in a particular beat; respelled "Mews Spark", which is the same phonemes, it came back correct and the transcriber wrote it back as "Muse Spark".

**Pace, and the beat that passes the gate and still gallops**. Two beats came back at exactly 165 words a minute, which check_timing passes, since it fails only above that. A listener does not. Their commas became full stops, not one word was cut, and they read at 147 and 154. The cost was about eight seconds across the two, which this episode could afford. That is worth stating as a rule rather than an anecdote: 165 is where the check fails, not where the writing is right, and the gap between them is a beat nobody can follow.

**One real screen reference, and it is the ordering rule rather than the reserve**.
