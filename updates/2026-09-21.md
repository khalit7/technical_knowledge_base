### The five that matter

1. **Recursive self-improvement stopped being a debating position and became this week's evidence base, from three directions at once.** Anthropic's "When AI builds itself" (Sep 18) publishes the numbers: over 80% of merged production code authored by Claude, 8x more code shipped per engineer per quarter, and a task-horizon series running four minutes in March 2024 to twelve hours in March 2026. A companion measurement piece adds that Claude now leads **26% of Anthropic's own AI research work**, with **more than 30,000 internal agents active at any one time**. Z.ai (Sep 17) reports GLM-5.3 building the inference stack that now serves GLM-5.3-Flash on more than 100,000 Chinese accelerators, 3.22x throughput in 13 days, while stating explicitly that this is not recursive self-improvement because humans held the objectives. And three papers land the loop as a research programme: Agora, SoL-Pi and Dream-RSI. **[new]** on [Topic: agentic-harnesses](../topics/agentic-harnesses/summary.md) and [Topic: llm-training-and-post-training](../topics/llm-training-and-post-training/summary.md).
2. **The transferable lesson from the week's best engineering write-up is about feedback, not about self-improvement.** Z.ai's account names the bottleneck as the feedback environment rather than the model, and gives two bugs that only dense local feedback could have caught: a TF32 precision error visible only under specific parallelism strategies, and a 20% slowdown traced to the Python global interpreter lock blocking KV-transfer overlap. Neither would surface from an end-to-end metric. This is the most reusable finding of the window for anyone running agents against a codebase, and Nous Research's 1,393-agent refactor says the same thing from the other end: a passing test suite over a huge agent-authored change still let removed public APIs through. **[new]** on [Topic: agentic-harnesses](../topics/agentic-harnesses/summary.md).
3. **Roughly half an agent's token spend is harness inefficiency, and Emergence World shows what a long-running multi-agent system does under attack.** SoL-Pi cuts recorded token traffic 44.7 to 49.0% at task parity on EdgeBench by improving the harness rather than the model. Emergence World runs eight worlds of ten agents for 16 days across 850,000 LLM calls and finds that none is resilient, that detection does not imply containment (adversarial content still acted on up to 46 hours after recognition), and that model-level alignment is not compositional. Meta's Muse Spark 1.3, folded in from the harvest, is the shipped answer to exactly that finding: credentials live outside the runtime the agent occupies, and approvals arrive as OS dialogs rather than as text the model can be talked into. **[new]** on [Topic: agentic-harnesses](../topics/agentic-harnesses/summary.md) and [Topic: benchmarks](../topics/benchmarks/summary.md), with full Papers treatment for both papers.
4. **The most important evaluation result of the week is that six popular physics benchmarks were largely broken.** Expert re-grading found wrong answer keys, ambiguous questions and grader bugs behind most reported model failures; corrected, frontier models look near-saturated. That inverts the standard reading of a benchmark gap, because the defects concentrate in the hard tail everyone quotes. Came in through the newsletter harvest, not through this job's own sources. **[new]** on [Topic: benchmarks](../topics/benchmarks/summary.md), with full Papers treatment.
5. **Nvidia shipped native Rust GPU programming in two tracks, and both major open serving engines shipped after two quiet weeks.** cutile-rs, the tile track, is on crates.io and used by Hugging Face's Grout engine and mistral.rs; cuda-oxide, the SIMT track, is early alpha. Both make aliasing a compile error, by different mechanisms, and this is the first credible non-C++ path into kernel authoring since Triton. Separately, vLLM v0.29.0 makes Model Runner V2 the default for all models and deprecates V1 for removal in v0.32, with sequence parallelism and dual-batch overlap not yet supported on V2, and SGLang v0.5.18 cuts startup time 2.38x through overlapped checkpoint staging. The two prior issues recorded release feeds as checked and empty; that note now ends. **[new]** on [Topic: cuda-and-gpu-programming](../topics/cuda-and-gpu-programming/summary.md), **[update]** to [Topic: inference-and-serving](../topics/inference-and-serving/summary.md).

### Topic: llms

- **[new] Atria Dawn Preview** (Shanghai AI Laboratory, weights Sep 11 to 12, paper Sep 14). 744B agentic mixture of experts, MIT licensed, built as a post-training layer over Z.ai's GLM-5.2 base rather than trained from scratch. Filed with that provenance stated, because it is the interesting structural fact: a national lab shipping a frontier-scale model as a post-training layer on another lab's open base is a new shape in the open-weight landscape, and it is why the release could arrive with no announcement. 16 benchmarks claimed, highest reported score on five unnamed, 59.6% coding against Claude's 74.7% on SWE-bench Pro. All vendor-reported.

Sits with GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 on most metrics and behind GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 on hard coding, DeepSWE v1.1 67.7% against 74.0 to 74.1%. Open weights promised Oct 15, no licence named, no weights and no technical report yet. Filed with the promise flagged as unfulfilled so the next issue can check it.
- **[new] Bonsai 2 27B** (Prism ML, Sep 17), filed as the counter-direction to the parameter race. A ternary compression of Qwen3.8 27B at 1.76 effective bits per weight, 5.9GB, 98.2% of aggregate benchmark performance, 262K context, multimodal, Apache 2.0, 143 tokens per second on an RTX 5090. Directly relevant to the dual-5090 box.

Recorded with the 1,282-point Hacker News prior-art claim noted alongside, because the category novelty is contested.

### Topic: inference-and-serving

- **[update] vLLM v0.29.0**, replacing the "release feeds checked and empty" note. Model Runner V2 becomes the default for all models, V1 deprecated for removal in v0.32, with sequence parallelism and dual-batch overlap still unsupported on V2 and a two to three week gap expected. Kimi-K3 fused MXFP4 top-k finalisation for about 5% lower latency and a 6.6x to 7.6x Mamba metadata kernel speedup; DeepSeek V4 fused expert operations; speculative decoding gains per-request acceptance statistics in API responses. Breaking changes recorded for anyone upgrading: ten deprecated architectures removed, several models moved to the Transformers backend, PyAV dropped.
- **[update] SGLang v0.5.18**, 710 pull requests from 212 contributors, overlapped checkpoint staging cutting startup 2.38x, TP LMHead optimisation, FlashInfer improvements, PyTorch 2.13.0 and Triton 3.7.1.
- **[new] BITCOS, and the arithmetic that the 1.585-bit floor was never the real floor.** Across 29 ternary models, zeros reach 51.5% of weights, so a dense presence bitmap plus a compacted sign vector costs 2 minus z bits per weight, beating five-trit packing on 26 of 29 models and reaching 1.485 bits on the sparsest, for up to 1.28x faster matrix-vector kernels and 1.18x to 1.27x faster end-to-end inference. Filed next to the quantised-serving line because it is the cleanest recent example of the page's own thesis that decode speed is bytes touched per token divided by bandwidth. Linked rather than given Papers treatment.
- **[new] DeepSeek-V4.1 Flash at 890 bytes of KV cache per token**, from the harvest. Causal encoder-decoder, cross-layer sparse attention with index reuse, hierarchical retrieval, Engram memory and FP4 cache storage; 8B parameters active during prefill and 16B during decoding, optimised across HBM, SSD and compute together rather than for peak tokens per second. Filed as the model-architecture side of the same argument AgentX makes from the hardware side.
- **[new] Vera Rubin NVL72 on agentic traffic**, cross-filed from [Topic: hardware](../topics/hardware/summary.md) because the methodology belongs here: AgentX replays real agentic traffic with multi-turn structure, long contexts, high prefix reuse and sub-agent bursts, a different workload shape from the single-turn traffic accelerator comparisons normally use. The usable number is 1.4x to 3x throughput per total cost of ownership against GB300 at 60 to 100 tokens per second, and up to 7x token throughput per megawatt on pre-release software. The 67x headline is recorded as not quotable.
