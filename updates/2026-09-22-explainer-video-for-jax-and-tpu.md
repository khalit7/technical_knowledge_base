# 2026-09-22: explainer video for jax-and-tpu

A single addition: the topic overview video for [Topic: jax-and-tpu](../topics/jax-and-tpu/summary.md), plus the two glosses the page gained while it was being made.

**[new] A narrated explainer video is attached to **[Topic: jax-and-tpu](../topics/jax-and-tpu/summary.md)**.** Six minutes forty-one seconds, 1080p30, one narrator with a second voice interrupting twice. It is a topic overview, so it names the whole board before explaining any of it, but this page is neither of the two clean kinds of overview. It is not a comparison, because the things on it do not compete, they stack; and it is not purely a mental model, because the page is explicitly a translation track from somewhere the viewer already lives. So the organising question is the one this page's own framing puts in the room and no other topic in the knowledge base has hanging over it: PyTorch already trains models on thousands of GPUs, so what is the extra explicitness buying? The page answers that itself, in the word it uses for it. The price is that functions must be pure and state is threaded explicitly; what the price buys is a compiler that takes one single-device program plus layout annotations and shards it across a pod. The episode is one instalment plan on that trade rather than a tour, and everything after the third beat is introduced as an instalment on it.

The map is four columns, which is the page's own Map of the space minus the two bullets that will not fit at a legible pill width: JAX core (jit, grad, vmap, shard_map), sharding (Mesh, NamedSharding, GSPMD), the training stack (Flax NNX, Flax Linen, optax, orbax, grain) and the TPU (MXU, HBM and VMEM, the ICI torus, Pallas). It is parked as the home frame and every later beat lights the part of it being discussed. Then the trade, stated flat on a card. Then the price in detail, where the parameters, the optimiser state and the random number keys are threaded by hand and nothing hides inside an object, and the four transformations that purity makes composable. Then the payoff, which is the longest beat in the episode because it is the page's centre of gravity: you write a single-device program, declare a Mesh, attach a NamedSharding to each array, and GSPMD inserts the collectives wherever two of your annotations disagree, so data parallel, FSDP and tensor parallel are the same program with different partition specs and shard_map is the escape hatch. Then the hardware the annotations are actually talking to, chip first and then wiring: the MXU as a 128 by 128 systolic array, HBM staged through the VMEM scratchpad that software manages rather than a cache that guesses, ICI wiring each chip straight to its neighbours in a torus with no switches so that ring collectives are cheap, a pod as hundreds to thousands of chips one job treats as one machine, and Pallas lowering through Mosaic when the compiler is not enough. Then what you actually write the model in, where the useful column is status rather than the names. It closes on what the map is for: a translation track rather than a rival, more explicit because you thread the state, more compiler-driven because you annotate and the partitioner does the rest, the cheap way in on Colab and Kaggle and the TPU Research Cloud, and the page's own goal state, which is small and concrete.

#### Production notes

**The inventory axis came from the cut of this episode cleared when the method changed, which found it independently, and it is the page's own**. Five things from that cut are not kept. It ran eleven beats, about eight and a half minutes, above the rung where the encode ladder gives up 1080p. Not one of its beats carried a reserve, so every panel finished drawing at the very end of its budget. Its sharding beat, the payoff and the longest in the episode, was a compare, and a compare has exactly two reveals, so a fifty-second beat on it is a twenty-second motionless frame. It split the hardware across two long beats and left Pallas hanging off the end of one of them with no reveal to land on. And its close was a claim, two reveals over a forty-five-second take, which is the motionless closing card every early overview in this series shipped. One claim from that cut was dropped rather than reworded: "on a single accelerator it is all price and no payoff". The page does not say it and it is not obviously true, since jit, grad and vmap are worth having on one chip.

**The finding worth carrying forward is that splitting a beat's turns is a third pace lever, alongside punctuation and seed selection**. The hardware beat came back at 171, 173 and 169 words a minute across fifteen seeds and two rewrites. The punctuation lever was genuinely exhausted: the beat is twenty-four sentences averaging under eight words and there was not a comma left to turn into a full stop. What fixed it was redistributing the same words from seven turns into eleven, all still spoken by the narrator. render.py builds a beat as a conversation with one item per turn, so more turns means more prosodic boundaries, and the identical text came back at 159. It costs no words and no seconds of narration, which is what makes it better than the alternatives. Part of the 169 was measurement rather than delivery, too: that beat carries eighteen spelled acronym letters out of 185 tokens, and a letter takes well under the time an average word does, so counting each as a third of a word puts the same take at about 154. The pace gate divides raw tokens by duration, so an acronym-heavy beat reads as a gallop it is not.

**Four text-to-speech defects cleared the character gate and were caught by reading the transcripts as text**. Only one is a class the gates are built for. A single real word swapped for another, on the episode's hinge line: "everything else on this map follows from that trade" came back as "that train" at a character error of 0.012, and a clause after it took the sentence off the end of the beat. A trailing phrase eaten: "Pallas is the Triton analogue for TPUs and GPUs" arrived without the GPU half of a claim the page makes, and it is its own sentence now with "both" in front of the pair to signal that two things are coming. An invented word spliced in: "Continuum." appeared between two sentences in the close, at 0.028, in the gap after "And more compiler driven", a verbless fragment, which is the shape fills get poured into. And a fragile name opening a sentence: "Grad differentiates a pure function" came back as "Grrr. Differentiates a pure function". Two more shapes were changed pre-emptively and came back clean.

**The reserve arithmetic wants correcting by half a second**. Measured on the rendered frames of this episode, the still frame comes out at reserve plus 0.4 on an ordinary beat, not at the reserve, so 6.0 trips the six-second cap and 5.5 is the real ceiling; and on the parked map it is reserve minus 2.35, so 8.3 is the ceiling there. Every reserve here was then fitted by least squares from the rendered durations so that reveal k lands about two seconds after the segment naming it. The weakest lead left is the map, whose fourth column is drawn about six seconds after the line says "and on the right, the hardware": its reserve is already at the cap, so the only remaining fix is to move words out of the fourth segment and re-roll a beat that is otherwise clean. A four-column map names its last column in the last quarter of the beat, and four is one more column than this vocabulary is comfortable with.
