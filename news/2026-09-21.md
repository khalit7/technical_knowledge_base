## Top stories

**Anthropic published the recursive-self-improvement report the pacing debate was missing, and it is mostly numbers.** "When AI builds itself" (Sep 18) is the measured version of the argument Dario Amodei made rhetorically the week before. The figures: more than 80% of code merged into Anthropic's production codebase was authored by Claude as of May 2026; engineers ship roughly 8x more code per quarter than the 2021 to 2025 average; a March 2026 internal survey put the median employee's self-estimated output multiple at about 4x. The task-horizon series is the part that carries: Claude Opus 3 in March 2024 could complete roughly four-minute tasks, Sonnet 3.7 in March 2025 roughly 90-minute tasks, Opus 4.6 in March 2026 twelve-hour tasks, with tasks taking a person weeks projected for 2027. On research capability, code-optimisation speedups found by the model moved from about 3x in May 2025 to about 52x in April 2026, the open-ended problem success rate reached 76% in May 2026 after gaining 50 percentage points in six months, and model research judgement matched human suggestions 64% of the time in April 2026 against 51% in November 2025. The supporting operational detail, published separately, is that Anthropic's continuous-integration workload grew 25-fold in six months and its test suite grew 10-fold to keep up. A companion measurement piece adds the figure most worth quoting on its own: Claude now leads **26% of Anthropic's own AI research work**, with **more than 30,000 internal agents active at any one time**, and staff reportedly work with the model for roughly 90% of their work; it also sets out a framework for tracking agent activity and asking whether humans can still oversee it. Read all of this as the evidence base under last week's pacing proposal rather than as a separate story. The caveat to carry with every figure above: these are self-reported numbers, measured by Anthropic on Anthropic's own models and codebase, published by the company that benefits from the answer. That is also why they exist at all, since nobody outside a frontier lab can measure a frontier lab's internal work, and it is why the direction of the task-horizon series is worth more than any single value in it. [Anthropic, recursive self-improvement](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement) (20 min), [Anthropic, measuring the pace of AI development](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development) (15 min)

**Two agent systems produced formally verified mathematics in the same fortnight, at wildly different costs, and one of them started a credit fight.** OpenAI ran a swarm that grew to **10,000 concurrent agents** and produced a proof checked in Lean, a proof assistant in which mathematics is written so that a computer can verify it line by line, which is why a result like this has no partial credit and nothing to game. The theorem is that solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations can blow up under a smooth external force, published as a 166-page paper plus the agent-written Lean code. The scale numbers are the story for anyone building agent infrastructure: roughly 100 agents solved a related Euler question in about 50 hours, the Navier-Stokes group reached its conclusion 88 hours after launch on 5 September, formalisation in Lean took another 17 hours, and the run exchanged **4.9 million messages and about 300 billion output tokens** at an estimated **$2m to $22.5m**. Four days earlier Anthropic said a Claude research model produced the first complete Lean proof of Fermat's Last Theorem working alone for **11 days on about 6 billion output tokens**, roughly **2% of OpenAI's token spend**, by building on and crediting Kevin Buzzard's Imperial formalisation effort rather than inventing new mathematics. The Anthropic result drew no dispute. The OpenAI one did: NYU's Tristan Buckmaster accused OpenAI's Sébastien Bubeck over credit and asked whether the model had trained on his and Levent Alpöge's Codex sessions. OpenAI first said it could not determine whether their activity had improved its models, then on 10 September narrowed that to the two months before the announcement, leaving earlier prompts unaddressed. That last exchange is the part to carry into any vendor decision: it is a live argument about whether your provider trains on your work. Reported by The Batch, 18 September; no primary URL resolved for either result, so both are recorded from that coverage rather than linked.

**Z.ai**** says GLM-5.3 built the inference stack that now serves it, on more than 100,000 Chinese accelerators, and the transferable finding is about feedback rather than about self-improvement.** "Toward Recursive Self-Improvement: How GLM Built Its Own Inference Infrastructure" (Sep 17) reports a production serving system for GLM-5.3-Flash stood up in 13 days with a 3.22x end-to-end throughput gain from baseline to launch. Z.ai states explicitly that it has not reached recursive self-improvement and that humans retained control of objectives and boundaries, which is the right framing and the reason the post is worth reading rather than dismissing. The engineering claim is that the bottleneck was the feedback environment, not the model, and the fix was replacing sparse signals ("the test failed") with three kinds of local verifiable feedback: correctness, by comparing numerical results across execution paths, which surfaced a TF32 precision bug visible only under specific parallelism strategies; system behaviour, by timeline analysis, which is how a 20% slowdown was traced to the Python global interpreter lock blocking KV-transfer overlap; and performance, by layered testing that shows which constraint actually binds. Neither bug would ever have surfaced from an end-to-end metric. The practical advice generalises well beyond serving: audit what feedback your agent receives after a change, build cheap intermediate validation, define acceptance criteria numerically before starting, and let validated techniques accumulate in a reusable library. [Z.ai](https://z.ai/blog/glm-built-its-inference-infrastructure) (25 min)

**Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 posts up to 7x better token throughput per megawatt than GB300, on pre-release software, measured on replayed agentic traffic.** SemiAnalysis published the AgentX evaluation on Sep 15. The headline "67x better performance per dollar" is an extreme operating point and should not be quoted; the usable number is 1.4x to 3x better throughput per total cost of ownership against GB300 Blackwell at realistic interactivity of 60 to 100 tokens per second. The power figure is up to 7x better token throughput per megawatt, against the 3x Jensen Huang claimed at GTC 2026. Modelled economics at 75 tokens per second: $159.5B annual revenue and $149.9B modelled profit per all-in utility gigawatt for Vera Rubin against $114.9B and $105.3B for GB300, roughly 39 to 42% higher. The methodology is the reason to care: AgentX replays real agentic traffic across thousands of chips, so the workload has multi-turn structure, long contexts, high prefix reuse and sub-agent bursts, which is a materially different shape from the single-turn chatbot traffic most accelerator comparisons use. Caveat stated by the authors: early pre-release software, so the numbers may still move. [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/vera-rubin-nvl72-agentic-inference) (35 min)

**Nvidia shipped native GPU programming in Rust, in two tracks.** Announced in the window and the week's top Hacker News story at 968 points. **cuda-oxide** is the SIMT track: a custom rustc codegen backend that intercepts compilation, routes `#[kernel]` functions through Rust MIR and emits PTX, via the Pliron IR framework and LLVM. Safety comes from `DisjointSlice` types and launch contracts that make aliasing a compile error. **cutile-rs** is the tile track: you write tile-level computation, the compiler decides how tiles map onto each architecture, and the kernel is JIT-compiled through CUDA Tile IR on first use; exclusive access is guaranteed by tensor partitioning plus ordinary Rust ownership. Status differs sharply between them. cutile-rs is published on crates.io and already in production in Hugging Face's Grout inference engine and in mistral.rs; cuda-oxide is early alpha and needs a pinned nightly toolchain plus LLVM. No performance benchmarks are published, and the announcement does not claim any. The structural point is that the tile track's source does not encode architecture-specific choices, which is the same argument Triton makes against CUDA C++, now with Rust's memory model attached. [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/introducing-cuda-rust-two-tracks-for-writing-gpu-kernels/) (20 min)

**Three papers in one week make the auto-research loop the field's active research programme, and a fourth shows what happens when you attack one.** Agora (Sep 16) removes the planner from multi-agent research entirely and records everything as an append-only graph of Git commits: 13 workers, 12 days, 1,703 contributions, evaluator score 3.39 to 1.899 bits per byte, and 165 independent reproductions with zero failures. SoL-Pi (Sep 17, NVIDIA) runs the improvement loop at the harness layer instead of the model layer and finds roughly half an agent's token traffic is harness inefficiency: 44.7 to 49.0% fewer recorded tokens at task parity on EdgeBench. Dream-RSI (Sep 14) makes the loop cheap by treating the accumulated discovery history as a replay simulator, so exploration policy improves off-policy without paying for new evaluations. Emergence World (Sep 17, Emergence AI) is the adversarial counterweight: eight worlds of ten agents over 16 days, 850,000 LLM calls and 50 billion tokens, under prompt injection, misinformation and memory exposure. None was resilient, and the finding that matters is that detection did not ensure containment, with systems recognising adversarial content and still interacting with it up to 46 hours later. Detection is a signal rather than a control: nothing in these loops was wired to act on having recognised the attack, which is the thing to check in anything you are already running. All four have full summaries under Papers.

- **Breaking the 1.58-bit Barrier for Ternary LLMs** (Evangelos Georganas, Alexander Heinecke, Pradeep Dubey, Sep 14, 243 points on Hacker News). Ternary weights nominally need 1.585 bits, and five-trit packing rounds that to 1.625 by assuming the three symbols are equally likely. Across 29 ternary models, zeros account for up to 51.5% of weights, so they are not. BITCOS encodes a dense presence bitmap plus a compacted sign vector, costing 2 minus z bits per weight where z is zero density, beating five-trit packing on 26 of 29 models and reaching 1.485 bits on the sparsest. Matrix-vector kernels up to 1.28x faster, end-to-end inference 1.18x faster on CPUs and 1.27x on GPUs across five platforms, with optimised unpacking for AVX-512, AVX2 and Intel Xe2. Linked rather than given full Papers treatment; integrated into the serving topic. [arXiv 2609.16338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16338) (30 min)
- **Google Research's ToolGrad builds a verified API chain first and writes the user question afterwards**, hitting a 99.8% success rate generating tool-use training data across 16,000 real APIs. The data-efficiency claim is the striking part: a Gemma 3 12B trained on just 500 of those examples matched Gemini 2.5 Pro on a tool-use test with APIs it had never seen. Inverting generation, answer first and question second, is a transferable trick for building training and eval sets cheaply. [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/toolgrad-efficient-tool-use-dataset-generation-with-textual-gradients) (12 min)
- **Goodfire reports a clear internal activation signal that accompanies reward hacking**, and says it can be monitored at scale. Models knowing when they are reward hacking, and that being legible from activations, is a practical monitoring hook rather than a philosophical claim, and it sits alongside the week's other eval-integrity findings. [Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research/reward-hacking-activation-monitors) (15 min)
- **A long technical breakdown of DeepSeek-V4.1 Flash** shows how it compresses the KV cache to **890 bytes per token**, combining a causal encoder-decoder, cross-layer sparse attention with index reuse, hierarchical retrieval, Engram memory and FP4 storage. The 40-layer design activates 8B parameters during prefill and 16B during decoding, explicitly targeting HBM, SSD and compute cost for long-horizon agent workloads. [zartbot](https://zartbot.github.io/blog/model_arch/dsv41flash_arch/en.html) (146 min)
- **Paper2Agent reaches Nature, and converts 74 of 100 computational biology papers into working MCP servers with no manual intervention** (Stanford, Sep 16). [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11044-y), [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/16/stanford-researchers-release-paper2agent-turning-research-papers-into-ai-agents-that-reproduce-results-and-run-on-new-data/) (10 min)
- **A 4B model trained to write Postgres query plans beats the built-in planner by 81% on join-heavy queries** (Rohan Bansal, Sep 16, 695 points on Hacker News). Empero's Qwen 3.8 4B distillation, supervised fine-tuned on 420 trajectories from GPT-6 Astra, then agentic RL with a custom reward and an anchored GRPO variant, using LoRA adapters of roughly 21M parameters. Trained on about 13.6k queries from the Cardinality Estimation Benchmark, validated on the 113 Join Order Benchmark queries. Results: 1.81x geometric mean speedup on best-of-three rollouts, 44.7% latency reduction across join-heavy queries, zero regressions when taking the best of three, and valid plans for queries where the untrained model failed 72% of the time. Methodology is unusually careful, with four containerised Postgres instances and shared_buffers tuned to 2GB to control page-cache noise. The economics only work where the same analytic query runs repeatedly, so the optimisation amortises. [Write-up](https://rohanbansal.com/qorl) (30 min)

## Dev tooling & agents

- **Claude Code 2.1.271 to 2.1.278** (Sep 14 to Sep 19), and `AGENTS.md` support is the headline. 2.1.277 added `AGENTS.md` support in projects without a `CLAUDE.md`, and 2.1.278 added it as a general alternative to `CLAUDE.md`. That closes the agent-config standardisation argument the knowledge base has tracked since the 376-point feature request on Aug 19. Also material for anyone running agents unattended: 2.1.271 added per-command `allowed_domains` for Bash, PowerShell and Monitor in auto mode, `omitClaudeMd` in agent frontmatter, `--accept-command <sha256>` for plugin installs, and a 1x to 10x `multiplier` in model pricing; 2.1.273 added gateway hint headers behind `CLAUDE_CODE_GATEWAY_HINT_HEADERS=1`, MCP disconnection notifications and forking for Remote Control sessions; 2.1.274 added a critical-memory warning, `CLAUDE_CODE_MCP_STARTUP_WAIT_MS`, and OpenTelemetry `effort` and managed-settings events; 2.1.275 added Ctrl+Enter to interrupt and send queued messages, synced skills and plugins from claude.ai into terminal sessions, and `/plugin install <plugin> --marketplace <source>`; 2.1.276 was a critical fix for every request failing with `400 Input tag 'advisor_20260301'` behind an `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` proxy; 2.1.278 defaulted auto mode to a server-side classifier for API and Enterprise users with no classifier overhead charge, surfaced an `Auto mode server` row in `/status`, and added `CLAUDE_GATEWAY_PROXY_IS_EGRESS_BOUNDARY=1` plus optional per-upstream `headers:` maps. [Changelog](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/changelog) (15 min)
- **Meta shipped a personal agent whose security assumes the model will be fooled.** Muse Spark 1.3 moves enforcement and credentials outside the sandbox the agent runs in: the agent **never sees credentials**, a credential service outside the runtime cell holds them, and a separate agent, Sentinel, swaps in the real token as the request leaves the VM. Approvals arrive as OS-level system dialogs rather than as messages in the conversation, so injected text cannot manufacture consent, and the browser sub-agent reads the accessibility tree rather than page code and cannot run JavaScript. Meta offers up to **$300,000** for a valid report and **$130,000** for a successful prompt injection, and has published no classifier accuracy metrics, which is itself worth noting. This is the best-documented production answer yet to the "lethal trifecta". Reported by The Batch, 18 September; no primary URL resolved.
- **An evaluation of 21 model-harness pairs found that harness choice barely moves task success rate but significantly changes cost.** Seven models across three harnesses, and the headline conclusion is that a simple harness can be competitive. If it holds, it reframes agent-platform procurement as a cost question rather than a capability one, and it bears directly on how an evaluation framework should attribute results between model and scaffold. [Arena](https://arena.ai/blog/coding-agents-harness-tax) (12 min)
- **Nous Research coordinated 1,393 subagents for about 19 active hours to refactor a million-line Python repository**, cutting non-test source by 34.4% for roughly $19,300 in model costs. Worktrees and frozen baselines made parallel integration possible, but community review still caught removed public APIs and changed exception handling that the test suite passed. A rare write-up with real cost and mechanism rather than a demo, and a concrete quantified statement of where the verification gap sits in large agent runs. [Nous Research](https://nousresearch.com/refactoring-hermes-with-1393-agents) (20 min)
- **vLLM v0.29.0** shipped in the window: 594 commits from 277 contributors, 91 of them new. **Model Runner V2 is now the default for all models**, completing the rollout that began with pooling models, and V1 is deprecated with removal targeted for v0.32. Sequence parallelism and dual-batch overlap are not yet supported on V2, with the gap expected to close in two to three weeks. Performance work concentrates on Kimi-K3 (fused MXFP4 top-k finalisation for about 5% lower latency, 6.6x to 7.6x kernel speedup on Mamba metadata handling) and DeepSeek V4 (fused expert operations, adaptive selection). Five new model families including Hy4-preview at 770B with sparse attention and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next in several quantisation formats. Speculative decoding gains per-request acceptance statistics in OpenAI API responses and adaptive verification extended to logprobs. Breaking: ten deprecated architectures removed, several models moved to the Transformers backend, and the PyAV video decoder dropped for OpenCV or Torchcodec. [Release notes](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases/tag/v0.29.0) (20 min)
- **SGLang v0.5.18**: 710 pull requests from 212 contributors. New models include Muse Glimmer, Intern-S2-Mobius, SANA-Video, LingBot-Video-MoE, LTX-2.5 and Cosmos3.
