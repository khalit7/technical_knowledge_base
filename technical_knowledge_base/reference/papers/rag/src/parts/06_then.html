Then and now

RAG's pipeline has four parts: a query encoder, a document index, a way to combine the retrieved documents, and a generator. Every later system keeps the four; what changed is which parts are trained, and how the documents are combined. Step through eight systems from 2020 to 2024: a blue box is trained for the task, a grey dashed one is frozen or off the shelf. Each step's claims are quoted from its own paper (abstracts saved in src/inputs/later_extracts.txt).

"Trained" means trained or fine-tuned for the system's task; every generator here was pretrained first. In the deployed pattern the embedding model and the LLM are usually off the shelf; teams that fine-tune an embedding model or a reranker are doing a small part of what this paper did jointly.

What survived, and what did not

  • Survived, everywhere: a frozen, pre-built index of chunked text searched by inner product; retrieve-then-generate; updating knowledge by rebuilding the index (Atlas's abstract: "the document index ... can easily be updated").
  • Survived in research: training the retriever from the generator's signal (Atlas end to end; REPLUG with a frozen LM).
  • Did not survive in deployment: marginalising over documents. FiD showed that letting the decoder read many passages at once beats it on QA, and a long-context LLM reading every chunk in one prompt is the same move.
