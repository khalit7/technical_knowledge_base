Lines quoted from later work, for the page's "beyond the paper" claims (fetched 2026-10-02; PDFs read with pdftotext, not kept).

DeBERTa, arXiv 2006.03654 (abstract): "we scale up DeBERTa by training a larger version that consists of 48 Transform layers with 1.5 billion parameters. The significant performance boost makes the single DeBERTa model surpass the human performance on the SuperGLUE benchmark ... for the first time in terms of macro-average score (89.9 versus 89.8), and the ensemble DeBERTa model sits atop the SuperGLUE leaderboard as of January 6, 2021"
mT5, arXiv 2010.11934 (abstract): "a multilingual variant of T5 that was pre-trained on a new Common Crawl-based dataset covering 101 languages"; body: "called mC4. mC4 comprises natural text in 101 languages"
Flan-T5, arXiv 2210.11416 (abstract): "Flan-PaLM 540B instruction-finetuned on 1.8K tasks ... We also publicly release Flan-T5 checkpoints"
FLAN, arXiv 2109.01652 (abstract): "instruction tuning -- finetuning language models on a collection of tasks described via instructions"
Switch Transformer, arXiv 2101.03961 (abstract): "We design models based off T5-Base and T5-Large to obtain up to 7x increases in pre-training speed with the same computational resources ... pre-training up to trillion parameter models on the "Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus" and achieve a 4x speedup over the T5-XXL model."
Ettin, arXiv 2507.11412 (abstract): "paired encoder-only and decoder-only models ranging from 17 million parameters to 1 billion, trained on up to 2 trillion tokens ... encoder-only models excel at classification and retrieval tasks while decoders excel at generative tasks ... a 400M encoder outperforms a 1B decoder on MNLI, and vice versa for generative tasks"
Encoder-Decoder Gemma, arXiv 2504.06225 (abstract): "Under similar inference budget, encoder-decoder LLMs achieve comparable (often better) pretraining performance but substantially better finetuning performance than their decoder-only counterpart. For example, Gemma 2B-2B outperforms Gemma 2B by ~7% after instruction tuning."
UL2, arXiv 2205.05131 (body): "(regular denoising) that is a standard span corruption objective introduced in (Raffel et al., 2019)"; "Mixture-of-Denoisers (MoD)"
ByT5, arXiv 2105.13626 (body): "uses the "span corruption" pre-training objective" ... "our mean mask span length to 20 bytes"
LongT5, arXiv 2112.07916 (body): "we use PEGASUS Principle Sentences Generation instead of default Span Corruption used in T5 as our pre-training objective"
Imagen, arXiv 2205.11487 (body): "Imagen comprises a frozen T5-XXL [52] encoder to map input text into a sequence of embeddings"
Stable Diffusion 3, arXiv 2403.03206 (Figure 2 and body): text encoders "CLIP-G/14 CLIP-L/14 T5 XXL"; "We observe T5 to be important for ..."
FLUX.1 (diffusers pipeline_flux.py docstring, https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/main/src/diffusers/pipelines/flux/pipeline_flux.py): "text_encoder_2 ([`T5EncoderModel`]): T5, specifically the [google/t5-v1_1-xxl] variant."
FineWeb, arXiv 2406.17557 §3.5: "Despite being one of the first large scale LLM training datasets, C4 is still frequently part of the pretraining data mixture of recent models such as Llama 1 ... the Terminal punctuation filter, by itself, gives the biggest individual boost, but removes around 30% of all tokens ... We decided to apply all C4 filters mentioned above except the terminal punctuation filter"
Dolma, arXiv 2402.00159: "C4 (Raffel et al., 2020) (175B tokens) and Pile ... (387B tokens) are high-quality datasets with demonstrated use in training language models"; on LLaMA: "67% CommonCrawl, 15% C4, 4.5% GitHub, 4.5% Wikipedia, 4.5% Books, 2.5% arXiv, and 2.0% StackExchange"
