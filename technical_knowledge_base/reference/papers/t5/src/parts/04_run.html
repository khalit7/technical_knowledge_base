Corrupt a sentence: the objectives, run on your text

The paper's pretraining objectives are data transforms: each turns a piece of unlabelled text into an (input, target) pair for the encoder-decoder. This tab runs them, using the T5 code's own masking functions ported line by line from t5/data/preprocessors.py ({{code|https://github.com/google-research/text-to-text-transfer-transformer/blob/main/t5/data/preprocessors.py}}; the excerpts are in src/inputs/t5_code_extracts.py). The scores beside them are the paper's; what you can measure here is the other half of its argument, the cost.

What each objective costs, and what it scored

The paper's conclusion is that the denoising objectives perform alike, so the choice should be made on cost ({{§3.3.5|ax:S3.SS3.SSS5}}). The decoder's target length is that cost's clearest part (the decoder runs over the target, and its self-attention grows with the target's square). The chart puts every objective row of {{Tables 4 to 7|ax:S3.T4}} at its target length per 512 tokens of original text, against its measured score; the grey band is two baseline standard deviations below the best, the paper's own "no real difference" zone.

Target lengths are computed, not published: full reconstruction (BERT-style, MASS-style, deshuffling) is all 512; prefix LM about 256 (a uniform random split); i.i.d. noise at rate p is np corrupted tokens plus one sentinel per clean run, np + (1 − p) + (n − 1)p(1 − p) + 1 on average (dropping tokens: np + 1); random spans are round(np) + round(round(np) / span) + 1 exactly.
