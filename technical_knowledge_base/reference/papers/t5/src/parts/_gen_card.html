Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer (T5)

Colin Raffel, Noam Shazeer, Adam Roberts, Katherine Lee, Sharan Narang, Michael Matena, Yanqi Zhou, Wei Li, Peter J. Liu
Google · October 2019 (arXiv v1 23 October 2019; JMLR 2020; arXiv v4 September 2023)

In one line: Casts every NLP task as text in, text out so that objective, architecture, corpus, fine-tuning scheme and multi-task mixing become swappable ablations of one fixed pipeline: encoder-decoder plus span corruption wins, denoising beats language modelling while the denoising variants barely differ, unfiltered Common Crawl is worst everywhere despite being 8x larger, and multi-task pretraining then fine-tuning matches plain pretraining; scaled to 11B on 1T tokens of the new C4 corpus for 90.3 GLUE and 88.9 SuperGLUE against a human 89.8, though translation stayed out of reach on English-only pretraining.

How far to trust it: Believe the big gaps (denoising over language modelling, an encoder-decoder over a causal LM, cleaned over unfiltered web text, pretraining over multi-task training alone): they are 8 to 42 standard deviations of the paper’s own run-to-run noise. Read its many “no difference” findings as “none detectable with one run each at 220M”, not as proof of equality. The evidence, point by point.
