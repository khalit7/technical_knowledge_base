Terminal-Universe: Turning Agent Trajectories into Scalable Terminal Environments

Jie Wu, Zhenru Zhang (project leader), Beichen Zhang, Xuwu Wang, Yuhui Su, Mouxiang Chen, Peng Wang, Zhihai Wang, Que Shen, Hao Zhou, An Yang, Fei Huang, Yujiu Yang, Dayiheng Liu (corresponding authors Yujiu Yang and Dayiheng Liu)
Qwen Team, Alibaba Group, and Tsinghua University (Jie Wu worked on it as a Qwen intern) · 3 September 2026 (arXiv v1, the only version so far)

In one line: Agent trajectories already contain their own environments, because the file operations they recorded can be replayed: rebuild the workspace from the log, fill missing dependencies, then grow tasks by breadth (cross-workspace queries) and depth (single-turn extended into multi-round with feedback), yielding 37,300 runnable environments from public traces; fine-tuning Qwen3.5-27B gives +11.9 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and +13.8 on EvoCode-Bench v2 MT@4, and closes an agents-to-trajectories-to-environments-to-agents flywheel. The database Takeaway, verbatim. Two corrections from the paper: the 37,300 (37,273) environments are judged task-sufficient by an LLM, not shown runnable, and 1,464 of them are SWE repositories outside the main corpus; and the flywheel is the idea, not what the paper ran (its sources are mostly one model's rollouts on generated tasks).

How far to trust it: Believe the core move: re-solving rebuilt environments with a strong teacher clearly beats imitating the original traces (52.1 against 36.7, far outside the noise), and the headline gains over the base model are real. Treat the rest as provisional: most component ablations differ by 0.4 to 2.8 points, within about two standard errors of run-to-run noise; the gains are distillation from Qwen3.7-Max as much as a property of the environments; the comparison table mixes base models, scaffolds and reported numbers; and an "environment" here is a mostly model-written workspace that an LLM judged sufficient, not one checked to run. The evidence, judged.
