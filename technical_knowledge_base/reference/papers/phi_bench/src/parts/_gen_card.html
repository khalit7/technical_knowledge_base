Φ-Bench: Can Large Language Models Engineer the Infrastructure That Powers Them?

Leilei Ding, Shumin Wang, Yuting Huang, Fanqi Wan, Yinmin Zhang, Qi Han, Yiming Xu, Feiyuan Zhang, Xiaomeng Chu, Guoliang You, Wuyang Zhang, Daxin Jiang, Yanyong Zhang
University of Science and Technology of China, StepFun, Peking University, HKUST, Yale, University of Pennsylvania (correspondence: Daxin Jiang, StepFun; Yanyong Zhang, USTC) · 9 September 2026 (arXiv v1, the only version so far)

In one line: 85 open-ended infrastructure tasks in nine categories and three escalating formats (55 single-file kernel completions, 20 multi-file repository implementations, 10 end-to-end optimisations where the model must find the bottleneck itself), built from 2,260 papers and 1,852 engineering artifacts; the best model, Claude Opus 5, resolves 36.53%, Kimi K3 28.12% and Qwen3.8-Max 27.73%, with Hardware and Edge collapsing to 5.4%, repository-scale work far harder than kernels, longer reasoning budgets not reliably helping, and the behaviours that separate the top of the table being cheap pre-submission validation plus deliberate variable isolation.

How far to trust it: Believe the ranking at the top (Claude Opus 5 first, by about one standard error of an unpaired bound) and the task set, which is released and well engineered. Read 36.53% as a mean reward in which matching the expert reference earns zero, not as tasks solved; treat the category and effort findings as anecdotes (Hardware and Edge is 3 tasks, the effort figure is mislabelled), and note the leaderboard's own selection rules differ from the paper's.
