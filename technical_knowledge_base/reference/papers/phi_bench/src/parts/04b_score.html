Score a submission

A performance task's reward depends on three things: how much faster the submission really is, how high that task's frozen reference anchor sits, and how noisy the timing is. This tab runs the paper's scoring rule on simulated measurements: the AB-BA paired protocol (baseline and candidate timed alternately, the ratio taken within each pair, the median across pairs), then the reward curve ({{Performance Metric|ax:Sx3.SSx4.SSS0.Px1}}). Pick any of the 77 released performance tasks; its anchor is the real one from {{tasks_index.json|https://github.com/one2piece2hello/faibench_Frontier_InfraBench/blob/main/tasks_index.json}}. The noise model is illustrative: each timing is the true time times a random per-measurement error, times a drift factor for the machine's state at that moment.

Try: put the true speed-up just above the anchor (1.05× the anchor), then raise the drift. With the paired protocol the drift cancels inside each pair; switch to "unpaired" (all baseline timings first, the candidate later) and watch the rewards scatter and the share of zeros jump.

2,000 simulated scorings of the same submission, with a fixed random seed. Top: the measured speed-up (median of the per-pair ratios) on a log scale, with the anchor (reward starts) and the anchor squared (reward 1). Bottom: the rewards those measurements earn. The real per-task noise is not published; one real figure: the MoE training task's baseline varied by a coefficient of variation of 0.75% over five calibration runs (a quality metric, not a timing).

Where the 77 anchors sit

The anchor is the reference solution's speed-up over the starting code, measured once at authoring time and frozen. A task whose experts found a 1,000× improvement asks for more than 1,000× before it pays anything.

Each dot is one performance task on a log scale, by format; the selected task is ringed. Implementation tasks (8) have no anchor. Data: {{tasks_index.json|https://github.com/one2piece2hello/faibench_Frontier_InfraBench/blob/main/tasks_index.json}}.
