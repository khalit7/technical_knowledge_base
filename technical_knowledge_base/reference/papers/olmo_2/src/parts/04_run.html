Replay the stability fixes

Every stability claim in {{§3|ax:S3}} rests on a pair of training curves: the same run with OLMo-0424's setting and with OLMo 2's. The arXiv source ships those figures as vector PDFs, so the curves can be read back exactly (every drawn vertex, calibrated on the axes' own tick marks by extract_figs.py) and re-measured. Here the paper's spike score runs over them: a value is a spike when it sits at least k standard deviations from the mean of the 1,000 values before it, and the score is the percentage of values that are spikes ({{§3.2|ax:S3.SS2}}).

The detector sweeps both runs at once, to the same scale. Shaded: the range of the drawn curve in each of 240 step bins (so single-step spikes stay visible). Thin line: the rolling mean of the last 1,000 values; dashed: the mean plus k standard deviations, averaged per bin. Triangles above a panel: values counted as spikes at the chosen threshold. Counts are exact at any threshold: they come from every drawn vertex, not from the bins. The detector starts after the first 1,000 values, so the warmup steps are not scored. The animation stops when off screen and starts paused if your system asks for reduced motion.

How much the number depends on the definition

Spike score in percent, threshold 7. "Drawn" is every vertex of the figure's path with the 1,000-value window; "per step" interpolates the path back onto every logged step first (every step for Figures 3, 4 and 9, every 10 steps for Figures 7 and 10, the spacing of the drawn points), since matplotlib drops points that lie within about 1/9 of a pixel of a straight line when it writes a PDF. Vertices beyond the top of a figure's axis are drawn at the edge, so their true values, and some z-scores, are larger than counted here (Figure 4's old initialisation has 29 such vertices on the gradient norm, Figure 2's OLMo-0424 164). The paper does not say whether its window counts values or steps, whether the deviation is the window's standard deviation, or which part of each run it scored.

The z-loss fork ({{Figure 8|ax:S3.F8}})

Both lines are the z-loss term of the same 7B run, read from the figure. The main run used Flash Attention's fused cross-entropy, z-loss included; a fork from about step 476,850 used the team's own PyTorch z-loss, which gives the same forward value but a different backward pass. Within a few hundred steps the fork's z-loss is about 30 times higher and stays there. The paper abandoned the fork ("the fork with custom z-loss implementation") and kept training from before the divergence; the old Notion summary had this the other way round.

What weight decay does to the embeddings ({{Figure 10|ax:S3.F10}}, right)

L2 norm of the token embedding matrix over 280,000 steps, read from the figure (36 to 42 drawn points per run). With decay the norm sinks and stays near 200 to 450; without it, it climbs to about 1,860. The same runs' gradient norms are in the replay above ("Embedding decay").
