Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (Kaplan et al.)

Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish (equal contribution), Tom Henighan, Tom B. Brown, Benjamin Chess, Rewon Child, Scott Gray, Alec Radford, Jeffrey Wu, Dario Amodei
OpenAI, Johns Hopkins University · January 2020 (arXiv v1, 23 January 2020)

In one line: LM loss follows smooth power laws in N, D, and C over 6+ orders of magnitude; prescribed spending compute mostly on model size (N ~ C^0.73) with early stopping, an allocation Chinchilla later corrected to equal N/D scaling (~20 tokens/param).

How far to trust it: Believe the measurements: loss really is a smooth power law in parameters, data and compute, shape barely matters, and bigger models learn from fewer tokens; every later scaling study confirms this. Do not plan with the compute allocation (N growing as C^0.73): it came from one fixed-length training schedule with a long warmup, uncounted output-layer compute and untuned optimisers, and Chinchilla and Porian et al. replaced it with N and D growing equally.
