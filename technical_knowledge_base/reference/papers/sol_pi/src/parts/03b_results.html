EdgeBench: half the tokens, a third off the bill, a few points of score

[[S3.SS1|§3.1]]

The benchmark. {{EdgeBench|https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/EdgeBench}} (ByteDance Seed) has 134 day-scale tasks, of which 51 are public; agents submit repeatedly within a time budget, get feedback, and the best submission is the score; a stop hook prevents them from quitting early. SoL-Pi uses the 51 public tasks ({{§3.1|ax:S3.SS1}}). The paper says 11 of them were used for "one-way acceptance of frozen candidates" and the other 40 "reserved for final evaluation" ({{§2.5|ax:S2.SS5}}); the tables, however, report all 51 (see {{How much to believe|tab:t-read:s-trust}}). Costs use API prices as of 17 August 2026 ({{Table 1 note|ax:S3.T1}}). The first summary said September prices; the paper says August.

Two operating points. SoL-Pi [Efficiency] is the fixed four-mechanism stack. SoL-Pi [Performance] is the single mechanism with the highest average score in the add-one table, chosen separately per model: ObservationPack for GPT-5.6 Sol, Action Fusion for Opus 5 ({{§3.1|ax:S3.SS1}}, {{Table 2|ax:S3.T2}}).

With GPT-5.6 Sol, the full stack used 1.10 B tokens against Pi's 2.15 B (49.0% fewer), cost $894 against $1,339 (33.2% less) and scored 42.003 against 44.833, which the paper reports as "retaining 93.7% of Pi's average score" ({{§3.1|ax:S3.SS1}}). The harness was then run unchanged on Opus 5, a model the search never saw: 44.7% fewer tokens, 33.5% lower cost, 94.3% of Pi's score ({{Table 2|ax:S3.T2}}). The authors call this "preliminary evidence of transfer to an unseen LLM backend" ({{§3.1|ax:S3.SS1}}). The Performance points instead raise the score (47.2 and 50.5, against 44.8 for Pi) at smaller savings (6.1% and 11.3% fewer tokens).

The model's own native harnesses are much more expensive than Pi here: Codex used 3.05 B tokens and $1,787 for a score of 34.7 with GPT-5.6 Sol; Claude Code 2.00 B tokens and $2,535 for 43.7 with Opus 5. Against them the full stack costs 50.0% and 54.3% less ({{Figure 1(b)|ax:S0.F1}}), and with GPT-5.6 Sol it also scores higher than Codex. The paper's third-party harnesses (OpenSquilla, Oh-My-Pi, OpenCode, Oh-My-Opencode) all scored below Pi, between 24.5 and 38.5 ({{Table 1|ax:S3.T1}}).

Why half the tokens is a third of the dollars

[[S3.SS4.SSS0.Px1|§3.4]]
On Opus 5, SoL-Pi sends 44.7% fewer recorded tokens than Pi. By how much does the bill fall?

About a third (33.5%). "Recorded token traffic" adds up four kinds of token at very different prices. Almost all of it, 97.9% for Pi on Opus 5, is cache reads: the unchanged prefix of the conversation re-read on every request, billed at a tenth of the input price. The table's dollar figures fit Anthropic-style list prices exactly: $0.50 per million cache reads, $6.25 cache writes, $25 output, residuals under $3 on all seven Opus 5 rows ({{the check|tab:t-tables:t-checks}}). So the saving below is mostly cheap tokens:

This is also the right reading of the first summary's caveat that "recorded token traffic is not the same as billed tokens once cache behaviour is accounted for". The paper does account for it: its cost column prices each kind of token separately, and the 33% figure is the billed one. What the 44.7 to 49.0% headline counts is mostly re-read cache. The mechanisms trade some of it for extra cache writes (the full stack more than doubled GPT-5.6 Sol's cache-write traffic, 0.0141 B to 0.0316 B), and the paper argues for judging "the full task cost alongside task quality, rather than cache reuse alone" ({{§3.4|ax:S3.SS4.SSS0.Px1}}).

The GPT-5.6 Sol costs do not fit any fixed price per token type: the best least-squares fit over the 11 distinct rows misses by up to $225 and needs a negative input price. Whatever the GPT-5.6 Sol price sheet was (a long-context tier, for instance), the paper does not state it, so its GPT-5.6 Sol dollar figures cannot be recomputed from its token counts; the Opus 5 ones can.

The abstract says SoL-Pi saves $8.75 to $13.50 per hour against Codex and Claude Code. Where does "per hour" come from?

The paper never says, but one constant reproduces all four printed figures: the total cost difference divided by 102 hours, that is 51 tasks run for two hours each (EdgeBench's "@2h" budget, which is also the setting of the paper's GPT-5.5 reference row).

The Opus 5 figure against Pi, $5.71, comes out at $5.716; the printed costs are whole dollars, so the difference is only known to about $1 and $5.71 is within that. The blog adds that the range "reflects the model backend and is calculated using official API-equivalent pricing" and frames it as savings "for a professional researcher working on a single problem" ({{blog|https://nvlabs.github.io/SoL-Pi/}}). It is a rate under a fixed time budget, not a measured hourly bill: EdgeBench runs until the budget ends, so a cheaper harness spends less over the same two hours rather than finishing sooner.

Terminal-Bench 4, IMO 2026, and an agent swarm

[[S3.SS2|§3.2]]

Terminal-Bench 4 (63 CPU-only tasks; GPU tasks excluded for infrastructure reasons): Codex and Pi each solved 18, SoL-Pi solved 15. SoL-Pi's total model cost was 26.3% lower than Pi's ($211.12 against $286.45) and its cost per solved task 11.6% lower ($14.07 against $15.91) ({{Table 3|ax:S3.T3}}). The paper reads this as efficiency gains that "generalize beyond EdgeBench" ({{§3.2|ax:S3.SS2}}); it is also three fewer tasks solved, and Codex solved as many as Pi for less.

IMO 2026 with GPT-5.6 Sol at xhigh, each proof formalised and checked in Lean 4, 150 minutes per problem: Codex passed 5 of 6 ($114.47), Pi 3 ($75.95), SoL-Pi 3 ($62.69). SoL-Pi has the lowest cost per passed problem, $20.90 against $22.89 for Codex and $25.32 for Pi ({{Table 3|ax:S3.T3}}); Codex passed two more problems.

Swarm. [[S3.SS3|§3.3]] On {{Anthropic's original performance take-home|https://github.com/anthropics/original_performance_takehome}} (a kernel to optimise, scored in simulated machine cycles; the frozen starter takes 147,734), three configurations ran one two-hour trial each: a single Codex agent; a Codex coordinator with 20 Pi workers; a Codex coordinator with 20 SoL-Pi workers. Coordinators and the single agent used GPT-5.6 Sol at xhigh, workers GPT-5.6 Luna at xhigh, in five groups of four with shared group boards; the shared best advanced only when an independent check confirmed a strict improvement ({{§3.3|ax:S3.SS3}}, {{Figure 5|ax:S3.F5}}). Results: the SoL-Pi swarm reached 1,127 cycles for $60.11, the single agent 1,333 for $39.20, the Pi swarm 1,366 for $82.12. All candidates passed the correctness check; the SoL-Pi swarm and the single agent passed all eight speed thresholds, the Pi swarm seven (it missed "fewer than 1,363 cycles"). The SoL-Pi swarm cost 26.8% less than the Pi swarm ({{§3.3|ax:S3.SS3}}). One run per arm; the blog adds that the two control trials ran one after the other, single agent first ({{blog|https://nvlabs.github.io/SoL-Pi/}}).

Each mechanism alone, and how they behave together

[[S3.SS4|§3.4]]

An add-one evaluation adds each mechanism to Pi on its own ({{Table 4|ax:S3.T4}}). Every one cut total tokens under both models, and every one lowered cost per score point; the full stack had the lowest tokens and cost in both blocks ({{§3.4|ax:S3.SS4}}). Online Context Compact did the most alone on GPT-5.6 Sol (40.2% fewer tokens), ObservationPack on Opus 5 (39.2%). The single-mechanism scores spread widely, from 41.99 to 47.21 with GPT-5.6 Sol and from 43.41 to 50.48 with Opus 5, around Pi's 44.8; the paper reports no repeat runs, so how much of that spread is noise is unknown. All rows are in the {{tables tab|tab:t-tables:t-abl}}.

Activation differs by model. Figure 6 measures how often each mechanism fired. Trigger rate is the share of tasks where it fired at least once, intensity the mean number of firings on those tasks ({{§3.4|ax:S3.SS4}}). Both were lower on Opus 5 for every mechanism, most strongly for Online Context Compact (92.2% of tasks with GPT-5.6 Sol, 33.3% with Opus 5) and Action Fusion's intensity (70.58 firings per triggered task against 13.54). The authors suggest this "may reflect the harness being optimized exclusively on GPT-5.6 Sol trajectories"; each mechanism still improved token efficiency on the tasks where it fired ({{Figure 6|ax:S3.F6}}). Combined, ObservationPack fired on fewer tasks (80.4% alone, 56.9% in the stack), plausibly because the reducer handles the same large outputs first, and every mechanism showed a larger efficiency gain on its triggered tasks in the stack than alone (for example ObservationPack 10.2% alone, 28.7% in the stack); the authors note that comparing each configuration on its own triggered subset "does not isolate interaction effects" ({{Figure 7|ax:S3.F7}}). Both figures are rebuilt in the {{tables tab|tab:t-tables:t-figs}}.

One lineage: how Action Fusion was found and kept

[[S3.SS5|§3.5]]

Action Fusion's lineage took 27 recorded iterations in four stages ({{Figure 8|ax:S3.F8}}, {{§3.5|ax:S3.SS5}}):

  1. Oracle analysis (1 iteration). Adjacent edit-or-write then command pairs made up 12.3% of cross-turn transitions, and bash was 85.1% of the next actions. If all 149 observed candidates had fused, model turns would fall from 1,386 to 1,237 (10.8%) and tokens from 32.38 M to 28.64 M (11.5%): a projection from recorded trajectories, "not a measured rerun" ({{blog|https://nvlabs.github.io/SoL-Pi/}}).
  2. Baseline build (6). Prompt-only triggering was unreliable, so the agent extended the tool schema to expose the fused action directly: 87.7% uptake on eligible calls and no invalid calls.
  3. Prompt and schema optimisation (18 explorations, 10 retained batches). Trigger rate moved non-monotonically between 28.3% and 100%; the agent introduced trigger rate as an intermediate acceptance metric alongside task score. Batch 10 was selected (100% trigger rate, task score 87.0); batch 9 had the higher score (88.9) at a 66.7% trigger rate.
  4. Final validation (2). Independent checks, then held-out validation, then the mechanism joined the stack.

The paper's point is that a lineage-local loop can stabilise a mechanism's interface and invent its own intermediate metric ({{§3.5|ax:S3.SS5}}). ObservationPack's lineage, in the blog, went through a clean-room rebuild, a freeze to 315 lines and two hooks, an eight-configuration sweep where only "V2" stayed inside the quality gate, and an 11-task paired run on EdgeBench (provider bill 23.58% lower, normalised score 22.92% higher). The blog describes V2 as a 2,048-byte head and 1,536-byte tail; the paper's Figure 4 and the released code use a 1 KB excerpt (512 bytes each), so the released mechanism may not be the swept V2 ({{blog, ObservationPack|https://nvlabs.github.io/SoL-Pi/}}).
