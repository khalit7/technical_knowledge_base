What comes from the paper and the released code: the rules and constants of the four mechanisms (10 KiB threshold and two full sends for ObservationPack, its 1 KB stub, 4 KiB logs for the reducer with verified receipts and fallback, Action Fusion's merged edit and run, Online Context Compact's gate ported line for line and checked against the original), Pi's 20,000-token kept tail and window reserve, and the price list (Opus 5 list prices, which reproduce the paper's Opus 5 dollar column exactly). What is illustrative: the session, the receipt size (450 tokens), the share of receipts that verify (90%), the reducer's price ($1 and $5 per million), how often a stubbed result is recalled (12%), and the context window. Each request re-sends the whole conversation; the part identical to the previous request is a cache read, the rest is a cache write.

Try: play Pi, then the full stack, on the same session; step through plan steps and watch the bars (each bar is one request's prompt, to scale). Then switch the window to 200K and look at ObservationPack and the reducer alone.

Which mechanism should save the most here, on its own?

In this session, Online Context Compact, because the cost of a request is dominated by re-reading the conversation, and compaction is the only mechanism that shrinks all of it. The paper agrees for GPT-5.6 Sol (40.2% fewer tokens alone, {{Table 4|ax:S3.T4}}) but not for Opus 5, where it fired on only a third of tasks and ObservationPack saved more (39.2%). Inside Online Context Compact's gate

At each completed plan step the gate asks one question: will the input saved by compacting now repay rewriting the cache? Compacting turns a long cached prefix (cheap to re-read) into a short one that must be written again (12.5 times the read price by default). The function below is the released decideCompaction (economics.ts), ported; set its inputs and read its decision. Defaults: a 120,000-token context of which 95% of the part older than the kept 20,000-token tail (95,000 tokens) can be summarised into a 1,000-token memo, 14 requests per completed step, 6 steps left.
