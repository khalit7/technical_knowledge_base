In one line (the database's Takeaway): Regularising recursive harness evolution with an annealed edit budget, a critic and a pruner buys +14.1 points in distribution, +4.7 out of distribution and 30% fewer policy tokens, which is the first evidence the loop's gains survive leaving the benchmark it was scored on.

Read with care: the +14.1 is the gain on the split the search was scored on, with a secondary policy (Gemini 3.5 Flash; with the main policy the evolve gains are 6.0, 1.1 and 4.9); +4.7 is the best of five out-of-distribution gains, not their sum or mean (the mean is 3.6); and the paper's own Table 2 gives 36% fewer tokens, not 30%. Concurrent work also measures generalisation, so "first" is too strong. {{Details|tab:t-read:s-trust}}.

How far to trust it: Believe the diagnosis, and that RRSI's rules run as described: the authors' released run records replay to the same decisions. Treat the transfer gains as plausible but unproven: every arm is a single run, each out-of-distribution gain is within about one standard error, most edits the runs kept were inside their own noise band, and the released held-out record shows +0.9 points where the paper prints +2.3.
