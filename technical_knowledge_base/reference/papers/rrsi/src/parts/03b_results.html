Results: every held-out split improves

[[S4.SS2|§4.2, Figures 1 and 3, Table 1]]

Across the three domains the evolved harness gains on its evolve suite and, more to the point, on every split it never saw. The evolve-set gains are 6.0 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 4.9 on EngDesign and 1.1 on Harvey LAB; SWE-bench Verified gains 1.8 although repository-level bug fixing was never scored; Harvey LAB's held-out 40 tasks gain 2.3; the three out-of-distribution agentic benchmarks gain 3.5 to 4.7 points (7.2% to 13.1% relative); Frontier-Eng gains 4.3 Medal points (24.3% relative). "No held-out split regresses anywhere, which is the failure a memorizing harness produces" ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}).

{{Figure 3|ax:S4.F3}} rebuilt from the values printed in the figure's vector PDF (the arXiv source). Evolve splits are what the search was scored on; the rest never entered selection. Claude Opus 4.8 is the policy throughout.

Against prior evolution methods ({{Table 1|ax:S4.T1}}), all run from the same H0 on the same evolve split and candidate budget, the ranking inverts once the harness leaves the evolve split. Meta-Harness has the best evolve score (93.0) and adds 0.9 points to the out-of-distribution average; HarnessX lands on the base; AHE and TTHE finish below the harness they started from, TTHE by 1.7. RRSI has the smallest evolve-set gain of any evolved harness and the only out-of-distribution average that clears H0 by more than a point, 43.6 against 39.7, "which is the trade the regularizers are designed to make" ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}). On the in-distribution held-out split the methods are close: RRSI's 89.2 ties Meta-Harness.

Judge-free grading. Harvey LAB, JobBench and GDPval are judged by language models, so a harness could in principle learn to write the way a judge rewards. The engineering domain closes that route: EngDesign and Frontier-Eng tasks are graded by their own simulators or testbenches, deterministically, and the gains survive there ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}). That argument is sound; the engineering out-of-distribution gain rests on 38 scorable tasks, though, and amounts to 2.0 medal credits in all (a Medal Score of 17.7% is 25 thirds of a credit over 47 tasks, 22.0% is 31 thirds).

Ablations, policies and cost

[[S4.SS3|§4.3, Tables 2 to 4, Figure 4, Table 6]]

Every run below uses the agentic workspace instance with the main experiment's base harness, policy, evolve split, round count and candidate budget, so arms differ only in the factor under study ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}).

Switch off all of RRSI's regularizers and the same loop reaches 92.8 on the Harvey LAB evolve split, against RRSI's 90.5. Out of distribution, unregularised evolution scores:

{{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}; tokens in millions of policy tokens per trial on the evolve split.

Removing either group of regularizers raises the evolve-set score and lowers transfer ({{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}). Without the acceptance constraints the evolve score rises from 90.5 to 91.5, the out-of-distribution average falls from 43.6 to 41.0 and tokens rise by half (3.59M against 2.42M), which the authors read as an unconstrained selector spending its accepted edits "on noise and on context rather than on mechanism". Without the proposal constraints the evolve score is 0.2 higher (the paper says "costs only 0.2 points", but the variant scores more, not less) and the out-of-distribution average 1.7 lower, so steering where the search looks matters even when nothing is rejected. Removing both gives the highest evolve score of any arm, 92.8, and an out-of-distribution average of 40.3, within a point of the unevolved harness, at 3.80M tokens per trial.

Other policies. Run independently with Gemini 3.5 Flash as the frozen policy, the coding search lifts Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 64.6 to 78.7 (+14.1, the abstract's "up to 14.1") and transfers +2.2 to SWE-bench Verified; with Claude Opus 4.8 the numbers are 74.2 to 80.2 and 82.0 to 83.8, with less room near the ceiling ({{Table 3|ax:S4.T3}}). The harness evolved with Gemini 3.5 Flash, run unchanged with the smaller Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite that never took part in the search, lifts Terminal-Bench from 11.2 to 14.6, a 30.4% relative gain on a base less than a fifth of the search policy's ({{Table 4|ax:S4.T4}}). "A harness is a program, not a set of weights", so a mechanism that helps only the policy it was searched against would be an artifact of that policy.

Cost. No prior method carries a cost constraint, and all four sit in the region RRSI dominates: more policy tokens per trial for a lower out-of-distribution average ({{Figure 4(a)|ax:S4.F4}}). AHE is the extreme, 3.82M tokens per trial (58% more than RRSI) for 4.4 points less out of distribution. Trajectories follow the same order: 26.3 steps per trial for RRSI against 27.3 to 34.6 for the prior methods. The paper is candid that no evolved harness is as cheap as H0, at 1.56M tokens and 21.2 steps, "so evolution does buy part of its gain with test-time compute; the budget decides how much" ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}).

{{Figure 4(a)|ax:S4.F4}} rebuilt from the vector PDF (baseline token costs appear only in the figure), with Table 2's ablation arms added as open circles. Shaded: everything RRSI dominates, more tokens for a lower out-of-distribution average.

The three numbers, read together. The old summary read the out-of-distribution gain as "about a third of the in-distribution gain, the honest shape of a generalisation claim", and the token reduction as showing that "the regularisation is not bought with compute". Both need correcting. The 14.1 and the 4.7 come from different policies and domains (Gemini on Terminal-Bench, Opus on JobBench), so their ratio means nothing; within one setting the ratio is above one (Harvey LAB evolve +1.1, out-of-distribution mean +3.9), because RRSI deliberately gives up evolve score. And RRSI's harness still uses 55% more tokens than H0 (2.42M against 1.56M): the regularisation holds compute growth down, it does not remove it, as the paper itself says. The old summary's point stands that a pruner which only removed cheap edits would raise the score by spending more; the cost rule exists so that cannot happen.

What the decisions look like. {{Table 6|ax:A5.T6}} picks four decisions from the released runs. In the first coding round two similar candidates (a bounded pre-completion verification audit plus polling guidance for long jobs) both scored higher: the one with +3.93 points was kept, the one with +1.69 points and +26.1% tokens rejected. In round 8 a candidate that pinned the original task instruction into the completion gate cut tokens by 13.6% but lost 2.81 points and fell below the floor. In engineering round 2 a bounded recovery hint for a recurring "workdir must be an existing directory" error went from 122 to 128 passes of 244 at +1.6% tokens and was kept.
