Results

[[S5|§5, Appendix F]]

All zero-shot comparisons run every method on the same base model and budget, without training, on a shared Mini-SWE-Agent backbone; baselines use their released code where it exists (MEM1 is re-implemented) ({{§5.1.1|ax:S5.SS1.SSS1}}, {{Appendix E|ax:A5}}).

Deep research and terminal coding (Qwen3.6-27B, 32K)

On BrowseComp-Plus (830 questions over a fixed corpus), CLM scores 59.4% against 53.3% for Codex-style summarisation, the strongest baseline: 11.4% higher in relative terms (6.1 points), with 21.5% fewer prefix-reuse FLOPs than summarisation and 28.9% fewer than MEM1. On TerminalBench 2.1 (89 tasks) it ties summarisation at 53.4% with 70% of its FLOPs (29.5% fewer); on TBLite it scores 73.7% against 67.0% with 91% of the FLOPs ({{§5.1.1|ax:S5.SS1.SSS1}}). The chart is {{Figure 5|ax:S5.F5}} rebuilt from its vector paths; the dashed line is the Pareto frontier (no other method is both cheaper and more accurate).

The smaller model is a different story ({{Figure 19|ax:A6.F19}}). The appendix reports that Qwen3.5-9B's CLM reaches 39.9% on BrowseComp-Plus, above summarisation's 37.7%, and that it edits less (1.4 edits per TerminalBench task against 2.6 for the 27B model, none in half the tasks), with a median peak context of 30.2K of 32K. What the text does not say, and the figure shows: on BrowseComp-Plus, Self-Compact reaches 42.3% at about 0.67 PFLOPs, more accurate than CLM and far cheaper (CLM sits past the axis break, beyond 3 PFLOPs); on TerminalBench 2.1, CLM is the cheapest point but the least accurate method except MEM1 (20.7% against 27.9% for summarisation). Switch the chart to Qwen3.5-9B to see it. The authors' own reading: CLM's gains "grow with the model's ability" to decide when and how to edit.

Open-ended optimisation (Claude 4.6 Sonnet)

On four AlphaEvolve problems, CLM with an in-context description of the evolutionary algorithm beats the specialised OpenEvolve workflow on all four best-of-run scores: circle packing 2.618 against 2.541 (3.0%), Heilbronn triangles 0.03653 against 0.03127 (16.8%), the min-max distance ratio 0.07758 against 0.07690, Erdős minimum overlap 0.38094 against 0.38123 (lower is better). With up to five subagents, circle packing reaches 2.636 ({{Table 1|ax:S5.T1}}). Each method ran once, for at most 100 scored attempts or five hours ({{Appendix E|ax:A5}}).

On EdgeBench-10 (ten repository-optimisation tasks, 12 hours each, best of three seeds per task, 32K budget), Qwen3.6-27B's CLM scores 44.6 using 179 PFLOPs per trial against 42.3 and 437 PFLOPs for summarisation: the abstract's "5% higher with 59% fewer FLOPs". Subagents add nothing (44.2 at 181). With Claude 4.6 Sonnet, CLM reaches 51.0 against 42.3 ({{Figure 8|ax:S5.F8}}). At a 128K budget the order changes: CLM 47.3, summarisation 47.8, CLM with subagents 50.2 ({{Figure 27|ax:A6.F27}}).

On Software World, six GPT-5.6-Sol agents (272K context) jointly speed up requests, urllib3 and four downstream packages for over 24 hours and are scored on 17 held-out benchmarks from four packages they never see. At the same API spend the CLM swarm reaches a geometric-mean speedup of 1.044 against 1.026 for a summarising swarm: 4.4% against 2.6%, the "65% greater speedup" of the abstract ({{Figure 8|ax:S5.F8}}). One run per swarm.

Steering, evolving and training it

One sentence steers it ({{Figure 9|ax:S5.F9}}). Told to compact at Y = 16K, 24K or 32K tokens, Claude 4.6 Sonnet's median context at its first compaction is 16.0K, 23.6K and 30.9K. Told to compact at sub-question boundaries, the share of compactions within two turns of a boundary rises from 0.40 to 0.88. Told to back up the context before every edit, 0.68 of edits are preceded by a full backup (0.09 more by a partial one), against 0.00 without the instruction.

Evolved skills work ({{Figure 10|ax:S5.F10}}, {{Figure 28|ax:A6.F28}}). Starting from no context-management instruction, assisted evolution raises Qwen3.6-27B's development accuracy on KV Store from 22.3% to 83.8% (Log Triage 0.0% to 100.0%), and the selected KV Store skill lifts the held-out test score from 38.3% to 74.2%: the abstract's "up to 35.9 points", measured on one of the four tasks.

RL trains it ({{Table 2|ax:S5.T2}}). Before training, Qwen3.5-9B's CLM scores 28.8% on BrowseComp-Plus, 5.9 points below the summary harness (34.7%). After 70 steps it scores 42.5% at 1.34 PFLOPs per question; the summary harness, trained the same way, scores 42.1% at 2.19. The abstract's "47.6% while using 12% fewer FLOPs" compares CLM with its own untrained start (42.5 against 28.8; 1.34 against 1.52 PFLOPs). Adding the FLOPs reward cut cost "without a clear loss in accuracy" for both ({{Figure 29|ax:A6.F29}}).

The abstract says RL improves Qwen3.5-9B's CLM by 47.6% on BrowseComp-Plus. After training, how much more accurate is it than the summary harness trained with the same recipe?

0.4 points, 42.5% against 42.1%. The 47.6% is CLM against its own untrained start (42.5 / 28.8 = 1.476); the summary harness gained 7.4 points from training too. With 830 questions, one run each, the standard error of that 0.4-point gap is about points. The real win is cost: 38.8% fewer FLOPs per question.

The settings also differ: CLM had a 28K budget, 80 turns not counting editing turns, the efficiency advantage and tool-call penalties; the summary harness compacted at 28,672 tokens with 100 turns and the task reward alone ({{Appendix E|ax:A5}}).

Suffix Cache Reuse ({{Figure 11|ax:S5.F11}}): on all 830 BrowseComp-Plus questions with Qwen3.6-27B, CLM served with SCR keeps 60.2% accuracy, the same as standard SGLang, at 7.14 against 10.98 measured PFLOPs per question: 65.0% of the compute, 35% less.
