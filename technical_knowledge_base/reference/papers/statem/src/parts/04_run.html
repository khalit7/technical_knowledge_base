Replay a real StateM run

These are real runs: the authors' {{released DeepSeek-V4-Flash trials|https://github.com/henryqin1997/statem/releases/tag/deepseek-policy9-tb21-artifacts-20260818}} on Terminal-Bench 2.1, 88 tasks × 5 trials, each with its full StateM history (every requested transition, every check the host ran, its output, and the time). The runbook is the one the trials used, terminal-bench-statem-codex-thin-review: solve directly, check the visible task contract, one bounded self-review, then either a light soft guard or a focused guard with a task-family gate, then handoff. Pick a run and step through it; a red step is a goto the runtime refused, with the check that refused it.

    Check outputs are trimmed to about 260 characters; checklist results the runbook marks as needing no confirmation are hidden. The list scrolls with the replay. Times are seconds since the run started. Data: src/audit_artifact.py on the artifact (job ); runs are picked by a stated rule, not by eye.

    What the released trials confirm, and what they add

    This page's audit of the {{artifact|https://github.com/henryqin1997/statem/releases/tag/deepseek-policy9-tb21-artifacts-20260818}} (checksums verified) against {{§4.4|ax:p13}}: of 440 trials pass (the paper's 392/440); of 88 tasks are solved at least once (only never are); the recorded API cost of the 440 trials sums to , leaving of the paper's $15.20 for the unreleased gpt2-codegolf runs; of the 2.13 billion input tokens were cache hits, which is why the run is cheap. Not in the paper: the job allowed up to two automatic retries of any trial whose exception was not one of four verifier errors (agent timeouts are not among the four) and recorded retries; every trial ran with reasoning effort "max" and a 750-second StateM deadline; and none of the 89 task names appears anywhere in the 54-file harness source.
