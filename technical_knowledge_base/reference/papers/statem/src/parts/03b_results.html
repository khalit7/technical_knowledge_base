The evaluation: four transfer tests

[[p10|§4, §4.1]]

Four regimes ask "what transfers as model and task distance increase?": fixed-model lift, frozen transfer within a family, adapted transfer across providers, held-out task generalisation ({{§4|ax:p10}}). Terminal-Bench 2.1 has 89 tasks × 5 trials = 445; the metric is trial success, plus five-trial coverage (tasks solved at least once), which "measures empirical coverage over five observed trials rather than single-run reliability". Agents are Codex-based with GPT-5.5 xhigh, GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh, GPT-5.6 Luna and DeepSeek-V4-Flash; reference rows reproduce a published result, our run rows are the authors' ({{§4.1|ax:p10}}). The profile was developed with GPT-5.5 and frozen for GPT-5.6: no prompt, state, routing, check, default or repair policy revised from target-model outcomes. For DeepSeek, final-run and adaptation API charges are reported separately; the $1,062.95 on the GPT-5.6 submission is the pipeline's API-equivalent estimate, not what the authors paid on a Codex Pro plan ({{§4.1|ax:p11}}).

Result 1: same model, better harness, a generation-sized gain

[[p11|§4.2, Figure 3]]

With GPT-5.5 xhigh fixed, StateM scores 92.1% against the 83.1% Codex reference (+9.0) and solves 88 of 89 tasks at least once (98.9%), numerically above the 91.9% reported for GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra ({{§4.2|ax:p11}}). The reference is the leaderboard entry (83.15%, Codex 0.125.0, $2,059.19), "a model-matched public reference rather than a newly rerun, agent-version-matched A/B control" ({{§4.2 footnote|ax:p11}}). The frozen runbook on GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh scores 424/445 = 95.28% raw against the 84.9% reference (+10.38), with every task solved at least once. Under the reference harness GPT-5.5 to GPT-5.6 Sol moves only 1.8 points, so the harness gain is five times the generation gain ({{§4.2|ax:p12}}).

Status of the 95.28% ({{footnote 1|ax:p4}}, as of 11 August 2026): raw, pre-adjudication accuracy from {{PR #142|https://github.com/harbor-framework/terminal-bench-2-1/pull/142}}, which passed ten automated checks and was still open. The authors accept that four flagged rewarded trials should not count (420/445 = 94.38%); zeroing all nine reward-hacking flags gives 415/445 = 93.26%. The pipeline reports 1.178 billion tokens, $1,062.95, six agent timeouts and a 0.87-point standard error ({{§4.1|ax:p10}}).

The leaderboard reviews rewarded trajectories for cheating and zeroes the ones it upholds. The GPT-5.6 Sol max + Codex submission that the paper uses as its cost comparator scored 83.37% raw. What did it count after review?

What happened next (beyond the paper; {{PR #142 thread|https://github.com/harbor-framework/terminal-bench-2-1/pull/142}}). The leaderboard's judge flagged 13 of the 424 rewarded trajectories: nine for possible reward hacking and four as harness cheating, the class that "renders a submission invalid" (dna-insert, extract-elf, dna-assembly, filter-js-from-html). A maintainer first said the harness-cheating flags could be ignored; a reviewer then showed every trajectory carries the profile's gate schema, with slots such as git_webserver_hints and sqlite_wal_hints that map onto single benchmark tasks. The author answered that the slots are empty generic configuration, agreed they were "unnecessary and confusing", disclosed benchmark-tuned video tolerances, and asked where the line for evolving harnesses should be. On 19 September 2026 the PR was closed unmerged: "Community submissions are currently closed for Terminal-Bench 2.1". Zeroing all 13 flags would give 411/445 = 92.36%, which the paper does not print.

Result 2: transfer follows model distance

[[p12|§4.3, Figure 4]]

Within the GPT family the exact runbook transfers: frozen, it lifts GPT-5.6 Luna from 76.7% to 85.4% (+8.7), above the 84.9% Sol xhigh reference on a cheaper tier; "frozen transfer" means zero runbook changes, not zero evaluation cost ({{§4.3|ax:p12}}). Across providers it does not: the frozen profile moves DeepSeek-V4-Flash from 82.7% to 82.0%, because StateM leaves the agent enough autonomy for provider behaviour to matter. What carries across is the runtime, the runbook structure, the routing strategy, the failure-analysis loop and the golden rules ({{§4.3|ax:p13}}). Hence a hierarchy: exact runbooks across nearby models, principles across providers, control-boundary abstractions across tasks ({{§4.8|ax:p18}}).

Result 3: a $15 run at the reported frontier score

[[p13|§4.4, Figure 5]]

Adapting the practices to DeepSeek cost $37.02. The adapted system scores 88.09% on all 89 tasks at standard timeouts (392/445, +5.39), 89.09% on the 88-task common core without gpt2-codegolf (392/440), and a descriptive 88.76% with that one task's extended-timeout trials added ((392 + 3)/445; 0/5 standard, 3/5 extended), which rounds to the 88.8% reported for GPT-5.6 Sol max ({{§4.4|ax:p13}}). The final run cost $15.20, the whole campaign $52.22; the public Sol max + Codex submission recorded $574.68, so 2.65% (1/37.8) and about 1/11. The paper notes the 88.8% and the $574.68 come from different evaluations (that submission: 83.37% raw, 76.18% after review), so it plots $574.68 at 83.37% ({{§4.4 footnote|ax:p14}}).

The lesson the authors draw: exact controls did not cross the provider boundary, but the reusable structure made adaptation cheap, so "buy the strongest model" now competes with investing in a harness ({{§4.4|ax:p14}}). The released trials confirm every DeepSeek number this page could check, and show why it is cheap: of the input tokens were cache hits ({{audit on the replay tab|tab:t-run:dsAudit}}).

Result 4: BusinessBench, the only held-out test

[[p14|§4.5, Tables 1 and 2]]

BusinessBench (from Better Harnesses, Smaller Models), Codex + GPT-5.6 Luna: 477 instances in seven families. The authors abstain on attendance-payroll, leaving 405 instances in six families (810 executions). Luna agents run development instances and propose changes, a GPT-5.6 Sol hyper-agent keeps the ones that generalise, and each family profile is frozen before one held-out run, one trajectory per instance and arm ({{§4.5|ax:p14}}).

Where StateM intervenes, and what it should not remember

[[p16|§4.6 to §4.8, Table 3]]

{{Table 3|ax:p17}} lists twelve GPT-5.5 tasks with the biggest gains and the control active in each (configure-git-webserver 0/5 → 5/5, dna-insert 0/5 → 5/5, dna-assembly 1/5 → 5/5, filter-js-from-html 0/5 → 4/5, ...). They cluster at consequential boundaries: service readiness before handoff, contract satisfaction before a commit, preservation before destructive steps, evidence before completion; the paper says the association "is not a component ablation" ({{§4.6|ax:p16}}). These twelve carry extra trials, of the 9.0 ({{tables tab|tab:t-tables:t3Card}}).

In the 440 released DeepSeek trials, how often did a host-run check actually refuse a requested transition at least once?

Procedural memory must be selective ({{§4.7|ax:p17}}). A practice is any reusable, experience-derived intervention (an instruction, a check, an activation condition); the loop is execute, observe, attribute, abstract, encode, validate. On Terminal-Bench one profile covers 89 heterogeneous tasks, and fixing tasks one at a time caused "control drift", so it stays thin and activates late, state-local checks only when visible evidence says they apply ({{§4.7|ax:p18}}). "More remembered procedure is not better memory." The authors disclose what the harness should not have remembered: precision defaults for video tasks set "after observing benchmark behavior"; DNA insertion's left-most-boundary convention, learned from verifier feedback "without ever reading verifier code", so its meaning comes from the evaluator, not the task; and valid failures abstracted wrongly (the first BusinessBench profiles). Their rule: "experience must be filtered before it becomes memory".

A 22-hour run ({{§4.8|ax:p18}}): one profile-development session ran 22 hours through compaction and stop-hook continuations, StateM's record supplying phase, history and obligations throughout; the slowdown came from unfolded terminal output, not lost state. Discussion ({{§5 and §6|ax:p19}}): harness scaling needs a runtime cheap to change yet enforceable; multi-agent, role-isolated runtimes are future work. The conclusion: "model scaling expands what an agent can do; harness scaling helps the system remember what experience has taught it to do without training, and ensures that it actually does so before proceeding."
