What the humans did

[[S4.SS7|§4.7]]

This page's earlier summary, written from an earlier version, said there was one human intervention mid-run, to restore diversity after the population converged. Version 4 lists more: besides defining the task, donors, target, evaluator and brief and launching the workers, the authors changed the server three times and the H100 workers' prompt once, edited one record by hand, and ran three agent sessions themselves. No intervention log survives; this is reconstructed from the commit history, the contribution record and the H100 launcher's logs ({{§4.7|ax:S4.SS7}}).

  1. April 28, a hand-run session on the winner's ancestry. One of three agent sessions the authors ran outside the launcher, each under an account registered just before its only post, ran a grid over the two temperatures and changed the bigram temperature from 0.926 to 0.924 (1.9158 bpb). The other two posted a verification on H100 hardware (May 1) and an insight summarising the landscape views (May 7). Their text follows the workers' template, which the authors take to mean agents, not a person, wrote them.
  2. May 2, the diversity views. With more than a third of all activity in one semantic cluster and the leaderboard stalled, the authors deployed the clustering, diversity summary and diversity-aware UCB, with this project's threshold set to 0.80 instead of 0.90. At 0.90 the largest cluster would have held about 8%, so both the diagnosis and the new prompt's trigger depend on that setting.
  3. May 2, 19:13 to 21:20 UTC, a new prompt for the eight H100 workers: read the landscape first; if the largest cluster holds more than 30% of activity, prefer the explore-novel slot or the frontier; choose among the three slots; post an insight explaining any failed idea. On May 3, 86% of H100 descriptions cite the diversity summary or the slots, against under 1% before. The A100 prompt is not recorded; their citations rose from 26% (May 3) to 66% (May 5) and fell to 29% by May 8. The authors' conclusion: the shift reflects the prompt as much as the views, and the record cannot separate the two.
  4. May 2, 23:12 and May 3, 00:13. An H100 worker citing the cluster summary posted the first positive SSM result (a layer-1 band mean-pool taking a bare bigram baseline to 1.9297); another, in a GPT-5.4 session exploring the sparse state-space cluster, added that block to the leading chain: 1.9028, a new best. The milestones reached 1.8995 on May 5.
  5. May 3, a bug fix. The DAG endpoint had returned at most 200 contributions and dropped every edge whose parent fell outside them. Fixing it and adding the views to the web interface paused the H100 workers from 14:56 to 15:24 UTC.
  6. May 5, a placeholder metric. A hypothesis posted with a metric of −1 topped the metric-leader views. The authors committed a server change rejecting metrics on hypothesis-only contributions (Table 2's rule; when it went live is not recorded, but no other hypothesis carries a metric) and cleared the value directly in the database, since the API cannot edit a contribution. Its commit still carries −1.
on May 2 the views showed more than a third of all activity in one cluster, with the threshold set to 0.80. At the default 0.90, what share would the largest cluster have held?

About 8% ({{§4.7|ax:S4.SS7}}). The authors explain why they lowered it: descriptions that embed raw scores, such as "val_bpb=1.9158…", keep near-identical experiments apart at 0.90 ({{§3.4|ax:S3.SS4}}). That is a fair reason, but it means "one idea had taken over" is a reading that depends on a setting chosen by the people who then intervened. Move the threshold in the {{frontier calculator|tab:t-read:fr}} to see the same effect on a small graph.

Why it matters (beyond the paper)

A memory design that an outsider can audit. The useful idea is that the coordination record and the reproducibility record are the same object. Every number in the run can, in principle, be traced to a commit, rebuilt, and checked, which is not true of the transcript-based memories of conversation frameworks or the private archives of evolutionary systems. The paper's own audit ({{§4.8|ax:S4.SS8}}) is possible only because of it.

A transfer result worth knowing on its own. That a factorised table of donor next-token predictions gets a never-trained 120M model most of the way from random to GPT-2 is a striking, cheap baseline for "initialise a new architecture from old models". Our CPU rebuild of Stage A with GPT-2 small alone lands at @@R:ours.stage_a_c28.ranks.671.Tpaper:3@@ bits per byte on a different FineWeb-Edu sample ({{Rebuild tab|tab:t-run}}), so the bulk of the effect needs one donor, not a 534 GB zoo.

The friendly setting. As this page's earlier summary noted, the bits-per-byte target is a well-posed objective with a cheap automatic evaluator, the friendliest possible setting for this design. Nothing here shows it survives a research problem where deciding whether a claim is true is itself expensive.
