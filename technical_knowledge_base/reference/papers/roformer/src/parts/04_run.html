Train short, test long: six trained toy models

The paper's selling points are that RoPE makes attention depend on relative position only, that it gives "the flexibility of sequence length", and that it works with linear attention ({{abstract|ax:S1}}, {{§3.3|ax:S3.SS3}}). This tab tests all three on real models trained offline and running in your browser. Six one-layer causal Transformers, identical except for how position enters, were trained on sequences of 32 digits and are tested here on up to 128.

The task, the models and the training

Task (illustrative, built for this page): a stream of random digits 0 to 9; at every position t ≥ 4 the model must output (xt−1 + xt−4) mod 10. It needs one look exactly one back and one exactly four back, whatever t is: a pure relative-offset task, the case RoPE is designed for ({{§3.1|ax:S3.SS1}}). Chance is 10%.

Models: width 32, 4 heads of 8 (so 4 rotation pairs per head), one pre-LN block (attention plus a ReLU MLP of 128), a final LayerNorm and a linear readout; 13,418 parameters (17,514 with the learned table). The variants:

  • RoPE: rotation on q and k by Equation 34, interleaved pairs, θi = 10000−2i/8; nothing added to the input.
  • Sinusoidal: Vaswani et al.'s encoding added to the input ({{Eq. 4|ax:S2.E4}}), the baseline the paper replaces.
  • Learned absolute: a trainable table of 128 rows added to the input (BERT's choice); rows 32 to 127 never receive a gradient, which is exactly what happens to a real model's table past its training length.
  • No position: the causal mask is the only order signal.
  • Linear + RoPE and Linear, no position: elu(x) + 1 linear attention, with RoPE in the numerator only as {{Eq. 19|ax:S3.E19}}, and without.

Training (train.py): Adam (0.9, 0.98, ε 10−9) with linear warmup over 400 steps to 3 × 10−3, then inverse square root decay, the schedule of {{§4.1.2|ax:S4.SS1.SSS2}}; 6,000 steps of 64 fresh random sequences of length 32; seed 0. Between 33 and 52 seconds per model on a laptop CPU (2 threads). Held-out sets are drawn with separate seeds; of 500 held-out length-32 sequences, 0 occur in a 16,000-sequence sample of each training stream (1032 possible sequences). Weights are shipped at 6 bits (one base64 character per weight, one scale per row; vectors as float16); quantisation cost at most 0.3 points of held-out accuracy (the learned table, 100% to 99.7%). The JavaScript forward pass gives identical predictions to PyTorch on every one of 1,160 test sequences, logits within 5 × 10−4 and attention weights within 1.1 × 10−5 (check_forward.py).

One configuration choice, said plainly: the first run used 2 heads of 16. There the RoPE model plateaued at 84% at the trained length after 6,000 steps (97% after 20,000; 100% after 20,000 at a peak learning rate of 10−3), while sinusoidal reached 99%. Switching to 4 heads of 8 brought every softmax model to 100% at the trained length in 6,000 steps. The choice was made on in-range accuracy only, before looking at lengths past 32; the logs are in model/sweep.

Where one query looks

Try: RoPE, length 64, query position 63. Inside the trained length the head puts its weight on offsets 1 and 4; here the weight drains to offsets past 31, angles the slow pairs never showed in training (the {{spectrum|tab:t-read:s-spec}} section says why), and the answer breaks. Now tick the sliding window: the query sees only offsets 0 to 31, all of them trained, and RoPE is right at every position up to 128, because nothing in its scores depends on absolute position. Do the same with Sinusoidal or Learned: the window does not help them, because their inputs at position 40 are themselves unseen.

What the toy reproduces, and what it does not

  • Relative dependence: reproduces, exactly. The RoPE model's attention weights do not change when a sequence is moved (the {{shift test|tab:t-read:s-shift}}), and with a window it answers correctly at positions it never saw. No absolute model can do that.
  • "Flexibility of sequence length": does not reproduce as extrapolation. With full attention, RoPE lasts only a few positions longer than the absolute encodings: at positions 32 to 35 it is still right (sinusoidal , learned ), by 36 to 39 it is down to , and from there all three sit at chance. RoPE is defined at any length, but unseen offsets are unseen. This matches what ALiBi measured on WikiText-103 ({{Press et al., 2021, Figure 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}) and the reason Position Interpolation gives ("catastrophically high attention scores", {{Chen et al., 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595}}). The paper's own long-text test never left the trained length ({{How much to believe|tab:t-read:s-believe}}).
  • Position Interpolation without fine-tuning does not rescue it here: squeezing 128 positions into 32 also squeezes offsets 1 and 4 into 0.25 and 1, which the model never saw. Chen et al. fine-tune for up to 1,000 steps after interpolating; this toy does not.
  • Faster convergence: reproduces at toy scale, one seed. RoPE reaches 99% held-out accuracy in fewer steps than either absolute encoding (training curves above), the direction of {{Figure 3|ax:S4.F3}} (left) and of EleutherAI's runs. One seed per model, so the size of the gap is not measured.
  • Linear attention: the direction reproduces, the size is a toy limit. RoPE lifts linear attention well above its position-free twin, as {{Figure 3|ax:S4.F3}} (right) and EleutherAI's Performer runs found, but neither linear model comes near softmax on this task.
