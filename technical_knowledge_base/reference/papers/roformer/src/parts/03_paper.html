Problem

[[S2|§2 Background, Eqs. 1 to 10]]

Self-attention is position-agnostic: permute the tokens and every output is permuted the same way, so position must be injected somewhere ({{§1|ax:S1}}). The paper writes every scheme as a choice of three functions, qm = fq(xm, m), kn = fk(xn, n) and vn = fv(xn, n), feeding the usual softmax of qm⊤kn/√d ({{Eqs. 1 and 2|ax:S2.SS1}}). Every prior choice is additive:

  • Absolute ({{§2.2|ax:S2.SS2}}, {{Eq. 3|ax:S2.E3}}): add a position vector before the projections, W(xi + pi), with p either learned (one trainable row per position up to a maximum length L: BERT, GPT, ALBERT, ELECTRA) or the fixed sinusoids of Vaswani et al. ({{Eq. 4|ax:S2.E4}}).
  • Relative ({{§2.3|ax:S2.SS3}}): expand qm⊤kn into four terms (content and position, crossed; {{Eq. 6|ax:S2.E6}}) and surgically edit them. Shaw et al. add clipped trainable relative vectors to keys and values ({{Eq. 5|ax:S2.E5}}); Transformer-XL swaps in a sinusoid of m − n plus two trainable vectors ({{Eq. 7|ax:S2.E7}}); T5 keeps the content term and adds a trainable bias bi,j ({{Eq. 8|ax:S2.E8}}); TUPE adds a separately projected position term ({{Eq. 9|ax:S2.E9}}); DeBERTa keeps only the two middle, content-position terms with relative embeddings ({{Eq. 10|ax:S2.E10}}).

The authors see two structural problems. First, position is entangled with content rather than stated as what attention should see, which they argue is only the relative offset. Second, the relative variants edit the expanded attention matrix, so they cannot be used with linear attention, which never materialises q⊤k for every pair ({{§1|ax:S1}}, {{§2.3|ax:S2.SS3}}).

The gap: a formulation where the query-key inner product provably depends on the two token embeddings and their offset m − n only, while each position is still encoded independently, an absolute operation per token. That last property is also what makes KV caching cheap: a key is encoded once, at its own position, and never needs recomputing.

Idea

[[S3.SS1|§3.1 Formulation, Eq. 11]]

State the requirement as an equation and solve it ({{Eq. 11|ax:S3.E11}}): find fq and fk such that

⟨ fq(xm, m), fk(xn, n) ⟩ = g(xm, xn, m − n)

Each token is encoded using only its own absolute position, yet the inner product sees only the relative offset. The answer, derived in two dimensions and then stacked, is a rotation.

Rotation

[[S3.SS2|§3.2, §3.4.1, §3.4.2, Eqs. 12 to 16 and 34]]

The 2D case ({{§3.2.1|ax:S3.SS2.SSS1}}, {{Eq. 12|ax:S3.E12}}). Identify the plane with the complex numbers. Then

fq(xm, m) = (Wqxm) eimθ,   fk(xn, n) = (Wkxn) einθ,   ⟨qm, kn⟩ = Re[ (Wqxm)(Wkxn)* ei(m−n)θ ]

Rotate the projected query and key by an angle proportional to the position. The conjugation makes the phases subtract, so the absolute rotations by mθ and nθ leave only the relative angle (m − n)θ. As a matrix, it is a 2 × 2 rotation by mθ applied after W ({{Eq. 13|ax:S3.E13}}). The one-pair picture, where you can shift both positions and watch the dot product stay put, is the dial on the {{LLM Architecture Gallery|n:3c65c17b0d0d81be8a07f2562fa2030a}} page (Positional encoding section); this page goes on from there to the many pairs and the trained models.

Is rotation forced, or just one solution? (§3.4.1)

{{§3.4.1|ax:S3.SS4.SSS1}} writes fq, fk and g in polar form ({{Eq. 23|ax:S3.E23}}), so the constraint splits into a radial equation and an angular one ({{Eq. 24|ax:S3.E24}}). Setting m = n and using the initial condition at position 0 ({{Eq. 25|ax:S3.E25}}, {{Eq. 26|ax:S3.E26}}) shows the angle minus its value at 0 cannot depend on the token, only on the position, φ(m) ({{Eq. 28|ax:S3.E28}}); putting n = m + 1 makes φ(m + 1) − φ(m) a constant, so φ is an arithmetic progression, φ(m) = mθ + γ ({{Eqs. 29 and 30|ax:S3.E30}}): rotation at constant angular speed.

The radial part is not forced in the same way: the paper takes "a straightforward solution", a norm that does not depend on position ({{Eq. 27|ax:S3.E27}}), then chooses f(x, 0) = Wx to match Equation 3 and sets γ = 0 ({{Eqs. 32 and 33|ax:S3.E33}}). So the angle is forced; the rest are choices, and the paper itself calls the result "a solution". (Eq. 32 also has a typo, Wqxn for Wqxm.)

The general form ({{§3.2.2|ax:S3.SS2.SSS2}}, {{Eqs. 14 and 15|ax:S3.E15}}). For even d, split the vector into d/2 independent pairs and rotate pair i by its own frequency: f(x, m) = RdΘ,m W x, with R block-diagonal, 2 × 2 rotations by mθ1, …, mθd/2 and θi = 10000−2(i−1)/d, i = 1 … d/2. Since rotations are orthogonal and (RΘ,m)⊤RΘ,n = RΘ,n−m,

qm⊤kn = xm⊤ Wq⊤ RdΘ,n−m Wk xn

which satisfies the constraint exactly ({{Eq. 16|ax:S3.E16}}; the printed equation drops the transpose on Wq and the subscript on the first x). Position enters multiplicatively, into q and k only: values are untouched, norms are preserved (the paper notes orthogonality "ensures stability"), there are no parameters, and it is applied in every layer rather than once at the input. The paper's two indexings of θ differ by one: {{§3.2.2|ax:S3.SS2.SSS2}} counts i from 1 with −2(i−1)/d, {{§3.3|ax:S3.SS3}} from 0 with −2i/d; they are the same spectrum.

No matrix multiply needed ({{§3.4.2|ax:S3.SS4.SSS2}}, {{Eq. 34|ax:S3.E34}}). The block structure makes R·x two elementwise products: x ⊗ cos(mθ) + (−x2, x1, −x4, x3, …) ⊗ sin(mθ), with each cos and sin repeated for the two members of its pair: a few fused elementwise operations per layer. The toy models on this page use exactly this form (train.py's rotate, the page's JavaScript rotate). Much later code instead pairs dimension i with i + d/2 (Hugging Face's Llama {{rotate_half|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/llama/modeling_llama.py}}); it is the same rotation on a permuted layout.

Try: each dial is one of the d/2 = 8 pairs of a 16-dimensional query (solid) and key (dashed), rotated by mθi and nθi. The bar under each dial is that pair's share of qm⊤kn. Press Shift both: every dial turns, every bar stays exactly where it was. Move only m: the fast pairs on the left spin through whole turns while the slow pairs on the right barely move, so the fast ones resolve nearby offsets and the slow ones carry coarse order. The vectors are random and illustrative; the frequencies are the paper's, θi = 10000−2i/16.

The frequency spectrum

[[S3.SS2.SSS2|§3.2.2, §3.3]]

θi = 10000−2i/d is the same geometric spectrum as the original sinusoidal encoding, now used multiplicatively. Wavelengths run from 2π (pair 0 turns one radian per token) up to about 2π × 10000 (the slowest pair). Fast pairs resolve nearby offsets sharply; slow pairs change little over hundreds of tokens and carry coarse long-range order. This spectrum is the knob every later context-extension method turns; the {{Then and now|tab:t-then}} tab steps through them.

It also explains the toy's main result. With 8-dimensional heads (4 pairs) trained at length 32, pair 0 turns about 5 times inside the training window, but pairs 1, 2 and 3 never complete a turn: their largest angle seen in training is 3.1, 0.31 and 0.031 radians (recompute.py). At an offset of 40 they show angles no training example ever produced. That is the mechanism behind the {{train short, test long|tab:t-run}} failure.

Long-term decay

[[S3.SS4.SSS3|§3.3, §3.4.3, Figure 2, Eqs. 35 to 37]]

{{§3.4.3|ax:S3.SS4.SSS3}} writes the RoPE inner product as a sum of complex per-pair terms, Re[Σi hi ei(m−n)θi] with hi = q[2i:2i+1]k*[2i:2i+1] ({{Eq. 35|ax:S3.E35}}), and bounds it by Abel summation: with partial sums Sj = Σi<j ei(m−n)θi, the magnitude is at most maxi|hi+1 − hi| · Σj|Sj| ({{Eqs. 36 and 37|ax:S3.E37}}). The paper plots the average (1/(d/2)) Σj|Sj| against distance and it falls ({{Figure 2|ax:S3.F2}}): distant pairs get systematically weaker interaction by construction, which matches the linguistic prior and is the paper's explanation for its long-text advantage.

take random query and key vectors (independent Gaussian entries). Averaged over many draws, does the RoPE score q⊤R k get smaller as the distance grows from 0 to 256?

The decay is a bound, not a value. It is reached when queries and keys are aligned pair by pair (the all-ones line in the chart below); for independent Gaussian vectors the expected score is 0 at every distance and its typical size, the grey line, stays flat. Barbero et al. prove this ({{Proposition 3.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06205}}, 2024) and find that Gemma 7B uses the highest frequencies to build positional heads and the lowest as semantic channels, not the decay.

Raise the base to see why long-context models do: the bound decays far more slowly.

Linear attention

[[S3.SS3|§3.3, Eqs. 17 to 19; §4.4]]

Write attention generally as Σn sim(qm, kn) vn / Σn sim(qm, kn) ({{Eq. 17|ax:S3.E17}}). Softmax attention costs O(N²). Linear attention (Katharopoulos et al.) replaces sim with φ(q)⊤φ(k) for a non-negative feature map such as elu(x) + 1, so the key-value products can be summed first ({{Eq. 18|ax:S3.E18}}). Because RoPE is a norm-preserving rotation applied to q and k rather than an edit to the attention matrix, it composes: rotate the feature-mapped vectors in the numerator, RΘ,mφ(qm) and RΘ,nφ(kn), and leave the denominator unrotated to avoid dividing by zero ({{Eq. 19|ax:S3.E19}}). The paper is candid that the weights are then no longer a probability distribution (the numerator can contain negative terms) and "kindly argue[s]" they still model importance. On this basis it presents RoPE as the relative encoding that works with O(N) attention, arguing that the earlier relative schemes cannot ({{§1|ax:S1}}).

The toy tests this too. The same task with linear attention: see the {{Train short, test long|tab:t-run}} tab.

Results

[[S4|§4 Experiments, Tables 1 to 5, Figure 3]]

All experiments ran on two cloud servers with 4 × V100 GPUs each ({{§4|ax:S4}}). Every number is one run. Each experiment is rebuilt in {{The paper's tables, rebuilt|tab:t-tables}}.

  • WMT 2014 English to German ({{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1}}, {{Table 1|ax:S4.T1}}): 27.5 BLEU against 27.3 for Transformer-base, both trained by the authors in fairseq with the Vaswani recipe (37k joint BPE vocabulary, Adam 0.9/0.98, linear warmup from 1e-7 to 5e-4 then inverse square root, label smoothing 0.1, average of the last 5 checkpoints, beam 4, length penalty 0.6). A sanity check, not the headline.
  • BERT-style pretraining ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}, {{Figure 3|ax:S4.F3}} left): bert-base-uncased with its absolute positions replaced by RoPE, BookCorpus plus Wikipedia, batch 64, length 512, AdamW at 1e-5. The training MLM loss falls faster than BERT's under the same settings. The paper calls BERT's encoding "sinusoidal"; BERT's is a learned table ({{google-research/bert, modeling.py|https://github.com/google-research/bert/blob/master/modeling.py#L489-L499}}).
  • GLUE ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}, {{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}): 3 epochs, length 512, batch 32, best of learning rates 2, 3, 4 and 5e-5 on the validation set. RoFormer is ahead on MRPC (89.5 against 88.9), STS-B (87.0 against 85.8) and QQP (86.4 against 71.2), and behind on SST-2 (90.7 against 93.5), QNLI (88.0 against 90.5) and MNLI (80.2/79.8 against 84.6/83.4). The paper reports the three wins as "significantly" better; v2 of the same table called the overall result "comparable" ({{v2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864v2}}).
  • Performer with RoPE ({{§4.4|ax:S4.SS4}}, {{Figure 3|ax:S4.F3}} right): a 12-layer character-level Performer (768 dimensions, 12 heads, lucidrains' performer-pytorch) on Enwik8, learning rate 1e-4, batch 128, length 1024. With RoPE the loss falls faster and ends lower, the linear-attention claim in practice.
  • Chinese long text ({{§4.5|ax:S4.SS5}}, {{Tables 3 to 5|ax:S4.T5}}): RoFormer is WoBERT (word-level Chinese BERT) with RoPE, pretrained on about 34 GB of Chinese Wikipedia, news and forums in six stages whose maximum length moves between 128 and 1,536 ({{Table 4|ax:S4.T4}}). On CAIL2019-SCM (8,964 triplets of Supreme People's Court cases; is case B or C closer to A?) RoFormer at 1,024 tokens reaches 69.79% test accuracy against 68.10% for WoBERT capped at 512, the paper's main evidence for sequence-length flexibility. At 512, RoFormer (68.29%) is comparable to WoBERT and slightly ahead of BERT (67.77%).
  • The authors' own limitations ({{§4.5.5|ax:S4.SS5.SSS5}}): they cannot explain why RoPE converges faster than other encodings, and although its decay property is shared with other relative schemes, they have no faithful explanation of why it does better on long texts. Both statements count in their favour.

How much of this to believe

[[S4|the paper's own evidence, judged]]

What holds up. The mathematics: the relative-offset property is exact, a one-line identity anyone can check (the shift test above does it on trained weights, to the last digit), and so are norm preservation, zero parameters and compatibility with KV caching. The practical claims were replicated independently and at larger scale on the day v1 appeared: {{EleutherAI|https://blog.eleuther.ai/rotary-embeddings/}} found lower validation loss than learned absolute and T5 relative positions in GPT-NeoX on OpenWebText2 (2.759 against 2.809 and 2.801 at about 30B tokens) and at 1.5B parameters on the Pile (2.173 against 2.240 and 2.223 at about 8B tokens), about 30% faster convergence than learned absolute, a 1 to 3% overhead with fused kernels, and a large gain for Performer. Then the field adopted it ({{Why it matters|tab:t-read:s-why}}).

What does not hold up as evidence:

  • GLUE compares validation against test. The BERT row of {{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}} (88.9, 93.5, 90.5, 85.8, 71.2, 84.6/83.4) is digit for digit BERT-Base in the {{BERT paper's Table 1|https://arxiv.org/html/1810.04805v2#S4.T1}}, the GLUE evaluation-server test results; RoFormer's row is the best of four learning rates on the validation set ({{§4.3.2|ax:S4.SS3.SSS2}}). QQP's 71.2 is test F1; on the validation set BERT-base scores about 87.5 F1 ({{Hugging Face's run_glue example|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/examples/pytorch/text-classification/README.md}}, bert-base-cased, one seed), so the 15-point QQP win is an artefact of the split. Against that validation-set reference RoFormer is ahead only on MRPC (89.5 against 88.85) and behind on the other five ({{The paper's tables, rebuilt|tab:t-tables:tb2}}).
  • The pretraining budgets differ by 13 to 40 times. The GLUE RoFormer was pretrained for 100k steps of 64 × 512 tokens (3.3B tokens, {{§4.2.2|ax:S4.SS2.SSS2}}); the BERT it is compared against trained for 1M steps of 256 sequences (43B tokens, since 90% of its steps used length 128; 131B if all were 512; {{BERT A.2|https://arxiv.org/html/1810.04805v2#A1.SS2}}). That alone could explain the three losses.
  • The text and the figure disagree on the pretraining run. §4.2.2 says 100k steps; the x-axis of {{Figure 3|ax:S4.F3}} (left) runs to 250k. The plotted quantity is training loss, though §4.2.1 sets aside a 20% validation split.
  • Single runs, no error bars, small margins. WMT's +0.2 BLEU comes from one run of each model with no significance test. On CAIL2019-SCM the gain over WoBERT is 1.69 points on test (2.0 on validation); with about 1,536 to 1,793 test triplets the standard error of that difference is about 1.6 points, so the gain is about one standard error (recompute.py; independent-samples bound, since per-example results are not released).
  • The headline arithmetic. The paper says RoFormer-1024 "outperforms WoBERT by an absolute improvement of 1.5%"; Table 5 gives 69.79 − 68.10 = 1.69. The 1.5 is the gap to RoFormer-512 (69.79 − 68.29).
  • "Long text" is not extrapolation. RoFormer-1024 runs inside the 1,536 tokens it was pretrained at (Table 4, stages 2 and 5), while WoBERT is capped at 512 by its learned table. The result shows that a model which saw longer inputs uses them; it does not test running past the training length. When that was tested (ALiBi, {{Press et al., 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}), rotary degraded soon after the training length, like sinusoids, and so does this page's toy.
  • Table 4 is not evidence of generalisation. Accuracy rises with the stage's maximum length (correlation 0.96 with log length), but those are masked-token accuracies at each stage's own length: more context makes masked tokens easier to guess for any model, RoPE or not, and the stages are not a controlled comparison.
  • The splits changed between versions. {{v1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09864v1}} (April 2021, which had only the Chinese experiments) listed 5,102 / 1,500 / 1,536 triplets, 8,138 in all; v5 says 8,964 split 6:2:2, with the same accuracies.
  • The decay is a bound. Figure 2 bounds the score; for generic queries and keys there is no decay ({{Barbero et al., 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06205}}). The paper's own limitations section already concedes it cannot say why RoPE helps.

Verdict: believe the mechanism, which is exact and was validated at scale by others and then by the whole field; treat the paper's tables as illustrations. Its evidence for "better than BERT" mixes test and validation splits and very different budgets, and its evidence for "long text" never leaves the trained length.

Why it matters (beyond the paper)

The paper's benchmark numbers were unremarkable; the formulation conquered the field anyway. Picked up first by the open-source side, in EleutherAI's codebases in April 2021 ({{blog|https://blog.eleuther.ai/rotary-embeddings/}}) and GPT-J that year, then GPT-NeoX-20B ("based on our positive prior experiences", {{2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06745}}), PaLM ("better performance on long sequence lengths", {{2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311}}) and LLaMA ({{2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971}}), RoPE has been the default positional encoding of essentially every notable LLM since 2023: Llama 1 to 3, Mistral and Mixtral, Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma, gpt-oss (the {{LLM Architecture Gallery|n:3c65c17b0d0d81be8a07f2562fa2030a}} tracks the configs). The properties that won: exact relative dependence with per-token absolute application (each cached key is rotated once, at cache time, so KV caching needs no recomputation), zero parameters, norm preservation, and applicability at every layer.

It also became the substrate for the whole long-context toolchain, which works by manipulating the θi spectrum of a pretrained model (each step is drawn in {{Then and now|tab:t-then}}):

  • Base frequency scaling: train with a base larger than 10,000 for longer contexts, slowing the spectrum so it stays informative at long range. Llama 3 uses 500,000 ({{2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}); 1,000,000 is common (Qwen3-8B, Qwen2.5, Mixtral 8x7B {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B/blob/main/config.json}} files).
  • Position Interpolation (Meta, {{2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595}}): rescale positions m to m · Ltrain/Ltarget so every angle stays in the trained range; within 1,000 fine-tuning steps LLaMA reached 32,768 tokens with minimal degradation. Its abstract names the failure it avoids: extrapolation "may lead to catastrophically high attention scores".
  • NTK-aware and dynamic NTK scaling (2023): rescale the base instead of the positions, b′ = b · s|D|/(|D|−2), so high-frequency pairs (local resolution) are interpolated less than low-frequency ones ({{YaRN, Appendix A.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}).
  • YaRN ({{2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}): per-pair interpolation by wavelength ("NTK-by-parts") plus an attention temperature, √(1/t) = 0.1 ln s + 1; the standard recipe behind many 128k+ models, including DeepSeek-V3's extension (factor 40 from 4,096, {{config|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/blob/main/config.json}}) and gpt-oss (factor 32).
  • Partial and interleaved RoPE: rotate only some of each head's dimensions (GPT-J and GPT-NeoX's rotary_pct, 25%); DeepSeek's MLA carries a small decoupled RoPE key (64 dimensions) beside the position-free compressed latent, because "RoPE is incompatible with low-rank KV compression" ({{DeepSeek-V2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434}}); Llama 4's iRoPE interleaves RoPE layers with attention layers that have no positional embedding at all, for length generalisation ({{Meta|https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-4-multimodal-intelligence/}}). Multimodal variants such as Qwen2-VL's M-RoPE factor the rotation into time, height and width, reducing to 1D RoPE for text ({{2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12191}}).

Method aside, the 2D-pair rotation has become a standard reasoning tool: attention-sink analyses, length-generalisation theory and positional-encoding follow-ups are routinely phrased in RoPE's complex-exponential language (for example {{Barbero et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06205}}).
