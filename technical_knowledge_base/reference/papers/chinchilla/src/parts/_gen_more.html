The paper

Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (arXiv HTML v1)

NeurIPS 2022, the paper itself; the HTML version has the anchors this page links to. abstract page, PDF.

(1h 15m)

no official release; Epoch AI's replication data

the paper released no code, data or model. Besiroglu et al. (2024) recovered 245 final losses from the vector graphics of Figure 4 and published them with their refitting notebook (no licence file); this page's Refit tab runs on those points.

(20 min)

Best resources

Chinchilla's wild implications

nostalgebraist (LessWrong): the best conceptual unpacking of what the result means; reframes scaling as data-bound rather than parameter-bound and works through the loss equation term by term.

(30 min)

Chinchilla data-optimal scaling laws: in plain English

Alan Thompson (LifeArchitect): the 20-tokens-per-parameter heuristic spelled out, with tables applying it to real model sizes.

(15 min)

Chinchilla scaling: a replication attempt

Besiroglu, Erdil, Barnett, You (Epoch AI), 2024: refits Approach 3 from data read off the paper's Figure 4, finds the fitting problems, and shows the corrected fit agrees with Approaches 1 and 2. The essential critique; the arXiv version is 2404.10102.

(30 min)

Beyond Chinchilla-Optimal: Accounting for Inference in Language Model Scaling Laws

Sardana, Portes, Doubov, Frankle (MosaicML), ICML 2024: extends the scaling law to include inference cost, formalising why everyone now overtrains past the Chinchilla point; trains 47 models up to 10,000 tokens per parameter.

(45 min)

Resolving Discrepancies in Compute-Optimal Scaling of Language Models

Porian et al., NeurIPS 2024: explains the Kaplan against Chinchilla gap cause by cause (output-layer FLOPs, warmup, optimiser tuning) and finds that matching the cosine schedule, this paper's explanation, matters little.

(45 min)

Scaling Laws, Carefully

Lilian Weng: modern end-to-end treatment of the scaling-law literature, from Kaplan through Chinchilla to current practice, with the fitting pitfalls spelled out.

(50 min)

Connected papers in this knowledge base

Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (Kaplan et al.)

2020: the result this paper corrects. Its Then-and-now tab animates both allocation rules spending the same budgets, and its toy sweep shows why a small CPU sweep cannot find the optimum.

Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)

2020: the archetypal under-trained model by this paper's account (175B on 300B tokens; Table 3 says 3.7T).

The Llama 3 Herd of Models

2024: fits its own IsoFLOP law to choose 405B, and deliberately overtrains the 8B and 70B models for inference economics.

DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report

2024: modern frontier practice, a heavily overtrained mixture of experts (14.8T tokens, 37B active parameters); MoE needs its own scaling treatment.

Switch Transformers

2021: sparse models change the N in the FLOP accounting; this paper's related work flags that MoE scaling analyses shared Kaplan's fixed-token-count flaw.

Mixtral of Experts

2024: an open sparse model whose active and total parameters differ, the case Chinchilla's dense law does not cover.

WSM

2025: decay-free learning-rate schedules with checkpoint merging, one answer to the schedule-length problem this paper raised against Kaplan.

Attention Is All You Need

2017: the architecture every one of the 400 runs uses.
