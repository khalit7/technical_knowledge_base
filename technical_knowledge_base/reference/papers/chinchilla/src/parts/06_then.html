Then and now

Two things happened after 2022. The allocation held: every later IsoFLOP study lands near equal scaling, and the Approach 3 outlier turned out to be a fitting bug. And the objective changed: once a model is served to millions, training compute is not the cost to minimise, so models are now trained far past 20 tokens per parameter. The Kaplan-against-Chinchilla comparison itself is animated on the {{Scaling Laws page (Then and now tab)|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b08a1debb0c15cd252}}, with both rules spending the 1021, GPT-3, Gopher and Llama 3 405B budgets; this tab picks up where it ends.

Train for the deployment: the inference-aware optimum

Sardana et al.'s problem ({{their Eq. 3|https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00448v2#S2}}): fix the quality, the loss ℓ of a Chinchilla-optimal model, and choose N and D on the iso-loss curve L̂(N, D) = ℓ to minimise 6ND + 2NDinf, where Dinf is every token the model will ever process at inference. Solved here by a one-dimensional search along the curve. "Chinchilla-optimal" means on the chosen fit's frontier, as in Sardana et al.; with the paper's constants that is more than 20 tokens per parameter (the counters show how many). With the paper's unrounded constants it reproduces their examples closely: a 13B-quality model with 2 × 10¹² inference tokens becomes (they report 7B and 1.7 × 10²² FLOPs, 17%). The exponent, replicated

a in Nopt ∝ Ca; b = 1 − a under 6ND. DeepSeek LLM measures model scale as non-embedding FLOPs per token rather than parameters, and finds the exponent rises with data quality ({{§3.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02954v1#S3.SS3}}); Llama 3 fits tokens, D* = 0.29 C0.53, so a = 0.47, and predicts 402B parameters on 16.55T tokens for its 3.8 × 1025 FLOPs ({{§3.2.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S3.SS2.SSS1}}).

What changed and what survived

Changed

  • The objective. Compute-optimal for training became deliberately overtrained for inference: Llama 2 7B at 286 tokens per parameter, Llama 3 8B at 1,875, Gemma 2 9B at 889 ({{Sardana et al. §1|https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00448v2#S1}}).
  • The constants. The Approach 3 fit was redone: E 1.82, A 482, B 2085, α 0.348, β 0.366 ({{Besiroglu et al.|https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10102v2#S3}}); and fits made only from typical ratios overstate what extra tokens buy at extreme ratios ({{Sardana et al. §5|https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00448v2#S5}}).
  • The explanation for Kaplan. Output-layer FLOPs, warmup and optimiser tuning, more than schedule length ({{Porian et al.|https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19146v1#S1}}).
  • The data. Quality moves the optimum: cleaner data favours larger models ({{DeepSeek LLM §3.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02954v1#S3.SS3}}); repeated data has its own law ({{Muennighoff et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264}}).

Survived

  • Equal scaling of parameters and tokens for a fixed training budget, near 20 tokens per parameter at these scales.
  • IsoFLOP sweeps of small models, with matched schedules, as the way to size a flagship (Llama 3, DeepSeek LLM).
  • The parametric form E + A/Nα + B/Dβ, now the starting point for inference-aware and data-constrained laws.
  • Dataset scale and quality as a first-class constraint.
