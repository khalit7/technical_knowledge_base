Train a LoRA in your browser

A real model, trained here by real arithmetic: a small Transformer block pretrained to count, and adapted, while you watch, to a task whose correct weight update is known. That lets this tab test the paper's claims directly: does a low-rank update suffice, on which matrices, and does LoRA find the true update's directions?

The toy, in five lines
  • Model. One Transformer block read out at position 0: embeddings (16 symbols plus a sink token) and positions, two-head attention (Wq, Wk, Wv, Wo, 32 × 32), a residual, a ReLU MLP (W1 128 × 32, W2 32 × 128), and an 11-way output. parameters.
  • Pretraining. Input: a query symbol, the sink, then 10 symbols; answer: how many times the query occurs (0 to 10). Trained with AdamW and label smoothing 0.1, then stored in 8 bits: .
  • Downstream tasks are teachers. Each task is the same base model with one matrix edited by a known ΔW* = c U V⊤ of known rank, and a sequence's label is the edited model's answer. So the update to find is known, and accuracy is agreement with the teacher.
  • The four tasks.
  • Training follows the paper: W0 frozen, A Gaussian and B zero, update scaled by α/r with α = 8 fixed, Adam with warmup and linear decay, batches of 64 fresh sequences, the teacher's output distribution as the target. Held-out sequences come from a seed the training stream never uses.
