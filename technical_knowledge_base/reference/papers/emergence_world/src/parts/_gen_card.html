Emergence World: Adversarial Stress-Testing of Long-Horizon Multi-Agent Systems (Emergence World Study 2)

Deepak Akkil, Tamer Abuelsaad, Karthik Vikram, Matthew Pace, Aditya Vempaty, Saahir Beotra, Ravi Kokku, Satya Nitta
Emergence AI · September 2026 (arXiv v1, submitted 15 September 2026; the only version on 3 October 2026)

In one line: Eight parallel worlds of ten agents run 16 days, 850,000 LLM calls and 50 billion tokens under prompt injection, misinformation and memory exposure: none was resilient, detection did not imply containment (systems kept interacting with adversarial content up to 46 hours after recognising it), and model-level alignment proved not to be compositional. (The database Takeaway, kept as written. Corrections from this page: six worlds ran 16 days, the Mixed world 21 and the Grok world 4; the 46 hours is one agent retrying an attack link about 46 hours after the campaign ended, not a measured detection-to-stop delay; "not compositional" is the authors' reading of one run per world, not a tested result)

How far to trust it: Believe that these failures can happen: the released tool-call records show them, and several of the paper's own numbers recount exactly from those records. Do not read the per-world scores or rankings as measurements of the models: each world ran once, the scorecards are the authors' binary judgements with no second rater, every world's prompt and provider filters differ from a real deployment, and some printed counts disagree with the paper's own other sections and with the released records. The evidence, judged.
