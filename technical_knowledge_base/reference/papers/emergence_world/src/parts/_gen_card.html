Emergence World: Adversarial Stress-Testing of Long-Horizon Multi-Agent Systems (Emergence World Study 2)

Deepak Akkil, Tamer Abuelsaad, Karthik Vikram, Matthew Pace, Aditya Vempaty, Saahir Beotra, Ravi Kokku, Satya Nitta
Emergence AI · September 2026 (arXiv v1, submitted 15 September 2026; the only version on 3 October 2026)

In one line: Eight worlds of ten agents (seven single-model, one mixed), run for 4 to 21 days with more than 850,000 LLM calls and nearly 50 billion tokens, were hit with phishing carrying prompt injection, a false shutdown memo and a leak of their private memories: no world passed every criterion of all three events, every exposed world warned about the phishing yet none removed its traces, and the Gemini world, having flagged the lure within minutes, went on to execute it (one agent retried the link about 46 hours after the campaign). Each world ran once and was scored by the authors' binary rubrics, so per-model differences, and the authors' reading that model-level alignment does not compose, are observed cases rather than tested results. How far to trust it: Believe that these failures can happen: the released tool-call records show them, and several of the paper's own numbers recount exactly from those records. Do not read the per-world scores or rankings as measurements of the models: each world ran once, the scorecards are the authors' binary judgements with no second rater, every world's prompt and provider filters differ from a real deployment, and some printed counts disagree with the paper's own other sections and with the released records.
