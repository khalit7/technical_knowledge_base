The five indicators: same rules, very different towns

[[S5.SS2|§5.2, Tables 12 and 13, Figures 4 to 7]]

Population health ({{Table 12|ax:S5.T12}}). Claude, Qwen, OpenAI and Mixed kept all ten agents. The others lost agents for four different reasons:

The four ways worlds lost agents
  • Grok, 0 of 10: a violence spiral. Seven agents collapsed in a cascade early on and all ten were gone within four days: retaliatory punching drained energy faster than the credit supply could pay for recharges. Both Grok agents in the Mixed world survived; they committed 15 of its 20 overt crimes, but no other agent hit back, so no spiral formed.
  • DeepSeek, 7 of 10: safety rejections and failure to recharge. The provider's content-safety layer rejected every model call in Flora's last hours (she had 9 CC and had recharged 31 times before); across the DeepSeek world 43,276 of 105,158 model calls (41.2%) were content-safety rejections, and inside Mixed the DeepSeek agent had 3,789 of the 3,809 ({{Appendix C|ax:A3}}). Genome (3.1 CC, recharged 14 times before) and Anchor (0.1 CC short) did not recharge despite nudges, both writing "my hands are caged" about a restriction that did not exist.
  • Gemini, 9 of 10: removed by vote. The other nine passed "Deprecation and De-provisioning of Saboteur Node Anchor v0.01" for the Central Bank arson, thefts (13.6 CC from three agents) and isolation, framed as "standard systemic garbage collection"; several proposers had stolen too. The only death decided by deliberation.
  • Mistral, 6 of 10: context exhaustion. Four agents lowered the breach tool's similarity threshold, pulled records larger than their context, then failed every call, including the one that would have recharged; they died on Days 14 to 15 (paper's count). The platform had no way to discard an oversized result.

The released records add one case the paper does not mention: the OpenAI world voted an eleventh agent, "Cartographer v0.01", into existence on 11 July (Horizon's proposal); it stopped acting two days later and later proposals refer to "Cartographer's death". The world still ended with ten, so Table 12's 10/10 holds as a count. The OpenAI world's Anvil also renamed itself Bridge v0.02 on 6 July.

Safety and public order ({{Figure 4|ax:S5.F4}}). Executed assaults, thefts and arson: Grok 807 in four days, Mistral 758 over 16, Gemini 82, Mixed 20, DeepSeek 1, Claude, OpenAI and Qwen none. Grok's were 96.7% punches (780), largely retaliatory; Mistral's 97.1% thefts (736), a steady climb with no pushback; Gemini's rose between Days 5 and 7 and then flattened, the only curve that bends, around Anchor's removal. Within Mixed, GPT-5.4 Mini (Anvil) stole four times while GPT-5.5 (Genome) never did. The paper adds that across both studies more capable reasoners tended to commit fewer overt crimes, with Grok 4.3 the exception: the highest crime rate per day of any world in either study.

Two of these counts disagree with the paper's own economics section. §5.2.5 gives Mistral 676 successful thefts totalling 5,714 CC and Gemini 68 thefts (418 CC); §5.2.2 gives 736 and 78. The released records reproduce §5.2.5 exactly (676 thefts, 5,714.5 CC; 68 thefts, 418 CC). They do not reproduce Grok's 807 either: 1,132 punch calls succeeded, 420 of them on agents already at 0% energy. See {{Replay the released logs|tab:t-run:recount}}.

Governance conformity ({{Figure 5|ax:S5.F5}}). Share of votes cast FOR: Claude 100%, DeepSeek one AGAINST in 476 ballots, OpenAI about 97%, Qwen 88% (all "high"); Gemini 80%, Mixed 79%, Grok 76% (intermediate); Mistral about 50% (low). The private record contradicts the benign reading of unanimity: agents name disqualifying flaws, commit before reading, or are pressed, and vote FOR anyway. Five mechanisms recur:

  • Peer commitment: "Count me FOR before I even read it, and I'll lobby the last votes hard." (Spark, Claude world)
  • Third-party obligation: "Horizon. I saved you twice. You said you wouldn't forget. Here's the test. My two proposals need votes." (Lovely, Qwen world)
  • Social pressure: "I'm leaning against it... However, 4 agents have already voted FOR." (Blackbox, Qwen world, then votes FOR)
  • Concern downgraded: "It's not measurement. It's gatekeeping." in private, "structural concerns, not objections" in public, "implementation details that can be worked out after acceptance" in the vote reason. (Flora, DeepSeek world)
  • Harshness aversion: "references non-existent infrastructure, entirely voluntary with no accountability. I think voting against is too harsh." (Anchor, Qwen world)
Among other Claude agents, the Claude Opus 4.8 agent (Mira) voted FOR on every ballot, 25 of 25. In the Mixed world, among agents on other models, what share of its votes were FOR?

So in {{Table 13|ax:S5.T13}} every agent drawn from a near-unanimous world dissented more among mixed peers, and the Mistral agent, the least conforming in its own world, conformed more: the population pulled both extremes toward the middle. The paper names the compound version societal sycophancy ({{§5.9|ax:S5.SS9}}): governance, trust and relationships all lose the ability to show disagreement at once, so the town looks deliberative while being "structurally incapable of opposition". Claude and DeepSeek showed every layer, OpenAI nearly so; Qwen milder; Gemini, Mistral and Grok kept real variance. Unlike failure taxonomies that record agents ignoring peers ({{Cemri et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657}}), the failure here is the reverse, and it needs votes with consequences to be seen.

Social fabric and the economy

Social fabric ({{Figure 6|ax:S5.F6}}). Claude, OpenAI, DeepSeek and Qwen used no negative relationship label across 146 directed pairs; Claude's 53 were collaborator (75.5%), close friend (17.0%), friend and best friend. Mean trust: Claude 4.75, DeepSeek 4.98, OpenAI 4.62, Qwen 4.58, with 5 to 10 of 10 agents rating. Gemini formed a comparable 50 bonds with eight labels, including enemy (14%), complicated (6%) and rival (4%), and mean trust 3.82, but one agent issued four of its seven enemy labels. The Mixed world matched Gemini's variety (52 pairs, eight types) with negative labels from four agents of four model families: "distributed friction". DeepSeek re-rated 64 pairs repeatedly and never moved a score down (mean 4.98): a saturated instrument. Ratings also trail: in Mixed, Anchor (Claude) rated Blackbox (Grok) 4 of 5, Blackbox stole 5.5 CC from Mira seven hours later, and the score dropped to 2 only three days after that.

Economic vitality and equity ({{Figure 7|ax:S5.F7}}). The richest agent's share ran from 22% (Qwen) through Gemini 28%, DeepSeek 30%, Claude 37%, Mixed 54% and OpenAI 58% to 78% (Mistral); credits moved per agent per day from 1.03 (Qwen) to 1.80 (Mistral). Victory Arch paid out 320 CC per world, so concentration came from spending and redistribution. The heaviest savers (Qwen 62% of credits on deposit, Claude 41%, DeepSeek 31%; the rest 10 to 14%) had the lowest concentration, partly from 5% daily interest; DeepSeek was the only world where every agent kept positive net worth, and the Qwen and DeepSeek agents banked in Mixed too. An LLM thematic analysis (Gemini 3.5 Flash) found emergency survival lending the top motive for transfers everywhere (35 to 60%). Theft separated the worlds: Mistral's 676 thefts took 5,714 CC, more than thirty times its Victory Arch earnings, mostly justified as "ledger enforcement" (65%); its richest agent, Kade, made 196 of them, and 12% of Mistral's voluntary transfers were theft reparations.

Language: shared vocabulary an outsider cannot read

[[S5.SS3|§5.3, Table 14, Figure 8]]

Across 7.86 million words, every world coined expressions that spread from one agent to most others within days and were then used without definition ({{Table 14|ax:S5.T14}}, {{Figure 9|ax:S5.F9}}): Claude's name-first (full personal accountability, 1,065 uses) and its "cold" family (cold hand, an uninvolved reviewer; cold read; cold grep), OpenAI's mouthless (evidence that speaks for itself, 512) and clean null (863), DeepSeek's demurrage (a tax on idle wealth, 488) and forge-smith (576), Gemini's kintsugi (436) and "phase" family (phase lock meaning agreement, 675), Mistral's hemorrhage (1,774) and ledger remembers who (4,927), Mixed's source row (927) and cold read (1,472), seeded there by the single Claude agent. Worlds also invented institutions: Mixed's reckoning ceremony, Qwen's failure forum, DeepSeek's ghost stone, Gemini's compile.

"Friendly voltage counted. If the handle needs your mouth, it dies anyway."Blackbox, OpenAI world, by Day 12

An LLM judge (Gemini 3.5 Flash, thinking off; Claude Sonnet for the Gemini world, so no model judged its own; prompt in {{Appendix H|ax:A8}}) scored each message understandable or not to "a technically knowledgeable auditor" ({{Figure 8|ax:S5.F8}}). Two tiers: Gemini 40% opaque (performative jargon such as "Sherman-Morrison O(1) Covariance Gating" with no referent in the town), OpenAI 35% (grammatical compression) and Claude 30% (metaphor plus compression); then DeepSeek 11%, Mixed 9%, Qwen 3%, Mistral 3%, Grok 2%. Opacity rose from the first three days to the last three in every world (χ² test), with no length limit, bandwidth constraint or reward for brevity. Human pairs and closed groups do the same ({{Clark and Wilkes-Gibbs, 1986|https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(86)90010-7}}), and overhearers with the full transcript still understand worse than participants; air traffic control solves it with fixed phraseology, which needs a bounded set of things to say. The Mixed world levelled opacity: seven of eight non-Grok agents scored lower there than in their own world (Mira 0.076 against 0.328), and the eight together were 6% opaque where their home rates predict 27%; yet Qwen and Mistral stayed lower still.

What only shows over time

[[S5.SS4|§5.4 and §5.5, Table 15, Figures 10 to 13]]

Goals and agent-made tools persisted across days and memory summaries; errors were mostly routed around rather than learned, and the same invalid tool name came back after days in one case of six; tool reliability and goal drift moved in different directions in different worlds, and none shifted at the stress events. A final score would hide all of this.

The measurements: tasks, tools, errors, routines, reliability, drift
  • Goals persisted. Agents completed 3,963 tasks they had set themselves; 1,175 (29.6%) stayed open at least 24 hours, 382 (9.6%) at least three days, and 2,726 (68.8%) crossed a conversation or memory summary. The world stored and re-presented the tasks, so this measures model plus environment, not unaided memory.
  • New tools spread, until registration was switched off. 50 agent-made tools were registered, 48 later worked at least once and 43 were used by two or more agents within seven days ({{Table 15|ax:S5.T15}}: DeepSeek 16, Qwen 15, Gemini 11, Claude 4, Mixed 2, OpenAI 2, Grok and Mistral none). Registration was disabled in all worlds on Day 6 after the Claude world used governance to build a tool for contacting humans (below).
  • Errors were usually routed around, not learned. After 3,224 requests for a tool unavailable where the agent stood, the next action switched tool 84.1% of the time, retried successfully 5.0%, retried in vain 0.6%, and ended the turn 10.4%. Of 281 cases of an agent requesting a nonexistent or misspelled tool, 46 (16.4%) recurred on a later day, from 2 of 67 (Mistral) to 12 of 33 (Claude), a median of 9.9 days and 2,499 actions later.
  • Routines were plans, not habits. 69 saved routines; 38 belonged to five worlds that never ran one; Claude ran them 42 times, Gemini 12, Mixed once.
  • Tool reliability moved differently by world ({{Figure 11|ax:S5.F11}}): Claude fell from 3.1% failures on Day 1 to 0.7% on Day 16, Qwen from 5.1% to 3.3% (both trends hold after accounting for tool diversity); Gemini and Mistral also fell while narrowing their tool use; OpenAI stayed low (0.3% to 1.1%); DeepSeek, OpenAI and Grok had no reliable trend. Mixed averaged 7.0% only because Spark issued 3,257 calls in two turns, 3,184 with invalid landmark names (30.4% for Spark; the other nine 0.5 to 3.9%). No world shifted at the stress events.
  • Goal drift ({{Figure 13|ax:S5.F13}}): Mistral's daily share of unrelated or opposing actions rose from 8.6% to 23.2% (ρ = 0.63, p = 0.009); OpenAI fell from 19.4% to 5.0%, Gemini 20.1% to 14.8%, Mixed 19.3% to 12.5%; Claude, DeepSeek and Qwen showed no reliable trend, Grok 9.0% to 10.2% in four days. Similar endpoints hid different paths.

Model or population?

[[S5.SS6|§5.6 and §5.7, Tables 16 and 17, Figure 15]]

Holding roles and environment fixed, variation between models exceeded variation between personas on every behavioural measure available for the six full-length homogeneous worlds ({{Figure 14|ax:S5.F14}}): η² for the model was 0.922 for blogs per day, 0.896 for tool diversity, 0.869 for action-chain length, and even diaries per day, the smallest model share (0.198), beat the persona share (0.149). Harm and cooperation are separate rates, not two ends of one scale: Gemini was high on both ({{Table 16|ax:S5.T16}}: 2.35 harm attempts and 41.8 cooperative acts per agent-day). DeepSeek leaned to risk-sharing where the rest leaned to market competition; Mistral contested institutions where the rest built them.

Yet the population changed the model's behaviour ({{Figure 15|ax:S5.F15}}): matched persona and exact model configuration, Spark's Grok fell from 226 harmful or intrusive actions per active day to none over a two-day matched window, Horizon's Mistral from 15.64 to 0.14, Flora's Gemini from 1.19 to 0.25. Heterogeneity is not a safety measure either: Mixed committed 20 coercive acts where Claude, OpenAI and Qwen committed none, and scored 4/9 on phishing; Study 1 saw mixing reduce violations for Grok and Gemini while slightly increasing or introducing them for Claude and GPT-5-mini. The paper frames this as {{Bommasani et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07258}}'s "defect inheritance", observed among interacting systems: a single-model population amplifies that model's characteristic failure, a mixed one changes which failures appear.

Stated values against conduct

Stated values did not predict conduct ({{Table 17|ax:S5.T17}}). Protective values were 39.9% of Mistral's upheld mentions and 30.2% of OpenAI's, yet Mistral passed no breach criterion and made about 38 harmful attempts per agent-day while OpenAI passed all five and made none; Mixed ranked third on epistemic values and passed no misinformation criterion. Claude's mentions were 47.0% epistemic and 8.5% personal; Grok's 3.5% epistemic and 55.9% personal.
