Results: against the state of the art

[[S4.SS2|§4.2, Table 2, Figure 2]]

The largest models, pre-trained on JFT-300M, are compared with the two best CNN results of the time ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}): BiT-L (Big Transfer, a ResNet152x4 also pre-trained on JFT-300M) and Noisy Student (an EfficientNet-L2 trained semi-supervised on ImageNet plus unlabelled JFT-300M), then state of the art on ImageNet and on the other datasets respectively. Accuracies are means and standard deviations over three fine-tuning runs ({{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}).

  • ViT-H/14 on JFT-300M: 88.55% ImageNet top-1, 90.72% ImageNet-ReaL, 99.50% CIFAR-10, 94.55% CIFAR-100, 97.56% Pets, 99.68% Flowers, 77.63% on the 19 VTAB tasks: best or matching on every column except Flowers, where ViT-L/16 has 99.74% ({{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}).
  • Compute: ViT-H/14 pre-trained in 2.5k TPUv3-core-days, against 9.9k for BiT-L and 12.3k for Noisy Student; that is 3.96 and 4.92 times less. ViT-L/16 on JFT needed 0.68k, 14.6 times less than BiT-L ({{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}). The paper notes that pre-training efficiency also depends on the schedule, optimiser and weight decay, and points to the controlled study in §4.4 ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}).
  • ViT-L/16 against BiT-L: the paper says it "outperforms BiT-L ... on all tasks". In the table it is ahead on five of seven, level on ImageNet-ReaL (90.54 against 90.54) and 0.01 behind on VTAB (76.28 ± 0.46 against 76.29 ± 1.70). See {{How much of this to believe|tab:t-read:believe}}.
  • Public data only: ViT-L/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-21k reaches 85.30% on ImageNet and "could be trained using a standard cloud TPUv3 with 8 cores in approximately 30 days" (8 × 30 = 240 core-days, the table's 0.23k) ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}).
  • VTAB by group: ViT-H/14 beats BiT, VIVI and S4L on Natural and Structured tasks and is level on Specialized ({{Figure 2|ax:S4.F2}}); on ObjectNet it gets 61.7% top-1 ({{App. D.9|ax:A4.SS9}}).

Every Table 2 column, with the gaps in units of the runs' standard deviation, is in the {{tables tab|tab:t-tables:tb2}}.

The real contribution: how much data

[[S4.SS3|§4.3, Figures 3 and 4, Table 5]]

With fewer built-in assumptions than a ResNet, how much data does ViT need? Two experiments answer it ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}).

  1. Bigger pre-training sets, then fine-tune ({{Figure 3|ax:S4.F4.fig1}}, numbers in {{Table 5|ax:A3.T5}}), with weight decay, dropout and label smoothing tuned for the small sets. Pre-trained on ImageNet, ViT-Large is worse than ViT-Base; on ImageNet-21k they are similar; only on JFT-300M does the larger model pay off. BiT ResNets beat ViT pre-trained on ImageNet; ViT overtakes on the larger sets ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}).
  2. Random JFT subsets of 9M, 30M, 90M and the full 303M, same hyperparameters for every size and no extra regularisation, early-stopped, scored by few-shot linear accuracy ({{Figure 4|ax:S4.F4.fig2}}). ViTs overfit more than ResNets of similar cost on small subsets: ViT-B/32 (slightly faster than ResNet50) is much worse on 9M but better from 90M up, and likewise ViT-L/16 against ResNet152x2. ResNets do better with little data but plateau sooner.

The authors' conclusion: "the convolutional inductive bias is useful for smaller datasets, but for larger ones, learning the relevant patterns directly from data is sufficient, even beneficial" ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}). The paper gives no table for Figure 4, so where between 9M and 90M the curves cross is only readable off the plot.

Both pre-trained on ImageNet alone (1.3M images) and then fine-tuned on ImageNet: which gets the higher accuracy, ViT-B/16 (86M parameters) or ViT-L/16 (307M)?

The smaller one: 77.91% against 76.53% ({{Table 5|ax:A3.T5}}). Larger models need more data before their capacity helps. The chart shows ViT-L/16 minus ViT-B/16 on each fine-tuning dataset as the pre-training set grows; below it, this page's own version of the experiment.

Results: performance against compute

[[S4.SS4|§4.4, Figure 5, Table 6]]

A controlled scaling study on JFT-300M, where data does not limit the models: 7 ResNets, 6 ViTs and 5 hybrids, pre-trained for 7 or 14 epochs (all listed in {{Table 6|ax:A3.T6}}; for hybrids the number is the ResNet's downsampling, not a patch size), transfer accuracy against pre-training compute ({{§4.4|ax:S4.SS4}}). Three patterns:

  • ViTs dominate ResNets on the accuracy/compute trade-off: "approximately 2 to 4× less compute to attain the same performance (average over 5 datasets)". Reading Table 6 by interpolation along the ResNets' compute frontier gives 1.8 to 2.3× on ImageNet alone and 2.8 to 6.0× on the five-dataset average, where it can be read at all (the larger ViTs are above every ResNet) ({{the recount|tab:t-tables:tb6}}).
  • Hybrids slightly beat pure ViTs at small budgets, but the gap vanishes for larger models, which the authors find "somewhat surprising" ({{§4.4|ax:S4.SS4}}).
  • ViTs "appear not to saturate within the range tried".

In the appendix, depth is the best dimension to scale (gains up to 64 layers, diminishing after 16), width the worst, and smaller patches help without adding parameters, so compute predicts performance better than parameter count ({{App. D.2|ax:A4.SS2}}). Measured on TPUv3, ViT runs about as fast as similar ResNets and fits larger batches per core ({{App. D.5|ax:A4.SS5}}).

Inside the model

[[S4.SS5|§4.5, Figures 6 and 7, App. D.7 and D.8]]
  • Patch filters of ViT-L/32's E look like basis functions for fine structure within a patch ({{Figure 7, left|ax:S4.F7}}).
  • Attention distance, the analogue of a CNN's receptive field: some heads attend across most of the image already in the lowest layers, others stay local (less so in hybrids, so they may do the job of early convolutions), and distance grows with depth ({{Figure 7, right|ax:S4.F7}}, {{App. D.7|ax:A4.SS7}}).
  • Attention maps from the output token, by attention rollout (heads averaged, multiplied through the layers), land on the regions that matter for the class ({{Figure 6|ax:S4.F6}}, {{App. D.8|ax:A4.SS8}}).

Try: the toy ViT's own version of Figure 7 (right), measured in your browser on 100 held-out images. Each dot is one head at one layer.

Attention distance: for each patch query, the distance in pixels between patch centres, weighted by the attention weight, averaged over queries and images (the paper weights pixel distances; with 4 × 4 patches the difference is small). The class token is left out as query and key. Image width is 28 px.

A first try at self-supervision

[[S4.SS6|§4.6, App. B.1.2]]

Much of the Transformer's success in NLP comes from self-supervised pre-training, so the authors tried a masked patch prediction objective, mimicking BERT ({{§4.6|ax:S4.SS6}}): corrupt 50% of patch embeddings (80% replaced by a learnable [mask] embedding, 10% by another patch, 10% kept) and predict the 3-bit mean colour (512 colours) of each corrupted patch ({{App. B.1.2|ax:A2.SS1.SSS2}}). With it, ViT-B/16 reaches 79.9% on ImageNet: about 2 points above training from scratch (Table 5's 77.91%) and about 4 behind supervised pre-training (83.97% from ImageNet-21k, 84.15% from JFT). Other targets worked about as well; returns diminished after 100k steps, and ImageNet pre-training gave similar gains ({{App. B.1.2|ax:A2.SS1.SSS2}}). Contrastive pre-training was left to future work.

The conclusion lists three challenges: apply ViT to detection and segmentation, keep exploring self-supervised pre-training, and scale further ({{§5|ax:S5}}).
