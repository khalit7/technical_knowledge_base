Why it matters (beyond the paper)

This is the paper that ended CNN dominance in vision at scale. Its lesson: hand-designed inductive bias stands in for data, and with enough data and compute a generic architecture that learns the structure catches up, which put vision on NLP's scaling playbook and one architecture under both. Each later claim has its own source.

  • Vision towers of contrastive and multimodal models. CLIP (400 million image-text pairs, ViTs among its image encoders, {{CLIP|https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020}}) and SigLIP (a pairwise sigmoid loss, {{SigLIP|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15343}}) train ViT image towers against text; ALIGN, often listed with them, uses an EfficientNet, not a ViT ({{ALIGN, §4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05918}}). Vision-language models attach such a tower to an LLM: LLaVA uses "the pre-trained CLIP visual encoder ViT-L/14" ({{LLaVA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485}}), Qwen-VL a ViT initialised from OpenCLIP's ViT-bigG ({{Qwen-VL|https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12966}}), InternVL a 6-billion-parameter InternViT ({{InternVL|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14238}}). For Gemini-class closed models the vision encoder is not described in enough detail to say (unconfirmed).
  • Self-supervised vision. The masked patch prediction tried in §4.6 was made to work by BEiT (masked image modelling with visual tokens, {{BEiT|https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08254}}) and MAE (mask 75% of patches and reconstruct pixels; a vanilla ViT-Huge reaches 87.8% using only ImageNet-1K data, {{MAE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377}}). DINO and DINOv2 built self-distillation on ViT backbones; DINOv2 trains a 1B-parameter ViT ({{DINO|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294}}, {{DINOv2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193}}).
  • Diffusion. The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) replaced the U-Net of latent diffusion with a transformer on latent patches, reaching FID 2.27 on class-conditional ImageNet 256 × 256 ({{DiT|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748}}); Stable Diffusion 3 uses a transformer-based rectified-flow architecture with separate weights for text and image tokens ({{SD3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206}}). OpenAI describes Sora as a diffusion transformer on spacetime patches ({{OpenAI's report|https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/}}; not re-checked here, the page blocks scripted fetches); for Flux there is no paper (unconfirmed).
  • Practice. Patchify-then-Transformer became the default for grid-shaped inputs: video ({{ViViT|https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15691}}, spatio-temporal tokens), audio spectrograms ({{AST|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01778}}). The fine-tuning tricks of §3.2, a higher resolution with interpolated position embeddings, are still standard; NaViT goes further and packs patches of images at their native resolution and aspect ratio ({{NaViT|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06304}}). What changed since is in {{Then and now|tab:t-then}}.

How much of this to believe

What holds up.

  • The headline gaps are far outside the noise. Over three fine-tuning runs, ViT-H/14 leads BiT-L by 1.01 points on ImageNet (standard deviations 0.04 and 0.02, about 23 combined sd) and 1.04 on CIFAR-100 (11 sd) ({{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}; every gap in the {{tables tab|tab:t-tables:tb2}}).
  • Same data, same recipe. BiT-L is also pre-trained on JFT-300M; in §4.4 the ResNets get the ViTs' optimiser, schedule and data, and Adam is checked not to handicap them ({{Table 7|ax:A4.T7}}).
  • The compute column reproduces: three times the forward FLOPs times the images seen gives Table 6's exaFLOPs within 2% for every ViT ({{recount|tab:t-tables:tb6}}).
  • Later work confirms the architecture: the same design scaled to 2B parameters reached 90.45% ({{Scaling ViTs|https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04560}}), and the vision towers above are ViTs. The authors also flag that schedule, optimiser and weight decay affect the compute comparison ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}), which counts in their favour.

What to discount.

  • One pre-training run per model. Error bars cover fine-tuning only; Tables 5 and 6, Figure 4 and the scaling study have none, and some differences the text leans on are small (ViT-L/16 over BiT-L on ImageNet: 0.22 points).
  • "ViT-L/16 outperforms BiT-L on all tasks" does not match the table: level on ReaL (90.54 each), 0.01 behind on VTAB, within BiT-L's ± 1.70 ({{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}}).
  • Table 2's core-days are not like for like: different papers' schedules and recipes. Only §4.4 holds the recipe fixed, and there the ResNets train 7 epochs, not BiT's 30 ({{App. D.1|ax:A4.SS1}}); the "2 to 4×" is a five-dataset average, 1.8 to 2.3× on ImageNet alone.
  • "Data trumps inductive bias" leaned on a weak small-data recipe: only weight decay, dropout and label smoothing were tuned ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}). Two months later DeiT trained ViT-B on ImageNet alone to 83.1%, against Table 5's 77.91% ({{DeiT|https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12877}}); the authors' AugReg follow-up made ImageNet-21k match JFT-300M ({{AugReg|https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10270}}); plain ViTs pass 76% on ImageNet-1k in 90 epochs with standard augmentation ({{Beyer et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01580}}).
  • CNNs were not out of the race: ConvNeXt reaches 87.8% ({{arXiv 2201.03545|https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03545}}), and NFNets on JFT-4B "match the reported performance of Vision Transformers with comparable compute budgets", up to 90.4% ({{Smith et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16764}}).
  • The main dataset is private; the ImageNet-21k result (85.30%) is the reproducible one. And small inconsistencies: Table 5's ViT-H/14 JFT row differs from Table 6's and matches Table 2 on four columns, "Figure 4" is cited for Figure 3, Table 1's 307M recounts to 303.3M ({{details|tab:t-tables:tbNo}}). None changes a conclusion.

Verdict

Believe the architecture and the direction: a plain Transformer on patches, pre-trained on enough data, matches or beats the 2020 CNNs, Table 2's gaps are far outside the noise, and every later vision tower is built this way. Treat "large scale training trumps inductive bias" and the 2 to 4× saving as statements about the 2020 recipes: better training later made ViTs work on ImageNet alone, and CNNs trained at the same scale match them. The method is now standard, so {{Then and now|tab:t-then}} replaces "What it takes to use this".
