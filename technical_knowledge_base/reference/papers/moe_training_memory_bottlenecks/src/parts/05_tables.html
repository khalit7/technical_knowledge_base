One execution per cell; at 65K the paper reports the repeat with the fastest PipelinedLLEP of three, with that repeat's own LLEP ({{Table 6|ax:A2.T6}}, {{B.5|ax:A2.SS5}}). On the balanced profile LLEP does no relocation, so it equals standard EP.

Choosing the token budget, and two latency modes

Peak falls monotonically with the chunk count but with decaying return: at the 65K shape the first ten chunks remove 38.4 GiB and the next ten 2.4 GiB. Latency does not follow: at 65K, K of 2, 3 and 5 are slower and larger than K = 10 while 10 to 20 is flat within 6%; at 32K latency rises past K = 3. A single chunk holds 8.3 GiB more than LLEP, since it adds a copy of the route-shaped tensors and partitions nothing ({{Figure 8|ax:A2.F8}}). A fixed K = 10 on a 128-token micro-batch pays ten collective rounds for about one token each (0.45 to 0.48 times LLEP's speed), where the budget derives K = 1 and runs 1.60 to 2.96 times faster than LLEP; the saving tracks the live set, from 9.0% at 128 tokens to 65.9% at 131,072 ({{Table 7|ax:A2.T7}}).

Forward latency at the 65K shape is bimodal: in the fast mode every profile repeats within 0.5%, the slow mode adds 12 to 56%, and which mode a run enters depends on the chunk count and the host-side launch pattern, not the routing (K = 10 was slow in 2 of 56 measurements, K = 16 in 21 of 84). Hence Table 6 reports the fastest of three repeats, and Kmax = 10 keeps the schedule where the slow mode is rare ({{B.5|ax:A2.SS5}}). Under the hood: the inner checkpoint must be reentrant because a non-reentrant one would keep the stacked expert weights alive through Python references for O(KL) cross-layer time; every chunk accumulates into one expert-weight gradient, so the weight-transfer backward fires once per layer; and the collective launches grow linearly with K ({{B.1|ax:A2.SS1}}).
