Sharpening Tax in Post-Training (and PTGS)

Changdae Oh, Qi Zeng, Qi Qi, Andrey Zhmoginov, Deren Lei, Yun He, Hoang Phan, Hangoo Kang, Azalia Mirhoseini, Sharon Li
Meta Superintelligence Labs, University of Wisconsin-Madison, NYU, Stanford University (the first author's work done at Meta) · 1 October 2026 (arXiv v1, the only version as of 19 October 2026)

In one line: On agentic tasks post-training trades pass@K coverage for pass@1: the Sharpening Tax is positive in 36 of 42 base/post-trained cases (base models beat their post-trained versions at pass@128 in 26), because post-training pushes tasks to always-solved or never-solved; per-prompt tempered sampling (PTGS) is a promising but not yet demonstrated mitigation.

How far to trust it: Believe the measurement: the released per-task counts reproduce every Sharpening Tax, coverage and task-category number in the appendix tables, and the 36 of 42 positive taxes hold. Keep the two counts apart: the tax is positive in 36 of 42 cells, while the base model reaches more tasks at pass@128 in 26, clearly (the interval excludes zero) in 18, mostly for the larger models. Treat PTGS as promising, not shown: five runs per arm in two grid-world games on one 7B model, the tax reductions sit inside the run-to-run noise, and a plain fixed temperature of 0.5 paid a smaller tax than PTGS in both games.
