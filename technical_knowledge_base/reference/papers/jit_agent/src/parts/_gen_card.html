JIT-Agent: Scaling Harness Intelligence via Just-in-Time Harness Evolution

Guibin Zhang, Leo Lu, Fangzhou Xie (core contributors); Kang Zhu, Junhao Wang, Zhifei Xie, Zhaochen Yu, Zihang Liu, Zhongxiang Sun, Qiankun Li, Yue Liao, Heng Chang, Xiaobin Hu, Qibing Ren; corresponding Wangchunshu Zhou, Chuanrui Hu, Yafeng Deng, Shuicheng Yan
National University of Singapore, EverMind AI, Nanyang Technological University (v2; v1 was signed "LV-NUS Lab" and listed only Zhou and Yan as corresponding) · 26 August 2026 (arXiv v1); revised 3 September 2026 (v2, the version this page links)

In one line: Makes harness construction a trained capability: factor any harness into (Memory, Planning, Action, Capability orchestration), then train a 27B model (Qwen3.6-27B, SFT plus repair supervision plus evolutionary GDPO against a harness archive) to synthesise, repair, and evolve one per task at inference time; +7.7 avg for GLM-5.2 and +8.8 for DeepSeek-V4-Flash across nine agent benchmarks, enough for a harnessed V4-Flash to beat bare GPT-5.6, at 14.9-54.1% lower cost than fixed harnesses.

How far to trust it: Believe that a harness written for each task can beat five off-the-shelf harnesses on score and cost on three benchmarks, and that the released code and model let anyone check. Treat the size of the gains as provisional: every number is one run with no error bars, most margins that can be tested sit inside sampling noise, nothing turns the three training stages or the archive off, the "vanilla" scaffold behind Table 2 is never described, and the largest gains come on DeepPlanning, where 520 of the 2,852 released training harnesses were written for the test cases' own databases (new requests, no copied test query).
