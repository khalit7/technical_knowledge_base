Results 1: nine benchmarks, two backbones

[[S5.T2|§5.1, §5.2, Table 2]]

Setup ({{§5.1|ax:S5.SS1}}). Nine benchmarks in four groups, all reported on 0 to 100: deep research, BrowseComp-Plus (accuracy), DeepSearchQA (answer F1), xBench-DeepSearch (accuracy); daily work, AgentIF-OneDay (normalised weighted rubric score), PinchBench (average score); planning, DeepPlanning-Shopping (cart match rate) and DeepPlanning-Travel (composite constraint score); workspace, OfficeBench and OdysseyBench (task success rate). JIT-Agent writes harnesses for two main executors, GLM-5.2 ("a strong open long-horizon model") and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Preview ("inference efficiency"). Baselines come in two groups: eight models as they come (Qwen3.7-Plus, GLM-5.2, DeepSeek-V4-Flash and V4-Pro, both previews, Kimi K2.7 Code, GPT-5.6, which v2's setup calls GPT-5.6-Sol, Gemini 3.1 Pro and Gemini 3.5 Flash), and five reusable harnesses on a fixed backbone (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Hermes, NanoBot). The first group measures end-to-end standing; the second isolates the harness.

What it reports ({{§5.2|ax:S5.SS2}}). Replacing each backbone's "default scaffold" with a JIT-written harness improves all 18 backbone and benchmark pairs. The nine-benchmark average rises 74.1 → 81.8 for GLM-5.2 (+7.7) and 66.7 → 75.5 for DeepSeek-V4-Flash (+8.8). The largest gains are on planning: +24.8 on Shopping for V4-Flash (59.1 → 83.9) and +20.2 on Travel for GLM-5.2 (62.8 → 83.0); V4-Flash also gains 11.9 on xBench-DS and 8.9 on DeepSearchQA. v1 read the planning gains as appearing "on tasks that require sustained state management and constraint tracking" ({{v1 §6.2|ax1:S6.SS2}}), which fits the pattern that harness lift concentrates where state management is the bottleneck. JIT-equipped systems lead eight of nine columns, GLM-5.2 with JIT seven of them; the exception is Travel, where GPT-5.6's 84.9 is 1.9 ahead. V4-Flash with JIT beats the vanilla V4-Pro on every benchmark, by 8.7 on average. In the abstract's words, V4-Flash with JIT "surpasses GPT-5.6 on DeepSearchQA (+9.1) and OdysseyBench (+4.3)"; v1's introduction added PinchBench (+8.7), GLM's xBench-DS (+12.0) and AgentIF (+6.9).

Averaged over all nine benchmarks, how does DeepSeek-V4-Flash with a JIT-written harness compare with bare GPT-5.6?

The answer the abstract leaves out: V4-Flash with JIT averages 75.5 against GPT-5.6's 76.5. It beats GPT-5.6 on five benchmarks (DeepSearchQA, xBench-DS, PinchBench, Shopping, OdysseyBench) and loses on four (BrowseComp-Plus, AgentIF, Travel by 23.6 points, OfficeBench). "Enough for a harnessed V4-Flash to beat bare GPT-5.6", as the first summary put it, holds benchmark by benchmark on the two the abstract chose, not overall.

{{Table 2|ax:S5.T2}} as a dumbbell per benchmark: open dot the vanilla backbone, filled dot the same backbone with a JIT-written harness, the tick GPT-5.6. Averages are recomputed here and match the paper (GLM's +7.7 is the difference of the rounded averages; unrounded it is 7.79).

Two things in the table are worth knowing before reading the gains. First, the paper never describes the "default scaffold" the vanilla rows ran in, so the size of each gain depends on an unstated baseline. Second, vanilla V4-Pro scores below vanilla V4-Flash on three benchmarks, including PinchBench by 20.6 points (61.1 against 81.7); "beats V4-Pro on every benchmark" partly measures that.

Results 2: against five fixed harnesses, with cost

[[S5.T3|§5.3, §5.4, Table 3, Figure 3]]

This is the controlled comparison: same backbone, only the harness changes, on DeepSearchQA, xBench-DS and AgentIF, with DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Qwen3.6-Flash ({{Table 3|ax:S5.T3}}). Per case it reports score, tokens and API cost.

In how many of the six backbone and benchmark settings does the JIT-written harness have the best score of the six harnesses?

JIT-Agent's harness has the best score in four of six: +4.7 over NanoBot on DeepSearchQA and +4.0 on xBench-DS with V4-Flash, +7.0 on xBench-DS and +2.9 on AgentIF with Qwen3.6-Flash. It trails Claude Code by 3.1 on V4-Flash AgentIF and NanoBot by 3.9 on Qwen DeepSearchQA ({{§5.3|ax:S5.SS3}}). It has the fewest tokens and the lowest cost in all six, cutting cost per case by 14.9% to 54.1% against the cheapest fixed harness, 36.0% on average; on V4-Flash xBench-DS tokens fall from 527K to 212K and cost from $0.075 to $0.039 while the score rises from 78.0 to 82.0. All of these recompute exactly. The paper reads this as "the gains therefore arise from task-conditioned scaffolds rather than longer trajectories."

The Pareto view ({{Figure 3|ax:S5.F3}}, {{§5.4|ax:S5.SS4}}) puts all twelve pairs on cost against score. On DeepSearchQA the V4-Flash JIT point alone dominates every other pair; on AgentIF three pairs are on the frontier (Qwen with JIT, V4-Flash with JIT, V4-Flash with Claude Code). Two limits: the "lower cost than fixed harnesses" claim exists for these three benchmarks only, not the nine of Table 2 (the first summary attached it to all nine); and the cost does not say whether it includes JIT-Agent's own work, writing three candidate harnesses (each may run to 64,000 tokens, from a prompt whose reference block alone is about 13,000 tokens) and scoring all three, which a fixed harness never pays (see {{How much to believe|tab:t-read:s-trust}}).

Results 3: six other backbones, against ReAct

[[S5.F4|§5.5, Figure 4]]

To test transfer, six executors from three families (DeepSeek V4 Flash and Pro, Qwen3.6 Flash and Plus, Mimo-V2.5 Flash and Pro) run under ReAct and under a JIT-written harness on four benchmarks, with 100 DeepSearchQA questions and 50-task subsets of the other three ({{Figure 4|ax:S5.F4}}). JIT wins all 24 pairs, by 7.6 points on average; by family 10.2 (DeepSeek), 4.0 (Qwen) and 8.6 (Mimo); DeepSearchQA gains most, 15.2 on average, up to +22.2 for Mimo-V2.5-Pro ({{§5.5|ax:S5.SS5}}). Figure 4 is not in the HTML version; this page decoded its printed labels from the vector PDF in the arXiv source, and the bar heights agree with the labels to within 0.31 points. Every number in §5.5 recomputes from them.

The gains are smaller off DeepSearchQA: 4.5 points on average on AgentIF and 3.4 on OfficeBench. And this figure's V4-Flash row repeats {{Table 2|ax:S5.T2}} exactly on all four JIT scores (85.1, 63.8, 83.9, 63.4) and on three of the four baselines, although the figure says it used subsets; the one difference is DeepSearchQA's baseline, 66.1 under ReAct here against 76.2 for the vanilla row there. So either Table 2's vanilla scaffold is not ReAct, or the two use different question sets; the paper does not say. Two of V4-Flash's OfficeBench scores, 61.0 and 63.4, cannot be a single pass over 50 tasks (that gives whole even numbers); they are reachable on the full 295.

Results 4: does the archive help? Static against streaming

[[A2.SS1|Appendix B.1, Figure 6]]

Evolvability is the third property, and the evidence for it is one appendix figure ({{Figure 6|ax:A2.F6}}): cumulative accuracy, cost per task and tool calls per task over a stream of tasks, with harnesses written independently (static) or retrieved from and added to the bank (streaming). v1 had this in the main text as §6.6; v2 moved it to the appendix. Decoded from the vector figure here:

Streaming finishes ahead on all three: Shopping 84.4 against 79.0, Travel 61.9 against 58.1, OfficeBench 64.9 against 60.6, with average cost and tool calls per task nearly identical. That is the paper's whole case for test-time evolution. It is one stream per benchmark in one task order, with no repeat and no error bar; the figure does not say which executor ran it; the bank's size and what it retained are not reported; and neither end point is the number in Table 2 for either backbone, so it is not clear whether Table 2 used static or streaming mode. The paper's own wording is careful: the pattern is "consistent with" Evo-GDPO's objective.

Results 5: what the generated harnesses look like

[[S5.F5|§5.6, Figure 5, Appendix C]]

Ten generated harnesses are drawn as flow diagrams ({{Figure 5|ax:S5.F5}}, {{Appendix C|ax:A3}}). Each makes a different module carry the task's difficulty, which is the qualitative argument for writing harnesses per task: "the shared protocol constrains interfaces, not behavior" ({{v1 §6.7|ax1:S6.SS7}}).

From the captions and text of {{Figure 5|ax:S5.F5}} and {{Figures C.1 to C.9|ax:A3}}; the coloured letter is the module that carries the idea.

These are chosen examples. The 2,852 harnesses the authors released from the training data tell a plainer story: by a keyword rule on each module's docstring, about three in four (2,209) have an F module that simply exposes every tool, while almost all write a planner (only 40 have none) and most manage memory beyond plain history.
