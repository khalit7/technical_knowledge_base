How much of this to believe

[[S5|§5, Appendix B, the release]]

What holds up.

  • The arithmetic. Every derived number the paper prints that this page could recompute, 47 of 47, reproduces from its own tables and decoded figures (averages, gains, margins, the 14.9 to 54.1% cost range, the Figure 4 family averages); v1 and v2 print identical tables. The full list is on the {{tables tab|tab:t-tables:chkH}}.
  • The direction. 18 of 18 pairs in Table 2 and 24 of 24 in Figure 4 favour the JIT-written harness. Each pair is noisy, but 42 out of 42 is not what a coin gives; the pairs are not independent (one generator, shared benchmarks), so read it as "consistently positive", not as a p-value.
  • The controlled comparison. Table 3 fixes the backbone and changes only the harness, reports tokens and money beside score, and the JIT harness is cheapest in all six settings while losing on score in two, which the paper states plainly ({{§5.3|ax:S5.SS3}}).
  • The release. The runtime is MIT-licensed, the 27B weights are Apache 2.0, and 2,852 training harnesses are public. Matching 1,652 test query texts from six of the benchmarks the repository ships against those training instructions finds no copied test query and no near-copy (the closest training instruction shares at most 6% of a test query's word sequences), though see point 3 below ({{the checks|tab:t-tables:ovH}}).

What is weaker than it looks.

  1. One run, no error bars, margins inside the noise. No table reports seeds, repeats or intervals. Where the metric is a success rate on a known number of tasks, sampling noise can be computed (unpaired binomial, an upper bound for runs on the same tasks): JIT against NanoBot on V4-Flash xBench-DS, +4.0 on 100 tasks, has a standard error of 5.6 (z = 0.7); V4-Flash with JIT against GPT-5.6 on OdysseyBench, +4.3 on 300 tasks, z = 1.2; the OfficeBench gains in Table 2, z = 0.6 and 1.4; every Figure 4 OfficeBench gain on 50 tasks, z under 0.7. Only the two xBench-DS gains in Table 2 (+11.9, +12.0) reach z = 2. {{The noise table|tab:t-tables:noiseH}}.
  2. Baselines that are not like for like. (a) The "default scaffold" of every vanilla row in Table 2 is never described, and on DeepSearchQA it scores 76.2 for V4-Flash where ReAct scores 66.1 in Figure 4. (b) JIT gets best of three harness drafts per task, picked by its own log-probability; each fixed harness gets one configuration. (c) Claude Code and Codex are coding runtimes built around their own vendors' models; here they run DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Qwen3.6-Flash on web research, which is not what they were tuned for. (d) The paper does not say whether "cost per case" includes JIT-Agent's own generation (three drafts of up to 64,000 tokens from a 27B model, plus scoring them). In the released runtime those tokens are logged under generate/, while the numbers "you report" come from execute/ ({{jit/README.md|https://github.com/bingreeky/JIT/blob/ababa06c2f54d799fd9fbc356e5368f61a452260/jit/README.md}}). If excluded, the 14.9 to 54.1% saving is a saving on executor tokens only.
  3. Training on the test cases' own environments. Of the 2,852 released training harnesses, 248 were written for tasks in the DeepPlanning-Shopping environment and 272 in DeepPlanning-Travel, with the benchmark's own system prompt and tools, and 189 for AgentIF-style workspaces. Their workspaces are named after the test cases (deepplanning_shopping/level1_case10, deepplanning_travel/travel_zh_1), and the tasks are new requests built on those cases' data: the training request in level1_case10 asks for four products that exist together in test case 10's product database and in none of the other 49 level-1 cases, and travel_zh_1 plans the same Harbin to Dalian trip on 12 November 2025 as test case 1. No test query is copied, but the generator was trained on harnesses for the very databases it is then scored on, and DeepPlanning is where the two largest gains appear (+24.8, +20.2). v1 listed DeepPlanning among the sources of Stage I training tasks ({{v1 §4.1|ax1:S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1}}); v2's list names WebWalker, TaskCraft, ClawGym and EnvScaler and drops it ({{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1.SSS0.Px1}}). The fixed harnesses and vanilla backbones had no such tuning ({{the checks|tab:t-tables:ovH}}).
  4. No ablations. Nothing turns a stage off: no Stage I alone against I + II against I + II + III, no "teacher writes the harness" baseline, no "pick the best of the 13 seeds for each task" baseline (which would separate writing a harness from choosing one), no streaming without Evo-GDPO. So the paper shows that the trained system helps, not which part of the recipe does. The first summary's "the paper's own ablation" does not exist.
  5. Scores that cannot be one pass over the released sets. A success rate on N tasks must be k/N. With the repository's set sizes, 5 of 10 OfficeBench cells in Table 2 (295 tasks; for example GLM-5.2's 63.0 and GLM with JIT's 68.4), V4-Flash's xBench-DS 70.1 (100 tasks) and Kimi's OdysseyBench 69.9 (300 tasks) are not reachable, nor are 4 of 12 OfficeBench values in Figure 4 (50 tasks). They may be averages of several runs or other subsets; the paper says neither ({{granularity checks|tab:t-tables:granH}}).
  6. Evolution rests on one figure. Test-time evolution, the "E" in JIT and the third property, is supported only by {{Figure 6|ax:A2.F6}}: one stream per benchmark, one task order, executor unstated, no repeats, bank contents unreported, and v2 moved it to the appendix. With 120 tasks the sampling error of a cumulative score near 80% is about 3.7 points for each curve, so end gaps of 3.8 to 5.4 points are about one standard error of their difference.
  7. The released model is not the evaluated model. The checkpoint "builds on the Stage-I harness-customization model and is further trained through distillation from the final research checkpoint" ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/JIT-Agent/jit-27b}}); there is no code for Stages I to III, no streaming bank, no ReAct baseline, and no adapters for BrowseComp-Plus or PinchBench. The headline numbers cannot be reproduced from the release as it stands.

In the authors' favour: v1 said openly that the four-module space is "substantially" poorer than production runtimes and that the gap was deliberate (the box is gone from v2); §5.3 reports the two settings it loses; §5.4 calls the frontier "task-dependent"; and releasing runtime, weights and training harnesses is more than most harness papers do.

Verdict. Believe that harnesses written per task by a trained model can be cheaper than off-the-shelf harnesses at similar or better scores on these benchmarks; that part rests on a controlled table with costs. Treat the size of every gain as provisional: single runs, an undescribed baseline scaffold, training data drawn from the environments of the benchmarks that gain most, no ablation of any stage, and test-time evolution shown once. It is a strong proof of concept with a release good enough for someone else to measure it properly.

What it takes to use this

[[S5.SS1|§5.1, and the release of 27 August 2026]]
  • Code and licence. {{bingreeky/JIT|https://github.com/bingreeky/JIT}} (MIT; benchmark data keep their own licences). It runs the static pipeline: generate N = 3 harnesses at temperature 1.0, select by the model's own log-probability (or by a judge model when the meta-model is a hosted API), execute, score. Seven benchmarks have adapters and data; the DeepPlanning-Travel database is a separate 748 MB download.
  • The model. {{JIT-Agent/jit-27b|https://huggingface.co/JIT-Agent/jit-27b}}, 27.36B parameters in BF16 (about 55 GB of weights), served with vLLM over four GPUs (the launcher's default TP=4) at a 163,840-token context, because a prompt with the reference catalogue plus a five-file harness does not fit in 32K. Use it with natural-language descriptions of the 11 seeds as references (about 13,000 tokens), the mode it was trained for.
  • Three model roles. The meta-model writes the harness, an execution model (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint) runs it, and a judge model grades xBench-DS, DeepSearchQA, AgentIF and Travel. Search tools need Serper and Jina keys.
  • What you cannot do yet. Retrain on your own tasks (no training code), run streaming mode with an evolving bank, or reproduce Table 2 (different checkpoint, missing benchmarks and baseline).
  • When it pays. On the paper's numbers, most on tasks whose difficulty is state and constraints (DeepPlanning, multi-hop search: +9 to +25) and least on workspace and instruction-following tasks (OfficeBench, OdysseyBench, AgentIF: +2 to +7, mostly within noise). The cost saving needs the generation overhead counted against it; for short tasks, three 27B drafts per task may cost more than the harness saves.

Why it matters (beyond the paper)

This knowledge base's reading when the paper came out, kept here: it is the first attempt to make harness intelligence a trainable axis rather than an engineering one, and the economics are the interesting part, because the lift comes with a cost reduction: a task-conditioned harness does not pay for machinery it does not need. If it holds, the harness stops being a fixed asset tuned per product and becomes a per-task artifact you generate, which changes what you cache, what you version and what you evaluate. The caveats were also there from the start: the generator writes in a deliberately impoverished language, so the comparison with OpenCode and Claude Code is closer to parity than to dominance, and the paper does not analyse failures of the repair loop or of evolution beyond the two-round cap.

A week later {{HarnessDev|n:3d45c17b0d0d81cd9883f783840e013f}} tested the untrained version of the same skill: frontier models building and then evolving their own harnesses, with a held-out check after every version. Their evolution steps moved the held-out score in the same direction as the feedback score in only 34 of 64 version changes. That is the failure JIT-Agent claims training fixes, and it is exactly the measurement JIT-Agent does not make: it never scores a harness version against held-out tasks, only the end of one stream.
