Speed: where the time of a pass goes

[[S6|§6, Figures 11 and 12, Eq. 12]]

Training raised the tokens per pass; Section 6 lowers the cost of a pass. Serving one request is memory-bound ({{PagedAttention|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bf8b90ca93fee19f5f}}; Pope et al.), so diffusion's extra FLOPs are cheaper than AR's extra passes. Reference implementations are in Hugging Face Transformers and vLLM ({{§6|ax:S6}}). {{Figure 11|ax:S6.F11}} times one step on an H100 in FP8, one request, 4,096 prompt tokens: a DiffusionGemma step processes 256 times more tokens but takes only 3.2x as long, 12.63 ms against 4.01 ms of GPU time, plus about 1 ms of CPU work each end to end.

{{Figure 11|ax:S6.F11}}'s printed labels ("Other" = total minus the rest). Speed = TPF / (GPU time + 0.93 ms overhead); AR = 1 / 4.90 ms. Defaults reproduce Section 6's 1,456 tokens/s, 7.1x and 4.8x, by construction. Checkboxes are what-ifs; the paper says only that a dense model would cut the feed-forward slowdown to under 2x.

Three operations account for the difference ({{§6 "GPU time breakdown"|ax:S6}}):

  • MoE experts, 4.3x (1.08 to 4.66 ms): with one request the MoE layer's time is moving expert weights from HBM. One token activates 8 experts per layer, a 256-token canvas about 84 (PG-19), so loading them no longer amortises: verification's penalty in speculative decoding, at larger parallelism.
  • Sampling, 5.5x (0.56 to 3.06 ms): a full-precision softmax over the 262k vocabulary at all 256 positions plus the self-conditioning matmul, in torch.compile rather than custom kernels, for extensibility.
  • Attention, 4.1x (0.45 to 1.84 ms): bidirectional attention over the canvas cannot use single-token decode kernels, so it runs FlashAttention-4 (the text says 4x and "at most 2x" for the rest; the figure prints 2.1x for "Other").
each token picks 8 of 128 experts. If the 256 tokens of a canvas chose their experts independently and uniformly, how many distinct experts would one MoE layer have to load?

Derived: 128 × (1 − (120/128)n); 84 measured ({{§6|ax:S6}}).

Two kinds of pass (denoise, cache update) with different masks can share a batch and stopping is decided on the GPU; the vLLM integration avoids every CPU-GPU synchronisation with asynchronous scheduling and a per-sequence causal-attention flag ({{§6|ax:S6}}; {{vLLM blog|https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-06-10-diffusion-gemma}}).

More than one user

{{Figure 12|ax:S6.F12}} adds concurrent requests (PG-19, DiffusionGemma at 16 TPF, Gemma 4 with an MTP draft of 4). DiffusionGemma leads in per-user and total throughput at low batch sizes; AR begins to win "around 32 concurrent requests", with nothing tuned for batches above 1 (top-k truncation in sampling is expected to help).

{{Figure 12(b)|ax:S6.F12}}'s printed ratios. Dashed: the total-throughput ratio's recent fall (about 0.75 per doubling, derived) carried one doubling on; the figure stops at c = 16.

Towards real traffic (outside the paper's scope): DiffusionGemma does TPF times fewer KV-cache transfers and more MoE FLOPs, trading data movement for compute, which suits GPUs whose compute outgrows their bandwidth and long-context agentic serving.
