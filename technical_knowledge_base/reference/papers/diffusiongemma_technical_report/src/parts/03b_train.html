Training: SFT, then SD·RL

[[S4|§4, §5, Eq. 11, Figures 6 to 10]]

Stage 1: supervised finetuning

An extended finetuning phase teaches the checkpoint to predict 256-token blocks from noisy inputs ({{§4|ax:S4}}). A block-diagonal attention mask gives bidirectional attention inside each block and none between blocks; clean context comes through the encoder's KV cache. Per canvas a noise level t ~ U[0, 1] is drawn and each token is replaced with probability t by a uniformly drawn token; the loss is the cross-entropy between the prediction and the clean canvas ({{Eq. 11|ax:S4.E11}}):

L(θ) = − Σi log pθ(x0i | xt, zt, H)

Quality rises fast, then log-linearly with training ({{Figures 6|ax:S4.F6}} and 7, at up to N = 192 steps). Non-thinking quality needs only moderate SFT; coherent thinking needs extended SFT (lower start, steeper log-linear slope), since the model first collapses into stuttering or cycles in long traces.

Stage 2: sampler distillation and reinforcement learning (SD·RL)

After SFT the model is good with many steps, somewhat behind the AR model on hard reasoning and coding, and it collapses in the few-step regime low latency needs ({{§5|ax:S5}}). Instead of separate alignment and distillation stages, SD·RL does both in one online stage: a joint objective, so one gradient update drives reward maximisation and sampler distillation (compressing the best output into few steps). The data adapts the Gemma 4 RL mix: helpfulness, mathematical reasoning, coding and instruction following, thinking and not. The model is its own online teacher, generating reference trajectories with many steps and mild annealing. The report gives no equation for the objective.

The self-paced curriculum. The objective drives entropy down; lower entropy makes adaptive stopping fire earlier; the teacher's trajectories, and so the training distribution, get shorter ({{Figure 8|ax:S5.F8}}: reward up, teacher steps down). Unlike RL for AR models, continuing SD·RL after the reward plateaus still pays: less entropy is more speed.

What it bought ({{Figure 9|ax:S5.F9}}, GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench-v6 averaged, thinking, 3 seeds): about 10 points more quality and TPF from 5 to nearly 20, SFT at its best (N up to 192) against SD·RL at N = 48. At N = 48 the SFT model falls into repetition loops whose collapsed entropy trips adaptive stopping early: in {{Figures 16 and 17|ax:A2}} it reasons correctly through a GPQA biology question and a harmonic-oscillator question, then degenerates into streams of "1" and ")" and answers nothing; after SD·RL both are right. SFT keeps improving with N up to 192 (roughly any-order AR); after SD·RL quality plateaus around 48 ({{Figure 10|ax:S5.F10}}).

RL on AR reasoning models usually makes answers longer (DeepSeek-R1's grew as it trained). What did SD·RL do to the length of DiffusionGemma's answers?

Nearly 2x shorter than the SFT checkpoint ({{§5|ax:S5}}): brevity multiplies speed, so the objective rewards it, and the model forgoes the gains of long reasoning. On Table 3's 7 fully covered benchmarks DiffusionGemma writes 4,001 tokens to Gemma 4's 7,207 and needs about 203 forward passes to Gemma 4 with MTP's 5,148: 3.9%, the paper's "less than 5%". On the {{DeepSeek-R1 page|n:3c65c17b0d0d813faca4f7a51eaa0c65}} answers grow with RL instead.
