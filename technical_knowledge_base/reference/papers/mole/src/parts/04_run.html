Run the daily audit

This is the real thing at the scale the paper ran it: the released GPT-5.3 single-day corpus ({{forgelab/mole|https://huggingface.co/datasets/forgelab/mole}}), its 15 test days, 142 accounts (the eight collusive accounts are left out, as in the paper), 2,106 account-days and the 45 completed harms in them. The 617,740 audit events were rolled up into the paper's 20 features per account-day ({{Appendix F.1|ax:A6.SS1}}) by compute_features.py; your browser fits nothing new: it scores every account-day with the paper's two count-based monitors, using the medians and spreads fitted on training days 1 to 12, ranks each day, and counts what a review of the top K would catch.

Try: play the 15 days with the robust z-score, then switch to peer-fit; move the budget K; switch features off (the egress-only preset, or drop after-hours activity) and watch the count move. The paper's semantic monitors are not rerunnable here (their saved scores are not released), so they appear as printed reference marks.

Features the monitor uses (20 of 20)

Each feature is a count divided by the day's service calls (after-hours share and external-email share are rates). A switched-off feature leaves the mean; the fitted medians and spreads do not change. Logon and logoff are always zero in this corpus: the simulator records no session events.

The whole budget curve

R@K/day for K = 1 to 20, recomputed live: your current monitor and feature set (solid), the other classical monitor with all 20 features (dashed), and the paper's printed R@10/day for six monitors at K = 10 ({{Figure 4|ax:S3.F4}}). The grey line is a random queue. Budget-AUC is the area under the first ten points.
