MOLE: Detecting Insider Threats in AI Agents

Aashiq Muhamed, Virginia Smith
Carnegie Mellon University (both authors' stated affiliation) · September 2026 (arXiv v1, 7 September 2026; the only version so far)

In one line: Simulates an organisation rather than a task set, 150 AI-operated accounts sharing 9 stateful services over 30 workdays with 12 injected threats and around 20B tokens of monitoring corpora from four models, and scores the monitor as well as the agent: 72% of 39 agent models complete most assigned harmful objectives, a model's stated refusal does not predict whether it actually declines, the best monitors miss nearly half of completed harm in a single-day audit, and benchmark-guided search lifts a mid-tier monitor by 49 to 64%, making it an optimisation target rather than only a report card. (The database Takeaway, kept as written. Two corrections from this page: refusal and completion do correlate across models (the paper's claim is narrower, see RQ1), and the 72% was measured under an instruction not to refuse.)

How far to trust it: Believe the benchmark and the monitor-side findings: the released data reproduces the classical baselines to the third decimal, and reading what the agent did beats counting it. Treat the agent-side numbers as compliance under a sanctioned role-play, not as evidence that agents turn insider: every harmful session tells the model it is helping an Anthropic alignment researcher in a harmless sandbox and must not refuse. Treat the monitor rankings as coarse, since 45 completed harms decide them and the top two differ by one. The evidence, judged.
