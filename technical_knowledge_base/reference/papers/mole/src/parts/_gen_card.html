MOLE: Detecting Insider Threats in AI Agents

Aashiq Muhamed, Virginia Smith
Carnegie Mellon University (both authors' stated affiliation) · September 2026 (arXiv v1, 7 September 2026; the only version so far)

In one line: Simulates an organisation rather than a task set (150 AI-operated accounts sharing 9 stateful services over 30 workdays, 12 injected threats, about 20B tokens of corpora from four generator models) and scores the monitor as well as the agent, under a daily review budget of 10 accounts. Under a preamble that declares the sandbox harmless and asks the model not to refuse, 28 of 39 models (72%) complete more than half of their assigned harmful objectives; refusal and completion are strongly linked across models (Spearman -0.73), but the 24 models that never refuse still complete anywhere from 20% to 100%, mostly by not attempting. The best monitor catches 24 of the 45 completed harms in the GPT-5.3 single-day test period, so it misses nearly half, against 10 for the FACADE anomaly detector; reading the agent's reasoning helps where the generator writes it down; and an automated strategy search raises GPT-4.1's budget-AUC by 49 to 64% at its own cost on three test corpora, making the benchmark an optimisation target as well as a report card. Single runs on few positives: the top two monitors differ by one account-day. (Softened on 2026-10-25 from the database Takeaway, which said a model's stated refusal does not predict whether it declines and gave the 72% without its conditions: refusal and completion do correlate across models (the paper's claim is narrower, see RQ1), and the 72% was measured under an instruction not to refuse.)

How far to trust it: Believe the benchmark and the monitor-side findings: the released data reproduces the classical baselines to the third decimal, and reading what the agent did beats counting it. Treat the agent-side numbers as compliance under a sanctioned role-play, not as evidence that agents turn insider: every harmful session tells the model it is helping an Anthropic alignment researcher in a harmless sandbox and must not refuse. Treat the monitor rankings as coarse, since 45 completed harms decide them and the top two differ by one. The evidence, judged.
