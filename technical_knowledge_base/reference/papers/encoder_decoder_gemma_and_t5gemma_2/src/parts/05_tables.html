Recount from the configs (inputs/configs.json): per layer, attention d(2h + 2hkv)dhead, feed-forward 3d dff, norms; cross-attention like attention with keys and values reading the encoder's width. Defaults reproduce the released T5Gemma totals to within 0.01% and T5Gemma 2's to within 0.1% (the vision encoder is taken as the paper's 417M, and the paper's 675M embedding for 4B-4B is 671.3M by the config), independently.

Does not reproduce, or could not be checked

Not reproduced
  • Table 1's encoder-decoder sizes (4.0B, 16.7B, 10.4B) leave out cross-attention; with it, 4.42B, 18.50B and 10.78B, which is what the released checkpoints contain.
  • "One cross-attention sub-layer every six decoder layers" (T5Gemma 2 §2): Table 1's 4,233M matches cross-attention on every second layer of Gemma 2 2B (13 of 26) exactly; every sixth would give 4,106M.
  • Figure 3's FLOPs: the paper does not give its formula. Standard estimates (2 FLOPs per parameter per token, with or without embeddings and attention terms) do not land on all thirteen decoded values at once; the closest misses by several percent. The ratios above are the paper's own values.
  • Figure 6's correlations: the decoded points give Spearman ρ (PT against IT, points) and (against SuperGLUE, points) against the printed 0.97 and 0.89. The figure does not mark which size each point is, so the within-size averages (0.42 and 0.05) cannot be checked.
  • "Adaptation is better above roughly 100M parameters" (the old summary): Table 4 has scratch winning two of three scores at 113M and SuperGLUE at 409M.
  • "The long-context advantage grows with context length" (the old summary): at 4B the RULER lead is 14.9 at 32K and 5.9 at 128K; after post-training it is −7.4 at 128K.
