Sample from a toy DDPM

Four small diffusion models, one per row of {{Table 2|ax:S4.T2}} that differs only in what the network predicts and what it is trained on, trained for this page and running here in plain JavaScript: every one of the 1,000 reverse steps of {{Algorithm 2|ax:S3.SS2.fig1}} is a real network evaluation in your browser.

  • Data: a 2-D Swiss roll, the toy of the {{original diffusion paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03585}}, each coordinate stored as an integer 0 to 255 and scaled to [−1, 1] exactly as §3.3 does for pixels, so the bound is a lossless codelength in bits per dimension like the paper's. 10,000 training points, 2,000 held-out test points from a different seed.
  • As the paper: T = 1,000, β linear from 10−4 to 0.02 ({{§4|ax:S4}}); t by the Transformer sinusoidal embedding added into every hidden layer; Adam, learning rate 2 × 10−4, batch 128, EMA 0.9999 ({{Appendix B|ax:A2}}); the discrete decoder of {{Eq. 13|ax:S3.E13}} for L0; the last step displayed noiselessly.
  • Toy-scale departures: an MLP (three hidden layers of 64, SiLU; parameters) instead of the 35.7M U-Net; 300,000 steps instead of 800,000; no dropout or flips. Same network, data, seed and steps for all four; CPU time .

Dashed square: the data range [−1, 1]²; the view zooms out with the noise. "On the roll": within 0.04 of the noiseless curve (real data: ). Sampling runs in short slices so the page stays responsive; the slider shows any stored step (every 10).

What each variant gets, measured

Bound on 2,000 held-out points with every t (bits per dimension, lower is better), and samples measured on 2,000 draws in PyTorch: precision is the share of samples within 0.04 of some test point, recall the share of test points within 0.04 of some sample (a simple form of {{Kynkäänniemi et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06991}}).

What reproduces and what does not

The learned score, drawn

The ε-network is a score model: −εθ(xt, t) / √(1 − ᾱt) estimates ∇ log q(xt) ({{§3.2|ax:S3.SS2}}, and the one-line derivation in The paper tab). Arrows show its direction on a grid; points are noised data q(xt | x0) at the same t.

Try: at large t every arrow points to the centre (the data's mean); lower t and the field bends into the spiral. Switch to a μ̃ model and the field is read from its mean by inverting Eq. 11.

Arrow length is proportional to the score's size times √(1 − ᾱt) (that is, to |εθ|), so lengths are comparable across t.
