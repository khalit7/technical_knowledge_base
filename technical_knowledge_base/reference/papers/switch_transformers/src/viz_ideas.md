# Visualisation ideas: Switch Transformers

The question the paper keeps returning to: **what does one expert per token cost and buy, and what keeps a sparse layer balanced and stable enough to scale?** Every visual below either runs the paper's router, loss or arithmetic on real numbers, or rebuilds one of its tables.

Scoring follows `html_utils/interactive-html-ideas.md` section 2 (0 to 2 each; reproduces and computable count double; build cost subtracted; plus one for a step-by-step animation against the method it replaced), with the paper-page criteria R (runs the paper's own mechanism) and P (supports a predict-then-reveal question).

Constraint from the brief: Topic: llms already has a "Deeper: inside an MoE" tab (`models_and_training/topic_llms/src/parts/35_*`) with a router card, a capacity and dropping animation (32 tokens, top-2, three capacity factors, bias balancing), global-batch balancing, expert parallelism and config rebuilds. This page links to it and builds only what is specific to the paper.

## Rejected

| P-switch_transformers.13 | Generic routing or capacity animation with many tokens | Exists on Topic: llms, Deeper: inside an MoE (E1); linked instead. |
| P-switch_transformers.14 | Toy trained offline in PyTorch and shipped as quantised weights | A single Switch layer trains in seconds in the browser, so shipping weights would hide the training the page is about; checking the gradients against PyTorch is the stronger check. |
| P-switch_transformers.15 | bfloat16 inside the toy's router | One shallow layer with small logits: rounding changes nothing measurable, and a null result would mislead about §2.4; the calculator shows the mechanism exactly. |
| P-switch_transformers.16 | Redrawing Figures 4 to 8 and 12, 13 (scaling curves, speedup histogram, per-language gains) | Images only, no printed values; the method forbids reading curves. Their numbers are quoted as the authors' readings. |
| P-switch_transformers.17 | A multi-layer toy LM to test the init-scale and instability results | Instability shows up at scale (Switch-XXL), not in a CPU toy; would cost training time on a shared machine and still not test the claim. |
| P-switch_transformers.18 | Training-cost or TPU-hours tab | A pattern Khalid removed; the paper gives no cost. |
| P-switch_transformers.19 | No-Token-Left-Behind as a toy variant | The paper found no benefit and gives no numbers to compare against; a one-line mention suffices. |

## What the methodology lacked here

A rule for **live training in the page**: when the mechanism is one layer and trains in seconds, train it in the browser rather than shipping weights, and make it deterministic (float64, seeded) so the offline sweep and the page give identical numbers, with the gradient (not just the forward pass) checked against PyTorch.
