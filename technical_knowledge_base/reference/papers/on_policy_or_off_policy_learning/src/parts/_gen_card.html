On-Policy or Off-Policy Learning? A Systematic Study of Distillation Dynamics (rollout policy against KL direction)

Julianna Piskorz*, Antonin Berthon*, Mihaela van der Schaar (* equal contribution)
University of Cambridge · 28 September 2026 (arXiv v1, the only version on 19 October 2026)

In one line: In controlled strong-to-weak distillation, the token-level KL direction, not who wrote the rollouts, shapes performance and coverage: forward KL is robust to rollout policy, reverse KL favours student rollouts, and the on-policy generalisation edge does not reliably survive later RLVR.

How far to trust it: Believe the main finding for the setting it tested: in matched three-seed comparisons (1B students, answers of about 100 tokens), KL direction and learning rate explain more than who wrote the rollouts, and the gradient argument for why forward KL shrugs off the rollout policy is correct. Treat the side results (the on-policy generalisation edge, the RLVR follow-up, the Spanish style test, the long-answer MATH run) as suggestive: one benchmark, one seed, or a best checkpoint picked on the test set, and the text sometimes describes its own figures more favourably than they read.
