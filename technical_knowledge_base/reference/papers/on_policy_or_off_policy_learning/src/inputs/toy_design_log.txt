Toy design log (written before the first sweep, 2026-10-19)

Task. 12 prompts. Each answer is two tokens: a route (5 choices) then an answer (5 choices). For every
(prompt, route) one answer is correct. The teacher (logits / 0.5) prefers two routes per prompt and knows the
correct answer on every route, more sharply on its own two. The student (logits / 1.0) starts with a habit:
one route the teacher seldom takes, where it knows the answer a little (+0.8 on the correct logit).
Parameters: the student's logits directly (12 x 5 route logits, 12 x 5 x 5 answer logits; 360 numbers), so the
logit Jacobian is the identity and Theorem C.1's B is 1.

Training: exactly the paper's loss (Eq. 1, full-vocabulary token KL at both positions, averaged over the two
positions), rollouts from pi_lambda (Appendix D.5 with alpha 0.2 and c = 2 log 3), 32 rollouts per step,
150 steps, Adam (0.9, 0.999, 1e-8), 10 warm-up steps, global clipping at 1.0, 3 seeds.
Learning rates fixed in advance: 0.02 (low) and 0.1 (high). Lambda grid as the paper: -2 to 2 in steps of 0.5.

What should differ, decided before running:
 1. Forward KL accuracy should vary little across lambda (the bounded gradient of Theorem C.1).
 2. Reverse KL should do worse with teacher-favoured rollouts (lambda < 0) than with student-favoured ones:
    on teacher prefixes it never trains the answers on the student's habit route, and its route gradient
    pushes little mass onto routes the student gives almost no probability (the pi_S weighting of Eq. 5).
 3. Forward KL should keep both teacher routes (more distinct correct routes in 10 samples); reverse KL fewer.
 4. Without clipping, reverse KL should suffer more than forward KL.
Points 2 and 3 follow from the mechanism the paper proposes; the toy shows that mechanism, it does not test
whether it is the cause in Llama models. If a point fails, the page says so; the task is not retuned.

Run 1 (directly parameterised logits), result: nothing differs. Every setting ends at the same place
(pass@1 0.69 to 0.77 at lr 0.02, 0.85 at lr 0.1 for both directions and every lambda; teacher 0.85), clipping
changes nothing. Reason, unrelated to the claim: with one free logit per (prefix, token) the student can
represent the teacher exactly, and Adam rescales every coordinate's step to about the learning rate, so a
tiny reverse-KL gradient on a token the student ignores still moves that logit at full speed, and 150 steps
visit every prefix often enough. Real students share parameters across prefixes and tokens, which is where a
rollout policy's weighting and a vanishing per-token gradient can matter.

Change made once (2026-10-19), before looking at any result of it: the student becomes a small network with
shared parameters: input one-hot(prompt) + one-hot(route or start), one tanh hidden layer of 6 units, a route
head and an answer head (184 parameters). It cannot memorise all 60 correct answers, so it must compromise,
and the rollout policy decides where. Its starting point is the run-1 student (fitted to the run-1 student's
distributions by forward KL over all prefixes, 400 Adam steps, lr 0.05, seed 7). Everything else unchanged,
including the learning rates 0.02 and 0.1, which were not retuned.

Run 2 (shared-parameter student, the page's toy), result, 3 seeds per point, pass@1 at temperature 1:
 1. Forward KL varies little with lambda: 0.60 to 0.65 at lr 0.02, 0.75 to 0.79 at lr 0.1. Reproduces.
 2. Reverse KL is just as flat: 0.68 to 0.69 at lr 0.02, 0.83 to 0.86 at lr 0.1. Does NOT reproduce the
    paper's sensitivity; reverse KL is also the more accurate direction here, as on the knowledge base's
    distillation page toy.
 3. Forward KL keeps more distinct correct routes in 10 samples: 1.78 to 1.83 against 1.62 to 1.69 at
    lr 0.02 (teacher 2.12). Reproduces the direction of the coverage result.
 4. Clipping changes nothing: the batch gradient norm stays under 1 (about 0.7 at step 1, under 0.25 after
    step 25), whereas the paper's norms exceed 1 at every logged step (Figure 7). Not testable at this scale.
Diagnosis recorded for the page: the toy's student-teacher log-ratio range at the start is about 8.3 nats over
5 tokens; an LLM's 128k-token vocabulary with a teacher at temperature 0.5 leaves far more extreme ratios
(the paper's own diagnostic, Corollary C.3). No further changes were made to the task.
