2. From GPU hours to arithmetic

Training a Transformer costs about 6 FLOPs per parameter per token (2 forward, 4 backward), counting only the parameters a token actually uses. For V3 that is 36.6B (37.55B active minus the 0.93B input embedding, which is a lookup, not a multiply; recounted from {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/blob/main/config.json}}). Attention over the context and the MTP module add more. Divide by GPU-seconds and by the H800's peak and you get the share of the hardware the run actually used, its model FLOPs utilisation (MFU). Llama 3.1 405B, trained on H100s with the same peak, is the yardstick: Meta reported 38 to 43% BF16 MFU ({{Llama 3, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S3.T4}}).

Peaks: H100 SXM, 1,979 and 3,958 TFLOPS "with sparsity", halved for dense ({{NVIDIA|https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/}}); the H800 has the H100's compute with less interconnect bandwidth ({{planetbanatt|https://planetbanatt.net/articles/v3fermi.html}}). Llama: 405B parameters, 15.6T tokens ({{Llama 3 §1|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S1}}), 30.84M GPU hours ({{model card|https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama3_1/MODEL_CARD.md}}). Attention FLOPs per token per layer, training: 3 × 2 × heads × (key + value dimension) × average causal context. V3 pre-training hours only (2,664K); Llama's figure is its model card's training time for 405B. Assumptions are first-order: no recomputation, no pipeline bubble, no failed steps.

5. What the $5.576M leaves out

  • Research and ablations on architectures, algorithms and data: excluded by the paper's own sentence ({{§1|ax:S1}}). The ablations alone include 15.7B and 228.7B models on up to 1.33T tokens, twice each for MTP and for balancing, and FP8 against BF16 at two scales ({{§4.5|ax:S4.SS5}}, {{Appendix B|ax:A2.SS1}}).
  • The R1-series expert models that generated V3's reasoning data ({{§5.1|ax:S5.SS1}}); R1's own later report gives its RL cost, quoted on the {{DeepSeek lab page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a2ae7ec6796947a235}}.
  • Hardware, power, staff and data. The 2,048 H800s cost far more to buy than $5.6M; the lab page quotes SemiAnalysis's estimate of DeepSeek's hardware. The paper never claims otherwise: $2 an hour is a rental-equivalent price.

The fair one-line version: "the final training run used 2.788M H800 GPU hours, worth about $5.6M at rental prices". That claim checks out.
