Train the pairs: a toy Ettin suite

The {{BERT page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e5ad9bd09cbf18ad7c}} already trains a toy bidirectional encoder against a left-to-right twin, on a task built so that the right-hand context decides the answer. This tab does what is specific to Ettin: a small suite of paired models, each pair built and trained identically, at three sizes; cross-objective continued training in both directions, at the paper's budget (2.5% of pretraining) and, for the middle size, at four and ten times it; and an encoder generating text by filling masks, the method the paper uses to score encoders on generative tasks.

The toy world

Every document lists two or three random facts and restates three to six of them: ann red cup . bob blue box . so bob blue box . The facts are drawn at random, so nothing can be memorised; every answer has to be read from the context. 30% of the documents are cut short at a random point, as packed pretraining sequences are cut at the context boundary. Three tasks, one for each family in the paper's Figure 1 except retrieval:

  • Classify (stands in for MNLI): facts plus a statement, "so bob blue box .", fine-tuned to say true or false. False statements borrow a colour or object from another fact, or use a colour in no fact.
  • Generate (stands in for LAMBADA and TriviaQA): facts plus "so bob", generate three tokens; correct only if all three are ("blue box .").
  • Choose (stands in for ARC and SciQ, which the harness scores by likelihood): the same prompt and four candidate colour-object pairs; the model picks the one it gives the highest log-probability.

Retrieval is left out: in a world where a query and its document share their words, a toy retriever would be measuring word overlap, not representations.

The same prompt generated by each of the four toy models of the small size (the four that ship with the page; the larger ones would push the page past 300 KB), running in your browser on their real (6-bit) weights. A decoder reads the prompt left to right and predicts the next token at the last position. An encoder is given the prompt plus three [MASK] tokens and an end token, fills the first mask, and repeats, the method of {{Samuel (2024)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04823}} that the paper uses (Appendix B). The encoder-from-decoder fills the mask too, but reads it one position earlier, as MNTP trained it. Bars: the model's top three tokens at each step. The small encoder never learned which colour belongs to which name within its budget, so it fills the masks with a plausible colour from the facts, right about half the time; the encoder-from-decoder, which inherited the decoder's lookup, fills them correctly.
