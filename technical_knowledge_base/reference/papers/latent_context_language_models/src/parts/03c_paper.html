Results

[[S6|§6, Figures 1, 4, 5, Table 6]]

Evaluation is mostly long-context: RULER (synthetic stress tests, 4K to 16K), LongBench (16 English and 5 Chinese subtasks) and LongHealth (clinical multiple choice over five concatenated patient records, about 64K tokens, no chain of thought). Instructions stay as normal tokens; only the long context is compressed ({{§6|ax:S6}}, {{Table 5|ax:A7.T5}}). GSM8K is the odd one out: the whole prompt is compressed. Baselines are SnapKV (plain, query-aware and self-study), Expected Attention, KVzip, FastKVzip and Attention Matching (fast and slow), all on the same Qwen3-4B decoder ({{§6.1|ax:S6.SS1}}).

A new frontier on time to first token against accuracy. In {{Figures 1 and 5|ax:S1.F1}} KV methods appear as vertical lines: they prefill the whole context whatever the target ratio, and eviction is cheap next to prefill, so their time barely moves; a higher LCLM ratio removes decoder work directly ({{§6.2|ax:S6.SS2}}). The printed speed-ups, recomputed from the decoded points, all compare LCLM 4x with FastKVzip 4x:

Defaults reproduce every printed speed-up, independently (TTFT ratio of two decoded points, method in the {{Tables tab|tab:t-tables:pareto}}). The last column is the comparison the figure leaves out: the uncompressed decoder's own TTFT at the same length, from Figure 4.

Memory scales differently. From 4K to 1M tokens ({{Figure 4|ax:S5.F4}}), the 16x model's peak memory stays flat at about 26 GB from 128K to 512K, because the encoder's batched activations (128 windows of 1,024 tokens, 131,072 tokens per pass) dominate until the decoder's prefill over the latents overtakes them; the 8x model is flat from 128K to 256K. Every KV baseline runs out of memory at 512K and 1M on the 141 GB H200, except that Attention Matching fails at 512K from numerical instability in its linear solver. At 256K the uncompressed decoder needs GB against GB for LCLM 4x and GB for 16x. At 4K everything costs about the same, GB, which is just the two models' weights in bf16 (computed from the configs: GB).

Quality against the uncompressed decoder ({{Table 6|ax:A7.T6}}):

At 4x the model is close to uncompressed and even above it on LongBench English (46.04 against 45.21). At 16x RULER 4K falls to 75.06 while GSM8K holds at 81.05, which is the notable one: 16x discards 93.75% of the positions of a short, dense maths problem where nearly every token matters ({{App. G.1|ax:A7.SS1}}). Most KV baselines collapse on GSM8K at 16x (none above 31.39), and SnapKV scores 0.08 at every ratio, which is one problem of 1,319; at 4x, though, KVzip reaches 89.08 against the LCLM's 91.05. LongBench Chinese drops furthest: 40.92 at 4x against 46.63 uncompressed, where KVzip keeps 47.45.

Scaling cuts against "more understanding effort helps". Scaling the decoder from 4B to 8B lowered the end-of-stage-2 loss from 0.6884 to 0.6591; scaling the encoder from 0.6B to 4B only to 0.6860 ({{Figure 11|ax:A5.F11}}). Downstream the picture is mixed ({{Table 4|ax:A5.T4}}): the 4B encoder wins LongBench, LongHealth and GSM8K, the 0.6B encoder wins every RULER length, and the 8B decoder wins nothing but LongBench (37.80 against 37.33). The authors' reading: a bigger encoder buys better semantic aggregation and worse exact retrieval; the 8B decoder is a hybrid-thinking model that the 4B-tuned data may not suit, and the 4B instruct model already beats it uncompressed on several tasks ({{App. E.3|ax:A5.SS3}}). Lower ratios give lower loss: 0.6643 at 4x, 0.6789 at 8x, 0.6884 at 16x.

The agent: skim, then EXPAND

[[S7|§7, Figure 6, Table 33]]

Agents usually find information by lexical or semantic search, which fails when the right keyword is not obvious before reading: a bug reported in the "dashboard login flow" may live in an indirectly called entitlement module that never mentions "dashboard" or "login" ({{§7|ax:S7}}). An LCLM makes it cheap to put everything in context at once. The harness: cut the input into 512-token chunks, compress each, give each an integer id, and give the model one tool, EXPAND(i), which returns chunk i's original text; at each turn the model can expand or answer.

The test is RULER's needle-in-a-haystack tasks at 16x ({{Table 33|ax:A7.T33}}): the agent lifts the 8-task average from at 4K, from at 8K and from at 16K, with the biggest gains on the hardest needles (ns3 and nm3, about +40 points each). For scale, the uncompressed decoder averages on the same eight tasks at 4K ({{Table 7|ax:A7.T7}}). The codebase in Figure 6 is the motivating illustration; no code task is evaluated.

Without EXPAND, the 16x model answers RULER's eight needle tasks at 4K from latents alone. What share of the uncompressed decoder's accuracy does it keep?

The hardest needles (ns3 and nm3, where the value to recall is a UUID in {{RULER's task config|https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER/blob/main/scripts/synthetic.yaml}}) are where compression loses most and where expanding recovers most: the latents still say where to look when the exact string is gone, which is why skim-then-expand works.

Why it matters (beyond the paper)

  • It closes a gap that had made the whole soft-token line look like a dead end. The prior verdict was that encoder-decoder compressors were the right shape but lost on quality (the map of that line is the {{prompt-compression survey|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12388}}: gist tokens, ICAE, AutoCompressor, 500xCompressor, xRAG). The paper's claim is that this was a training-scale artefact rather than an architectural limit, and its evidence is that fixing the recipe and spending 350B tokens moves the time-accuracy frontier past KV cache compression.
  • It is the strongest published argument for a small encoder feeding a large decoder. Most of the compression literature freezes the decoder or trains an adapter only; this paper reports both underperform full training. For an asymmetric reader-writer system this is the recipe with the most evidence behind it, though that particular comparison is not shown in numbers.
  • The systems argument is the underrated one. Soft-token compression keeps the standard KV cache layout, so it composes with vLLM, SGLang and paged attention, while much of the KV compression literature reports theoretical cache reduction that no released kernel realises. That distinction, algorithmic against systems-realisable compression, is worth carrying into any evaluation of this area. It also composes with compression inside attention, such as MLA ({{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}), one layer down.
  • The causal-encoder result is a live disagreement. It sits against the {{T5Gemma|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}} line, where making the encoder bidirectional is worth several points. The two encoders do different jobs (compressing into latents against building a representation for cross-attention), but nobody has resolved the tension, and it is a good thing to be suspicious about.
  • Open directions the authors name ({{§8|ax:S8}}): compress at several granularities and allocate capacity by information density or perplexity, so detail is kept where it exists without an explicit expand tool; and compress the model's own generated state (long chains of thought, tool observations, accumulated history), which is what actually dominates the context budget of long-horizon agents. They call LCLMs naturally compatible with Recursive Language Models.

Connections

  • {{Encoder-Decoder Gemma and T5Gemma 2|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}: the other route to spending more parameters on reading than writing, restructuring the model rather than compressing the input; the two disagree on whether the encoder should be causal.
  • {{Trained Persistent Memory|n:3ce5c17b0d0d81978137dd8f8cab1c74}}: the same instinct applied to memory rather than input context, putting state in continuous latents so reads and writes are differentiable.
  • {{PagedAttention (vLLM)|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bf8b90ca93fee19f5f}}: the KV cache layout this paper's baselines are constrained by, and the reason systems-realisability is a real distinction.
  • {{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}: MLA compresses the KV cache inside attention, the same goal one layer down, which composes with this rather than competing (animated on the {{DeepSeek page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a2ae7ec6796947a235}}).
  • {{Prime Agent|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81579a02f0431f24c6cb}}: the recursive-language-model direction the authors name as naturally compatible, where a compressed working memory is the substrate.
  • {{Mamba|n:3c65c17b0d0d8179984fc3a1fee4c36a}}: the other way to avoid a growing cache, a fixed-size recurrent state, with the same exact-recall cost that shows up here at high compression.
