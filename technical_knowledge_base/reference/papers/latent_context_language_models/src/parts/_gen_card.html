End-to-End Context Compression at Scale (Latent Context Language Models, LCLM)

Ang Li, Sean McLeish, Haozhe Chen (equal contribution), Nimit Kalra, Zaiqian Chen, Artem Gazizov, Venkata Anoop Suhas Kumar Morisetty, Bhavya Kailkhura, Harshitha Menon, Zhuang Liu, Brian R. Bartoldson, Tom Goldstein, Sanae Lotfi, Micah Goldblum, Pavel Izmailov
NYU, Modal Labs, University of Maryland, Princeton, Columbia, Harvard, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, FAIR at Meta (advisory only) · 8 June 2026 (arXiv v1, the only version as of 3 October 2026)

In one line: Revives encoder-decoder soft-token compression by arguing its weakness was training scale rather than architecture: a 0.6B encoder (causal, window 1,024, mean pooling, MLP adapter, chosen in a from-scratch search of single runs with loss gaps of 0.002 to 0.022) plus a 4B decoder, trained end to end on 350B tokens at 4x/8x/16x, reaches its first token 4.7 to 8.8x sooner than FastKVzip at the same ratio (a baseline itself slower than not compressing; against no compression the gain is 1.45x at 4K and 4.5x at 64K) and cuts peak memory at long context, while staying within about 2.6 RULER points of uncompressed at 4x; the slower KV methods remain more accurate on RULER at 4x and 8x. The standard KV layout lets the decoder run in standard engines (the released vLLM path runs encoder and decoder as separate processes), and an EXPAND agent recovers 17 to 20 points on RULER needle tasks at 16x (a codebase is illustrated, not evaluated).

How far to trust it: Believe the systems result: shrinking the sequence before the decoder sees it cuts time and memory, by a lot at long context, and the released checkpoints let anyone check. Treat the quality claims more carefully: at 4x and 8x the slower KV methods are still more accurate on RULER, the 8.8x headline is measured against a baseline that is itself six times slower than not compressing at all, the 16x points in Figures 1 and 5 sit 1.5 to 2.8 points above the paper's own Table 6, and several architecture choices rest on single-run loss gaps of 0.003 to 0.02 that the downstream tables do not always confirm. The evidence, judged.
