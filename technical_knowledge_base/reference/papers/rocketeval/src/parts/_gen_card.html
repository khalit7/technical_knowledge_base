RocketEval: Efficient Automated LLM Evaluation via Grading Checklist (RocketEval)

Tianjun Wei*, Wei Wen*, Ruizhi Qiao, Xing Sun, Jianghong Ma (* equal contribution; Wei as an intern at Tencent; Qiao and Ma corresponding)
Tencent Youtu Lab, City University of Hong Kong, Harbin Institute of Technology (Shenzhen) · 7 March 2025 (arXiv v1, the only version); OpenReview publication 22 January 2025

In one line: Move the judgment out of the judge: a frontier model writes each query a checklist of yes/no questions once (5 to 10 by the paper, 3 to 25 in the released WildBench lists), then a small model answers each item in its own prompt and the score is its normalised Yes/No probability rather than the emitted token. On WildBench, Gemma-2-2B ranks 12 well-separated models at 0.965 Spearman with Arena Elo against GPT-4o's 0.979 (one discordant pair of 66, a single ranking with no error bars; Mistral-Nemo's 0.986 is one pair the other way), for $27.7 per 1,000 benchmark runs against $3,400 at batch prices. On single MT-Bench verdicts it reaches 57.9% agreement with humans, below the 64.7% at which humans agree with each other and GPT-4o's 66.6%.
