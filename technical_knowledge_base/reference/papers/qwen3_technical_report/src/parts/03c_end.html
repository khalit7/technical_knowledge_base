How much of this to believe

What holds up.

  • The tables are internally consistent. Every win count the text states reproduces from its table except one (below); Table 22's printed changes recompute from its scores to within rounding; Table 22's final Qwen3-32B is the released model of Table 13; Figure 2's non-thinking lines land on Table 12's scores. The parameter counts reproduce from the released config files.
  • The artefacts are public and checkable. All eight models are open under Apache 2.0, the chat template with the mode switch is on Hugging Face, and third parties have run them since; the budget dial and the mode switch can be tried by anyone.
  • The authors report their own costs. Table 22 shows the fusion and general RL lowering AIME'24 and LiveCodeBench, and the appendix shows thinking hurting RULER; both are stated, which counts in their favour.

What does not.

  • No error bars, one run each. Every table is a single run. AIME has 30 questions a year: one question is 3.3 points. Averaging 64 samples per question keeps sampling noise under about 1.1 points (at worst, every question at 50%), but the 30 questions themselves are a small sample: a model at 70% has a question-sampling standard error of about 8 points. Many comparisons in Tables 11 to 20 sit inside that.
  • The distillation result is one comparison. Table 21 is one student (8B), one teacher, math and code queries only, one run each, and its pass@64 gain is one question per year. The "1/10 of the GPU hours compared to the four-stage training method" in {{§4|ax:S4}} is never itemised; Table 21 compares against the RL stage alone.
  • "Emerges naturally" is asserted, not tested. No ablation trains a model without fusion, or with cut-off thinking, to compare. Figure 2 shows the real model answering usefully from partial thinking; it cannot show why. The toy on this page shows fusion alone does not guarantee it.
  • Figure 2 and Table 11 disagree at the same budget and mode (LiveCodeBench 67.8 against 70.7) without explanation.
  • Baselines are not all run in one pipeline. The DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeek-V3 columns of Tables 11 and 12 equal DeepSeek's own published numbers on 8 of 9 shared benchmarks ({{R1 paper, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1#S3.T4}}); only BFCL is flagged as taken from a leaderboard. Rivals' numbers come from their own prompts and settings.
  • One text claim does not match its table: Qwen3-32B-Base is said to beat Llama-4-Scout-Base on all 15 benchmarks; Table 4 has Scout ahead on INCLUDE. Small slips also: "about twice" for Maverick's 1.7 times; "consistently superior" for small models that lose math to R1 distills.
  • The pre-training recipe cannot be checked. The data, the instance-level mixture, the annotation labels, the scaling laws and the training compute are not released or quantified.

Verdict: believe the recipe and the shipped behaviour (one checkpoint that switches modes on a flag, a budget that trades tokens for accuracy, strong small models distilled cheaply), all of which the open weights let anyone confirm. Treat the margins in the leaderboards as single runs on small benchmarks with partly borrowed baselines, and the two experiments behind the report's ideas, distillation against RL and the budget emerging from fusion, as suggestive: one model, one run, no ablation.

What it takes to use this

  • Weights and licence. All eight models, base and post-trained, under Apache 2.0 on {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/Qwen}} and ModelScope; FP8 versions exist. Training code and data are not released.
  • Hardware. In BF16 the weights alone are 2 bytes per parameter: about 1.2 GB for 0.6B, 16.4 GB for 8B, 65.5 GB for 32B, 61 GB for 30B-A3B and 470 GB for 235B-A22B (from the recounts above). The MoE models need all their weights in memory but compute like 3.3B and 22.2B models.
  • Switching modes. enable_thinking=True (the default) or False in apply_chat_template; with it on, /think and /no_think in a message switch per turn and the last one wins. With it on, the output always has a think block, empty in non-thinking turns; with it off, the flags are ignored ({{Qwen3-235B-A22B card|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B}}). vLLM (0.8.5 and later) and SGLang (0.4.6.post1 and later) parse the reasoning out.
  • Sampling. The card says thinking mode needs temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20 and "DO NOT use greedy decoding"; non-thinking mode 0.7 and 0.8. For competition math it recommends up to 38,912 output tokens.
  • The budget is not a switch in the libraries. It is a serving-side loop: count thinking tokens, stop at the budget, append the stop sentence and the closing tag, and let the model continue. Expect the payoff only once the budget approaches the length a problem needs (Figure 2).
  • Long context. 32,768 tokens natively; up to 131,072 with YaRN at factor 4, which the card advises only when needed, since static YaRN may hurt short texts.
  • Hybrid or split. The hybrid checkpoints remain; for the 235B, 30B-A3B and 4B sizes the July 2025 "2507" releases are separate Instruct and Thinking models with 262,144-token context, and the Instruct model "does not generate <think></think> blocks" ({{Instruct-2507 card|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507}}). One checkpoint saves memory and operations; two give the best of each mode.
  • Distillation for your own small model needs the teacher's logits on the student's own samples, so the teacher has to run beside training (open weights make that possible; an API rarely exposes full distributions).

Why it matters (beyond the paper)

Qwen3 is the release that made Qwen the default open family to fine-tune: a complete Apache 2.0 ladder from 0.6B to 235B with one tokenizer, one template and one behaviour contract, whose small models punch far above their size because they inherit flagship post-training through distillation ({{Interconnects, "the new open standard"|https://www.interconnects.ai/p/qwen-3-the-new-open-standard}}). Most 2025 open fine-tunes, RL research baselines and distillation studies build on these checkpoints, and it is the family Khalid has fine-tuned professionally.

Three ideas proved durable: budgeted thinking as a first-class inference dial; the minimal cold start followed by GRPO on aggressively filtered verifiable data; and on-policy logit distillation as a far cheaper substitute for per-model RL, a result that shaped later small-model pipelines. (The industry-wide influence is this knowledge base's reading, not a measured claim.)

The hybrid did not survive at the top. On 21 July 2025 Qwen announced: "After talking with the community and thinking it through, we decided to stop using hybrid thinking mode. Instead, we'll train Instruct and Thinking models separately so we can get the best quality possible" ({{Qwen on X|https://x.com/Alibaba_Qwen/status/1947344511988076547}}). The 2507 releases split the 235B-A22B, 30B-A3B and 4B models into Instruct and Thinking checkpoints with 256K context. The report's own Table 22 had already priced the trade (a couple of points of peak math and code), and the toy on this page shows why answering from partial thinking is not free either. The old summary said the team "concluded fusion taxed peak quality"; the announcement says only "the best quality possible", without numbers. Read together with Table 22, it is still an instructive negative result about capability interference: two behaviours in one set of weights cost each a little.

The family then grew on this base: Qwen3-Coder (480B-A35B), Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B (hybrid gated and linear attention with an ultra-sparse MoE), Qwen3-VL, Qwen3-Omni and the closed Qwen3-Max, keeping Qwen the most actively iterated open line through 2025, and on through Qwen3.5 to 3.8; the {{lab page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fd8c36ce06672bb235}} has the dated lineage and licences.
