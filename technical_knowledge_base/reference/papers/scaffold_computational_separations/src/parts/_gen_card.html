Understanding the Benefits of Scaffolds through Computational Separations (harness against distilled student)

Jingchu Gai, Adam Block, Andrej Risteski
Carnegie Mellon University (Gai, Risteski) and Columbia University (Block) · 19 October 2026 (arXiv v1, the only version as of 26 October 2026)

In one line: Same-size students trained on a multi-call harness's traces stay below it (Branching on Qwen3-1.7B: up to 43.6% for the harness, at most 35.8% for any student; a 4B student catches up), and a breadth-first-search harness over trivial edge lookups decides a problem that no fixed-depth transformer with a short chain of thought can if NL is not in TC⁰; the theorem covers short-chain students, not the long-trace students the experiments train.

How far to trust it: Believe the measured direction: same-size students trained on these traces fell short, and larger students or simpler harnesses closed the gap (single runs; the gaps at the larger trees are 5 to 16 points, at the smallest within noise). Read the theorem as a clean result about students with a logarithmic chain of thought, not as the cause of the measured gaps, because every distilled student in the experiments writes a long trace.
