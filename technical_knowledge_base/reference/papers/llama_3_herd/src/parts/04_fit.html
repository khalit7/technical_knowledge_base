Refit the forecast

Meta sized the 405B and predicted its benchmark scores before training it ({{§3.2.1|ax:S3.SS2.SSS1}}). The arXiv source ships Figures 2 to 4 as vector PDFs, so every marker's position can be read exactly, calibrated on the figures' own gridlines (the ten budgets come back as exactly 6 × 1018 … 1022, and the human-evaluation bars match their printed labels to 0.05 points). Everything below is refitted from those points by {{recompute.py|tab:t-fit:fitnote}}, not copied from the paper's curves.

Step 1: IsoFLOPs parabolas (Figure 2)

At each budget, models of different sizes trade parameters for tokens. Validation loss against training tokens is fitted with a second-degree polynomial in log tokens; the minimum is the compute-optimal model for that budget.

Dots: the paper's runs. Lines: this page's least-squares parabolas. Diamonds: the minima drawn in the paper. Defaults reproduce the paper's minima independently: across the ten budgets the refitted minimum lands within of the drawn one in tokens. Curvature (the parabola's coefficient on (log10 tokens)2) is shown so the "flatter at higher compute" claim can be checked.

Step 2: the power law and the 405B (Figure 3)

Upper: tokens of the compute-optimal model against budget, log-log. Lower: each point's residual from the chosen law, in percent. Default reproduces the paper's "402B parameters on 16.55T tokens" independently, at 4.0 × 1025 FLOPs; at the stated 3.8 × 1025 it gives 16.1T and 393B. The text's rounded constants do not reproduce it (10.5T). Parameters use N = C / 6D.

Step 3: from compute to ARC Challenge accuracy (Figure 4)

Stage 1: the normalised NLL of the correct answer falls linearly in log compute (seven scaling-law models). Stage 2: accuracy is a sigmoid of that NLL, fitted with the Llama 2 models, which reach much lower NLL. The sigmoid's floor and ceiling decide most of the answer.

Diamonds: scaling-law models; circles: Llama 2 models; triangle: the paper's prediction; square: the 405B as drawn. The drawn sigmoid is recovered by fitting a four-parameter logistic to the plotted curve (it comes out with floor 0.250 and ceiling 1.000, so the paper fixed them at chance and 100%). The default (floor and ceiling fixed, slope and midpoint refitted on the 11 points) reproduces the drawn curve independently and predicts at 3.8 × 1025; the 405B scored 96.0% as drawn and 96.1 ± 1.1 in {{Table 12|ax:S5.T12}}. Letting the ceiling float instead gives : the data alone never constrain the top of the curve, because the best Llama 2 model reaches 86.8%.

Sources: points from the arXiv v3 source files assets/isoflops.pdf, datacompute.pdf and scaling_laws_benchmark.pdf, read by this page's extract_figs.py; fits by recompute.py (least squares; logistic fits by Nelder-Mead). The forecast's power law is on tokens, as the paper's Equation 1 states.
