Then and now beyond the paper

CLIP's recipe has five parts: the data, the image encoder, the text encoder, the loss, and the batch that supplies the negatives. The step-through changes one part at a time, each with the paper that changed it, and keeps the zero-shot ImageNet number each paper reports. The numbers are not like for like (different encoders, data, compute, and some start from image towers pre-trained on labels); they show where the field went, not which change was worth how much.

One recipe card, changed one step at a time.

The loss that replaced CLIP's: softmax against sigmoid, on one toy batch

CLIP's loss normalises each row and each column of the batch's similarity matrix, so every cell's loss depends on the whole row: the batch is the set of negatives, which is why it wants 32,768 pairs. SigLIP scores every cell on its own, as a yes or no: logits = t · cos + b, labels +1 on the diagonal and −1 elsewhere, loss −Σ log σ(label · logit) / n, with t and b learned and started at 10 and −10 so training starts near the prior that almost every pair is negative ({{SigLIP §3.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15343}}). It "requires just a single pass" and less memory, performs better at small batches, and saturates at about 32k ({{SigLIP|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15343}}).

What survived, and what changed

  • Survived: two encoders meeting in one space through a single dot product; zero-shot classifiers written as prompts; web-scale image-text pairs as the supervision; ViT image towers.
  • Changed: the data became the main research object (LAION, DataComp, DFN, MetaCLIP), and CLIP-style models now filter it; the softmax over the batch gave way to a per-pair sigmoid in the most used open towers; captioning came back as an extra loss (CoCa, SigLIP 2) after CapPa showed captioners scale "the same or better" for representations ({{CapPa|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07915}}); and the robustness CLIP was praised for was traced to diverse data ({{Fang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01397}}).
  • Where CLIP itself still runs: LLaVA's vision encoder is "the pre-trained CLIP visual encoder ViT-L/14" ({{LLaVA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485}}), and Stable Diffusion v1 conditions on "a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder" ({{CompVis|https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion}}).
