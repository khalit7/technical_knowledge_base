The task-horizon series, and where the trend reaches "weeks"

The length of task (measured in how long it takes a skilled person) at which a model succeeds half the time, on a log scale: each gridline is four times the one below it. Squares are Anthropic's three rounded figures from the report; circles are METR's own measurements of the same and neighbouring Claude models (Time Horizon 1.1). The line projects forward from Claude Opus 4.6 at a doubling time you choose. Try Anthropic's "roughly every four months", METR's fit since 2023 (128.7 days) and the older seven-month trend, and watch when the line crosses one and two working weeks.

Sources: {{Anthropic, When AI builds itself|https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement}} ("about four minutes" for Opus 3 in March 2024, "about an hour and a half" a year later for Sonnet 3.7, "12-hour tasks" a year after that for Opus 4.6; weeks "could" come into range in 2027, with no number given; doubling "roughly every four months, up from an earlier trend of doubling every seven months"); {{METR time horizons|https://metr.org/time-horizons/}}, Time Horizon 1.1, page updated 8 May 2026 (50% horizons and the 128.7-day doubling since 2023, 95% interval 104 to 158 days; 187.8 days over all models; METR says values above 16 hours are unreliable with its current tasks, shaded). Derived: the projection, h(t) = 718.8 min x 2^((t − 5 Feb 2026) / doubling), and the crossing dates, taking a working week as 40 hours (in src/recompute.py). The projection is an extrapolation, not a forecast either source makes.
