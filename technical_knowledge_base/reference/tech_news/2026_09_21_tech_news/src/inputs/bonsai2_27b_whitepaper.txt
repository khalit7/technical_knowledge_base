1. Executive Summary
Bonsai 2 27B uses the same hybrid-attention architecture as Qwen3.8-27B [3]. Its matrix
weights are ternary in a fixed rotated basis, taking values in {−1, 0, +1} with FP16 group scales.
This gives an effective footprint of 1.76 bits per weight as shipped, or 5.93 GB, compared with
53.8 GB in FP16.
Conventional low-bit quantization methods fail qualitatively and unevenly. Long context rea-
soning and coding deteriorate first, while surface fluency and short tool calls retain high per-
formance. As such, the failure is easy to miss in casual testing and expensive to discover in
production.
Ternary Bonsai 2 27B averages 83.9 on the 20-benchmark suite of Section 4, retaining 98.2% of
the Qwen3.8-27B FP16 baseline’s 85.4 and slightly exceeding the 83.6 average of Qwen3.6-
27B, at roughly a ninth of their size. The suite spans reasoning, math, coding, instruction fol-
lowing, tool calling and vision.
This release also delivers on the roadmap set out in our initial Bonsai 27B release [2], where we
identified long-horizon, tool-driven software engineering as a major next step. On Terminal-
Bench 2.1, Ternary Bonsai 2 27B demonstrates strong capability at this footprint despite ag-
gressive compression. See Section 4 for details.

2.1 Ternary Weight Format
Each weight takes a value from {−1, 0, +1}, with one shared FP16 scale for every group of 128
weights. The ternary weight in the rotated basis is

                                 wi = sg · ti ,       ti ∈ {−1, 0, +1},

where sg is the scale for group g. A ternary value carries log2 3 ≈ 1.585 bits of information, so
with one FP16 scale per 128 weights the effective storage cost is

                              beff ≈ log2 3 +         ≈ 1.71 bits/weight,

That rate covers the ternary tensors only. Including the high-precision tensors of Section 2.2,
the figure tabulated over all language-model parameters in Table 3 is 1.72 bits per weight, a
reduction of ≈9.3× relative to FP16.

2.3 Storage Footprint
While the weights of Bonsai 2 27B can be represented using 1.72 bits per weight in princi-
ple, practical deployment requires packing formats that support efficient inference kernels. We
implement two such formats, PTQ1_0 and PQ2_0. PTQ1_0 has smaller footprint, while PQ2_0
uses a simpler 2-bit representation that is cheaper to unpack. Neither format is uniformly faster.
Section 3 compares their throughput.

                Table 3. Storage footprint of the language model. Sizes are decimal GB.

MLX packaging. The MLX release uses a 2-bit, group-size-128 packing analogous to PQ2_0,
but its block format stores both an FP16 scale and an FP16 bias per group. Bonsai’s ternary
weights require only a scale: the bias is redundant and could be omitted. As a result, the MLX
block costs 36 rather than 34 bytes per 128 weights, increasing the packed rate from 2.125 to
2.250 bits per weight. Together with the full-precision vision tower and the small high-precision
state path, this gives the published 8.49 GB package.

3. Inference: Low-Bit Kernels for Hybrid-Attention Models
A compact weight representation is useful only if the runtime can execute it directly. Existing
low-bit inference paths primarily target standard full-attention transformers and do not cover
the hybrid-attention structure of Qwen3.8-27B, which interleaves linear- and full-attention
layers. Ternary Bonsai 2 27B therefore uses custom kernels for this hybrid backbone on Apple
Silicon and CUDA. The kernels operate directly on the packed ternary weights, avoiding mate-
rialization of dense FP16 weights. This is essential to realizing the representation’s memory-
bandwidth advantage during inference. Implementation details for both backends are given in
Appendix A.
The rotated representation requires an activation-side Walsh–Hadamard transform at runtime.
Its implementation, optimization, and backend-specific overhead are described in Appendix A.

Cross-Platform Throughput We report throughput with the standardized tg128/pp512 mea-
surements defined in Appendix D: tg128 is token-generation throughput over 128 generated
tokens and pp512 is prompt-processing throughput over 512 input tokens, both in tokens per
second. On the edge platforms, such as a laptop with 16GB of RAM, the full-precision base-
line (54 GB) and even Q4_K_XL [8] (17.6 GB) do not fit. In contrast, both packings PTQ1_0 and
PQ2_0 of Bonsai 2 27B defined in Section 2.3 fit and Table 5 reports throughputs for them.

The footprint advantage is most visible on edge hardware. On the M5 Pro, the ternary model
sustains 27.7 tok/s, corresponding to ≈201 GB/s of weight traffic and indicating a decode path
dominated by memory bandwidth. On an RTX 5090, the same model reaches 142.5 tok/s at
0.582 mWh/token.
The Apple row does not report energy per token because power measurements are not directly
comparable across platforms. NVIDIA board power includes HBM/GDDR power, whereas Apple
powermetrics does not include DRAM. Since batch-1 decoding is largely memory-bandwidth-
bound, excluding memory power would systematically bias the Apple energy figure downward.
We therefore report measured power directly rather than deriving a cross-platform energy-per-
token comparison.
For sustained plain-autoregressive decoding of the PQ2_0 pack on the M5 Pro, measured at
27.0 tok/s over a longer window than the tg128 measurement in Table 5, powermetrics reports
27.0 W on the GPU rail and 32.8 W across CPU and GPU. The corresponding idle floor is 0.17 W
for the GPU and 1.47 W for the CPU. We report these values separately rather than subtracting
them. Additional Apple Silicon measurements are given in Table 6. The measurement protocol
and architecture-specific throughput discussion are provided in Appendix D.

The practical takeaway is not simply the throughput itself, but what it enables: interactive, fully
on-device generation from a 27B-class model on an everyday laptop.


4. Benchmarks
We evaluate all models in thinking mode using EvalScope [4] with the vLLM [5] backend on
NVIDIA H100 GPUs. The evaluation suite contains 20 benchmarks spanning knowledge and
reasoning, math, coding, instruction following, agentic tool use, and vision. Category-level av-
erages are reported in Table 8.
All results in the present section refer to the xhigh reasoning effort. Full per-benchmark results
at both xhigh and medium reasoning effort are reported in Appendix C.
At 5.93 GB, Ternary Bonsai 2 27B achieves an average score of 83.9, retaining 98.2% of the
Qwen3.8-27B FP16 baseline’s 85.4. Its average is also slightly above the 83.6 obtained by
Qwen3.6-27B. The conventional IQ2_XXS [7] build achieves 75.2 at 7.3 GB, despite being
1.23× larger.

More importantly, Bonsai holds full-precision quality on the capabilities that matter most for

for Qwen3.6, while coding reaches 81.58 compared with 82.17 and 82.57. Instruction following
improves to 82.66, above both Qwen3.8 at 81.25 and Qwen3.6 at 74.53. Agentic and tool-
calling performance holds at 77.57, compared with 79.74 for Qwen3.8 and 80.05 for Qwen3.6.
