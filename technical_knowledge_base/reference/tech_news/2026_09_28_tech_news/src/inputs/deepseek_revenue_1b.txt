DeepSeek’s Revenue Tops $1B as It Pursues $7.5B Round and Shanghai IPO
According to The Information, DeepSeek’s annualized revenue run rate has crossed $1 billion, more than doubling from just a few months ago. Founder and CEO Liang Wenfeng reportedly disclosed the figure himself during a recent investor meeting. The jump is attributed to a mix of continued demand for DeepSeek’s models and a recent price hike of roughly 2.3 to 4.5 times. It’s worth noting that a $1 billion run rate isn’t $1 billion already booked -- it’s what the current pace would produce over 12 months if it holds. Liang reportedly told investors that more than 70% of DeepSeek’s compute is still going toward training new models, with less than 30% allocated to inference -- a sign the company is betting on its next generation rather than milking current demand. Earlier this month, DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, which it says offers faster inference, higher throughput, and support for scaling to larger models.
That first external round closed in June. Per Reuters, DeepSeek raised roughly $7.4 billion (about 51 billion yuan) at a post-money valuation exceeding $50 billion. Tencent, CATL, JD.com, NetEase, and China’s National AI Industry Investment Fund participated -- through a limited partnership structure managed by Liang, giving investors economic exposure with a five-year lockup but no voting rights, preserving Liang’s roughly 84% economic stake and near-total voting control via dual-class shares.
Now DeepSeek is back for a second round. Reuters reports the company is raising about $7.45 billion (50 billion yuan) at a reported valuation of 500 billion yuan (about $75 billion), aiming to close by the end of October. Separately, Reuters reported DeepSeek has hired CITIC Securities to prepare for a listing on Shanghai’s STAR Market, though the timing, size, and target valuation remain undecided.

DeepSeek's revenue surges tenfold to 475 million yuan in first seven months, API gross margin nears 83%
Chinese AI company DeepSeek posted revenue of approximately 475 million yuan (approximately $70.7 million) in the first seven months of this year, roughly a tenfold increase over its full-year 2025 figure, with API business gross margin reaching 82.9%--significantly higher than US rivals OpenAI and Anthropic. Net losses narrowed to 715 million yuan over the same period, while infrastructure spending surged to 11 billion yuan. The company is advancing a second funding round targeting 50 billion yuan and preparing for a Shanghai listing next year. Meanwhile, the global AI industry is undergoing structural shifts: enterprise users increasingly favor cost-effective models over the most expensive flagship offerings. Anthropic's flagship Claude Fable 5 has seen enterprise spending share stall at 11%, a trend that could undermine the scale-centric business models of leading AI labs.
Chinese artificial intelligence company DeepSeek's commercialization is accelerating significantly. According to a report by The Information on the 26th citing people familiar with the matter, the company generated revenue of approximately 475 million yuan (approximately $70.7 million) in the first seven months of this year, roughly a tenfold increase over its full-year 2025 revenue. Over the same period, its API business achieved a gross margin as high as 82.9%, demonstrating strong earnings quality.
According to Bloomberg, DeepSeek posted a net loss of approximately 715 million yuan (approximately $106.4 million) in the first seven months, down from the 935 million yuan (approximately $139.1 million) net loss recorded for all of 2025, indicating a narrowing loss trajectory. The rapid revenue growth combined with shrinking losses provides strong support for the company's ongoing second-round financing negotiations.
People familiar with the matter revealed that DeepSeek is in discussions with both new and existing investors regarding a new funding round targeting 50 billion yuan (approximately $7.4 billion), at a corresponding valuation of 500 billion yuan (approximately $74.4 billion). The company completed its first round of financing--also sized at 50 billion yuan--in June this year, then almost immediately initiated a second round, and has already hired investment banks to assist with preparations for a Shanghai listing next year. DeepSeek did not respond to media requests for comment.
API gross margin significantly outpaces leading US competitors
DeepSeek's high gross margin is primarily driven by its effective control of AI infrastructure operating costs. The company has substantially reduced model inference costs by improving AI system efficiency and executing more tasks with fewer chips.
In the first seven months, DeepSeek's overall gross margin was 44.6%, with the business of selling model access to external users through application programming interfaces achieving a gross margin of 82.9%.
This figure is among the highest in the global AI industry. By comparison, OpenAI posted revenue of $5.7 billion in the first quarter of this year with a gross margin of 39%; Anthropic reported second-quarter revenue of $11.5 billion and expects its full-year 2026 gross margin to rise to 63% from 40% in 2025. While DeepSeek's revenue scale remains smaller than these leading US AI companies, its earnings quality already demonstrates clear competitiveness.
Pricing remains a fraction of competitors even after price hikes
While revenue is expanding rapidly, DeepSeek's spending is also scaling up. In the first seven months of this year, the company spent approximately 11 billion yuan (approximately $1.6 billion) on AI infrastructure-related expenses, covering the rental of servers equipped with AI chips and the procurement of chips and other computing equipment. For all of 2025, the company's comparable spending was only about 1.2 billion yuan (approximately $178.6 million)--a nearly tenfold increase in just over a year.
