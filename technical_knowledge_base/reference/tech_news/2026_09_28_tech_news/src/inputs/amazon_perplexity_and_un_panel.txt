Amazon blocks Muse, and Meta's users now hit a popup saying an unauthorised AI agent has breached its terms. On the same day, Amazon accused Perplexity of telling the Ninth Circuit something its own servers contradicted. Both moves lean on contract law, not hacking law.
Amazon fought AI shopping agents on two fronts on Monday. It blocked Muse, Meta’s new AI assistant, from Amazon.com. It also filed an amended complaint against Perplexity, accusing the company of misleading a federal appeals court.
None of this is happening between strangers. Amazon products have been purchasable inside Facebook and Instagram since 2023. Meta also signed a multibillion-dollar deal in April to run agentic AI workloads on Amazon’s cloud. Amazon told GeekWire it was in direct conversation with Meta, and declined to say whether it would sue.

The new material concerns Comet for iOS. Perplexity launched that version on 18 March 2026, nine days after the district court enjoined it. On iOS, Amazon alleges, Comet copies the user’s Amazon session cookie to Perplexity’s own cloud servers. Those servers run a virtual browser and request pages from Amazon directly. “No user device touches Amazon’s servers,” the complaint states.
That architecture matters because of what Perplexity told the appeals court. In briefs filed on 1 April and 6 May 2026, it represented that “no Perplexity computer ever has direct access to an Amazon computer”. Amazon says its engineers watched exactly that traffic every day between 18 March and at least 11 May.
Amazon’s counsel wrote to Perplexity’s counsel on 6 May. The letter identified each statement by page and asked for a correction. Perplexity repeated the statements in a reply brief filed later that day. Five days later its counsel wrote back. “As a measure of good-faith, Perplexity has temporarily disabled the assistant feature on Amazon.com in Comet for iOS,” the letter said.
