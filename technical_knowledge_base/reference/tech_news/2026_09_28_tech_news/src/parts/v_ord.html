Ten times the agents, or ten times the tokens: Ord's arithmetic

Ord's model: a swarm of N agents achieves as much as one agent given Nλ times the tokens, where λ (between 0 and 1, the economists' "stepping on toes" parameter) is fitted per benchmark from OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol launch charts (1, 4 and 16-agent swarms). The swarm buys speed: it finishes about Nλ times sooner, at N1−λ times the compute of the single agent it matches. Move the swarm size and the benchmark; the second slider asks the question the other way round.

Source: {{Toby Ord, swarm scaling|https://www.tobyord.com/writing/swarm-scaling}} (posted 21 September; λ = 0.68, 90% interval 0.63 to 0.76, for BrowseComp; 0.57, 0.52 to 0.61, for SEC-Bench Pro; 0.48, 0.40 to 0.57, for Terminal-Bench). Defaults reproduce Ord's figures independently from his λ values (recomputed in src/recompute.py): 10 agents equal 100.48 = 3.0x to 100.68 = 4.8x the tokens ("3x to 5x"); 16 agents at λ 0.57 equal 4.9x; matching a 100x token scale-up needs 1001/0.68 = 873 to 1001/0.48 = 14,678 agents ("900x to 15,000x"). The fit is to three swarm sizes; beyond 16 agents the lines are Ord's extrapolation.
