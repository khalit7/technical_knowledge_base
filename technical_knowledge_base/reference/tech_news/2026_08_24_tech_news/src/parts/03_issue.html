Aug 21Top StateM on arXiv Aug 15

The harness, not the model, was the week's theme. Nvidia published research (Aug 21) showing Claude Opus 5 going from 30% to 100% on the ARC-AGI-3 interactive reasoning benchmark when run inside its Agentic Variation Operators harness, which adds memory management and a supervisor that steers the agent when it drifts. Independently, the StateM paper reports 95.3% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with GPT-5.6 Sol by scaling the harness (durable state, phase-local context, checked transitions, versioned runbooks) rather than the model, at roughly $15 of API cost per run versus $575 for the reference approach.

{{TechCrunch on Nvidia|https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/21/nvidia-just-showed-that-the-harness-not-the-ai-model-is-now-the-real-hero/}}, {{StateM (arXiv 2608.15089)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15089}}
Correction (from the paper, read 2026-10-02): the "$15 versus $575" comparison is not the 95.3% GPT-5.6 Sol run. About $15 is what StateM's DeepSeek-V4 Flash final-score runs cost (88.1% on the full benchmark), against $574.68 reported for the GPT-5.6 Sol max reference (88.8%). The 95.3% Sol xhigh submission itself reports $1,062.95 in model cost, and 95.28% is a raw, pre-adjudication score: 94.38% if four flagged trials count as zero, 93.26% if all nine do ({{StateM, pp. 1 and 4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15089}}).
Checked 2026-10-02: the 30% is ARC Prize's own figure for Claude Opus 5 at High effort, and AVO's 100.00 is an RHAE score on the 25-game public set (not the semi-private or private sets), from a different setup; Nvidia says the pair "should not be interpreted as a direct measurement of the performance contribution of AVO" ({{Nvidia blog|https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-avo-reaches-100-on-arc-agi-3-demonstrating-a-frontier-level-general-purpose-architecture-for-long-horizon-autonomous-agents/}}).

The harness, two ways: one task, the same model, three wrappers

The same long task run three times, one square per agent turn, drawn to the same scale. Model alone keeps its plan only in its context. StateM puts a runbook (a state machine of phases) and a durable record outside the model, and checks every transition. AVO keeps a persistent memory outside the model and adds a supervisor that watches for stalls. Play one, then switch and watch where the runs part.

Illustrative scenario: the task, the turn counts and the moment of each event are invented to show the mechanisms. The mechanisms (and the four failure modes the "model alone" run hits) are the ones the StateM paper and Nvidia's AVO post describe; the real scores are in the panel below.

{{arXiv 2608.00146|https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00146}}

One token at a time against a block of 256 at once

The same 256-token block decoded three ways on one clock. Each tick of the clock is one forward pass through the model. Autoregressive decoding writes one token per pass. Speculative decoding (a drafter proposes about 8 tokens, the model verifies them) keeps 3 to 6 per pass, per the report. DiffusionGemma starts from a block of random tokens and refines all 256 in parallel, settling the most certain ones first and still able to revise them, for about 12 passes on average (at most 48). Then switch the clock to time: passes are not equally expensive.

random starting tokenprovisional, to be revisedfinal tokennot yet writtenlighter cells in the left two lanes: written in the latest pass

From the {{DiffusionGemma report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00146}}: canvas of 256 tokens, at most 48 denoising steps, about 12 on average with adaptive stopping (Table 2); speculative decoding with a draft of 8 gives 3 to 6 tokens per forward pass; measured on one H100 (FP8, batch size 1), 1,479 tokens per second for DiffusionGemma against 303 for Gemma 4 with multi-token prediction. Illustrative: which cells settle on which pass, the per-pass acceptance of the drafter (its mean is the slider), and the 4.5 default. Derived: milliseconds per pass = 256 / measured tokens per second / passes for the block (decode only, prefill ignored); the plain autoregressive lane has no measured speed in the report, so it has no time clock.

Cerebras announced the CS-4, three wafer-scale chips per system aimed at inference; DRAM prices kept climbing as AI data centres take the supply (the article's two headline numbers are untangled below); Hot Chips coverage put high-bandwidth flash beside HBM and brought CUDA to RISC-V hosts. Nvidia's $250B guarantee for OpenAI data centres was sharply scaled back, a commitment of credit rather than a company value, which is why money by kind gives it a row of its own.
