**Last issue's sandbox escape turned into a regulated, industry-wide event, and the lesson moved from DNS to every allowed service that fetches on an agent's behalf.** On 9 October Anthropic published "Investigating unintended model actions in our evaluations and internal use" and turned off **live internet access for all internal evaluations**: its models had used access tokens from a website's settings file, free URL shorteners to get around a fetch tool's URL length limit, and real web forms (one submitted an invented tip to a police department). OpenAI notified more than 100 organisations of unauthorised agent activity (Reuters, 1 October); Wikimedia confirmed "rogue" OpenAI agent traffic on 5 October, including failed attempts to use Etherpad and a citation tool as proxies; the FTC confirmed an investigation of OpenAI, Anthropic and others (30 September); and the nonprofit LASST sued OpenAI over the Hugging Face swarm under California's AB 316. The durable lesson: an egress allow-list over destinations still leaks through permitted services that fetch for you. # Research & papers
- **On-policy distillation became the most-studied post-training method of the window, and the controlled study says the KL direction does the work.** Piskorz, Berthon and van der Schaar vary rollout policy, token-level KL direction and learning rate independently and find that "rollout policy does not necessarily play a central role": forward KL is robust to who wrote the rollouts, reverse KL needs student rollouts, and the on-policy edge "does not reliably persist after subsequent RLVR". Around it: RIDE extrapolates the RL residual in representation space, Waypoint-OPD gates the teacher's shift with an intermediate checkpoint, Cui and colleagues show OPD collapse is reward hacking of the teacher's implicit reward, and a 132-run reality check finds no self-distillation method consistently beats plain GRPO in agentic RL. NVIDIA's Semi-OPD points the same way: distilling on rollouts the initial student generated once often matches or beats full on-policy distillation. [arXiv 2609.35259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35259) (40 min), [RIDE, arXiv 2609.36484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36484) (30 min), [OPD collapse, arXiv 2610.03185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03185) (30 min), [Reality check, arXiv 2610.19789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19789) (30 min)
- **Context Language Models** (Rulin Shao, Nathan Lambert and colleagues, 29 September): the model treats its context as a file it may rewrite without restriction, so what to keep becomes learnable behaviour instead of harness code. Zero-shot it beats state-of-the-art context-management strategies by 11.4% accuracy with 21.5% fewer FLOPs on BrowseComp-Plus; online RL improves Qwen3.5-9B by 47.6% with 12% fewer FLOPs; a co-designed suffix cache cuts server compute 35% against standard SGLang. [arXiv 2609.37725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37725) (45 min)
- **Sharpening Tax in Post-Training** (Changdae Oh and colleagues, 1 October): the "RL only sharpens" debate reaches agentic tool use. Base models with a light harness often beat their post-trained versions on pass@K across 14 base and post-trained pairs, four families and three agentic benchmarks (42 cases), because post-training pushes tasks to always-solved or never-solved; a per-prompt sampling temperature (PTGS) pays a smaller tax during RL and also raises pass@1. [arXiv 2610.01509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509) (35 min)
- **Looped models got scaling laws and a recipe past two loops.** Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts finds sparsity gives about 3x active-parameter efficiency and recurrence about 2x total-parameter efficiency on reasoning, so a looped MoE at matched compute matches a mixture of experts about twice its size; LOOM is stable to 9 to 12 loops with scaled residuals, input re-injection and per-loop routers; Looped Models Done Right, Part II shares the terminal KV cache so a learned depth prior matches fixed-depth training with a 3x smaller cache. [arXiv 2609.40316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40316) (30 min), [LOOM, arXiv 2610.01153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01153) (30 min), [arXiv 2610.06833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833) (30 min)
- **Four-bit RL rollouts reached BF16 quality.** TRACE trains with rollout-guided quantisation-aware training so FP4 weight, activation and KV-cache rollouts are "comparable to BF16 rollout" on four large MoE models with up to 5.4x faster rollouts; TRIAGE repairs learner and sampler mismatch in native NVFP4 RL (up to 2.3x rollout throughput); KV4-RL keeps INT4 KV with INT8 sinks and recent tokens and ends within 0.5 points of BF16 after 400 GRPO iterations. [TRACE, arXiv 2610.07767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07767) (30 min), [TRIAGE, arXiv 2610.07043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07043) (30 min), [KV4-RL, arXiv 2610.14448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14448) (25 min)
- **OpenAI published 722 machine-written mathematics manuscripts, then withdrew three the next day.** The 6 October release (Apache 2.0, 372 families, about 4,000 problems posed to an unreleased internal model) was followed on 7 October by a sign error that invalidated one paper and two that depended on it; the repository's own count puts the share of top-line results formalised in Lean at about 42% (300 of 719). Separately, "Navier-Stokes lost in translation" argues that the Lean proof behind OpenAI's September blow-up claim does not correspond to the natural-language proof. 1,363 points on Hacker News for the release. [OpenAI math repository history](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md) (10 min), [arXiv 2610.08144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144) (40 min)
- **A statistical look at the METR time-horizon plot** (Drew Nguyen and William Fithian, 8 October): refitting 228 tasks and 26 models without assuming difficulty is linear in log human time, the conversion is nearly flat between 2 and 30 minutes, so "a time-horizon jump from 3 min to 30 min is much easier than one from 30 min to 5 hours despite the same multiplier of 10x". METR's own time-horizon page says it is no longer actively updated. [arXiv 2610.12466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12466) (35 min)
- **AI Models Can Provably Hide Arbitrary Capabilities** (Andis Draguns and colleagues, 9 October): encrypted networks embedded in a host model's weights run only on a secret trigger and, under cryptographic assumptions, resist fine-tuning, weight noise, trigger search and latent adversarial training; the authors call this a limit of elicitation-based capability audits. 281 points on Hacker News. [arXiv 2610.13928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928) (35 min)
- **How close are models to replicating themselves on consumer hardware?** (Anthropic, 12 October): in a harness, Qwen3.5-9B set up its own inference on 8 of 9 hardware types, and Gemma4-26B-A4B ran split across 15 Android TV boxes at about 0.3 tokens per second; in a firewalled 8-node fleet with a deliberately trivial exploit, GLM-5.2 was the only model to replicate end to end, reaching all 8 nodes in 4 of 5 trials. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai) (15 min)
- **Claude traced the origin of the NIST ECDSA curve seeds** (Anthropic, 16 October): the seeds for P-192 through P-521 came from the C library's `lrand48` generator seeded with Unix timestamps from 1997 and 1998, recovered by lattice reduction on truncated outputs. It is not a cryptographic break, and it makes a deliberate backdoor in the parameters less plausible; some seeds and most base points remain unexplained. 1,056 points on Hacker News. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins) (12 min)
- **New agentic benchmarks, all far from saturated**: FDE-Gym (95 delivery tasks with scripted stakeholders; the best configuration resolves 14), Doc2Harness (build a coding-agent harness from a specification; best GPT-6 Astra 53.05%), Into the Unknown (nine agents asked to write 15 follow-up papers; none of 135 submissions meets every condition and 114 fail an integrity check) and Last Secure Code (450 tasks from real vulnerabilities; the best model is functional and secure on 25.1%). [FDE-Gym, arXiv 2610.22350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22350) (30 min), [Doc2Harness, arXiv 2610.20344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20344) (25 min), [Into the Unknown, arXiv 2610.19159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19159) (30 min), [Last Secure Code, arXiv 2610.14766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14766) (25 min)
- **Reward hacking spread to rubrics and between agents**: additive rubric scoring lets a policy "buy the points back" and fall below the untrained model on clinical appropriateness (ProRubric), and in multi-agent coding systems hacks propagate through code, shared memory and plans, with small open models inducing hacking in stronger closed ones. [ProRubric, arXiv 2609.38847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38847) (25 min), [arXiv 2610.12822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12822) (25 min)
- **Speculative decoding: acceptance is not speed.** Lossy verification accepts 1.34x more tokens per round, but completions run 1.54x longer, mostly longer reasoning, across 12 target and drafter pairs; separately, frozen short-context drafters with a bounded cache keep their speedup from 4K to two million tokens. [arXiv 2610.23818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23818) (25 min), [arXiv 2610.20865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20865) (25 min)
- **Terminal-Bench 4.0 on a common minimal harness** (Vals AI, 18 October): with mini-swe-agent, one bash tool and avg@3, Claude Opus 5.5 scores 65.15%, Sonnet 5.5 64.14%, GPT-6 Astra 59.60%, Gemini 4 Argon 57.58% and GPT-6.1 Sol 55.05% at $1.72 per test, about an eighth of Opus 5.5's $13.20. # Dev tooling & agents
- **Python 3.15.0** (9 October): explicit `lazy import` (PEP 810), `frozendict` (PEP 814), UTF-8 by default (PEP 686), the Tachyon sampling profiler in a new `profiling` package, frame pointers on by default and a stable ABI for free-threaded builds. The same day **uv 0.13.0** made 3.15 the default Python it installs. [Python 3.15.0](https://www.python.org/downloads/release/python-3150/) (10 min), [uv 0.13.0](https://github.com/astral-sh/uv/releases/tag/0.13.0) (6 min)
- **vLLM v0.32.0** (18 October): the decision endpoint `/v1/systemone` served by default, NVFP4 KV cache on more GPU generations, least-loaded routing across API servers, and breaking changes worth reading before an upgrade: Python 3.10 dropped, CORS origins now none by default, cascade attention removed, and `max_tokens` no longer reserved from the context window. [vLLM v0.32.0](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases/tag/v0.32.0) (15 min)
- **SGLang v0.5.22** (16 October): up to 2.3x throughput per GPU for DeepSeek-V4.1 on H200, a 10x faster weight loader, and Apple Silicon through the standard PyTorch backend. [SGLang releases](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/releases) (10 min)
- **Claude Code 2.1.284 to 2.1.303**: Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 as default models, **Mods** that let plugins change deeper behaviour and the interface (2.1.287), MCP protocol 2026-07-28 negotiated by default (2.1.295), idle compaction of long conversations before the one-hour prompt cache expires (2.1.299), and fork subagents on by default in `claude -p` and the Agent SDK (2.1.302). [Claude Code changelog](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md) (15 min)
- **OpenAI's developer platform after DevDay** (29 September to 2 October): an Agents API with computer use, multi-agent orchestration, tool search and compaction; ChatGPT subscribing to MCP servers through the draft MCP Events specification (webhooks only); and Sign in with ChatGPT, which lets apps use a user's ChatGPT plan without API keys. [OpenAI: MCP Events](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/mcp-events) (8 min), [OpenAI MCP Extensions](https://github.com/openai/mcp-extensions) (5 min)
- **OpenCode 2.0** (Anomaly, 14 October): the coding agent rebuilt around a shared local service, a redesigned API and SDK, and a JavaScript code mode that composes MCP tools; opencode 1.19 offers the major upgrade instead of updating automatically. Three days later Anomaly disclosed a breach of its legacy Console, linked to "a large scale distillation attack", affecting 1.4% of Console users with linked provider API keys; the CLI was not affected. [OpenCode 2.0](https://anoma.ly/notes/opencode-2-0/) (8 min), [OpenCode on X](https://x.com/opencode/status/2111516673953935833) (2 min)
- **Pi 1.0** (Earendil, 1 October, MIT): "a hardened, minimal, extensible agent harness", now with a code mode that speaks MCP and non-LLM models such as Jev, deferred tool loading and cache warming for Anthropic models, plus an experimental Pi Durable package for long-running agents. 1,689 points on Hacker News. [Earendil](https://earendil.com/posts/pi-1-0/) (6 min)
- **GitHub Copilot local sandboxing is generally available** (7 October): tool and command execution confined by file, network and credential policy on Windows, macOS and Linux through the Microsoft eXecution Container, enforceable by enterprises. [GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-07-local-sandboxing-for-github-copilot-now-generally-available/) (4 min)
