Same model, same tasks, two harnesses: ARC-AGI-3 before and after the Provider Adapter

ARC-AGI-3 is a set of interactive game-like environments; a run is scored over all 55 semi-private tasks with partial credit. ARC Prize now verifies each model twice. The standard harness is provider-agnostic: between requests the model carries forward only the notes it chooses to keep. The Provider Adapter uses the lab's own API features: it preserves the model's opaque reasoning state between requests and compacts context. Play the standard harness first, then switch to the adapter: the models and the tasks stay the same, and only the harness changes.

Source: ARC Prize results and the Gemini 4 Argon scorecard, read 2026-10-19 (verified, semi-private set). Research & papers 14 items

The window's papers keep asking what post-training actually adds. The on-policy distillation study finds the KL direction matters more than who wrote the rollouts; the Sharpening Tax paper finds post-training narrows what agents can solve at pass@K; Context Language Models moves context management from harness code into the model. Sep 28Research OPD collapse Oct 2 reality check Oct 14

On-policy distillation became the most-studied post-training method of the window, and the controlled study says the KL direction does the work. Piskorz, Berthon and van der Schaar vary rollout policy, token-level KL direction and learning rate independently and find that "rollout policy does not necessarily play a central role": forward KL is robust to who wrote the rollouts, reverse KL needs student rollouts, and the on-policy edge "does not reliably persist after subsequent RLVR". Around it: RIDE extrapolates the RL residual in representation space, Waypoint-OPD gates the teacher's shift with an intermediate checkpoint, Cui and colleagues show OPD collapse is reward hacking of the teacher's implicit reward, and a 132-run reality check finds no self-distillation method consistently beats plain GRPO in agentic RL. NVIDIA's Semi-OPD points the same way: distilling on rollouts the initial student generated once often matches or beats full on-policy distillation.

Oct 1Research

Sharpening Tax in Post-Training (Changdae Oh and colleagues, 1 October): the "RL only sharpens" debate reaches agentic tool use. Base models with a light harness often beat their post-trained versions on pass@K across 14 base and post-trained pairs, four families and three agentic benchmarks (42 cases), because post-training pushes tasks to always-solved or never-solved; a per-prompt sampling temperature (PTGS) pays a smaller tax during RL and also raises pass@1.

arXiv 2610.01509 (35 min)
Checked 2026-10-19 by replaying the authors' released per-task outcomes (on the Sharpening Tax paper page): the tax is positive in 36 of the 42 cases, while the base model's pass@128 beats the post-trained model's in 26 (the interval clears zero in 18). PTGS's smaller tax is about one standard error in both games, so it is a claim the paper's runs do not yet establish.

Sep 30Research LOOM Oct 1 Part II Oct 5

Looped models got scaling laws and a recipe past two loops. Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts finds sparsity gives about 3x active-parameter efficiency and recurrence about 2x total-parameter efficiency on reasoning, so a looped MoE at matched compute matches a mixture of experts about twice its size; LOOM is stable to 9 to 12 loops with scaled residuals, input re-injection and per-loop routers; Looped Models Done Right, Part II shares the terminal KV cache so a learned depth prior matches fixed-depth training with a 3x smaller cache.

Claude traced the origin of the NIST ECDSA curve seeds (Anthropic, 16 October): the seeds for P-192 through P-521 came from the C library's lrand48 generator seeded with Unix timestamps from 1997 and 1998, recovered by lattice reduction on truncated outputs.
