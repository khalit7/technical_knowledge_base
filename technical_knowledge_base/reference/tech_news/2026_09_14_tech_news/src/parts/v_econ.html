Who gets the growth: GDP and the labour share, multiplied out

Total labour income is the labour share times the size of the economy. Pick one of Anthropic's three scenarios for 2030, or move the two sliders, and the bars redraw both economies to scale against the no-AI path ($33.5 trillion, 60% to labour). The extreme case is the one the issue quotes: an economy a third larger in which workers as a whole earn about what they would have earned without AI, and capital takes all of the gain.

Scenarios from Anthropic's {{scenario explorer|https://www.anthropic.com/institute/econ-scenarios}} and its working paper, {{Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI|https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/cf58f84d46a4a76bf5a5b039ac695fba6b80041c.pdf}} (Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter and McCrory, September 2026), Table 3: GDP 1.6%, 8.3% and 32.4% above the no-AI path ($34.1T, $36.3T, $44.4T at 2025 prices), labour share 59.4%, 56.1% and 45.2% against 60.0%, unemployment 3.9%, 4.6% and 11.9% against 3.8%. Derived: no-AI GDP = $44.4T / 1.324 = $33.5T; labour income change = share x (1 + GDP gain) / 0.60 - 1, capital the same with 1 - share and 0.40. Defaults reproduce Table 3's capital income (+3.1%, +18.9%, +81.4%) independently and to the decimal, and its labour income for the modest and substantial cases (+0.6%, +1.3% against +1.4%); for the extreme case the shortcut gives -0.3% where Table 3 says +0.5%. Both are "roughly flat", which is the paper's own reading (0.45 x 1.32 is about 0.60); the gap comes from the paper measuring the labour share of income rather than of GDP. A scenario, not a forecast.
