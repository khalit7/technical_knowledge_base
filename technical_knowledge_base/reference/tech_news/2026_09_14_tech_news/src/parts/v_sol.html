What "62% of speed-of-light" means: decode at batch size 1 is a memory-bandwidth problem

At batch size 1, every generated token has to stream the active weights and the cache of the whole context out of memory once. Speed-of-light is the memory bandwidth divided by those bytes: no kernel can beat it, and the gap to it is time spent waiting between kernels. The defaults are Cohere's own: North Mini Code (30B parameters, 3.3B active), BF16, 8K of context, one H100. Then raise the context, or switch the precision or the GPU, and watch the ceiling move.

From {{Cohere's megakernel post|https://cohere.com/blog/megakernels}} (8 Sep): 3.3B active parameters in BF16 is 6.6 GB of weights per decode step, plus about 0.5 GB of cache at 8K context; 3.35 TB/s on an H100 puts speed-of-light at about 470 tokens per second; vLLM serves 185 (39%), the megakernel 292 (62%), 1.58x faster at batch size 1 and 1.25x to 1.41x end to end. Defaults reproduce 470, 39%, 62% and 1.58x independently (3.35 TB/s / 7.1 GB = 472). Derived: cache bytes scale linearly with context from Cohere's 0.5 GB at 8K (about 61 kB per token); the measured speeds are only shown at Cohere's own settings. The H200 and B200 bandwidths are Nvidia's published figures; the model has not been measured on them here.
