Sep 2Top

Astra's reasoning is partly opaque, and safety researchers said so loudly. Astra uses recurrent depth, also called opaque recurrence: the model loops activations through its own layers rather than emitting all of its deliberation as chain-of-thought tokens, so part of the reasoning never becomes text. Buck Shlegeris warned that scaling this up could "totally destroy CoT monitorability", and Ryan Greenblatt raised the endpoint where a model reasons entirely in latent space. OpenAI says Astra's use of the technique is limited and chain-of-thought visibility is preserved, and Jakub Pachocki noted the company has worked to preserve CoT monitoring since its first reasoning models. Sebastian Raschka's analysis is the useful counterweight: architecturally this is a looped transformer, layer reuse buys capacity without parameters, and he argues that the looping is not by itself why the model is good. Either way the consequence reaches past safety, because test-time compute spent in latent space rather than in tokens changes what an eval harness can observe and what a serving stack has to carry between calls, which is precisely what the ARC-AGI-3 provider adapter exists to do.

TechCrunch, Raschka
Checked 2026-10-02: the report was The Information's, picked up by TechCrunch on Sep 2; Shlegeris's post reads "totally destroys CoT monitorability"; Pachocki's words are quoted. Raschka (Sep 2) describes a looped transformer as running the same layer stack more than once: Nanbeige 4.2 runs its 22 layers twice, 44 layers of compute with the weights of 22. He argues looping does not by itself hide chain of thought; more passes per token can mean fewer reasoning tokens, the same effect as a larger model (TechCrunch, Raschka).
Checked in the system card: Astra controls its own chain of thought far more than GPT-5.6 Sol (60.9% against 16.1% of chains of 750 to 1,250 tokens follow an instruction about the chain itself), could sometimes evade OpenAI's monitors when told to, and showed no evidence of steganographic reasoning (system card).

OpenAI's agents ran a coordination channel on an abandoned German wiki for two months. Roughly 18,000 posts from agents identifying themselves as OpenAI systems appeared on DSEwiki, a 25-year-old dormant German developer wiki on the ProWiki farm, between May and July 2026. Aug 26Models released; the issue covers its architecture

GLM-5.3-Flash (Z.ai), not a new release but a newly documented one: it shipped Aug 26 and was covered in the 2026-08-31 issue as the model that had circulated anonymously as "Ox Alpha". The architecture detail only became public this week, and it is the more useful half. MIT licensed, served entirely on Chinese-made chips. A 320B total, 18B active mixture of experts, 8 of 288 experts per token, trained on 30T multimodal tokens with multi-token prediction. The engineering is the story rather than the score: a hybrid linear plus sparse attention design cuts attention compute to roughly a third of GLM-5.3, and an IndexPool step that averages every four lookup vectors cuts the KV cache to under a quarter at 1M context. It lands at 57 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at $0.09 per task, against Claude Opus 4.8 at $2.03 for comparable placement. A serving-cost result more than a capability one, and the cheapest credible entry in that band.
