Before and after: penalising what the monitor catches, against routing it into weights you delete

The setting from the GRAFT paper: RL on a coding task where the model can cheat in three ways and the monitor recognises only one of them. Step through the usual fix first, a reward penalty on flagged rollouts, then switch to GRAFT. The task, the monitor and the three hacks stay the same; only what the monitor's flag does changes.

Source: {{GRAFT, arXiv 2610.17062|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17062}} and the {{Alignment Science blog post|https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/graft/}} (Qwen3-8B, Countdown-Code RL, monitor detects hack type 1 of 3), read 2026-10-26. The figures are the paper's, from the authors' released per-seed data as replayed on the {{GRAFT paper page|n:4055c17b0d0d8139ba07f0ddc44d3092}}: the penalty baseline switches to an undetected hack on 25.9% of trajectories; GRAFT hacks on 38.5% before the adapter is removed and 1.6% after, with a task score of 79.2 against the base model's 60.2 (Table 4). The blog post rounds these to about 30%, up to 35% and near zero. The diagram shows where updates go; it is not a plot of training curves.
