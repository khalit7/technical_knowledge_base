How much of this to believe

What holds up: every mechanism, and the direction of the result.

  • The reasoning is sound and checkable. Stochastic rounding is unbiased by construction, re-quantising the forward tensors is TetraJet-v2's and Quartet II's published idea, and the toy reproduces each qualitative claim of section 4. The printed arithmetic checks out: 4.5 and 8.25 bits, 16,667 operations per NVLink byte, the last 16.6% being layers 40 to 47 of 48.
  • It is a production recipe, with its cost stated. A 594B model trained this way is released under Apache 2.0, and the post gives the 2% efficiency cost, not only the gain.
  • Two small replications see the same direction. On one B200, an 840M GPT with an NVFP4 forward and 8-bit backward ended 0.003 above BF16 in validation loss (1.6911 against 1.6880, two seeds each; derived from its table), with steps 1.006 to 1.035 times faster than all-8-bit, growing with width; a 10.6M laptop run ended 1.5% above full precision. Both changed the recipe (per-tensor scales, nearest rounding), so neither tests it exactly.

What it does not show.

  • No curve, run count or seed for any number. 8%, 20% and 2% are single readings from unnamed "largest jobs", so whether 2% is outside run-to-run noise is unknown.
  • Every design decision is backed by a word, not a number: "very large hits", "too coarse", "too much", "no measurable" degradation, "no discernible difference in reward".
  • "8% wall-clock speedup" is ambiguous: 1.08 times the throughput is 7.4% less time; section 7's net (5.5% or 6.1%) depends on which.
  • Compute efficiency is measured by loss, which can miss what low precision costs. At 40M parameters (six seeds, emulated FP4), NVFP4 needed 1.32 to 1.52 times as many exposures to recall rare facts while validation loss moved 0.5% to 2% ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}, section 6). No downstream comparison with an MXFP8-trained model is reported.
  • Nothing to reproduce it with. BBBK and the training code are not released, and Transformer Engine's MXFP8 quantiser has no stochastic rounding, so both replications round to nearest there (a fixed 1.6% to 3.6% on the toy's tiles).
  • The release is NVFP4 and MXFP8 only. Fine-tuning it with small learning rates meets the 4-bit grid (responses, below), and the model card's "pretrained, midtrained and post-trained in NVFP4" is broader than the post: only the experts' forward matmuls were.

Verdict. Believe the mechanisms and the lesson behind them: put 4 bits where error is random and averaged (high-volume forward passes and sampling), keep the gradients in 8 bits, and make every hand-over between block layouts unbiased. Treat 8%, 20% and 2% as one lab's readings on one model, and expect a smaller net gain at equal loss (about 5.5% in pretraining, derived). Before adopting it, measure more than loss (rare-fact recall, downstream tasks), and check that your stack does stochastic rounding in the MXFP8 re-quantisation.

Where the responses are right. The Miles comment is fair about the release, not the recipe: whatever higher-precision state the lab trained with (the post never says how its master weights or optimiser state are kept), it published only 4-bit and 8-bit weights, so anyone fine-tuning them with a small learning rate hits the same rounding dead zone that {{Fair Cast|n:40c5c17b0d0d81c39641d49880e39f73}} measured in bf16. And the replications' note about Transformer Engine is a real gap between the recipe and what open tools can run today.

Where it moves the knowledge base

  • {{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}, section 6, holds the recipe; its "no parameter count" is out of date (594B total, 17B active), its "8% less wall-clock time" overstates what the article says, and the transpose mismatch, the MXFP8 stochastic backward and Transformer Engine's missing stochastic quantiser extend it.
  • {{Numerical computing for ML|n:3f05c17b0d0d81d6af0be4a9a2edba8d}}, section 2: stochastic rounding used to move a tensor between block layouts while keeping its expectation.
  • {{RL for LLMs: RLHF, GRPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}: one numerics configuration from pretraining to the last rollout, so the sampler needs no calibrated 4-bit checkpoint; 20% faster RL than full MXFP8 (vendor).
  • {{Fair Cast|n:40c5c17b0d0d81c39641d49880e39f73}}: the same rounding dead zone at 4 bits, on released NVFP4 weights.
  • {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}: dispatch and combine quantised separately, NVFP4 out and rotated S2F6 back.
