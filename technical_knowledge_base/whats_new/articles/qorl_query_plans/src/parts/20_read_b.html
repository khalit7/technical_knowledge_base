5. The measurement rig: a reward is only as good as its timer

[[ah:how-to-muffle-an-elephant|How to muffle an elephant]]

The same query does not take the same time twice, and the whole method rests on telling a faster plan from noise. The rig ran four Postgres containers on a 16-core, 64 GB desk machine, 4 cores and 8 GB each, against the 8.5 GB IMDb slice, with a shared queue of queries ({{in the article|ar:If we run the exact same query on Postgres 20 times}}). Each query was warmed until Postgres's buffer counters (pages found in its own cache, pages asked of Linux) changed by under 2% between runs, at least two and at most five warm-ups, then timed 20 times. That warm-up rule is the cold-against-warm trap of {{Query planning and performance|n:3ef5c17b0d0d817f9e15f445b079e9c8}}, made into a protocol.

Noise was not a blur but two speeds. At Postgres's default 128 MB of shared_buffers, job-13b ran 14 times between 186 and 204 ms and 6 times between 227 and 253 ms ({{in the article|ar:14 of the 20 landed between 186}}): the counters were stable but not zero, because the cache was far smaller than the data and pages kept coming back from Linux. A no-op candidate scored on medians of three can then land one side in the slow clump.

Predict before you move the toy: one timing per side fools the reward 61% of the time on job-13b. With three timings, the article's protocol, how often? With seven?

The toy answers it exactly: 39.7% with three, 25.9% with five, 15.5% with seven. Repetition helps slowly against two clumps; the article's text figure, "~20%", is the share of triples whose median sits in the slow clump (230 of 1,140, {{in the article|ar:230 of them contain at least two slow runs}}), not the rate at which the reward is fooled, which needs only one of the two sides to land there; its figure's caption gives the right number, "~40%". The author's conclusion follows: minimise the fooling rate itself, not the coefficient of variation, since two queries with the same spread can be fooled at very different rates ({{in the article|ar:The actual number to minimize is this fooling rate}}).

The fix was memory, not more repeats. Measured over all 113 queries with three interleaved pairs, the no-op error rate was 5.0% and 5.4% (two calibrations) at 128 MB of shared_buffers, and 1.8% and 1.2% at 2 GB; the 90th-percentile query went from fooled 13% to 20% of the time to 0% to 1.3%; work_mem (4 or 32 MB) made no difference ({{in the article|ar:work_mem had no effect on noise}}). Job-13b's coefficient of variation fell from 10.3% to 0.9%, and the 113 default plans got faster, 95 s to 60 s in total, from cache residency alone. Everything after this ran at 2 GB and 4 MB ({{in the article|ar:I locked in 2 GB shared_buffers}}). Training's timings had to move to the desk machine too: on the rented GPU server the noise was "off the charts", most likely from sharing its CPUs.

The toy at the article's 5% tie zone, and the author's own fooling metric

Phantom wins and losses are equal because the draw is symmetric. The article's own metric is different: it slides a window of six consecutive runs over the 20 (15 windows), forms three interleaved pairs, and tries both roles in each pair (8 cases), 120 cases a query. On job-13b that gives 68 of 120 = 56.7%, higher than the 39.7% of random draws, because all six slow runs fall in the first 13 (runs 3, 5, 7, 9, 12 and 13), so most windows hold two or three of them where a random draw of six holds 1.8 on average. The calibration table's 5.0% is that metric averaged over all 113 queries.

6. Two scores, a frontier check, and a model that could not use the harness

[[ah:baselines-and-metrics|Baselines and metrics]]

Every result is reported two ways. The geometric mean of per-query speedups weights every query equally: a 2x and a 0.5x cancel to 1x ({{in the article|ar:The geometric mean speedup gives all queries}}). The total workload speedup divides summed default time by summed chosen time, so long queries dominate. The author reads the first as learning and the second as practical value, since one lucky plan on a long query can carry a workload total.

Frontier models showed the task is solvable. On a 10-query JOB sample, GPT-6 Astra (medium reasoning) reached 2.54x geometric mean with five candidates and 0.85x with one; Qwen 3.8 2.4T 2.26x and 2.02x ({{in the article|ar:frontier intelligence is capable of agentically doing}}). Single runs on ten queries, as the article says of one odd result.

The untrained 4B model mostly could not drive the harness. Of 113 searches, 81 produced no valid candidate, 16 failed at selection and 1 timed out; only six measured a new plan, five at 1.02x to 1.30x and one at 0.05x ({{in the article|ar:The results were dismal}}). Its PlanActions were malformed, its join trees missed or repeated tables or joined tables the query never joins, and it called tools at the wrong time or tools that did not exist.
