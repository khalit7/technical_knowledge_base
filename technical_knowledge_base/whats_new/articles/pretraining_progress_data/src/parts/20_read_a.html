The idea in one sentence: train every year's open model recipe on every year's open corpus at small budgets, ask how much less compute each newer ingredient needs to match the 2019 pair's benchmark score, and the corpora from 2019 to 2025 turn out to be worth 12.0 times the compute and the recipes 3.7 times; together they are worth 15 times, not 45, because the two gains overlap.

You need: compute-optimal allocation and the 6ND cost of a run ({{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}}, section 3); what curation stages and quality classifiers do to a web corpus ({{Topic: data-curation-and-datasets|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}}, sections 1 and 5).

New terms: recipe (a year's published model and training setup: architecture, optimiser, initialisation, schedule, hyperparameters); compute multiplier (reference compute divided by the compute a candidate needs to reach the reference's score); OLMES (Ai2's evaluation suite: ten fairly easy tasks, mostly multiple choice; every score here is its average).

1. The experiment: seven recipes crossed with seven corpora

[[ar:relatively small scale, and for pretraining specifically|the opening]]

One open recipe and one public corpus stand for each year from 2019 to 2025, and every pairing is trained from scratch at 1017 to 1019 FLOPs ({{in the article|ar:relatively small scale, and for pretraining specifically}}): at most a 290M-parameter model on 6B tokens (derived, 6ND at 20 tokens a parameter).

The training setup in detail

Shared by every run: GPT-2's BPE tokenizer (50,257 entries), 2,048-token context, 262,144-token batch ({{in the article|ar:GPT-2 BPE (tiktoken, 50,257 vocab)}}). Budgets 1017, 3.16 × 1017, 1018, 3.16 × 1018 and 1019 FLOPs, counted as 6ND with N the non-embedding parameters ({{in the article|ar:The compute accounting convention is to use}}). At each budget several sizes are trained and the one with the lowest held-out loss on its own corpus is kept ({{in the article|ar:We use held-out loss on the corpus to determine this compute-optimal point}}; allocation is {{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}}, section 3). Peak learning rate is the one hyperparameter tuned: swept at five anchor points (three sizes, two tokens-per-parameter ratios) and fitted as a power law in both, except OLMo-2, which uses Ai2's published small-model settings ({{in the article|ar:we consider peak learning rate as the main hyperparameter}}).

2. A compute multiplier is a horizontal gap between two curves

[[ar:finding the left-most point of the compute scaling curve|the appendix]]

Take the 2019 pair's score at a reference budget, find where the candidate's curve first reaches it, and divide the two computes ({{in the article|ar:finding the left-most point of the compute scaling curve}}). Reading between budgets along a straight line in log compute (the article does not say how) reproduces its 3.7x, 12.0x and recipe table to the printed digit.

The answer depends on the reference. The newer recipe saves 3.7x at 1019, 1.6x at 1018, and 0.66x (it is worse) at 3.16 × 1017; the corpus gain reads 9.1x, 8.3x, 12.0x from 1018 up. The headline is the highest level measured.

Predict first: the recipe gain grows from 1.6x to 3.7x between the 1018 and 1019 references. At a frontier budget, 1025, larger or smaller than 3.7x?
Reveal

The data cannot say: nothing was trained above 1019. The authors expect larger, since the recipes' main value is making big runs work at all (section 5).
