Responses, in date order

Very similar architectures, multiple-choice evaluation, release the checkpoints

Elie Bakouch, on X, 8 September 2026, critique: All seven recipes are dense full-attention transformers (no mixture of experts, "a big multiplier"); multiple-choice scores can be lifted by adding question-answer text, which newer corpora select for; asks for the checkpoints. Patel replied that larger scale may show more and they would try to release checkpoints (none by 27 November).

(1 min)

Two confounders: contamination and eval hacking

Luca Soldaini (Ai2, OLMo and OLMES), on X, 8 September 2026, critique: Newer corpora may contain benchmark test data, and data recipes are developed against OLMES's own tasks; both would inflate the data gain. No reply found.

(1 min)

Use generative perplexity on a stratified held-out set

GertLek, with Dwarkesh Patel and Jerry Han, Substack comments, 8 September 2026, critique and reply: Proposes perplexity on a held-out set stratified across corpora; Han: held-out loss chose sizes and checked the recipes, but cannot compare corpora, and a better held-out set "would be really additive".

(2 min)

The 2025 corpus has a trained model inside it

Nothing Is Accidental, with Jerry Han, Substack comments, 9 September 2026, critique and reply: Ultra-FineWeb is filtered by a trained classifier, so model work sits inside the 12.0x; Han agrees that much data progress is algorithmic filtering, distinct from architecture.

What it rests on

Algorithmic progress in language models

Ho, Besiroglu, Erdil, Owen, 2024 (Epoch AI, third-party): The estimate the post compares itself with: compute to reach a fixed perplexity on WikiText and Penn Treebank halved every 8.4 months (95% CI 4.5 to 14.3) over 2012 to 2023, about 2.7x a year, data quality included.

(1 hour)

The least understood driver of AI progress

Anson Ho, Epoch AI Gradient Updates, 2026-02-25 (third-party): The essay the post starts from: estimates of software progress range from about 1.1x to 300x a year, and much of it may be data quality and a few scale-dependent algorithms. Its table is where the post's "3x per year (95% CI: 1.5x to 64x)" comes from.

(25 min)

On the Origin of Algorithmic Progress in AI

Gundlach, Fogelson, Lynch, Trisovic et al., 2025 (third-party): The model-side twin of this experiment: ablations of 2012 to 2023 innovations account for under 10x at small scale; most of the rest is scale-dependent (LSTM to Transformer).

(1 hour)

A Bitter Lesson for Data Filtering

Mohri, Duchi, Hashimoto, 2026 (third-party): The post's evidence that filtering may matter less for large models: with enough compute, the best filter is no filter.

(1 hour)

Updates, June 2026 (on A Bitter Lesson for Data Filtering)

Colin Raffel (third-party): Reads that paper the other way: the compute at which filtering stops paying is far beyond foreseeable budgets, so filtering still pays, especially for small models.

(10 min)

DataDecide: How to Predict Best Pretraining Data with Small Experiments

Magnusson et al., ICML 2025 (Ai2): The precedent Han cites for ranking corpora by small-model benchmark scores, and how far small-scale rankings transfer.

(45 min)

OLMES

Ai2 (maker): The evaluation suite: task formats, multiple-choice against cloze scoring, and the ten tasks behind every score here.

(15 min)

Architecture research as addressing constraints to scaling

Beren Millidge, 2026-08-23 (third-party): The argument the post leans on for why model work matters despite small multipliers: it removes what stops scaling.
