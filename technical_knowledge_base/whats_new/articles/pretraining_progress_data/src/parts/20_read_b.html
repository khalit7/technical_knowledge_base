3. The headline: 12.0x from data, 3.7x from recipes

[[ar:12.0x for data and 3.7x for models|the result]]

From the 2019 pair, the 2025 corpus saves 12.0x compute and the 2025 recipe 3.7x, so data gave 3.24 times as much ({{in the article|ar:12.0x for data and 3.7x for models}}). Per year: 1.24x for recipes, 1.51x for data, and 1.57x for both at once, measured, not multiplied ({{in the article|ar:not the product of the 1.24x model side improvement}}).

The year-by-year tables show when each gain arrived: recipes (on FineWeb-Edu) were done by 2021, corpora (with OLMo-2) jumped in 2024 with FineWeb-Edu, the first here filtered by a classifier trained on LLM grades. NeoX and the Pile dip below 1x ({{in the article|ar:NeoX performs worse than GPT-2 at 1e19}}).

The recipe table reproduces from its chart; the corpus table does not (FineWeb-Edu 4.80x and Ultra-FineWeb 5.40x from the curves against 8.63x and 8.66x printed). The article does not explain it; the headline comes from the four-corner chart, which reproduces exactly.

The two tables, recomputed

With the article's rule and linear interpolation in log compute, every recipe multiplier comes out to the printed digit (GPT-3 1.36, GPT-J 1.71, LLaMA 1.68, Llama 3 1.69, OLMo-2 1.82). The corpus curves give C4 1.40 (printed 1.37), OLM CC 2.39 (2.59), RefinedWeb 1.81x against 2.62, FineWeb-Edu 4.80 (8.63), Ultra-FineWeb 5.40 (8.66). NeoX and the Pile never reach the reference score, and their printed multipliers are extrapolated ({{in the article|ar:obtained by extrapolation}}). Datawrapper's metadata dates the tables to 7 September and the curve charts' last edits to 8 September, so the tables may come from an earlier run or another estimator; the cause is unconfirmed. The per-year rates are the six-year roots of the four-corner multipliers (3.721/6 = 1.245, 12.021/6 = 1.51, 15.071/6 = 1.57), not of the tables.

4. "Mostly independent": true in score points, not in compute

[[ar:using a linear model|the claim]]

The article's additive fit to the 7 × 7 grid reproduces exactly: R2 = 0.88 ({{in the article|ar:gives an R squared of 0.88}}), the corpus explaining 81% of the variance and the recipe 7% (F = 3.5 on 6 and 36 degrees of freedom, p = 0.008), with residuals (0.70 points) near seed noise (0.60). In score points, a better corpus adds about the same to every recipe.

In compute, the gains overlap: together they are worth 15x, a third of their 45x product. The same points of score buy less compute on a steeper curve, and the better corpus steepens it (GPT-2 gains 1.4 points over the last half-decade on OpenWebText, 2.5 on Ultra-FineWeb). So OLMo-2's recipe is worth 3.7x on OpenWebText but 1.25x on Ultra-FineWeb (the hero); the article's "don't interact" misses this.

Split fairly, data takes 72% of the 15x and recipes 28%: averaging each upgrade's log gain over both orders (Shapley) gives the corpus 7.0x and the recipe 2.2x of the 15.1x (derived). The 3.24 ratio survives (12.0 / 3.7 = 4.05 / 1.25); single-axis multipliers need the other ingredient named.
