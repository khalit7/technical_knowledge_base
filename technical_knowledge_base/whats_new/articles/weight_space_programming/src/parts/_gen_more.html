Responses, in date order

The 'Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen' post is misleading

Matthew Honnibal (spaCy, Explosion), 3 November 2026, rebuttal: Tabulates what each experiment gave Astra: raw weights with no tools only in Experiments 1, 2 and 4 (17K to 172K weights); every experiment on a real language model uses Python, and the Qwen edit also ran the model on up to 50 batches of practice problems. Argues the two kinds of result do not belong under one claim.

(5 min)

Qwen 3.8 27B on my laptop can interpret model weights, too

Simon Willison, 3 November 2026, replication: Ran Experiment 1's four released prompts once each through an open 27B model on a laptop (4-bit GGUF in LM Studio): Breakout, Pong and Pac-Man recognised, Freeway missed, 46 to 65 minutes a prompt. Thinks a patient human with a notepad could do the same.

(8 min)

Can an LLM Actually Reason and Program about Neural Network's Weights or is it just memorization on steroids? (arXiv 2611.08070)

Nazmus Sakib Ahmed, 5 November 2026, audit: Claude Opus 5.5 on 125,000-weight CNNs for five image datasets, raw weights, no tools: accurate arithmetic at the first and last layers, but semantic answers follow familiar datasets (KMNIST read as MNIST, satellite images as CIFAR-10 animals) and break when CIFAR-10's labels are permuted. Mostly single runs.

(40 min)

What do LLMs Really Learn About Model Weights? A Stratification Approach

CCaptainX (LessWrong), 8 November 2026, replication: Re-ran Experiment 1 with ablations, 15 runs per condition: shuffled weights made Astra refuse 52 of 60 times and answer right once; the first layer alone gave 100%, the last layer alone 62%. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash leaned on the prompt's metadata.

(15 min)

Response to Comments (the authors' addendum)

Takehi, Teoh, Zhang, Khattab, 12 November 2026, author reply: Weight-Space Programming is a concept, not a method; "natively" means the LLM does the reasoning, with Python as a tool; the baselines put a price in experience on each narrow claim; Astra was not compared with interpretability researchers.

(6 min)

Replicating and Extending Weight-Space Programming (arXiv 2611.21449)

Roksana Goworek, Alexander Hickey, Federico Nanni, Martin O'Reilly, 16 November 2026, replication: Breakout writing reproduces (514, 550 and 307 against 517); the written networks decompile to Python that matches their actions; reading is mostly reading the input layer (board layout recovered on three new games, game rules mostly missed); weight-only audits flag poisoned models but never find the trigger.

(25 min)

A closer look at "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming"

Zhi Wang, Brandon Hsu, Tianhao Wang, Mikhail Belkin (UC San Diego; LessWrong linkpost), 17 November 2026, analysis: Argues, with reruns, that the Qwen edit is context-dependent activation steering written into the MLP output matrix, with a direction built from four short synthetic looping and non-looping pairs. On 1,152 practice problems: unedited 12.3% (87.0% looping), thinking off 53.3%, Astra-style steering 57.7%; trained steering vectors cut looping further.

(20 min)

Since the article (papers that build on it)

WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers (arXiv 2611.18553)

13 November 2026: 42 tasks in five tracks on networks of 2K to 7B weights: agents read small networks and write control policies, but cannot yet patch language models, compress without data, or tell an impossible edit from a hard one. Macro score: Claude Opus 5.5 0.535; GPT-6.1-sol 0.568 against Opus 0.546 on the 38 tasks both ran.

(30 min)

Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights (arXiv 2611.21885)

16 November 2026: An open 27B model, with its weights, its graded answers and the ability to run itself, edits its own weights: +6.6 points on unseen problems; only the largest model of one family improves, smaller ones make no edit or break themselves.

(30 min)

TrojanMaxxing: One-shotting VLM Backdoors with Weight-Space Programming (arXiv 2611.27425)

20 November 2026: Claude Opus 5.5 and Mythos 5.2 agents implant backdoors in vision-language models by editing weights, with no training: attack success 93% to 100% on content moderation, 78% to 100% on OCR, clean accuracy kept.
