• rebuttalThe 'Astra can read and write weights to improve Qwen' post is misleading
    Matthew Honnibal (spaCy, Explosion), 3 November 2026

    Tabulates what each experiment gave Astra: raw weights with no tools only in Experiments 1, 2 and 4 (17K to 172K weights); every experiment on a real language model uses Python, and the Qwen edit also ran the model on up to 50 batches of practice problems. Argues the two kinds of result do not belong under one claim.

  • replicationQwen 3.8 27B on my laptop can interpret model weights, too
    Simon Willison, 3 November 2026

    Ran Experiment 1's four released prompts once each through an open 27B model on a laptop (4-bit GGUF in LM Studio): Breakout, Pong and Pac-Man recognised, Freeway missed, 46 to 65 minutes a prompt. Thinks a patient human with a notepad could do the same.

  • auditCan an LLM Actually Reason and Program about Neural Network's Weights or is it just memorization on steroids? (arXiv 2611.08070)
    Nazmus Sakib Ahmed, 5 November 2026

    Claude Opus 5.5 on 125,000-weight CNNs for five image datasets, raw weights, no tools: accurate arithmetic at the first and last layers, but semantic answers follow familiar datasets (KMNIST read as MNIST, satellite images as CIFAR-10 animals) and break when CIFAR-10's labels are permuted. Mostly single runs.

  • replicationWhat do LLMs Really Learn About Model Weights? A Stratification Approach
    CCaptainX (LessWrong), 8 November 2026

    Re-ran Experiment 1 with ablations, 15 runs per condition: shuffled weights made Astra refuse 52 of 60 times and answer right once; the first layer alone gave 100%, the last layer alone 62%. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash leaned on the prompt's metadata.

  • author replyResponse to Comments (the authors' addendum)
    Takehi, Teoh, Zhang, Khattab, 12 November 2026

    Weight-Space Programming is a concept, not a method; "natively" means the LLM does the reasoning, with Python as a tool; the baselines put a price in experience on each narrow claim; Astra was not compared with interpretability researchers.

  • replicationReplicating and Extending Weight-Space Programming (arXiv 2611.21449)
    Roksana Goworek, Alexander Hickey, Federico Nanni, Martin O'Reilly, 16 November 2026

    Breakout writing reproduces (514, 550 and 307 against 517); the written networks decompile to Python that matches their actions; reading is mostly reading the input layer (board layout recovered on three new games, game rules mostly missed); weight-only audits flag poisoned models but never find the trigger.

  • analysisA closer look at "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming"
    Zhi Wang, Brandon Hsu, Tianhao Wang, Mikhail Belkin (UC San Diego; LessWrong linkpost), 17 November 2026

    Argues, with reruns, that the Qwen edit is context-dependent activation steering written into the MLP output matrix, with a direction built from four short synthetic looping and non-looping pairs. On 1,152 practice problems: unedited 12.3% (87.0% looping), thinking off 53.3%, Astra-style steering 57.7%; trained steering vectors cut looping further.
