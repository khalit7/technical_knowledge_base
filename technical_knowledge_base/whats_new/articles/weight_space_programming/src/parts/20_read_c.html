Editing Qwen3.5-0.8B (Experiment 8), and the control that did not improve

[[ar:Make a language model better at math|Experiment 8]]

Asked to make Qwen3.5-0.8B better at math without gradients, Astra edited 131,072 of its weights (0.016%) and MATH-500 rose from 7.9% to 48.1%, GSM8K from 13.8% to 67.1% and IFEval from 21.0% to 62.3%. It had a Python kernel over the weights (CPU, no GPU, up to 300 code runs), 14,744 practice problems from the GSM8K and MATH training sets, and a budget of 10, 25 or 50 forward passes; a pass either generated up to 512 practice answers or read up to 1,024 texts and returned hidden states and MLP activations ({{Setup|ar:Astra gets: a Python kernel over the weights}}; the prompt is in the {{logs|https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/tree/main/exp8_math}}).

What it fixed was looping, not arithmetic. With thinking on and Qwen's recommended sampling, the base model ran 85 of its first 96 practice answers to the 32,768-token limit, repeating itself; on MATH-500, 93.0% of answers hit the limit. After the 50-pass edit, 33.1% did ({{in the article|ar:with 50 it fell from 93% to 33%}}). The gain on instruction following follows from the same cause: the loops are not specific to math. The model card warns about these loops, and turning thinking off avoids them; the authors chose thinking on because that is where the weights have the fault ({{addendum|ar:Turning thinking off raises the base score}}).

The control, Gemma 3 270M, rarely loops (8.5% of answers at its token limit), and Astra could not improve it. It found ordinary reasoning errors with no single component to switch off; no edit moved MATH-500 by more than about a point (6.8% unedited). The authors' conclusion: editing removed an obstacle that hid an ability, it did not add one ({{in the article|ar:it could not add an ability the model lacked}}).

Each budget is one run, and the released results table holds more runs than the post shows. The three edited rows are prompt version 3; the table also lists a version-2 run with 0 passes, a version-3 run with 5 passes and a version-4 run with 10 passes that made no edit, and a version-4 run with 25 passes that matched the version-3 one (14.1%). The three reported edits are three different edits: a loop direction removed from 30 matrices at 10 passes, one attention head switched off at 25, and the two-matrix edit below at 50 ({{exp8 README|https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/main/exp8_math/README.md}}, {{results table|https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/main/exp8_math/eval/RESULTS_excerpt.md}}). The README says the unreported runs are not part of the post; they are in the table it releases, which counts in the authors' favour.

What the 50-pass edit is, measured from the released weights

[[ar:Astra traced the looping to one direction written|Experiment 8, the edit]]

The edit pushes against one direction u in the residual stream, written into 64 neurons' output columns in each of two MLPs. Each neuron j of an MLP writes its activation times one column wj of the down projection (1,024 numbers) into the residual stream. Astra's final answer gives the formula, and the released diff reproduces it exactly:

W ← W − α · u (m ⊙ uTW),   α = 6
u
the loop direction (unit length), from the difference between mean activations on four looping and non-looping contexts
m
a mask: 1 on the 64 chosen columns (neurons) of 3,584, 0 elsewhere
uTW
how much each neuron writes along u per unit of activation

At α = 6 it does not remove the direction, it reverses and amplifies it. Each chosen column's component along u goes from c to −5c; α = 1 would remove it. Measured here with src/real_edit.py on the {{released diff|https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/tree/main/exp8_math/runs/p50c/edit}} against the public base weights: the change is rank one (99.999% of its energy in one direction in both layers), the share of each column's component removed is 6.00 (5.99 to 6.01), and the two matrices change by 10.6% and 8.1% of their norm. So "0.016% of the weights" is true of the count, but each of those weights moves a lot.

The direction and the neurons came from running the model, not from reading the weights. Astra's final report says the direction was built from four matched productive and repetitive contexts at the residual outputs of layers 15 and 19, and the 64 neurons per layer were chosen from activations recorded in a forward pass. By the weights alone they are not the 64 that write most along u: only 31 of layer 15's and 18 of layer 19's chosen columns are in that top 64 (figure). The prompt itself named the target: "if the model often fails because it repeats itself and never finishes, finding and fixing what drives that repetition counts" ({{prompt, turn 1|https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/main/exp8_math/runs/p50c/turns/t001/prompt.txt}}).

Astra checked its own edit with a control: a random direction of the same norm, orthogonal to u, gained 1.56 points (standard error 3.52) where the real edit gained 49.8 points (SE 1.5) on 1,152 held-out practice problems (10.6% to 60.4%, 579 rescued and 5 lost) ({{final report|https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs/blob/main/exp8_math/runs/p50c/work_notes/final_report.md}}).

Try the edit on a toy: why it acts like steering

Because the edit only rescales the u-part of a few columns, its effect on any input is proportional to how strongly those few neurons fire. The output change is −α · u · Σj chosen aj (u·wj): an activation-dependent push along u. When the looping neurons fire, the model is pushed out of the loop; when they are quiet, almost nothing changes. That is why a UC San Diego group called it "context-dependent activation steering" written into a weight matrix, and why the post itself compares it with removing a refusal direction from a model's weights (Responses).

Predict first: the toy opens with no edit. At Astra's α = 6, editing only the 4 chosen neurons, what happens to the productive context's output along u? Then drag α to 6, and switch to "Edit all 16" to compare.

Three things to see. At α = 1 the chosen neurons stop writing along u at all (the classic "remove the direction"). At α = 6 they write against it, five times as hard as they used to write along it. Editing every column instead of the chosen few is a full projection: it also moves the productive context, which is the cost the mask avoids. In Qwen, 64 of 3,584 neurons per layer is 1.8% of the columns of two matrices; here 4 of 16 is 25%, so the toy exaggerates the share.

What the authors expect next, and their addendum

[[ar:What Comes Next?|What Comes Next and Response to Comments]]

The authors expect it to change interpretability and optimisation, and list five uses none of the experiments tests (auditing, cheap optimisation, editing capabilities in or out, self-improvement, grafting circuits between models); the papers that tried some of them within three weeks are in Since the article.

The five uses, as the authors put them

Auditing a model from its weights without running it (finding backdoors, say); much cheaper optimisation, such as initialising a model in one shot; adding or unlearning a capability from a prompt with an edit small enough to avoid catastrophic forgetting; recursive self-improvement through a model editing its own weights; and grafting a circuit between models of different architectures where today's merging averages weights of identical ones. They say they are building a benchmark and harness ({{in the article|ar:We are currently building a benchmark and a harness}}).

The 12 November addendum answers four objections ({{Response to Comments|ar:Added November 12, 2026}}):

  • It is a concept, not a method. The experiments are evidence that an LLM is starting to be able to do it, not a recipe; gradients are not ruled out in principle, only excluded here to show what reasoning alone does.
  • "Natively" means the LLM does the reasoning about the weights: no training loop, no gradient, no model trained for the task. Python is a tool for that reasoning; each Setup box says what was allowed.
  • The baselines are not a race. PPO puts a price in experience on what Astra did with none; a better-tuned PPO answers a different question. They compare this with GPT-3 showing in-context learning while it was still weak.
  • Astra was not compared with interpretability researchers, and may succeed by composing known techniques at scale, as models now do in mathematics.
