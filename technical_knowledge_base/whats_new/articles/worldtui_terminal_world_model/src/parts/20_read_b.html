3. Training it to repair its own mistakes

[[ah:learning-from-its-own-mistakes|Learning from its own mistakes]]

Plain fine-tuning drifted: {{a mistake in a filename propagated|ar:a mistake in a filename propagated}}, since at use time the model reads its own screens, never seen in training. That is behaviour cloning's compounding error; the fix is DAgger's ({{Offline RL and imitation|n:3ee5c17b0d0d81d4995ec47e428de9b3}}, sections 2 and 3), the recording standing in for the expert:

  1. Pick a point in a recorded session, usually just before a command's output.
  2. Let the model play {{the next 1 to 4 events|ar:Let the model predict the next 1-4 events}} on its own screens.
  3. Compare with the recording; use the frame {{if the difference is small enough|ar:If the difference is small enough}}.
  4. Write the correction: the target for the next keypress is the edit from the model's wrong screen to the real next one.
  5. Train on it, then on a few real events after it.
  6. Skip what cannot be known: {{words a correction introduces must|ar:Any words introduced by a correction must}} have appeared on an earlier real screen or in the typing; otherwise training {{would reward hallucinations|ar:would actually reward hallucinations}}.

{{About a third of sampled sessions|ar:About a third of the sampled sessions}} gave a correction, over {{three rounds|ar:I repeated this in three rounds}}. {{With some additional training data|ar:Along with some additional training data}}, it took PresetBench from 24 to 38 of 47. All training took {{about 75 GPU-hours on an RTX 4090|ar:roughly 75 GPU-hours on an RTX 4090}}.

4. Three small tests, all the author's own

[[ah:testing|Testing]]

Three small tests. ShellBench is {{106 commands in 24 handwritten sessions|ar:106 commands across 24 handwritten bash sessions}}; PresetBench {{47 cases drawn from failures|ar:47 scripted cases drawn from failures}} he hit in the demo; {{the memory test 32 worlds|ar:The memory test has 32 worlds}}, each writing two random values to two files and reading both back in separate sessions. {{Neither test appears in the training data|ar:Neither test appears in the training data}}.

Fine-tuning gained most on memory (the table), though the prompted baselines {{predict whole command outputs|ar:They predict whole command outputs rather than individual screen updates}}, as he notes. {{Combined in new ways, commands break|ar:combining them in new ways can quickly break things}}: after an append, cat {{often doesn't show the appended line|ar:often doesn't show the appended line}}. Memory tests are short-gap, zero or one command between write and read ({{ShellBench spans 18|ar:ShellBench also tests persistence across 18 commands}}).
