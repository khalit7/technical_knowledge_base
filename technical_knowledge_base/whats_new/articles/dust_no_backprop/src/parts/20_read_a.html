@@CARD@@

The idea in one sentence: add independent noise to a layer's outputs at every token, score each token's noise by the change in that token's loss, and average over many noisy forward passes: this estimates the error backprop would deliver to the layer, the update is formed as backprop forms it (error times input), and since every token is a separate trial, one pass runs thousands of trials where weight-space evolution strategies run one.

You need: that a layer's weight gradient is the error arriving at its output times its stored input, an outer product, and that a backward pass costs about two forward passes ({{Matrix calculus and backprop|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93}}, sections 5 and 8); SGD with momentum and Adam ({{Optimisers and learning-rate schedulers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817080bbc90954681d09}}); a cosine between two vectors. Helpful, not required: the score-function estimator, reward times noise ({{Policy gradients and actor-critic|n:3ee5c17b0d0d8101a262d3620e75747c}}).

New terms: zeroth-order method (uses loss values only, never derivatives); evolution strategies, ES (perturb the weights at random; the reward-weighted average perturbation estimates the gradient); node perturbation (perturb a layer's outputs instead of its weights); draw (one forward pass in which every token gets its own noise); population K (draws per update); EGGROLL (a recent weight-space ES method with cheap low-rank perturbations, the baseline); cosine to backprop (1: the exact gradient's direction; 0: no better than chance).

The bet: search may beat gradients once compute is plentiful

[[ah:introduction|1 Introduction]]

The post argues that backprop and differentiability help while compute is scarce and may limit learning once it is plentiful, citing the bitter lesson and AlphaGo Zero ({{in the article|ar:general methods that scale with compute eventually win}}). It does not try to be compute-efficient, nor to train the models it would enable, such as long-looped transformers ({{in the article|ar:We do not attempt to make it compute-efficient enough}}). So every "competitive with backprop" below is at equal tokens, not equal compute.

The estimator: reward-weighted noise is the layer's output error

[[ah:credit-assignment|2.2 Credit Assignment]]

For a linear layer yt = W xt, Dust adds Gaussian noise σat to the output at every token and runs the forward pass ({{in the article|ar:we jitter its output at all tokens}}). Token t's reward is its centred loss reduction ct (the mean perturbed loss there across draws, minus this draw's), plus, with a decay γ, the reductions at later tokens its jitter reaches through attention:

rt = Σs≥t γs−t cs,   ĝt = −(1/Kσ) Σi rt(i) at(i),   ĜW = Σt ĝt xt⊤

The middle term is the idea: the reward-weighted noise, averaged over K draws, points along the error at the layer's output (Equations 1 to 3); it is REINFORCE for a Gaussian policy with a baseline ({{Policy gradients and actor-critic|n:3ee5c17b0d0d8101a262d3620e75747c}}). The last term is backprop's outer product with the same cached input: "The only difference is that it gets the output error from the chain rule and we get it from the population" ({{in the article|ar:The only difference is that it gets}}). Each draw's error has no preferred direction, so the signal grows as K and the error as √K ({{in the article|ar:the gradient adds up linearly while the errors}}); the table beside this shows one token converging.
