The article

Dust: Pretraining Transformers Without Backpropagation

Samip Dahal, Bishwas Mandal, Serdar Gülbahar, Akshay Vegesna (Q Labs): The post itself (Q Labs Research (qlabs.sh), published 5 October 2026). Sections 2 (Method) and 3.2 (Main Results) carry the argument; the appendix (about 11 minutes) holds Table 1 with every loss, the extrapolation behind the 10^3 to 10^4 claim, the draw allocation and the tuning protocol.

(20 min)

qlabs-eng/dust, MIT

the authors: Created 2 October, last commit 6 October 2026 (b20f7c0): dust.py (the estimator with its tuned per-population allocations and the training loop), model.py, a backprop SGD baseline, data preparation and the BPE-4096 tokenizer. A minimal version that "omits execution optimizations used in the full experiments", for the 1M and 10M budgets at 256 to 16,384 draws on one to eight GPUs. No logs, checkpoints or result files, and no code for EGGROLL, Adam, the model-size study or the cosine measurements. Two open issues on 27 November, neither answered.

Responses, in date order

Hacker News: parallel along depth, and less memory

SerdarGl (co-author Serdar Gülbahar) and nbutton762, Hacker News, 5 October 2026, author's reply: The co-author's one reply: at massive scale zeroth-order methods "will parallelize better than backprop especially along depth" (pipelines without bubbles). Another commenter adds that Dust stores only each layer's inputs. Neither is measured in the post.

(2 min)

Hacker News: the gradient tells you where to go

blt and okintheory, Hacker News, 6 October 2026, discussion: The top comment bets no derivative-free optimiser will matter for neural networks: their objectives are smooth, and the gradient's edge over random directions grows with the parameter count. A reply notes the gradient-free complexity gap it cites is textbook, not new. Neither engages with per-token credit.

(3 min)

Hacker News: is this a bitter-lesson method at all?

syntacticsalt, Hacker News, 6 October 2026, discussion: Argues that zeroth-order estimates smooth the loss without addressing non-convexity, that the post never discusses convexity, and that better first-order methods will keep the lead wherever gradients exist.

(3 min)

How to read the claim that Dust trained without backpropagation

BrainDetox, 6 October 2026, critique: The one careful outside reading: it counts the updates (about 6, 61, 610, 1,221), tabulates Dust minus backprop (-0.046, -0.043, +0.060, +0.169), notes every measured Adam point sits above backprop (best 5.368 against 5.361), and finds no compute or wall-clock figures.

(30 min)

The backprop breakthrough with a massive catch

Stork.ai editorial team, 10 October 2026, press: The catch is compute: token-level parallelism reallocates GPU-hours rather than saving them. Every number is the post's own; its body ("surpassed tuned backpropagation") is stronger than its own FAQ ("in some short training runs").

(6 min)

Noise: a speedrun for pretraining without backpropagation

Q Labs (Gülbahar, Mandal, Vegesna, Dahal), 12 October 2026, authors' follow-up: A week later the authors benchmarked the 10M-token setting with both optimisers (validation loss, one run each, 8xH100): Dust with SGD 5.058 in 1 h 19 min against backprop's 5.007; with Adam 4.167 against 3.769. Their summary: Dust "rivals backprop under SGD" but "still trails backprop under Adam".

(10 min)

Since the article (follow-ups, checked 27 November 2026)

Noise records at 10M tokens (archived)

22 October 2026: In ten days outside entrants took the validation-loss track from 4.167 (Dust with Adam) to 3.676, past backprop with Adam (3.769), and the speedrun to loss 4.2 from 1 h 46 min to 1.6 s. The fast entries replace Dust's random draws with structured probes (a Hadamard cycle measuring every neuron's credit at every token; credit handed down from the top of the network), allowed because a measured error may take one step through a nonlinearity: Dust's successors, not Dust.

(10 min)

Noise at 30M tokens

25 November 2026: From 22 October the benchmark trains on 30M tokens. Dust with Adam at 24k draws scores 3.767 in 5 h 17 min against backprop with Adam's 3.419; the best forward-only entry, 3.300, is below that, though backprop with a stronger optimiser (ANVIL II) scores 3.340. One run each, scored by the repository's own script.

(10 min)

Q Labs Dust and Noise: is the 4,000x claim verified?

22 October 2026: Checks the viral "4,000x faster" figure: the arithmetic holds (1.76 h to 1.6 s), but it is a community speedrun to a loose target (4.2, where backprop with Adam reaches 3.769), and "the fast entries are not Dust as published".

What it rests on

Evolution strategies at the hyperscale (EGGROLL)

Sarkar et al., arXiv 2511.16652: The weight-space baseline: low-rank weight perturbations that make large populations cheap on GPUs. The post re-implements it on its own transformer (EGGROLL-Transformer) rather than running the paper's code.

(40 min)

Learning curves for stochastic gradient descent in linear feedforward networks

Werfel, Xie and Seung, NeurIPS 2003: Node perturbation against weight perturbation and backprop on linear networks: the journal version (Neural Computation, 2005) states that node perturbation learns slower than gradient descent by a factor of the number of output units, and weight perturbation slower again by the number of input units; the toy on this page lands on both factors.

(30 min)

Evolution strategies as a scalable alternative to reinforcement learning

Salimans, Ho, Chen, Sidor and Sutskever, arXiv 1703.03864: The modern weight-space ES recipe (antithetic Gaussian perturbations, fitness shaping, a shared seed so workers exchange only scalars) that EGGROLL scales up.

(25 min)

Fine-tuning language models with just forward passes (MeZO)

Malladi et al., NeurIPS 2023: The best-known zeroth-order result for language models: fine-tuning, not pretraining, with one random weight direction per step and memory close to inference.

(35 min)

Scaling forward gradient with local losses

Ren, Kornblith, Liao and Hinton, ICLR 2023: Activation perturbation with forward-mode derivatives and local losses to cut the estimator's variance; the closest earlier attempt to make perturbation scale with width.

(35 min)
