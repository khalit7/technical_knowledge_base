Dust: Pretraining Transformers Without Backpropagation

Samip Dahal, Bishwas Mandal, Serdar Gülbahar, Akshay Vegesna (Q Labs) · published 5 October 2026

In one line: A research post with code: Dust adds independent noise to each layer's activations at every token, credits each token's noise with that token's own loss change, and forms the weight update as backprop does (estimated error times input); on a 37.7M-parameter GPT trained with constant-rate SGD (three seeds per cell), 16,384 draws per update end slightly below backprop at 100k and 1M tokens (6 and 61 updates) and 0.06 and 0.17 above it at 10M and 20M, at about 5,000 times backprop's compute per update by our count, while the authors' weight-space ES baseline would need an extrapolated 3,000 to 23,000 times the population to match Dust's smallest setting.

How far to trust it: Believe the mechanism and its large lead over weight-space ES. Read "competitive with backprop" narrowly: the measured wins are at 6 and 61 updates against constant-rate SGD, the gap widens as training lengthens, the sub-backprop figure at 20M tokens is a fitted extrapolation, the lab's own benchmark puts Dust 0.40 behind once both use Adam, and an update costs thousands of backprop updates.
