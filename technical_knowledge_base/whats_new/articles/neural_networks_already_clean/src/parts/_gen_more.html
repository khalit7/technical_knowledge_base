Responses, in date order

Comment on the LessWrong linkpost (crossposted from X)

Neel Nanda, 16 October 2026, critique: "Interesting work, but I don't think it's a significant update to the big picture." Agrees neuron-based exploratory work (neuron attribution graphs) may be practical, and that it is far easier on a frontier model than training dictionaries; disagrees with "solving interpretability" readings. Still holds superposition is real: more concepts than residual dimensions, and far more memorised facts than neurons, which he expects to sit in weak activations whose ablation would hurt; doubts the vision results transfer; finds the essay's definition of monosemantic too loose.

(4 min)

Neural networks are not already clean

leogao (LessWrong; 371 karma, 24 comments on 27 November), 17 October 2026, rebuttal: Accepts the observation that people wrote neurons off as polysemantic too fast. Argues a pattern that fits the top examples is not an explanation: it can miss which inputs of the pattern never fire it (precision against recall) and what the activation magnitudes carry. Proposes simulation scored by downstream KL on a large dataset, which would have flagged "cats and cars" too; reports that in his results ablating small activations substantially degrades models (data not yet shared); raised the Colorado and Tornado neuron as a counterexample.

(15 min)

Why Clean Neurons Are Not Enough

Goutham Nalagatla and Carlos Guerrero Alvarez (LessWrong), 20 October 2026, critique: Grants the empirical claim and asks what follows: a coherent abstraction (the bow-tie neuron with its dress shoes and flip-flops) need not have a compact description, and knowing what each neuron responds to is not knowing how they compose into a computation at frontier scale. Asks for a fixed-budget comparison of neuron-based against SAE-based explanations.

(7 min)

Is GPT 2XL already clean?

Jasper Blank (LessWrong), 25 October 2026, replication: Reran OpenAI's 2023 explain-and-simulate method with Claude Opus 5.5 on 50 random GPT-2 XL neurons (layers 8 to 40): about 8% scored 0.8 or more (3 to 19% across his thresholds), against about 0.3% for GPT-4 in 2023; "if clean means every neuron can be explained in a single sentence, GPT-2 XL is not clean." leogao replied that the 2023 method itself should not be replicated.

(10 min)

Takes on "Already Clean"

neverix (LessWrong), 6 November 2026, commentary: From someone who worked on neuron-basis interpretability: the agenda is promising, and neurons give an "overlay" of probes and steering handles native to the model; but high activations are probably not expressive enough for gears-level explanation (the wallet graph does not say why honesty connects to the wallet), and steering single neurons worked less well in MoE models.

(15 min)

Datasets Determine the Difficulty of Interpretability

testingthewaters (LessWrong), 14 November 2026, critique: On the companion paper: the distribution of text a neuron is explained on decides how clean it looks, and interpretability is wanted for behaviour on inputs nobody anticipated, where a corpus-based explanation has not been tested.

(4 min)

Since the article (papers that build on it)

Neurons as an Interpretable Basis (Goodfire, the companion paper)

10 November 2026: Tigges, Kowal, McGrath, Lewis and Cammarata on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (about 5 million neurons): keeping the strongest 320 of 4,096 routed neuron activations per token moves web-text loss from 1.886 to 1.940 (reasoning needs a light fine-tune to recover); 47% of 5,468 neuron poles have one mode and 69% are dominated by one mode; judged descriptions detect firing with 95% accuracy against random text. Its appendix answers the Colorado and Tornado counterexample: about 98.8% of the neuron's effect is places, the Tornado mode is causally real but rare.

What it rests on

Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits

Olah, Cammarata et al., Distill, 2020 (the work the essay revises): Where the cat-and-car neuron and the car-into-dogs circuit were first offered as the two examples of polysemanticity and superposition.

(40 min)

Toy Models of Superposition

Elhage et al., Anthropic, 2022: The definition the essay sets aside (more features than dimensions) and the toy world where packing does happen; the essay finds no fault with its experiments, only with how far they transfer.

(2h)

Distributed Representations: Composition & Superposition

Chris Olah, Anthropic, 2023: The informal note whose framing (composition against superposition as strategies) the essay calls closest to its own question.

(20 min)

Towards Monosemanticity: Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning

Bricken et al., Anthropic, 2023: The sparse-autoencoder program the essay argues against starting from; its rubric gave the median neuron of a one-layer model 0 of 14.

(1h 30m)

Multimodal Neurons in Artificial Neural Networks

Goh, Cammarata et al., Distill, 2021: CLIP's neurons (the Trump neuron), and the "conjoined features" subsection the essay credits as the first description of recursive generalization.

(1h)

Language Models Can Explain Neurons in Language Models

Bills, Cammarata et al., OpenAI, 2023: The automated interpretability project behind the 1.7% figure, and the simulation score the essay distrusts.

the authors: Labels for the companion paper's neurons, browsable.
