The two canonical examples were a shared shape and a shared pose

[[ah:cars-and-animals|Cars and Animals]]

InceptionV1's neuron 4e:55, the field's best-known polysemantic neuron, groups things by a shared V, a pointed two-sided shape. Feature Visualization (2017) and Zoom In (2020), which the author co-wrote, presented it as an arbitrary mixture of cat faces and car fronts; Zoom In said it "isn't responding to some commonality". Looking again at the image grid, the author saw cat and fox faces and cars at similar angles sharing one V, and so did pool tables and the Shinkansen train ({{in the article|ar:it’s bringing things together because of a shared V shape}}). Three checks followed: its strongest upstream connections include V-shaped flames and a fireboat's V-shaped water jets; its negative pole is an O, round where the V is pointed ({{in the article|ar:I looked at its negative pole: an O}}); and a feature visualization of what its clustered inputs share shows a pointed shape.

Revised by the authorThe first version said "it's not a cat and car neuron, it's a V-shape neuron". A footnote dated 30 October 2026 calls that "too strong": as a 1×1 convolution it does take in upstream cat, fox and car detectors; the claim is about why those ones, a shared shape rather than things packed because they never meet ({{the footnote|ar:Thanks to Liv Gorton for pushing on this}}). A fuller study of 4e:55 is promised, not yet published (checked 27 November 2026).

The car-into-dogs circuit, the original example of superposition, is a shared pose. The author found it in 2019: a car neuron (4c:447) feeding a set of dog neurons, read as the car being hidden in a high-dimensional place for later neurons to read back out. The essay finds no later neuron reads that combination; the dog neurons are among more than a hundred pose neurons, each detecting a way of facing the camera shared by cars, dogs and other objects, and later layers keep refining them into car specialists ({{in the article|ar:Cars and dogs were being brought together because of shared pose}}). The misreading had a tool cause too: deep in InceptionV1, feature visualization falls into the many dog neurons and draws dogs whatever the route ({{in the article|ar:ends up being reward hacked in deeper layers}}). Nudging the optimisation along the paths that real top images take (often for only the first 64 steps) shows two of the four readers as car neurons and two as pose neurons shared by cars and dogs. The same shape appears in DINOv2, ten times larger, so it is not only a small-model compromise.

The lesson the essay draws: describe a neuron as a pattern, not a list of nouns. Human and automated interpreters favour "detects cats and cars"; asking "how would I describe it without nouns?" often reveals the shared pattern, and the negative pole is a quick check. The author suspects this is the largest single source of misattributed polysemanticity in middle layers.

InceptionV1's last layer: the microscope and the architecture, not the network

[[ah:better-microscopes|A History of Microscope Errors]]

The last layer of InceptionV1 was the main evidence that networks are almost fundamentally uninterpretable, and the essay shows it was mostly fixable. With the tools of 2014 to 2020 almost none of its neurons could be read: Liv Gorton wrote "essentially no interpretable neurons", the author puts it near 5% ({{in the article|ar:I put it around 5% with tools of that era}}). Two kinds of fix change that, without changing the model's size or data:

  • A better microscope. Feature visualization once lacked transformation robustness and came out blurred; deeper, it reward-hacks into dogs and its regularisation was slightly off. With those fixed and attribution views added, the same 2014 checkpoint reads at about 50% ({{in the article|ar:it’s more like 50% on the same network}}).
  • A model that learns well. Retraining InceptionV1's architecture with modern regularisation gives about 70% ({{in the article|ar:it’s more like 70%}}); a ConvNeXt with the same parameter count (7.06 million) and dataset, which adds residual connections and LayerNorm, about 99% ({{in the article|ar:In the ConvNeXt of the same size, it’s about 99%}}).

The essay's reading: superposition was proposed to explain InceptionV1's late layers, and they were a learning failure, not a second learning principle. Residual connections were invented to train deeper networks, by people with no interest in interpretability, and appear to make deep layers conceptually clean as a side effect. The author trained ConvNeXts across a 300× range of size; all are legible, the smaller ones with coarser neurons (one car neuron where a bigger model has many). The last layers can be browsed in the essay's microscope.

Two explanations of a mixed neuron, with opposite predictions

[[ah:recursive-growth|Recursive Generalization]]

Recursive generalization is a neuron generalizing along more than one axis, then sprouting offshoots from the offshoots. Retraining InceptionV1 and saving checkpoints let the author watch a last-layer neuron form: it began on yellow rapeseed fields, in photos that sometimes held a dog, then grew toward other nature, green objects such as balls, and dogs with no flowers, until the fields were nearly gone; the ConvNeXt neuron that starts on the same fields drops the side branches ({{in the article|ar:We call this recursive generalization}}). The language example is a neuron for "iy" (as in Miyazaki) that grows to "diy" and then to "do it yourself", which has no "iy" at all. The essay credits the idea to the "conjoined features" subsection of Multimodal Neurons (2021), which the author co-wrote and had forgotten.

[[ah:what-is-superposition|Superposition]]

Superposition, in the sense the essay tests, is a different learning principle: unrelated things placed together because they do not interfere. The two predict opposite shapes, which Figure 1 draws: generalization is local (a floppy-ear-dog neuron covers many floppy ears), superposition non-local ("a floppy ear dog + Latin poetry neuron", {{in the article|ar:Superposition would want a floppy ear dog + Latin poetry neuron}}). The essay is explicit that "superposition" has several meanings ({{in the article|ar:So “superposition” is itself in superposition}}): Toy Models of Superposition (2022) defines it as representing more features than dimensions, which the essay says even a fully monosemantic network satisfies, because neurons compose. It sets that sense aside and tests only arbitrary packing in neurons.

[[ah:superposition-of-the-gaps|Superposition of the Gaps]]

"Polysemantic" says more about the observer than the neuron: it means no good explanation yet. The essay lists five causes and argues that superposition, the most radical and least testable, became the default when it should be the last hypothesis ({{in the article|ar:I now think I was making superposition the null hypothesis}}):

CauseExample in the essayHow it is tested
Bad toolsFeature visualization reward-hacking into dogs; a top-k labeller that ignores the negative poleA better tool resolves the neuron or does not
Bad explanationsA "polysemantic comma neuron" in a dense Qwen model that tracks Colorado, writing its running summary on commas (steering it makes the model plan a hike)Steer it, read its circuit
Bad learningInceptionV1's last layer (no residual stream)Retrain with a better architecture
Recursive generalizationA frontier-model "pr" neuron that also fires on Base64 URLs and Bengali: decoded and translated, they are "pr" too ({{in the article|ar:decode the URLs: “pr” words}})Watch it grow over training checkpoints
SuperpositionNone found since the 2019 car circuit, which was an errorIts only symptom is that something does not make sense

The author anticipates the obvious objection, an explanation of the gaps, and gives two answers ({{in the article|ar:risks the opposite error, an explanation of the gaps}}): each other cause can be tested and fails visibly when wrong, while superposition cannot; and real explanations are narrow (a Colorado neuron either makes the model talk about hiking when steered or not). Also cited: Anthropic's Softmax Linear Units (2022), "superposition is often treated as the default hypothesis simply because it seems intuitively more compelling than other explanations, but there is little evidence" (the essay notes that paper calls its own results moderate, circumstantial evidence for it).
