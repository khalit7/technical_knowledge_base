How much of this to believe

The mechanics are correct wherever we recomputed them. The rotated pairs (−2.448, 4.473) and (−5.098, 0.117), the dot products 13 and 19.92, the frequencies 1, 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001 for d = 8, the 0.30% and 22% shares, and the 128 MiB and 36 MiB table savings all come out as printed (closed block below). The code is the standard split-half RoPE and its asserts hold by the algebra our owner page derives. For an explainer this is the main test, and it passes.

Where it simplifies or is loose (none of it changes what a reader learns about the mechanism):

  • It calls RoPE {{relative positional embeddings (aka RoPE)|ar:relative positional embeddings (aka RoPE)}} in the introduction. RoPE rotates each vector by its own absolute position; only the dot product is relative, and the keys are cached already rotated. Section 3 of the article gets this right; the introduction's label does not.
  • RoPE is called {{a staple in most (open-weight) LLMs|ar:a staple in most (open-weight) LLMs}} since the original LLaMA; GPT-J and GPT-NeoX-20B used it before (GPT-NeoX on 25% of each head, the first partial RoPE), which our owner page's variants table records.
  • Section 3.2 says {{Figure 12 below follows a token representation|ar:Figure 12 below follows a token representation}}; the figure meant is Figure 15. The full-vector rotations in Section 3.3 use a schedule the article never states (section 3 above).
  • The reason given for split-half pairing, that {{it makes it easier, in code|ar:it makes it easier, in code}}, to rotate all pairs at once, is his reading; interleaved pairing is just as elementwise with strided views, and which layout a checkpoint needs is fixed by how its weights were trained or converted.

The weak spot is the verdict on partial RoPE. The article weighs it only by the cosine and sine tables ({{point behind partial RoPE? It saves some memory|ar:point behind partial RoPE? It saves some memory}}), finds the saving small, and calls the technique hardly worth it. The saving is indeed negligible, and showing that is useful, because the Fractional Rotation paper itself advertises up to 10× savings of the "RoPE cache". But the tables are not why models rotate part of a head:

  • MLA needs it. DeepSeek-V3, Mistral Large 3 and Kimi K2 rotate a separate 64-number key so that the rest of the key can stay a compressed latent whose up-projection is absorbed into the query; a fully rotated key would block that absorption ({{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}). Two readers said so in the comments.
  • Unrotated dimensions match on content alone. Barbero et al. found Gemma uses the fastest pairs for positional patterns and prefers the lowest frequencies, which they suspect carry semantic information ({{Round and Round We Go|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06205}}); Gemma 4's global layers keep only fast pairs, Qwen3-Next only a quarter of each head. Our owner page shows both kinds.
  • The evidence he cites cuts the other way. In the Fractional Rotation paper about 10% rotation matched full RoPE, and NoPE alone could cause loss spikes that a little RoPE or QK-Norm mitigated: so rotating a small part is cheap insurance, not a waste.

Read the census as a dated count over one curated gallery. The gallery is Raschka's own selection of notable open models, each card counted once (so {{each gallery card counts once, so multiple variants|ar:Each gallery card counts once, so multiple variants}} of one family can count several times), split into three small groups. The directions (full RoPE falling, mixes and partial RoPE rising) are consistent with the variants our owner page read from configs in October (partial RoPE in GLM-4.5, Qwen3-Next and Gemma 4; NoPE layers in Llama 4, SmolLM3 and Command A), though that page counted no trend; the sizes of the shifts are not a measurement of the field. The Inkling description goes beyond what its maker has published (above).

The appendix (added 19 November) has one wrong attribution. Its 1.3B zero-shot comparison, ALiBi 43.70, rotary 41.46, learned 41.71 on 112B tokens of OSCAR, is credited to the ALiBi paper; it is Table 2 of {{Le Scao et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15424}} (BigScience), the study that chose ALiBi for BLOOM. The WikiText-103 numbers beside it (rotary 20.07 at 512 tokens rising to 31.58 at 2,512; ALiBi 19.73 falling to 18.41) are the ALiBi paper's, as stated.

Verdict. Believe it and use it as the gentlest correct derivation of RoPE with code; take the census as one author's dated view of the trend; replace its partial-RoPE verdict with the reasons above.
