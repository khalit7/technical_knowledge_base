3. 47% a quarter is an average over a lattice, not a fitted slope

[[ah:average-rates-of-decline|Average rates of decline]]

The headline is the staircase's own fall, averaged. For each benchmark, lay a lattice over the data: dates every 13 days, 100 score levels spread evenly over the benchmark's observed range and kept only below that date's best score; at each node divide the record cost a quarter later by the record cost now ({{in the article|ar:we compute the empirical frontier cost decline ratio}}). The geometric mean of those ratios, subtracted from 1, gives 43.0% (Chess Puzzles) to 53.1% (FrontierMath tiers 1-3); pooled over the five, 47.0% a quarter, which is 1 / 0.534 = 12.7x a year ({{Table 1|ah:table-1}}). Our port of the authors' R code reproduces all eleven benchmarks' model-free rates to the printed digit.

Most nodes never move. The mean is carried by a minority of quarters with big drops: on GPQA 46% of the 5,039 nodes see the record fall within the quarter, on Mystery Game Puzzles only 25% of 3,518 (computed here). That is what a staircase averages to; it also means a few records decide the rate.

Fitted models agree only when they fit the frontier. A smooth cost surface fitted to the staircase (log cost in Box-Cox transforms of date and accuracy odds, the authors' preferred "Box-Tidwell" model) averages 47.0% too; forcing the surface under every data point, or fitting all runs rather than the frontier, gives implausible rates down to rising costs ({{in the article|ar:also produces implausible results, including many negative signs}}). The authors report no standard errors at all: the frontier is an extreme, the sampling ad hoc, and a resampling bootstrap can never push the frontier outward, so they treat every number as a rough measurement ({{in the article|ar:we abandon the formal paradigm of statistical inference}}).

Coding (SWE-bench Verified, 27.5%) and the hardest math (FrontierMath tier 4, 26.0%) fall slowest; they are secondary benchmarks, outside the headline. 4. Faster than any earlier technology, compared in log points

[[ah:discussion-and-conclusion|Discussion and conclusion]]

On the authors' series, 13x a year beats every transformative technology they could price: 1.84x a year for DNA sequencing (from 2001), 1.51x for compute (1940 to 2001), 1.16x for lithium batteries (1991 to 2024) and 1.05x for US residential electricity (1892 to 1973) ({{in the article|ar:The average residential price of electricity in the US}}). Measured in log points the AI decline is 4, 6, 18 and 54 times faster ({{in the article|ar:Expressing decline rates in log points}}).

The longer claim rests on one pair of models. That prices fell "at least this fast" since November 2021 comes from MMLU: GPT-3 at $60 per million tokens scored 43.9; Llama 2-7B, July 2023, scored 45.3 at $0.20, a 31x yearly fall per token ({{in the article|ar:for a compound price decline rate of 31× per year}}). Neither model reasons, so tokens per question are plausibly similar, but it is one comparison, per token, from Epoch's own earlier data.

The windows do not match: three years for AI against 24 to 81 years for the others, which include their slow decades. Over its fastest three years, DNA sequencing fell 37.9% a quarter (the authors' Figure 1 data), close to AI's 47%.

5. Fastest right after a level is first reached, on three of five benchmarks

[[ah:variation-in-the-rate-of-cost-decline|Variation in the rate of cost decline]]

The fitted model says a fresh score level gets cheap fastest: averaged over the five, 66% a quarter at SOTA debut, 42% a year later and 32% after two years ({{in the article|ar:On average for the primary benchmarks, the decline rate drops}}). It holds on AIME (83% to 18%), FrontierMath and GPQA (81% to 25%); on Chess and Mystery Game Puzzles the rate creeps up instead, by a few points. The authors' story: the first maker charges a premium until rivals, closed and open, catch up ({{in the article|ar:its maker can briefly charge a premium}}).

A model-free check agrees on direction, with a different shape. Binning the lattice nodes of section 3 by how long ago their level was first reached, the pooled rate is 59% and 63% in the first two quarters, 42% to 50% in quarters two to four, and 5% to 18% from the fifth quarter on (computed here). The late quarters hold only low levels first reached in 2023, so age, level and calendar time are entangled, as they are in the model. Chess and Mystery do not follow the pattern model-free either.

The "second dot" claim depends on a date. The article notes that in only one of its traces does an open-weight model take the second record (QwQ-32B at AIME's 25%) ({{in the article|ar:In only one of the eight traces}}). QwQ-32B is dated to its November 2024 preview; at its own release, 5 March 2025, it is no longer a record, and the second dot becomes DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, also open weights. Figure 2 has seven traces, not eight.
