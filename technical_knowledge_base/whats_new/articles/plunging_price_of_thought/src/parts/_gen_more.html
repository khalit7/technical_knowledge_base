Responses, in date order

Has anything got 100,000x cheaper? The headline merges capability and cost

Toby Ord, with David Roodman's replies, on X, 23 September 2026, critique and reply: Ord called the comparison chart and headline "incorrect": the method folds rising capability into one cost number, and the AI line in the chart does not flatten as a slowing rate should. Roodman: read 47% like inflation in chained dollars; the slowdown is in time since a level was SOTA, not since GPT-3; comparing technologies is "fraught". The same day the authors added the Limitations caveat that credits Ord. Ord then called the 725x GPQA drop "solid (though not necessarily representative)" and asked for more early examples.

(5 min)

The price of intelligence is falling

Alex Tabarrok, Marginal Revolution, 23 September 2026, agrees: Quotes the 47% and adds his own reading: if frontier models get cheap to run this fast, open models are less of a threat to the labs than they look.

(2 min)

Price is not cost: subsidies and operating losses

Replies to the launch thread and to Epoch's newsletter version (Jeffrey Lee Funk, Nils H. Lehr and others), 28 September 2026, critique: List prices can sit below cost while labs lose money and hyperscalers subsidise; adding the losses back would flatten the curve. Another commenter: production work adds retries, checks and review, so the cost of a checked task falls more slowly. No author reply found.

(3 min)

A quality-adjusted price index for AI services (v2)

Louis Yiven Zhu, arXiv 2608.29843, 2 November 2026, neither: A hedonic index over models on sale falls about 0.51 a year in log price, "entirely through the rising capability" of the models; its per-task data cover only 14 of the 87 cost-frontier models in this article's released data, so it says it cannot test the article's rate (and its v2 withdraws its own per-task result).

(1 hour)

LLMflation: LLM inference cost is going down fast

Guido Appenzeller, Andreessen Horowitz, 2024 (third-party): The price-per-token version: 10x a year for a fixed MMLU score in the three years after GPT-3.

(8 min)

LLM inference prices have fallen rapidly but unequally across tasks

Epoch AI data insight, March 2025 (the authors' organisation): Epoch's earlier per-token estimate: 9x to 900x a year depending on benchmark and threshold.

(10 min)

Inference costs for hard coding tasks halve roughly every two months

Håvard Tveit Ihle, LessWrong, September 2025 (third-party): Cost against score on two coding suites: halving every 1.4 to 2 months (64x to 380x a year).

(12 min)

Are the costs of AI agents also rising exponentially?

Toby Ord, 22 December 2025 (third-party): The other side of the ledger: a fixed level gets cheap while the frontier agent's hourly cost may be rising.
