@@CARD@@

The idea in one sentence: fix a benchmark score, track the cheapest dollars per question that any released model needs to reach it, and that record cost falls by about half every quarter: 47% a quarter, 13 times a year, on five benchmarks from 2023 to mid-2026.

You need: why a reasoning model's bill depends on tokens spent, not only on its price per token ({{Topic: llm-landscape|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}, section 6; effort levels and thinking budgets on {{Test-time compute and reasoning|n:4035c17b0d0d813f9fb2d62cff1f09ef}}); what GPQA Diamond and FrontierMath test ({{Knowledge and reasoning benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d810f8574da3ffb860be5}}, {{Math benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81ec942edf0040a2f596}}).

New terms: price of thought (dollars per benchmark question at a fixed score); cost frontier C(t, a) (the lowest cost per question among runs of models released by date t that score at least a); record (a run that sets that lowest cost); SOTA debut (the date a score level is first reached by any model); model-free rate (the frontier's own quarter-on-quarter fall, averaged over a lattice, with no fitted curve).

1. A price is dollars per question, at every budget

[[ah:data|Data]]

The cost of a run is the tokens it actually used, reasoning included, times the list price on the model's API, archived from OpenRouter; a few open-weight models with no API were run on rented GPUs and priced at that hardware cost, checked within 30% on five models that had both ({{in the article|ar:the discrepancy between our direct costs and API pricing}}). Per-token prices, which earlier estimates used, miss that a reasoning model can be cheap per token and dear per question ({{in the article|ar:it has become more problematic to ignore this distinction}}).

One run gives a whole cost curve. Following CAISI, a run's transcript is cut at a budget of X tokens per question: questions answered within X count as solved, the rest score the chance of guessing ({{in the article|ar:For any arbitrary per-question budget of X tokens}}). Sweeping X traces the model's cost against score without paying for new runs; telling models their budget gave about the same curves as lower effort settings ({{in the article|ar:Announcing a budget cap to a model traces}}). Scores are rescaled from the guessing floor, so GPQA's "75%" is 81.25% raw ({{in the article|ar:That number is actually 81.25%}}).

2. The frontier is a staircase for each score level

[[ah:the-challenge|The challenge]]

For each benchmark the record cost C(t, a) can only fall: a new model either undercuts it or leaves it alone ({{in the article|ar:The empirical cost frontier is the lowest cost}}). The staircase explorer (beside this section; below it on a phone) draws it from the authors' released runs: pick a benchmark and a level and the steps are the models that set each record. It opens on the article's example: o3 (high) first reached GPQA's 75% level at $0.30 a question; GPT-5.6 Luna (low) does it for $0.0004, 725 times less ({{in the article|ar:That is a 725-fold drop in the price of thought}}).

The steps are rare and big. A model that moves the frontier is sparse, one benchmark result can shift it for months, and the authors only tested models that looked near the frontier, so they treat the staircase as a noisy view of a true frontier ({{in the article|ar:A single benchmark result -- or a single missing one}}). Move the level: at GPQA's 25% the record fell 42% a quarter, at 50% 61%, at 75% 64% (each over the quarters since that level was first reached).

Predict first: the article dates o3 to 31 January 2025. Tick "corrected release dates" in the explorer: does the 725x get bigger, smaller, or happen faster?
