Responses, in date order

The memory analogy is weak once training is in the picture

a Hacker News commenter, with the author's reply, 17 October 2026, objection: Layers act on deep representations shaped by a loss, so treating Q, K and V as a lookup table says little about what trained layers do; attention is better seen as a kernel method. The author agreed the post is an intuition that ignores what gets written into the weights and the learning dynamics, and pointed to the papers in his closing list.

(3 min)

Random keys are white noise; real data has structure

two Hacker News commenters, 17 October 2026, objection: The capacities hold for random keys and values; real keys are correlated and real values are structured, which both helps (compression) and hurts (correlated keys interfere more). A second commenter adds that truly random key-value pairs would be served better by an exact associative array. The post states the first limit itself in its closing section.

(2 min)

Why not take the hard maximum?

a Hacker News commenter, answered in the thread, 17 October 2026, question: If higher powers suppress interference better, the limit is argmax over keys. The answer, already in the post: argmax is not differentiable, and a very large β saturates the softmax so the gradients to other keys vanish; hence β grows only as fast as M requires.

(2 min)

Undefined terms and notation

several Hacker News commenters, 17 October 2026, reader comment: The largest sub-thread: readers without linear algebra found d_k, ℝ^{d_k}, M and "similarity" not defined at first use and asked for a worked example with real data structures; others answered that the terms paragraph defines them. A reading-level complaint, not an error.

What it rests on

Attention Is All You Need, arXiv v2 (19 June 2017), appendix "Two Feed-Forward Layers = Attention over Parameters"

Vaswani et al. (maker): The passage the article cites: the FFN as attention with ReLU for softmax, and Table 5's two attention-over-parameters models (PPL 4.92 and 4.86, BLEU 25.5 and 25.9 against the base model's 4.92 and 25.8). In v2 and v3 only; gone from v4 (30 June 2017) on.

(5 min)

Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories

Geva, Schuster, Berant, Levy, 2021 (maker): The measurement behind the MLP-as-memory reading: keys in trained FFNs fire on recognisable input patterns, values push the next-token distribution.

(40 min)

Understanding Factual Recall in Transformers via Associative Memories

Nichani, Lee, Bietti, 2024 (maker): The article's footnote 3: when recall only has to pick the right answer from a fixed set (argmax), a d by d matrix holds about d² facts, not d.

(45 min)

Hopfield Networks is All You Need

Ramsauer et al., 2020 (maker): Softmax attention as the update rule of a modern Hopfield network with exponential storage capacity: the older form of the article's self-attention section.

(60 min)

Test-time regression: a unifying framework for designing sequence models with associative memory

Wang, Shi, Fox, 2025 (the author's own paper): The regression objective of the article's optimal-store section, used to derive linear attention, the delta rule and softmax attention as memories written during the forward pass.

(60 min)

Linear Transformers Are Secretly Fast Weight Programmers

Schlag, Irie, Schmidhuber, 2021 (maker): The outer-product store as the state of linear attention, its capacity limit when the sequence outgrows the key dimension, and the delta rule as the fix (footnote 2).
