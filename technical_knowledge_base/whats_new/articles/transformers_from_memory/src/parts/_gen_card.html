You could have invented transformers from memory

Alex Wang (independent; Stanford PhD, formerly Cartesia) · published 15 October 2026

In one line: An explainer (about 19 minutes, derivations, a NumPy script and an in-page slider) that reads a linear layer, an MLP and softmax attention as stores of key-value memories and ranks them by how much the other memories leak into a recall. For random unit keys and values at key dimension 64, queried with a stored key, the leak equals the recalled value at about 64 memories for an outer-product matrix, 128 for a least-squares matrix or ReLU MLP, about 2,700 for a squared ReLU and about 1.2 million for softmax at the usual sharpness; our independent run and the author's 5-seed data agree with each.

64 to 2,700 memories
capacity at key dimension 64: an outer-product matrix holds about d = 64 random memories before interference equals the signal; a squared-ReLU MLP about 2d²/3 = 2,731
derived in the article; ours: 67 and 2,853 measured (3 seeds) · in the article: introduction
1.2 million memories
softmax attention at the usual sharpness β = √64 = 8: capacity e^{2β(1 − β/d)} = e^14; the author's run crosses 1 at about 1.23 million
derived, then simulated to 2 million keys · in the article: Self-attention
0.004 at 2M
interference over signal with 2 million stored memories when the sharpness grows with log M (Su's and Qwen's rules, reference 1,024); without it the ratio passes 1 near 1.2 million
the author's simulation, 5 seeds; released data · in the article: Long context

How far to trust it: Believe the derivations and every number: we recomputed them from scratch and they agree with the author's released data. Read "capacity" as how many rows one readout can hold before crosstalk equals the signal, for random vectors queried exactly, not as how much a layer stores per parameter: the matrix holds its 64 memories in 4,096 numbers, while the MLP's 2,700 and attention's 1.2 million each take their own 128-number row.
