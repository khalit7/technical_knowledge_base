# Visuals: built and rejected

## Built
- **Fig. 1, hero: cost against fully correct runs, both efforts** (the post's own chart, redrawn from its labels), coloured by family, Default in the page's highlight colour, effort averages ringed, one 95% interval as a scale bar. It carries the page's main point in one picture: effort moves every condition, the instruction barely does.
- **Fig. 2, what the agents did**: every count the post reports from reading runs, as "did the named thing" against "the deeper step". It is the post's real evidence and is otherwise scattered over 30 sections.
- **Fig. 3, two bugs easy inputs cannot see**: four identical streams under a stream swap (the post's example), and the Number_of_Sequences formula against the agents' version over every count the 3-byte form can encode, calculated here (they agree only below 32,768). Shows why passing self-written tests meant little.
- **Fig. 4, toy: the real results beside no-effect worlds** (one variable: draw again; effort as a switch), with the exact 95% band and expected spread from recompute.py. Opens on the real data, so the prediction prompt beside it is not answered on screen.
- **Fig. 5, medium against xhigh per condition** (correlation -0.03): the rank-stability test in one picture.

## Rejected
- A forest plot of every condition's difference from Default with intervals: Fig. 4 shows the same intervals and the no-effect comparison, which is the stronger test; the p-values are in the prose.
- Redrawing the follow-up's Pandoc chart: its data is a compressed per-turn format with fitted curves; the Since block states its conclusions and the IMAP chart's numbers, recomputed.
- An ECC exposure-gradient chart: the post gives only two counts (7 never read, 9 late), a sentence says it.
- Any 3D view: nothing here has spatial structure.
