How well do agents use test/verification techniques?

Dan Luu (independent) · published 7 September 2026

In one line: On one task (a Rust Zstd decoder written from the format spec by GPT-5.6 Sol in Codex, single-turn, 80 runs per condition and effort, judged by 37 hidden tests), a one-line instruction to use one of 26 testing or verification techniques, or one of four testing skills, did not measurably raise the share of fully correct runs over no instruction (63.8% at medium effort, 81.2% at xhigh); reading the runs, agents mostly applied each technique superficially, and the two largest skills raised cost by up to 41%.

How far to trust it: Believe the transcript reading and the negative result: no instruction measurably helped, and the long tutorial-style skills really did cost more. Do not read the per-technique order (TDD near the bottom, the author's own skill at the top, Default "well above average") as findings: 80 runs a side detect only gaps of about 17 to 21 points, the 30 xhigh results are as alike as 30 draws of one coin, and a condition's medium score says nothing about its xhigh score. One model, one task, and no released prompts, transcripts or tests. The evidence, judged.
