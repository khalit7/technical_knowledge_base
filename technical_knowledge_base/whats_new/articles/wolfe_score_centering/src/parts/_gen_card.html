Score Centering: Understanding and Fixing Instability in RL

Cameron R. Wolfe (Netflix) · published 9 November 2026

In one line: A long explainer of Marek and Ryabinin's score-centering paper: when rollouts come from a sampler q that differs from the trainer p, the expected policy-gradient update at each prefix splits into drift (the expected reward times the trainer's expected score under the sampler, a pull toward the sampler that ignores which rollouts won) and signal (a covariance), and subtracting that expected score, computed from the sampler's top 128 log-probabilities, cancels drift without importance ratios; the evidence is the paper's, on deliberately severe mismatch with Qwen3-0.6B on Countdown and Qwen3-30B-A3B on math, measured as training accuracy.

How far to trust it: Believe the derivation: drift is exact algebra, the article states it correctly, and its code matches the paper's loss term by term. Read the experiments narrowly: deliberately severe mismatch, short sequences, plain SGD and training accuracy, mostly single seeds at 30B, where score centering ties truncated importance sampling at an FP4 cache and wins 30% to 12% only in the harshest setting; under heavy staleness it needs importance sampling, and a later paper finds its top-128 shortcut weaker than Monte Carlo centering. "Labs quietly use it" is Nathan Lambert's unsourced line; what is public is framework support (TRL's default, verl, slime, Miles, NVIDIA's Molt), not a lab's report. The evidence, judged.
