3. Offline training breaks with negative rewards; online training breaks with positive ones

[[ah:§exploring-instability-and-tim-in-rl|Exploring Instability (and TIM) in RL]]

The puzzle the paper starts from: supervised fine-tuning learns happily from data another model wrote, yet RL breaks under tiny numerical differences. Policy gradient with rewards +1/0 trained fully offline is exactly SFT on the successful samples (rejection sampling), so the two differences between SFT and RL are the reward's sign and whether the data is regenerated by the policy being trained ({{in the article|ar:We only have non-negative advantages}}).

The diagnostic: Qwen3-1.7B on Countdown with a sampler whose weights carry a fixed Gaussian offset, so the mismatch is controlled, under three reward modes, offline and online. The results point in opposite directions (table beside): offline is stable only with non-negative rewards, online is least stable with them and most stable with group-centred rewards ({{in the article|ar:completely opposite stability trends are observed}}).

Offline failure is the known one: a negative reward makes the objective unbounded, replaying failed completions keeps pushing their log-probabilities down, and the softmax hands the freed mass to tokens already likely (the "squeezing effect", {{in the article|ar:also known as the squeezing effect}}). Online sampling stops it by itself; the paper sets it aside.

Online failure needs a new explanation. Online training on +1/0 rewards under mismatch is distillation from a teacher that is a slightly biased copy of the student and is re-copied from it after every step; offline distillation from a fixed teacher is stable, so the moving teacher must be the cause ({{in the article|ar:creating a feedback loop where errors accumulate}}). Sections 4 to 6 make that precise.
