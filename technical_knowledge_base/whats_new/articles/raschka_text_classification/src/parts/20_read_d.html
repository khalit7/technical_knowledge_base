How much of this to believe

The history and the mechanics are right wherever we checked them. The accuracies match the linked notebooks, ULMFiT's 95.4% is its paper's 4.6% error, the Jev run is internally consistent (24,117 of 25,000; 15,456,663 tokens at $0.042 per million is $0.6492; the 14-token gap per review between Choice and Noul matches the example responses), and the RLCR rewards and Table 1(a) values are exact (closed block below). The author marks his guesses and the contamination risk, and changed the post within three days in answer to four reader comments.

  • The ladder mixes three test sets and single runs: one or two points between rows say little.
  • The Jev-like design and the "small ModernBERT" guess are not Jev. Measurements published twelve days earlier show Jev reads options jointly and has frontier-scale knowledge; TypeSafe says "neither small nor an LLM". The article does not cite them.
  • Calibration is the theme and is not tested on Jev. The benchmark it links shows Jev's ECE at 0.351 on emotion, worse than the open model; a later test found Jev at 1.00 on 58% of complaints while right on 90.2% of those. Hume found ECE 0.031 on 1,200 MMLU items: calibrated on some tasks, not all.
  • Small slips: Luna where TypeSafe names Terra; "Direct Policy Optimization"; temperature scaling put in the RLCR comparison; state-space models "processed sequentially" in training.

Verdict. The best single walk from bag-of-words to decision models, with a cheap, honest test of Jev on one task. Take its history and its RLCR explanation as written, its Jev-like recipe as a way to build a clone, and "calibrated" as the vendor's word until a reliability diagram on your own task says so.
