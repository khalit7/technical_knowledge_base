Jev's Architecture Unmasked

Archer Hume, 17 September 2026, earlier measurement: Twelve days before the article, from about 10,000 API calls: 84.6% on MMLU-Pro (frontier-scale knowledge); an irrelevant fifth option lowered the log-odds between two others from +0.38 to +0.11 in all ten blocks, so Jev reads its options together. The article does not cite it.

(28 min)

BTZSC pilot v1: Jev against GLiNER2.5

AbdelStark (jev-benchmarks), 17 September 2026, benchmark the article cites: Preregistered, 100 examples per dataset: Jev far more accurate on AG News (0.910 against 0.700) and Banking77 (0.870 against 0.610), tied on emotion with much worse probabilities (ECE 0.351 against 0.117). The article quotes it only as Jev being stronger.

Isn't cross-entropy already self-calibrating?

a reader, Substack comments, 29 September 2026, reader comment, answered: The author agreed it is, in expectation, cited Guo et al. on overfitting the log-likelihood, and wrote the answer into the post as Section 5.3 the same day.

(2 min)

Temperature scaling is brittle under shift

a reader, Substack comments, 3 October 2026, reader comment: A calibration researcher: one fitted temperature breaks under covariate shift, where an agent's branches lead, and a decision model must be sharp as well as calibrated; he cited his own SDM activations (arXiv 2509.12760). No reply by 27 November.

(2 min)

One pass for all options, or a 255-slot head?

two readers, Substack comments, 10 October 2026, reader comments: Section 4's design runs one forward pass per option; one reader proposed one input with a classification token per option, another a fixed 255-way head with unused slots masked. No reply by 27 November.

(2 min)

TF-IDF still beats Jev in many enterprise jobs. Here is where Jev wins.

Eugene Orlov and Olga Matveeva, 14 October 2026, measurement: Jev against TF-IDF and a fine-tuned mmBERT on human labels: behind on complaint issues (46.6% against 61.2%); 1.00 on 581 of 1,000 complaints but right on 90.2% of those, so no threshold reached 95%; one ordinary sentence moved 55% of its decisions. Jev won on checking AI answers and on unseen languages.
