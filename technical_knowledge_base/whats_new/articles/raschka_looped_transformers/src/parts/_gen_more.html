Responses, in date order

How concerned should we be about Astra's recurrent architecture?

Rauno Arike (LessWrong), 2 September 2026, earlier analysis: A week before the article: agrees a looped transformer is not an RNN and that a 32-layer model looped twice is no harder to monitor than a 64-layer one, and guesses three or four loops from Pachocki's "within a factor of two". Its worry is the dial: a loop count turned up later, when about 1.3 times the layers already doubles a model's no-CoT time horizon.

(10 min)

GPT-6 Astra can do a lot of multi-hop reasoning without chain of thought

Rohan Subramani (LessWrong), 9 September 2026, measurement: Hours before the article: 24 made-up multi-hop questions, at least 20 samples each, zero reasoning tokens. Astra was never worse than GPT-5.6 Sol and handled about 4 to 5 hops where Sol managed 2 to 3. Informal, by its author's account, but a measurement of Astra, which the article has none of.

(5 min)

Recurrent KV-cache sharing may undermine the bounded-depth argument for CoT monitorability

MBaert (LessWrong), 9 September 2026, argument and benchmark: The mechanism the article's cache section misses (section 8): if early passes read keys written by later passes of earlier tokens, as in the Huginn paper's cache sharing, hidden depth grows with the answer. A follow-up mental-arithmetic benchmark put Astra's 50% horizon about 4 times Sol's, with the caveat that Astra shortcuts some tasks statistically.

(8 min)

Comment thread under the article

readers and the author (Substack), 10 September 2026, author's replies: A reader quoted the system card's controllability numbers (93% against 48%); the author answered that needing less reasoning is efficiency, and the reader replied that the card measures length and controllability separately. Another asked about early exits and missing keys (section 3); a third pointed to OpenAI's ARC-AGI-3 harness post, which the author added on 2 October.

(20 min)

GPT-6.1 Sol nearly matches the no-CoT performance of GPT-6 Astra

Rauno Arike (LessWrong), 30 September 2026, measurement: On 27 no-CoT tasks, GPT-6.1 Sol closes 80% (95% CI 72 to 87%) of the gap from GPT-6 Sol to Astra. His explanation, unconfirmed, is that 6.1 Sol is a smaller looped model; if so, its Astra-like controllability weakly suggests the architecture does matter for monitorability.

(5 min)

Looping a transformer increases no-CoT capabilities more than general capabilities

Alek Westover and Nick Kuhn (Alignment Forum), 12 November 2026, experiment: The first test of the article's question on an open looped model, NVIDIA's Dory 4B: 1 to 8 loops raised no-CoT reasoning (NCRI) by about 18 points while general ability (mini-ECI) moved at most about 1.5, worth about 2 NCRI points on the trend. In recurrent cache mode, under RL against a CoT monitor, it learned to solve tasks behind almost all-filler chains. One small model, single seeds, no matched non-looped control; Neel Nanda's comment finds most of the jump in 2 of 19 task domains.

What it rests on

GPT-6 Astra system card: CoT controllability and monitorability

OpenAI (maker): What the article summarises in two sentences: controllability 60.9% against 16.1% for GPT-5.6 Sol (chains of 750 to 1,250 tokens), UK AISI's 93% against 48% and no-CoT math time horizon of 30.9 minutes against 3.6, and OpenAI's view that controllability is not differentially due to architecture.

(30 min)

OpenAI Astra and Looped Transformers

Sebastian Raschka, 2 September 2026: The short note the article grew from ("Let's debunk this a bit"), with Nanbeige's 75% token efficiency, which the article leaves out.

(4 min)

Nanbeige4.2-3B: Unlocking Agentic Capabilities in a Compact Model

Nanbeige LLM Lab, 2026 (maker): The running example. Section 2.1: two passes keep about 75% of a standard model's token efficiency; a shared KV cache halves the cache but gains less; training looped from scratch beats upcycling.

(40 min)

Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach

Geiping et al., 2025 (maker): Huginn, the design The Information compared Astra to. Its KV-cache sharing note is the source of the recurrent-depth argument the article does not cover.

(40 min)

Mixture-of-Recursions: Learning Dynamic Recursive Depths for Adaptive Token-Level Computation

Bae et al., 2025 (maker): Routed loop counts per token, 135M to 1.7B parameters, and a KV-sharing variant that reuses the first recursion's keys.

(35 min)

SMELT: Scaling Laws for Compute-Matched MoE Looped Transformers

Shaowen Wang et al., 2026 (maker): Looping the middle half twice while matching per-token FLOPs, non-embedding parameters and KV cache: 6.8 to 18.0% of training FLOPs saved on the compute-optimal frontier, up to 54B.

(35 min)

Full-bandwidth transformer

Xi Wang et al., 2026 (maker): Latent feedback from one token's top layer to the next token's input at 1B: shorter reasoning in the base model, an effect that disappears after instruction tuning.

(30 min)

Beyond Parameters: Exploring Virtual Logic Depth for Scaling Laws

Zhu et al., 2025 (maker): Reusing weights leaves knowledge capacity nearly unchanged at fixed parameters but improves multi-step reasoning: the article's section 6.2.
