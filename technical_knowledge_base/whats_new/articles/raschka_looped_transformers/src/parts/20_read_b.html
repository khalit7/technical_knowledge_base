2. A looped transformer reuses blocks: weights saved, compute and cache not

[[ah:§21-reusing-transformer-blocks|Sections 2.1 and 2.2]]

The hidden states go through the same blocks more than once, and {{the weights stay the same across these passes|ar:the weights stay the same across these passes}}. Nanbeige4.2-3B (July 2026) applies its 22 blocks twice: application 23 uses block 1's weights, and 44 applications come from 22 blocks (the figure at the top, the article's Figures 5 and 6). Its report found two passes the best trade-off; more gained little and destabilised training.

Looping saves storage of the blocks, nothing else. The embedding and output tables are outside the saving; the article puts them at {{embedding and output layers make up ~25%|ar:embedding and output layers make up ~25%}} of "the total 3B" and 12.5% if tied. From the config they are 1.02B of 4.17B, 24.5% ("3B" counts non-embedding parameters only), and tying would leave 13.9%.

Compute and KV cache stay those of the deeper model, the article's most useful point. Every pass is real work in the forward pass and in backpropagation, {{similarly expensive as having 44 distinct blocks|ar:similarly expensive as having 44 distinct blocks}}. The second pass sees different hidden states, so its keys and values need their own cache: {{there are no KV cache-related savings either|ar:there are no KV cache-related savings either}}. Nanbeige {{tried sharing the KV cache between passes|ar:tried sharing the KV cache between passes}}, halved the cache and lost quality, and found training looped from scratch better than converting a trained model. Left out, though his 2 September note quoted it: two passes keep only about 75% of a standard model's token efficiency. A Hacker News commenter added that looping saves memory capacity, not bandwidth, since each pass streams the weights again. Cache sizing is on {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}}.

3. How many passes, and who decides

[[ah:§23-universal-transformers-and-flexible-loop-counts|Sections 2.3 to 2.5]]

The count can be fixed or chosen per token. The Universal Transformer halts a position when a learned halting probability sums past a threshold; Ouro-2.6B-Thinking loops 48 blocks four times with a learned exit gate, though its released code {{computes all configured passes before selecting an output|ar:computes all configured passes before selecting an output}} (confirmed in modeling_ouro.py); Mixture-of-Recursions {{uses a small, learned router|ar:uses a small, learned router}} that reads each token in context and assigns one to three passes. The mechanisms are drawn on {{Test-time compute and reasoning|n:4035c17b0d0d813f9fb2d62cff1f09ef}}, section 9.

Whether it pays depends on scale. In the Mixture-of-Recursions plot at equal training compute, {{At the smallest model scale, the regular transformer performs best|ar:At the smallest model scale, the regular transformer performs best}}, and the routed model catches up or wins at the larger scales, up to 1.7B, partly by seeing more tokens. Hence his rule that looping helps {{if the model is large enough|ar:if the model is large enough}}: a reading of one figure. In the comments, asked what a later token reads when an earlier one skipped passes, he gave the paper's two answers (attend only to tokens still active, or reuse the first recursion's keys). Which keys a pass reads turns out to be the crux of the monitoring question (section 8).

4. Not an RNN: recurrence over depth, not over tokens

[[ah:§3-side-note-recurrent-neural-networks-rnns|Section 3]]

An RNN carries a hidden state from one token to the next; a looped transformer carries a token's state through the same blocks again, and tokens still talk only through attention. In the article's words, {{RNNs reuse their weights across time steps|ar:RNNs reuse their weights across time steps}}, while looping is across depth: best thought of as a bigger model with shared weights. That holds with a cache per pass; section 8 shows the cache arrangement, described in the Huginn paper and available on NVIDIA's Dory, that puts an RNN-like path back.

5. Does Astra loop? Probably, he says, but that is not why it is good

[[ah:§4-does-astra-even-use-looped-transformers|Section 4]]

The architecture is a report, not a disclosure, and the article says so: The Information's claim {{is still just a rumor or scoop|ar:this is still just a rumor or scoop}}. He still rates it highly likely, from the reporting, the technique's published promise, and Jakub Pachocki's statement that OpenAI's frontier models, Astra included, have a computation graph within a factor of two of GPT-4's depth (which, he grants, could also mean twice the ordinary blocks). Where Astra's quality comes from is his opinion, labelled as one: {{likely primarily due to other reasons, namely improved training recipes|ar:likely primarily due to other reasons, namely improved training recipes}} and data.

6. Does looping hide the chain of thought? His answer: no more than a bigger model would

[[ah:§5-hiding-chains-of-thought|Section 5]]

For users nothing changed: {{OpenAI has been hiding (most of) the reasoning traces|ar:OpenAI has been hiding (most of) the reasoning traces}} since o1. The question is whether developers can still read the chain. Written reasoning tokens {{work as a scratch pad|ar:these intermediate steps work as a scratch pad}}; looping adds computation inside each token instead, so a looped model may need fewer of them.

The argument: fewer tokens is what capability looks like. At a fixed accuracy, OpenAI's chart shows {{it is true that GPT-6 Astra uses fewer tokens|ar:it is true that GPT-6 Astra uses fewer tokens}} than GPT-5.6 Sol. Within one family, with no looping, {{Luna uses 80% more tokens than Sol|ar:Luna uses 80% more tokens than Sol}} at similar scores, and nobody calls Sol less interpretable. Chains were never guaranteed faithful, so {{the only valid concern is that looped transformers purposefully mislead|ar:the only valid concern is that looped transformers purposefully mislead}} more often, which he sees no evidence for. The system card's reduced monitorability he reads as {{mostly associated with shorter, less informative traces|ar:mostly associated with shorter, less informative traces}}. Pachocki's clarification closes the section:

"{{I want to prevent a race into unmonitorability|ar:I want to prevent a race into unmonitorability}} kicked off by confused reporting. [...] I do think it is fragile and unfortunately trending in a negative direction, {{for reasons not contingent on architecture changes|ar:for reasons not contingent on architecture changes}} that I will write about soon."

7. The research: where extra passes help

[[ah:§6-looped-transformer-research|Section 6 and the conclusion]]

Huginn (2025) makes the loop count a test-time dial: {{the loop count is randomly sampled|ar:the loop count is randomly sampled}} in training, so at inference it runs 8 to 64 loops, and HellaSwag levels off after about eight while GSM8K and HumanEval keep gaining (to scale on {{Test-time compute and reasoning|n:4035c17b0d0d813f9fb2d62cff1f09ef}}). Looping buys reasoning, not knowledge: at fixed parameters {{looping leaves the amount of stored information nearly unchanged|ar:looping leaves the amount of stored information nearly unchanged}} while multi-step maths improves (Zhu et al., 2025), the split the later responses measure. At matched compute it still wins a little: SMELT keeps per-token compute, non-embedding parameters and KV cache equal ({{they narrow the hidden dimension to compensate|ar:they narrow the hidden dimension to compensate}} and add experts back) and its scaling laws, up to 54B, save 6.8 to 18% of training compute. Latent feedback across tokens shortened reasoning, then lost the effect: in the 1B full-bandwidth transformer {{the shortening effect disappears after instruction tuning|ar:the shortening effect disappears after instruction tuning}}, with no comparison against simply enlarging the model.

The conclusion restates the thesis: Astra probably loops, {{Looped transformers simply give better modeling performance at a fixed compute budget|ar:Looped transformers simply give better modeling performance at a fixed compute budget}}, and shorter traces are what a capable model does, as {{a smart and well-prepared student|ar:a smart and well-prepared student}} needs less scrap paper.
