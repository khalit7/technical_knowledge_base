    Rauno Arike (LessWrong), 2 September 2026

    A week before the article: agrees a looped transformer is not an RNN and that a 32-layer model looped twice is no harder to monitor than a 64-layer one, and guesses three or four loops from Pachocki's "within a factor of two". Astra was never worse than GPT-5.6 Sol and handled about 4 to 5 hops where Sol managed 2 to 3. Informal, by its author's account, but a measurement of Astra, which the article has none of.

  • argument and benchmarkRecurrent KV-cache sharing may undermine the bounded-depth argument for CoT monitorability
    MBaert (LessWrong), 9 September 2026

    The mechanism the article's cache section misses (section 8): if early passes read keys written by later passes of earlier tokens, as in the Huginn paper's cache sharing, hidden depth grows with the answer. A follow-up mental-arithmetic benchmark put Astra's 50% horizon about 4 times Sol's, with the caveat that Astra shortcuts some tasks statistically.

  • measurementGPT-6.1 Sol nearly matches the no-CoT performance of GPT-6 Astra
    Rauno Arike (LessWrong), 30 September 2026

    On 27 no-CoT tasks, GPT-6.1 Sol closes 80% (95% CI 72 to 87%) of the gap from GPT-6 Sol to Astra. His explanation, unconfirmed, is that 6.1 Sol is a smaller looped model; if so, its Astra-like controllability weakly suggests the architecture does matter for monitorability.

  • experimentLooping a transformer increases no-CoT capabilities more than general capabilities
    Alek Westover and Nick Kuhn (Alignment Forum), 12 November 2026

    The first test of the article's question on an open looped model, NVIDIA's Dory 4B: 1 to 8 loops raised no-CoT reasoning (NCRI) by about 18 points while general ability (mini-ECI) moved at most about 1.5, worth about 2 NCRI points on the trend. In recurrent cache mode, under RL against a CoT monitor, it learned to solve tasks behind almost all-filler chains. One small model, single seeds, no matched non-looped control; Neel Nanda's comment finds most of the jump in 2 of 19 task domains.
