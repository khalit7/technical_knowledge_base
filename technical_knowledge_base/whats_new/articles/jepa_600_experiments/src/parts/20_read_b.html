1. The predictor is not the bottleneck; the search horizon is

[[ar:how much LeWM is held back|The opening and section 1]]

Swapping LeWM's predictor for the simulator's exact physics changes almost nothing. With an expert frame as subgoal every 25 steps, on 250 tasks with the goal 75 steps away, LeWM succeeds 78% and the simulator 77% ({{in the article|ar:The version with true physics succeeded 77%}}): a tie, since two seeds of one planner disagree on 15% of tasks.

Far goals fail because of the search, not compounding error. LeWM as published solves 22% of 75-step tasks but 89.7% of 25-step ones, within a point of the simulator ({{in the article|ar:within a point of the exact-physics version}}). Exact physics, which cannot drift, lifts one 75-step search only to 42%; a subgoal every 25 steps lifts LeWM to 80% (the bars). CEM cannot find a 75-step plan with or without a correct model, and four planner runs added under a point ({{in the article|ar:the four runs almost always succeeded}}).

2. Subgoals from a graph of the model's own memories

[[ah:2-a-latent-memory-graph-with-a-cost-on-every-jum|2. A latent memory graph]]

The memory graph replaces the expert's subgoals. LeWM's encoder embeds every frame of its training demonstrations, about 460,000 states, each linked to its successor and to nearby states of other demonstrations; a shortest path from the current state to the goal gives the subgoals ({{in the article|ar:searches this graph for a path}}).

A cost on every jump took it from 62% to 85%, the expert-subgoal score ({{in the article|ar:adding a cost on every jump}}). With free jumps the search chained them into routes the block could not follow (318 of 1,015 plans used six or more). Nearness in embedding space is not a move the pusher can make (the toy). The idea is Search on the Replay Buffer (Eysenbach et al., 2019), which the post does not cite.

3. The standard test is easier than it looks

[[ah:the-standard-push-t-test-is-easier-than-it-looks|The standard Push-T test is easier than it looks]]

85% flatters the method, as the author shows. Test goals lie further along the start's own demonstration, and all demonstrations end in one place, so for 84% of goals the graph held a nearly identical state ({{in the article|ar:the memory graph already held a nearly}}). Further: LeWM's evaluation samples its tasks from pusht_expert_train, the training set (config/eval/pusht.yaml), so the test demonstrations are in the graph unless removed, which the post does not say.

The sample is also small and noisy: 68% on the official 50 episodes, 44% on 250 fresh ones; a new seed flipped 38 of 250 ({{in the article|ar:flipped 38 of 250 episodes}}). On the harder test the memory graph falls from 75% to 45% ({{in the article|ar:succeeds 75% of the time on standard}}).

4. Fill gaps with rotated and mirrored memories

[[ah:3-fill-gaps-in-memory-with-rotated-and-mirrored-|3. Fill gaps in memory]]

The graph is only as good as its coverage. Deleting the stored states near the goal dropped standard success from 93% to 32% ({{in the article|ar:success fell from 93% to 32%}}). Push-T's physics is unchanged by shifting, rotating or mirroring the table, so transformed segments from elsewhere can fill the gap: back to 85%, and 1% of the demonstrations plus copies did about as well as 10%. On the harder test copies help most where the toy needs jumps: 45% to 56% overall, 27% to 46% against the flow.

5. The goal image also scores the pusher

[[ah:4-score-goals-on-the-block-alone|4. The cost was "wrong"]]

A goal image shows the pusher somewhere, so a planner matching the whole image tries to put the pusher there too. In one run the robot reached the block's target, then {{pushed it away|ar:Then it pushed the block away}} chasing the pusher's goal position.

With the model removed, the effect is large. The simulator planner ran on the same 50 harder-test tasks with a cost on block and pusher, then on the block alone: 9 against 39 successes, 30 tasks flipping one way and none the other (the table), a split with chance probability 2 × 0.530, about 1.9 in a billion. LeWM's goal embedding mixes block and pusher, so its workaround scores each prediction against 16 copies of the goal with the pusher moved and keeps the closest: 11% to 23% on the harder test ({{in the article|ar:from 11% to 23%, without retraining}}).

6. What comes next: subgoals beyond the data

[[ah:what-comes-next|What comes next]]

The open problem is goals no demonstration leads to: the best planner reaches 56% on the harder test, failing mostly against the flow. The author wants a high level that proposes its own subgoals, better than H-JEPA, which "match or slightly beat flat LeWM" on Push-T ({{in the article|ar:which so far match or slightly}}).
