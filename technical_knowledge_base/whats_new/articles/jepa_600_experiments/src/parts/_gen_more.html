Responses, in date order

Why take test goals from the demonstrations at all?

robotresearcher, Hacker News, 13 November 2026, critique: Even the harder test takes goals from demonstrations; the real task is any pose to any pose. Asks for independently sampled goals. No reply.

(2 min)

Deleting the memories near the goal broke the planner

nightpool and light_hue_1, Hacker News, 13 November 2026, critique: The fall from 93% to 32% shows the method needs a stored state near the goal: "more or less testing on the training set".

(1 min)

Planning through subgoals is known work, not a discovery

reasonabl_human, Hacker News, 13 November 2026, critique: Subgoal graphs over stored states are established (Search on the Replay Buffer); suggests a title naming LeWM and Push-T.

(2 min)

Would a classical planner solve this without a world model?

pj_mukh and whiplash451, Hacker News, 13 November 2026, question: Would probabilistic roadmaps, or a Gumbel MCTS repository that solves Push-T without training, do as well? Not compared on the post's tasks.

(1 min)

Latent planning is model-predictive control; TD-MPC also learns the cost

hazrmard and ainch, Hacker News, 14 November 2026, context: Latent planning is MPC with a learned model; TD-MPC also predicts the reward, so it ranks plans better.

(1 min)

What it rests on

LeWorldModel: Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels

Maes, Le Lidec, Scieur, LeCun, Balestriero, arXiv 2603.19312 (third-party): LeWM: a 15M-parameter JEPA trained from pixels with two loss terms (prediction and SIGReg); 96.0% on Push-T over 50 tasks with the goal 25 steps away, CEM planner, receding horizon of 25 steps. Its limitations section already names short horizons and dataset coverage.

(45 min)

lucas-maes/le-wm and the stable-worldmodel library

LeWM authors (maker), MIT licence: config/eval/pusht.yaml: 50 episodes from pusht_expert_train, seed 42, goal 25 steps ahead, 50-step budget. The Push-T success test (stable-worldmodel, envs/pusht/env.py) needs the pusher and the block together within 20 pixels and the block within 20 degrees.

(20 min)

LeJEPA: Provable and Scalable Self-Supervised Learning Without the Heuristics

Balestriero and LeCun, arXiv 2511.08544 (third-party): Where SIGReg, the regulariser that keeps LeWM's embeddings from collapsing, comes from.

(1 hour)

H-JEPA: End-to-End Learning of Hierarchical World Models for Visual Planning

Zhang, Terver, Rabbat, LeCun, Balestriero, arXiv 2610.06805 (third-party): The learned hierarchy the post names as today's alternative: on Push-T two levels match or exceed LeWM, three levels fall back because Push-T's short episodes leave too few training clips. Its Push-T tasks use held-out expert frames 75 steps apart.

(50 min)

Search on the Replay Buffer: Bridging Planning and Reinforcement Learning

Eysenbach, Salakhutdinov, Levine, NeurIPS 2019 (third-party): The memory-graph idea in 2019: a graph over stored states, edges weighted by a learned distance, shortest path as waypoints for a short-horizon controller.

(40 min)

Semi-parametric Topological Memory for Navigation

Savinov, Dosovitskiy, Koltun, ICLR 2018 (third-party): An earlier graph of observations from a walkthrough, with learned shortcut edges and subgoals for a local policy.

(35 min)

DINO-WM: World Models on Pre-trained Visual Features enable Zero-shot Planning

Zhou, Pan, LeCun, Pinto, arXiv 2411.04983 (third-party): The Push-T dataset, success test and planning setup LeWM inherits.

(40 min)

What Drives Success in Physical Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models?

arXiv 2512.24497 (third-party): A systematic study of the design choices in planning with JEPA world models, the paper-scale counterpart of the post's experiments (listed, not read for this page).
