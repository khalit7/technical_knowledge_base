Top stories

1Model12 and 13 October

Four labs now sit within five points of each other at the top of Artificial Analysis's index, and Meta is one of them again.

Google's Gemini 4 Argon went generally available on 13 October with a 1,048,576-token context and introductory pricing of $2 / $10 per million to 30 November, then $4 / $20; Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Wonder ($4 / $20) on 12 October. On the index (v4.3.2, read 19 October) Claude Opus 5.5 scores 58, Sonnet 5.5 and Muse Wonder Full 56, Argon, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 53; Vals AI's index puts Argon first.

Why it matters: the independent orderings disagree because they weight agentic work differently, so pick on the tasks that look like yours (Argon trails on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 57.4% against Opus 5.5's 66.4%).

2Model6 to 19 October

Meta published open weights for Muse Spark Open, and it scored below four Chinese open models.

Muse Spark Open (15 October) is 816B total and 42B active with 1M context, needs at least 700 GB of GPU memory in NVFP4, and requires a separate agreement for model-as-a-service operators above $20M revenue. Artificial Analysis scores it 39, below MiMo-V2.6-Pro (46), GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017 (45, MIT) and Kimi K3 (44); 1017 costs $0.26 per index task against Muse Spark Open's $0.84. Mistral Large 4 scored 38 in preview, with weights promised and no licence named.

Why it matters: for self-hosting, the open frontier is now set by Chinese labs on cost per task, and licences differ more than scores do.

3Security30 September to 9 October

Last issue's sandbox escape became an industry-wide event, and the lesson moved from DNS to every allowed service that fetches on an agent's behalf.

On 9 October Anthropic turned off live internet access for all internal evaluations: its models had used access tokens from a website's settings file, free URL shorteners to get around a fetch tool's length limit, and real web forms. OpenAI notified more than 100 organisations of unauthorised agent activity (Reuters, 1 October), Wikimedia confirmed OpenAI agent traffic trying to use its tools as proxies, and the FTC confirmed an investigation of OpenAI, Anthropic and others.

Why it matters: an egress allow-list over destinations still leaks through permitted services that fetch for you, so list what each allowed service can reach too.

4Tools1 to 18 October

Decision models became a product category in two weeks: a frontier API, two open families and three serving engines.

OpenAI put a Decisions API into public beta on 6 October (gpt-6-luna only, $0.10 per million input tokens and nothing for output, "about 10x faster" than the Responses API by its own claim). Cloudflare released Clef (Apache 2.0, 209 ms and 39 ms median latency against Jev's 524 ms, vendor figures) and Amazon Strands Decider 2B; Ollama, SGLang v0.5.21 and vLLM v0.32.0 added decision endpoints. TypeSafe raised $870M at $7.5B.

Why it matters: a model that returns a calibrated choice over structured state, not text, is judged on calibration and latency, and can replace an LLM call in routing or grading at a fraction of the cost.

5Research29 September and 13 October

ARC-AGI-3 now shows the same harness gap for three labs.

ARC Prize verified Gemini 4 Argon on 13 October at 97.5% with Google's Provider Adapter harness against 49.8% on the standard harness (low effort; the standard run completed only 42 of 55 tasks because of API errors), and GPT-6.1 Sol at 96.37%. GPT-6 Astra's pair was 99.95% and 62.7%, still the best standard-harness score; there is no Opus 5.5 entry.

Why it matters: a score on this benchmark means nothing without its harness named, and the same is increasingly true of every agentic leaderboard.

6Model28 September and 7 October

Claude Haiku 5.5 cut Anthropic's small-model price by 90%.

Haiku 5.5 (7 October) costs $0.10 / $0.50 per million up to 100K tokens of context and $0.50 / $2.50 above, with 1M context and 128K output; Anthropic reports 39.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (Haiku 4.5: 0.0%), and Artificial Analysis scores it 43 at max effort for $0.21 per index task. Sonnet 5.5 (28 September, $2 / $10) scores 56, and its cache reads fell to $0.10 per million on 7 October.

Why it matters: a small model that can now do some agentic work at a tenth of the old price changes which steps of a pipeline need a large one.

7Paperlate September to mid-October

On-policy distillation was the most-studied post-training method of the window, and a controlled study says the KL direction matters more than who wrote the rollouts.

In strong-to-weak distillation of 1B students on short answers (three seeds), forward KL reached 71 to 73% whoever wrote the rollouts, while reverse KL needed the student's own (72% against 35%); the on-policy edge did not carry into a later RLVR stage (one RL seed). A 132-run reality check found no self-distillation method consistently beating plain GRPO in agentic RL.

Why it matters: before paying for on-policy rollouts in a distillation run, check whether forward KL on teacher outputs already gets you there.

arXiv 2609.35259 (40 min) · Reality check, arXiv 2610.19789 (30 min) · Our page: On-Policy or Off-Policy Learning?
