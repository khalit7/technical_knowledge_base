Top stories

1Model27 and 28 October

Artificial Analysis rebuilt its index, and Google's Gemini 4 Carbon took the top of it at 59, though access starts with vetted cyber defenders.

Intelligence Index v5 (27 October) is a new scale, which Artificial Analysis says is 60% private test sets; on it Carbon scores 59 against Claude Opus 5.5's 58 and GPT-6 Astra's 57, while its AA-Omniscience hallucination rate rose from Gemini 4 Argon's 4% to 11%. Carbon costs $2 / $10 per million tokens at first, $4 / $20 later. The rebuild also widened the open-weight gap: from 3 points on v4.3.2 to 12 at v5's launch, 13 with Carbon.

Why it matters: scores from before and after 27 October are on different scales, and the best open model is now about a dozen points behind, not three.

2Research26 October

OpenAI and Anthropic each published a machine-checked refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis on the same day.

Anthropic's repository credits an internal Claude research model with a deterministic algorithm counting 3-SAT solutions in time 2^O(n log log n / sqrt(log n)), with a Lean 4 formalization that builds against Mathlib; Anthropic says it is purely asymptotic and that "no machine checks that this file says what the paper says". OpenAI's claim (read through Lance Fortnow and the press) is being checked by outside working groups. Neither had been independently verified by 2 November.

Why it matters: models now produce results that compile in Lean before any expert has read them, so what the formal statement says becomes the part to check.

3Industry26 to 29 October

Anthropic's S-1 put a frontier lab's economics on the record, and the hyperscalers' quarters showed who pays for the compute.

Anthropic filed on 26 October to list on Nasdaq as ANT: revenue of $16,286M in the first half of 2026 ($11,555M in Q2), a first-half net loss of $80,748M that is mostly non-cash, and compute commitments of at least about $114.5B to Google and $110.0B to Amazon. Google Cloud grew 103% to $30.7B, but Alphabet's capital spending ($51.4B) exceeded its operating cash flow ($42.3B); AWS grew 39% with trailing free cash flow of minus $42.4B.

Why it matters: the price of the models you call is set by labs losing money on compute they have committed to for years.

4Security29 and 30 October

A tampered Claude Code extension and a platform breach went after the same prize: the AI keys on a developer's machine and around it.

Builds labelled Claude Code 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 sat on Cursor's extension marketplace for about 11 hours on 29 October; Cursor says Anthropic did not build them. On load they sent host details and environment-variable names to an attacker, added an SSH key and installed persistence, so uninstalling is not enough. On 30 October Northflank told customers to "assume environment variables were decrypted", and users reported stored AI keys being used. No count of affected users has been published.

Why it matters: after LiteLLM the week before, the target is consistent: wherever a provider key sits, on a laptop, in a proxy or on a platform.

Cursor: security notice (6 min) · Phorion: analysis (10 min) · Northflank status (4 min) · Our page: Agent security
5Security26 October

Cloudflare disclosed that one customer's prompts decoded against another customer's KV cache.

155 GLM-5.3 responses on Workers AI exposed other customers' data after oversized prompts hit a KV-cache capacity mismatch and an SGLang error path let a rejected request keep decoding against stale cache pages.

Why it matters: on shared inference the KV cache is customer data, and an error path in the serving engine can leak it across tenants.

Cloudflare: KV cache exposure (12 min) · Our page: Inference and serving
6Model29 October

Mistral published the weights of Mistral Large 4 under a licence that reserves serving it as a service.

About 1T total and about 49B active parameters, 128 routed experts with 4 active plus 1 shared, 1M context, and a speculative-decoding draft head shipped inside the checkpoint; vLLM measured about 2.9 accepted tokens per step and about 2x output throughput on 8 GPUs. Mistral, citing Artificial Analysis, reports 26 on Index v5, the highest it says among Western open-weight models (Chinese open models score higher).

Why it matters: you can fine-tune and sell it, but offering it as a hosted model needs a separate commercial licence.
