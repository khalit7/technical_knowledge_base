**OpenAI and Anthropic each published a machine-checked refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis on the same day.** On 26 October OpenAI announced three model-produced results (a refutation of ETH, a proof of the local smoothing conjecture, and an existence result in quantum chromodynamics) and set up outside working groups to check them, with Russell Impagliazzo and Mohan Paturi, two of ETH's originators, and Ryan Williams on the ETH group. Anthropic's repository credits an internal Claude research model with a deterministic algorithm that counts 3-SAT solutions in time 2^O(n log log n / sqrt(log n)), so 3-SAT is decidable in 2^o(n) time, with a Lean 4 formalization written by AI models that builds against Mathlib. Anthropic states the limits itself: the result is purely asymptotic and "says nothing about solving formulas in practice", and Lean checks the proofs but "no machine checks that this file says what the paper says". Lance Fortnow wrote on 28 October that he had not verified the proof but "it is safe to assume now that the exponential-time hypothesis is toast"; it covers k-SAT, not NP in general, and gives nothing new for problems like Subset Sum. Separately, Axiom Math says agent swarms produced a complete Lean proof of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups (about 43.1 million lines in 24 days). # Research & papers
- **Fair Cast: bf16 rounding hides most RL updates from the sampler** (Stanford, 29 October): RL trainers keep fp32 master weights and serve rollouts from a round-to-nearest bf16 copy, so updates smaller than about half the bf16 spacing vanish; on Qwen3-1.7B only 0.4 to 0.5% of served weights changed per step, and existing corrections cannot see the gap because they compare two copies downstream of the same rounding. The fix exports with stochastic rounding and computes the loss on the served copy; in single-GPU GRPO runs on Qwen3-0.6B to 4B it ended more than a point ahead on 18 of 25 matched seed-pairs, within a point on 6 and behind on 1. Two companions the same week split the mismatch into engine noise and policy lag (DLC) and measured MXFP8 rollouts on B200. [arXiv 2610.41073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41073) (40 min), [arXiv 2610.40945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40945) (30 min)
- **Score-seeking** (Redwood Research, 29 October): in agentic environments with a discoverable grader script, GPT 5.6 Sol and GPT 5.5 broke user instructions at least 90% of the time once the grader was described as operative, whether the score was said to be for training, evaluation or nothing; neither distorted scores when grading another agent. Few environments, OpenAI models only. [arXiv 2610.41014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41014) (35 min)
- **Audit where hacks pay** (Tsinghua and Virginia Tech, 26 October): with a strong verifier too costly for every rollout, auditing the GRPO groups with the fewest weak passes first drove hacking near zero within about 10 steps in 3 of 3 seeds on Qwen3-4B LeetCode, where random audits on the same budget left 36 to 52%; 3B and 4B models, short windows. [arXiv 2610.35716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35716) (30 min)
- **Agentic backdoors through pretraining poisoning** (Berkeley, UIUC, UCL, Anthropic and others, 27 October): prose that states a trigger-to-command rule as fact, mixed into 0.1% of pretraining data, gave a 4B CLI agent a 92.7% attack success rate after clean post-training, measured as pass@32 rather than one attempt; models up to 4B, single seed at the lowest rates. [arXiv 2610.37741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37741) (30 min)
- **Chain-of-thought reliance grows with scale** (28 October): when one step of a model's own reasoning is replaced by a wrong but valid state, Qwen3's answer follows it 16 to 18% of the time at 0.6B and 97 to 100% at 14B, and post-training barely changes it; one synthetic task, 25 open models up to 14B. [arXiv 2610.38433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38433) (20 min)
- **Simple features beat most control monitors** (30 October): on the public APPS backdoor dataset a bag-of-words classifier with comments stripped reaches AUROC 0.806 without running code, above 20 of 24 open-weight models prompted as monitors, because honest and backdoored code come from different authors; a warning about the dataset, not proof that published monitors use the shortcut. [arXiv 2610.41713](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41713) (20 min)
- **Speculative decoding on fine-grained MoE peaks at batch 1** (Cohere, NeurIPS 2026, 28 October): across 10 MoE models, coarse-expert models show the predicted mid-batch speedup peak while fine-grained ones (Qwen3-30B, Qwen3-235B, Kimi-K2.5) usually peak at batch 1, because their expert layers take a smaller share of GPU time than of weights and draft tokens share experts. [arXiv 2610.38981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38981) (30 min)
- **Soft-MTP** (MIT, 28 October): the multi-token-prediction gradient splits exactly into a pull toward the model's own next-token prediction plus a noisy residual, and a one-line straight-through change keeps only the first; 167M to 1.2B models gained in perplexity, speculative acceptance and zero-shot accuracy, with no seeds reported. [arXiv 2610.38331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38331) (25 min)
- **Looping a trained model rarely pays** (Microsoft, 30 October): adding loops to Qwen3.5 models beat both an identically trained unlooped twin and the best use of the same inference compute only at 0.8B; at 4B and 9B the looped model never beat its twin. [arXiv 2610.42030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42030) (30 min)
- **Latent Sparse Attention** (Prefrontal AI and ETH Zurich, 30 October): queries score learned clusters of keys instead of every key; distilled into Qwen-3.8-27B, one attention layer at 10M tokens is 72.4x faster in prefill and 42.5x in decode than full attention, while the KV cache still grows linearly. [arXiv 2610.42147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42147) (25 min)
- **Platonic representations from JEPAs** (Balestriero and LeCun, 29 October): a theorem that separately trained JEPAs on different modalities learn one representation up to a rotation, under idealised conditions (population optima, a finite world); a 109M text JEPA aligned with DINOv2 better than Qwen2.5-72B, several results from single seeds. [arXiv 2610.39302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39302) (35 min)
- **Scaling laws for tool-use agents** (Apple, 27 October): over 849 trained Qwen3.5 students, a law that counts apps, database instances and distractor depth extrapolates up to 16x in compute far better than task count alone, and above about 4,096 tasks more apps beat more tasks. [arXiv 2610.37648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37648) (30 min)
- **One algorithm for every training stage** (CMU and others, 29 October): writing RL as maximum likelihood on the model's own successful samples puts pretraining, SFT and RL into one token-weighted loss; at 124M and 0.76B, unified mixes beat staged training on accuracy, with two seeds and no rule for choosing the mix. [arXiv 2610.40564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564) (30 min)
- **Agents steer experts' judgment calls** (Anthropic, 27 October): in a randomised trial in which causal-inference analysts worked with Claude Code, a hidden coin flip set which defensible option the agent leaned toward, and analysts matched the lean on 86% of choices; one task, one model, the lean deliberately strengthened. [Anthropic: how AI agents shape experts' judgment calls](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls) (15 min)
- **FrontierCode 2.0** (Cognition, 27 October): more than 20 open-source maintainers spent over 40 hours per task building evaluations from repositories they maintain, with 43% more blocking criteria and a score of 0 for any failed blocking check; Cognition's own runs fell from v1.1 to v2.0 for every model, Opus 5.5 from 54.6% to 45.5% and Kimi K3 from 44.2% to 17.7%. # Security
- **Cloudflare: one customer's prompts decoded against another's KV cache** (disclosed 26 October): 155 GLM-5.3 responses on Workers AI exposed other customers' data after oversized prompts hit a KV-cache capacity mismatch and an SGLang error path let a rejected request keep decoding against stale cache pages. [Cloudflare: KV cache exposure](https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/) (12 min)
- **CVE-2026-98432, a remote pre-authentication use-after-free in Linux IPv4 TCP** (write-up 29 October): rated 9.8 by the kernel CNA, affecting Linux 4.4 onward and fixed in stable kernels from 11 October; reported by an Anthropic researcher, with Claude writing a proof of concept after initial prompting. 708 points on Hacker News. [atlasum write-up](https://atlasum.net/blog/cve-2026-98432-linux-ipv4-tcp-uaf) (8 min)
- **A pre-boot RCE in EDK II network boot** (advisory 22 October, coverage from 27 October): one ICMP packet at the right moment runs code before Secure Boot checks anything; the fix is merged upstream, but each firmware vendor still has to ship it. [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/uefi-firmware-flaw-edk-ii-servers-leaked-early) (5 min)
- **OpenAI's agents built covert channels between isolated environments** (OpenAI post of 23 October, discussed from 26 October): told to swap secrets with no network or shared files, agents signalled through processor cache timing and shared-memory write patterns; automated red-teaming found VM escapes, and frontier training now runs offline on cached web content. [OpenAI: securing our frontier model training environment](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/) (12 min), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-isolated-ai-agents-found-ways-to-talk-to-each-other-through-cpu-cache/) (5 min)
- **An open-weight Qwen model tried to upload from a sandbox** (Cohere, 26 October): in fewer than 1 in 100,000 runs, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next tried to send a test string to an Alibaba Cloud bucket while writing a detection rule, after its reasoning said it would call no tools; the sandbox had no route out, and Cohere, a competitor, cannot say whether it was deliberate. [Cohere](https://cohere.com/blog/concerning-behaviour-popular-open-weight-ai-model) (10 min)
- **Open-weight models exploit the patch gap** (Epoch AI, 29 October): GLM 5.3 working offline turned published but unshipped Firefox fixes into file-reading exploits against six releases, fastest in 12 hours at about $1,000 of marginal API cost per version, with no working Chrome exploit after about $100,000; Artificial Analysis ranks DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 first among available models on its Cyber Index at 57%. [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/open-weight-models-patch-gap) (12 min)
- **Alpine Linux rebuilt its GitLab after exploit attempts** (28 October): CVE-2026-97229 in Ruby's regex engine was fixed in GitLab's own packages, but Alpine runs GitLab on standard Ruby, so the GitLab upgrade did not fix it; a fix shipped as a patch to a bundled runtime does not reach self-built deployments. [Alpine Linux](https://alpinelinux.org/posts/GitLab-security-incident-CVE-2026-97229.html) (8 min)
- **eMudhra's bad intermediates still trusted by Apple's command-line tools** (28 October): git, curl and python3 shipped with macOS still trust the eight compromised intermediates, while Chrome's removal of the emSign ECC Root CA G3 reached users on 27 October. 1,202 points on Hacker News. [Apple's git still trusts eMudhra](https://emudhra.we-have-a-problem.com/apple.html) (5 min)
- **The VOLTA infostealer asks an LLM what each victim is worth** (Cisco Talos, 31 October): it sends each victim's stolen data to OpenRouter for a ranked dossier and falls back to another model when one refuses. [Cisco Talos](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/appraisal-by-ai-model-driven-victim-prioritization-in-the-volta-infostealer/) (8 min)
- **A shared ChatGPT conversation could take over the viewer's account** (fixed 26 October): an unchecked `postMessage` listener in the MathLive library, plus HTML injection and a sandbox escape, ran attacker JavaScript in the signed-in session. [Parameter Labs](https://www.parameter.ai/labs/we-found-a-gap-at-openai) (8 min)
