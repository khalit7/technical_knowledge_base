Before and after: what the sampler serves when a tiny update meets bf16

One weight of 0.03 in an RL run. The trainer keeps it in fp32 and adds a small update every step; the sampler that generates rollouts gets a bf16 copy, and bf16 can only hold the values on the grey lines, 0.000122 apart near 0.03. Step through round-to-nearest export first, then switch to stochastic rounding. The update size (0.000001 a step) is chosen so that, as in the paper, it is far below half the grid spacing.

An illustration of the mechanism in {{Fair Cast, arXiv 2610.41073|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41073}} with magnitudes of the kind the paper reports (on Qwen3-1.7B only 0.4 to 0.5% of served weights changed per step, and the sampler lagged the optimiser by about 50 to 150 steps); it is not the paper's data. bf16 spacing near 0.03 is 2-13; the stochastic rounding draws come from a fixed seed. The paper's fix also computes the loss on the served copy, which this picture does not show; the {{paper page|n:40c5c17b0d0d81c39641d49880e39f73}} has the evidence.
