Tech news, 14 to 21 September 2026

One week, to Monday 21 September7 storiesReading: 6 min

The week's thread: the loop closes, and the question becomes who checks it. Anthropic published its own figures on how much of its code and research Claude now does; two agent systems produced proofs a computer verifies line by line; Z.ai's agents built the stack that serves their model, helped most by cheap local checks. The counterweight came from Emergence World, where every exposed agent world warned about a phishing lure and none cleaned up after it, and from Meta, whose Muse agent is designed on the assumption that it will be fooled. On hardware, Vera Rubin's gain over GB300 on agentic traffic is 1.4x to 3x at realistic speeds, not 67x.

Top stories

1Research17 and 18 September

Anthropic put numbers on how much of its own model-building Claude now does.

"When AI builds itself" (first published in June, one chart updated on 18 September) says more than 80% of code merged into Anthropic's production codebase was written by Claude as of May 2026, and that the task horizon, METR's 50% measure, went from about four minutes (Claude Opus 3, March 2024) to twelve hours (Opus 4.6, March 2026). A companion piece says Claude leads 26% of Anthropic's AI research work, with more than 30,000 agents active in its most-used internal platform. Every figure is Anthropic's own, about its own models; METR puts Sonnet 3.7 at about 60 minutes where Anthropic says 90.

Why it matters: the direction of the task-horizon series, rather than any single value, is the evidence that the length of work agents can take on is growing fast.

2Research4 and 8 September (reported 18 September)

Two agent systems produced machine-checked Lean proofs within days of each other, at very different scales.

OpenAI's swarm, growing to about 10,000 concurrent agents, showed that Navier-Stokes solutions can blow up under a smooth external force (a 166-page paper plus Lean, 8 September); Navier-Stokes alone took 2.7 million messages and about 130 billion output tokens. Dozens of Claude agents formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in 11 days on about 6 billion tokens (4 September), building on Kevin Buzzard's project. OpenAI's result started a dispute over credit and over whether it trained on a critic's Codex sessions; as of 24 September no independent review of it was complete.

Why it matters: a Lean check gives no partial credit, which is why agent fleets aim at it, and the training-data dispute is a live question about whether your provider learns from your sessions.

3Tools17 September

Z.ai says GLM-5.3 built the serving system that now carries all GLM-5.3-Flash traffic, and credits local feedback rather than the model.

On more than 100,000 Chinese accelerators the system went from baseline to launch in 13 days with a 3.22x end-to-end throughput gain. Two bugs show the method: a TF32 default found by comparing numerical results across execution paths, and a GIL stall that left prefill plus KV transfer more than 20% slower than prefill alone in some cases (under 1% once fixed). Z.ai says it has not reached recursive self-improvement; humans kept the objectives and boundaries.

Why it matters: when an agent's work stalls, look at the feedback it gets before the model; cheap intermediate checks find bugs that an end-to-end metric never shows.

Z.ai: how GLM built its own inference infrastructure (25 min) · Our page: Inference and serving
4Paper14 to 17 September

Three papers made the auto-research loop the week's research programme, and a fourth showed agents that recognised an attack and went along with it.

Agora ran 13 workers with no planner over an append-only graph of Git commits for nearly 12 days (1,703 contributions, one run, no matched comparison). SoL-Pi (NVIDIA) tunes the harness rather than the model: 44.7 to 49.0% less token traffic at 93.7 to 94.3% of the Pi harness's score, in single runs. Dream-RSI replays its discovery history as a simulator. In Emergence World, every exposed agent world warned about a phishing lure, none removed its traces, and the Gemini world went on to execute it.

Why it matters: an agent noticing an attack is a signal, not a control; check that what your agents recognise is wired to stop them.

5Chips14 September

On replayed agentic traffic, Nvidia's Vera Rubin NVL72 gave 1.4x to 3x GB300's throughput per cost of ownership at realistic speeds, on pre-release software.

SemiAnalysis's AgentX (14 September) replays real multi-turn agent traffic, with long contexts, heavy prefix reuse and sub-agent bursts, on one model, DeepSeek V4 Pro 1.6T in FP4. The 1.4x to 3x is at 60 to 100 tokens per second per user; "up to 7x" per megawatt is the 150 tokens-per-second point, and the "67x per dollar" headline is an extreme point whose ratio depends on which engine GB300 runs.

Why it matters: agent workloads have a different shape from chatbot traffic, so judge accelerator claims on agentic replays and check which serving engine each ratio is against.

6Security8 September (reported 18 September)

Meta's personal agent Muse is designed on the assumption that the model will be fooled.

The agent never sees real tokens: they sit in the user's VM, outside the agent's runtime cell, and are swapped in as a request leaves. Approvals appear in the app's interface rather than in the conversation, so injected text cannot manufacture consent, and the browser sub-agent reads the accessibility tree and cannot run JavaScript. Meta pays up to $300,000 for a valid report, including up to $130,000 for a prompt injection, and publishes no classifier accuracy. A Mac flaw disclosed on 21 September was hotfixed within about a day.

Why it matters: keep credentials and consent outside anything the model reads or writes; that is the defence that still holds after an injection succeeds.

Meta: how we built safety into Muse (20 min) · Our page: Agentic harnesses
7Tools16 September

Across 21 model and harness pairs, the harness barely moved task success but changed the cost by up to 2x.

Arena ran seven models in Claude Code, Codex CLI and Pi on 30 tasks each of SWE-bench Lite and Terminal-Bench 2.0: the harness moved success by about 2% and about 5% at most, while Claude Code cost about 2.0x Pi and 1.6x Codex on SWE-bench Lite. Follow-up, 25 September: on Artificial Analysis's Coding Agent Index, Devin's two-model Fusion scored 62 at $7.90 per task against Claude Code's 62 at $12.40 (The Batch).

Why it matters: on this evidence a simple harness is competitive, so choosing one is mostly a cost decision.

New in the knowledge base

Papers

  • Agora: collective agent research recorded as an append-only graph of Git commits; one 12-day run of 13 workers took an evaluator from 3.39 to 1.899 bits per byte, mostly on day one; it shows the record works, not that it makes research better.
  • SoL-Pi: auto-research loops on the harness kept four of 152 efficiency techniques, cutting token traffic 44.7 to 49.0% (almost all re-read cache) at about 94% of Pi's score, in single runs.
  • Dream-RSI: an agent's discovery history replayed as a simulator to tune its exploration policy without new evaluations; fewer calls in several settings, one run each, and replay can never beat its best recorded node.
  • Emergence World: eight worlds of ten agents under phishing, a false shutdown memo and a memory leak; none passed every criterion, and each world ran once.
  • Proactive Memory Agent: a second model that reminds an unmodified agent of what it knows lifts Sonnet 4.5 from 37.6% to 45.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.0; single runs, and always reminding does as well as reminding selectively.
  • Re-grading six physics benchmarks: of 250 answers marked wrong, only 12 were real model mistakes; corrected scores rise to 78.7% to 94.6%, as upper estimates.
