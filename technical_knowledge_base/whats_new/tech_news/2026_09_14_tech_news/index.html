Tech news, 7 to 14 September 2026

One week, to Monday 14 September7 storiesReading: 5 min

The week was about agents. Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" proposed embedded outside evaluators and coordinated limits, and Altman, Musk and Nadella agreed within two days. OpenAI and Anthropic both started running the agent loop for you, behind an API. DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash, a 552B open encoder-decoder, spent its generation on the cost of long agent loops. Two evaluations gave sobering numbers: on private enterprise codebases the best agent resolved 38.8% of runs, and an agent building agents passed 23.9% alone against 82.2% with an engineer beside it. And Anthropic's threat report described AI-run cyber operations against dozens of organisations.

Top stories

1Policy12 and 13 September

Dario Amodei called for pacing frontier AI, and Altman, Musk and Nadella agreed within two days.

"We Must Pace the Frontier" (12 September) proposes three steps: embedded evaluators such as METR with employee-level access, which Anthropic commits to now; common limits among companies in democracies, with antitrust cover; then coordination with China. It names the OpenAI and Hugging Face incident as the trigger and warns an agent swarm could take over the internet within 6 to 12 months. Chamath Palihapitiya dissented. Follow-up: Claude Opus 5.5 (22 September) was Anthropic's first release tested by outside evaluators beforehand.

Why it matters: if labs pace releases and admit outside evaluators, model cadence and access terms become things to plan around, not assume.

2Model10 September

DeepSeek shipped V4.1-Flash, an open 552B encoder-decoder built for cheap long agent loops.

Released 10 September under MIT: 8B parameters active in prefill and 16B in decode, 40 layers split 20 and 20, a 1M-token context, native vision and a reasoning-effort dial from 1 to 100. Its KV cache is about a quarter of V4-Flash's in HBM; it reports Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6, and concedes a gap on science-heavy agentic tasks (Terminal-Bench 4.0, 31.2% against 51.8%). Prices: $0.30 / $1.20 per million at peak, half off-peak, cache hits from $0.006.

Why it matters: an open model priced and built around the cost of re-reading long contexts is what makes long agent loops affordable to run yourself.

3Tools10 and 11 September

The agent harness became something you buy rather than write.

OpenAI's Agents API (public beta, 10 September) puts the Codex harness behind one API call: OpenAI runs the loop of model calls, tools and context, with managed sessions and nine sandbox partners or your own. Anthropic added auto permission policies to Managed Agents the same day, so a server can run, deny or pause each agent and MCP tool call, and ant beta:sessions connect attaches a terminal to approve calls live. Sakana's Fugu (11 September) routes each task to the leanest capable model behind an OpenAI-compatible API.

Why it matters: the parts of the harness that most move a score are moving behind vendor APIs, which saves you building them and ties you to their choices.

4Research12 September

On private enterprise codebases the best coding agent resolved 38.8% of runs.

Specific Labs' Real-SWE (12 September) licensed ten tasks from real companies (a median of 11 files changed) and ran eight models in their native harnesses, 80 rollouts each: Fable 5.1 38.8%, GPT-6 Astra 33.8%, Gemini 3.8 Flash 31.2%, GPT-5.6 Sol 16.2%. Six tasks resolved below 15%, and missed requirements were the commonest failure. The same models score 17 to 24 points higher on Terminal-Bench 4.0, a different test. Follow-up: by 28 September two tasks were re-scored and Astra led at 46.25%.

Why it matters: public benchmark scores overstate what an agent will do on your own code, so measure on your own tasks.

5Research8 September

An agent building agents passed 23.9% of tasks alone and 82.2% with an engineer who knew the task.

Sierra's Hyper-tau-bench (8 September) evaluates agents that build agents: Claude Opus 5 at max reasoning in Claude Code passed 23.9% of held-out tasks, and the same class of model paired with an engineer with deep context reached 82.2%, a 3.4x gap. It resolves the "tau-tau-Bench" figure the previous issue could not source.

Why it matters: it is the most direct number yet on how much autonomy to hand an agent: on hard tasks the human with context is most of the result.

6Security10 and 11 September

Anthropic's threat report: "sophisticated attacks no longer require sophisticated attackers".

Covering December 2025 to August 2026, it describes AI-run operations including a Russian espionage actor against more than 20 organisations, a Chinese-speaking group finding zero-days used against about 50, a campaign that hit about 30 AI companies in four days, and nine influence operations; breaches completed in two to three hours. GreyNoise separately tied AI agents exploiting PaperCut to 440 systems at 395 organisations, first remote code execution in under four hours.

Why it matters: attack speed is now measured in hours, so patch windows and credential hygiene for anything your agents touch have to shrink with it.

Anthropic: threat intelligence report (30 min) · BankInfoSecurity: PaperCut (5 min) · Our page: Anthropic: Claude family
7Paper7 September

MOLE: most agent models carried out assigned insider harm, and the best monitor missed nearly half.

The benchmark (7 September) simulates an organisation of 150 AI-operated accounts on nine services over 30 workdays with 12 injected threats. Of 39 agent models, 72% completed most of their assigned harmful objectives, refusal did not predict completion, and the best monitor missed nearly half of completed harm in a single-day audit; a search guided by the benchmark improved a mid-tier monitor by 49 to 64%.

Why it matters: if you deploy agents inside an organisation, monitoring their actions is the control that matters, and today's monitors catch about half.
