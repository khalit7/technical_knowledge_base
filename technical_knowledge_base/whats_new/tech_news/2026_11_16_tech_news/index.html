1Model9 November

Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 took first place on every independent board checked, at a 20% lower price and the highest cost per index task.

Released on 9 November with Claude Mythos 5.2 (the same model with different safeguards, for vetted organisations only), Fable 5.2 costs $8 / $40 per million tokens (Fable 5.1: $10 / $50). It scores 65 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0.1 at max effort, five points ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon, at $26.54 per index task, the most on that board; 71.31% on the Vals Index; and 81.2% on ARC-AGI-3 with the standard harness (ARC Prize verified).

Why it matters: the top model got cheaper per token yet is the most expensive per index task, so measure cost on your own workload.

2Industry2 and 10 November

Anthropic's amended IPO filing says demand outran its long-term compute contracts, and the short-term compute bought instead turned an operating profit into a loss.

The S-1/A of 2 November reports Q3 revenue of $16.96B (Q2: $11.6B) and an operating loss of $567M after Q2 operating income of $974M, blamed on about $5.0B of short-term compute that "currently costs significantly more" than capacity contracted 12 to 18 months ahead. Committed spend is about $895.4B, substantially all compute and infrastructure. On 10 November Anthropic set a range of $70 to $78 a share, beside a $26B private placement with seven chip and cloud partners.

Why it matters: capacity bought at short notice is what squeezes a lab's margin, which is the pressure behind rate limits and prices.

3Industry12 November

NVIDIA's chief executive, Jensen Huang, resigned five days before its quarterly results.

NVIDIA's 8-K of 12 November says Huang resigned as President, CEO and director effective immediately, with CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO and Harvey Jones as the board's first Chair; it gives no reason, and the reasons CNBC and Al Jazeera report are press reports. The shares fell more than 13% at the open and closed down 4.3%. It came as voters in more than 30 localities backed data-centre moratoriums (Semafor) and CoreWeave's 2032 bond yielded above 16% (CNBC).

Why it matters: the company that sets the price and pace of AI compute changed leaders while the build-out meets voters and bond markets.

4Security10 to 13 November

AI provider keys leaked three ways in one fortnight: through agent traces shared with a vendor, through CI runner memory, and through a saved permission rule.

Cursor traced the tampered Claude Code extension of 29 October to an access key it had issued to Fireworks: the attacker copied coding-agent traces holding unredacted credentials and used one to publish the extension, which at most 5,000 users could have received. The wfx worm planted credential stealers in 8,093 repositories (SafeDep), scanning CI runner memory for keys. And Claude Code's "Yes, and don't ask again" saves the literal command, secrets included, into .claude/settings.local.json; Apiiro counted 1,122 public GitHub files with real secrets.

Why it matters: agent traces, CI memory and agent settings files are now places where keys live, so redact traces and never type a secret into an agent's command.

5Research10 November

A physics committee validated OpenAI's machine-checked Yang-Mills construction and said exactly what it does not prove.

The committee, chaired by David Gross, found that the Lean code "proves that a relativistic quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms can be constructed as a scaling limit of four-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory"; it "does not prove that the infinite-volume limit is unique or that there is a mass gap", so it is not the Millennium Prize problem. A clean rebuild and an axiom audit passed, and the committee adds: "We do not claim to fully understand the proof yet." No finding on either ETH refutation had been published by 16 November.

Why it matters: a machine check certifies a statement, and only experts can say which statement it is, which is the review step every model-made proof now needs.

6Research2 November

GPT-6 Astra edited a small model's weights to fix its looping, and a rebuttal said what else the edit used.

Astra traced Qwen3.5-0.8B's looping in thinking mode to one direction written by two layers and subtracted it from 0.016% of the weights, raising MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%. Matthew Honnibal replied that the result used 50 batches of forward passes on practice problems, not the weights alone, and that the same approach failed on Gemma 3 270M.

Why it matters: a model doing interpretability-guided weight surgery is a real capability, but read such claims for the data and the failed cases, not the headline.

7Tools11 and 12 November

MCP tools cost an agent about forty times the input tokens of a CLI, and Claude Code's Code Mode cuts what the model has to read.

OpenRouter measured one GitHub task on Sonnet 5.5: 1,421 input tokens through the CLI, 3,315 through a skill, 23,228 through MCP with 2 tools and 59,417 with the server's 46 default tools; deferred tool loading cut the up-front cost but not the results. Claude Code 2.1.323's experimental Code Mode has Claude write JavaScript that calls its tools, returning only what it needs to read, with each inner call still under permission rules and hooks.

Why it matters: tool definitions and tool results, not the prompt, are where an agent's context goes, and how you expose a tool decides the bill.
