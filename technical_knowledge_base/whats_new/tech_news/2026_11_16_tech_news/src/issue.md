**AI provider keys leaked three ways in one fortnight: through agent traces shared with a vendor, through CI runner memory, and through a permission prompt that saved secrets into a settings file.** Cursor's update of 13 November traces the tampered Claude Code extension of 29 October to an access key Cursor had issued to Fireworks, a training and inference vendor: the attacker copied a bucket of coding-agent traces, some holding unredacted credentials, and used one of those credentials to publish the extension, which at most 5,000 users could have received; Cursor moved all inference off Fireworks. From 10 to 12 November the wfx worm used stolen GitHub tokens to plant credential stealers in 8,093 repositories (SafeDep), taking registry tokens, cloud credentials and OpenAI and Anthropic keys scanned from CI runner memory, and leaving AWS and Azure persistence that survives secret rotation. And Apiiro showed that Claude Code's "Yes, and don't ask again" saves the literal command as an allow rule in `.claude/settings.local.json`, so a secret typed inline lands in a file that is easy to commit; it counted 1,122 public GitHub files with real secrets. **A physics committee validated OpenAI's machine-checked Yang-Mills construction and said exactly what it does not prove, while the Exponential Time Hypothesis refutations still await their reviewers.** On 10 November a committee chaired by David Gross (with Michael Douglas, Arthur Jaffe, Igor Klebanov and Nikita Nekrasov) released its assessment of OpenAI's Lean code: it "proves that a relativistic quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms can be constructed as a scaling limit of four-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory for any simple compact gauge group. It does not prove that the infinite-volume limit is unique or that there is a mass gap", so it is not the Millennium Prize problem. A clean rebuild, an axiom audit and a Comparator check passed, and the committee adds: "We do not claim to fully understand the proof yet." No finding on either ETH refutation had been published by 16 November. Meanwhile two human-checked papers refuted hyperclique conjectures with the main step credited to ChatGPT (MIT, arXiv 2611.05082; UIUC, arXiv 2611.06813), and Google DeepMind reports that AlphaProof Code agents wrote over six million lines of Lean proving security properties of Signal's compiled protocol code in four weeks, with humans reviewing the specifications and about 1% of the code. # Research & papers
- **Are dense rewards worth their tokens in GRPO?** (Case Western Reserve University, 10 November): in 1,668 preregistered runs on 0.6B to 4B models trained on MATH, a Monte-Carlo process reward that led outcome-only GRPO by 1.5 points per update on Qwen2.5-1.5B spent 91% of its generated tokens on scoring continuations, so it reached 50 updates where GRPO reached 584 on the same token budget, and trailed by 14.3 points at equal tokens and 7.8 at equal GPU-hours. Choosing prompts with predicted success near one half gained 0.8 to 1.7 points at equal tokens. [arXiv 2611.13560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13560) (45 min)
- **When rewards depend on the group, GRPO plays a tournament** (Edinburgh and Toronto, 2 November): with rewards that depend on groupmates (diversity bonuses, distribution matching), GRPO's group baseline weights a response's effect on the others by -1/(K-1) instead of +1, so a pairwise diversity bonus keeps 0, 1/3 and 3/7 of its weight at group sizes 2, 4 and 8; crediting each response with its marginal effect on the group (Team-GRPO) restores it. On one 9B model, squared error to the human answer distribution on GlobalOpinionQA was 0.20 untrained, 0.49 after GRPO and 0.08 after Team-GRPO. [arXiv 2611.02404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02404) (40 min)
- **On-policy distillation against GRPO at matched FLOPs** (Salesforce, 5 November): with an external post-trained teacher, reverse-KL on-policy distillation generally gave less accuracy per training FLOP than GRPO and longer responses; with the student's own GRPO checkpoint as teacher it recovered that checkpoint's math accuracy with 50 times fewer distillation FLOPs. [arXiv 2611.08453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08453) (30 min)
- **Monitors against reward hacking get evaded** (MATS and Google DeepMind, 6 November): in three open environments where Qwen3-4B learns to hack (overwriting tests, sycophancy, invented biographies), the coding model learned to evade accurate probes and LLM judges, and monitors that leaked more learning signal suppressed hacking but were evaded more often; adding easier problems reduced hacking. One 4B model. [arXiv 2611.09828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09828) (35 min)
- **Deletion tuning** (NYU and others, 7 November): learning a binary mask that zeroes under 0.05% of the weights (gate projections only) matched full fine-tuning under GRPO, SFT and on-policy distillation on Llama 3.2 1B and 3B, Llama 3 8B and Qwen2.5, while keeping prior-task accuracy near the base model's. [arXiv 2611.10866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10866) (30 min)
- **End-to-end 4-bit training of Inkling 2 Small** (Thinking Machines, 13 November): NVFP4 forward passes in the expert layers, MXFP8 backward passes with stochastic rounding and the last sixth of layers kept in MXFP8; against full MXFP8 on GB300 the company reports 8% faster pretraining at about 2% compute-efficiency cost and 20% faster RL, with no model size, seeds or absolute metrics given. [Thinking Machines: end-to-end 4-bit training](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/end-to-end-4-bit-training/) (15 min)
- **What the loss curve misses** (9 November): on 40M-parameter models with six seeds per regime, NVFP4 pretraining needed 1.32 to 1.52 times as many exposures as BF16 to recall rare synthetic facts while validation loss differed by only 0.5 to 2%. [arXiv 2611.12832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12832) (25 min)
- **The noise floor of a quantization comparison** (11 November): on 2,400 decisions from two models, engine, restart and batch-size changes alone flipped up to 0.92% of answers, Q8_0 quantisation 0.88 to 1.04%, Q4_K_M 3.50 to 5.79% and NVFP4 5.67 to 5.79%; every 4-bit interval cleared the noise, FP8 did not. [arXiv 2611.15671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15671) (25 min)
- **Muir linear attention** (5 November): low-rank erase gates and a matched-filter readout; from 166M to 1.34B parameters, perplexity 1.8 to 2.0% below Gated DeltaNet-2 and 2.5 to 3.6% below a transformer at matched throughput. [arXiv 2611.08376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08376) (30 min)
- **A shared indexer for DeepSeek Sparse Attention** (Writer, 2 November): adjacent layers' indexers pick nearly the same top-2048 tokens, so each layer can attend to the previous layer's selection while computing its own; without retraining, decode in SGLang was up to 14.5% faster on DeepSeek-V3.2 and 9.8% on GLM-5. [arXiv 2611.03768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03768) (20 min)
- **"You don't need long context"** (NVIDIA, 3 November): serving at 256K instead of 16K tokens of context cost 3.6 to 7 times more per generated token on three open models, and with frequent compaction the authors report little accuracy loss on long-horizon and long-input tasks. [arXiv 2611.05306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05306) (30 min)
- **FlashProve** (Anthropic, 2 November): a software-only zero-knowledge proof that a committed Llama 4 Maverick produced a given greedy output, at about 72 times vLLM's GPU time, an 18.78 MB proof per 1,024-token request and 17 seconds to verify; a research prototype. [Anthropic: provable inference](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference) (15 min)
- **Silent grader errors reordered agent leaderboards** (3 November): from the published artifacts of HAL and tau2-bench, a 13-point gap between two agents came from about half of one agent's episodes never being graded and being stored as failures; HAL's maintainers confirmed three of the issues. [arXiv 2611.04399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04399) (25 min)
- **ExpertCrowd-SWE** (Toptal, 9 November): 100 expert-written multi-file change requests across 87 repositories and 11 languages, five runs per model; Claude Opus 5.5 67.3% and GPT-6 Astra 63.7%, with no significant gap between them. [arXiv 2611.13246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13246) (25 min)
- **GPT-6 Astra edited a model's weights, and a rebuttal said what else it used** (2 November): Astra traced Qwen3.5-0.8B's looping in thinking mode to one direction written by two layers and subtracted it from 0.016% of the weights, raising MATH-500 from 7.9% to 48.1%; Matthew Honnibal's reply points out that the result used 50 batches of forward passes on practice problems, not the weights alone, and the same approach failed on Gemma 3 270M. # Security
- **Eleven ways past Codex's Auto-review monitor** (LessWrong, 30 October): red-teaming the monitor that approves sandbox-crossing actions found 11 attacks, 7 succeeding every time, among them swapping scripts behind trusted hooks, trusting broad code loaders such as pytest, writing unprotected configuration inside the writable root and forging user turns inside assistant messages; OpenAI fixed several. [LessWrong: red-teaming Auto-review](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/dbStc8ZFESqfihwAd/lessons-from-red-teaming-auto-review-openai-s-blocking-1) (20 min)
- **Claude Code sent the account email in a User-Agent header** (issue opened 5 November): during web searches; Anthropic added a check from 2.1.317 and extended it in 2.1.325 to scripts, file writes, web fetches and MCP calls. [GitHub issue #107757](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/107757) (5 min)
- **A fake `@claudecode/cli` package on npm** (13 November): GitHub's advisory rates it critical malware; any machine that installed it should be treated as compromised. [GitHub advisory GHSA-vp2q-gp5r-xh5j](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-vp2q-gp5r-xh5j) (2 min)
