Before and after: one token budget, two ways to spend it

The budget and the update counts are the paper's for Qwen2.5-1.5B ({{arXiv 2611.13560|https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13560}}: 12.25M generated tokens, 584 GRPO updates, 50 for the Monte-Carlo reward with 4 continuations at each of 5 cuts, 91% of its tokens on continuations); tokens per iteration are the budget divided by the updates, so they are averages. The accuracy figures are the paper's matched comparisons. The {{paper page|n:41a5c17b0d0d81eb8e0cc16195491027}} has the evidence and its limits.

The paper gives every method the same budget of generated tokens, counting every token the method generates. The bar is that budget; each slice is one training iteration (8 prompts, 8 completions each, then one update). The chart plays outcome-only GRPO by itself once in view; then switch to the process reward with the buttons above it. The budget stays the same; only what an iteration spends changes.

Step by step

  1. One budget. 12.25M generated tokens for the whole run, the same for every method.
  2. One iteration, one update. GRPO samples 64 completions, scores each 0 or 1 with the verifier, and updates: about 21,000 tokens, all of them completions the update trains on.
  3. Halfway. 292 updates in, half the budget spent.
  4. The budget runs out at 584 updates. Every generated token went into a completion that was trained on.
  1. Same budget. The same 12.25M tokens, the same 64 completions per iteration.
  2. Each completion is cut and continued. To credit segments, every completion is cut at 5 points and 4 continuations are sampled from each cut; the continuations are scored and thrown away. One iteration costs about 245,000 tokens, 91% of them continuations.
  3. Halfway. 25 updates in, half the budget spent.
  4. The budget runs out at 50 updates. Each update was better (1.5 points ahead of GRPO at equal updates) but there were 534 fewer of them: at equal tokens it ended 14.3 points behind, and 7.8 behind at equal GPU-hours.
