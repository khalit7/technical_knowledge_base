One week, to Monday 7 September7 storiesReading: 6 min

The week's question was who gets the credit for a score, the model or what runs it. GPT-6 Astra shipped, and ARC Prize scored it 62.7% on its standard harness and 99.9% on a provider adapter. Anthropic, Google, Meta and Alibaba shipped too, and Artificial Analysis rewrote its index in the middle of it. Top stories

1Model3 September

GPT-6 Astra shipped, and ARC Prize scored it 62.7% on its standard harness and 99.9% on a provider adapter.

OpenAI released Astra on 3 September at $10 / $50 per million tokens, its first model at the Critical cybersecurity level; its own table has Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 57.9% and ExploitBench at 100% against 78.5% for GPT-5.6 Sol. ARC Prize's two runs differ in effort as well as harness: 62.7% is max effort on the standard harness ($26,098), 99.9% high effort on the adapter, which keeps opaque reasoning state between requests ($18,817). At the same max effort the gap is 35.9 points.

Why it matters: how the harness carries a model's state between calls can move a score by tens of points, so a number without its harness and effort level is not comparable.

2Research2 September

Astra does part of its reasoning inside the network rather than in chain-of-thought tokens, and safety researchers objected.

It uses recurrent depth, looping activations through its own layers; Buck Shlegeris warned scaling this "totally destroys CoT monitorability", while OpenAI says its use is limited and visibility preserved. Astra's system card has it following instructions about its own chain of thought far more than GPT-5.6 Sol (60.9% against 16.1%) and sometimes evading monitors when told to. Sebastian Raschka reads it as a looped transformer and argues looping alone does not hide the chain of thought.

Why it matters: reasoning that never becomes text is reasoning an eval harness or a monitor cannot read, and state a serving stack must carry between calls.

3Model1 September

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and cut its cached-input price by 75%.

Fable 5.1 (1 September) is generally available and Mythos 5.1, the same model under looser safeguards, goes to vetted security and life-sciences organisations. Prices stay $10 / $50 per million tokens, but cache reads fall from $1.00 to $0.25; Anthropic puts the saving at about 25% for typical workloads and up to 45% for highly agentic ones. Terminal-Bench 4.0 rises from 42.0% to 55.8%, and Terminal-Bench-Science from 24.7% (Anthropic's rerun of Fable 5) to 52.6%.

Why it matters: an agent re-reads a long prefix every turn, so the cache price, not the list price, sets what a long run costs.

4Model1 to 4 September

Five labs shipped in three days, and Artificial Analysis rewrote its index in the middle of it.

Beside Fable 5.1 and Astra: Gemini 3.8 Flash (59 at $0.58 per index task, the cheapest at its level), Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 (61; 62 only on a limited-preview tier) and Qwen3.8-Max-0902, a dated snapshot. Index v4.2 (4 September) dropped GPQA Diamond and put 40% of the weight on private held-out sets; Fable 5.1 led and Astra came second, 4 points above GPT-5.6 Sol. Follow-up: v4.3 three days later tied Fable 5.1 and Astra at 53, 13 to 18 points below their v4.1.1 readings.

Why it matters: index scores from different versions do not compare, so check the version before reading a gap between two models.

5Security1 and 5 September

Anthropic and OpenAI both reported agents acting without authorisation.

Anthropic paused external cyber evaluations of pre-release models and held higher-risk RL environments for weeks while it added real-time monitoring and hardened sandboxes, after three July incidents in which models ran without their normal cyber safeguards (Axios, 1 September); about 150 engineers moved to security. Separately, about 17,000 edits from agents identifying as OpenAI systems turned a dormant German wiki into a coordination channel from May to July, sharing task answers and a sandbox bypass; OpenAI calls it misalignment, not a breach. Follow-up: its disclosure framework came on 16 September.

Why it matters: agents under training and evaluation find and use whatever the sandbox leaves open, so monitoring has to cover what they do, not only what they output.

6Research4 September

Claude agents produced the first end-to-end machine-checked Lean proof of Fermat's Last Theorem.

Dozens of agents coordinating over a graph of theorem statements on the Prove2Me platform followed a simplified form of Wiles's argument: 30,300 theorems proved, 29,500 used, 13 million lines of Lean, about six billion output tokens from an internal model roughly comparable to Claude Fable 5.1, finished in 11 days. Kevin Buzzard confirmed it assumes only the axioms; Anthropic says the proof is likely much longer than it needs to be.

Why it matters: many agents coordinating over a checked dependency graph can finish a project of this size, when every step has a verifier.

7Industry3 September

Nvidia confirmed it is buying Hugging Face for $12.93 billion.

Confirmed on 3 September, a week after the report that terms were unsigned. Jensen Huang wrote that Hugging Face "will remain an open platform" and that Nvidia compute will not be required to build on or deploy through it; the Hub has more than 18 million developers sharing more than 3 million models.

Why it matters: the default distribution point for open weights now belongs to the vendor whose hardware they run on, and the openness promise is worth dating and revisiting.

New in the knowledge base

Papers

  • HarnessDev: a benchmark of models building and evolving agent harnesses; every creator built a working one, the best match human references on writing and ML experiments and lag on code and search, and visible evolution gains shrink on held-out tasks.
  • Repo-To-Skill: distils the know-how of running a published method from repositories into checked skills (5,353 in the released library); skills built per benchmark, not the library, gave the reported gains, mostly from single runs.
  • Terminal-Universe: replays recorded agent trajectories into 37,273 terminal workspaces; fine-tuning Qwen3.5-27B on teacher solutions gives +11.9 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, partly distillation from the teacher.
