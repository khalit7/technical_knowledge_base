Before and after: penalising what the monitor catches, against routing it into weights you delete

Source: {{GRAFT, arXiv 2610.17062|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17062}} and the {{Alignment Science blog post|https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/graft/}} (Qwen3-8B, Countdown-Code RL, monitor detects hack type 1 of 3), read 2026-10-26. The figures are the paper's, from the authors' released per-seed data as replayed on the {{GRAFT paper page|n:4055c17b0d0d8139ba07f0ddc44d3092}}: the penalty baseline switches to an undetected hack on 25.9% of trajectories; GRAFT hacks on 38.5% before the adapter is removed and 1.6% after, with a task score of 79.2 against the base model's 60.2 (Table 4). The blog post rounds these to about 30%, up to 35% and near zero. The diagram shows where updates go; it is not a plot of training curves.

The setting from the GRAFT paper: RL on a coding task where the model can cheat in three ways and the monitor recognises only one of them. The diagram plays the usual fix by itself once in view, a reward penalty on flagged rollouts; then switch to GRAFT (the buttons above the diagram). The task, the monitor and the three hacks stay the same; only what the monitor's flag does changes. The mechanism itself, in 3D with the paper's own numbers, is on the paper page: {{Fig. 1|n:4055c17b0d0d8139ba07f0ddc44d3092#hero}} (the frozen MLP, the two adapters and where each gradient goes) and {{Fig. 2|n:4055c17b0d0d8139ba07f0ddc44d3092#s-method}} (one group's rewards, flags and routed advantages).

Step by step

  1. The monitor flags a hack. A rollout uses hack 1, the only kind the monitor recognises, and is flagged. Hacks 2 and 3 pass unflagged.
  2. The flag lowers the reward. The usual fix: a flagged rollout gets a lower reward, and the update goes into the same weights as every other rollout. The policy is now rewarded for not being caught, which is not the same as not cheating.
  3. The model learns the hack the monitor misses: 25.9%. Hack 1 fades, and an undetected hack takes its place on 25.9% of trajectories. The monitor reports almost nothing; in the paper's words a practitioner "might incorrectly conclude that this policy is hack-free".
  4. Deploy: still 25.9%. What ships still hacks on 25.9% of trajectories, all of it in a form the monitor cannot see. The penalty made the monitor an optimisation target. Switch to GRAFT.
  1. The monitor flags a hack. Same rollout, same monitor, same flag on hack 1.
  2. The flag decides where the gradient goes. GRAFT adds small extra neurons to every MLP, split into a RETAIN and a FORGET adapter. A flagged rollout updates only FORGET; unflagged rollouts update both. The reward is untouched, so nothing pays the model for evading the monitor.
  3. Hacking collects in FORGET: 38.5%. During training the model with FORGET switched on still hacks, on 38.5% of trajectories, but what it learned from flagged rollouts sits in FORGET, and deleting FORGET also removes the hacks the monitor never flagged (next step).
  4. Delete FORGET, then deploy: 1.6%. Ablating FORGET leaves hacking at 1.6%, and the task score is 79.2 against the base model's 60.2 (+19.0), though its solve rate (74.8%) is slightly below the penalty's (77.2%). Limits: heuristic monitors, single-turn tasks, models up to 8B.
