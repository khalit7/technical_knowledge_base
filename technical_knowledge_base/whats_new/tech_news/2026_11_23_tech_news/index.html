One week, to Monday 23 November6 storiesReading: 4 min

The frontier got cheaper rather than better: GPT-6.2 Astra and Gemini 4 Neon landed within four points of Claude Fable 5.2 on the main index at a quarter to a seventh of its cost per task. Top stories

1Model17 and 18 November

OpenAI and Google closed most of the gap to Claude Fable 5.2 at a fraction of its cost per task.

OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra (17 November, $8 / $40 per million tokens) scored 62 on Artificial Analysis's index against Fable 5.2's 65, at $4.00 per index task against $26.54; Google's Gemini 4 Neon (18 November, $3 / $15) scored 61 at $6.44. On ARC-AGI-3's standard harness Fable 5.2 still leads Astra by 10.9 points; the "first to 100%" headlines describe a provider adapter, not the standard harness.

Why it matters: for most workloads the choice is now about price and behaviour rather than raw capability, and index readings depend on the harness, so compare like with like.

2Research20 and 21 November

A 17-million-line Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, found by an Anthropic mathematician working with Claude, passed every machine check within a day.

Levent Alpöge released only Lean (175,187 files on top of Mathlib) on 20 November; on 21 November the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative certified Mathlib's statement of the Riemann Hypothesis and its proof with Lean's kernel and independent checkers. The human-readable draft still rests on thirteen hypotheses it does not prove, and no mathematician has yet reviewed the mathematics.

Why it matters: it is the clearest case yet of a result that is certified correct before anyone understands it, which is exactly what a Lean check does and does not give you.

3Industry19 November

Anthropic priced the largest IPO on record at $83 a share and closed its first day 17% higher.

It raised about $81.3B (range $70 to $78) beside a $26B private placement with seven chip and cloud partners; trading began on Nasdaq on 19 November under ANT and closed at $97.17. Renaissance Capital puts the fully diluted valuation at about $1.38T.

Why it matters: the lab behind the top model now answers to public shareholders, with quarterly disclosures of its costs and compute.

4Policy16 and 17 November

The US now requires a licence to ship AI chips to any data centre, anywhere, that may serve Chinese companies.

The Commerce Department's interim rule of 16 November covers data centres above roughly 750 H100s of compute, gives existing operators 90 days to get licences and rescinds the 2025 AI Diffusion Rule. NVIDIA, reporting $115.8B of quarterly revenue the next day, said it has stopped shipping licensed Hopper chips to China.

Why it matters: renting GPUs abroad was the main way Chinese labs reached frontier hardware; this closes it, which will show in their next models.

5Security19 and 22 November

About two million canary prompts caught attackers inside three inference providers for months, including on requests marked zero data retention.

Dragos seeded prompts with unique links and credentials from July to November; attackers inside three hosting providers captured requests and used planted AWS keys for tens of thousands of API calls, and one unnamed Chinese lab fetched secret links from more than half of the prompts sent to its models. OpenRouter removed three providers and took 125 endpoints off zero-data-retention eligibility.

Why it matters: a prompt sent through a router or reseller can be read by whoever hosts the model, so secrets do not belong in prompts and zero data retention is a promise, not a guarantee.

Dragos: canary tokens in the AI supply chain (15 min) · OpenRouter: incidents (4 min) · Our page: Agent security
6Paper19 November

A fixed rollout cap in GRPO teaches small models to stop thinking early.

JetBrains Research trained three 2B to 4B models with GRPO on math under a fixed cap (three seeds): AIME accuracy at the cap rose 10.9 to 42.4 points, while accuracy at the largest budget tested fell 13.1 to 30.2 points below the base model. None of the fixes tried (single runs) kept both.

Why it matters: a budget set for training cost becomes a limit the model learns, so a cheap RL run can quietly remove test-time scaling.
