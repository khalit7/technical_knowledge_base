Nov 20Top Lean statement Nov 212,909 points2,094 comments (on 2026-11-23)

A 17-million-line Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, released by an Anthropic mathematician working with Claude, was certified by the Lean and Mathlib communities within a day; the mathematics behind it is not yet understood or reviewed. On 20 November Levent Alpöge posted a four-page note saying he had, "working with Claude", found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis that extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and released only Lean: a library of 175,187 files depending only on Mathlib. On 21 November a statement endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative said "both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct" for Mathlib's vetted RiemannHypothesis statement, checked with Lean's kernel and independent checkers (con-leche, con-ron and others) on an exported proof; the statement covers the Riemann Hypothesis only. Andrew Sutherland (MIT) and Michael Rubinstein (Waterloo) report independent rebuilds passing, with some of the longest kernel runs still pending on 22 November. No Claude model version is named. The human-readable draft reduces the result to thirteen hypotheses it does not yet prove, Nature quotes Andrew Granville calling the release "garbled", and Scientific American reports a rival human claim by Alessandro Masullo that is still under review.

Research & papers 12 items

Several of the week's papers found a failure in a knob everyone treats as harmless. A training-time rollout cap taught small models to stop thinking early; a sampler, not the model, explained why generations fall apart at high temperature; a few heads, not the sparsity budget, explained why sparse attention misses needles; and a verifier that only checks sufficiency taught padding. Around them, the labs reported results on their own terms: black-hole physics, chip layouts and a proposal to cap pretraining by perplexity.

Nov 19Research

A fixed rollout cap in GRPO becomes a learned limit on test-time scaling (JetBrains Research, arXiv 2611.26172, 19 November): on three small models (Qwen3.5-4B, Gemma4-E2B, Nemotron-3-Nano-4B) trained with GRPO on math under a fixed rollout cap (three seeds for the main comparison), AIME accuracy at the cap rose 10.9 to 42.4 points over the base model while accuracy at the largest budget tested fell 13.1 to 30.2 points below it, and none of the tested fixes (single runs on Qwen3.5-4B) kept both the in-budget gain and the base model's test-time scaling. The introduction's wider ranges (11.7 to 46.7 and 17.2 to 35.0) are not reproduced by the paper's own tables. Mean output length on AIME at 64K tokens fell from 27.3K tokens for base Qwen3.5-4B to 5.0K after training at a 4K cap. Loss masking, DAPO's overlong penalty and Dr. GRPO did not prevent it (single-seed ablations); fine-tuning on budget-truncated traces reproduced it without RL. Checkpoints are on Hugging Face.

Before and after: what doubling the temperature does to the candidate pool

The bars are an illustrative next-token distribution (30 likely tokens and the rest of a 128K vocabulary as one tail bar), softmax at temperature 1 and 2, on a log probability axis. The pool sizes, 4 and 51,845 tokens for top-p and 3 for top-nsigma, are the paper's Llama-3.1-8B example (arXiv 2611.22148); the coding results are its 100-issue agent runs on three Qwen3.5 models.

Nov 17Research

Long generations collapse at high temperature because of the sampler, not the model (arXiv 2611.22148, 17 November, NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets track): across 29 open models of 1B to 122B, coding agents at temperature 2 resolved 0 of 100 GitHub issues with top-p, top-k or Top-H and 46 to 62 with top-nsigma (three Qwen3.5 models); in one Llama-3.1-8B example the top-p candidate pool grew from 4 to 51,845 tokens between temperature 1 and 2 while top-nsigma's stayed at 3, because top-nsigma keeps tokens within n standard deviations of the top logit, a set that temperature does not change. At temperature 1 most samplers perform about the same. Code and data released.

arXiv 2611.22148 (40 min)
Feeds Sampling and Decoding

Both samplers start from the same logits and the same temperature. The chart plays top-p by itself once in view; then switch to top-nsigma with the buttons above it. Highlighted bars are the tokens the sampler may draw.

Step by step

  1. Temperature 1. The logits are divided by 1; a few tokens hold most of the probability.
  2. Top-p cuts at a probability mass. It keeps the most likely tokens until their probabilities add up to p: here 4 tokens.
  3. Temperature 2. Dividing the logits by 2 flattens the probabilities: the likely tokens lose mass and the long tail of the vocabulary gains it.
  4. The same mass now needs the tail. To reach p, top-p has to take tens of thousands of unlikely tokens: 51,845 in the paper's example, and long generations drift into nonsense (0 of 100 issues resolved at temperature 2).
  1. Temperature 1. The same logits, the same distribution.
  2. Top-nsigma cuts on the logits. It keeps the tokens whose logit is within n standard deviations of the top logit: here 3 tokens.
  3. Temperature 2. The probabilities flatten exactly as before.
  4. The cut does not move. Dividing every logit by the same temperature divides the gaps and the standard deviation alike, so the same 3 tokens pass; temperature only reweights them. The agents resolved 46 to 62 of 100 issues at temperature 2.

Weight-space programming went from one demonstration to about twenty arXiv papers in a week (16 to 20 November): in "Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights" (arXiv 2611.21885) Qwen3.5-27B, given its weights, graded answers and a way to run itself, edited its own weights without gradients for 6.6 points more accuracy on 490 held-out problems, while the 2B made no edit and the 4B and 9B broke themselves; a replication of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra demonstrations (arXiv 2611.21449) matched its Breakout networks and found its MNIST networks averaged 50% against 82% for a simple classifier; WeightBench (arXiv 2611.18553) has 42 tasks; and TrojanMaxxing (arXiv 2611.27425) had agents backdoor vision-language models by direct weight edits.
