**A 17-million-line Lean proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, released by an Anthropic mathematician working with Claude, was certified by the Lean and Mathlib communities within a day; the mathematics behind it is not yet understood or reviewed.** On 20 November Levent Alpöge posted a four-page note saying he had, "working with Claude", found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis that extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and released only Lean: a library of 175,187 files depending only on Mathlib. On 21 November a statement endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative said "both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct" for Mathlib's vetted `RiemannHypothesis` statement, checked with Lean's kernel and independent checkers (con-leche, con-ron and others) on an exported proof; the statement covers the Riemann Hypothesis only. Andrew Sutherland (MIT) and Michael Rubinstein (Waterloo) report independent rebuilds passing, with some of the longest kernel runs still pending on 22 November. No Claude model version is named. The human-readable draft reduces the result to thirteen hypotheses it does not yet prove, Nature quotes Andrew Granville calling the release "garbled", and Scientific American reports a rival human claim by Alessandro Masullo that is still under review. # Research & papers
- **A fixed rollout cap in GRPO becomes a learned limit on test-time scaling** (JetBrains Research, arXiv 2611.26172, 19 November): on three small models (Qwen3.5-4B, Gemma4-E2B, Nemotron-3-Nano-4B) trained with GRPO on math under a fixed rollout cap (three seeds for the main comparison), AIME accuracy at the cap rose 10.9 to 42.4 points over the base model while accuracy at the largest budget tested fell 13.1 to 30.2 points below it, and none of the tested fixes (single runs on Qwen3.5-4B) kept both the in-budget gain and the base model's test-time scaling. The introduction's wider ranges (11.7 to 46.7 and 17.2 to 35.0) are not reproduced by the paper's own tables. Mean output length on AIME at 64K tokens fell from 27.3K tokens for base Qwen3.5-4B to 5.0K after training at a 4K cap. Loss masking, DAPO's overlong penalty and Dr. GRPO did not prevent it (single-seed ablations); fine-tuning on budget-truncated traces reproduced it without RL. Checkpoints are on Hugging Face. [arXiv 2611.26172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26172) (45 min)
- **Long generations collapse at high temperature because of the sampler, not the model** (arXiv 2611.22148, 17 November, NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets track): across 29 open models of 1B to 122B, coding agents at temperature 2 resolved 0 of 100 GitHub issues with top-p, top-k or Top-H and 46 to 62 with top-nsigma (three Qwen3.5 models); in one Llama-3.1-8B example the top-p candidate pool grew from 4 to 51,845 tokens between temperature 1 and 2 while top-nsigma's stayed at 3, because top-nsigma keeps tokens within n standard deviations of the top logit, a set that temperature does not change. At temperature 1 most samplers perform about the same. Code and data released. [arXiv 2611.22148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22148) (40 min)
- **Weight-space programming went from one demonstration to about twenty arXiv papers in a week** (16 to 20 November): in "Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights" (arXiv 2611.21885) Qwen3.5-27B, given its weights, graded answers and a way to run itself, edited its own weights without gradients for 6.6 points more accuracy on 490 held-out problems, while the 2B made no edit and the 4B and 9B broke themselves; a replication of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra demonstrations (arXiv 2611.21449) matched its Breakout networks and found its MNIST networks averaged 50% against 82% for a simple classifier; WeightBench (arXiv 2611.18553) has 42 tasks; and TrojanMaxxing (arXiv 2611.27425) had agents backdoor vision-language models by direct weight edits. [arXiv 2611.21885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21885) (30 min), [arXiv 2611.21449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21449) (25 min)
- **Physics of Language Models 4.2** (Zeyuan Allen-Zhu, arXiv 2611.24963, first announced in the window): 64 from-scratch runs of Transformers and three linear-attention families at 1.3B to 8B parameters on 1 to 2T tokens; architecture differences that nearly vanish at 1.3B and 100B tokens become measurable at 1 to 2T, Canon layers (local token mixing) save about 30% of tokens for the same MMLU at 8B, and no model reliably beats chance on a simple two-hop question. [arXiv 2611.24963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24963) (60 min)
- **Sparse attention loses long-context retrieval in a few "blind" heads** (arXiv 2611.21917, 16 November): on MiniCPM4.1-8B at 64K, giving full attention to three heads lifts multi-key retrieval from 54.1% to 97.5% without training, keeping 84 to 89% of the time-to-first-token savings at 32K to 128K; on Qwen3-8B with SeerAttention-R gates, full attention in 18 of 288 KV heads lifts it from 29.8% to 97.7%. Code released. [arXiv 2611.21917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21917) (25 min)
- **Optimistic verifiers get hacked by padding** (arXiv 2611.21538, 16 November): on authorization-policy tasks where the true process reward is exactly computable, training against a verifier that only checks sufficiency drove its proxy score to 0.940 while true faithfulness fell from 0.731 to 0.667 on 96 held-out episodes; outcome-only verifiers saturated and stopped teaching; an audit of the verifier plus a short pilot predicted which failure a run would show. [arXiv 2611.21538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21538) (30 min)
- **Gumbel substitution for rollouts from untrusted samplers** (arXiv 2611.27375, 20 November): with public per-position Gumbel noise, the trainer recomputes the token its own policy would have drawn and trains on that, an exact sample from its policy without trusting the sampler's probabilities; a 9B model trained on tokens from heterogeneous samplers, with 1.21% of tokens substituted, improved on all eight held-out benchmarks. Code released. [arXiv 2611.27375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27375) (25 min)
- **Claude solves two open problems in black-hole physics** (Anthropic, 17 November): Fable 5.2 agents ran for about six weeks; one bound for the 3D gravity modular bootstrap was proved optimal for the spinless case and verified in Lean, the other completes a weak-coupling calculation in N=4 super-Yang-Mills; a physicist checked the main results. Anthropic's own account. [Anthropic: Claude and black-hole physics](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics) (12 min)
- **OpenAI's chief scientist proposes perplexity as a shared limit between labs** (Jakub Pachocki, 22 November): OpenAI's internal log-perplexity on a code dataset fell from about 0.99 for GPT-3.5 to 0.91 for GPT-4, with notable results on open math problems from a model near 0.76; he proposes common perplexity limits that labs would not cross before monitoring milestones are met, and lists the caveats himself (datasets differ between labs; reasoning does not lower perplexity).
