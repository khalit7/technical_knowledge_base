Top stories

1Research15 and 21 August

Two results moved agent scores by changing the harness around a model, not the model.

Nvidia's AVO harness, which adds memory management and a supervisor, scored 100% on ARC-AGI-3's 25-game public set with Claude Opus 5, against ARC Prize's 30% for Opus 5 at High effort, a different setup Nvidia itself says is not a direct comparison. The StateM paper's state-machine runbook took GPT-5.6 Sol to 95.3% raw on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (93.3 to 94.4% once flagged trials count as failures); its $15 run is DeepSeek-V4 Flash at 88.1%, against $575 for a Sol max reference at 88.8%.

Why it matters: state, checks and memory outside the model are something you can build today, and a harness can be worth more than a model upgrade, but compare scores only under the same harness.

2Industry17 and 19 August

Stripe is acquiring OpenRouter, the largest neutral LLM routing marketplace.

First reported on 17 August at a rumoured $7B or more and confirmed by OpenRouter on 19 August without terms; TechCrunch, citing the New York Times, puts the price at $7.5 billion, against OpenRouter's $1.3 billion valuation in May. OpenRouter says it serves 10+ trillion tokens a day from 400+ models and that the product, name and roadmap continue unchanged.

Why it matters: a router that many apps use to reach many models now belongs to a payments company, and whether it stays neutral was the week's Hacker News argument.

3Industry24 August

Hugging Face was reported to be weighing acquisition offers of $13 billion or more.

The report, from Business Insider via TechCrunch on 24 August, rests on that one outlet: offers from unnamed bidders, evaluated with bankers, against the $4.5 billion valuation of Hugging Face's last raise in 2023. Follow-up, 3 September: Nvidia confirmed it will buy Hugging Face for $12.93 billion; Jensen Huang said it stays an open platform and Nvidia compute will not be required.

Why it matters: the main hub for open weights and datasets now belongs to Nvidia, so watch its terms and defaults.

4Paper20 August

Google's open-weight DiffusionGemma writes about 1,500 tokens a second on one H100 by refining 256-token blocks in parallel.

Fine-tuned from Gemma 4 26B A4B (3.8B active) with under 10% of its training tokens, it averages about 12 denoising passes per block and about 20 tokens per forward pass: 1,479 tokens per second against 303 for Gemma 4 with multi-token prediction, at FP8 and batch size 1. Apache 2.0; submitted 31 July, it trended on 20 August.

Why it matters: diffusion decoding is now a real option for latency-bound generation, but the speed is measured one request at a time and the quality cost is on the paper page.

5Model21 August

No frontier launch: DeepSeek opened image input and OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol's developer price by more than 20%.

DeepSeek-v4-flash-vision-exp (21 August) takes up to 600 images per request at up to 384 tokens each, and was then DeepSeek's only model to accept images. The Sol cut (21 August, in effect until at least 21 November) came days after OpenRouter halved its Sol price; both rest on the issue's sources, as Reuters could not be re-read. Follow-ups: GPT-6 Sol launched on 22 September at $2 / $10 per million tokens; by 2 October deepseek-flash took images at up to 1,024 tokens each.

Why it matters: an image's cost in tokens is now a number you can budget, and frontier prices were falling within weeks of each other.

6Tools17 and 20 August

Cursor launched Origin, Git hosting built for agents, on the day GitHub was down for almost eight hours.

Origin (17 August) rolled out in early beta on all paid plans with repos, pull requests, code browsing and GitHub sync, with agent-native features to follow, beside a 27-minute post on scaling Git. GitHub's postmortem (20 August) blamed a component in its Central US data centre that failed to scale at a new traffic peak, for an outage of 7 hours 47 minutes; monthly commits grew from 1.4 billion to 2.9 billion since April.

Why it matters: agents are multiplying the load on Git hosting, so where your agents push is now a reliability choice.

New in the knowledge base

Papers

  • StateM: a versioned state-machine runbook with checked transitions around a fixed CLI agent; strong Terminal-Bench 2.1 scores, but the runbook was developed on the same 89 tasks it is scored on, and the only held-out test moved 0.55 to 1.34 points.
  • EnvHarness: wrapping frozen training environments and customising them to a policy's weaknesses gave skills that scored above those from the original environments on all five benchmarks, mostly within three-run noise.
  • Demystifying Agent Skills: the same trajectories distilled into a SKILL.md beat them pasted in as workflow memory, but do not clearly beat no prior experience; skills add a misapplication failure.
  • Learn What's Left, Not What's Mastered: multi-reward GRPO that down-weights objectives the policy has already saturated; small gains over GDPO, each from one training run.
  • DiffusionGemma Technical Report: Gemma 4 turned into a block diffusion model at about 7x the speed of the same weights decoding one token at a time, at a real quality cost on harder benchmarks, measured at one request.
