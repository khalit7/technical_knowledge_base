Why two models in one session can cost less: the prompt cache

One illustrative coding session of twelve turns, run three ways and drawn to one scale. Back row: one model throughout. Next row: a router that switches models mid-session. Front two rows: Fusion, a frontier lead and a cheaper sidekick, each in its own context. Each column is the input a model reads on that turn: the pale part comes from that model's prompt cache (the glass plate at the head of its row), the solid part is processed at full price, and a red column is a full re-read. Cached work belongs to one model, so switching models re-reads the whole history; Fusion passes short briefs and results (purple dots) between its two models, so neither cache breaks. Illustrative session: the turn sizes (20K to start, 4K a turn, an 8K sidekick prompt, 2K briefs and results) are invented to show the mechanism. The mechanism is Cognition's; the measured runs are in the table under the step list.

Step by step

  1. Turn 1: every session starts cold. All three read the same 20K starting context (system prompt and task) at full price; nothing is cached yet. Cached computation belongs to one model on one provider, so each row has a cache plate per model it uses.
  2. Turns 2 to 4: the prefix is cached. Every turn resends the whole history (24K, 28K, 32K), but the provider caches the shared prefix, so only the new 4K a turn (solid) is processed at full price; the rest (pale) is read from cache at a small fraction of the price. At turn 4 Fusion's lead hands the sidekick a 2K brief: constraints and success criteria, not the transcript.
  3. Turns 5 and 6: the sidekick works in its own context. The cheaper sidekick starts from its own 8K prompt plus the brief (10K, read once at full price), then explores, edits, builds and tests in a context cached in its own cache. Nothing is resent to the lead.
  4. Turn 7: a router switches to a cheaper model. The cheaper model has never seen this session, so the whole 44K history is processed at full price: a cache miss as big as the context (red).
  5. Turns 8 and 9: the new model caches its own copy. The switching session's cheaper model now caches the prefix for itself and the full-price part shrinks back to 4K a turn; the sidekick carries on in its own context.
  6. Turn 10: back to the frontier model. Most cached inputs expire after about five minutes, and this illustration takes the frontier model's cache to have expired while it sat idle: the whole 56K history is re-processed, at frontier prices this time.
  7. Turns 11 and 12: a result comes back, not a transcript. The sidekick reports a short 2K result; the lead appends it to a prefix it still has cached (Cognition: each agent keeps its own persistent, cached context), so it re-reads nothing, then reviews at turn 12. The switching session is warm again, after paying one full re-read per switch.
  8. The session: add up the rows. One model: 504K of input, 440K of it from cache (87%), no re-reads. Switching: the same 504K, but 156K at full price (69% from cache) after two full re-reads. Fusion: 296K, 228K from cache (77%), no re-reads; its total is small because the sidekick's context starts small, and its hit rate is below one model's only because the sidekick starts cold. In Artificial Analysis's measured runs (below) Fusion used more tokens and nearly three times the steps, but a higher share came from cache.
