Why the exponents differed

Porian et al. (2024) reproduced Kaplan's setup in a modern training stack and undid its choices one at a time ({{Porian Figure 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19146v1#S1.F1}}):

Two corrections to the usual story. First, the uncounted cost that matters is the output layer (the "head" that produces logits), which Kaplan's N and C leave out; the input embedding costs no matrix multiply. Second, matching the cosine decay to each run's length, which Chinchilla proposed as the explanation, barely moved the exponent; per-size tuning of learning rate, batch and AdamW β2 did the rest, and with it even a constant learning rate gives Chinchilla's exponent ({{Porian §3.4, §3.5|https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19146v1#S3.SS4}}). Porian et al. also recover Kaplan's adjusted 0.73 (they get 0.717) by reintroducing the FLOP count and warmup issues with tuned hyperparameters (their Appendix, Figure 17).

What changed and what survived

Changed

  • The allocation. N ∝ C0.73 became N ∝ C0.46 to 0.50, tokens per parameter roughly constant near 20 instead of falling with scale ({{Chinchilla Table 2|https://arxiv.org/html/2203.15556v1#S3.T2}}).
  • Counting. The output layer goes into N and C (Porian et al., who also read Chinchilla's Approach 3 as counting the embeddings: {{Porian Appendix B|https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19146v1#A2}}).
  • Fitting. A saturating form with an irreducible loss, L = E + A/Nα + B/Dβ with E = 1.69, replaced pure power laws that bend nowhere ({{Chinchilla Eq. 2 and D.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2203.15556v1#S3.SS3}}); isoFLOP sweeps replaced reading frontiers off checkpoints.
  • The objective. Training-compute optimality gave way to deliberate overtraining for cheaper inference: Llama 3 trains its 8B and 70B models "much longer than is compute-optimal" ({{Llama 3 §1|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v1#S1}}).

Survived

  • Loss as a smooth, predictable power law in scale, used to plan runs: Llama 3 fits its own isoFLOP law to choose 405B for 3.8 × 1025 FLOPs (its fit predicts 402B on 16.55T tokens) ({{Llama 3 §3.2.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v1#S3.SS2.SSS1}}).
  • C ≈ 6ND as the budget equation ({{Chinchilla §3.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2203.15556v1#S3.SS3}}).
  • Shape is second order; scale is first.
  • Larger models are more sample-efficient; batch size grows with scale (the critical batch).
  • The L(N, D) overfitting picture: data can grow sublinearly in model size before repetition hurts.
