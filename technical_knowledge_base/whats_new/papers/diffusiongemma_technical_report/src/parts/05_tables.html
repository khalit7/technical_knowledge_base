How Mercury 2's speed was estimated (Appendix E)

Mercury 2 is closed, so the authors queried it through OpenRouter (maximum sequence 50,000 tokens, caching disabled) in two rounds, 9 to 11 and 25 to 26 July 2026, with consistent results, and fitted Total time = α · fresh prefill tokens + β · output tokens + γ by non-negative least squares per benchmark, taking 1/β as the generation speed ({{Eq. 14|ax:A5.E14}}): 452.6 tokens/s on GPQA-Diamond and 525.5 on LiveCodeBench-v6 (489 average, used in {{Figure 1|ax:S0.F1}}), and 600 (high effort) and 547 (medium) over the seven benchmarks of Table 3. Some HumanEval and Natural2Code outputs were corrupted runs stuck in a thinking loop to the 50,000-token limit at very high speed, which biases the estimates upwards and is why high effort comes out faster than medium. Inception reports about 1,000 tokens/s on Blackwell GPUs and Artificial Analysis a median of about 987 on undisclosed hardware (both retrieved 28 July 2026); the authors attribute the difference to hardware, serving optimisations and datasets ({{Appendix E|ax:A5}}).

Derived: tokens / E2E should equal TPS (it does, within 0.7%); ms per pass = TPF / TPS, the per-pass time implied at each benchmark's context length; tokens / forwards is the ratio of the printed averages, which sits below the printed TPF, so the TPF column is presumably an average of per-answer ratios; 256 / (DNS + 1) is Eq. 10 for answers made of whole canvases (each canvas costing its denoising steps plus one encode pass); it lands within 9% of the printed TPF on every benchmark, the rest being partial last canvases and per-answer averaging.
