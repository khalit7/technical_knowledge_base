Score a group of harnesses

Stage III is where JIT-Agent is supposed to learn to beat the harnesses it already has. Each training step samples a group of candidate harnesses for one task, runs them, and turns their reward, latency and cost into one advantage per candidate with Evo-GDPO ({{Eq. 6 and 7|ax:S4.E6}}); the same measurements decide which candidates join the bank. This tab runs those two equations on a group you choose, next to the GRPO-style alternative that GDPO was designed to replace: add the three channel rewards first, then normalise once.

Try: start with the Table 3 group and step through both methods. Under GRPO the token channel, measured in thousands, swamps the score; under Evo-GDPO each channel is put on the same footing first. Then raise the incumbent's score until no candidate matches it, and watch both efficiency channels go silent.

The group, step by step

  1. Measure the group. Each candidate harness ran under the same executor, budget and seeds as the incumbent, the best harness retrieved from the bank. Bars: score r, latency ℓ and cost κ; the line in each column is the incumbent.
  2. Three channel rewards (Eq. 6). Reward channel: the score plus a bonus λevo·[r − br]+ for beating the incumbent. Latency and cost channels: the saving against the incumbent, paid only if the candidate at least matches its score; greyed rows are gated off.
  3. Normalise each channel separately. Each channel becomes a z-score within the group, (x − mean) / (std + ε). A channel measured in dollars and one measured in thousands of tokens now sit on the same scale, so neither can drown the other.
  4. Mix with reward dominant (Eq. 7). AΣ = wrewArew + wlatAlat + wcostAcost, with wrew > wlat + wcost. This is the advantage the PPO update pushes each candidate's tokens up or down by.
  5. Update the bank. Independently of the gradient, a candidate joins the bank if it matches or beats the incumbent's score and is strictly better on score, latency or cost. Kept harnesses become references for later tasks.
  1. Measure the group. The same group and the same incumbent.
  2. The same three channel rewards. Identical to Evo-GDPO up to here: the gate and the bonus are part of the rewards, not of the normalisation.
  3. Mix the raw channels first. GRPO-style training adds the weighted channel rewards into one number per candidate before any normalisation. The stacked bars show what each channel contributes; whichever channel has the biggest raw scale dominates.
  4. Normalise once. The single sum becomes a z-score within the group. Candidates whose sums differ only through a small-scale channel end up with nearly the same advantage: the collapse GDPO describes.
  5. Update the bank. The bank rule does not depend on the advantages, so the same candidates are kept; only the training signal differs.

Advantages are within-group z-scores, (x − mean) / (standard deviation + 10⁻⁶), as in {{Eq. 7|ax:S4.E7}}; the batch-level renormalisation that follows only rescales one group and is left out. The bank column applies the retention rule of {{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1.SSS0.Px6}} against the incumbent: keep a candidate if its score is at least br and it is strictly better on score, latency or cost. The paper gives no values for λevo or the weights; the defaults here are illustrative and only respect wrew > wlat + wcost.

The harnesses it was trained on

The authors released 2,852 harnesses from the training data, written for 1,427 task instructions, each as the four Python modules plus a prompt file ({{JIT-Agent/jit-meta-harness|https://huggingface.co/datasets/JIT-Agent/jit-meta-harness}}). The bars count, per source of task, how many have an F module that exposes every tool, how many have no planner, and how many keep plain full history; the counts come from a keyword rule on each module's docstring ({{harness_stats.py|tab:t-tables:ovH}}), checked by eye on samples, so read them as close rather than exact.
