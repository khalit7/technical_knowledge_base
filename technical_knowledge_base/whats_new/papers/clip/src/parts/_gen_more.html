openai/CLIP

Best resources

CLIP: Connecting text and images

OpenAI blog: the authors' condensed account, with the zero-shot classifier construction shown visually.

(15 min)

Contrastive Representation Learning

Lilian Weng: places CLIP's loss in the InfoNCE family and derives the surrounding contrastive-learning theory.

(40 min)

Multimodality and Large Multimodal Models

Chip Huyen: CLIP as the foundation of the modern vision-language stack, with a clear walkthrough of the training objective and what came after.

(40 min)

mlfoundations/open_clip

LAION and collaborators: the open reproduction (repo, about 20 minutes for the README and the entry path); the fastest way to read a working implementation and see how the recipe scales on LAION-scale data.

(20 min)

Data Determines Distributional Robustness in CLIP

Fang et al., 2022: the controlled follow-up on section 3.3: of five candidate causes, the diversity of the training distribution explains the robustness; language supervision and the contrastive loss contribute little.

(40 min)

Sigmoid Loss for Language Image Pre-Training (SigLIP)

Zhai et al., 2023: the loss most open vision towers now use: each pair is a separate yes or no, so the batch no longer has to be normalised as a whole.

(40 min)

Connected papers in this knowledge base

An Image is Worth 16x16 Words (ViT)

2020: supplies CLIP's best image encoder; CLIP is the main route by which ViT reached multimodal models.

Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3)

2020: the zero-shot task transfer and prompt engineering CLIP imports into vision, and the WebText-style data behind WIT.

Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models

2020: CLIP's zero-shot error follows the same kind of smooth log-log trend in compute.

High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models (Stable Diffusion)

2021: conditions image generation on CLIP text-encoder outputs through cross-attention.

Attention Is All You Need (Transformer)

2017: both of CLIP's encoders are Transformers (the image one in its ViT variants).

Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)

2020: the diffusion line that DALL-E 2 (unCLIP) later conditioned on CLIP image embeddings.
