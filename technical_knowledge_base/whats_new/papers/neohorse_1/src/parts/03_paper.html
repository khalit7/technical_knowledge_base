Problem

[[S1|§1 Introduction]]

Post-training data for agents is usually curated once, offline, against a fixed task distribution. The serving harness that actually meets the traffic is treated as downstream infrastructure with no say in what the next checkpoint learns. That wastes the one signal a deployed system has that a curated dataset never will: which requests the current model handled badly, and which model in the fleet had to take them.

The report frames this as the missing piece of recursive self-improvement (RSI): a system improving itself needs "a concrete mechanism through which an AI system observes its own capabilities and converts that evidence into the next round of learning" ({{Abstract|ax:abstract1}}). Its claim is that a deployed routing harness already contains one. An agent working inside a harness leaves a record of decisions, tool calls and outcomes, and a router that picks which model serves each turn also leaves, as a side effect, a judgement of how hard that turn was ({{§1|ax:S1}}). The thesis, stated as a loop: "what the system learns to do shapes what it learns from next".

A harness here is the execution layer that manages an agent's context, tools and interaction with its environment; agentic routing lets that layer choose a model per request and per interaction state ({{§1|ax:S1}}; routing by difficulty in general, routers and cascades, is on {{Gateways and routing|n:3f15c17b0d0d81e4a964c129a3e520ae}}). The system builds on the authors' earlier {{Agentic Routing|https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11399}} paper, combining a heterogeneous model pool with several harnesses, including their open {{OpenSquilla|https://github.com/TokenRhythm/opensquilla}}, across real-world tasks.

Idea: let the router write the syllabus

[[S1.F2|Figure 2]]

Five components, and the router is the spine: the hero above names them in order ({{§3|ax:S3}}, {{§4|ax:S4}}). The router produces a labelled judgement of task difficulty as a side effect of serving; curation makes subscene-level labels, which is what makes partial-trajectory supervision possible; the curriculum takes its difficulty ordering from observed traffic rather than a heuristic; and allocation turns evaluation feedback into the next mixture, which closes the loop.

The five components as the paper describes them
  • Heterogeneous model pool with intelligent routing. Requests are routed across service tiers by predicted capability demand rather than by a static rule, so the router is continuously producing a labelled judgement about task difficulty as a side effect of serving.
  • Data curation pipeline. User interactions become training examples through structural validation, a six-dimensional semantic evaluation, and subscene-level labelling, which is finer-grained than per-conversation labelling and is what makes partial-trajectory supervision possible.
  • Three-stage curriculum. Supervised fine-tuning is ordered by those routing signals, so difficulty ordering comes from observed traffic rather than from a heuristic.
  • Routing-guided on-policy distillation. A fixed teacher supervises the student's own generated responses, with starting contexts introduced under the same staged progression.
  • Capability-guided allocation. Evaluation feedback is converted directly into training-mixture decisions, which closes the loop back to the pool.

Toggle between the usual way agent post-training data is made (curate once, train, ship) and NeoHorse-1's loop, where the shipped model goes back into the pool and its traffic seeds the next round. The last step of the loop is drawn dashed because the report ran it once (§6).

Same request stream; one round.
  1. Serve and route. Each user turn reaches the routing harness. The router estimates its capability demand from the request, recent dialogue, earlier routing decisions and execution state, and assigns tier C0 to C3 (bounded, default, multi-step, maximum capability); policy may adjust it, and a model from the pool serves it ({{§3.4|ax:S3.SS4}}). The streams follow the illustrative pool of 120 turns: 39, 36, 29 and 16 assigned to C0 to C3.
  2. Record three routing fields. For every turn the harness keeps the trajectory (requests, reasoning, tool calls, observations, recovery, outcome) and three separate fields: the router's raw prediction, the policy-adjusted decision and the tier actually served. Joined, that is a prediction, action, outcome record; five signals kept per served turn ({{§3.1|ax:S3.SS1}}, {{§3.4|ax:S3.SS4}}).
  3. Curate. Deduplicate and decontaminate; a rule-based structural gate sorts each trajectory into complete, partially recoverable (only its causally closed pieces go on) or quarantined; then six semantic verdicts (PASS, WARN, FAIL, NOT_EVALUATED) and Scene, Goal and Outcome labels per subscene ({{§3.2|ax:S3.SS2}}, {{§3.3|ax:S3.SS3}}). The paper prints no count for any gate, so the bins have no sizes.
  4. Curriculum SFT in three stages. Admitted harness turns plus public data form the mixture, one user turn per example. Re-estimated routing scores order the examples into three stages of about a third each, harder ones later, some easy ones held back (outlined in red); one optimizer and schedule throughout ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}). On the illustrative pool the mean soft score of the three stages is 0.46, 1.19 and 2.15, with 8 easy examples held back.
  5. On-policy distillation. The student generates from recorded starting contexts, scheduled in the same three stages; a fixed teacher scores each of its tokens; the loss is a reverse KL over the student's top-K candidates plus one tail bin ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}). Drawn: one position of the toy 16-token vocabulary at K = 4, the student's bars in front and the teacher's behind. The teacher puts 0.570 in the tail bin against the student's 0.169, and the coarsened KL is 0.349 of the full 0.522 nats.
  6. Evaluate. The new checkpoint is scored. Real numbers: NeoHorse-1 minus its own base on the ten benchmarks of {{Tables 1 and 2|ax:S5.T1}}, the 4B row in front (up on all ten, macro +5.93) and the 9B row behind (macro +3.44; LiveCodeBench and IFBench 0.00, IFEval −0.37). In the design, a stratified suite gives a model-deficiency profile by attribute, quality dimension, outcome and tier ({{§3.5|ax:S3.SS5}}); the paper prints none.
  7. Allocate and return. The profile shifts the next mixture toward regions where the model underperforms; this changes the data, not the objective. The checkpoint rejoins the pool behind the router, its traffic produces new trajectories, and the loop can run again ({{§3.5|ax:S3.SS5}}). Dashed: the report ran this loop once, so whether gains accumulate is untested ({{§6|ax:S6}}).
  1. Curate once. The usual route: a dataset of instruction and agent trajectories is collected or synthesised against a fixed task distribution, then frozen ({{§1|ax:S1}}, {{§2.1|ax:S2.SS1}}). The serving harness stands apart.
  2. Train, shuffled. Supervised fine-tuning on the fixed set, in shuffled order; nothing says which examples are hard. The same illustrative pool split at random has stage means 1.24, 1.23 and 1.32: every third looks like the whole pool.
  3. Evaluate and ship. Benchmarks are reported and the checkpoint is shipped.
  4. Serve. The harness serves the model and routes requests, but its records (which turns were hard, which model had to take them, what failed) never reach the next dataset: zero signals reused per served turn. The paper's point is that this signal is free.
