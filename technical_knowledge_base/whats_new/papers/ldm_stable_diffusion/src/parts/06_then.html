Then and now: what changed and what survived

9 systems in eight stretches, December 2021 to August 2024.

The LDM recipe as a card, changed one system at a time from 2021 to 2024, in eight stretches (the two video systems share one). Highlighted lines are what that stretch changed; the rest is carried over. It plays once when scrolled into view; any control stops it. Each change is quoted or paraphrased from the system's own paper, model card, config or report (src/inputs/later_extracts.txt, src/inputs/code/).

  1. December 2021: LDM, this paper's text-to-image model. The starting card: Table 15, Table 17 and the released txt2img-1p4B config.
  2. August 2022: Stable Diffusion v1. "A Latent Diffusion Model on 512x512 images from a subset of the LAION-5B database", "a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 text encoder" (SD README; v1-inference.yaml). The architecture is this paper's, with open weights.
  3. Late 2022: Stable Diffusion 2.0 and 2.1. SDXL's Table 1 lists the 2.x line: the same UNet layout with a larger open text encoder (release date not in a fetched source).
  4. December 2022: DiT. "We use an off-the-shelf pre-trained variational autoencoder (VAE) model from Stable Diffusion": the latent is kept and the backbone replaced ({{ViT|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa231cfcde6c1954d}}).
  5. July 2023: SDXL. Still a latent UNet: "a latent diffusion model for text-to-image synthesis" with a larger cross-attention context.
  6. November 2023 to February 2024: video. {{Stable Video Diffusion|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127}}: "Latent diffusion models trained for 2D image synthesis have been turned into generative video models by inserting temporal layers." {{Sora|https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/}}: "Sora is trained on and subsequently generates videos within this compressed latent space" (OpenAI report; no paper, sizes undisclosed).
  7. March 2024: Stable Diffusion 3. Latent space, a Transformer and a flow objective together; f = 8 is kept for every model in the paper.
  8. August 2024: FLUX.1. "A 12 billion parameter rectified flow transformer" (model card); the autoencoder in the code is ch_mult [1, 2, 4, 4], z_channels 16, the same f = 8 layout as this paper's.
What survived

Generating in the latent of a separately trained, mildly compressing autoencoder with f = 8 (every system on the card), a rescaled KL latent, conditioning on a text encoder's token sequence, and classifier-free guidance. What did not: the UNet (Transformers in DiT, SD3, FLUX and Sora), noise prediction (velocity and rectified flow), 4 latent channels (16 in SD3 and FLUX), and a text encoder trained from scratch (frozen CLIP, OpenCLIP and T5 encoders instead).

The plug-ins built on the cross-attention interface, ControlNet and IP-Adapter, and the latent models for audio, are in the paper tab's Why it matters.
