Re-rank the leaderboard

RocketEval's headline is a ranking claim: on WildBench, a 2B judge orders 12 models about as well as GPT-4o does. This tab reruns that ranking from the grades the authors released, and lets you ask the two questions the paper does not: how much does the correlation move with the sample of queries, and how noisy can a judge be on single responses before the ranking breaks?

Data: GPT-4o's released grades (1 to 10) of the 12 test models' answers on the 1,013 WildBench queries all twelve share, and the Arena Elo ratings (hard prompts, English, 2024-09-17) with their 95% intervals from the authors' config/rankings/wildbench_test.json ({{Joinn/RocketEval|https://huggingface.co/datasets/Joinn/RocketEval}}, {{RocketEval-ICLR|https://github.com/Joinn99/RocketEval-ICLR}}).

1. GPT-4o's ranking, rerun

Try: with all queries and no added noise, the defaults reproduce Table 3's GPT-4o row (Kendall 0.909, Spearman 0.979) independently, from the released grades. Lines that cross are discordant pairs. Then lower the number of queries and draw again.

2. How much does one ranking move?

Each draw resamples the queries with replacement (a bootstrap over WildBench's queries), adds the noise you chose, ranks the 12 models by their mean grade and compares with Arena Elo. The histogram shows 200 such rankings; the shaded band holds the middle 95%. The dashed lines mark Table 3's printed values for GPT-4o (0.979), Gemma-2-2B (0.965) and Mistral-Nemo (0.986).

3. Which pairs it gets wrong

The 66 pairs of the 12 models, in Arena order. A filled cell is a pair the current run orders differently from Arena; its shade in the 200-draw view is how often that happens.

What this can and cannot show
  • Measured: GPT-4o's ranking and its sensitivity to the query sample come straight from released grades; nothing is fitted.
  • Illustrative: the "noisy judge" is GPT-4o's grade plus independent Gaussian noise per response (σ) and per model (τ). The presets choose σ so that a run's correlation with GPT-4o's grade of the same response matches what the released small-judge gradings measure (0.47, 0.41 and 0.29; The paper tab, Replay). Real small judges are not GPT-4o plus noise: their errors are correlated across responses of one model (a style the judge likes or dislikes, as in Figure 11's Llama series). τ is that systematic part, and it does not average out.
  • The lesson: with 1,013 queries, independent noise large enough to bring single-response agreement down to 0.29 still leaves the ranking near 0.96 (mean Spearman over 200 draws); a per-model bias of only 0.2 grade points costs more than all the noise that takes single-response agreement from 1 down to 0.41 (try the last two presets). That is the paper's split between Table 2 and Table 3, made quantitative.
