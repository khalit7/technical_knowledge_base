Checkpoint boundaries: SCO

[[S3.SS3|§3.3, Figure 4, Appendix D]]

Gradient checkpointing throws a layer's internals away after forward and recomputes them in backward (the trade in general: {{Distributed training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}), but one tensor per checkpointed layer survives: its input hℓ (Nℓ × H), held from forward until that layer is recomputed. That is the checkpoint boundary, and with many layers and long sequences the boundaries add up ({{§3.3|ax:S3.SS3}}). SCO (selective checkpoint offload) walks the layers in forward order and copies each boundary that still fits a pinned host-memory budget to the CPU, asynchronously, then frees the device copy; layers that do not fit keep theirs. In backward, while layer ℓ recomputes from hℓ, the next boundary hℓ−1 comes back on a separate stream, so at most two restored boundaries are on the device at once ({{Eq. 4|ax:S3.E4}}):

MHBMSCO ≤ 2NmaxHb,    Mhost ≤ |S| NmaxHb

Step through it on gpt-oss-20b as in {{Table 3|ax:S3.T3}}: 47 checkpoint boundaries of 0.3735 GiB each per GPU (557,056 tokens / 8 ranks × hidden 2,880 × 2 bytes); the top row is device memory, the bottom pinned host memory. The counters show boundary memory only; the measured peak, which includes everything else, is beside them.

  1. Forward: offload while the budget lasts. Forward reaches each layer in turn, and its input, the boundary, would stay on the GPU until backward. SCO copies each one that still fits the host budget to pinned host memory on a copy stream and frees the device copy; once the budget is full (21 boundaries at 8 GiB, 42 at 16 GiB) the rest stay on the device.
  2. Backward through the boundaries kept on the device. Backward visits the layers in reverse, recomputing each from its boundary and then freeing it. The last layers' boundaries were never offloaded (unless the budget is unlimited), so they are used and freed in place.
  3. Backward through the offloaded ones. While one layer recomputes, the next boundary is copied back on a separate stream, one layer ahead, so at most two restored boundaries are on the device at once (Eq. 4). With checkpointing only there is nothing to restore: every boundary was on the device all along.
  4. Step done. The pinned host memory is released at the end of the step. The measured peak HBM ({{Table 3|ax:S3.T3}}) is 139.790 GiB with checkpointing only, 133.546 at 8 GiB, 125.677 at 16 GiB and 123.728 with every boundary offloaded, at about the same throughput.

The host cap is a correctness condition: a rank failing an allocation while the others enter a collective would deadlock the job, so the cap is set at or above |S|NmaxHb and is per micro-batch ({{Appendix D|ax:A4}}).

On gpt-oss-20b (8 H200s, 47 boundaries), an 8 GiB host budget takes 21 boundaries, about 7.8 GiB per GPU, off the device. How much does the measured peak HBM fall?

6.2 GiB, from 139.790 to 133.546 GiB ({{Table 3|ax:S3.T3}}). Each step of the budget lowers the peak by somewhat less than the payload it offloads, because the peak includes the restored boundaries and everything else live at that moment. The boundary size itself recomputes from the configuration alone: (557,056 tokens / 8 ranks) × 2,880 hidden × 2 bytes = 0.3735 GiB, so 8 GiB holds 21 and 16 GiB holds 42, exactly the paper's counts.

Bars: Table 3's peak HBM; the darker part is the boundary payload still on the device (47 minus the offloaded count, times 0.3735 GiB). Throughput moves by 1.9% with no trend; node RAM rises by about what is pinned (190.9 GiB against 8 × 23.5). Step through it {{above|tab:t-read:scx}}.

Optimizer: OffloadStreamAdamW

[[S3.SS4|§3.4, Figure 5, Appendix E]]

Offloading AdamW's fp32 master weights and moments to CPU memory (ZeRO-Offload, ZeRO-Infinity) frees 12 bytes per parameter on the GPU, but the CPU update is slow, and while it runs the GPU is idle and, having released its activations, nearly empty ({{§3.4|ax:S3.SS4}}). OffloadStreamAdamW uses that idle GPU as the update engine: each rank's state is cut into buckets of at most β parameters that rotate through s staging slots and three streams: copy master weights, moments and final gradients to the GPU; run a fused AdamW update and refresh the BF16 working weights; write the updated fp32 state back ({{Eq. 5|ax:S3.E5}}):

Tstream = G · max(TH2D, Tupdate, TD2H) + O(TH2D + Tupdate + TD2H),   Mstage = O(sβ)

For G buckets the step costs about one bucket's slowest stage per bucket, plus one pipeline fill. The floor for any offloaded optimizer is the host-link round trip of 12Θ/W bytes (116 GiB per rank at Θ = 1012, W = 96, {{Figure 5|ax:S3.F5}}), so the design hides the update and the write-back behind that transfer.

Step through it: one optimizer step on one GPU's share of gpt-oss-20b (about 2.6B parameters, 26 buckets of up to 142M, {{Table 14|ax:A5.T14}}) on one time axis, with s staging slots limiting how many buckets are in flight; CPU Adam is the measured 3.95 s serial pass on the host.

  1. The first bucket uploads. Bucket 1's fp32 master weights, two moments and final gradient, 16 bytes per parameter, cross the host link into a staging slot on the GPU; nothing else can start yet.
  2. The pipeline fills. Bucket 1 is updated by the fused AdamW kernel and written back (12 bytes per parameter) while bucket 2 uploads. With two or three slots the next upload never waits; with one slot, bucket g + 1 cannot upload until bucket g has been written back, so upload, update and write-back take turns.
  3. Steady state: the link is the limit. With two slots the upload stream is never idle, so the step costs about one bucket's slowest stage per bucket, the transfer floor of Eq. 5; a third slot buys almost nothing, as Table 14 measured (1.930 against 1.951 s). With one slot the link is busy only about half the time.
  4. Drain and done. The last update and write-back finish the step: two slots at the measured 1.93 s by construction, three the same up to the fill, one slot about twice as long (one slot was not measured). The overlap, not the GPU, is what buys the speed.
  1. One serial pass on the host. The host CPU runs AdamW over all of this GPU's 2.6B parameters in one serial pass (ZeRO-Offload's AVX kernel). The GPU has released its activations and waits, nearly empty.
  2. Done at 3.95 s. Measured ({{Table 4|ax:S3.T4}}). The GPU was idle for all of it.

Illustrative timing. Bucket times are not printed. Upload and write-back times are set in proportion to bytes (16 : 12), the update to a quarter of the upload, and scaled so that two slots take the measured 1.93 s under Eq. 5; one and three slots then follow from the same times. The CPU bar is the measured 3.95 s.

Results ({{Table 4|ax:S3.T4}}, gpt-oss-20b on 8 H200s, the same fp32 state in host memory on both sides): the step takes 1.93 s against 3.95 s for ZeRO-Offload's AVX CPU Adam, 2.05 times faster, with 4.234 GiB of staging per GPU. A 100M-parameter bucket with two slots wins; a third slot is within 1.1% and adds a bucket's worth of staging, which the paper reads as the pipeline being transfer-bound ({{Table 14|ax:A5.T14}}). The staging column recomputes exactly as slots × largest bucket × 16 bytes (fp32 master, two moments, gradient) in every row. Exactness has one condition: a bucket can only start once its gradient is final and any cross-bucket transform is known, so global gradient clipping must wait for the global norm; per-bucket or predictive clipping would change the update rule and sits outside the exactness claim ({{Appendix E|ax:A5}}).

Composition and the per-rank budget

[[S3.SS5|§3.5, Eq. 6]]

The four operators bound disjoint live sets (dispatch temporaries inside MoE layers, logit strips at the head, boundaries spanning layers, optimizer state at update time), none needs another, and each can be enabled alone ({{§3.5|ax:S3.SS5}}). For the full system they sit inside the authors' Mixture-of-Parallelisms (MoP) layout ({{arXiv 2607.01844|https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01844}}): ZeRO-3 for dense weights, a sequence-to-head all-to-all for attention (Ulysses-style), and expert-parallel placement for experts. The same W ranks form overlapping sub-groups of sizes D (sequence), Ep (experts) and P (vocabulary), chosen independently rather than as factors of W = d · t · p. The per-rank budget is then ({{Eq. 6|ax:S3.E6}}):

Mpeak = 4Θ/W + O(sβ) + O(NHb) + O(EpqHb) + O(NVb/P) + O(NHb)

Weights and gradients, optimizer staging, attention, dispatch, logit strips, boundaries. Every term is fixed by the model and launch configuration once N ≤ Nmax, so feasibility can be checked before launch. Two savings meet in the first terms: host offload takes persistent state from 16Θ/W to 4Θ/W (ZeRO-Offload's contribution), and OffloadStreamAdamW reduces the time that offload costs.

Results end to end

[[S4|§4, Figures 6 and 7, Appendix A]]

All four together inside MoP train three MoE models of 120B, 241B and 667B parameters on 16, 32 and 64 H200s, from scratch ({{§4|ax:S4}}, {{Appendix A|ax:A1}}). The models share one architecture and differ only in depth (7, 14 and 39 layers): hidden width 7,168, dense FFN width 18,432, 384 routed experts of width 2,048, top-8, vocabulary 200,000, the widths of {{Kimi K2|https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K2-Instruct/blob/main/config.json}} (which has 61 layers). Routing is dropless, balanced by {{DeepSeek-V3's|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} auxiliary-loss-free bias, with Ep = W, P = 8 and D from 2 to 16 by context length. The baseline, FSDP2-best, is the highest-throughput fit from a sweep of FSDP2 with tensor, context and expert parallelism and optimizer CPU offload on a TorchTitan-based stack, at the same model and GPU count.

FSDP2-best runs out of memory past 128K, 32K and 64K tokens at the three scales; the stack trains at 1M at all three, 8, 32 and 16 times the reach ({{Figure 7|ax:S4.F7}}). Where both run, it is 7.6 times faster at 128K on 120B and 10.4 times at 64K on 667B ({{Figure 6|ax:S4.F6}}). Its TFLOP/s rises with context as attention work per token grows, from 91 to 110 at 128K to 213 to 233 at 1M (9 to 11% and 21.5 to 23.6% of the H200's 989.5 dense BF16 TFLOP/s, derived), while FSDP2-best stays below 40. The largest global batch is 1.5M, 1.8M and 3M tokens, 12, 7 and 3 times the baseline's. For quality, fine-tuning gpt-oss-20b on Nemotron-Math at 128K gives 59.8% Avg@8 on AIME 2025 on the stack against 59.6% on FSDP2 with the same recipe (45.2% before fine-tuning, {{Appendix A|ax:A1}}).

Limits and related work

[[S5|§5 Related Work, §7 Limitations]]
Related work (§5): who else chunks dispatch, fuses cross-entropy or offloads

Related work ({{§5|ax:S5}}). FasterMoE, Lina, PipeMoE and ScheMoE also chunk dispatch, but size the chunks for bandwidth, so buffers still follow the realised load; placement methods (FlexMoE, SmartMoE, LLEP) move skew around and compose with the budget. Cut Cross-Entropy and Liger assume one device, Megatron's vocabulary-parallel loss a replicated batch, Ring Attention a ring where only one operand can move. DeepSpeed and PyTorch offload whole checkpoint regions where SCO offloads a budgeted subset; 8-bit optimizers and Adam-mini shrink optimizer state by changing numerics, which this paper excludes.

Limits the authors state ({{§7|ax:S7}}): all-to-all and ring traffic assume a fast interconnect, and the cheap extra chunks measured on intra-node NVLink may not be cheap on a slower fabric; a smaller budget tightens the bound but raises the chunk count by the same factor, and forward time is flat only over part of that range, so c comes from a measured curve; checkpoint and optimizer streaming spend host memory and link bandwidth, which binds on nodes with less than the 2 TB used here; topology sensitivity and automatic choice of (D, Ep, P, c, β) remain open.
