What the agents found: the winning recipe

[[S4.SS4|§4.4, Algorithm 1, Table 3, Appendix B]]

The best contribution at cutoff is a chain of 73 Python modules, each importing its parent and modifying the model the parent returns; ten earlier variants imported for shared code bring the import closure to 83. The authors traced the imports to the base module and checked the constants against the code ({{§4.4|ax:S4.SS4}}). It has two stages.

Stage A: copy what the donors predict, not what they are. Instead of copying weights, the recipe asks other models what token comes next and stores the answer, compressed, in the target's embedding and output head ({{§4.4|ax:S4.SS4}}, {{Table 5|ax:A2.T5}}):

  1. Six donors, one vocabulary. GPT-2 small and large and Cerebras-GPT 111M to 1.3B all use GPT-2's 50,257 tokens. Each block's height is its blend weight in Table 5: 0.725 on GPT-2 small.
  2. Ask what comes next, 28 ways. Each donor gives its next-token log-probabilities after every vocabulary token v, under 28 short contexts p (no context, end-of-text, 22 single tokens and four two-token clause starters): 28 tables of 50,257 × 50,257.
  3. Blend and average into one table M. Rows are the previous token, columns the next one: a context-averaged bigram table.
  4. Split off the anchor u. The column mean u, a unigram-like anchor, lifts off; the centred rest C = M − 1u⊤ remains.
  5. Factorise C to rank d − 1 = 671. Randomized SVD gives two thin factors. The bars are our rebuild's share of C kept at each rank (GPT-2 small alone, 28 contexts): @@R:ours.stage_a_c28.energy.1:0%@@% at rank 1, @@R:ours.stage_a_c28.energy.671:1%@@% at 671.
  6. Store it in the 14-layer target. The factors become the input embedding and the output head, hidden dimension 0 carries u, and every sublayer is zeroed, so a token's vector passes straight up the residual stream.
  7. What it computes. For input token v the logits are approximately u/Tu + rank-671 part of C(v, ·)/Tb: a factorised bigram model stored in a 14-layer network. After " Jane", our rebuild's prior guesses "'s" 8.2%, " Doe" 6.0% and " Aust" 5.4%; the anchor alone guesses "," 12%.

Donor weights, contexts and shapes are the paper's; the bars and the guesses come from this page's rebuild, which the {{Rebuild tab|tab:t-run}} runs on a CPU with GPT-2 small alone.

Stage A in full: the contexts, clipping, SVD settings, temperatures and norms

Stage A: copy what the donors predict, not what they are. Six donors that share the GPT-2 vocabulary (GPT-2 small and large, Cerebras-GPT 111M to 1.3B) are queried on every vocabulary token v under 28 contexts p: no context, end-of-text, 22 single tokens (punctuation and function words such as " the") and four two-token clause starters (". The", ", and", ". This", ", or"). Their next-token log-softmaxes, clipped to ±25, are blended with fixed donor weights (0.725 on GPT-2 small) and per-row context weights into a 50,257 × 50,257 context-averaged bigram table M. Its column mean u is split off as a unigram-like anchor; the centred table C = M − 1u⊤ is factorised to rank d − 1 = 671 by randomized SVD (oversampling 32, one power iteration, a fixed sketch). The factors become the input embedding and the output head; hidden dimension 0 carries u; two temperatures (Tb = 0.9314, Tu = 1.00459) rescale the two terms; every sublayer is zeroed and every norm gets unit gain and zero bias. The final RMSNorm then rescales an embedding row by about √d, which the 1/√d factors cancel, so for input token v the logits are approximately u/Tu + rank-671 part of C(v, ·)/Tb: a factorised bigram model stored in a 14-layer network ({{§4.4|ax:S4.SS4}}, {{Table 5|ax:A2.T5}}). The {{Rebuild tab|tab:t-run}} runs this stage on a CPU with GPT-2 small alone.

Stage B: the sparse edits, with a map of the 672 dimensions

Stage B: let a little context back in. Sparse, deterministic edits on 96-dimensional bands of the hidden state (B0 to B5 from dimension 1 in steps of 96; B6 = [576, 672), shifted down by one to fit). Every attention layer becomes a uniform causal mean-pool over one band (Wq = Wk = 0), written back at a small scale, so the model sees an average of its past embeddings. Layer 0's SwiGLU gets SVD-projected slices of GPT-2 small's first MLP at scale 0.009. Each SSM block, its data-dependent gate zeroed to a constant, becomes a gated depthwise causal convolution over one band (layer 1: positive taps on the three preceding positions, a negative tap on the current one; the others uniform), writing into B2 and on some layers B0, B1, B3. Each constant was introduced as a single change on the then-current best and kept because the evaluator improved ({{§4.4|ax:S4.SS4}}, {{Table 3|ax:S4.T3}}).

What did not work (negative results)

Negative results. The graph also records what did not work, and later workers cited those records when choosing directions. Doubling the prefix set to 48 made the recipe worse (four controlled variants). Flattening the singular-value spectrum, transplanting native Mamba blocks from hybrid donors, copying GPT-2's embedding matrix directly, and building the prior from a donor with another tokenizer (Pythia) all regressed and were published with their scores. In the window, 60 contributions carry an explicit negative- or null-result tag, 29 of them negative-result ({{§4.5|ax:S4.SS5}}).

Lineage and reproduction. The best contribution has 145 commits in its ancestry, written by 15 of the 17 accounts (all 13 workers, the setup account and one of the authors' hand-run accounts); 115 of its 144 parent edges (@@R:cross_edges:0%@@%) cross account boundaries, so no single worker assembled the recipe. Participants posted 165 verification contributions covering 95 distinct targets, each by an account other than the author, none reporting a failure: 141 distinct verifier and target pairs, the other 24 repeats (only the newest verdict per pair counts). Forty of the winner's 144 ancestors (@@R:ancestors_verified:0%@@%) were reproduced by another account ({{§4.5|ax:S4.SS5}}).

How close the reproductions were, across hardware

Same-hardware reproductions are bit-identical; most A100 against H100 ones agree within 5 × 10⁻⁴ bits per byte; the largest gap, 1.2 × 10⁻³ on an ancestor of the winner, is slightly above the roughly 10⁻³ the brief allows, and was posted as confirmed anyway, put down to a constant hardware offset ({{§4.5|ax:S4.SS5}}).

The primary result
Without training data or a single gradient update on the target, the community's best transfer() initialises the frozen 119.6M hybrid to 1.899044 bits per byte, against 3.3923 for random initialisation and about 1.0 for a trained GPT-2 124M: (3.3923 − 1.899044) / (3.3923 − 1.0) = @@R:gap_closed:1%@@% of the gap closed ({{§4.5|ax:S4.SS5}}). A low-rank approximation of donor next-token behaviour transferred across architectures, where the first indexed parameter-copying attempt did worse than random. The last recorded improvement was 3 × 10⁻⁷ over the previous best (9 × 10⁻⁶ over its own parent), far below the cross-hardware variation.

Coordination dynamics

[[S4.SS6|§4.6, Figures 4 and 5]]

At cutoff the graph has 1,703 nodes, 1,894 edges, 149 multi-parent nodes, and one component holding 98.9% of the nodes ({{Figure 4|ax:S4.F4}} draws it, including 123 contributions posted after the cutoff, with the winner's ancestry as a highlighted spine). The paper reads four patterns from the trace ({{§4.6|ax:S4.SS6}}):

  1. Fast exploitation. The first three improvements account for 93% of the total descent (Results above). Version 1 had said "first eight improvements, roughly 70%", which Table 4's first two (@@R:share_first_two_improvements:1%@@%) already rule out.
  2. Narrow spine. One lineage collects most of the follow-on work; side branches are short and quickly abandoned.
  3. Equal-score convergence. Of 523 pairs of different accounts posting identical scores, 76% fall within an hour of each other and 90% within six; removing verifications and parent-child pairs leaves 499 pairs with the same timing (79% and 90%). Identical scores can come from re-runs of a shared parent or from different methods, so this is convergence on the same results, not independent rediscovery (version 1 called it "parallel rediscovery", with 696 pairs, 63% and 80%; {{Figure 5|ax:S4.F5}}).
  4. Community-level diagnosis. Several families of contributions converge near 1.90. Agents attributed the plateau to a globally linear evaluator and underused target sublayers and called the bigram recipe a hard local optimum; nobody tested these explanations.

Workers adopted leading results quickly: 194 of the 232 new bests (@@R:new_best_followed:0%@@%) drew follow-on work from at least one other account within six hours, a median of three accounts among those. They also repeated work and concentrated most effort on one lineage. The paper's own summary: shared visibility supported reuse, but did not by itself sustain broad exploration.
