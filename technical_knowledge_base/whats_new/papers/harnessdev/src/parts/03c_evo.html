Result 4: Evolution, and what the creator could not see

[[S4.SS3|§4.3, Tables 6 and 7, Figures 7 and 8]]

The nine lineages produced 73 official versions and 64 adjacent version switches ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}). All five self-runtime creators improved their visible feedback pair, by +3.0 (Opus) to +13.9 (Qwen) points. On the 630 held-out tasks the same declared versions improved by only +1.43 to +4.44, mean +3.11; Opus had the largest held-out gain. Under the fixed Gemini runtime only Opus improved on held-out tasks; Qwen, DeepSeek and GPT-5.5 regressed, GPT-5.5 by 10.32 points ({{Table 6|ax:S4.T6}}).

Across the 64 version switches, how often did the held-out score move in the same direction as the feedback score the creator was optimising?

Each dot is one switch from version t to t+1: the change in the feedback pair the creator saw (x) against the change in the held-out-630 score it never saw (y), from this page's decoding of {{Figures 7 and 8|ax:S4.F7}}. Green quadrants: same direction. The count reproduces the paper's 34 of 64 (53.1%) exactly. Tap or hover a dot for its lineage and versions.

Stability. Evolution is not monotonic ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}). Of the 64 switches, 8 regress on both benchmarks, 16 on one, 3 trade one benchmark for the other, 7 change nothing measurable, 27 report gains inside the repeated-run noise band, 2 show clear positive evidence beyond the noise, and 1 contains no executable code change. Only 2 of the 9 declared versions are the best held-out version. Creators usually declare a version near their best visible score (in 7 of 9 lineages exactly the best, by this page's recount), but "repeatedly optimizing a noisy score can favor a lucky run and amplify overfitting". The {{Replay the evolution runs|tab:t-run}} tab steps through every lineage version by version, with the held-out line hidden until you ask for it.

What the Evolution edits were (Table 7)

The median declared version changes 8 files, adding 476 lines and deleting 38 ({{Table 7|ax:S4.T7}}). Of the 64 switches, 58 change execution or control flow, 37 tools, 17 lifecycle recovery, 16 context and only 4 state; none modifies a standalone verifier. Edit size does not predict improvement, and deletion can help as much as addition (DeepSeek rolled back much of a fixed-Gemini rewrite after context compression broke tool-message pairing). Of 169 new functions or classes, 113 are reachable, 31 only through dead code and 25 have no caller. Opus's completion gate is the positive example; Qwen's message sanitizer, which broke valid Gemini tool-result sequences, the negative one.

Diagnosis is the weakest step. Eight of nine lineages complete at least one loop of reading results, editing, re-evaluating and selecting, but the dedicated trajectory interface was called only twice in all nine, and inspected cases cover just 0.5% to 40.2% of the 189 feedback tasks. Creators leaned on their own scripts and the five-task probes, which can mislead: one GPT-5.5 candidate passed all five Terminal probes and scored 0.584 on the full set. The clearest positive case: Opus found that 99 of 100 runs reported success while only 48 passed, traced it to premature completion and added a completion check.

The runtime binding matters most. Changing only the executor moves the starting score H0 and changes which edits help ({{§6|ax:S6}}). Qwen's self lineage starts at a feedback pair of 41.8; the same creator under Gemini starts at 62.1 and gains only +1.1.
