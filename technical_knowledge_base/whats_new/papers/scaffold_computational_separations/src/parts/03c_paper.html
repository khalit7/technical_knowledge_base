The theorem: a harness over trivial lookups decides a problem no short-chain student can

[[S4|§4, Appendix D]]

The task. Directed s-t reachability (STCON): given an N-vertex graph as its adjacency matrix A, a source s and a target t, is there a path from s to t? Read vertices as reasoning states and edges as valid steps, and this is the abstract shape of search ({{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1}}).

The base model's job is trivial. It only answers "is there an edge from u to v?", EA(u,v) = Auv. Written as a circuit it is one big OR over all N² cells of (Aij AND u = i AND v = j), each equality an AND of O(log N) bits: constant depth, polynomial size, so in AC⁰ ({{Appendix D.1|ax:A4.SS1}}).

Where each piece sits: the lookup is in the smallest class, the problem in NL, the student in TC⁰
The standard inclusions AC⁰ ⊆ TC⁰ ⊆ L ⊆ NL ⊆ P ({{§1.2|ax:S1.SS2}}). Whether NL ⊆ TC⁰ is open; nearly everyone believes it is false, and the theorem assumes so.

The student is in TC⁰. A transformer with a fixed number of layers, polynomial size and logarithmic precision can be simulated by a uniform TC⁰ circuit ({{Merrill and Sabharwal, 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729}}). The paper lets the student also write a short chain of thought: Tc(n) = ⌈c log₂(n + 1)⌉ tokens for input length n, each token checked by a TC⁰ circuit (Definition 1, {{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1}}).

Lemma 1: a short chain of thought stays in TC⁰. There are only |Σ|Tc(n) possible transcripts, which is polynomial in n; a circuit checks all of them in parallel and accepts if one passes, adding two layers of depth ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}, {{proof in Appendix D.2|ax:A4.SS2}}).

Theorem 1. Assume NL ⊄ TC⁰. (i) A breadth-first-search harness with oracle access to EA decides reachability in polynomial time with at most N² oracle calls and O(N) bits of state. (ii) No student in the class of Definition 1 decides STCON on all inputs ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}).

Lemma 1 on numbers. On numbers: 100 vertices give n = 10,000 input bits; with a binary alphabet and c = 1 the chain is 14 tokens long and there are 16,384 transcripts to try, about n.

Part (ii) is two lines: Lemma 1 would put a successful student in TC⁰, and since every NL problem reduces to STCON by a uniform AC⁰ reduction, which composes with a TC⁰ circuit without losing constant depth, all of NL would be in TC⁰ ({{Appendix D.3|ax:A4.SS3}}). The reduction must be that weak; an ordinary logspace reduction could do work outside TC⁰ itself ({{Appendix D.1|ax:A4.SS1}}).

Why depth is the limit, on a picture. The harness's rounds are its depth: round r reaches the vertices r hops from s, and it runs as many rounds as the graph needs. The obvious fixed-depth strategy squares the Boolean adjacency matrix once per layer (each entry an OR of N ANDs, constant depth), so k layers see paths of up to 2k hops; on graphs whose paths grow with N, any fixed k eventually answers "no" to a reachable target. The {{lab|tab:t-run}} runs both on graphs you choose: with N from 8 to 256 on long-path graphs, the reader needs k = 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 layers to answer every graph right, one more each time N doubles.

What the lab does and does not show. It shows one constant-depth strategy failing as paths lengthen, and the harness never failing. It does not show that every constant-depth circuit fails: that is exactly the open NL ⊄ TC⁰ question the theorem assumes. Its random-graph family also shows the other side: when paths are short, as on typical random graphs, a shallow reader is right almost every time, because the theorem is about the worst input, not the typical one.

What the theorem does not say

  • It is conditional. NL ⊄ TC⁰ is a widely believed conjecture, not a theorem.
  • It is about the worst case. "No student decides STCON on all inputs" leaves room for students that are right on the inputs a task actually produces.
  • It covers only short chains of thought. With a chain of thought polynomial in the input length, a transformer recognises exactly the problems solvable in polynomial time ({{Merrill and Sabharwal, 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07923}}), BFS included; a transformer whose depth grows like log n decides reachability outright ({{Merrill and Sabharwal, 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03961}}). The paper says so ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}). Every distilled student in its experiments writes a long trace (VGB and A* search logs, the whole flattened Branching tree), so none of them is in the class the theorem rules out. Their failures are a finite-size capacity effect the theorem motivates, not one it proves.
  • It says nothing about learning. It bounds what a student can represent, not what supervised fine-tuning finds; the paper's own related work notes that when capacity is not the constraint, supervised fine-tuning on the teacher's outputs is provably optimal ({{Appendix A|ax:A1}}).
