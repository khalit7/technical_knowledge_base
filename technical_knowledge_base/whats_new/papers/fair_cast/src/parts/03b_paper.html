Method: Fair Cast, two changes at the export

[[S5|§5]]

1. Export with an unbiased rounding rule. For a master θ between neighbouring bf16 values ⌊θ⌋ and ⌊θ⌋ + u, stochastic rounding exports the upper value with probability p = (θ − ⌊θ⌋)/u. Then E[q] = θ exactly, and an update Δ raises the probability by Δ/u, so the served weight moves by Δ on average at any learning rate. The released kernel adds 16 uniformly random bits to the fp32 bit pattern and truncates the low 16 (sr_cast_bf16). The deterministic alternative, error feedback, keeps one fp32 residual per weight: qt = round(θt + rt−1), rt = θt + rt−1 − qt. The export errors telescope, so their sum stays within u forever ({{Eq. 6|ax:S5.E6}}): unbiased over time rather than per export.

One weight between two bf16 neighbours: where each rule sends it

2. Compute the loss on the served copy. The trainer's forward pass uses the exact bf16 qt that generated the rollouts, and the gradient reaches the fp32 master through a straight-through estimator (the standard trick of quantisation-aware training, owned by {{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}). The sampled and the differentiated policy are then the same object. The change sits in the weight-export call, adds no hyperparameters and leaves the optimizer, loss, sampler kernels and framework alone ({{§5|ax:S5}}).

Why does a perturbation of at most |w|/256, harmless in a forward pass, matter in training? Round-to-nearest holds the same weights back in the same direction for 50 to 150 steps, so the served policy is a stale copy rather than a noisy one; and the optimizer never learns of it, because its gradient is computed on a copy that shares the rounding. Stochastic rounding makes the perturbation zero-mean and independent across steps ({{§9|ax:S9}}). Its single-export error is larger (up to u, against u/2), so the gain comes from removing the correlation, not from shrinking the error ({{§5|ax:S5}}).

What is and is not guaranteed (§5), and the two ways to train on the copy

Unbiasedness is about weights, not the policy: E[q] = θ does not give E[πq] = πθ, because the network is nonlinear. The paper's precise statements ({{§5|ax:S5}}): (i) rollouts are exactly on-policy for qt, whose log-probabilities enter the loss, so the policy-gradient estimate at qt has no importance-weighting error; (ii) πq differs from πθ by a rounding of each weight by less than u. Under round-to-nearest that error has the same sign step after step, so its first-order effect ∇π·(q − θ) accumulates over the lag; under stochastic rounding it is zero-mean and independent, the first-order term vanishes in expectation and a second-order term ½ tr(HΣ) with Σ ⪯ (u²/4)I remains; (iii) the straight-through gradient is the gradient at qt, not at θt, with bias bounded by the same rounding width. The residual is measured: |log(πθ/πq)| stays near 0.01 under Fair Cast. The objective becomes the expected reward of the exported model, Eq[J(q)], the model that samples and the model that is deployed.

Two implementations are equal in value: substitute qt for the parameters in the forward call, or write qt into the parameter storage for the step and restore the master before the optimizer update. With activation checkpointing only the second is exact, since the recomputed backward pass then also sees qt. The main runs used the first; the second gave the same results on Qwen3-0.6B (knights-and-knaves 0.977 against 0.967, GSM8K 0.756 against 0.742, seed 0; {{Table A1|ax:A1.T1}}). The code README calls the second "the verified form".

Variants ({{§5|ax:S5}}): dithered error feedback (stochastic rounding of the error-fed value, residual still carried) and the EMA export of section 7.5.

Setup

[[S6|§6]]

GRPO-style clipped policy gradient, group size 8, 24 prompts per step, 150 optimizer steps (500 for long runs), rollouts up to 2,048 tokens. Each arm runs on one H100, a vLLM sampler and a Hugging Face trainer sharing the device. Models: Qwen3-0.6B, 1.7B (default) and 4B, plus OLMoE-1B-7B and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct; AdamW at η = 10−6 by default, Muon where stated. Tasks: Reasoning Gym logic puzzles (knights-and-knaves, zebra puzzles, list functions, string manipulation, Countdown), MBPP+ and HumanEval+, MATH, GSM8K and games with exact solvers (Nim). Evaluation: greedy decoding of the served copy on a held-out set every 10 steps, 512 problems where available. One seed per arm unless stated; a seed changes the trainer and sampler randomness, never the order of training prompts ({{§6|ax:S6}}).

All settings (Table A1) and the MBPP+ overlap

Clip 0.2; AdamW β = (0.9, 0.999), weight decay 0.01; Muon at 10−6 with momentum 0.95 and RMS-matched updates (0.2·√max(m, n)) on 2-D hidden weights, AdamW for embeddings, norms and the output head; temperature 1.0; 6,144 tokens for Nim; the 1.7B MATH levels 3 to 5 pair ran 300 steps with cosine decay to 10−7; lag 1 between sampler and trainer ({{Table A1|ax:A1.T1}}). The harness multiplies Muon's learning rate by a default factor of 20, but every Muon configuration in the repository sets it to 1.0, matching the text. Result 1: both halves are needed

[[S7.SS1|§7.1, Table 1]]
Keep the trainer's forward pass on the fp32 master (as frameworks do now) but switch only the export to an unbiased rule. What happens?

It collapses ({{§7.1|ax:S7.SS1}}). Error feedback without the served-copy forward fell to 0.00 held-out accuracy in 4 of 4 long runs; stochastic rounding without it degenerated on Qwen3-0.6B into token loops on GSM8K (0.61 to 0.13 by step 19) and unterminated reasoning on MATH (0.002). Each export now flips many weights at random, the trainer never sees those flips, so the rounding noise enters the visible ratio: the clip fraction rose in step with the flip fraction, and the gradient vanished once every group was uniformly wrong.

Result 2: long-rollout tasks, where the lag does the most damage

[[S7.SS2|§7.2, Figure 5]]

Zebra puzzles (Qwen3-1.7B). Round-to-nearest learns for about twenty steps, then collapses on every seed, from a peak of 0.57 ± 0.01 to 0.33 ± 0.10 (3 runs). Between steps 30 and 60 its responses lengthen into the 2,048-token cap (held-out truncation 0.47 to 0.89) while training reward falls: a policy pushed toward longer derivations by rewards computed on a model it no longer matches. A DAPO-style truncation penalty on the round-to-nearest arm did not prevent the collapse (final 0.45). Three of four Fair Cast runs ended at 0.62 to 0.70 and tracked an fp32-sampler oracle (0.69); the fourth, a repeat of seed 0, held 0.53 to 0.63 for 130 steps and then fell to 0.34 in its last fifteen, so the four-run mean is 0.57 ± 0.16 ({{§7.2|ax:S7.SS2}}, {{Figure 5|ax:S7.F5}}).

Knights-and-knaves. Here the lag costs speed. With AdamW at 1.7B, Fair Cast reached 0.88 at step 9 and about 0.97 by step 40; round-to-nearest needed about 40 more steps and ended 3.7 points lower. With Muon, whose updates almost all fall below u/2, Fair Cast led at all 15 checkpoints on both seeds (0.988 ± 0.003 against 0.907 ± 0.016). At 0.6B the gain is 10.2 points; at 4B both reach the ceiling (0.992 against 0.988).

Nim (6,144-token cap). Both arms gain about 38 points; Fair Cast leads by 10 to 30 points through step 100 and peaks at 0.736 against 0.596, then the two converge (0.580 against 0.570 at step 150). The lag is a delay here, not a different destination.
