Result 4: under round-to-nearest, the checkpoint is not the model

[[S7.SS4|§7.4, Table 3]]

The fp32 master you save and the bf16 copy that was trained and evaluated are different objects. Under round-to-nearest they agree at step 0 and then drift apart, by up to 50 points on HumanEval+ (served 59.8, master 9.1 at step 99) and 20 to 30 on MBPP+. The gap is not monotone: the withheld residual is flushed into the copy only when weights cross boundaries, so the master can be 30 points below its export at one checkpoint and level 50 steps later. Of 9 round-to-nearest runs with master evaluations, 5 showed a gap of at least 12 points at some checkpoint and 2 at the final one; under Fair Cast master and export agreed within 3 points at every checkpoint of 12 runs ({{§7.4|ax:S7.SS4}}, {{Figure 8|ax:S7.F8}}).

Saved fp32 masterbf16fp16FP8 via bf16FP8 via fp16

{{Table 3|ax:S7.T3}}, held-out accuracy (%) of one saved master exported four ways. Formats at or coarser than bf16 discard the same residual the sampler never saw and reproduce the training score; finer formats (fp16, fp32 evaluation, continued training) inherit it. The fp16 export of the HumanEval+ master wrote responses of 1,679 tokens on average against 344 for bf16: a different model.

The practical rule: evaluate and ship the served copy; deploy from bf16, or FP8 calibrated on bf16; do not ship fp16 or resume training from the fp32 master after round-to-nearest training without re-evaluating. The text says such a pipeline "can deploy a model 30 to 40 points worse" than its curve; in Table 3 that holds for HumanEval+ (42.0 and 30.4 points lost), while Countdown loses 11.7.

Result 5: the lag was also a regularizer, and can be put back on purpose

[[S7.SS5|§7.5, Figure 9]]

Round-to-nearest passes a weight's motion on only once it has built up past a boundary, so the served policy is a low-pass-filtered master: fast, small changes are suppressed and only sustained drift gets through. That is a bias (the lag) and also a regularizer (the sampler cannot commit to whatever the current step's reward favours). Fair Cast removes both. The filter can be restored explicitly by exporting an exponential moving average mt = (1 − α)mt−1 + αθt with the same unbiased rounding and served-copy training; α sets a lag of about 1/α steps in place of an accidental one that depends on each weight's magnitude ({{§7.5|ax:S7.SS5}}).

List functions (Qwen3-1.7B, 512 held-out problems): which ends higher, round-to-nearest with its accidental lag, or Fair Cast with no lag at all?

Round-to-nearest, by 14.5 points: 0.668 against 0.523. Fair Cast committed to answer-only outputs by step 29 (median response 13 characters, every sample a bare boxed answer), while round-to-nearest kept writing derivations of about 600 tokens. With the EMA export at α = 0.05 Fair Cast reached 0.707 and 0.709 on two seeds, above round-to-nearest, with full derivations; α = 0.01 gave 0.666 ({{§7.5|ax:S7.SS5}}, {{Figure 9|ax:S7.F9}}). This is the one pair of the 25 where Fair Cast is behind.

The authors have not characterised in advance which tasks need the explicit lag ({{§9|ax:S9}}), and the conclusion speaks of "two configurations" where removing the lag is harmful while section 7.5 reports one.

Transfer, ablations and cost

[[S7.SS7|§7.6, §7.7]]

Transfer. MATH-trained 1.7B checkpoints evaluated on competition problems (8,192 tokens, temperature 1, avg@16): on the cosine-schedule pair Fair Cast scored 0.204 against 0.152 on AIME24, 0.548 against 0.495 on AMC23, 0.790 against 0.754 on MATH-500, and GSM8K was unchanged at 0.83; on the level-5 pair the two were within noise (AIME24 0.163 against 0.165) ({{§7.6|ax:S7.SS6}}).

Ablations ({{§7.7|ax:S7.SS7}}). Stochastic rounding and error feedback tie on knights-and-knaves and HumanEval+; error feedback is stronger only on the 500-step Countdown run. Fair Cast composes with TIS, which corrects version lag, and the effects add. An FP16 sampler shrinks the dead zone eightfold but does not remove it and risks overflow. Raising round-to-nearest's learning rate closes part of the speed gap but at 3 × 10−6 and 10−5 produced two MATH collapses. On OLMoE-1B-7B and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (MATH, 128 problems) the two rules were within noise, Fair Cast never worse.

Cost. One full export of Qwen3-1.7B on an H100: round-to-nearest 4.3 ms, stochastic rounding 21.7 ms with the bit-trick kernel (136.6 ms for a naive seven-operation one), error feedback 37.2 ms plus 6.9 GB of fp32 residuals. Against optimizer steps of 31 to 130 s that is under 0.1% for stochastic rounding (error feedback reaches 0.12% at the fastest step). Time per step is identical when output lengths match (knights-and-knaves 56.2 against 56.0 s). One real cost: Fair Cast changes 2 to 15% of served weights per step instead of 0.4 to 0.5%, so delta-sync schemes that ship only changed elements compress several times less ({{§9|ax:S9}}).
