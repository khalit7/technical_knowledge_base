Round a weight: one fp32 master, three export rules

One weight of the fp32 master receives a small update every optimizer step, and after every step the export makes its bf16 copy. Round-to-nearest, stochastic rounding and error feedback are computed on the actual float32 bit patterns, exactly as the paper's code does (this page's round-to-nearest matches torch's bf16 cast bit for bit). Change the weight's size, the learning rate and how steady the update is, then press Play to watch the copies follow, or fail to follow, the master.

The master (black) and its served bf16 copy under each rule, in units of the bf16 gap u

What to try

  • Defaults (weight 0.02, η = 10−6, mean update 0.5η): the round-to-nearest copy (orange) sits still while the master climbs for about a hundred steps, then jumps a whole gap once the master passes the midpoint and sits still again, now ahead: its error keeps one sign for over a hundred steps at a time. The stochastic copy (blue) flickers between neighbours, and the average of 256 runs (dotted) lies on the master: E[q] = θ.
  • Learning rate 10−5: updates clear the dead zone in a dozen steps and the three rules nearly agree. The paper says the same: Fair Cast is inert once updates clear the dead zone unaided ({{§5|ax:S5}}).
  • Weight 0.08: the gap grows with the weight, so large weights freeze longest; weight 0.005 at 10−5 shows the copy tracking within a gap.
  • Noise 1η: Adam's per-step direction wobbles; the mean drift, not the step size, decides when round-to-nearest moves ({{§3|ax:S3}}, τ ≈ u/(2|Δ̄|)).
What this reproduces, and what it cannot. The dead-zone arithmetic is exact: at the defaults the first round-to-nearest change comes at step (steady update: step ), inside the paper's 50 to 200 step range for τ, and the copy changes on of steps, close to the measured 0.4 to 0.5%. Both agree by construction: the update size and noise are illustrative choices, because the paper does not publish the distribution of Adam's normalized update or of weight sizes. One weight is not a model: the stochastic copy here changes on about a third of steps, while the paper measures 2 to 15% of all weights changing per step under Fair Cast, since most weights sit near a grid point where the flip probability is small. The simulator cannot show the effect on learning; that needs the paper's GPU runs.
How the engine was checked

The engine (src/parts/20_js_sim.js, the same file in the page and in the checks) implements round-to-nearest as round half to even on the low 16 bits of the fp32 pattern, stochastic rounding as "add 16 random bits, truncate" (the paper's sr_cast_bf16) and error feedback as the paper's recursion. check_sim.mjs and check_sim.py: 1,993 fp32 values (random bit patterns and exact midpoints) cast identically to ml_dtypes' bfloat16, which rounds like torch; for 300 values, enumerating all 65,536 noise patterns gives a probability of rounding up equal to the fractional position with zero error, so the expected copy equals the master exactly; the gap function equals 2⌊log₂|w|⌋ − 7. The master is accumulated in fp32, as a trainer does. The 256-run average uses independent noise per run.
