Then and now: GRPO's objective, term by term

Almost every reasoning model trained with RL since 2025 runs something called GRPO, but rarely the objective of Eq. 3 as printed. The animation changes one thing per step, in the order it was published, with its source; the toy box under it says whether this page's toy can see the change.

  1. 2024, DeepSeekMath: GRPO as printed. Eq. 3 with a learned reward model, z-scored group advantages, per-sequence length averaging, the k3 KL in the loss with β = 0.04, G = 64 and one policy update per batch, so the clip never binds.
  2. January 2025, DeepSeek-R1: rules for rewards. R1 and R1-Zero replace the reward model with rules (accuracy and format, equal weight), sample 16 outputs, set β = 0.001 and reset the reference every 400 steps, and split each rollout into 16 mini-batches, so the clip now matters ("plays a crucial role"). Its supplement is also the first GRPO against PPO comparison: GRPO ahead at λ = 0.95, close at λ = 1.0.
  3. March 2025, DAPO: four fixes for long chains. Clip-Higher (εlow = 0.2, εhigh = 0.28) to keep low-probability tokens rising; Dynamic Sampling, which drops groups whose rewards are all equal because their advantage is zero; a token-level loss (average over all tokens of the batch, so long answers count per token); Overlong Reward Shaping; and the KL term removed ("this restriction is not necessary").
  4. March 2025, Dr. GRPO: remove two biases. Dividing by |oi| penalises long wrong answers less, so they grow; dividing by std(r) over-weights questions that are almost always or almost never solved. Dr. GRPO drops both, leaving an unbiased REINFORCE-style estimator with a group-mean baseline, and normalises by a constant such as the generation budget.
  5. June 2025, Tang and Munos: the KL gradient. Differentiating a KL estimate as a loss does not give the KL's gradient; for k3, the expected gradient is that of KL(πref ‖ π). The fix is either the correct score-function estimator or no KL at all, which most verifiable-reward recipes now choose.
  6. July 2025, GSPO: ratios per sequence. GSPO (used for Qwen3) argues the token-level ratio is the wrong unit, since the reward is per sequence: it uses the length-normalised sequence likelihood ratio and clips whole responses, and reports more stable training, notably for mixture-of-experts models.
