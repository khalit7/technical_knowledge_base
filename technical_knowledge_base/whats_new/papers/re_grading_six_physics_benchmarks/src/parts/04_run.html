Follow the audit

The paper's method is a pipeline: pick the questions, grade them, send some to experts, label, exclude or repair, re-grade. Its appendices give the count at every stage for every benchmark ({{Appendix B|ax:A2}}, {{Appendix C|ax:A3}}), so the whole pipeline can be replayed question by question: one square per question, drawn to scale.

Try: play HLE-Physics and watch how many squares leave at the exclusion step; then play CMT-Benchmark, where every question was reviewed and almost none left. Compare the counters at the last step.

  1. 230 physics questions. Humanity's Last Exam has 230 physics questions. The 28 that need an image (faded) are set aside, leaving 202 text-only questions ({{Appendix B.2.1|ax:A2.SS2.SSS1}}).
  2. 202 text-only questions. The pre-audit score is measured on these 202 with the original materials and HLE's own judge: GPT-5.6-Sol 47.28% mean@4, 55.94% pass@4 ({{Table 1|ax:S3.T1}}).
  3. A separate audit run. GPT-5.6-Sol at High effort gets up to five attempts per question (tools on the fifth) and stops at the first accepted answer. 104 are accepted at least once; 98 are rejected every time and go to the experts ({{Appendix B.3|ax:A2.SS3}}).
  4. Experts label the 98 rejections. 86 benchmark errors (broken question or wrong key), 4 grader errors (a correct answer marked wrong), 8 model errors. The 104 accepted answers are not reviewed.
  5. Benchmark errors are excluded. The 86 leave the benchmark (faded). 116 of 202 questions remain: 57% of the text-only set.
  6. Re-graded with the HLE-adapted judge. On the 116 kept, GPT-5.6-Sol scores 78.66% mean@4 and 91.38% pass@4. The audit counts alone imply (104 + 4) / 116 = 93.1%: the corrected score is mostly fixed before the re-run.
  1. 100 of 500 problems. PHYBench has 500 problems but public reference solutions for only 100; the authors asked for the rest and did not get them, so all analysis uses these 100 ({{Appendix B.1.1|ax:A2.SS1.SSS1}}).
  2. Pre-audit score. Graded by PHYBench's Expression Edit Distance, where acceptance needs an EED score of 100: GPT-5.6-Sol 26.50% mean@4, 34.00% pass@4.
  3. A separate audit run. Up to five attempts without tools, stopping at the first EED score of 100. 44 accepted, 56 rejected on all five.
  4. Experts label the 56 rejections. 13 benchmark errors, 40 grader errors, 3 model errors. Most rejections here were correct answers the edit-distance grader could not recognise.
  5. Benchmark errors are excluded. The 13 leave (faded); 87 questions remain.
  6. Re-graded with the HLE-adapted judge. GPT-5.6-Sol: 90.23% mean@4, 95.40% pass@4 on the 87. The audit counts imply (44 + 40) / 87 = 96.6%.

  2. Pre-audit score. With PRISM-Physics's own final-answer grader: GPT-5.6-Sol 13.00% mean@4, 24.00% pass@4.

  4. Experts label the 74 rejections. 26 benchmark errors, 48 grader errors and no model errors at all.

  6. Re-graded with the HLE-adapted judge. GPT-5.6-Sol: 94.59% mean@4, 95.95% pass@4 on the 74. The audit counts imply (26 + 48) / 74 = 100.0%.

  2. Pre-audit score. GPT-5.6-Sol 83.00% mean@4, 85.00% pass@4: already high, because the LLM judge catches most equivalent answers.

  5. Benchmark errors are excluded. The 18 leave (faded); 82 questions remain.

  1. 50 expert-written questions. All 50 condensed matter theory questions. CMT-Benchmark has no public grader, so the HLE judge is adapted and used before and after. Pre-audit, GPT-5.6-Sol scores 61.00% mean@4, 72.00% pass@4 ({{Appendix B.2.2|ax:A2.SS2.SSS2}}).
  2. Every question reviewed. Condensed matter experts check each statement and reference, whatever the model answered: 30 of 50 have benchmark errors; 2 model errors are found on questions valid as written. Grader errors cannot be counted (no per-question pre-audit grades).

  4. Re-graded with the same judge. GPT-5.6-Sol: 87.24% mean@4, 97.96% pass@4 on the 49. The judge did not change, so this rise is the materials alone.
  1. 71 research challenges. CritPt has 71 challenges; Artificial Analysis evaluates 70 (one faded) and reports only an aggregate: GPT-5.6-Sol Max 32.29% mean@5, which is 113 correct attempts of 350 ({{Appendix B.2.3|ax:A2.SS2.SSS3}}).
  2. 56 challenges selected for audit. The audit covers a 56-challenge subset; the other 14 (faded) are not reviewed. The paper does not say how the 56 were chosen.
  3. Every selected challenge reviewed and re-solved. A specialist per challenge checks the statement and, because the official references are private, solves it independently. 21 benchmark errors and 5 model errors; grader errors cannot be counted.
  4. Repair, or exclude. 19 of the 21 are repaired (teal), for example by writing out the Hamiltonian of challenge 44; 2 leave (faded), as do the 14 never audited. 54 remain, with references written by the auditors.

What the protocol can and cannot see

The animation shows what the paper counted. This simulator asks what it could not count. It builds a benchmark from a few rates, runs the paper's protocol on it (audit only the rejections, exclude benchmark errors, count grader errors as correct, re-grade the rest) and compares the result with the model's true accuracy on the well-posed questions. Each preset sets the first three rates from that benchmark's audit counts, with the other three at zero; at those settings the protocol is exact by construction and reproduces the audit's counts. The last three are what the paper never measured.

Try: on the HLE-Physics preset, raise "judge accepts a wrong answer" to 5%: the protocol's corrected score rises while the true accuracy does not move. Then raise "reviewers call a model error a defect": the excluded set swallows real failures.

The simulator uses expected counts, not random draws, and one attempt per question, like the paper's PRISM-Physics and UGPhysics audit runs. Formulas: with d defective, accuracy a, false rejection g, false acceptance f, defective-accepted c and mislabel e, the share accepted is (1 − d)(a(1 − g) + (1 − a)f) + dc; the protocol keeps everything except the rejections labelled defective, d(1 − c) + e(1 − d)(1 − a)(1 − f), and scores the kept accepted answers plus the grader errors as correct.
