Pre-training

[[S4.SS1|§4.1 to §4.3]]

Data ({{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1}}): 14.8T tokens, with more maths and code than V2, more languages beyond English and Chinese, and less redundancy. Documents are packed without cross-sample attention masking. Fill-in-the-middle in the prefix-suffix-middle layout, inherited from DeepSeekCoder-V2, is applied to 10% of documents.

A tokenizer fix (§4.1)

The tokenizer's new tokens that join punctuation and line breaks caused a token-boundary bias on multi-line prompts without a final newline, so a share of them are randomly split during training.

Hyper-parameters (§4.2)

Hyper-parameters ({{§4.2|ax:S4.SS2}}): AdamW with β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.95, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0, sequence length 4K. Learning rate: linear warm-up to 2.2 × 10−4 over 2K steps, constant until 10T tokens, cosine decay to 2.2 × 10−5 over 4.3T tokens, then 2.2 × 10−5 for 333B tokens and 7.3 × 10−6 for the last 167B. Batch size ramps from 3,072 to 15,360 sequences over the first 469B tokens.

Long context ({{§4.3|ax:S4.SS3}}): YaRN applied only to the decoupled key kR (scale s = 40, α = 1, β = 32, √t = 0.1 ln s + 1; YaRN itself is taught on {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}), then two 1,000-step phases that take the window from 4K to 32K and then to 128K, at learning rate 7.3 × 10−6: 32K sequences in batches of 1,920, then 128K in batches of 480. After fine-tuning the model passes "Needle in a Haystack" across the whole 128K window ({{Figure 8|ax:S4.F8}}). Those two phases are about 126B tokens for 119K GPU hours, about 950 GPU hours per billion tokens against 180 in pre-training (derived), because attention over long sequences is expensive.

Pre-training costs 180K GPU hours per trillion tokens, 3.7 days on the 2,048-GPU cluster, so under two months in all ({{§1|ax:S1}}). Recomputed, with the price as the one input you can change:

The paper is explicit that this "include[s] only the official training of DeepSeek-V3, excluding the costs associated with prior research and ablation experiments on architectures, algorithms, or data". It does not mention hardware purchase, staff, failed runs, or the R1-series models whose outputs became V3's reasoning data. Much of the January 2025 discussion ignored that sentence; the honest reading is "the marginal compute cost of one training run", still roughly an order of magnitude below contemporary dense frontier runs.

What the $5.576M leaves out, item by item
  • Research and ablations on architectures, algorithms and data: excluded by the paper's own sentence ({{§1|ax:S1}}). The ablations alone include 15.7B and 228.7B models on up to 1.33T tokens, twice each for MTP and for balancing, and FP8 against BF16 at two scales ({{§4.5|ax:S4.SS5}}, {{Appendix B|ax:A2.SS1}}).
  • The R1-series expert models that generated V3's reasoning data ({{§5.1|ax:S5.SS1}}); R1's own later report gives its RL cost, quoted on the {{DeepSeek lab page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a2ae7ec6796947a235}}.
  • Hardware, power, staff and data. The 2,048 H800s cost far more to buy than $5.6M; the lab page quotes SemiAnalysis's estimate of DeepSeek's hardware. The paper never claims otherwise: $2 an hour is a rental-equivalent price.

The fair one-line version: "the final training run used 2.788M H800 GPU hours, worth about $5.6M at rental prices". That claim checks out.

Llama 3.1 405B's pre-training took 30.84M H100 GPU hours, 11 times V3's total. An H800 has the same compute as an H100. How much of that 11× is explained simply by Llama doing more work: 405B active parameters on 15.6T tokens against 37B on 14.8T?

Nearly all of it. Active parameters × tokens is 11.5 times larger for Llama, against 11.1 times the GPU hours (11.6 against pre-training alone). Counted as 6 × active parameters × tokens, both runs used about 34% of the GPUs' dense BF16 peak.

From GPU hours to utilisation. Training costs about 6 FLOPs per active parameter per token (the 6ND rule and what it leaves out are on {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}). For V3 that is 36.6B (37.55B active minus the 0.93B input embedding, which is a lookup, not a multiply; recounted from {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/blob/main/config.json}}). Divide by GPU-seconds and by the H800's peak and you get the run's model FLOPs utilisation (MFU); Meta reported 38 to 43% BF16 MFU for Llama 3.1 405B ({{Llama 3, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S3.T4}}). Predict before ticking: does counting attention and the MTP module push V3 above or below Meta's band?

How the utilisation is counted, the cost per unit of work, and the wall clock

Peaks: H100 SXM, 1,979 and 3,958 TFLOPS "with sparsity", halved for dense ({{NVIDIA|https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/}}); the H800 has the H100's compute with less interconnect bandwidth ({{planetbanatt|https://planetbanatt.net/articles/v3fermi.html}}). The cheapness, then, is mostly the architecture: an MoE does a dense 405B model's job with a tenth of the arithmetic per token. What FP8, DualPipe and the kernels bought is that the MoE's communication overhead did not eat that saving: counting attention and the MTP module, V3 reached about 41% of the BF16 peak (about 20% of FP8's), in line with the 38 to 43% BF16 utilisation Meta reported for Llama 3 ({{Llama 3, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S3.T4}}). {{planetbanatt's Fermi estimate|https://planetbanatt.net/articles/v3fermi.html}} reaches the same verdict from the other side: plausible.

Post-training

[[S5|§5.1, §5.2, §5.4]]

Supervised fine-tuning ({{§5.1|ax:S5.SS1}}): 1.5M instances. Reasoning data (maths, competition code, logic puzzles) comes from an internal DeepSeek-R1 model, whose answers are accurate but overthink, are poorly formatted and run long. So DeepSeek first trains a domain expert (code, maths, general reasoning) with SFT and RL on two kinds of samples, <problem, original response> and <system prompt, problem, R1 response>, the system prompt asking for reflection and verification; RL with high-temperature sampling teaches the expert to blend R1's patterns into answers even without the prompt; rejection sampling from the experts then produces the final SFT data. Non-reasoning data (creative writing, role-play, simple QA) comes from DeepSeek-V2.5 and is checked by human annotators.

Reinforcement learning ({{§5.2|ax:S5.SS2}}): rule-based rewards where an answer can be checked (a boxed final answer for maths, compiler test cases for LeetCode problems), because they resist reward hacking; elsewhere a reward model trained from V3's SFT checkpoints, either matching a free-form reference answer or judging open-ended answers, trained on preference data that includes the chain of thought behind each reward. The optimiser is GRPO ({{Eq. 26 to 28|ax:S5.E26}}), introduced in {{DeepSeekMath|n:3c65c17b0d0d817f9fc5cb9a9fbcbee5}}; how its group baseline replaces PPO's critic is taught on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}. For open-ended questions V3 judges itself, with voting, in the style of Constitutional AI ({{§5.4.2|ax:S5.SS4.SSS2}}); {{Table 8|ax:S5.T8}} shows why it can: on RewardBench V3 scores 87.0 (89.6 with six-vote majority), level with the best GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet versions.

SFT settings and RL prompt domains

SFT: two epochs, cosine learning rate from 5 × 10−6 to 1 × 10−6, packed sequences with sample masking ({{§5.1|ax:S5.SS1}}). RL prompts span coding, maths, writing, role-play and QA ({{§5.2|ax:S5.SS2}}).

Distillation, measured ({{Table 9|ax:S5.T9}}): on DeepSeek-V2.5, R1-style data lifted LiveCodeBench-CoT from 31.1 to 37.4 and MATH-500 from 74.6 to 83.2, while MATH-500 answers nearly doubled in length (769 to 1,510 tokens). The settings for V3 were chosen to balance accuracy against length ({{§5.4.1|ax:S5.SS4.SSS1}}).

Results

[[S4.SS4|§4.4, §5.3]]

Base model ({{Table 3|ax:S4.T3}}, all models run in DeepSeek's internal harness with the same settings): MMLU 87.1, BBH 87.5, HumanEval 65.2, MATH 61.6. Counting with the caption's rule that gaps of 0.3 or less are a tie, V3-Base beats LLaMA-3.1 405B Base on 20 of 32 rows, ties 7 and loses 5 (PIQA, RACE-Middle, RACE-High, NaturalQuestions, C3), with 11 times fewer active parameters; it beats Qwen2.5 72B Base on 28. The paper calls it the strongest open base model, "especially in code and math".

Chat model ({{Table 6|ax:S5.T6}}, output capped at 8K tokens, AIME and CNMO averaged over 16 samples at temperature 0.7): MMLU 88.5, MMLU-Pro 75.9, GPQA-Diamond 59.1, MATH-500 90.2 (above o1-preview, per §1), AIME 2024 39.2, LiveCodeBench 40.5 with chain of thought, Codeforces 51.6th percentile, SWE-bench Verified 42.0 (below Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 50.8, far above the other open models). V3 has the best score in 13 of the 22 rows and the best open-model score in 20; Claude 3.5 Sonnet leads on MMLU-Pro, IF-Eval, GPQA, SWE-bench and Aider-Edit, GPT-4o on SimpleQA and FRAMES. Open-ended ({{Table 7|ax:S5.T7}}): Arena-Hard 85.5, the first open model above 85, level with Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 85.2; AlpacaEval 2.0 length-controlled 70.0.

Efficiency: the whole run in 2.788M H800 hours; 1.8× decoding speed from MTP drafts; and an end-to-end generation speed more than twice V2's ({{§6|ax:S6}}).
