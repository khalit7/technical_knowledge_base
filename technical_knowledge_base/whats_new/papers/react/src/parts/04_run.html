Replay the traces

Six episodes from the paper, stepped through turn by turn, each one under every method the paper ran on it. Nothing here is generated: every thought, action and observation is the paper's own text. Figures 1, 4 and 5 are transcribed by hand from the figure images; Table 10 and Appendix D.1 are cut from the paper's text by mk_traces.py. The counters show what each method puts into the model's context and who wrote it, and the code panel lights the line of the harness that runs at each step.

Try: on the Apple Remote question, step Act and ReAct side by side and see that both reach the right page, but only ReAct has a step in which to read it. On the keychain episode, compare the run as generated with the one where a person edited two thoughts. On FEVER, every method is wrong, in three different ways.

Reading the counters

  • Thoughts are entries from the language space L: they go into the context and nothing is sent to the environment. In the Wikipedia tasks a thought is its own line ("Thought i:"); in ALFWorld and WebShop it is written as an action, think[...], which the environment answers with "OK." (the released {{alfworld.ipynb|https://github.com/ysymyth/ReAct/blob/master/alfworld.ipynb}}; the paper's figures omit those replies).
  • Context now is the words of the exemplars (where the paper prints all of them: the HotpotQA and FEVER prompts of {{Appendix C|ax:A3}}) plus everything in this episode so far. Words, not tokens: a tokenizer would give a larger number, and the ratio between methods is what matters. The ReAct prompt is the longest before the question is even asked; that is the "token overhead" that later function calling and trained tool use removed (see {{Then and now|tab:t-then}}).
  • Model against environment words shows the factuality trade-off of {{§3.3|ax:S3.SS3}} directly: CoT writes everything itself, Act mostly reads, ReAct does both.

What these episodes do and do not show

They are the paper's showcase examples, chosen by the authors to illustrate each behaviour, so they say nothing about how often each outcome happens; the rates are in {{Table 2|ax:S3.T2.fig1}} and the tables tab. Observations are truncated where the paper truncates them. One episode is a failure for every method (FEVER, from {{Appendix D.1|ax:A4.SS1}}, randomly selected by the authors) and one is a success that exact match scores as a failure (the Mystere hotel, {{Figure 4|ax:A1.F4}}).
