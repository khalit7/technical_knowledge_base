Then and now: one pair, many losses

Almost every successor keeps DPO's frame (a margin between the chosen and the rejected answer, measured against a reference) and changes one thing: how hard a pair is pushed as a function of its margin. The animation of that push for unlikelihood, DPO, the SLiC hinge, IPO, cDPO and SimPO, with what each did on this page's toy over 4,000 steps ({{the long runs|tab:t-run:runLong}}), is on {{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO family, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}, section 5, which owns the DPO family.

What else changed, and what survived

Oct 2023
Zephyr-7B: SFT then DPO on UltraFeedback's AI-ranked pairs, "a few hours of training without any additional sampling", ahead of Llama2-Chat-70B on MT-Bench ({{Zephyr|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16944}}). DPO becomes the open-model default.
Jan 2024
CPO (contrastive preference optimisation, for translation, no reference model) and KTO (Feb 2024: one thumbs up or down per answer instead of pairs, matching preference methods from 1B to 30B) ({{CPO|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08417}}, {{KTO|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01306}}).
Feb to Mar 2024
DPO-Positive shows the chosen likelihood can fall and adds a term against it; rDPO de-biases label noise with provable guarantees; ORPO drops the reference model and the separate stage by adding an odds-ratio term to SFT ({{2402.13228|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228}}, {{2403.00409|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00409}}, {{2403.07691|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07691}}).
Apr to May 2024
The online question. Xu et al.: well-tuned PPO beats DPO in every testbed, and DPO "might find biased solutions that exploit out-of-distribution responses"; Tajwar et al.: on-policy samples and a negative gradient both matter; Tang et al. (Google DeepMind): offline methods classify pairs better but generate worse; TR-DPO updates the reference during training to fight over-optimisation ({{2404.10719|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10719}}, {{2404.14367|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14367}}, {{2405.08448|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08448}}, {{2404.09656|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09656}}).
Jun 2024
DiscoPOP: an LLM proposes and tests preference losses; the best "adaptively blends logistic and exponential losses" ({{2406.08414|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08414}}).
Jul 2024
Llama 3 chooses DPO over PPO for compute and instruction following, with β = 0.1, formatting tokens masked and an NLL term on chosen answers; Qwen2 runs offline DPO, then online DPO on pairs from its own samples ranked by a reward model ({{Llama 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}, {{Qwen2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10671}}).
Oct 2024
Likelihood displacement: training to prefer "No" over "Never" can raise "Yes"; on refusals it cut Llama-3-8B-Instruct's refusal rate from 74.4% to 33.4% ({{2410.08847|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08847}}).
Nov 2024 to 2025
Tülu 3 and OLMo 2: SFT, then DPO on synthetic pairs that include on-policy samples, then RL with verifiable rewards. In Tülu 3's controlled test PPO came close to DPO's average (slightly lower) at about 14 times the node-hours (28 hours on two nodes against 4 on one) ({{Tülu 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}, {{OLMo 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656}}). For reasoning, GRPO and RLVR take over ({{DeepSeekMath|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300}}, {{DeepSeek-R1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948}}).
part of the 2023 recipestatus in 2026
The closed form of Eq. 4 and the implicit reward β log π/πrefSurvived everywhere: the shared starting point of IPO, KTO, cDPO, rDPO and TR-DPO, and the logged rewards/* of every DPO trainer.
The sigmoid (Bradley-Terry) lossStill the default (Llama 3, Tülu 3), with fixes for its unbounded push: label smoothing, a bounded loss (IPO), an NLL term on the chosen answers, or early stopping.
A frozen reference modelOptional: dropped by SimPO, ORPO and CPO, updated during training by TR-DPO, cached as log-probabilities at scale.
Offline pairs collected onceMostly replaced by on-policy or iterative pairs (Qwen2, Tülu 3, OLMo 2), which recovers part of online RL's advantage.
"No RL needed"True for preference and style alignment; not for reasoning, where online RL with verifiable rewards is the standard after DeepSeek-R1.
