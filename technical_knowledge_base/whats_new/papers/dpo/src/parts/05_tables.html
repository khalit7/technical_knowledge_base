The paper's figures and tables, rebuilt

DPO's evidence is mostly in figures that print no numbers. The arXiv HTML ships them as vector SVGs, so decode_figs.py reads every marker, line vertex and error bar from the paths and converts them with each figure's own tick marks (the straight-line calibration fits the ticks to within 1e-6 of a unit; values are good to about 1e-4). That is not reading curves by eye, and it turned up two facts the paper does not state: the TL;DR win rates are exact multiples of 1/256, and the error bars are one binomial standard error.

Figure 2 left: the IMDb reward-KL frontier

{{Figure 2 left|ax:S6.F2}}: 177 evaluation points from 22 runs, evaluated every 100 steps; runs are not distinguished in the figure, so per-run curves cannot be recovered. "Beaten" counts a method's points for which some DPO point has both lower or equal KL and higher or equal reward.

Figure 2 right: TL;DR win rate against the reference summaries

{{Figure 2 right|ax:S6.F2}}. Every plotted value times 256 is an integer to within 0.013 (so 256 prompts per point), and every error bar's half-height equals √(p(1 − p)/256) to within 0.2%: the bars are ±1 binomial standard error, which the paper does not say. Temperature 0 is plotted at 0.01.

Figure 3: Anthropic HH, by temperature and over training

{{Figure 3|ax:S6.F3}}. The values are not on a 1/n grid, but the bars' sizes imply about 250 prompts per point if they are ±1 binomial standard error, as on TL;DR. Methods at the same temperature are drawn slightly offset in the original; here they share the temperature.

Figure 4: Best of N

{{Figure 4|ax:A4.F4}}: "performance plateaus after roughly 64-128 samples". On TL;DR the three N are identical at temperature 0 (greedy sampling gives one candidate).
