The idea in one sentence

[[S1|§1]]

A dense reward that scores each step by sampling continuations makes every GRPO update better, but spends most of its generated tokens on continuations it never trains on, so at the same tokens or GPU-hours it loses to plain outcome-only GRPO; spending one forward pass to choose which prompts to train on wins instead, a little, early ({{abstract and §1|ax:S1}}).

Fig. 1 · Each method minus outcome-only GRPO, on four yardsticks

  1. Per update. Give each method the same number of optimizer updates. The Monte-Carlo reward leads outcome-only GRPO by 1.4, 1.8 and 1.0 points with 1, 2 and 4 continuations per cut (12 seeds; 1.5 [1.0, 2.1] for 4 continuations on 5 seeds). These leads are exploratory: outcome-only GRPO is interpolated to the reward's update count ({{Table 3|ax:A3.T3}}). This is the comparison the paper argues against.
  2. Per generated token. Stop every run at the same budget B = 12.25M generated tokens. The reward with 4 continuations has made 50 updates, outcome-only GRPO about 600, and the lead becomes a loss of 14.9 points (14.3 in the first, preregistered study); fewer continuations lose less: 11.2 with 2, 6.8 with 1. The two learned critics generate no extra tokens and are even (−0.4 and +0.7); the prompt filter gains 0.8 ({{§3.2|ax:S3.SS2}}, {{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1}}).
  3. Per GPU-hour. Continuations are generated in large batches and are cheap per token, so the Monte-Carlo runs finish in a third of the time. Stopping outcome-only GRPO at the reward's own training time narrows the loss to 7.8 points, still in all 12 seeds. The critics now trail (their fits add 23 to 24% to training time); the filter keeps 0.5 of its 0.8 (exploratory, H200 GPUs; {{Table 13|ax:A4.T13}}).
  4. Given outcome-only GRPO's full training time. A separate preregistered study (768-token completions, 8 seeds, A100 GPUs) trains four dense variants past the budget until they have run as long as outcome-only GRPO. None gains: 4 continuations trail by 3.3, training on the continuations by 5.2, and the VinePPO-style and 1-continuation variants by 1.1 each, the latter not significant ({{Table 15|ax:A6.T15}}).

New terms on this page

Outcome-only GRPO
plain GRPO with a 0/1 verifier reward: every token of a completion gets the same credit, its group advantage.
Dense (process) reward
adds a token-level term to the credit, so steps within one completion get different credit.
Monte-Carlo reward
cuts each completion at 5 fixed token positions and samples k continuations from each prefix; the fraction that end correct is the prefix's value, and each segment's credit is the change in value across it.
Learned critic
a ridge regression on the policy's hidden states that predicts a prefix's value instead of sampling it; TD form (credit the change in prediction) or baseline form (subtract the prediction from the outcome).
Prompt filter (critic filter)
scores 24 candidate prompts per iteration with a ridge critic on the hidden state at the end of the prompt and trains on the 6 whose predicted success is nearest 1/2, plus 2 at random.
Random choice
the control: 8 of the same 24 candidates at random.
Matched compute
every method gets the same budget B of generated tokens (continuations and discarded groups included), and is also compared at equal training GPU-hours.
Gradient efficiency
(E⟨u, ĥ⟩)² / E‖u‖²: how much one tuned SGD step along the update u can raise expected accuracy, given an estimate ĥ of its gradient; reported per update or per generated token.
Preregistered, TOST
hypotheses, primary contrasts and equivalence margins written before the runs; two one-sided tests show two configurations equal within a margin.
