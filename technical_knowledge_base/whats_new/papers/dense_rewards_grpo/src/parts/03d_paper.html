Prompt selection: a gain the protocol confirms

[[S4.SS1|§4.1]]

It gains on two of six models, and both gains replicate. On the level 3 to 5 pool, the critic filter raises accuracy at equal tokens by 1.7 [1.2, 2.1] points on Qwen3-0.6B over 54 seeds from four studies (higher in 42), including 1.2 [0.5, 1.9] in a preregistered replication on 24 fresh seeds (Holm p = .003), and by 0.8 [0.4, 1.1] on Qwen2.5-1.5B over 60 seeds, significant in each of three preregistered studies (12, 24 and 24 seeds). Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B and both Llama models show no significant gain on this pool ({{§4.1|ax:S4.SS1}}).

It carries over to unseen problems. On the 200 shared test problems the crossed bootstrap interval for the Qwen2.5-1.5B gain reached zero every time, so the last study on each model also scored the 1,543 numeric MATH test problems of levels 3 to 5 outside every evaluation set: +0.8 [0.5, 1.1] on Qwen2.5-1.5B and +1.1 [0.6, 1.5] on Qwen3-0.6B (Holm p < 10−4 each), and +1.1 and +1.3 on GSM8K, which neither configuration trains on (secondary).

It comes from which prompts are chosen. Drawing 24 candidates to train on 8 also moves through the prompt stream three times as fast. The control, random choice of 8 among the same 24, trails the filter by 1.7 [0.5, 2.9] on Qwen3-0.6B and by 1.0 and again 0.9 on Qwen2.5-1.5B (preregistered primary contrasts), and is itself equivalent to outcome-only GRPO within ±2 points on all three models tested.

Most of it survives the clock. Scoring the candidates costs a prefill pass, so filter runs take 9 to 14% longer to reach B; at equal training time the lead is 1.0 [0.6, 1.5] on Qwen3-0.6B and 0.5 [0.2, 0.8] on Qwen2.5-1.5B (exploratory), and on the seeds of the Qwen3-0.6B replication alone 0.4 [−0.1, +1.0].

Cheaper signals do less. A history filter close to GRESO (each prompt's last group reward, no forward pass) gains 0.6 [0.1, 1.1] on Qwen2.5-1.5B over 36 seeds, while the critic adds an early lead of 1.9 [1.6, 2.1] over it in the first half of the budget (secondary). Filtering by MATH difficulty level gains nothing (+0.2, equivalent to zero). DAPO's dynamic sampling loses 1.8 [0.8, 2.7] points, because the groups it generates and discards count toward the budget.

When it helps

[[S4.SS2|§4.2]]

Not where it adds the most informative groups. The usual reason for training near 1/2, that only mixed-outcome groups have nonzero advantages, does not predict the gain: the filter raises the share of mixed groups by 8 to 19 points in every setting, but accuracy rises only in some, and not where the share rises most ({{Figure 4b|ax:S4.F4}}).

It helps where the candidates are predicted to fail more often than not (Fig. 5). The authors formed this account after the fact from five settings, where the critic's mean predicted success over the 24 candidates separated the two that gained (0.42 and 0.40) from the rest (0.54 to 0.70). The mechanism: when the mean is below 1/2, keeping the candidates nearest 1/2 moves training toward easier prompts and away from all-wrong groups; above 1/2 it moves training toward harder prompts. Over all twelve settings the filter helps significantly in four of the five below 1/2 and in none of the seven others. Outcome-only GRPO's own training reward separates the settings the same way, so a short pilot run is enough to apply the rule; the base model's starting accuracy is not ({{Table 8|ax:A3.T8}}).

Six preregistered tests, five held ({{Table 1|ax:S4.T1}}). The two most direct keep model and pool fixed and only aim the filter at predicted success 0.25, harder than its candidates: on both models where the gain had replicated, it disappears (−2.8 and −1.0 against the filter aimed at 1/2). Changing the pool, the filter helps on Qwen3-1.7B with level 5 (+1.1, candidates 0.39; crossed interval reaches zero) and not on Qwen3-0.6B with levels 1 and 2 (0.79). Llama-3.2-3B (0.68) shows no gain, as predicted; Llama-3.2-1B (0.42) also shows none, the one failed prediction, so no gain has been shown outside the Qwen family. Four of the five confirmed predictions are of no gain, the more common outcome, so the two direct manipulations carry most of the weight (Appendix B).

What the gain is worth

[[S4.SS3|§4.3]]

A head start worth about a fifth of the budget. On Qwen2.5-1.5B the lead peaks at 2.6 [2.2, 3.0] points at 3B/16 and falls to 0.8 at B (Fig. 6). On both models the filter reaches outcome-only GRPO's final accuracy with 0.78 of the token budget, and with 0.85 and 0.87 of its training time once scoring is counted (exploratory; {{Table 11|ax:A3.T11}}). On Qwen3-0.6B the gain also holds at pass@8 (0.9 [0.2, 1.7]) and at every test level; on Qwen2.5-1.5B it shrinks as k grows.

With longer training, outcome-only GRPO catches up. Trained to twice the budget on Qwen3-0.6B (strict verifier, 24 seeds), the filter leads by 1.9 [1.3, 2.6] at B but only 0.5 [−0.5, +1.6] at 2B, equivalent to zero within ±2 (TOST p = .004).

A retuned baseline gains about as much (Fig. 7). On Qwen3-0.6B, doubling outcome-only GRPO's constant learning rate raises it by 1.9 [0.5, 3.3] points (Holm p = .018), and the faster baseline minus the filter is −0.03 [−1.3, +1.2], equivalent within ±2. On Qwen2.5-1.5B a doubled constant rate lowers both configurations by about three points and the filter's gain holds (1.1); a rate decaying linearly from 2 × 10−6 to 0 at B keeps the fast early rise without that loss, and then outcome-only GRPO ends 0.5 [−0.3, +1.2] below the filter at the usual rate, while the filter's own gain under that schedule, 0.2 [−0.5, +0.9], is equivalent to zero within ±1. The paper's summary, "within half a point of it", rests on these two: matched on Qwen3-0.6B, half a point short on Qwen2.5-1.5B. No warmup, other peak rates or other schedules were tried, and none was tuned for the filter (Appendix B).

A lenient verifier changes what training optimizes

[[A3.SS7|App. C.7]]

With the default verifier, which falls back to the last number when nothing is boxed, some runs gradually stopped boxing their answers: default-verifier accuracy exceeded strict-verifier accuracy by more than 10 points in up to 49 of a study's 408 evaluations, in either configuration ({{Appendix A|ax:A1}}). Retrained on the strict verifier, drift disappeared (0 of 2,196 evaluations) with no loss in accuracy: on Qwen3-4B with level 5, +0.8 [−1.9, +3.6] (non-inferior at −2, Holm p = .022), and 0 of 204 evaluations drifted against 16 of 204 ({{C.7.1|ax:A3.SS7.SSS1}}). Why a lenient checker gets exploited is owned by {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}} (verifier failures) and {{Reward Hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}}; the lesson here is procedural: fix the verifier before comparing interventions, because drift can bias a comparison in either direction.
