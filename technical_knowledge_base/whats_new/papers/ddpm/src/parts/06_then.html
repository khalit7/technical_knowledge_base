Then and now: what changed and what survived

DDPM's recipe as a card, changed paper by paper from 2020 to 2024, in seven stretches. Highlighted lines are what the papers of that stretch changed; everything else is carried over. It plays once when scrolled into view; any control stops it. Each change is quoted or paraphrased from the paper's own abstract or tables (inputs/later_abstracts.txt, inputs/later_extracts.txt).

  1. June 2020: DDPM, this paper. The starting recipe: everything on the card is from §3 and §4 of the paper.
  2. October to November 2020: a new sampler, then a framework. {{DDIM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502}} keeps "the same training procedure as DDPMs": only the sampler changes, so a DDPM checkpoint can be sampled in far fewer steps. {{Score-based SDEs|https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456}} make the score connection DDPM made explicit into the framework: the reverse process "depends only on the time-dependent gradient field (aka, score)".
  3. February to May 2021: better DDPMs, then guidance. {{Improved DDPM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09672}}: "a few simple modifications" give competitive likelihoods; learned variances, unstable in DDPM's Table 2, now work. {{Diffusion Models Beat GANs (ADM)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233}}: guidance trades diversity for fidelity, and diffusion passes GANs on ImageNet.
  4. July to December 2021: likelihood closed, then latent space. {{Variational Diffusion Models|https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00630}} close the paper's admitted weakness, likelihood: diffusion reaches state-of-the-art likelihoods (VDM's own table lists an augmented Sparse Transformer at 2.53, so the comparison depends on augmentation). {{Latent diffusion (Stable Diffusion)|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}: pixel-space diffusion "often consumes hundreds of GPU days"; running the chain in a compressed latent makes high-resolution text-to-image affordable.
  5. June to July 2022: the design space, and guidance without a classifier. {{EDM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00364}}: separating the design choices shows how much of DDPM's setup was incidental; the same pretrained networks sample better. {{Classifier-free guidance|https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598}}: guidance without a separate classifier, the form text-to-image systems use.
  6. September to December 2022: flows and Transformers. {{Flow Matching|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747}} "subsumes existing diffusion paths as specific instances"; rectified flow ({{arXiv 2209.03003|https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003}}) learns straight paths, which are cheap to simulate. {{DiT|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748}}: FID falls consistently as the Transformer's Gflops grow, and the backbone question is settled in favour of the {{ViT|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa231cfcde6c1954d}} design.
  7. March 2023 to March 2024: one step, and the modern stack. {{Consistency models|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469}} map noise "directly" to data: DDPM's 1,000 evaluations become one, at DDPM's own CIFAR10 quality. {{Stable Diffusion 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206}}, "Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis": latent space, a Transformer and a flow objective together; OpenAI's Sora report likewise describes a diffusion Transformer for video.
What survived

The forward process as Gaussian noise added in closed form, training by drawing a random noise level and regressing a noise-like target with a plain MSE, and the idea of generation as many small denoising steps (shortened to a few, or one, by later samplers and distillation). What did not: pixel space and the U-Net in the largest systems (latent space and Transformers in Stable Diffusion 3 and Sora), the linear schedule and 1,000 sampling steps, fixed variances, and unconditional generation as the goal.

FID values across steps are on different datasets and resolutions and are not comparable with each other; each is the paper's own headline.
