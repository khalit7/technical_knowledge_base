7. Past the trained length: the position wall

A model is trained on windows of some length; nothing stops a longer prompt at inference. With RoPE (each query and key rotated by an angle proportional to its position, so their dot product depends on distance), a longer prompt produces angles, in the slowly rotating dimensions, that training never showed; with learned absolute positions there is no row in the table at all (the topic's tiny GPT raises IndexError: learned positions stop at 256; got position 299, on purpose; no scaling rule exists for a learned table: retrain or switch scheme). The schemes and extension methods (position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling, YaRN, Llama 3's rule) belong to {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}; here is what the wall costs and why training at length is still needed.

The tiny GPT past its 256-token training length: scaling trades the short range, training fixes both

The Attention lab's RoPE model (at_mha: 6 layers, 8 heads, 1,500 steps on 256-token windows) reads 24 held-out windows of 2,048 TinyStories tokens; loss at each position, averaged over 64-position bins; grey band, the trained length. Two runs continued training for 300 steps at 1,024 tokens (2.46M tokens, a fifth of the original 12.3M); at_swa64 is the window-64 model. measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU)

What it shows. Plain RoPE goes from 2.07 inside the trained length to 3.82 at 1,024 to 2,047. Scaling at inference trades: linear x4 holds better far out (3.15) but costs the short range (2.74); YaRN x8 does best (2.43). 300 steps of training at 1,024 tokens beat every scaling rule. The window-64 model has no wall at all, because no query looks further than 64 tokens, which is also why it cannot retrieve from further.

At this scale the training, not the scaling rule, did the work. The window-64 model has no wall at all (2.1 in range, 2.09 at 1,024 to 2,047): no query ever looks further than 64 tokens, which is also why it cannot retrieve from further.

Why: a distance is learned only as often as it is seen

In one training window of length L, the distance d between a query and a key occurs L − d times: distance 1 at 255 query positions, distance 250 at 6. A model trained on 256-token windows has seen distances past 200 in about 5% of its query-key pairs and past 255 never. Drag to see the share of training pairs at or beyond a distance.

An et al. trace short effective contexts in open models to exactly this "left-skewed frequency distribution of relative positions", and find effective lengths "typically not exceeding half of their training lengths" ({{An et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18745}}, third-party). So production recipes pair a scaling rule with training at the new length: Gemma 3 pre-trains with 32K sequences and rescales RoPE by 8 to reach 128K ({{Gemma 3 report, section 5.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19786}}, vendor); Llama 3 grew from 8K to 128K over six stages (section 6). How to check: read rope_scaling and original_max_position_embeddings, then plot loss by position on long held-out documents; a curve that turns up at the trained length is the wall.

8. Effective context: accepting a token is not using it

A model can fit a 128K prompt in memory, read it without a position error, and still ignore most of it. The effective context is the longest input at which it still does a given task well: it depends on the task and the threshold and is measured, never read from the config. max_position_embeddings is an allocation, not a training fact: Qwen3 32B's config says 40,960 for a model trained to 32,768 natively, MiniMax-M1's 10,240,000 (root's Architecture map). Published measurements at scale (RULER found that of models claiming 32K or more "only half" hold up at 32K; NoLiMa removes the shared words between question and needle and finds most models effective to 2K or less; and position matters even inside the window: in Liu et al.'s lost-in-the-middle experiment accuracy traces a U, highest when the evidence comes first or last and lowest in the middle) are owned by {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}.

SmolLM2-135M retrieves one number up to its configured 8,192 tokens, then nothing

The pass-key prompt of section 4, full cache, seven lengths, five needle depths, four seeds per cell (140 prompts); cells give correct answers out of 4. Config: max_position_embeddings 8,192, RoPE base 100,000, no rope_scaling; the model card names the base model "SmolLM2-135M-8k" (read 2026-10-18); the SmolLM2 paper describes a 2K to 8K extension for the 1.7B model only ({{Allal et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02737}}, vendor). measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU)

Read it as a wall, not a slope. Inside 8,192 tokens the misses are near-misses (at 1K and 2K always the first digit, 1 for 6 or 7; at 8,192 only with the number 8K tokens back); past it every answer is broken text such as "a1...." for 66226. Short prompts are not easier for this 135M model, a reminder that 20 prompts per length is coarse.

Four causes, each with a section above: positions trained rarely (7); attention spread thinner over more keys, so one relevant token gets a smaller share (a length-dependent temperature, such as YaRN's, counters it); windows that cannot see far (5); caches compressed by policies the model never trained with (4). The shape of the heads matters too: at a fixed parameter budget, wider heads (a larger head dimension) barely changed pretraining loss or short-task scores but improved long-context retrieval, with more retrieval heads (heads that copy a token from far back) forming, and the gain held even with the attention projections frozen during long-context continued pretraining ({{Head Dimension Matters for Long-Context Retrieval (arXiv 2610.32213)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32213}}). How to check, at the length and prompt format you ship: loss by position, a retrieval grid over length and depth, then a task string matching cannot solve (NoLiMa-style). Report the length at which your threshold still holds.
