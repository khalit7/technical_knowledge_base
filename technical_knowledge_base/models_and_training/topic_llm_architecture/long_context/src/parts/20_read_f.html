4. Local and global layers: a smaller cache designed in, not cut out

The problem. Eviction (section 3) decides after training which entries to drop, and the model was never trained to cope. A local (sliding-window) layer makes the decision part of the architecture: during training and inference each query attends only to the last W tokens, so the layer's cache never holds more than W entries. A global layer attends to everything. Interleaving the two keeps a direct path to any old token in the global layers while most layers stay cheap. The root's Attention lab (section 6) shows the mechanism and the ring-buffer cache; {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}} owns windows as a cache design. Here: what the layouts in production cost and buy at length.

Gemma 3 keeps RoPE base 10,000 on local layers and 1,000,000 on global ones (config rope_local_base_freq, rope_theta), because only global layers ever see long distances.

What it costs in quality, published. Google reports that for Gemma 3 changing the local:global ratio has "minimal impact on perplexity", "even with 7-to-1 local to global", and that the window "can be reduced significantly without impacting perplexity" ({{Gemma 3 report, sections 5.1 and 5.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19786}}, vendor); on memory, "global only" gives "a memory overhead of 60%" at 32K, "reduced to less than 15%" with sliding windows of 1,024 (same section; the report writes the ratio as "1:3" there and 5:1 elsewhere, an inconsistency in the source). Gemma 2's Table 10 shrinks the trained 4,096 window at inference to 2,048 and 1,024: validation perplexity 1.63, 1.63, 1.64 (vendor). Two cautions: all of these are perplexity, which section 3 measured to be the wrong test for long-range use, and all are models trained with their layout. Databricks' MixAttention study, which trained sliding-window plus cross-layer sharing variants and tested them on RULER, found that long-context ability needs "at least one standard attention KV cache that is computed in the deeper layers" ({{Rajput et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15012v1}}, third-party to Character.AI, whose 2024 blog on the same combination it builds on).

Measured here: imposing a layout does not work. SmolLM2-135M was trained with full attention in all 30 layers. Turning 25 of them into local layers after training (keep the first 4 tokens plus a window, every sixth layer global, at the same memory as the other policies) is the local_global policy of the {{Eviction lab|#t-evict}}: result pending. The model never learned to route old information through its global layers. A layout is a training-time decision; converting a trained model needs continued training with the layout in place.

Cross-layer sharing attacks the other factor of the formula, the layer count: adjacent layers reuse one set of keys and values. Cross-Layer Attention reports it can "reduce the size of the KV cache by another 2x" beyond MQA "while maintaining nearly the same accuracy as unmodified MQA", trained from scratch at 1B and 3B parameters ({{Brandon et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12981}}, third-party). The extreme is a model with a constant-size state in most layers: hybrids of attention with Mamba-2 or Gated DeltaNet layers (Nemotron-H 8B keeps 4 attention layers of 52; Qwen3-Next one in four), owned by {{Beyond attention|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8142bc9ecac1fc366966}}.

When it matters, how to check. Local layers pay off once the context is several times the window; below that they cost nothing and save nothing. Check the layout in config.json (sliding_window with layer_types or sliding_window_pattern, attention_chunk_size), and check that your engine actually frees windowed entries: a cache that allocates every layer at full length gives the quality cost of windows without their memory. Then test the long-range path with retrieval at the target length, not perplexity.
