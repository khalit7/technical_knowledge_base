One trained model, one 4,096-token prompt, and seven ways to keep only part of its KV cache, against two ways to keep all of it in fewer bits. Every number here is a forward pass of SmolLM2-135M on this machine measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), fp32, 4 threads, deterministic. Reading time about 10 minutes with the animation.

{{Batching, KV cache and scheduling|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}} already ran H2O, SnapKV and random eviction on Qwen3-0.6B at about 1,000 tokens and showed which savings a paged engine can actually reclaim. This lab goes where that one did not: four times the context, the policies an architecture builds in (a window, local and global layers), quantisation on the same memory axis, a question that arrives after compression, and which heads must keep a fact for the model to use it.

1. The test

Prompt. TinyStories validation stories (CDLA-Sharing-1.0) concatenated to 4,096 SmolLM2 tokens, half SmolLM2's 8,192 maximum. Three tasks, each scored with the full cache and under every policy:

  • Pass-key (30 prompts: needle depths 10%, 30%, 50%, 70%, 90% of the prompt, 6 seeds): one planted sentence, "The secret number is 66226. Remember it.", and at the end "Question: What is the secret number? Answer: The secret number is". Correct means all five digits right under greedy decoding.
  • Copy (6 prompts): one story planted at a depth, then repeated after the prompt; the loss on its first 128 tokens is low only if the earlier copy can still be read (the model's induction heads copy it).
  • New story (6 prompts): the continuation is an unseen story, so the old context hardly helps: this is what perplexity-style evaluations measure.

Protocol. Prefill the whole prompt with full attention (as an engine does), then compress the cache, then score only the continuation tokens against what survived: prefill-then-compress, the setting of SnapKV and of the Batching page's run. Each policy keeps the same share of entries in every layer and KV head (SmolLM2 has 30 layers of 3 KV heads: 90 caches); quantised caches keep every entry in fewer bits.
