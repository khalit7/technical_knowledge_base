The context a model accepts is a number in its config; the context it uses is measured. Two measurements here, both on this machine: the topic's tiny GPT (trained on 256-token windows) read far past its trained length with and without RoPE scaling, and SmolLM2-135M asked to retrieve one planted number from 1,024 to 16,384 tokens, twice its max_position_embeddings. measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), 4 threads Reading time about 7 minutes.

How the scaling methods work (position interpolation, NTK, YaRN, Llama 3's rule) belongs to {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}; how long-context ability is benchmarked at frontier scale (RULER, NoLiMa, MRCR, an effective-length calculator over published rows) to {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}. This tab supplies the measured mechanism behind both.

1. The tiny GPT past its trained length

The Attention lab's RoPE model (at_mha: 6 layers, 8 heads, trained 1,500 steps on 256-token windows) reads 24 held-out windows of 2,048 TinyStories tokens. The curve is the loss at each position (averaged over 64-position bins); the grey band is the trained length. Scaling is applied at inference only, except the two fine-tuned runs, which continued training for 300 steps at 1,024 tokens (2.46M tokens, a fifth of the original 12.3M) with plain RoPE or with position interpolation (linear x4), and the Attention lab's sliding-window model (at_swa64: window 64, RoPE, trained at 256) as read.

2. Why: a relative distance is learned only as often as it is seen

In one training window of length L, the distance d between a query and a key occurs L − d times: distance 1 at 255 query positions, distance 250 at 6. A model trained on 256-token windows has seen distances past 200 in about 5% of its query-key pairs (counting distance 0, a token and itself), and distances past 255 never. The same left-skew, measured over real pretraining data, is the explanation An et al. give for why "the effective context lengths of open-source LLMs often fall short, typically not exceeding half of their training lengths" ({{An et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18745}}, third-party).
