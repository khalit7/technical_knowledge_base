5. What this means, what transfers, how to check

  • Perplexity is the wrong test for a compressed cache. H2O at 12.5% raises the new-story loss from 2.07 to 2.10 and answers 0% of pass-key questions (full cache 97%). Test with retrieval and copying at your real length, as {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}} explains for models in general.
  • Question-aware selection works only if the question is there. SnapKV keeps 97% at a quarter of the cache when the question is in its observation window, and 0% when the cache was compressed before the question arrived. Multi-turn chats, agents and prefix caches compress before the question: they need a question-agnostic method or no eviction.
  • Fewer bits beat fewer entries at equal memory here. KIVI-style int4 at 31% of the bytes answers 93% and keeps copying (loss 0.66 against 0.61); no eviction policy at 25% keeps copying. At 2 bits, per-channel keys matter: 50% against 7% for per-token rounding, the effect KIVI's authors report (keys have outlier channels, so one scale per token wastes its range).
  • Never drop the first tokens. The recent-only window sends the new-story loss to 4.02 at 50%; keeping 4 sink tokens brings it back to 2.08, the same lesson as the root's Inside a real model sink demo.

What transfers: the ranking of policies on each task, the gap between perplexity and retrieval, the role of the question and of sinks, and the per-channel effect at 2 bits; these are mechanisms. What does not: a 135M model at 4,096 tokens is far from a 70B model at 128K, where caches are larger, more redundant and eviction is usually gentler; absolute percentages will differ. Thirty prompts give a resolution of about 3 percentage points (one prompt). Memory is counted as entries kept; a paged engine reclaims only blocks that every head emptied ({{Batching, KV cache and scheduling|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}} measured that per-head selections free almost none).

The code that ran (src/kv/smol.py, lines 102 to 104) and its check

SmolLM2's attention is written out by hand so eviction can be a mask over the cached entries, per layer and KV head; masking an entry is exactly removing it. The continuation tokens attend to the surviving prompt entries and causally to each other:

kp = keep[li].repeat_interleave(g, 0)                       # (nh, T)
mask = torch.cat([kp[:, None, :].expand(nh, n, T), causal[None].expand(nh, n, n)], -1)
o = F.scaled_dot_product_attention(q[None], Kall[None], Vall[None], attn_mask=mask[None])[0]

Check against transformers 5.20.1's own forward on 1,024 tokens (prefill 900, continue 124, full cache): largest logit difference 1.9e-04 on logits up to 41.9 in size (src/kv/out/check.json). Runs: python run_kv.py evict (about 10 minutes on 4 CPU threads).
