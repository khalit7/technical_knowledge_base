The idea in one sentenceEvery token a transformer has read leaves a key and a value in every attention layer, so the KV cache grows in a straight line with the context at a rate the architecture fixes; long context is the craft of lowering that rate (or the number of tokens kept), and then proving the model still uses what it kept.

One 128K-token request to Llama 3.1 8B holds as much memory as the whole model

Memory of one request on one 80 GB GPU, to scale, against context. The weights (16.06 GB in bf16) are paid once; the cache grows by 128 KiB with every token, reaching 17.18 GB at 131,072 tokens. The same model with one KV head per query head (MHA, as Llama 2 was built) would grow four times as fast and run out of the GPU before 110K tokens. derived from config.json; the {{KV-cache calculator|#t-kv}} does this for 45 models.

KV cache: the keys and values of every earlier token, per layer, kept so decoding does not recompute them.
Prefill / decode: reading the prompt in one pass / generating one token at a time against the cache.
Local (sliding-window) layer: attends to the last W tokens only, so its cache stops at W entries; a global layer keeps all.
Eviction: dropping cached entries by a score; attention sink: the first tokens, which soak up attention and must be kept.
Position wall: the loss rising past the trained length, where RoPE angles were never seen.
Effective context: the longest input at which the model still does a given task well; measured, never read from a config.

1. What one token costs

The numbers first. Llama 3.1 8B has 32 layers, each with 8 KV heads of 128 numbers (its 32 query heads share them). One token therefore stores 2 (K and V) × 32 × 8 × 128 = 65,536 numbers, 2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 131,072 bytes in bf16: 128 KiB per token. At its advertised 131,072 tokens that is 16 GiB (17.18 GB) for one sequence, as much as its 16.06 GB of bf16 weights; one 80 GB GPU holds three such sequences after the weights, thirteen at 32K. Llama 3.1 70B (80 layers, same heads) needs 320 KiB per token, 40 GiB per 128K sequence. derived

What one token leaves in one layer, by attention design, to scale

One square is 64 numbers. MHA, GQA-8 and MQA at Llama 3.1 8B's shape (32 query heads of 128); MLA at DeepSeek-V3's (a 512-number latent plus a 64-number RoPE key, whatever the 128 heads); DSA adds a 128-number indexer key (DeepSeek-V3.2, GLM-5). A sliding-window layer stores the same entry as its full twin but only for the last W tokens; a recurrent layer stores nothing per token and keeps a fixed state instead. How each design works: {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}.

The mistake worth one line: sizing with query heads

With num_attention_heads Llama 3.1 70B comes out at 2,560 KiB per token instead of 320 KiB, eight times too much (1 sequence at 128K on eight 80 GB GPUs instead of 10). For MLA, the same slip gives 3.81 MiB per token for DeepSeek-V3 instead of 68.6 KiB.

How to check. Read num_hidden_layers, num_key_value_heads, head_dim from config.json, then the fields that change the sum: sliding_window with layer_types or sliding_window_pattern, MLA's kv_lora_rank, an indexer's head dim, linear-attention layers. vLLM prints the cache it allocated at start-up, and its metric vllm:kv_cache_usage_perc shows how full it is ({{Batching, KV cache and scheduling|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}).

Why two pages can give two cache sizes for the same model (accounting conventions)
  • Windows credited or not. The "card" convention ({{Raschka's gallery|https://sebastianraschka.com/llm-architecture-gallery/kv-cache-calculations/}}: "the logical cache added by one token for one sequence") counts every layer at full cost, so Gemma 3 27B is 496 KiB per token; what one 128K request actually holds, windows credited, is 10.41 GiB, not 62 GiB.
  • DSA indexer keys counted or not. DeepSeek-V3.2 is 68.6 KiB per token for its MLA latents alone and 83.9 KiB with its 128-number indexer keys in bf16; in its released FP8 decode layout the DeepSeek page reports 48,068 bytes per token (FlashMLA). GLM-5 likewise: 87.75 KiB of latents, 107.3 KiB with indexer keys.
  • Published mixed layouts. DeepSeek-V4 stores FP8 entries with bf16 RoPE dimensions and an FP4 indexer: 4,939.5 bytes per token for V4-Pro on the DeepSeek page, against about 9.7 KiB if everything were bf16 (the attention-variants convention).
  • Recurrent state dtype. FP32 here and in the root calculator (Qwen3.5 and 3.8 configs say mamba_ssm_dtype: float32); 16-bit on the Qwen, GLM and Memory technology pages, which halves every state figure. How serving engines store it varies.
  • "1M". 1,048,576 tokens here and on Attention variants; 1,000,000 on the DeepSeek page. Ratios do not change; absolute figures differ by 4.9%.
  • Units. KiB and GiB are powers of 2; GB is 109 bytes: 16 GiB is 17.18 GB.
Family by family: the sizing facts gathered from the provider and neighbour pages
  • Llama and MHA. Llama 2 7B (32 KV heads) needs 512 KiB per token: 4.29 GB at 8K, 17.18 GB at 32K, more than its 13.48 GB of fp16 weights. A Llama 3.1 8B conversation of 32,768 tokens holds 4.29 GB, a quarter of the weights, so 4 such conversations hold more than the weights. Llama 3.1 70B at 32,768 tokens: 10.74 GB per sequence; on eight 80 GB H100s at 92% that leaves room for 41 of them.
  • Mistral. Mistral 7B: 2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 131,072 bytes, 128 KiB per token; 32,768 tokens would need 4 GiB, the 4,096-token rolling buffer (slot i mod W) keeps it at 512 MiB; full MHA would need 16 GiB, more than the model's own weights (about 14.5 GB at 16 bits). Mixtral 8x7B has the same 128 KiB per token without the window; Mixtral 8x22B 224 KiB; Ministral 3 3B, 8B and 14B 104, 136 and 160 KiB; Mistral Medium 3.5 and Devstral 2 352 KiB over 88 layers; the latent models Mistral Small 4 (256 + 64) and Mistral Large 3 (512 + 64) 22.5 KiB and 68.6 KiB. One sequence at 256K: 5.6 GiB on Small 4, 17.2 GiB on Large 3 and 88 GiB on Medium 3.5 (Mistral AI page).
  • Qwen. Only full-attention layers grow. Qwen3-235B-A22B: 94 × 2 × 4 × 128 × 2 = 192,512 bytes, 188 KiB per token, 47 GiB at 262,144 tokens; Qwen3-32B 64 GiB. Hybrids stay under 25 GiB including their DeltaNet states: Qwen3-Next 24 KiB per token, about 6 GiB at 262,144; Qwen3.5-397B-A17B 30 KiB; Qwen3.8-27B 64 KiB; Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B 92 KiB, 23.3 GiB, half of Qwen3-235B's cache. A DeltaNet state beats the cache it replaces after 512 tokens in Qwen3-Next and 768 in Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (786,432 / 1,024 numbers); Flash-Next's QSA indexer keys are not counted (Qwen page; QSA cuts reads and arithmetic, not storage).
  • GLM. GLM-4.5: 92 × 4,096 = 376,832 bytes, 368 KiB per token, 46 GiB at 128K. GLM-5 and 5.1: 78 × (576 + 128) numbers, 107.3 KiB; GLM-5.2 and 5.3 share indexer keys (21 of 78 layers): 47,616 numbers, 93 KiB, 93 GiB at 1M, and the vendor says IndexShare "does not proportionally reduce per-token KV-cache size". GLM-5.3-Flash: 11 sparse layers × (512 + 128/4) = 5,984 numbers, about 11.7 KiB per token plus 68 MiB of KDA state at 16 bits; 8.0 times less than GLM-5.3 per token, 4.59 times per layer against the published 4.44 (layer mix 4.09 × rotary key 1.125 × IndexPool 1.18; 3.9 without pooling). DSA saves reading, not storing.
  • DeepSeek. DeepSeek LLM 67B: 389,120 bytes per token (95 layers, 8 KV heads). V2: 69,120; V3 and V3.2's latents: 70,272; V4-Pro 4,939.5 (an FP4 indexer assumed; FP8 indexer keys give 2.17% of GQA-8 and none 1.78%); V4-Flash 3,514 reported; V4.1-Flash 890 reported, 528 bytes per entry in FP8 or 288 in FP4. At 1M tokens: MHA at V3's shape about 3.6 TiB per sequence, GQA-8 233 GiB, MLA 65 GiB, V4-Pro 4.6 GiB, V4.1-Flash under 1 GiB (DeepSeek page, 1M = 1,000,000).
  • Vision-language decoders. Image tokens cost cache like text tokens: SmolVLM-500M keeps 2 × 32 × 5 × 64 × 2 = 40,960 bytes per token (the measured slope is exactly the config arithmetic); LLaVA-1.5-7B 524,288, so one 576-token image holds 302 MB; Qwen3.5-0.8B 12,288 bytes from its 6 attention layers of 24 plus a fixed 19,759,104-byte FP32 state, worth about 1,608 tokens. How many tokens an image becomes is on {{Vision-language models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81adbb7be5bc41c4d9d0}}.
