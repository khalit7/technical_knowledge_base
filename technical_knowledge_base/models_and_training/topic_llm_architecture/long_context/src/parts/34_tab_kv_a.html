Units: KiB, MiB, GiB are powers of 2; GB is 109 bytes; 1M on the slider is 1,048,576 tokens.

Predict, then drag. Llama 3.1 8B keeps 128 KiB per token and its weights are 16.06 GB in bf16. At what context does one request's cache weigh as much as the whole model?
16.06 × 109 / 131,072 bytes = about 122,500 tokens, inside the model's advertised 131,072. The "Cache = weights at" tile below gives it for any model and design.

What this does not model. Activations and engine workspace (they live in the 8% vLLM leaves by default), paged-block rounding and fragmentation (PagedAttention keeps waste under one block per sequence; before it, "only 20.4% to 38.2%" of KV memory held tokens, {{Kwon et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180}}), tensor-parallel replication when there are fewer KV heads than GPUs, MXFP4 or other mixed weight formats (weights here are params × one dtype, all experts counted), prefix caching, and quality: every design but the dtype changes the model, and eviction is counted as if every dropped entry were freed. The decode ceiling is (weights + this sequence's cache) / bandwidth, an upper bound at batch 1.

Accelerators (memory and bandwidth as vendors state them, from {{Memory technology|n:3f05c17b0d0d818ab063e327e3d7009f}}): H100 SXM 80 GB, 3.35 TB/s; H200 141 GB, 4.8 TB/s; B200 (HGX) 180 GB, 8.0 TB/s; B300 288 GB, 8.0 TB/s; MI300X 192 GB, 5.3 TB/s; MI355X 288 GB, 8.0 TB/s; TPU7x (Ironwood) 206.2 GB, 7.38 TB/s; RTX 5090 32 GB, 1.792 TB/s; Apple M1 Pro 16 GB, 0.2 TB/s. With several devices the calculator splits weights evenly and adds bandwidth, as tensor parallelism would.
