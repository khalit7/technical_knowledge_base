Reading it. No single lever reaches 32. fp8 is free but only doubles capacity; MQA and a local/global layout cut the cache 8 and 6 times but are training-time choices (section 5); only the windowed layout also cuts prefill compute (attention from 70% to 28% of the FLOPs). The design review answer: a model trained with a local/global layout (as Gemma 3 and gpt-oss are), an fp8 cache, chunked prefill so the 128K prompts do not stall the 31 streams already decoding, sinks never evicted, and a retrieval acceptance test at 128K, because none of these numbers says the model uses the context (section 8).

Other budgets. Many short chats favour MLA or few KV heads plus prefix caching. Long single sequences (agents, codebases) make one sequence's cache and its prefill dominate: hybrids with a constant-state mixer in most layers or trained sparse attention change the curve rather than shift it. Mixture-of-experts models move the weight bar, not the cache: DeepSeek-V3 needs 671 GB of fp8 weights on eight 141 GB GPUs but only 9.21 GB of MLA cache per 128K sequence, so 39 fit. Depth

Design question: a coding agent that reads 1M tokens of repository per task

Concurrency is low, so one sequence's cache and the prefill dominate. At 1,048,576 tokens Llama 3.1 8B's cache alone is 128 GiB; DeepSeek-V3's MLA 68.6 GiB; Qwen3-Next's hybrid 24 GiB; DeepSeek-V4-Pro's compressed attention under 5 GiB (calculator). So: a hybrid with constant-state layers in most of the stack plus a few global attention layers (Qwen3-Next's 1 in 4, Nemotron-H's 4 of 52), or trained sparse or compressed attention; context parallelism for the prefill across GPUs; an fp8 cache for the global layers; no question-aware eviction (the question changes every turn); evaluation of retrieval and multi-hop use at 1M, and retrieval into a shorter context when the effective length falls short ({{Agentic retrieval, long context and memory|n:3c65c17b0d0d8105a68cfb504ed4b0e8}}).

Design question: eviction or quantisation at the same memory?

Quantisation first: it keeps every entry, needs no attention scores, works with fused kernels and paged blocks, and 8 or 4 bits cost little (here int4 kept retrieval and copying; eviction at 25% kept neither copying nor, unless question-aware, retrieval). SnapKV beats H2O on a planted fact (87% against 0% at 12.5%) because it scores by the question's own attention, while H2O keeps tokens many queries attended, which a fact read once is not. Evict when the context itself must be bounded (streaming), with sinks kept.

Design question: why not make a trained full-attention model local/global after the fact?

Its retrieval heads are spread over many layers; making most of them local removes the paths it learned (SmolLM2 with 25 of 30 layers made local: 37% at half the cache, 3% at a quarter, copy loss 1.56). Models with a layout are trained with it (Gemma 2 and 3, gpt-oss); conversion needs continued training.
