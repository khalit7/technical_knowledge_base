6. Past the trained length: the position wall

The problem. A model is trained on windows of some length; at inference nothing stops a longer prompt. With RoPE (rotary position embedding: each query and key is rotated by an angle proportional to its position, so their dot product depends on the distance), a longer prompt produces angles, for the slowly rotating dimensions, that training never showed. With learned absolute positions there is no row in the table at all. {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}} owns the schemes and the extension methods (position interpolation, NTK-aware scaling, YaRN, Llama 3's rule); this section shows what the wall costs and why training at length is still needed.

Measured here on the tiny GPT trained on 256-token windows (the {{Effective context lab|#t-eff}}, section 1; loss per token on 2,048-token held-out windows): plain RoPE goes from 2.07 inside the trained length to 3.82 at positions 1,024 to 2,047. Scaling at inference changes the trade: linear interpolation x4 holds better at length (3.15) but costs the short range (2.74 in the first 256 positions); YaRN x8 stays at 2.43 at 1,024 to 2,047 with 2.29 in range. A learned-position model simply raises IndexError: learned positions stop at 256; got position 299. measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU)

Training at length, and a model with no wall. 300 more steps at 1,024 tokens (2.46M tokens, a fifth of the original budget) bring positions 512 to 1,023 down to 2.03 with position interpolation x4 and to 2.03 with plain RoPE, below the in-length loss of the original model; past the new length the interpolated model hits a new wall (3.29 at 1,024 to 2,047, where x4 runs out) while the plain one degrades gently (2.14). At this toy scale the training, not the scaling rule, did the work. The Attention lab's sliding-window model (window 64, RoPE, trained at 256) shows no wall at all: 2.1 in range, 2.09 at 1,024 to 2,047, because no query ever looks further than 64 tokens, so no unseen angle appears. It extrapolates by never using long distances, which is also why it cannot retrieve from them.

Why scaling alone is not enough. In a window of length L, distance d between a query and a key occurs L − d times, so long distances are rare in training even inside the window (the lab's histogram). An et al. trace short effective contexts in open models to exactly this "left-skewed frequency distribution of relative positions", and find effective lengths "typically not exceeding half of their training lengths" ({{An et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18745}}, third-party). Production recipes therefore pair a scaling rule with a training phase at the new length: Gemma 3 pre-trains with 32K sequences and rescales RoPE by a factor of 8 to reach 128K ({{Gemma 3 report, section 5.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19786}}, vendor); Llama 3 grew from 8K to 128K over six stages (section 5).

How to check. Read rope_scaling and original_max_position_embeddings in the config; plot loss by position on long held-out documents past the length you need; a curve that turns up at the trained length is the position wall.

7. Effective context: accepting a token is not using it

The problem. A model can fit a 128K prompt in memory, read it without a position error, and still ignore most of it. The effective context is the longest input at which the model still does a given task well; it depends on the task and the threshold, and it is measured, never read from the config. Published measurements at scale (RULER found that of models claiming 32K or more "only half" hold up at 32K; NoLiMa removes the shared words between question and needle and finds most models effective to 2K or less) are on {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}, which owns them.

Why it happens, architecturally. Four causes, each with a section above: positions trained rarely (section 6); softmax attention spreading thinner over more keys, so one relevant token gets a smaller share of a head's attention as the context grows (a length-dependent attention temperature counters it; YaRN's is one, owned by Positional Encodings); local layers and windows that cannot see far (section 4); and caches compressed by policies the model never trained with (section 3).

Measured here on SmolLM2-135M, full cache (the {{Effective context lab|#t-eff}}, section 3): pending Within the config's length the small model retrieves most numbers (its misses at 1K and 2K are all the first digit, 1 for 6 or 7); at 8,192 it fails only when the number sits at the very start, 8K tokens back; at 12,288 and 16,384 it answers none and produces broken text such as "a1...." for 66226: a hard wall at the advertised length, with no scaling configured.

How to check. At the length and with the prompt format you will use: a retrieval grid over depth, then a task that cannot be solved by string matching. Report the length at which your threshold is still met, not the config's maximum.
