4. Fewer entries: eviction

Most cached tokens get little attention most of the time; eviction scores each entry and keeps the best within a budget. StreamingLLM keeps the first few tokens (the attention sinks) plus a recent window: fluent forever, but nothing that left the window is remembered ({{Xiao et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453}}). H2O keeps the "heavy hitters", tokens that "contribute most of the value when computing attention scores", plus recent ones ({{Zhang et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14048}}). SnapKV keeps what the last prompt tokens, usually the question, attend to ({{Li et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14469}}).

The test here: SmolLM2-135M, one 4,096-token prompt of TinyStories with a planted sentence ("The secret number is 66226. Remember it.") and a question at the end; prefill with the full cache, compress, then score the continuation against what survived (prefill-then-compress, SnapKV's setting). Each policy keeps the same share in every one of the 90 caches. measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), fp32, 4 threads, deterministic.

Fewer bits beat fewer entries at equal memory; perplexity hides the difference

What the runs show. Only a question-aware policy survives: SnapKV keeps 97% at a quarter of the cache, but 0% when it scored the cache before the question arrived. Every eviction policy loses copying already at half the cache, while int4 at 31% of the bytes keeps it. Perplexity sees none of this: H2O at 12.5% moves the new-story loss from 2.07 to 2.10 while answering 0% of pass-key questions. And keep the first tokens: a recent window without sinks sends the new-story loss to 4.02; with 4 sinks, 2.08.

Two lessons a paper's headline will not give you

An eviction method is only as good as the test: perplexity or summarisation can stay flat while retrieval of a single fact goes to zero. And question-aware methods need the question at compression time; in a multi-turn chat, an agent loop or a shared prefix cache the document is compressed before anyone asks, and the method becomes another heuristic.

Cost, and how to check. Scoring needs attention weights that fused kernels never materialise; the prefill peak is untouched; per-head selections free no paged blocks (the Batching page measured 0% of 16-token blocks freed). Test retrieval and copying at the budget, with the question where your application puts it, and measure memory the engine actually frees. (The root's Inside a real model tab finds SmolLM2's sink switching on at layer 12.)

The protocol, all nine policies, every result, which heads must keep the fact, and what transfers

Every result. At a quarter of the cache SnapKV still answers 97% and at 12.5% 87%; scored before the question arrived it answers 0% at a quarter (30% at half); H2O answers 17% even at half the cache, sink plus recent 10% at 12.5%. Re-reading an earlier story, every eviction policy loses most of the benefit already at half the cache (copy loss 0.61 full, 1.42 for the best policy at 50%), while KIVI int4 at 31% of the bytes keeps it (0.66). On an unseen story, the perplexity-style test, all of them look fine: H2O at 12.5% moves the loss from 2.07 to 2.10 while answering 0% of pass-key questions. And never drop the first tokens: a plain recent window at half the cache sends the new-story loss to 4.02; keeping 4 sink tokens brings it back to 2.08.

What transfers from a 135M model at 4,096 tokens: the ranking of policies per task, the perplexity gap, the role of the question and of sinks, the per-channel effect at 2 bits. What does not: absolute percentages (a 70B model at 128K has a larger, more redundant cache); 30 prompts resolve about 3 points. H2O's authors report up to 29 times the throughput of DeepSpeed Zero-Inference and Hugging Face Accelerate with 20% heavy hitters (authors' claims, not run here).

Three tasks. Pass-key (30 prompts: needle depths 10%, 30%, 50%, 70%, 90%, 6 seeds; correct means all five digits right under greedy decoding). Copy (6 prompts: one story planted at a depth, then repeated after the prompt; the loss on its first 128 tokens is low only if the earlier copy can still be read by the induction heads). New story (6 prompts: an unseen continuation, what perplexity evaluations measure). Prompts: TinyStories validation (CDLA-Sharing-1.0) concatenated to 4,096 SmolLM2 tokens, half its 8,192 maximum.
