5. Local and global layers: a smaller cache designed in, not cut out

Eviction decides after training which entries to drop, and the model was never trained to cope. A local layer makes the decision part of the architecture: in training and inference each query sees only the last W tokens, so the layer's cache never holds more than W entries. Interleaving local layers with a few global ones keeps a direct path to any old token while most layers stay cheap (the ring-buffer mechanism: section 4 of {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}).

Mistral's paper says the window "reduces the cache memory usage by 8x" at its 32,768-token sequences (32,768 / 4,096, reproduced independently), and stacking 32 windowed layers reaches 32 × 4,096 = 131,072 tokens in theory. Gemma 3 keeps RoPE base 10,000 on local layers and 1,000,000 on global ones (rope_local_base_freq, rope_theta), because only global layers see long distances. On one 80 GB GPU in bf16, Gemma 3 27B as released fits one 128K request (11.17 GB of cache after 54 GB of weights) and, with every layer global, none (66.57 GB for one): the local layers are what make 128K servable at all on one GPU.

Published quality cost: small, when trained in. Google reports for Gemma 3 that changing the local:global ratio has "minimal impact on perplexity", "even with 7-to-1 local to global" (vendor). Two cautions: that is perplexity, which section 4 measured to be the wrong test, and on models trained with their layout.

Measured here: imposing a layout does not work. SmolLM2-135M was trained with full attention in all 30 layers. Making 25 of them local after training (sinks plus a window, every sixth layer global, at the same memory as the other policies; the local_global policy of section 4): result pending. The model never learned to route old information through its global layers; converting a trained model needs continued training with the layout in place.

Other ways to cut the per-token rate, each a training-time design: share keys and values across adjacent layers; replace most layers with a fixed recurrent state (hybrids); or compress and sparsify what is stored and read (DSA saves reading, not storing; DeepSeek-V4 stores about 2% of a GQA-8 cache at 1M tokens). The published evidence in full, and the other per-token levers

Gemma 3 report: the ratio changes have "minimal impact on perplexity", "even with 7-to-1 local to global", and the window "can be reduced significantly without impacting perplexity"; on memory, "global only" gives "a memory overhead of 60%" at 32K, "reduced to less than 15%" with sliding windows of 1,024 ({{Gemma 3 report, sections 5.1 and 5.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19786}}, vendor; the report writes the ratio as "1:3" there and 5:1 elsewhere). Our arithmetic for 27B in bf16 gives 31% and 5.8%: the same fivefold cut, not the same numbers; we did not find which model size and precision the report's figure uses. Gemma 2's Table 10 shrinks the trained 4,096 window at inference to 2,048 and 1,024: validation perplexity 1.63, 1.63, 1.64 (vendor). Databricks' MixAttention study, testing on RULER, found long-context ability needs "at least one standard attention KV cache that is computed in the deeper layers" ({{Rajput et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15012v1}}, third-party, building on Character.AI's 2024 blog).

Cross-layer sharing attacks the layer count: adjacent layers reuse one set of keys and values; Cross-Layer Attention reports it can "reduce the size of the KV cache by another 2x" beyond MQA "while maintaining nearly the same accuracy as unmodified MQA", trained from scratch at 1B and 3B ({{Brandon et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12981}}, third-party). Hybrids keep a fixed recurrent state in most layers: Nemotron-H 8B keeps 4 attention layers of 52 (16 KiB per token plus about 99 MiB of state per sequence), Qwen3-Next one in four (24 KiB per token; its DeltaNet state equals the cache of 512 tokens of the layers it replaces), GLM-5.3-Flash 11 sparse layers of 45 (owned by {{Beyond attention|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8142bc9ecac1fc366966}}). Sparse and compressed attention changes what is read or stored: DSA (DeepSeek-V3.2, GLM-5) stores every latent plus an indexer key and reads only the top 2,048, so it saves reading, not storing; DeepSeek-V4 compresses entries 4 or 128 to one, about 2% of a GQA-8 cache at 1M tokens (mechanisms on {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}; sizes in the {{KV-cache calculator|#t-kv}}).

When it matters, how to check. Local layers pay off once the context is several times the window; below that they cost and save nothing. Check the layout in config.json (sliding_window with layer_types or sliding_window_pattern, attention_chunk_size), check that your engine frees windowed entries (a cache that allocates every layer at full length gives the quality cost of windows without their memory), then test the long-range path with retrieval at the target length.

6. Long prompts: the compute side

A 128K-token prompt is a 128K-row attention matrix per head per layer, and it costs in four separate ways, each fixed exactly (no quality cost) by a neighbouring page's method: FlashAttention keeps the score matrix out of memory, chunked prefill stops one long prompt stalling everyone else's stream, context parallelism splits one sequence across GPUs, and only windows, trained sparse attention or recurrent layers cut the quadratic FLOPs themselves.
