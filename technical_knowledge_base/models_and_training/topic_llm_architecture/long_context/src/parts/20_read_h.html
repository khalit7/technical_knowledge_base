The question. "Serve 128K-token requests, 32 at a time, on one 80 GB GPU." Start from Llama 3.1 8B and move one lever at a time in the {{Memory wall|#t-wall}} (bf16 weights, 92% of memory usable; derived, src/wall/out/examples.json):

Reading the table. No single lever reaches 32. The fp8 cache is free to adopt (section 2 measured 8 bits as lossless here) but only doubles capacity. MQA or a local/global layout cut the cache 8 and 6 times, but both are training-time choices: on a released model they mean uptraining or continued training with the new layout, and the Eviction lab shows that imposing a layout without training fails. Only the windowed layout also cuts prefill compute (attention from 70% to 28% of the FLOPs), which matters as much for time to first token as memory does for concurrency. The answer a good design review gives: a model trained with a local/global layout (as Gemma 3 and gpt-oss are), an fp8 cache, chunked prefill so the 128K prompts do not stall the 31 streams already decoding, and an acceptance test on retrieval at 128K, because none of these numbers says the model uses the context (section 7).

Other budgets. Long single sequences (agents, codebases) favour fewer, longer requests: there the cache of one sequence and the prefill dominate, and hybrids with a constant-state mixer in most layers ({{Beyond attention|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8142bc9ecac1fc366966}}) or trained sparse attention (DeepSeek's DSA) change the curve rather than shifting it. Many short chats favour MLA or few KV heads plus prefix caching. Mixture-of-experts models move the weight bar, not the cache: DeepSeek-V3 needs 671 GB of fp8 weights on eight 141 GB GPUs but only 9.21 GB of MLA cache per 128K sequence, so 39 fit.

Common mistakes

"max_position_embeddings is the context the model was trained for."
It is the size of an allocation and of the RoPE cache in the implementation. Qwen3 32B's config says 40,960 for a model trained to 32,768 natively; MiniMax-M1's says 10,240,000 (root's Architecture map). Read the model card and rope_scaling, then measure (section 7).
Sizing the cache with num_attention_heads.
The cache holds KV heads. With num_attention_heads Llama 3.1 70B comes out at 2,560 KiB per token instead of 320 KiB, eight times too much (1 sequence at 128K on eight 80 GB GPUs instead of 10). Use num_key_value_heads, and for MLA kv_lora_rank + qk_rope_head_dim.
Running a learned-position model past its table.
The tiny GPT with learned positions, given 300 tokens: IndexError: learned positions stop at 256; got position 299 (the topic's model raises on purpose, with a clear message). There is no scaling rule for a learned table: retrain or switch position scheme.
Evicting the first tokens.
A plain recent window applied to SmolLM2 at half the cache: new-story loss 4.02; keep 4 sink tokens as well: 2.08 (full cache 2.07). Every eviction policy must protect the sink tokens.
Validating a compressed cache with perplexity.
H2O at 12.5% of the cache: new-story loss 2.07 to 2.10, pass-key 97% to 0%. Test retrieval and copying at your length.
Question-aware eviction where the question comes later.
SnapKV at a quarter of the cache: 97% with the question in its window, 0% when the cache was compressed first. Prefix caches, multi-turn chats and agents compress before the question.
2-bit keys quantised per token.
Pass-key 7% against 50% with keys per channel (KIVI's layout); the new-story loss also rises to 2.45. Keys have outlier channels; quantise them along the channel.
Stretching RoPE at inference and calling it done.
Linear interpolation x4 on the tiny GPT costs the short range: 2.07 to 2.74 in the first 256 positions. Every production recipe adds training at the new length (section 6).

Interview questions

KV cache of Llama 3.1 70B at 128K tokens, in your head?
2 (K and V) × 80 layers × 8 KV heads × 128 head dim × 2 bytes = 327,680 bytes, 320 KiB per token; times 131,072 tokens = 40 GiB per sequence. Weights are 141 GB in bf16, so eight 80 GB GPUs hold about 10 such sequences.
Why does going from GQA to MQA not speed up a long prefill?
Prefill attention FLOPs scale with query heads times positions read; fewer KV heads only shrink what is stored and what decode reads. MQA helps decode (bandwidth) and concurrency (memory), not prefill compute. Windows, sparse attention or recurrent layers cut prefill.
StreamingLLM runs for millions of tokens. Does the model remember them?
No. It keeps the sink tokens so attention stays well behaved, plus a recent window; anything that left the window is gone. It is stable, not long-memory.
SnapKV against H2O: which keeps a planted fact, and when does the better one fail?
SnapKV, because it scores by the question's own attention; H2O keeps tokens that many queries attended, which a fact read once is not (here: 87% against 0% at 12.5%). SnapKV fails when the cache is compressed before the question exists.
Eviction or quantisation at the same memory?
Quantisation first: it keeps every entry, needs no attention scores, works with fused kernels and paged blocks, and 8 or 4 bits cost little (here int4 kept retrieval and copying; eviction at 25% kept neither copying nor, except question-aware, retrieval). Evict when the context itself must be bounded (streaming), with sinks kept.
Why can't you make a trained full-attention model local/global after the fact?
Its retrieval heads are spread over many layers; making most of them local removes the paths it learned (SmolLM2 with 25 of 30 layers made local: 37% at half the cache). Models with a layout are trained with it (Gemma 2 and 3, gpt-oss); conversion needs continued training.
What is effective context and how do you measure it for your product?
The longest input at which the model still meets your quality threshold on your task. Measure loss by position for the position wall, a retrieval grid over length and depth, then a task without literal overlap (NoLiMa-style) or your own long-document evaluation, at the prompt format you ship.
Design: 128K context, 32 concurrent requests, one 80 GB GPU.
An 8B-class model trained with local/global layers (5:1, window 1,024) or MQA, an fp8 cache (41 sequences fit with the layout and fp8; 3 as Llama 3.1 8B ships), chunked prefill for the 128K prompts, sinks never evicted, and a retrieval acceptance test at 128K. Section 8 has the arithmetic.
Design: a coding agent that reads 1M tokens of repository per task.
Concurrency is low, one sequence's cache and the prefill dominate: a hybrid with constant-state layers in most of the stack plus a few global attention layers (Qwen3-Next's 1 in 4, Nemotron-H's 4 of 52), or trained sparse attention; context parallelism for prefill across GPUs; fp8 cache for the global layers; no question-aware eviction (the question changes every turn); evaluate retrieval and multi-hop use at 1M, and prefer retrieval into a shorter context when the effective length falls short.
