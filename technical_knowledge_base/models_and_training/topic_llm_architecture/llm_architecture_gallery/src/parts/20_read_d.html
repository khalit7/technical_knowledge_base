7. Delta three: whether it is stored at all (linear and state-space layers)

A hybrid replaces most attention layers with a recurrence that folds each token into a fixed-size state: Gated DeltaNet in Qwen3-Next and Qwen3.5 to 3.8, Kimi Delta Attention in Kimi Linear and Kimi K3, Mamba-2 in Nemotron 3 and Granite 4.0-H, lightning attention in Ling, short convolutions in LFM2.5. The state is a lossy summary, so every released hybrid keeps some attention layers for exact recall; the question the configs answer is how many. The mechanisms are on {{Beyond attention|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8142bc9ecac1fc366966}}.

The ablation picked 3 : 1, and so did most of the market

Top, published (vendor): Moonshot's Kimi Linear ablation ({{arXiv 2510.26692|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692}}, Table 1), validation perplexity 5.65 at 3:1 against 5.66 at 1:1, 5.70 at 7:1, 5.82 at 15:1 and 5.77 for the pure full-attention baseline (0:1). Bottom, derived from configs: every gallery hybrid with attention layers at its own linear-to-attention ratio (Nemotron's FFN-only blocks left out); 13 of the 23 sit between 2.9 and 3.1 to 1, the rest from 1.7:1 (LFM2.5) to 7:1 (Ling). Two sources on two panels: one vendor's small-scale ablation is not evidence about the other models.

The state is a cost too. Below some context a hybrid holds more state than cache (the closed table gives, per model, the context at which the fixed state equals the growing cache). How to check: count linear_attention, mamba or M entries; the state per layer is heads × key dim × value dim (heads × head dim × state size for Mamba-2), in FP32. Calling a hybrid "cache-free" is wrong on both counts: Kimi K3's attention layers still grow 27 KiB per token, and its 414 MiB of state is larger than the cache up to about 15,700 tokens.

8. Delta four: how many FFN weights run (experts)

A mixture-of-experts (MoE) layer holds many expert FFNs and a router sends each token to k of them, often plus an always-on shared expert; many models keep their first layers dense. Of the gallery's 2026 models with a config, 52 of 66 are MoE; 39 of those add a shared expert and 31 keep a dense prefix; 8 experts per token is the most common choice (31 models). Routing, balancing and expert parallelism are owned by {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}.

The bigger the model, the smaller the share that runs

Active parameters as a share of total, every MoE model of 2025 and 2026 in the gallery, against total parameters (both from the gallery's lines, gallery, from vendor cards). From 500B parameters up, 21 of 22 models run 2.9 to 6.4% of their weights per token (DeepSeek V4-Pro 3.1%, Kimi K2.5 to K2.7 3.2%, Kimi K3 3.7%; the exception, Nemotron 3 Ultra, 10%); at 35B and below the share is 8 to 21% (Mellum2 Thinking 20.8%).

What it means for serving. All experts must be in memory, so the total, not the active count, sets the hardware; at small batch, decode reads every expert some token picks. The MoE choice and the attention choice are independent: Kimi K3 is extreme on both. How to check: n_routed_experts, num_experts_per_tok, n_shared_experts, and the dense prefix from first_k_dense_replace or a per-layer list.

9. Position, norms and gates

Position. Almost every model rotates queries and keys by their position (RoPE, 61 of the 66 models of 2026), and the deltas are in the details, each readable from the config and visible in the bottom strip: partial RoPE (rotate only some dimensions: 25 models, the Qwen3.5 to 3.8 line and Gemma 4's global layers among them); no position in some layers (5 models in 2026: Kimi K3 and Ember-1 in all 24 attention layers, Solar Open 2, Muse Glimmer, GLM-5.3-Flash; earlier Llama 4 Maverick in 12 of 48 and SmolLM3 in 9 of 36); different bases per layer type (10,000 on local layers against 500,000 to 10,000,000 on global ones in Gemma 4, MiMo, Laguna and Naive); and scaling past the trained length (YaRN factors in 9 of the 2026 configs). Why each works is on {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}; read rope_parameters per layer type.

Norms and gates: on the card, rarely in the config. Pre-norm RMSNorm is close to universal; the 2025 to 2026 deltas are QK-norm (an RMSNorm on queries and keys before the dot product, which bounds attention logits), extra norms after each sublayer (OLMo 2, Gemma's sandwich) and output gates on attention (Qwen3-Next, Trinity, Laguna, Kimi K3's "Gated MLA"). The modeling code does these, not config fields, so this page does not decode them; the gallery's attention line names QK-norm for 19 of the 2026 models, an undercount because it is named only when the card mentions it. The root {{Architecture map (on Topic: llm-architecture)|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8172b490dc9f9ac281df}} records norm placement and QK-norm, sourced, for its 46 lineage models; {{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}} owns why they work.
