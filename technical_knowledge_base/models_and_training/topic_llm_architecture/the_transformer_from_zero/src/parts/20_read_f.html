Depth: mistakes with their real messages, and interview rehearsal

Common mistakes, with the real messages and outputs (nine, each run on the running example)

Each was run on the running example by src/zero/mistakes.py (CPU); silent bugs were applied to the trained model at inference, so the change in loss is the bug's alone. The root's Block lab has four more silent attention bugs (no 1/√d scale 1.96 to 2.88, head split without transpose 3.64, merge without transpose 5.03, no causal mask 3.64): not repeated here.

Loud: the error tells you

Cross-entropy on (B, T, V) logits. F.cross_entropy(logits, targets) with logits (1, 255, 4,096) and targets (1, 255) reads dimension 1 as the classes:

[[err:ce_shape]]

Flatten first: F.cross_entropy(logits.view(-1, V), targets.view(-1)). If the shapes happen to line up (T = V), it runs and trains the wrong thing.

Merging heads with .view after .transpose.

[[err:view_after_transpose]]

Use .reshape(B, T, D) or .contiguous().view(B, T, D) (nanoGPT [[ref:nano_merge]]).

Using an nn.Linear weight as if it were (in, out).

[[err:linear_in_out]]

nn.Linear(256, 1024).weight has shape (1,024, 256); the layer computes x WT. GPT-2's original checkpoints use a Conv1D that stores (in, out), which is why nanoGPT transposes four weight names when loading them.

Reading past the learned position table.

[[err:past_ctx]]

A 300-token input to a model with a 256-row position table. The message names neither positions nor lengths; nanoGPT crops the context to block_size before every generation step ([[ref:nano_crop]]).

Temperature 0 by division.

[[err:temp_zero]]

Logits divided by 0 are plus and minus infinity; the softmax of that is NaN. Temperature 0 means greedy: take the argmax instead.

Silent: it runs, and the loss tells you

[[tbl:silent]]

Leak: how far the output logits at position 150 move when only token 200 changes (0 for a causal model). Zeroing future weights after the softmax looks like masking, but the softmax's denominator already summed every future score, so earlier positions still depend on later tokens, and the rows no longer sum to 1.

Training on unshifted targets: the loss goes to zero and the model learns nothing useful. model(x, targets=x) asks every position for its own token, which it can read straight from its embedding. From scratch (batch 8 × 256, lr 1e-3, CPU), the training loss falls from [[f:us_first|f2]] to [[f:us_last|f3]] in 150 steps, faster than any real run; the held-out loss with correctly shifted targets is [[f:us_val|f2]] nats, worse than a uniform guess (8.32), and the greedy continuation of prompt 7 is "[[f:us_greedy|s]]". Even the first loss is low ([[f:us_first|f2]] against 8.36 for real targets): with tied embeddings the output layer scores each position's own token with its own embedding, so the identity is favoured from the start. A loss that drops this fast is a bug, not a success.

The mirror image is shifting twice. Hugging Face's causal-LM loss shifts the labels itself ([[ref:hf_shift]]); if you also shift them, each position is trained to predict the token after next. Scoring the trained tiny GPT that way gives [[f:ds_t2|f2]] nats on the running sequence against [[f:ds_t1|f2]]: a different task, not a small error.

Counting tied parameters twice. sum(v.numel() for v in model.state_dict().values()) gives [[f:n_state_dict|i]] for a [[f:p_total|i]]-parameter model (section 7). Count model.parameters(), or, for a checkpoint, check whether lm_head.weight and the embedding hold the same data.

Interview rehearsal: nine questions with short answers

Write the shapes through one attention layer for a batch of B sequences of T tokens. x (B, T, D) → q, k, v (B, T, D) → view (B, T, H, hd) → transpose (B, H, T, hd) → scores q kT (B, H, T, T) → mask and softmax over the last axis → weights × v (B, H, T, hd) → transpose (B, T, H, hd) → reshape (B, T, D) → Wo (B, T, D). With GQA, k and v are (B, Hkv, T, hd) and each serves H / Hkv query heads.

Why divide the scores by √dhead, and what breaks without it? A dot product of hd unit-variance terms has variance hd; unscaled, the softmax saturates onto one key and its gradient vanishes. Measured: standard deviation 5.65 at hd = 32, the largest of 256 weights 0.62 on average unscaled against 0.04 scaled; removing it from the trained tiny GPT raises the running sequence's loss 1.96 to 2.88 (root Block lab).

Count the parameters of a GPT-2 style model in your head. 12 L D2 for the blocks plus V D for the tied embedding (plus positions). GPT-2 small: 12 × 12 × 7682 = 84.9M, plus 50,257 × 768 = 38.6M, plus 1,024 × 768 = 0.8M, about 124.3M; the exact count with biases and LayerNorm terms is 124,439,808.

How many FLOPs to train a 7B dense model on 2T tokens? About 6 N Dtokens = 6 × 7e9 × 2e12 = 8.4e22 FLOPs, plus the attention term (12 L D T per token: for 32 layers, D 4,096, T 4,096 that is 6.4 GFLOPs per token, 15% on top of 6N = 42 GFLOPs). With the attention term, about 9.7e22; at 40% of an H100 SXM's 989 TFLOP/s dense bf16 (NVIDIA's datasheet figure, vendor) that is about 2.4e8 GPU-seconds, roughly 68,000 GPU-hours (calculated); the hardware side is {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}.

What does the residual connection buy, measured? Without it, this 6-block model at the same budget ends at [[f:abl_noresid_val|f2]], the unigram level ([[f:unigram|f2]]): it learned token frequencies and nothing else. Each sublayer has to rebuild the whole representation instead of adding a correction, and gradients reach the embedding only through twelve stacked layers (Dong et al. 2021: without skip connections and MLPs, attention's output "converges doubly exponentially to a rank-1 matrix").

Why does a causal decoder still work without any position encoding? The mask itself tells a position how many predecessors it has, so order can be inferred (Haviv et al. 2022). Measured here: no positions [[f:abl_nopos_val|f3]] against [[f:abl_base_val|f3]] at an equal budget. Without the mask (an encoder), no positions means a bag of words.

Why tie the input and output embeddings, and why do large models stop? Tying saves V D parameters, a large share of a small model (18% here, 21% of SmolLM2-135M) and gives the rare tokens' output rows the gradient of their input use. In an 8B model the table is 6.5% of the weights and untying lets input and output spaces differ: Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen3-8B and Mistral 7B are untied, SmolLM2 and Qwen3-0.6B tied (configs).

Your training loss reaches 0.07 in 150 steps on a language model. What do you check first? The targets: they are probably the inputs, not the inputs shifted by one (measured above: 0.067 train, 13.4 held-out). Then the mask (a missing causal mask lets each position read its own next token; the {{Families and objectives|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8105b616c17c1439f766}} page measured a leaky model at 0.21 held-out under the leak and 7.02 under the causal mask).

Design: a 125M-parameter decoder for a 4,096-token context on one 24 GB GPU. What do you choose and why? Start from the 2026 default rather than GPT-2: about 12 layers of D 768 (12 × 12 × 7682 = 85M in blocks) with a 32k to 50k tied vocabulary (25 to 38M); RoPE instead of a learned table (no hard length wall, extendable later); pre-norm RMSNorm with QK-norm for stability; SwiGLU at about 8/3 D; GQA with 4 KV heads to cut the cache 3x for serving. At 4,096 tokens the attention term is 12 L D T = 0.45 GFLOPs per token against 6N = 0.75: use FlashAttention or SDPA (never materialise (B, H, T, T)); bf16 weights and activations with fp32 master weights and AdamW states, 16 bytes a parameter, 2 GB, leave the rest to activations with checkpointing if needed. Then verify like this page: initial loss ln V, a falling curve past the unigram and bigram lines, and one ablation to make sure every component pulls its weight.
