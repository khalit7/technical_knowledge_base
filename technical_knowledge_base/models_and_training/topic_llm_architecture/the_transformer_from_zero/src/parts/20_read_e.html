FLOPs. A matrix multiply of a (1, n) row by an (n, m) matrix is n × m multiply-adds, which hardware counts as 2nm FLOPs (floating-point operations). So one token's forward pass through every weight matrix costs 2 FLOPs per weight it touches: 2 × (L (4D2 + 2DF) + VD) = [[f:per_token_2N|i]] for the tiny GPT (the embedding lookup is free; the tied matrix is used once, as the output layer). Attention adds the part that has no weights: each token's query against T keys and its weights against T values, 2 × 2 × T × D per layer. Over the 256-token running sequence:

Cforward = 2 Nmatmul T + 4 L T2 D = [[f:fl_weights|i]] + [[f:fl_attn|i]] = [[f:fl_total|i]]
measured
PyTorch's FlopCounterMode on the file's explicit attention path: [[f:fl_counter|i]], equal to the formula (the counter sees the full T × T product; a fused causal kernel can skip the masked half, [[f:fl_attn_causal|i]])
backward
about twice the forward (a gradient for the input and one for the weights of every matmul): forward plus backward measured at [[f:fl_ratio|f2]]× the forward

So training costs about 6N FLOPs per token plus the attention term, Kaplan et al.'s rule (2020, {{arXiv 2001.08361|https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361}}, Table 1, which drops the attention term when D > T/12). nanoGPT's MFU estimate is the same accounting in one line: flops_per_token = 6*N + 12*L*H*Q*T ([[ref:nano_mfu]]), where H × Q = D. At T = 256 attention is [[f:attn_share|pct0]] of the tiny GPT's forward; the root's Block lab moves T and shows where attention overtakes the weights (about 28.6k tokens for Llama 3.1 8B in decode).

Predict: Llama 3.1 8B (D 4,096, L 32, H 32, 8 KV heads of 128, F 14,336, V 128,256, untied). How many parameters in one decoder layer?

Attention: D × D for q and o (2 × 16.8M), D × 1,024 for k and v (2 × 4.2M), 41.9M in all; SwiGLU: 3 × 4,096 × 14,336 = 176.2M; norms 8,192. 12D2 assumes full-size K and V and a 4D two-matrix FFN; GQA shrinks attention and SwiGLU at 3.5D grows the FFN (the figure above, second view). Times 32 layers, plus two 525M tables and the final norm, gives 8,030,261,248, the Hub count.
