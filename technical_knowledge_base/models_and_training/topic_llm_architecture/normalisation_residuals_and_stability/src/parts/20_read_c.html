0. In one screen

A transformer trains because every block adds to a residual stream instead of replacing it, and because a norm keeps what each block reads at a fixed size. Everything on this page is a refinement of those two ideas: where the norm sits, which norm, what bounds the softmax logits inside attention and at the output, how small the branches start, and what to do when the loss jumps anyway. Each row below was trained on the running example (the root's tiny GPT on TinyStories, 1,500 steps, the same batches and seed for every run), so the numbers compare like with like; published results at scale sit beside them.

This page is the transformer-specific depth: controlled runs of every placement and fix on one model, the production choices read from code, and the forensics of a spike.

The running example. The root's tiny GPT: 6 blocks, d_model 256, 8 heads of 32, LayerNorm, learned positions, tied embeddings, 5.8M parameters, trained on TinyStories with a 4,096-token BPE; batches of 32 × 256 tokens, AdamW (0.9, 0.95), lr 3e-3 with 100 warmup steps and cosine decay, clipping at 1.0 ({{the root's src/data|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8172b490dc9f9ac281df}}). This page's training loop is the root's train.py with a probe added (src/runs/ns_train.py); it reproduces the Block lab's pre-norm run ({%rerun_ns%} here against {%rerun_bl%}, MPS reruns differ in the third decimal). All runs: measured on Apple M1 Pro (MPS), about 5.5 minutes each.
