7. Massive activations

What they are. In trained LLMs a handful of stream entries, at fixed channels and mostly on the first token, are thousands of times larger than the rest. {{Sun et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17762}} found in LLaMA2-7B a largest activation "about 2,000" (2,622 in their Table 1) "approximately 10,000 times larger than the median magnitude (about 0.2)", appearing "in layer 2" and staying "nearly constant values until layer 30", on the starting token and the first period or newline, in two channels (1415 and 2533). They act as fixed biases: setting them to zero makes perplexity infinite and drops mean zero-shot accuracy from 68.95% to 36.75%, while setting them to their mean value changes almost nothing (their Table 3; published, not run here).

Fig. 5: In SmolLM2-135M one entry of the stream is about 12,900 times the median, from layer 12 to the end. The root's {{Inside a real model lab|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8172b490dc9f9ac281df}} (Apple M1 Pro), the running text: per layer, the median |entry| of the stream (front), its largest |entry| (middle) and the attention weight on token 0 (back, 0 to 1); log scale against the wall, one band per decade, until the last step.

  1. The median entry grows slowly. The median |entry| rises from 0.069 after the embedding to 1.56 at layer 12 and 14.5 after the final norm (the last hidden state is already normalised).
  2. Layers 3 to 10: one channel near 750. From layer 3 the largest entry, about 750, sits in channel 507 of token 4 (the first comma), already hundreds of times the median.
  3. Layer 12: 20,155. Channel 507 of token 0 reaches 20,155 against a median entry of 1.56, about 12,900 times larger; Sun et al. saw about 10,000 times in LLaMA2-7B.
  4. It rides the residual path. It stays near 20,000 to layer 28, falls to 4,047 at layer 29, and the largest entry after the final norm is 633. Under pre-norm nothing normalises the stream in place, so every later layer's input carries it.
  5. The attention sink switches on at the same layer. The weight on token 0 is at most 0.085 before layer 12 and 0.31 to 0.74 after; masking token 0 raises the loss on the running text from 1.563 to 1.648.
  6. In the tiny GPT: none. The root's reference tiny GPT, in front (embedding and 12 sub-layers): largest entry 35.8 against a median of 2.69, 13 times. For a second real model, Qwen3-0.6B's largest entry is 6,160 (channel 35 of token 0, layer 26) against a median of 5.54 (not drawn).
  7. On a linear scale. The same numbers without the log: one tower, every other bar flat. That is the range a low-precision format has to hold while almost every value is near 1.

Why they belong on this page. Under pre-norm the stream is never normalised in place, so a huge entry rides the residual path to the last layer and dominates the RMS of every norm that reads that token. Sun et al. link them to the attention sink ({{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}); how the norm takes part is our reading, not their claim. They set the range low-precision formats must hold (Pre-LN hidden states pass the float16 maximum "as early as 0.5B training tokens", {{Kim et al. 2025, section 7.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02732}}), so quantisation keeps these channels apart ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}).

What removes them (published), and the gate trained on the tiny GPT

Sun et al.: "in GPT-2 with explicit attention biases, massive activations disappear" (section 4.3). Gated attention (an elementwise sigmoid gate on each head's output, Qwen3-Next) cut the largest activation from 1,053 to 94 and first-token attention from 0.467 to 0.048 in Qiu et al.'s 15B MoE (Table 4); the {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}} child trained that gate on the tiny GPT, where there is no sink to remove: largest stream entry {%av_gate_maxres%} with the gate against {%av_mha_maxres%} without, first-token attention at most {%av_gate_first%} against {%av_mha_first%}.

How to check. Per layer, the largest |entry| of the stream with its token and channel, against the median |entry|, on a few real prompts. A ratio in the thousands at a fixed channel on token 0 is a massive activation: normal in a pretrained model, a design input for quantisation, not a bug.
