6. Residual scaling and initialisation

The problem. The stream after L blocks is a sum of 2L branch outputs. If each branch starts with variance v, the sum starts with variance about 2L · v (independent terms add their variances), so a deeper model starts with a larger, noisier stream and with branches whose first updates are large relative to what they are added to. Initialisation is the choice of the weights' random scale before step 0; the basics (Xavier, He, why 0.02 is not variance-preserving on its own) are on the {{ML-fundamentals Normalisation and initialisation page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81369e1cc38ed4e11d48}}. This section is the transformer-specific part.

GPT-2's fix. Scale the weights of the layers that write into the stream (attention's output projection and the FFN's down projection) by 1/√N, N the number of residual layers (Radford et al. 2019, section 2.3). With N = 2L the sum of 2L branches then starts with the variance of one. In practice: N(0, 0.02) everywhere and 0.02/√(2L) for those two matrices; for the tiny GPT (L = 6) that is 0.02/√12 = {%init_small%}. The tiny GPT and transformers' GPT-2 do exactly this:

Embeddings are part of the init. The tiny GPT's token and position tables start at N(0, 0.02) each, so a token enters the stream with a norm of {%rs_pre_init_first%} (0.02 × √(2 × 256) = 0.45, calculated), against blocks whose outputs must be added to it. A tiny embedding makes the first norm's Jacobian large (it divides by the input's size), which is Takase et al.'s argument for scaling it up and the OLMo 2 team's argument for not weight-decaying it (spike score 0.16 to 0.092 without decay on embeddings).

When it matters and how to check. Init is decided once and decides the first thousand steps; its effects grow with depth and width. Check it before training: one forward pass at step 0, the stream's RMS by layer and the gradient norm by layer (the {{Residual stream|#t-resid}} tab is that check on the tiny GPT). A 2× change in the last layer's stream between two inits is worth a short learning-rate sweep on each.
