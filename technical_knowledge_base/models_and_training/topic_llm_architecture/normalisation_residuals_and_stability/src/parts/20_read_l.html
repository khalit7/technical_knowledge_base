Common mistakes

Each one ran here unless marked; the losses are on the 256-token running sequence (the trained reference model scores {%mis_correct%}) or held-out windows, from src/analysis/mistakes.py and the runs above.

Training post-norm at a pre-norm learning rate
No error and a smooth curve: post-norm at lr 3e-3 with 100 warmup steps reaches the unigram level by step 30 and stays there (held-out {%final_bl_post_lr3e-3%}, unigram {%unigram%}), so it looks "converged". It learned token frequencies only. A longer warmup ({%final_bl_post_warm500%} with 500 steps) or a lower rate ({%final_bl_post_lr1e-3%} at 1e-3) fixes it. Always compare the first loss plateau with the unigram cross-entropy of your data.
Leaving out the final norm in a hand-written pre-norm stack
Pre-norm never normalises the stream, so the last block's output reaches the unembedding at its full grown size. Removing the final LayerNorm from the trained model raises the loss from {%mis_correct%} to {%mis_final_norm_dropped%}, yet the greedy continuation is {%mis_final_greedy%}: the logits are scaled, the top token mostly is not, so greedy text hides a broken probability distribution that sampling and the loss expose.
Loading a zero-centred gain into a plain norm (or the reverse)
Gemma 1 to 3 and Qwen3-Next multiply by (1 + w), Llama-style norms by w. Simulated on the trained model by subtracting 1 from every trained gain: loss {%mis_gain_off_by_one%} (worse than the uniform guess, ln 4096 = 8.318) and greedy output "{%mis_gain_greedy%}". No error is raised because the shapes match.
An ε that is not small
With ε = 1.0 instead of 1e-5 in every LayerNorm, rows whose variance is below 1 are no longer normalised to unit size: loss {%mis_eps_1%}, greedy output "{%mis_eps_greedy%}". The subtler version is PyTorch's nn.RMSNorm without eps (section 3).
Dropping QK-norm at inference (a converter that forgets q_norm and k_norm)
{%mis_qk%}
Taking initializer_range in a config as the training init
DeepSeek-V3's config says 0.02; its report says every parameter was initialised with σ = 0.006. The field is what Hugging Face uses to initialise a fresh model of that class, not a record of training (not run here: read from the config and report).
Calling OLMo 2's or Gemma's norm "post-norm"
Both keep the stream unnormalised and normalise the branch output before adding it; the 2017 post-norm normalises the stream itself. The difference is the whole point: the measured init streams differ ({%rs_output_init_last%} for the output norm against {%rs_post_init_last%} for post-norm, section 1).
Switching the loss implementation in the middle of a run
OLMo 2's fork that moved from Flash Attention's fused cross-entropy with z-loss to a PyTorch z-loss saw the z-loss jump about thirty-fold within a few hundred steps, though the forward values matched (published; replayed on the {{OLMo 2 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fb9857fb956165ae1c}}).

Interview rehearsal (optional)

Your 70B run's loss spikes at step 40,000 and recovers. What do you look at, in order?
Whether the gradient norm spiked first (OLMo 2's pattern); the per-layer largest attention logit and entropy in the steps before (logit growth: QK-norm or a lower rate for the rest of the run); mean log Z (z-loss); the batches at the spike, replayed from an earlier checkpoint (PaLM: if replaying does not spike, it is data plus state: roll back about 100 steps and skip 200 to 500 batches); the optimiser's ε and the learning rate actually applied; any non-finite tensor in low precision. Do not change the loss implementation mid-run.
Massive activations: what are they and why do they matter for FP8 training and quantisation?
A few fixed channels, mostly on the first token, thousands of times the median (LLaMA2-7B: about 2,000 against 0.2; SmolLM2: 20,155 against 1.56). They set the dynamic range a format must hold, so per-tensor scaling either clips them (breaking the model: zeroing them makes perplexity infinite) or wastes precision on everything else; outlier-aware quantisation and fine-grained (per-block) scaling exist for this reason ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}} owns them).
Design: the stability recipe for a 120-layer MoE trained in bf16 at an aggressive learning rate.
Pre-norm RMSNorm (statistics in float32) with a final norm; QK-norm per head (or, with MLA, norms on the latents plus qk-clip); z-loss 1e-4; GPT-2-style depth-scaled or small branch init and a large shortcut (do not weight-decay embeddings); warmup of a few thousand steps; global-norm clipping at 1.0 as a backstop; AdamW ε 1e-8 or smaller. Monitor per-layer max logit, entropy, stream RMS and the largest entry, log Z and the gradient norm with a spike score; keep checkpoints every few hundred steps and batch indices for rollback and skip. Sweep the peak rate at small scale first and pick from the flat region, since sensitivity grows with size (Wortsman et al.). Consider the output norm (OLMo 2) or sandwich (Gemma) if the stream grows into the bf16 danger zone.
