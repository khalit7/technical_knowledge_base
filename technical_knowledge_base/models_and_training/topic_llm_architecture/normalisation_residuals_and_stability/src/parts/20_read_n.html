Interview questions

Why did LLMs move from post-norm to pre-norm, and what did it cost?
Pre-norm leaves an identity path from the loss to every block, so gradients do not have to pass through 2L norms; it trains without the long warmup post-norm needs (Xiong et al. 2020). The cost: the stream grows with depth (the tiny GPT's median token norm goes from {%rs_pre_trained_first%} to {%rs_pre_trained_last%}), later blocks add a shrinking fraction to it, and large entries survive to the end, which hurts low precision. Post-norm can be better when it trains: here {%final_bl_post_warm500%} with 500 warmup steps against {%final_bl_pre_lr3e-3%}.
Why is RMSNorm as good as LayerNorm?
Because a trained model's stream rows are nearly mean-zero, so the mean subtraction does almost nothing: in the tiny GPT |μ| / RMS has a median of at most {%mis_mean_over_rms_med%}, and swapping the trained LayerNorms for RMSNorms changes the loss by {%mis_ln_rms_diff%}. RMSNorm drops one reduction and the bias, so it is cheaper.
Derive GPT-2's residual init scale.
The stream after L blocks sums 2L branch outputs. If each starts with variance v and they are roughly independent, the sum has variance 2L · v; to keep it at v, scale each branch's output by 1/√(2L), i.e. initialise the two output projections with std 0.02/√(2L). For L = 6: {%init_small%}.
What does QK-norm bound, and what does it not?
It bounds |q| and |k| by the norm's learned gains, so logits cannot grow through the projection weights or the stream; it does not bound them at √dh, because the gains are learned ({%qk_gain_short%}).
Why can't DeepSeek's MLA use QK-norm, and what do MLA models do instead?
At decode MLA absorbs the key up-projection into the query and attends against the cached latent, so per-head keys never exist to be normalised. DeepSeek-V3 puts RMSNorm on the compressed latents; Kimi K2 clips per-head query and key weights in the optimiser when a head's largest logit passes a threshold (qk-clip).
What is z-loss and why 1e-4?
c · (log Z)2 on the output softmax, pulling the logits toward log-probabilities without changing the argmax; 1e-4 is PaLM's value, small enough not to compete with the cross-entropy. It treats output-logit drift, not attention-logit growth ({%z_short%}).
Your 70B run's loss spikes at step 40,000 and recovers. What do you look at, in order?
Whether the gradient norm spiked first (OLMo 2's pattern); the per-layer largest attention logit and entropy in the steps before (logit growth: QK-norm or a lower rate for the rest of the run); mean log Z (z-loss); the batches at the spike, replayed from an earlier checkpoint (PaLM: if replaying does not spike, it is data plus state: roll back about 100 steps and skip 200 to 500 batches); the optimiser's ε and the learning rate actually applied; any non-finite tensor in low precision. Do not change the loss implementation mid-run.
Massive activations: what are they and why do they matter for FP8 training and quantisation?
A few fixed channels, mostly on the first token, thousands of times the median (LLaMA2-7B: about 2,000 against 0.2; SmolLM2: 20,155 against 1.56). They set the dynamic range a format must hold, so per-tensor scaling either clips them (breaking the model: zeroing them makes perplexity infinite) or wastes precision on everything else; outlier-aware quantisation and fine-grained (per-block) scaling exist for this reason ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}} owns them).
Why does DeepSeek-V4 project its stream-mixing matrices onto doubly stochastic matrices?
With n mixed streams the identity add becomes a product of learned n × n matrices, whose gain compounds over depth (3,000 in DeepSeek's unconstrained 27B run; {%hc_short%} in the random-matrix calculation here). Doubly stochastic matrices are closed under products and can only average, so the gain stays 1.
Design: the stability recipe for a 120-layer MoE trained in bf16 at an aggressive learning rate.
Pre-norm RMSNorm (statistics in float32) with a final norm; QK-norm per head (or, with MLA, norms on the latents plus qk-clip); z-loss 1e-4; GPT-2-style depth-scaled or small branch init and a large shortcut (do not weight-decay embeddings); warmup of a few thousand steps; global-norm clipping at 1.0 as a backstop; AdamW ε 1e-8 or smaller. Monitor per-layer max logit, entropy, stream RMS and the largest entry, log Z and the gradient norm with a spike score; keep checkpoints every few hundred steps and batch indices for rollback and skip. Sweep the peak rate at small scale first and pick from the flat region, since sensitivity grows with size (Wortsman et al.). Consider the output norm (OLMo 2) or sandwich (Gemma) if the stream grows into the bf16 danger zone.
