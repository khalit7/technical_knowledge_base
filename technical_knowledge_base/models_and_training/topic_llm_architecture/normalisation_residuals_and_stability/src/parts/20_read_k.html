8. Loss spikes: reading them and fixing them

What a spike is. A loss spike is a sudden jump of the training loss after a healthy stretch; sometimes it recovers in a few hundred steps, sometimes the run never comes back (divergence). PaLM's 540B run "observed spikes in the loss roughly 20 times during training, despite the fact that gradient clipping was enabled", "not observed when training the smaller models"; restarting "roughly 100 steps before the spike" and skipping "roughly 200-500 data batches" stopped each one, and replaying the same batches from an earlier checkpoint did not spike, so "spikes only occur due to the combination of specific data batches with a particular model parameter state" ({{PaLM, section 5.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311}}). OLMo 2 found gradient-norm spikes to be more frequent than loss spikes and to come first, and measured them with a spike score: the share of values at least seven standard deviations from a rolling average of the last 1,000 (the {{OLMo 2 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fb9857fb956165ae1c}} replays every ablation).

What does not transfer. The tiny GPT shows the mechanisms (logits growing until attention is one-hot, a placement that cannot leave the unigram floor, warmup rescuing it) at a learning rate ten times its default; real runs show them at their own default rates because instability grows with model size, which is Wortsman et al.'s central finding. Absolute thresholds (a logit of 1e4, a spike score of 0.1%) do not transfer; the direction of each fix does.

A run log that makes a spike diagnosable (all cheap, all from the sources above): loss and global gradient norm every step; per-layer largest attention logit, attention entropy and stream RMS on a fixed batch every few hundred steps; mean log Z; the learning rate actually applied; the batch indices of every step, so the batches around a spike can be replayed; checkpoints frequent enough to roll back about 100 steps. The {{When training goes wrong tab of Topic: ml-fundamentals|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d796ccc0a293218c57}} is the general symptom map; this page is the transformer-specific part.
