7. Massive activations

What they are. In trained LLMs a handful of stream entries, at fixed channels and mostly on the first token, are thousands of times larger than the rest. {{Sun et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17762}} found in LLaMA2-7B a largest activation "about 2,000" (2,622 in their Table 1) "approximately 10,000 times larger than the median magnitude (about 0.2)", appearing "in layer 2" and staying "nearly constant values until layer 30", on the starting token and the first period or newline, in two channels (1415 and 2533). They act as fixed biases: setting them to zero makes perplexity infinite and drops mean zero-shot accuracy from 68.95% to 36.75%, while setting them to their mean value changes almost nothing (their Table 3; published, not run here).

Why they matter for this page. Under pre-norm the stream is never normalised in place, so a huge entry, once written, rides the residual path to the last layer; every RMSNorm that reads that token divides by an RMS dominated by one channel. Sun et al. link the massive activations to attention concentrating on those same tokens (the attention sink, which the {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}} child owns); how exactly the norm takes part is our reading, not their claim. They also set the range that low-precision formats must hold: Peri-LN's authors report Pre-LN hidden states passing the float16 maximum "as early as 0.5B training tokens" ({{Kim et al. 2025, section 7.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02732}}), and quantisation must keep these channels apart ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}).

Measured on real models (the root's {{Inside a real model lab|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8172b490dc9f9ac281df}}, Apple M1 Pro): in SmolLM2-135M, channel 507 of token 0 reaches 20,155 at layer 12 against a median entry of 1.56, the same layer where its attention sink switches on (weight on token 0 at most 0.085 before layer 12, 0.31 to 0.74 after); masking token 0 raises the loss on the running text from 1.563 to 1.648. In Qwen3-0.6B the largest entry is 6,160 (channel 35 of token 0, layer 26) against a median of 5.54.

Measured on the tiny GPT: none. {%massive_meas%} The {{Residual stream|#t-resid}} tab's last table lists every trained model of this page. A 6-layer model trained on 12M tokens is far too small and too short for the phenomenon, which the Inside lab saw only in pretrained models; what the tiny GPT does show is where the largest entry lives (token 0 in the middle blocks), a hint of the same first-token specialisation.

What removes them, published. Sun et al.: "in GPT-2 with explicit attention biases, massive activations disappear" (section 4.3). Gated attention (an elementwise sigmoid gate on each head's output, Qwen3-Next) cut the largest activation from 1,053 to 94 and first-token attention from 0.467 to 0.048 in Qiu et al.'s 15B MoE (Table 4); the {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}} child trained that gate on the tiny GPT, where there is no sink to remove: largest stream entry {%av_gate_maxres%} with the gate against {%av_mha_maxres%} without, first-token attention at most {%av_gate_first%} against {%av_mha_first%}.

How to check. For each layer, the largest |entry| of the stream, its token and its channel, against the median |entry|, on a few real prompts (forward hooks, or output_hidden_states=True). A ratio in the thousands at a fixed channel on token 0 is a massive activation; it is normal in a large pretrained model and a design input for quantisation and for low-precision training, not a bug.
