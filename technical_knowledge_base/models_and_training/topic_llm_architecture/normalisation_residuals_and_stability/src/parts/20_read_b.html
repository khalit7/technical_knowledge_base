The idea in one sentence. A deep transformer trains because every block adds to a residual stream instead of replacing it and a norm fixes the size of what each block reads; every other stability tool bounds one more quantity that would otherwise grow: the stream (norm placement, initialisation), the attention logits (QK-norm, soft-capping), the output logits (z-loss), or the step (warmup, clipping).

One block, and the five places a stability fix acts

The stream (thick line) runs from the embedding to the LM head; each sub-layer reads it through a norm and adds its branch back at a +. Highlighted: the controls this page measures, tagged with their section; dashed: norms that only some placements have. Loss spikes (section 8) are what you see when one of them fails.

You need the decoder block ({{The transformer from zero|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8174980ddde4a20c7b3c}}) and the maths of LayerNorm, BatchNorm and init schemes (Xavier, He, μP), which the ML-fundamentals page {{Normalisation and initialisation|n:3c65c17b0d0d81369e1cc38ed4e11d48}} derives; its parent's {{When training goes wrong|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d796ccc0a293218c57}} tab maps training symptoms to causes for any model. This page is the transformer-specific depth.

New terms. Residual stream: the running vector per token that blocks add to. Branch: what one sub-layer adds. Pre-norm / post-norm / sandwich / output norm: the norm before the branch, on the stream after the add, around the branch, after the branch. QK-norm: a norm on each head's query and key. log Z: the log of the output softmax's normaliser; z-loss penalises it. Attention entropy: how spread a head's weights are (0 nats is one-hot). Massive activation: one stream entry thousands of times the median. Spike score: the share of steps far outside a rolling average.

The running example. The root's tiny GPT (6 blocks, d_model 256, 8 heads of 32, LayerNorm, 5.8M parameters) on TinyStories: batches of 32 × 256 tokens, AdamW, lr 3e-3 with 100 warmup steps and cosine decay, clipping at 1.0, 1,500 steps. Every run changes one thing and keeps the same batches and seed (Apple M1 Pro, MPS, about 5.5 minutes each). The unigram floor, a model that ignores context and predicts token frequencies, is {%unigram%} nats.
