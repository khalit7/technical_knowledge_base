Why post-norm needs warmup. {{Xiong et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745}} show that at initialisation post-norm's gradients near the output are large, so a large learning rate early on is unstable and warmup (ramping the learning rate up from near zero) is what makes it train; pre-norm's gradients are "well-behaved at initialization". Widening the stream: hyper-connections and DeepSeek-V4's mHC

The problem. Every placement above keeps one stream of width D per token. {{Hyper-connections (Zhu et al. 2024)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606}} keep n copies of it (n = 4) and learn, per sub-layer, how to collapse them into the branch input, how to spread the branch output back, and an n × n matrix Hres that mixes the streams in place of the plain identity add: xl+1 = Hresl xl + Hpost⊤l F(Hprel xl) ({{mHC paper, Eq. 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880}}). The cost of the freedom is the identity path: after L sub-layers the stream carries the product of L mixing matrices, and DeepSeek report that in a 27B run the composite's largest gain reached "peaks of 3000, a stark divergence from 1", with "an unexpected loss surge around the 12k step" (section 3.1, figures 2 and 3; published by the authors, not run here).

How mHC fixes it. Manifold-constrained hyper-connections project each Hres onto the doubly stochastic matrices (non-negative, every row and every column sums to 1) with 20 iterations of Sinkhorn-Knopp (alternately dividing by row and column sums). A product of doubly stochastic matrices is doubly stochastic, so the composite can only average streams, never amplify them. The authors report a 6.7% time overhead at n = 4 and a final loss 0.021 below the baseline at 27B (sections 1 and 5.2). Small per-layer deviations compound geometrically when nothing constrains them; the projection pins the gain at 1 whatever the noise. These are random matrices, not trained ones, so they show the mechanism, not the paper's 3,000.

When it matters. At 6 layers, pre-norm and a well-warmed post-norm differ by about a tenth of a nat, while the output placements without a logit control lose much more (the table above). Placement matters more when the learning rate is pushed (a larger rate is a shorter run), when depth grows (post-norm's narrow window narrows further), and in low precision, where pre-norm's growing stream is the one that overflows. How to check: sweep the learning rate for three or four values on a short run before committing; the placement with the widest flat region is the robust one (Wortsman et al. call the width "LR sensitivity", section 8).
