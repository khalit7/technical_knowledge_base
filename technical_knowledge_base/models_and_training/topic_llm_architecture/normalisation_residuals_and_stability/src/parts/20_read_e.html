3. LayerNorm or RMSNorm

A norm runs twice per block on every token, in training and at inference. LayerNorm subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation; {{RMSNorm (Zhang and Sennrich 2019)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07467}} drops the mean and divides by the root mean square, on the hypothesis that "re-centering invariance in LayerNorm is dispensable". Every model in section 9 after GPT-2 uses RMSNorm.

A trained model barely uses the mean: |μ| / RMS at every point of the stream

Replacing every trained LayerNorm by an RMSNorm with the same gains, at inference and without retraining, moves the loss on the running sequence by {%mis_ln_rms_diff%} nats ({%mis6_correct%} to {%mis6_layernorm_as_rmsnorm%}), and the greedy continuation is identical: the RMSNorm paper's hypothesis, seen from inside. Trained from scratch (same batches, schedule, lr 3e-3): {%lnrms_meas%}

LN(x) = γ ⊙ (x − μ) / √(σ2 + ε) + β      RMSNorm(x) = γ ⊙ x / √(mean(x2) + ε)
μ, σ2
mean and variance over the D = 256 channels of one token (two reductions)
mean(x2)
one reduction; equals σ2 + μ2, so the two norms agree exactly when μ = 0, which the chart shows is nearly true
γ, β
learned gain and bias per channel; the tiny GPT, like most LLMs, has no β (bias=False)
ε
keeps the division finite for an all-zero row: 1e-5 in Llama and Mistral configs, 1e-6 in Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma, OLMo and Kimi configs (read 2026-10-19)

The cost. The paper reports RMSNorm "reduces the running time by 7%~64% on different models" (published, 2019 GPUs): one fewer reduction and no bias. The saving depends on kernel fusion; on this Mac it does not reproduce (RMSNorm made the whole run {%rms_slow%}% slower, below). Real implementations keep the statistics in float32, and Gemma 1 to 3 and Qwen3-Next multiply by (1 + w) where others use w, so a checkpoint loaded into the other convention is wrong by exactly 1 in every gain (the mistakes at the end).

The code: Llama's RMSNorm (float32 statistics) and Gemma 3's (1 + w) gain

transformers' Llama RMSNorm computes the statistics in float32 whatever the input dtype, then casts back before the gain. Gemma 1 to 3 and Qwen3-Next store the gain as an offset from 1; Gemma 4 went back to a plain w.

PyTorch's nn.RMSNorm has a different default ε (measured)

torch.nn.RMSNorm(dim) without eps uses the machine epsilon of float32, {%eps_fp32%}, not the 1e-5 or 1e-6 of model configs (torch 2.14.1 docstring: "If not specified, uses the machine epsilon of the computation (opmath) type"). On a row of 256 entries all equal to 0.001 (RMS 0.001) the output entry is {%eps_default%} with the default, {%eps_1e5%} with 1e-5 and {%eps_1e6%} with 1e-6. Ordinary rows have an RMS near 1, where the three agree to many digits, so the difference only bites on near-zero rows; but a reimplementation that leaves eps unset does not reproduce the reference model bit for bit. The tiny GPT passes eps=cfg.norm_eps (1e-5) explicitly.
