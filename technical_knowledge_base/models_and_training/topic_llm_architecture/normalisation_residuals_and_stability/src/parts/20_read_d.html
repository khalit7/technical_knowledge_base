Post-norm trains only in a narrow window

{{Xiong et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745}} show that at initialisation post-norm's gradients near the output are large, so a large early learning rate is unstable and warmup (ramping the rate up from near zero) is what makes it train; pre-norm's gradients are "well-behaved at initialization" (the theorem and a toy that reproduces it are on the {{ML-fundamentals Normalisation page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81369e1cc38ed4e11d48}}).

Widening the stream: DeepSeek-V4's mHC

Hyper-connections keep n = 4 copies of the stream and learn an n × n matrix per sub-layer that mixes them in place of the identity add; over depth the stream carries the product of those matrices, whose gain reached "peaks of 3000" in DeepSeek's unconstrained 27B run. mHC projects each matrix onto the doubly stochastic matrices (rows and columns sum to 1), which are closed under products and can only average, so the gain stays 1; DeepSeek-V4 ships it (hc_mult 4) with ordinary pre-norm RMSNorm inside each branch.

mHC in detail: the equations, the published numbers, a random-matrix calculation and the code

Every placement above keeps one stream of width D per token. {{Hyper-connections (Zhu et al. 2024)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19606}} keep n copies (n = 4) and learn, per sub-layer, how to collapse them into the branch input, how to spread the branch output back, and an n × n matrix Hres that mixes the streams in place of the identity add: xl+1 = Hresl xl + Hpost⊤l F(Hprel xl) ({{mHC paper, Eq. 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880}}). The price is the identity path: after L sub-layers the stream carries the product of L mixing matrices. DeepSeek report that in a 27B run the composite's largest gain reached "peaks of 3000, a stark divergence from 1", with "an unexpected loss surge around the 12k step" (section 3.1, figures 2 and 3; published, not run here).

The fix. Manifold-constrained hyper-connections project each Hres onto the doubly stochastic matrices (non-negative, every row and every column sums to 1) with 20 iterations of Sinkhorn-Knopp (alternately dividing by row and column sums). A product of doubly stochastic matrices is doubly stochastic, so the composite can only average streams, never amplify them. The authors report a 6.7% time overhead at n = 4 and a final loss 0.021 below the baseline at 27B (sections 1 and 5.2); DeepSeek-V4's released code does exactly this (config hc_mult 4, hc_sinkhorn_iters 20), with ordinary pre-norm RMSNorm inside each branch.

The arithmetic, on random matrices (calculated, illustrative; src/analysis/hc_toy.py): 120 sub-layers (60 blocks), 4 streams, each mixing matrix the identity plus Gaussian noise of standard deviation σ, 20 seeds. The largest row sum of the product:

The code:

When placement matters, and how to check. At 6 layers pre-norm and a well-warmed post-norm differ by about a tenth of a nat, while the output placements without a logit control lose much more. Placement matters more when the learning rate is pushed, when depth grows (post-norm's window narrows) and in low precision, where pre-norm's growing stream is the one that overflows. Sweep three or four learning rates on a short run; the placement with the widest flat region is the robust one (Wortsman et al.'s "LR sensitivity", section 8).
