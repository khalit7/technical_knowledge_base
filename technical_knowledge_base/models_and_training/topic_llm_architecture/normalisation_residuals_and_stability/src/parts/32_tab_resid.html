Residual stream: one sequence through four placements, before and after training

The residual stream is the running sum each block reads from and adds to. Where the norm sits decides what happens to its size. This lab runs the same 256-token running sequence (the Roxy story, never seen in training) through the tiny GPT under four placements and records the stream's size after the embedding and after every sub-layer (12 sub-layers in 6 blocks): pre-norm x + F(Norm(x)) (GPT-2, Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek), post-norm Norm(x + F(x)) (the 2017 Transformer, BERT), sandwich or peri-norm x + Norm(F(Norm(x))) (Gemma 2 and 3), and output norm x + Norm(F(x)) (OLMo 2). Same seed, same initial weights where the shapes allow, same data; measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU forward of the recorded checkpoints). The root's {{Block lab|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8172b490dc9f9ac281df}} shows pre- against post-norm on one token in more detail; this lab adds the two placements used in 2024 to 2026 models, the initialisation switch, and the largest single entry.

Massive activations are single dimensions of the stream, usually on the first token, thousands of times larger than the median entry (Sun et al. 2024). The table checks whether the tiny GPT grows any.

Scripts: src/analysis/inspect_runs.py (steps each block by hand for its placement and checks the logits equal the model's own forward; largest difference 0 on every run) and src/runs/ns_train.py. Norms are L2 norms over the 256 channels (an RMS of 1 is a norm of 16), as in the Block lab.
