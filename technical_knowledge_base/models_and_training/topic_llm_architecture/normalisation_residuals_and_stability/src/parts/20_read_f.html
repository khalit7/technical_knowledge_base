3. LayerNorm or RMSNorm

The problem. A norm runs twice per block on every token, in training and at inference. LayerNorm subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation; {{RMSNorm (Zhang and Sennrich 2019)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07467}} drops the mean and divides by the root mean square, on the hypothesis that "re-centering invariance in LayerNorm is dispensable".

LN(x) = γ ⊙ (x − μ) / √(σ2 + ε) + β      RMSNorm(x) = γ ⊙ x / √(mean(x2) + ε)
μ, σ2
mean and variance over the D = 256 channels of one token (two reductions)
mean(x2)
one reduction; equals σ2 + μ2, so the two norms agree exactly when μ = 0
γ, β
learned gain and bias per channel; the tiny GPT, like most LLMs, has no β (bias=False)
ε
keeps the division finite for an all-zero row: 1e-5 in Llama and Mistral configs, 1e-6 in Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma, OLMo and Kimi configs (read 2026-10-19)

How much the mean matters in a trained model, measured. In the root's reference tiny GPT (LayerNorm, pre-norm), the mean of a token's stream row is small next to its RMS: |μ| / RMS has a median of at most {%mis_mean_over_rms_med%} at any of the 13 points and a maximum of {%mis_mean_over_rms_max%}. Replacing every trained LayerNorm by an RMSNorm with the same gains, at inference and without retraining, moves the loss on the running sequence by {%mis_ln_rms_diff%} nats ({%mis6_correct%} to {%mis6_layernorm_as_rmsnorm%}), and the greedy continuation is identical. The model barely uses the re-centring, which is the RMSNorm paper's hypothesis, seen from the inside.

{%nb_say%} What transfers: RMSNorm does strictly less arithmetic and one fewer reduction, which is why it is universal; the size of the saving depends on whether the framework fuses the norm into neighbouring kernels, which is why measured savings range so widely. With fused kernels the norms are a small share of a training step; here the unfused RMSNorm made the whole run {%rms_slow%}% slower.

The code real models run. transformers' Llama RMSNorm computes the statistics in float32 whatever the input dtype, then casts back before the gain; Gemma 1 to 3 and Qwen3-Next store the gain as an offset from 1 and multiply by (1 + w), while Gemma 4 went back to a plain w, so a checkpoint loaded into the other convention is wrong by exactly 1 in every gain (section "Mistakes" shows what that does):

PyTorch's nn.RMSNorm has a different default ε (measured)

torch.nn.RMSNorm(dim) without eps uses the machine epsilon of float32, {%eps_fp32%}, not the 1e-5 or 1e-6 of model configs (torch 2.14.1 docstring: "If not specified, uses the machine epsilon of the computation (opmath) type"). On a row of 256 entries all equal to 0.001 (RMS 0.001) the output entry is {%eps_default%} with the default, {%eps_1e5%} with 1e-5 and {%eps_1e6%} with 1e-6. Ordinary rows have an RMS near 1, where the three agree to many digits, so the difference only bites on near-zero rows; but a reimplementation that leaves eps unset does not reproduce the reference model bit for bit. The tiny GPT passes eps=cfg.norm_eps (1e-5) explicitly.
