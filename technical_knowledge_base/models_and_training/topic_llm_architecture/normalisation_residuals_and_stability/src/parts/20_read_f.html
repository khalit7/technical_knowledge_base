4. Attention-logit growth: QK-norm, soft-capping, qk-clip

The problem. An attention logit is the score before the softmax, s = q · k / √dh = |q| |k| cos θ / √dh (dh = 32 in the tiny GPT). The norm before attention fixes the size of x, but q = Wq Norm(x) and k = Wk Norm(x), and nothing stops Wq and Wk from growing. When a few logits get large the softmax goes one-hot: its entropy (about {%uniform_ent%} nats for uniform attention over this sequence) collapses, the gradient to every other key vanishes, and the head stops learning (attention entropy collapse). It broke ViT-22B at about 8B parameters, whose attention logits, without normalisation, "grow to over 50000 in magnitude" ({{Dehghani et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05442}}, appendix B; near float16's largest value, 65,504, which {{Numerical computing|n:3f05c17b0d0d81d6af0be4a9a2edba8d}} explains), and {{Wortsman et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322}} showed it in small models at high learning rates: in their sweeps "all points with attention logits above 1e4 diverged" (section 3.3).

The fix. QK-norm normalises each head's query and key before the dot product, so |q| and |k| are set by the norm's learned gains instead of by the projection weights. In the tiny GPT (RMSNorm per head, before RoPE, as in Qwen3):

Who saw it, in their words (published)

ViT-22B's team saw "divergent training loss after a few thousand steps" at about 8B parameters, "caused by extremely large values in attention logits" that gave "(almost one-hot) attention weights with near-zero entropy" ({{Dehghani et al. 2023, section 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05442}}). {{Zhai et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06296}} name it attention entropy collapse. Wortsman et al. showed it "also appear[s] in small models when training at high learning rates". Entropy here is −Σ p log p over the keys, averaged over the sequence's positions; 0 is one-hot.

Mixed-modal training makes it worse. Chameleon shares every weight between text and image tokens, and "each modality will try to 'compete' with the other by increasing its norms slightly"; with image generation disabled it trained without instability. Its fixes were QK-norm ("essential" at both 7B and 34B) and z-loss, and its lesson was that "monitoring uncontrolled growth of output norms is strongly correlated with predicting future loss divergence" ({{Chameleon, section 2.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09818}}; the whole story is on {{Native multimodal and any-to-any models|n:3c65c17b0d0d8139bd6cd4137dd2a4d8}}, section 6).

QK-norm does not cap the logit at a small number. With unit gains an RMS-normalised head vector has length √dh, so s ≤ √dh = {%sqrt32%} here. But the gains are learned: {%qk_gain_meas%} The ML-fundamentals page measures the same on Qwen3-0.6B (ceilings 184 to 12,897 from its trained gains, real largest logits 12.5 to 48) ({{Normalisation and initialisation|n:3c65c17b0d0d81369e1cc38ed4e11d48}}, section 5, closed block "Measured: does QK-norm cap attention logits in Qwen3-0.6B?"). What QK-norm removes is growth through the projection weights and the stream; the gain vector is small and moves slowly.

Where real models put it, with the code: Qwen3 per head, OLMo 2 over all heads, Llama 4's L2 norm

Qwen3 normalises per head before RoPE; OLMo 2 normalises the whole query projection (all heads at once) before splitting into heads; Llama 4 Scout uses a weightless L2 norm, only in its RoPE layers, and Maverick turns it off (use_qk_norm false in its config; the code comment says "the 128E model does not use qk_norm").

Soft-capping instead

Gemma 2 squashes the logits themselves: s ← c · tanh(s / c), "50.0 for the self-attention layers and 30.0 for the final layer" ({{Gemma 2 report, section 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00118}}): logits well under c pass unchanged and none can exceed c. Gemma 3 "replace[d] the soft-capping of Gemma 2 with QK-norm" ({{Gemma 3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786}}); Gemma 4 keeps only the final-layer cap of 30 (configs read 2026-10-19). {%cap_meas%}

The code: Gemma 2's attention soft-cap and Gemma 4's final-layer cap

Without QK-norm: MLA and qk-clip. Multi-head latent attention never materialises per-head keys at decode (they are absorbed into the query, see {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}), so a per-head key norm has nowhere to go. DeepSeek-V3 instead "employs additional RMSNorm layers after the compressed latent vectors" ({{DeepSeek-V3 report, section 4.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437}}). Kimi K2 keeps the forward pass untouched and, after each optimiser step, rescales a head's query and key weights when that head's largest logit passes a threshold (qk-clip, part of MuonClip); the {{Moonshot AI: Kimi|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f5abf5dbdbac821a12}} page animates it.

How to check. Log the largest attention logit per layer on a fixed batch every few hundred steps (one extra forward with explicit scores; fused kernels never return them). Steady growth past a few hundred with falling entropy predicts trouble before the loss shows it: Wortsman et al. predicted this way that a 4.8B model "will be unstable at LR 1e-2", and it was.
