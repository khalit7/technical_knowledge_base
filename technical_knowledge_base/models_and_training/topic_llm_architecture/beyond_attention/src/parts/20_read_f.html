8. What a fixed state is bad at, and good at

Associative recall is producing a token because it followed the same cue earlier in the context ("Hakuna Matata means no worries Hakuna Matata it means no" → "worries"). {{Zoology (Arora et al. 2023)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927}} found that such tokens are "6.4% of all tokens in the Pile" yet "account for 82% of the perplexity gap to attention" of attention-free models, and that "a 70M parameter attention model outperforms a 1.4 billion parameter gated-convolution model on associative recall". A recurrent model's "required ... hidden state dimensionality grows with the number of key-value pairs that the model needs to recall", attention's does not. For copying, {{Jelassi et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032}} find that "even the smallest transformer (410M parameters) outperforms the largest GSSMs (2.8B parameters) when the phone-book size is long enough (L >= 70)", although "the Mamba models achieve lower perplexity than the Pythia models".

MQAR, BASED and the copying proof (depth)
Zoology's multi-query associative recall (MQAR) task lists key-value pairs and then asks for several keys; the state-dimension quote above is from it. {{BASED (Arora et al. 2024)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668}} turns it into a trade-off curve: "a key tradeoff between a model's state size and recall ability". Mamba-2's own MQAR experiment: going "from N=16 to N=64 and N=256 consistently improves performance". Jelassi et al. prove a two-layer transformer can copy strings of exponential length while a fixed-state model "fails to solve the copy task, unless its latent state grows linearly with the sequence length"; the paper prints the phone-book curves, not the numbers. authors
Predict: Pythia-160M and Mamba-130M are asked to repeat a list of random words. Mamba's state is 24 layers × 1,536 channels × 16 = 589,824 numbers; Pythia's KV cache holds 18,432 numbers per token. At about what list length should Mamba start to fall behind?
Mamba keeps up for short lists and falls behind as the list outgrows what its state can hold (the chart below). The break is gradual, not at the 32-token point where the two memories are equal in size: a state is not a list of slots, and a trained model compresses.

Does not reproduce at this budget
Only SSM-then-attention learned 8 pairs within the budget (99%); two attention layers stalled near 35%, the same plateau as the pure SSMs, and nothing learned 32 or 64 pairs. Zoology and Mamba-2 report attention solving MQAR after far longer training: does not reproduce at this budget (8,000 steps, 1 to 29 minutes per run on MPS); read it as a statement about this budget, not about attention. What the toy does show: a short convolution plus an SSM in front of one attention layer finds the lookup much sooner than attention alone, the same effect as the hybrid name-recall result. Larger state (64 against 16) did not help within this budget. measured on Apple M1 Pro (MPS)

What a fixed state is good at. Constant memory and constant work per generated token, so long generations and many concurrent sequences are cheap (section 10); no position limit inside the recurrence (the tiny SSM GPT has no positions at all); and some state-tracking problems (parity, permutations) that delta-rule and Mamba-3-style layers solve and plain attention does not at fixed depth (RWKV-7, Mamba-3 papers, published). The trade is exact, unbounded lookup, which is why hybrids keep attention.
