0. In one screen

A sequence mixer is the part of a block that lets a token use earlier tokens (attention in a transformer; the feed-forward then works on each token alone). Attention keeps every past key and value (the KV cache) and reads all of it for each new token. The designs on this page keep a fixed-size state per layer instead, updated once per token. What this page measured (Apple M1 Pro, MPS or CPU)
  • At an equal budget (1,500 steps × 8,192 tokens of TinyStories), the attention-free tiny GPT reaches held-out loss against for multi-head attention with RoPE: a tie within seed spread. On the running sequence (a story that repeats "Roxy" 11 times) it is against nats per token: the average hides the recall gap (section 9).
  • Pythia-160M and Mamba-130M (same data, same tokenizer) copy short strings equally well; at words Pythia copies of tokens and Mamba ; in a -entry phone book Pythia finds of numbers and Mamba (section 8, {{Recall probes|#t-recall}}).
  • Where the state stops being cheap: a KV cache overtakes a fixed state only past a crossover context. For Nemotron-H 8B its four attention layers' cache equals its 98.8 MiB Mamba-2 state at tokens; for the tiny SSM GPT the state equals 38 tokens of the baseline's cache ({{State vs KV cache|#t-mem}}).

The one-sentence answer. A fixed state makes memory and decode cost independent of context length and is as good as attention at predicting the next word on average, but it cannot hold an arbitrary amount of text exactly; hybrids keep one attention layer in every 4 to 8 so the model can still look things up, and that is what shipped in 2025 and 2026.
