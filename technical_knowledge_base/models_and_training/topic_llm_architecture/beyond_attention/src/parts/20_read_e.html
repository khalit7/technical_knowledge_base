5. RWKV: an RNN built from attention's pieces

RWKV-4 ({{Peng et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13048}}) computes, per channel, a weighted average of past values whose weights decay with distance: wkvt = (∑i<t e−(t−1−i)w + ki vi + eu + kt vt) / (same sum without v). The vector w is "a channel-wise time decay", u a "bonus" for the current token. It is softmax-like attention whose scores are a key-only term plus a fixed linear distance penalty, which is what lets numerator and denominator be carried as running sums: a normalised linear attention with exponential decay. The RWKV authors trained up to 14B parameters, "by far the largest dense RNN ever trained", and state the cost plainly: the architecture "inherently limits its ability to 'look back' at previous tokens", and "carefully designed prompts may be even more crucial". transformers 5.20.1 ships only RWKV-4; its CPU fallback carries the two running sums plus a running maximum for numerical safety (quoted in section 11).

RWKV-5 and 6 ({{Eagle and Finch, 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05892}}): "multi-headed matrix-valued states" (a dk × dv state per head, as in linear attention) and, in RWKV-6, decay that changes with the input, per channel: "each channel of w_t can now vary independently over time, in a data-dependent manner". RWKV-7 ({{Goose, 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14456}}) moves to "a newly generalized formulation of the delta rule with vector-valued gating and in-context learning rates", and proves it "can perform state tracking and recognize all regular languages" (solving an S5 permutation tracking problem with one layer), which the authors set against transformers' TC0 limit under standard complexity conjectures. Models from 0.19B to 2.9B, Apache-2.0. authors RWKV has stayed a community line: none of the 2026 frontier hybrids below uses it, but its trajectory (decay, then matrix state, then input-dependent decay, then the delta rule) is the same path the others took.

6. Gated DeltaNet and Kimi Delta Attention in production

Gated DeltaNet is section 3's gated delta rule with learned, input-dependent αt (per head) and βt, L2-normalised queries and keys, a short convolution on q, k and v, and an output gate. The authors report at 1.3B parameters and 100B tokens: Wikitext perplexity 16.42 against 16.56 for Mamba-2, 17.71 for DeltaNet and 18.53 for their Transformer++ (Table 3); on recall-heavy tasks with 2K inputs, average 30.6 against 37.0 for the Transformer++ and 39.0 to 40.1 for their hybrids with sliding-window attention (Table 4); on single-needle retrieval at 8K, 91.8 (pass key) where Mamba-2 falls to 30.4 because it "decays historical information too quickly" (Table 2). authors

What ships. Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B (September 2025): "Hybrid Layout: 12 * (3 * (Gated DeltaNet -> MoE) -> 1 * (Gated Attention -> MoE))", Gated DeltaNet heads "32 for V and 16 for QK; Head Dimension: 128". The Qwen blog: "When we mix Gated DeltaNet with standard attention at a 3:1 ratio ... the model consistently outperforms any monolithic architecture". Qwen3.5 keeps the layout from 0.8B ("6 x (3 x (Gated DeltaNet -> FFN) -> 1 x (Gated Attention -> FFN))") to 397B-A17B ("15 * (3 * ...)"), context "262,144 natively". Qwen model cards, read 2026-10-19

Kimi Delta Attention (KDA), {{Kimi Linear 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692}}: "While GDN, similar to Mamba2, employs a coarse head-wise forget gate, KDA introduces a channel-wise variant in which each feature dimension maintains an independent forgetting rate". In code the difference is one broadcast: Qwen3-Next's loop multiplies the state by decay[:, :, i].exp()[..., None, None] (one number per head), Kimi Linear's by g[:, i][..., None].exp() (one number per key channel); the delta step is line for line the same (section 11). Kimi Linear 48B-A3B interleaves KDA and MLA "in a uniform 3:1 ratio", reports "reducing KV cache usage by up to 75% and achieving up to 6x decoding throughput for a 1M context", and uses no positions in its MLA layers: "we apply NoPE to all full attention (MLA) layers. This design delegates the entire responsibility for encoding positional information and recency bias to the KDA layers." Kimi K3's config keeps the pattern at 93 layers (69 KDA, 24 MLA). authors

In transformers, torch_recurrent_gated_delta_rule (Qwen3-Next) and recurrent_kimi_delta_attention are the readable references; with the flash-linear-attention kernels missing, transformers falls back to them, slowly.
