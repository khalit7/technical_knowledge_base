What it shows

At 131,072 tokens one layer reads 2 GiB under MHA (Llama 2 7B), 512 MiB under GQA-8, 144 MiB under MLA, 64 MiB under MQA and 16 MiB with a 4,096-token window. Every grouped-query layer does (query heads / KV heads) FLOPs per byte, 4 or 8 for these models, at every length: memory-bound, so its bytes are its time, and batching does not change that because each sequence has its own cache. Absorbed MLA makes 128 heads reuse one 576-number entry, about 242 FLOPs per byte (260.9 counting the query and output folds): near the H100's balance point, which is why DeepSeek can afford 128 heads. Windowed layers stop growing at their window. DSA's layer reads the indexer keys of every token plus 2,048 entries, so its read keeps growing with the context, at about a quarter of V3's at 131,072 tokens. V4's CSA and HCA layers read the least of the long-context designs.

Real deployments store some caches in FP8 or FP4 (V4 keeps entries in FP8 with the 64 RoPE dimensions in BF16; DeepSeek runs indexers in FP8 or FP4); the bf16 convention keeps the rows comparable.
