The idea in one screen

Attention is blind to order, so position has to be put in. Nearly every open model does it with RoPE: each pair of query and key dimensions is turned by position × its own frequency, so a score depends only on the distance between two tokens. Its slow pairs, whose period is longer than the trained length, are what break past that length, and every context-extension method (PI, NTK, YaRN, Llama 3) is a rule for slowing them down.

Fig. 1, step by step: RoPE is a bank of clocks

  1. A head is a row of dials. The tiny GPT (head 32, base 10,000, trained on 256 tokens) has 16 pairs of dimensions, each a dial turning at its own speed: pairs 0 to 6 in the front row, 7 to 15 behind. After one token pair 0 has turned 1 radian (57°) and pair 15 has barely moved. Under each dial, its wavelength: 6.3 tokens for pair 0 up to 35,333 for pair 15.
  2. Training covers 256 tokens. Shaded is every angle a pair reached in training: pairs 0 to 6 turn at least once within 256 tokens (full disc, blue), so they have seen every angle; pairs 7 to 15 only cover a wedge (green), pair 15 just 2.6 degrees.
  3. Distance 300, just past the trained 256. All nine slow pairs point outside their wedges (orange), at angles no training example produced, while the fast pairs point at angles they have seen many times. The scores those slow pairs feed are untested.
  4. YaRN ×8 slows the slow dials. The tiny GPT's own scaling divides the frequency of pairs 7 to 15 by 8 (÷ above each dial), ramps pairs 1 to 6 between ÷1.14 and ÷4 and keeps pair 0: at distance 300 every hand is back inside the angles it learned.
  5. And at 2,048, eight times the trained length, every hand is still inside. That is all a context-extension method does; section 8 measures what it buys on real text.

Absolute, relative
"where is this token" (added once to the input) against "how far apart are these two" (acting on each attention score, in every layer).
Embedding, encoding
a learned table of position vectors against a fixed function of position. The naming is loose: RoPE is "rotary position embedding" although nothing in it is learned.
Pair, θi, wavelength
RoPE turns dimensions two at a time; pair i turns θi radians per token and completes a turn every λi = 2π/θi tokens.
Base
rope_theta: θi = base−2i/d, so a larger base makes the slow pairs slower.
L, s
the length the model was pretrained at, and the stretch factor s = Lnew / L.
Fast, slow pair
wavelength shorter or longer than L: a fast pair has seen every angle in training, a slow pair only a wedge.
NoPE, partial RoPE
no position signal at all (the causal mask still orders tokens); rotating only some of a head's dimensions.

1. Why attention needs positions at all

A head scores the token at i against every key j with sij = qi · kj / √d, and q, k, v are linear maps of each token's vector, which says nothing about where the token is. Shuffle the inputs and the outputs shuffle the same way: attention is permutation-equivariant, so "Roxy climbed the hill" and "the hill climbed Roxy" are the same bag of vectors. The causal mask leaks some order: one layer still sees a set (shuffle tokens 0 to i − 1 and token i's output is unchanged), but each earlier token saw a different prefix, so from the second layer on the stack can count. A decoder with no positions (NoPE) can learn order; a bidirectional encoder cannot.

Where the model keeps position. Fit a linear probe (ridge regression) from the residual stream after each block to the absolute position 0 to 255, on windows the probe never saw: R2 = 1 means position is written in plainly, 0 that a linear readout cannot find it.

Fig. 2, step by step: five tiny GPTs that differ only in their position scheme

  1. Shuffle the first 255 tokens; NoPE's first block does not notice. Each column is how much token 255's residual stream changes, ‖h − hshuffled‖ / ‖h‖, after the embedding (column 0) and after each of the six blocks, on the running sequence. For NoPE the change after block 1 is 2 × 10−7, rounding error: one layer sees a set.
  2. Its second block does. From block 2 on NoPE's change grows (4% after block 2, 45% after block 6): each earlier token saw a different prefix, so the stack can count.
  3. Every scheme with positions notices at once. Learned and sinusoidal (the run with embeddings × 16) change by 21% and 42% after block 1, because the shuffle moved their position vectors; ALiBi by 39% and RoPE by 5%, because their scores depend on distances.
  4. Now the probe: where position is written. The same columns, now R2 of the linear probe. The learned and sinusoidal schemes write position into the input (0.99 and 0.98 at the embedding); NoPE has none there (−0.04) and builds it in the middle blocks, 0.94 by block 3 and 0.97 by block 4.
  5. RoPE and ALiBi leave little in the stream. Their R2 stays at or below about 0.27 in every block: a relative scheme acts inside attention, on q and k or on the scores, and never needs to write an absolute position anywhere.
