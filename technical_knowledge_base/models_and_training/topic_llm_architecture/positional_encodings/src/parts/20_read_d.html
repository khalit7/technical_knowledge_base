What transfers. The ordering of schemes inside the trained length at this scale is weak evidence about large models: at 12.3M tokens and 5.8M parameters the gaps are a few hundredths of a nat, close to seed noise. What does transfer is the mechanism the next sections measure: what each scheme does once the input is longer than anything it saw, which depends on the scheme's arithmetic and not on scale.

What breaks past the trained length

A model trained on sequences of length L has only seen relative distances below L. For pair i of RoPE that means angles from 0 to Lθi. A fast pair (wavelength shorter than L) has turned through the full circle, often many times, so every angle it meets later is familiar. A slow pair (wavelength longer than L) has only covered a wedge of its circle; the first distance beyond L pushes it into angles that no training example produced, and the attention scores it feeds are untested. Position Interpolation's authors describe the result as "catastrophically high attention scores that completely ruin the self-attention mechanism" ({{Chen et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595}}); ALiBi's authors measured rotary models gaining for only 100 to 200 tokens past their training length ({{Press et al. §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}).

Correction to the old page
It said RoPE fails past L "because high-frequency dims see unseen rotation angles". It is the other way round: the low-frequency pairs, whose wavelength exceeds L, are the ones pushed into unseen angles, which is why YaRN leaves high frequencies alone and interpolates the low ones fully ({{YaRN §3.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}). High frequencies matter for the opposite reason: squeezing them (as Position Interpolation does) blurs neighbouring tokens. On the tiny GPT the slow pairs are pairs 7 to 15.

Stretching RoPE: the context-extension methods

Every method rewrites the frequencies θi (or, equivalently, the positions they multiply) of a model that already uses RoPE, then usually fine-tunes briefly at the new length. With s = Lnew / Ltrain, in historical order:

  • Position Interpolation (PI), Meta, June 2023: divide every position by s, f′(x, m) = f(x, mL/L′), so all angles stay inside the trained range. Its interpolation bound is "at least ∼600× smaller" than extrapolation's (Theorem 2.1); LLaMA 7B to 65B reached 32,768 tokens "within 1000 steps" of fine-tuning, with "minor degradation" on the original 2,048-token window, regressions of up to 2% on standard benchmarks ({{Chen et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595}}). The cost is that every pair is slowed by s, including the fast ones that resolve neighbouring tokens. Gemma 3 uses it: pretraining at 32K, then "a scaling factor of 8" on the global layers for 128K ({{Gemma 3 §5.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786}}). Hugging Face's linear type divides the frequencies by the factor ().
  • NTK-aware scaling, bloc97 on Reddit, June 2023: instead of the positions, raise the base, b′ = b · s|D|/(|D|−2) ({{YaRN Eq. 16|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}), "scaling high frequencies less and low frequencies more". Pair 0 is untouched and the slowest pair is slowed by exactly s: for base 10,000, d = 128 and s = 4, b′ = 40,890; for the tiny GPT (d = 32) at s = 8, b′ = 91,896 (recompute.py). It works surprisingly well with no fine-tuning, but some pairs end up slightly past their trained angles (try it in the animation). Dynamic NTK (emozilla, also on Reddit) sets s = max(1, l′/L) from the current length l′ at every step, so short inputs run unchanged ({{YaRN §3.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}); Hugging Face's dynamic type uses a slightly different base, b · ((s l′/L) − (s − 1))d/(d−2) (). The original posts: {{NTK-aware|https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/14lz7j5/ntkaware_scaled_rope_allows_llama_models_to_have/}}, {{dynamic|https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/14mrgpr/dynamically_scaled_rope_further_increases/}}.
  • YaRN, Peng et al., August 2023: "NTK-by-parts" plus a temperature. With r(i) = L/λi, the number of turns pair i makes within the trained length, pairs with r > β (32 for Llama) are not interpolated, pairs with r < α (1) are divided by s, and a linear ramp blends between; then the logits are scaled by 1/t with √(1/t) = 0.1 ln s + 1 (Eqs. 17, 18, 22). It needed "10x less tokens and 2.5x less training steps than previous methods": Llama 2 7B and 13B were fine-tuned 400 steps at s = 16 (64K) and 200 more at s = 32, which then extrapolated to 128K ({{Peng et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}). DeepSeek-V2, V3 and V4, Qwen3, gpt-oss, Olmo 3, Kimi K2 and Mistral Large 3 ship it in their configs, and SmolLM3 at inference.
  • Llama 3 scaling (the llama3 rope type, July 2024): the same idea with the boundaries set by wavelength. Pairs with a wavelength under L/4 = 2,048 tokens are kept, those over L = 8,192 are divided by 8, and a smooth blend covers the six in between: 29 kept, 6 blended, 29 divided in Llama 3.1 8B (recompute.py, from its {{config|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct/blob/main/config.json}} and ). The Llama 3 paper itself does not describe this scaling; it describes the long-context stage (below).
  • LongRoPE, Microsoft, February 2024: one rescaling factor per pair, found by evolutionary search, which "extends the context window of pre-trained LLMs to an impressive 2048k tokens, with up to only 1k fine-tuning steps at within 256k training lengths", progressively, then readjusted at 8K to recover short-context quality; shown on LLaMA2 and Mistral ({{Ding et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13753}}). Phi-3-mini's 128K version uses it ("a long context version via LongRope", {{Phi-3 report §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14219}}): its config carries 48 "long" factors from 1.07 to 64.76 and 48 "short" ones (1.10 to 3.05); Hugging Face picks the long set only when the sequence is longer than the original 4,096 (). LongRoPE2 (February 2025) blames the remaining failures on "insufficient training in higher RoPE dimensions", searches the factors with a "needle-driven" perplexity, and adds "mixed context window training" that keeps the original RoPE for short sequences; it extends LLaMA3-8B to a 128K effective length "while retaining over 98.5% of short-context performance, using only 10B tokens", 80 times fewer than Meta's own extension ({{Shang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20082}}).

Recipes in production


No current model trains its whole run at its final window. The pattern: pretrain short with a base chosen for the long target, extend in a short continued-pretraining stage on long documents with the positions rescaled, and check that short-context quality recovers. The training side of this is on the {{Continued Pretraining (CPT)|n:3c65c17b0d0d8119b8f0e801caa09474}} and {{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}} pages; whether a model then actually uses its window is the {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}} sibling's subject.
