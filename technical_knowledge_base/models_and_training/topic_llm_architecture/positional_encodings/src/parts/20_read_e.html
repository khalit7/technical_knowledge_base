Beyond RoPE

  • NoPE. A decoder-only model with no positional encoding still learns order from the causal mask (measured above: a linear probe finds position in the NoPE model's middle blocks). Kazemnejad et al. found NoPE "outperforms other explicit positional encoding methods" on their length-generalisation tasks, and showed it "can represent both absolute and relative PEs, but when trained with SGD, it mostly resembles T5's relative PE attention patterns" ({{NeurIPS 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466}}). At scale, NoPE alone "lags behind" RoPE on standard benchmarks, although in needle tests it put the most attention on the needle ({{Yang et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18795}}). Hence the hybrids in the variants table: Cohere's RNoPE-SWA (RoPE in sliding-window layers, NoPE in one full-attention layer of four) reached 256K with "almost no loss" on needle tests and trains "nearly 2x faster at 128k", shipped in Command A; Llama 4 adds a position-dependent temperature on its NoPE layers ("inference time temperature scaling", from {{Scalable-Softmax|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19399}}); SmolLM3 found that removing RoPE from every 4th layer "improves long context performance without affecting short context capabilities". In 2026 hybrids (Kimi K3, Nemotron 3 Super, Granite 4.0-H) the attention layers carry no positions at all, because the recurrent layers between them already see the tokens in order.
  • CoPE (Contextual Position Encoding, Meta, 2024). Positions are counted, not indexed: each key gets a gate gij = σ(qi·kj), the position of key j from query i is the sum of the gates between them, and fractional positions interpolate between learned integer embeddings (§4). A head can therefore measure "the i-th sentence" or "the i-th verb" instead of tokens. It solves the selective copy, counting and Flip-Flop tasks where standard encodings fail and improves perplexity on language modelling and code ({{Golovneva et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18719}}); not used at scale yet. Not run here: it needs a trained model and a counting task to mean anything.
  • Fixing the spectrum. {{FoPE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17739}} (Fourier Position Embedding, ICML 2025) treats each dimension as a Fourier series and "zero-outs the destructive frequency components", those insufficiently trained because of truncation. {{HoPE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21216}} (2024) argues "long-term decay is outdated" and keeps "only high-frequency signals", replacing the others with position-independent components, which is close to what Gemma 4's proportional RoPE ships. {{Wavelet-based positions|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02004}} (ICLR 2025) capture several window sizes at once "without limiting the model's attention field". And {{Barbero et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06205}} found that Gemma uses RoPE's highest frequencies for positional attention patterns and "greatly prefers to use the lowest frequencies", which they suspect carry semantic information; this is one argument for partial RoPE.
  • Long context is not only positions. Sliding windows with a few global layers, chunked attention, KV compression and sparse attention (DeepSeek's NSA and DSA) decide what a long context costs to serve and whether the model uses it; that side lives on {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}} and {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}, with the benchmarks on {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}.

Common mistakes, with real outputs

Running a learned-position model past its table
It fails loudly, which is the good case: The fix is a scheme without a table, or retraining the table at the new length; there is nothing to stretch.
Adding sinusoids to small embeddings
Loading RoPE weights with the other pairing layout
Copying another model's rope_theta into a checkpoint's config
Setting original_max_position_embeddings to the new length
Turning on YaRN for every request
"RoPE breaks past L because the high-frequency dimensions see new angles"
The low-frequency pairs do: fast pairs have seen every angle. On the tiny GPT the culprits are pairs 7 to 15, whose wavelengths (353 to 35,333 tokens) exceed the 256 it was trained on. This was the old version of this page.
"The config has rope_theta, so the model uses RoPE"
Nemotron 3 Super's config says rope_theta 10000; transformers 's modeling_nemotron_h.py defines apply_rotary_pos_emb and calls it nowhere (counted by src/code/code_refs.py: ). Read the modeling code of hybrids before quoting their position scheme.
"NTK scaling means a base like 500,000"
NTK-aware scaling for s = 4 at d = 128 takes 10,000 to 40,890. 500,000 is Llama 3's pretraining base, chosen before training (ABF, Xiong et al.), not a scaling applied to a trained model.
"factor × original length = the context window"
Llama 3.1's 8 × 8,192 = 65,536 is half its configured 131,072; the model was trained at 128K in its long-context stage, so the rescaled spectrum does not have to cover the window on its own. Gemma 3's 8 × 32,768 = 262,144 is twice its 131,072. DeepSeek-V3's 40 × 4,096 = 163,840, Phi-3's 32 × 4,096 = 131,072, DeepSeek-V4-Pro's 16 × 65,536 = 1,048,576 and Mistral Large 3's 36 × 8,192 = 294,912 do match their configs' max_position_embeddings.
"RoPE extrapolates because it is relative"
Relative does not mean unbounded: the score at distance 10,000 uses angles a model trained at 4,096 never saw. Rotary models gained only 100 to 200 tokens past their training length in ALiBi's measurement, and the tiny GPT's RoPE loss rises from the first band past 256 (above); ALiBi, whose penalty is linear and has nothing to go out of range, is the relative method that extrapolates.
"LongRoPE is behind Phi-3's 2M-token context"
The 2,048K result is the LongRoPE paper's, on LLaMA2 and Mistral. Phi-3-mini uses LongRoPE for 128K.
"NoPE layers are worse at retrieval"
The old version of this page said so. In Yang et al.'s needle tests the NoPE variant put the most attention on the needle; its weakness was on standard benchmarks, which is why it is used in one layer of four rather than everywhere.

Interview questions

Why does a transformer need positional information, and does a decoder without any still know order?
Attention is permutation-equivariant: scores are dot products of per-token vectors. A causal decoder still leaks order through the mask (each token sees a different prefix), so from the second layer on it can count; measured above, NoPE's first block is exactly order-blind and later blocks are not. An encoder with full attention and no positions is a bag of words.
Derive why RoPE gives relative scores.
Rotate q by mθ and k by nθ: (R(mθ)q)·(R(nθ)k) = qTR(mθ)TR(nθ)k = qTR((n−m)θ)k, because rotations are orthogonal and compose by adding angles. Per pair of dimensions, with θi = base−2i/d.
Why are keys cached after rotation, and what does that break for MLA?
The rotation depends only on the key's own position, so it can be applied once. MLA wants to absorb the key up-projection into the query and cache only a latent; a position-dependent rotation between them blocks the absorption, so DeepSeek carries position in a separate 64-dimension RoPE key shared by all heads.
A model trained at 4K is run at 32K. Which RoPE dimensions misbehave and why?
The slow pairs, whose wavelength exceeds 4K: they only saw a wedge of their circle and now meet new angles. Fast pairs have seen every angle. Fixes interpolate the slow pairs (PI, NTK, YaRN, Llama 3), ideally leaving the fast ones alone.
PI against NTK-aware against YaRN in one sentence each.
PI divides every frequency by s (safe angles, blurred local detail); NTK raises the base so the slowest pair is divided by s and the fastest by 1 (good zero-shot, a few pairs slightly out of range); YaRN keeps pairs that turn more than β times, divides those that turn less than α times, ramps between, and adds a logit temperature (best per fine-tuning token).
What does YaRN's temperature do, and how big is it at s = 32?
It sharpens the softmax, which flattens when attention spreads over more tokens: √(1/t) = 0.1 ln s + 1, so logits × (0.1 ln 32 + 1)2 = 1.813. Hugging Face folds it into cos and sin, so the factor applies once to q and once to k.
Read this config: base 10,000, qk_rope_head_dim 64, YaRN factor 40, original 4,096, beta_fast 32, beta_slow 1. What is it and what happens?
DeepSeek-V3's decoupled RoPE key: 32 pairs, of which 11 are kept, 12 on the ramp and 9 fully interpolated, for a 163,840-token ceiling (recompute.py). Only the 64 rotated dimensions are stretched; the 128 latent-derived dimensions per head carry no position.
Design: you must ship a 128K model, have budget to pretrain at 8K, and must not lose short-context quality. What position recipe?
RoPE with a base chosen for the long target at pretraining (ABF: 500,000 or more at 8K), optionally NoPE or a fast-pair-only RoPE in a few global layers; then a continued-pretraining stage on long documents at increasing lengths with YaRN or Llama 3 scaling (a few billion to hundreds of billions of tokens), mixed with short data; ship the scaling in the config, prefer dynamic scaling at inference so short prompts run unscaled, and verify with needle and multi-hop long-context evaluations plus the short-context suite at every stage.
Design: an agent must handle 1M-token contexts on a hybrid. Where do positions go?
Let the linear or SSM layers carry order (they are recurrent), keep the few full-attention layers position-free or fast-pairs-only so nothing goes out of range at 1M (Kimi K3, Nemotron 3 Super, Gemma 4's global layers), and spend the remaining effort on what long context really costs: KV memory and effective use, owned by the {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}} page.
How would you check that RoPE scaling is configured correctly on a checkpoint you were handed?
Print the resolved rope parameters (transformers 5 standardises them into config.rope_parameters), compare original_max_position_embeddings with the paper's pretraining length, compute the inverse frequencies with the library's function and with your own, and measure loss per position band on long held-out documents with and without the scaling: a correct setup costs a little below the original length and saves a lot beyond it (the tiny GPT's table above).
