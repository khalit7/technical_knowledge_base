2. Four answers: add a vector, bias the score, rotate, or nothing

An absolute scheme adds a position vector pm to the token's embedding once, xm = e(tokenm) + pm, and position must survive in the residual stream. A relative bias adds a number that depends on the distance to every attention score, in every layer. RoPE rotates queries and keys so the dot product itself depends on the distance. NoPE adds nothing. The chart puts all of them on one query: one token's content vector (64 dimensions, seeded random, illustrative) repeated at every position, so content adds the same to every score and only position differs.

Only the absolute schemes change when the same pair of tokens moves along the sequence

Predict, then try: which curves move when you shift the pair along? Only the absolute ones (their unshifted curve stays faint): the same two tokens at the same distance get a different score at a different place. Switch to Attention weights: NoPE spreads attention evenly over everything the mask allows, ALiBi decays exponentially (head 1 looks a few tokens back, head 8 hundreds), T5 is flat from 113 tokens on, RoPE with base 10,000 decays and ripples. At 0 to 2,048 the learned table stops at 512: there is no row 512.

Absolute: a learned table, or fixed sinusoids

A learned table (GPT-2 has 1,024 rows, BERT 512) costs Lmax × d parameters (65,536 in the tiny GPT, 786,432 in GPT-2) and a hard cap. Ask the learned-position tiny GPT for position 300 and it fails loudly, as Hugging Face's GPT-2 does:

position, frequency index (0 to d/2 − 1) and model width; each pair of dimensions is a clock, from one radian per token down to 1/10,000
sin and cos together
for any offset k, PE(m + k) is a fixed rotation of PE(m), so PE(m) · PE(n) depends only on m − n; the cross terms with content do not, which is why the scheme stays absolute

The 2017 sinusoids were chosen because they "may allow the model to extrapolate to sequence lengths longer than the ones encountered during training" ({{Vaswani et al. §3.5|https://arxiv.org/html/1706.03762v7#S3.SS5}}); learned embeddings gave "nearly identical results" (Table 3, row E). In practice they do not extrapolate: sinusoidal perplexity stopped improving about 20 tokens past a 512-token training length and degraded from about 50 ({{Press et al. 2021 §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}). On the tiny GPT, Both kinds also mix position and content in one vector from the first layer, which DeBERTa argued against (closed block below). The {{Attention Is All You Need page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81999af7f16f8ed8ee9e}} draws the sinusoid heatmap and retrains a toy without positions.

Relative biases: T5 and ALiBi

T5 (2019) adds one learned scalar per distance bucket to the logit: 32 buckets per head, shared across layers. Exact offsets first, then logarithmically wider buckets, with 113 onwards in the last one. One layer is blind beyond 128, though stacked layers pass information further. It extrapolated best of the pre-ALiBi baselines (600 to 800 extra tokens), at the price of training at least twice as slowly ({{ALiBi §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}); details on the {{T5 page|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}.

ALiBi (2021) adds a fixed penalty −m · distance to each score, a different slope per head: 1/2, 1/4, ..., 1/256 for 8 heads ({{Press et al. §3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}). A 1.3B model trained on 1,024 tokens "extrapolates to input sequences of length 2048, achieving the same perplexity as a sinusoidal position embedding model trained on inputs of length 2048 but training 11% faster and using 11% less memory". Its cost is that every head is forced to prefer nearby tokens, so a head that should retrieve something far back fights a penalty that grows with distance, and FlashAttention needed an explicit bias path for it. Used by {{BLOOM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100}} and MPT; every open family since 2023 in the configs read here uses RoPE (or, in hybrids, no positions) instead. Measured here:

Shaw, DeBERTa, MPT's slopes, the T5 outlier bucket and the code (depth)

Shaw, Uszkoreit and Vaswani (2018) added a learnable vector for each relative distance, clipped at a maximum k, to the keys when scores are formed and optionally to the values. On WMT 2014 it beat absolute positions by 1.3 BLEU (English to German) and 0.3 BLEU (English to French), and "combining relative and absolute position representations yields no further improvement" ({{Shaw et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02155}}). T5 simplified it to one scalar per bucket.

DeBERTa's disentangled attention (2020): "each word is represented using two vectors that encode its content and position, respectively, and the attention weights among words are computed using disentangled matrices on their contents and relative positions" ({{He et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03654}}): content-to-content, content-to-position and position-to-content terms. Absolute positions enter only in an "enhanced mask decoder" near the output. Not run here: it is an encoder design, and no decoder config read on this page uses it.

ALiBi's slopes beyond 8 heads. For 16 heads, the geometric sequence from 1/√2 with ratio 1/√2. MPT-7B's config generalises this as 2−8h/n over its 32 heads (alibi_bias_max 8: slopes from 0.84 to 1/256).

T5's buckets. In the decoder, offsets 0 to 15 get a bucket each, then 16 to 18, 19 to 20 and so on, growing logarithmically, with 113 onwards in bucket 31 (recomputed with T5's {{bucket function|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.20.1/src/transformers/models/t5/modeling_t5.py}}). The T5-base decoder's last bucket, offsets 113 and beyond (the dotted line in the chart), holds values from −40.8 to 32.8 across heads where every other bucket stays between −22 and 5.4; the chart clips them. Span corruption makes a 104-token target per 512 tokens of text ({{T5 page|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}), shorter than 113, so the denoising objective alone never reaches that bucket; the released checkpoints were also trained on supervised tasks with longer targets, so why its values sit so far out is unconfirmed.

3. RoPE from zero: position as a rotation


We want a score that depends on how far apart two tokens are and not where the pair sits, with no parameters, without touching the values, and with keys that can still be computed once and cached. Rotary position embedding ({{RoFormer|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cfa5e9f54459720098}}, Su et al. 2021) gets all four by rotating each query and key instead of adding a vector.


One pair, real numbers. Take θ = 0.5 radians per token and q = k = (1, 0). At m = 3 and n = 5 the query is turned by 1.5 radians to (0.0707, 0.9975) and the key by 2.5 radians to (−0.8011, 0.5985); their dot product is 0.0707 × (−0.8011) + 0.9975 × 0.5985 = 0.5403 = cos(1.0). Move both to m = 10, n = 12: the angles become 5 and 6 radians, the gap is still 1 radian, and the dot product is again 0.5403.




Shift both tokens: both vectors turn, the angle between them does not








Defaults are the worked example (θ = 0.5, m = 3, n = 5): slide the shift and q · k stays 0.5403; change the offset and it follows cos((n − m)θ). θ = 0.5 is illustrative; real pairs run from 1 radian per token down to millionths.




(R(mθ) q) · (R(nθ) k) = qT R(mθ)T R(nθ) k = qT R((n − m)θ) k


why
a rotation's transpose is its inverse, and two rotations compose by adding their angles: R(α)T R(β) = R(β − α)
in complex numbers
read the pair as q0 + iq1; rotating is multiplying by eimθ, and Re[(q eimθ)(k einθ)*] = Re[q k* ei(m−n)θ]


Each vector is rotated by its own absolute position, so a key is rotated once and stored rotated in the KV cache; the offset appears only inside the dot product. Rotations keep lengths, and values are not rotated: position shapes where a head looks, not what it reads. RoPE is absolute in form and relative in effect.


A whole head is a bank of clocks. Split each query and key of a head (dimension d) into d/2 pairs and give pair i its own speed. In the tiny GPT (head 32, base 10,000) there are 16 pairs: pair 0 turns once every 6.3 tokens, pair 6 once every 199, pair 7 once every 353 and pair 15 once every 35,333. Within the 256-token training window pairs 0 to 6 go round at least once, so the model has seen every angle they can take; pairs 7 to 15 only covered a wedge, pair 15 just 2.6 degrees. Fast pairs resolve neighbouring tokens, slow pairs measure long distances, and that split, 7 fast and 9 slow, is the hero figure and the whole story of section 6. At Llama 3's shape (d = 128, base 500,000) the spectrum runs from pair 0 at 1 radian per token (a turn every 6.3 tokens) to pair 63 at 2.46 × 10−6 radians per token (a turn every 2.56 million tokens).

Cost and the one check. No parameters; about 3 operations per element of q and k per layer and token, 6 × 256 = 1,536 in the tiny GPT against about 1.57 million for the layer's matrix products, 0.1% (calculation); on datacenter GPUs it is fused into the attention or projection kernels. What matters is that every RoPE layer uses the convention the weights were trained with: the base, the rotated dimensions and the pairing layout. Getting any of them wrong runs without an error and degrades the text (measured in Depth below); check by comparing the loss on a few hundred tokens with a reference.
