Absolute positions: a learned table, or fixed sinusoids

The idea. Give every position its own vector pm and add it to the token's embedding before the first block: xm = e(tokenm) + pm. Everything after that is ordinary attention; position has to survive inside the residual stream. Two distinctions organise the whole field.

  • Learned or fixed. A positional embedding in the strict sense is a learned table: row m is a trained vector (GPT-2 has 1,024 rows, BERT 512). Simple, but there is nothing to look up past the last row. A positional encoding is a fixed function of position. The 2017 Transformer's sinusoids were chosen because they "may allow the model to extrapolate to sequence lengths longer than the ones encountered during training" ({{Vaswani et al. §3.5|https://arxiv.org/html/1706.03762v7#S3.SS5}}); learned embeddings gave "nearly identical results" there (Table 3, row E). The naming is loose in the literature: RoPE is "rotary position embedding" although nothing in it is learned.
  • Absolute or relative. Absolute methods answer "where is this token?" and are added once. Relative methods answer "how far apart are these two tokens?" and act inside attention, at every layer, on the score between a query and a key, which is what attention uses. A relative method gives the same score to the same pair of tokens wherever the pair sits.
PE(m, 2i) = sin(m / 100002i/d),   PE(m, 2i+1) = cos(m / 100002i/d)
m, i, d
position, frequency index (0 to d/2 − 1) and model width; each pair of dimensions is a clock running at its own speed, from one radian per token down to 1/10,000
why sin and cos together
for any offset k, PE(m + k) is a fixed rotation of PE(m), so PE(m) · PE(n) depends only on m − n; the cross terms with the token's own content do not, which is why the scheme stays absolute

What it costs. The learned table costs Lmax × d parameters (65,536 in the tiny GPT, 786,432 in GPT-2) and a hard cap. The sinusoids cost nothing. Both cost something less visible: position and content share the same vector from the first layer, which DeBERTa (below) argued against. The relative family


    

  • Shaw, Uszkoreit and Vaswani (2018). Add a learnable vector for each relative distance, clipped at a maximum k, directly into the attention computation: to the keys when scores are formed, and optionally to the values when they are summed. On WMT 2014 translation it beat absolute positions by 1.3 BLEU (English to German) and 0.3 BLEU (English to French), and "combining relative and absolute position representations yields no further improvement" ({{Shaw et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02155}}).
    • 

  • T5's relative bias (2019). Simplify Shaw to one learned scalar per distance bucket, added to the logit. There are 32 buckets per head, shared across layers: the first half are exact offsets, the rest grow logarithmically up to an offset of 128, and everything further shares the last bucket. In the decoder, which looks back only, offsets 0 to 15 get a bucket each, then 16 to 18, 19 to 20, and so on, with 113 onwards in bucket 31 (recomputed with T5's {{bucket function|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.20.1/src/transformers/models/t5/modeling_t5.py}}). Cheap, per head, learned; one layer is blind beyond 128, though stacked layers can pass information further. Its extrapolation was the best of the pre-ALiBi baselines (600 to 800 extra tokens), at the price of training at least twice as slowly ({{ALiBi §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}). Details on the {{T5 page|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}.
    • 

  • ALiBi (2021). No embeddings at all: add a fixed, non-learned penalty −m · distance to each attention score, with a different slope m per head. For 8 heads the slopes are 1/2, 1/4, ..., 1/256; for 16, the geometric sequence from 1/√2 with ratio 1/√2 ({{Press et al. §3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}). MPT-7B's config generalises this as 2−8h/n over its 32 heads (alibi_bias_max 8: slopes from 0.84 to 1/256). Closer tokens score higher, steeply in some heads and gently in others. Its strength is extrapolation: a 1.3B model trained on 1,024 tokens "extrapolates to input sequences of length 2048, achieving the same perplexity as a sinusoidal position embedding model trained on inputs of length 2048 but training 11% faster and using 11% less memory". Used by {{BLOOM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100}} and MPT; every open model family since 2023 in the configs read here uses RoPE (or, in hybrids, no positions) instead, with scaling for long contexts. Its cost is that every head is forced to prefer nearby tokens; a head that should retrieve something far back has to overcome a penalty that grows with distance, and FlashAttention needed an explicit bias path for it.   • DeBERTa's disentangled attention (2020). Summing content and position into one vector mixes the two from the first layer. DeBERTa keeps them apart: "each word is represented using two vectors that encode its content and position, respectively, and the attention weights among words are computed using disentangled matrices on their contents and relative positions" ({{He et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03654}}): content-to-content, content-to-position and position-to-content terms. Absolute positions enter only in an "enhanced mask decoder" near the output, for masked-token prediction. Not run here: it is an encoder design, and none of the decoder configs read on this page uses it.
