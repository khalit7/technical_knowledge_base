2. One token through one block

A block does two things to the residual stream x of every position, each wrapped the same way: normalise a copy, transform it, add the result back. In the pre-norm layout every current model uses (section 7):

h = x + Attention(Norm(x))
x′ = h + FFN(Norm(h))
x, h, x′
the residual stream before, between and after the two sublayers: one vector of d numbers per position
Attention
the only step where a position reads other positions (section 3)
FFN
the feed-forward network: the same small MLP applied to each position on its own (section 6)

Because each sublayer only adds to x, information written by layer 3 is still there, unchanged, for layer 30 to read, and gradients flow back along the same identity path. That is why a stack of 80 or 126 such blocks trains at all. Predict: in one Llama 3.1 8B block, what share of the weights sits in the feed-forward network rather than in attention?
derived 176.2M of 218.1M, 81%. The classic block (full multi-head attention, FFN width 4d) has 4d² in attention and 8d² in the FFN, two thirds; GQA shrinks K and V by 4 times and the FFN here is 3.5d wide with three matrices, which pushes the FFN share up. That is why mixture of experts (section 6) targets the FFN: it is where the parameters are.

Each weight is used once per token in one multiply and one add, so a forward pass costs about 2 FLOPs per weight per token; the backward pass costs about twice the forward, giving the 6N FLOPs per training token rule ({{Kaplan et al. 2020, section 2.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361}}). Attention adds a term that grows with how many tokens are attended to (section 8).
