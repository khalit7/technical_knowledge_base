2. Tokens in, a distribution out

A tokenizer cuts text into integer ids from a vocabulary of size V (Llama 3.1: V = 128,256, {{config|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/raw/main/config.json}}). Each id picks a row of a V × d embedding table; the blocks transform that vector; a final norm and a d × V unembedding give one score (logit) per vocabulary entry, and a softmax turns the logits into next-token probabilities. In training every position predicts its own next token at once, and a causal mask stops it seeing later ones, so one pass over a 4,096-token sequence gives 4,096 training examples; in generation the model predicts one token, appends it and runs again (picking the token is {{Sampling and decoding|n:3c65c17b0d0d8115bafcda29a2c7086e}}).

The distinction that crosses pages: vocabulary size. A bigger vocabulary needs fewer tokens per sentence, so attention and the KV cache (sections 4 and 9) are cheaper per sentence, but the two V × d tables grow and each token is rarer in training. Tied embeddings use one matrix for both ends ({{Press and Wolf 2016|https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05859}}), which small models do because the table is a large share of them: in a 135M-parameter model with a 49,152-token vocabulary at d = 576 it is 28M parameters, a fifth of the model, so count it once when tie_word_embeddings is true. Llama 3.1 8B does not tie ("tie_word_embeddings": false); its two tables are 1.05 billion of its 8.03 billion parameters (derived as 2 × 128,256 × 4,096).

3. The block and its residual stream

A block does two things to the residual stream x of every position, each wrapped the same way: normalise a copy, transform it, add the result back. In the pre-norm layout every current model uses (section 7):

h = x + Attention(Norm(x))
x′ = h + FFN(Norm(h))
x, h, x′
the residual stream before, between and after the two sublayers: one vector of d numbers per position
Attention
the sequence mixer: the only step where a position reads other positions (section 4)
FFN
the feed-forward network: the same small MLP applied to each position on its own (section 6)

Because each sublayer only adds to x, what layer 3 writes is still there for layer 30 to read, and gradients flow back along the same identity path; that is why a stack of 80 or 126 such blocks trains at all.

derived from the {{Llama 3.1 8B config|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/raw/main/config.json}} (hidden_size 4,096, 32 heads, 8 KV heads, head_dim 128, intermediate_size 14,336, no biases): 218.1M weights per block, 41.9M in attention and 176.2M in the FFN. The tiny GPT of the running example (d_model 256, 8 query heads of 32 with 8 KV heads, a GELU FFN of 1,024, two matrices): 786,944 weights per block, 262,144 in attention and 524,288 in the FFN, the classic 4d² and 8d² split measured (model.param_groups()).

That split is why mixture of experts (section 6) targets the FFN, where the parameters are, while the attention variants (section 4) target the cache. Each weight is used once per token in one multiply and one add, so a forward pass costs about 2 FLOPs per weight per token; the backward pass costs about twice the forward, giving 6N FLOPs per training token ({{Kaplan et al. 2020, section 2.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361}}). Attention adds a term that grows with the context (section 8).
