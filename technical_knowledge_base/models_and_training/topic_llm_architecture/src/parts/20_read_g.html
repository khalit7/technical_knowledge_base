10. Beyond attention: state-space models, linear attention and hybrids

The problem. Attention keeps everything: memory grows with every token and each new token reads all of it. A recurrent layer keeps a fixed-size state instead and updates it once per token, so memory and work per token stay constant however long the context. Linear attention writes each token's value into a matrix state indexed by its key (St = St−1 + vtkt⊤, read out as Stqt); a state-space model (SSM) runs a linear recurrence ht = Atht−1 + Btxt, yt = Ctht. What made them competitive was making the update depend on the input, so the layer chooses what to keep and what to forget: Mamba's selective SSM ({{Mamba|n:3c65c17b0d0d8179984fc3a1fee4c36a}}), and gated delta rules that erase what a key stored before writing (Gated DeltaNet, {{Yang et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464}}). Training still runs in parallel over the sequence with a scan or chunked algorithm.

The same 8 tokens through one attention layer and one recurrent layer

Shapes from the {{Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B config|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct/blob/main/config.json}}: its attention layers keep 2 KV heads of 256 (1,024 numbers per token); its Gated DeltaNet layers keep 32 value heads with a 128 × 128 state (524,288 numbers, whatever the context). The chart is derived: one layer of each, numbers held against context; they cross at 512 tokens.

What it costs. A fixed state is a lossy summary: whatever was not written into it is gone. {{Jelassi et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032}} prove that "a two layer transformer can copy strings of exponential length while" fixed-state models "are fundamentally limited by their fixed-size latent state", and find pretrained transformers "dramatically outperform state space models at copying and retrieving information from context". Hence hybrids: mostly recurrent layers, with a few attention layers kept for exact recall. Jamba uses one attention layer per seven Mamba layers and reports a KV cache of about 4 GB at 256K tokens in 16-bit ({{Lieber et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19887}}, vendor); NVIDIA's Nemotron-H "replace[s] the majority of self-attention layers" with Mamba layers and reports "up to 3× faster" inference than comparable transformers ({{Nemotron-H|https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03624}}, vendor); Qwen3-Next keeps full attention in every fourth layer (full_attention_interval: 4) and Gated DeltaNet in the rest, 24 KiB of cache per token against 96 KiB had all 48 layers been attention (derived).

By 2026 the hybrid is a flagship design (layer patterns and caches derived from the configs on the {{Architecture map|#t-map}}; sizes from the model cards, vendor): Qwen3.5-397B-A17B keeps Qwen3-Next's 3 Gated DeltaNet layers per attention layer; NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Super (120B, 12B active) interleaves 40 Mamba-2 layers with 8 attention layers, 8 KiB of cache per token; Moonshot's Kimi K3 (2.8T, 104B active) puts 3 Kimi Delta Attention layers before each MLA layer, 27 KiB per token.

Measured on the tiny GPT ({{Beyond attention|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8142bc9ecac1fc366966}} child; 1,500 steps of 8,192 tokens each, held-out loss on 100 windows / loss on the running sequence): an SSM-only model ties attention on held-out text, 2.152 against 2.160, but loses on the running sequence, whose repeated names reward exact recall, 2.024 against 1.741. Hybrids beat both: one attention layer in six 2.089 / 1.688, two in six 2.082 / 1.629. The copying limit shows in public pretrained models too: Mamba-130M copies a 32-word list 85% of the time, but 29% at 256 words and 7% at 1,000, while Pythia-160M stays at 86% to 93% (CPU, transformers v5.20.1).

11. Families and training objectives

The same blocks build three families, differing in which positions may see which, and in what they are trained to predict:

FamilyAttention maskObjectiveGood atExample
Decoder-onlyCausal: each token sees itself and earlier tokensPredict the next token at every positionGeneration; everything, at scale{{GPT-3|n:3c65c17b0d0d8193ac92c7648cfaca12}}, Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek
Encoder-onlyBidirectional: every token sees every tokenRecover masked tokens (15% in BERT)Embeddings, classification, retrieval; cannot generate fluently{{BERT|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e5ad9bd09cbf18ad7c}}
Encoder-decoderBidirectional encoder over the input; causal decoder that also attends to itRebuild corrupted spans, or input-to-outputTranslation, summarisation; the input is encoded once{{T5|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}, {{T5Gemma 2|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}

Decoder-only won because one objective, next-token prediction, turns any text into training data and any task into a prompt, and it scales simply. Two newer objectives change what a decoder predicts. Multi-token prediction (MTP) trains extra heads to predict tokens further ahead ({{Gloeckle et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737}}): DeepSeek-V3 trains one extra token and can reuse that module as a draft for {{speculative decoding|n:3f35c17b0d0d81e086fde85940ebac1c}}. Diffusion language models start from a fully masked sequence and unmask many tokens per step in any order, trading the strict left-to-right loop for parallel refinement ({{LLaDA, Nie et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992}}; {{DiffusionGemma|n:3c65c17b0d0d818390bfd7969757211e}}).

Measured on the tiny GPT (MTP lab of {{Families and objectives|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8105b616c17c1439f766}}): one MTP module trained jointly lowers the main model's held-out loss from 2.278 to 2.241 at an equal number of tokens, a gain of 0.036; but the module adds 32% training FLOPs per step, and the plain model given those FLOPs reaches 2.160. At equal compute MTP loses at this scale. DeepSeek-V3 reports gains from MTP at a far larger scale (vendor), not tested here, and its module doubles as a draft for speculative decoding.
