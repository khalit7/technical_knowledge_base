12. Reading a model's config in 30 seconds

A Hugging Face config.json is the architecture in about thirty fields. Read it with five questions, here on Llama 3.1 8B (excerpt, field order kept; {{full file|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/raw/main/config.json}}):

"head_dim": 128, "hidden_act": "silu", "hidden_size": 4096, "intermediate_size": 14336, "max_position_embeddings": 131072, "num_attention_heads": 32, "num_hidden_layers": 32, "num_key_value_heads": 8, "rms_norm_eps": 1e-05, "rope_scaling": {"factor": 8.0, "high_freq_factor": 4.0, "low_freq_factor": 1.0, "original_max_position_embeddings": 8192, "rope_type": "llama3"}, "rope_theta": 500000.0, "tie_word_embeddings": false, "torch_dtype": "bfloat16", "vocab_size": 128256
  1. Shape: num_hidden_layers × hidden_size: 32 × 4,096.
  2. Attention and cache: num_attention_heads against num_key_value_heads: 32 against 8 is GQA with groups of 4; with head_dim 128 that is 2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 128 KiB per token in BF16. Other variants announce themselves: kv_lora_rank and qk_rope_head_dim mean MLA, sliding_window or layer_types mean windowed layers, linear-layer fields mean a hybrid.
  3. FFN: intermediate_size 14,336 with silu means SwiGLU at 3.5d. MoE adds num_experts or n_routed_experts, num_experts_per_tok and a separate expert width (moe_intermediate_size).
  4. Position and context: rope_theta, rope_scaling and max_position_embeddings: trained at 8,192 and stretched 8 times to 131,072.
  5. Size: vocab_size and tie_word_embeddings for the embedding share, then section 8's formula: 8.03B.

Never derive head_dim as hidden_size / num_attention_heads when the field exists: Gemma 3 27B has hidden size 5,376 and 32 heads but head_dim 128, not 168 ({{config|https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-27b-pt/blob/main/config.json}}), and Qwen3-0.6B has 1,024 and 16 heads with head_dim 128 ({{config|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B/blob/main/config.json}}).

13. Choosing: which design for which constraint

ConstraintChooseWhy, and what you give up
Short contexts, high throughput, standard serving stacksDense or MoE with GQA, SwiGLU, RoPE, pre-norm RMSNormMature kernels everywhere; the cache is small at short context anyway
Long contexts at high concurrencyMLA, or GQA with few KV heads; FP8 cache; windowed layers mixed with global onesShrinks what each token keeps or how long; costs kernel complexity (MLA) or far recall through fewer layers (windows)
Very long streams, fixed memoryHybrid: mostly SSM or linear layers, one attention layer in 4 to 8Near-constant memory; exact recall rests on the few attention layers
More quality at the same latency, memory availableMoE with many small experts and a shared expertActive parameters set latency; total parameters set memory, and balancing must be engineered
Small device, little memorySmall dense model, deep and thin, tied embeddings, GQAMoE does not help when every expert must fit in memory
Stable training at scalePre-norm RMSNorm plus QK-normTwo extra norms per layer, almost free
