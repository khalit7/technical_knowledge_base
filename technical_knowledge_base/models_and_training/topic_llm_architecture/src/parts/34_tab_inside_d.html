Massive activations and the growing residual stream

The problem. In a pre-norm block (the norm sits before each sub-layer, {{Block lab|#t-block}}), every sub-layer adds its output to the residual stream and nothing ever rescales it. So the stream's size is free to grow with depth, and a model can use that freedom to make a few numbers enormous. Sun et al. (2024, {{Massive Activations in Large Language Models|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17762}}) found such "massive activations" across many LLMs, sometimes about 100,000 times the typical value (paraphrase of their abstract); they sit in a few fixed dimensions on a few tokens (often the first), stay nearly constant whatever the input, act like bias terms, and are what lets attention concentrate on those tokens. That is the mechanism behind the sinks of the previous section.

Why an architect cares.

  • Quantisation. A single entry of 20,000 next to entries of about 1 wrecks a per-tensor int8 scale: Dettmers et al. ({{LLM.int8()|https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07339}}, 2022) keep such outlier dimensions in 16 bits. See {{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}.
  • Number formats. float16 tops out at 65,504; a stream that grows to tens of thousands is close. bfloat16 has the range (and less precision), which is one reason training moved to it ({{Numerical computing|n:3f05c17b0d0d81d6af0be4a9a2edba8d}}).
  • The final norm. Because the stream's scale drifts with depth, pre-norm models need a norm before the unembedding (self.norm at modeling_llama.py line 402). Post-norm models renormalise after every block instead and pay for it in stability ({{Block lab|#t-block}}).
  • Designs. Learned sinks (gpt-oss) and QK-norm (Qwen3, OLMo 2, Gemma 3) give heads other ways to say "nothing here", which may reduce the pressure to grow such values (unconfirmed for these models); Sun et al. trained GPT-2 with explicit attention biases and report that "massive activations disappear" (section 4.3; not run here).

How to check: hook the output of every decoder layer (a forward hook, as in extract_pretrained.py; the hidden_states output of a Hugging Face model applies the final norm to its last entry) and print the largest |x| per layer with its token and dimension.

The logit lens: what would the model say if it stopped here?

The idea (nostalgebraist, 2020, {{interpreting GPT: the logit lens|https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/AcKRB8wDpdaN6v6ru/interpreting-gpt-the-logit-lens}}). The residual stream is one shared space that every layer writes into and the unembedding finally reads. So apply the model's own final norm and unembedding to the stream after layer k and read off a next-token distribution. If the stream is a gradually refined prediction, the early guesses should drift towards the final one.

When it matters and the caveat. The lens is a cheap first look at where in depth a prediction forms, useful when deciding where to cut layers (early exit, layer pruning) or where to read features from. It is not exact: middle layers may encode the answer in directions the final unembedding does not read, which is why the tuned lens (Belrose et al., 2023, {{arXiv 2303.08112|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112}}) learns a small map per layer (not run here). How to check: model.lm_head(model.model.norm(h)) for each hooked h; the last layer must reproduce the model's logits exactly (ours: max difference ).

Common mistakes, drills and interview questions

Mistakes we made or saw on this tab

Asking for attention weights with SDPA or FlashAttention
transformers prints a warning and outputs.attentions is not a usable list of tensors (section 1). Load with attn_implementation="eager", or switch with model.set_attn_implementation("eager"), and keep the sequence short: eager attention materialises every weight.
Reading the last hidden_states entry as the raw residual stream
In Hugging Face models the last entry comes after the final norm, so its size is not comparable to the others, and applying the norm again in a logit lens normalises twice. Hook the decoder layers instead (section 5).
Expecting induction heads in a small, briefly trained model
We did: the tiny GPT trained here for 12.3M tokens has none (section 3). Whether a mechanism exists is a question of training tokens as much as of architecture; Pythia-70M, same shape, needed far more. When an ablation of a tiny model "shows" that an architecture cannot copy, check the budget first.
Evicting the first tokens from a sliding cache
The loss on SmolLM2 roughly doubles once the window slides past the start (section 4). Keep the sink tokens, or train with a sink.
Calling every head with high weight on token 0 "broken"
The sink is a learned no-op, not a fault. Forbidding it raised the loss and broke generation (section 4).
Quantising activations with one scale per tensor
One entry of about 20,000 (section 5) forces a scale that rounds every ordinary entry to zero in int8. Use per-token or per-channel scales, or keep outlier channels in 16 bits.

Predict, then reveal

Interview questions

What is an attention sink, and why does it form?
Softmax weights sum to 1, so a head with nothing to read must still put its weight somewhere; models learn to dump it on the first token, which every query can see and whose vector they make distinctive (massive activations). In SmolLM2 the mean weight on token 0 jumps above 0.3 from layer 12, in the same layer where one dimension of the first token's residual reaches about 20,000.
Why does a sliding-window KV cache fail, and what is the fix?
Once the first tokens leave the window, the heads lose their sink and spread weight over tokens they would have ignored. Keep the first few tokens' keys and values (StreamingLLM), or train with a learned sink (gpt-oss). Measured here: 1.62 full, 3.36 window only, 1.81 window plus sink (mean loss after the first 64 tokens).
What is an induction head, and why does it need two layers?
[A][B] ... [A] → [B]. A previous-token head first writes "my predecessor was A" into position B's residual; a later head's query (current token A) matches that key and copies B. The second head can only match on information a first head has already moved, hence two layers.
Design: you replace 3 of every 4 attention layers with SSM layers. What do you test first, and why?
Copying and retrieval from context, because that is what a fixed-size state cannot do and induction heads do: the repeated-random-tokens loss (first copy against second), a needle or name-recall test at the target length, and the loss on long documents by position. Check which attention layers held the induction heads in the original model, and keep attention where they were; keep the sink behaviour if the remaining attention layers use windows.
Your int8 model's quality collapses but the weights quantise fine. Where do you look?
The activations: a few dimensions on a few tokens (often the first) are thousands of times larger than the rest. Print max |x| per layer with its token and dimension; use per-token or per-channel scales or mixed precision for the outlier channels.
What does the logit lens tell you, and when does it mislead?
Where in depth the next-token prediction forms, using the model's own unembedding on intermediate states. It misleads where intermediate layers use directions the unembedding ignores (SmolLM2's middle layers give worse than uniform guesses although the model is fine); the tuned lens corrects that with a learned map per layer.
