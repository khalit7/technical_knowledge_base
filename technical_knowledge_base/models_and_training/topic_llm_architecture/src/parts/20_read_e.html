8. Shape and the arithmetic that ties the parts together

Three numbers answer most questions about a model, and all three come from its config in a minute: parameters, FLOPs per token, and KV bytes per token (section 4).

N ≈ L × (attention + FFN) + 2Vd
FLOPs per token ≈ 2N + 4 L T H dh
attention
2d² for Q and output, plus 2 d Hkv dh for K and V (4d² with full MHA)
FFN
3 d dff for SwiGLU (2 d dff for a plain MLP; 8d² at dff = 4d, hence the classic 12 L d²)
2Vd
embedding and unembedding (Vd once if tied)
4 L T H dh
the attention term for one token attending to T earlier tokens: scores Q K⊤ and the weighted sum with V, 2 FLOPs per multiply-add each; it does not depend on Hkv, so GQA shrinks the cache, not these FLOPs

Worked: Llama 3.1 8B. 32 blocks of 218.1M = 6.98B, plus two 525.3M embedding tables and a final norm = 8.03B parameters, the published size (derived). Forward FLOPs per token: 2N = 15.0 GFLOP. The attention term at T = 131,072: 4 × 32 × 131,072 × 4,096 = 68.7 GFLOP, more than four times the weights' share. The two are equal at T ≈ 28,600 tokens: below that a token's cost is set by the parameter count, above it by the context (derived). Training costs about three times the forward (plus three times the attention term).

Shape (width against depth) changes latency more than quality at a fixed parameter count: each layer is a sequential step, and small models are the exception that go deep and thin (closed block below).

Read the fields, do not derive them. Never derive head_dim as hidden_size / num_attention_heads when the field exists: Gemma 3 27B has hidden size 5,376 and 32 heads but head_dim 128, not 168 ({{config|https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-27b-pt/blob/main/config.json}}), so every derived cache and FLOP figure would be 31% too high; Qwen3-0.6B has 1,024 and 16 heads with head_dim 128 ({{config|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B/blob/main/config.json}}).

Depth against width, with five real models

Depth against width (large models run about 100 to 130 times wider than deep). At a fixed parameter count shape matters less than one might think: {{Kaplan et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361}} found that "other architectural details such as network width or depth have minimal effects within a wide range" (abstract). It matters for speed: each layer is a sequential step, so a deeper model of the same size has more latency per token. Small models are the exception that go deep and thin: Meta's MobileLLM reports that under a billion parameters depth beats width ({{Liu et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905}}, vendor), and SmolLM2-135M is 30 layers of 576.

9. Long context: four walls, four pages

A model "supports 128K tokens" only if it clears four separate limits. Each is owned by a different page, which is why long context is a map question:

Wall 4 is the one a spec sheet hides. RULER found that of models claiming 32K or more, "only half of them can maintain satisfactory performance at the length of 32K" ({{Hsieh et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654}}, third-party), and information in the middle of a long prompt is used worse than at its ends ({{Liu et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172}}). The claimed context is a ceiling; the effective context is measured.

Measured on the Long context child (CPU)

SmolLM2-135M, whose config allows 8,192 positions, finds a pass key 80% of the time at 8,192 tokens and never at 12,288 or 16,384 (wall 3). Wall 4 hides behind perplexity: at 4,096 tokens, evicting three quarters of its KV cache with scores computed before the question is seen drops retrieval from 97% to 0%, while H2O eviction down to 12.5% of the cache moves the loss on a new story only from 2.07 to 2.10. A model trained with a window has no position wall: the tiny GPT with window 64 (RoPE, trained on 256 tokens) scores 2.09 on positions 1,024 to 2,047, where the same model with full attention scores 3.82 measured.
