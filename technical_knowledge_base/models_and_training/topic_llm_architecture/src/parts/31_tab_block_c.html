Pre-norm against post-norm: the same token, the same seed, the same budget

The problem. A stack of blocks is hard to train when the size of the activations or the gradients changes from layer to layer. Normalisation fixes the size of a vector; the question is where to put it. Post-norm (the 2017 Transformer, BERT) normalises after the residual add, x ← Norm(x + F(x)): the stream itself is rescaled at every sub-layer. Pre-norm (GPT-2 onward, every current LLM) normalises only the copy the sub-layer reads, x ← x + F(Norm(x)), and leaves the stream alone, so there is an untouched identity path from the embedding to the last block. The tiny GPT has both as a switch (norm_pos); everything else (seed, data order, schedule, 1,500 steps of 8,192 tokens) is equal.

measured With both models trained at the same learning rate (1e-3, the highest where post-norm trains with the default 100-step warmup), the traced token’s stream grows from 0.9 after the embedding to 14.8 after block 6 under pre-norm, and stays between 16.0 and 16.4 under post-norm (√256 = 16 times a gain near 1) until the last norm, whose learned gain takes it to 24.6. The trained reference model of the walkthrough (pre-norm, lr 3e-3) grows from 1.3 to 65.8, most of it in block 1’s FFN (a branch of norm 53.0). That growth is the cost of pre-norm: later blocks add updates that are small next to the stream, so each layer changes the representation less (one argument for the depth-scaled initialisations and the extra norms of newer models), and the final norm (ln_f in the tiny GPT, self.norm at modeling_llama.py:402) is mandatory.

measured At the default learning rate 3e-3 with a 100-step warmup, post-norm fails: by step 30 its training loss is at the unigram level (6.04) and it ends at 5.943, while pre-norm reaches 2.281 with identical settings. Lower the learning rate and post-norm trains (2.534 at 1e-3, 3.215 at 3e-4); keep 3e-3 but warm up over 500 steps and it reaches 2.138, the best of the eight runs. Pre-norm trains at every rate tried, degrading gracefully: 2.464 at 1e-3, 2.734 at 1e-2, 4.361 at 3e-2, where its gradient norm (before clipping) spikes to 40.8. That is the trade in one chart: pre-norm is forgiving, post-norm can be better but only inside a narrow window of learning rate and warmup.

Does not reproduce at this depth: Xiong et al. 2020, Figure 3
Xiong et al. ({{arXiv 2002.04745|https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745}}) show that at initialisation post-norm’s gradients grow toward the output layers while pre-norm’s stay level, and derive that a warmup is needed for post-norm. In this 6-layer model at initialisation, post-norm’s block gradients are level (0.074 to 0.075) and 20 to 30x smaller than pre-norm’s, which decrease slightly with depth (2.21 at block 1 to 1.57 at block 6). Their warmup conclusion does reproduce (the 500-step warmup rescues lr 3e-3); the depth profile likely needs a deeper stack to appear. Not run here: a 24-layer version.

When it matters. Post-norm’s fragility grows with depth, which is why it vanished from LLMs; pre-norm’s stream growth is what newer recipes tame with QK-norm, sandwich norms (Gemma 2 and 3) and depth-scaled initialisation ({{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}} owns those). How to check a run. Log the per-block gradient norm and the residual-stream norm per layer (one forward with hooks, as in trace.py); a training loss that sits at the unigram entropy of your data, with a small, flat gradient norm, is a model that stopped using context.
