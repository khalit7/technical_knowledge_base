10. Families and training objectives

The same blocks build three families, differing in which positions may see which (the mask) and in what they are trained to predict (the objective):

FamilyAttention maskObjectiveGood atExample
Decoder-onlyCausal: each token sees itself and earlier tokensPredict the next token at every positionGeneration; everything, at scale{{GPT-3|n:3c65c17b0d0d8193ac92c7648cfaca12}}, Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek
Encoder-onlyBidirectional: every token sees every tokenRecover masked tokens (15% in BERT)Embeddings, classification, retrieval; cannot generate fluently{{BERT|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e5ad9bd09cbf18ad7c}}
Encoder-decoderBidirectional encoder over the input; causal decoder that also attends to itRebuild corrupted spans, or input-to-outputTranslation, summarisation; the input is encoded once{{T5|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}, {{T5Gemma 2|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}

Decoder-only won because one objective, next-token prediction, turns any text into training data and any task into a prompt, and it scales simply. Two newer objectives change what a decoder predicts. Multi-token prediction (MTP) trains extra heads to predict tokens further ahead ({{Gloeckle et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737}}): DeepSeek-V3 trains one extra token and can reuse that module as a draft for {{speculative decoding|n:3f35c17b0d0d81e086fde85940ebac1c}}. Diffusion language models start from a fully masked sequence and unmask many tokens per step in any order, trading the left-to-right loop for parallel refinement ({{LLaDA, Nie et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992}}; {{DiffusionGemma|n:3c65c17b0d0d818390bfd7969757211e}}).

Measured: MTP on the tiny GPT

In section 7 of {{Families and objectives|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8105b616c17c1439f766}}, one MTP module trained jointly lowers the main model's held-out loss from 2.278 to 2.241 at an equal number of tokens, a gain of 0.036; but the module adds 32% training FLOPs per step, and the plain model given those FLOPs reaches 2.160. At equal compute MTP loses at this scale. DeepSeek-V3 reports gains from MTP at a far larger scale (vendor), not tested here measured.

Go deeper: {{Families and objectives|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8105b616c17c1439f766}} owns the masks, the objectives, MTP and diffusion LMs; {{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}} how the objective is run at scale.

11. Reading a model's config in 30 seconds

A Hugging Face config.json is the architecture in about thirty fields, and every part of the map above has its fields. Llama 3.1 8B (excerpt, field order kept; {{full file|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/raw/main/config.json}}):

"head_dim": 128, "hidden_act": "silu", "hidden_size": 4096, "intermediate_size": 14336, "max_position_embeddings": 131072, "num_attention_heads": 32, "num_hidden_layers": 32, "num_key_value_heads": 8, "rms_norm_eps": 1e-05, "rope_scaling": {"factor": 8.0, "high_freq_factor": 4.0, "low_freq_factor": 1.0, "original_max_position_embeddings": 8192, "rope_type": "llama3"}, "rope_theta": 500000.0, "tie_word_embeddings": false, "torch_dtype": "bfloat16", "vocab_size": 128256
  1. Shape: num_hidden_layers × hidden_size: 32 × 4,096.
  2. Mixer and cache: num_attention_heads against num_key_value_heads: 32 against 8 is GQA with groups of 4; with head_dim 128 that is 2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 128 KiB per token in BF16. Other variants announce themselves: kv_lora_rank and qk_rope_head_dim mean MLA, sliding_window or layer_types mean windowed layers, linear-layer fields mean a hybrid.
  3. FFN: intermediate_size 14,336 with silu means SwiGLU at 3.5d. MoE adds num_experts or n_routed_experts, num_experts_per_tok and a separate expert width (moe_intermediate_size).
  4. Position and context: rope_theta, rope_scaling and max_position_embeddings: trained at 8,192 and stretched 8 times to 131,072.
  5. Size: vocab_size and tie_word_embeddings for the embedding share, then section 8's formula: 8.03B.

12. Choosing: which design for which constraint

ConstraintChooseWhy, and what you give up
Short contexts, high throughput, standard serving stacksDense or MoE with GQA, SwiGLU, RoPE, pre-norm RMSNormMature kernels everywhere; the cache is small at short context anyway
Long contexts at high concurrencyMLA, or GQA with few KV heads; FP8 cache; windowed layers mixed with global onesShrinks what each token keeps or how long; costs kernel complexity (MLA) or far recall through fewer layers (windows)
Very long streams, fixed memoryHybrid: mostly SSM or linear layers, one attention layer in 4 to 8Near-constant memory; exact recall rests on the few attention layers
More quality at the same latency, memory availableMoE with many small experts and a shared expertActive parameters set latency; total parameters set memory, and balancing must be engineered
Small device, little memorySmall dense model, deep and thin, tied embeddings, GQAMoE does not help when every expert must fit in memory
Stable training at scalePre-norm RMSNorm plus QK-normTwo extra norms per layer, almost free
