8. Depth, width, and the arithmetic that ties it together

Three numbers answer most questions about a model, and all three come from its config in a minute: parameters, FLOPs per token, and KV bytes per token (section 3).

N ≈ L × (attention + FFN) + 2Vd
FLOPs per token ≈ 2N + 4 L T H dh
attention
2d² for Q and output, plus 2 d Hkv dh for K and V (4d² with full MHA)
FFN
3 d dff for SwiGLU (2 d dff for a plain MLP; 8d² at dff = 4d, hence the classic 12 L d²)
2Vd
embedding and unembedding (Vd once if tied)
4 L T H dh
the attention term of the forward pass for one token attending to T earlier tokens: scores Q K⊤ and the weighted sum with V, 2 FLOPs per multiply-add each

Worked: Llama 3.1 8B. 32 blocks of 218.1M (section 2) = 6.98B, plus two 525.3M embedding tables and a final norm = 8.03B parameters, the published size (derived, matches the 8.03B on its model page). Forward FLOPs per token: 2N = 15.0 GFLOP. The attention term at T = 131,072: 4 × 32 × 131,072 × 4,096 = 68.7 GFLOP, more than four times the weights' share. The two are equal at T ≈ 28,600 tokens: below that a token's cost is set by the parameter count, above it by the context (derived). Training costs about three times the forward, the 6N rule of section 2 (plus three times the attention term).

Depth against width. At a fixed parameter count, shape matters less than one might think: {{Kaplan et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361}} found that "other architectural details such as network width or depth have minimal effects within a wide range" (abstract). It matters for speed: each layer is a sequential step, so a deeper model of the same size has more latency per token and less to parallelise per step. Small models are the exception that go deep and thin: Meta's MobileLLM reports that at under a billion parameters depth beats width ({{Liu et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14905}}, vendor), and SmolLM2-135M is 30 layers of 576. 9. Long context: four walls

A model "supports 128K tokens" only if it clears four separate limits, each with its own remedy:

  1. Memory: the KV cache grows linearly with context (40 GiB per 128K request for Llama 3.1 70B, section 3). Remedies: fewer KV heads (GQA, MQA), compressed caches (MLA), windowed layers, FP8 caches, layers without a cache (section 10).
  2. Compute: prefill attention grows with the square of the prompt length; past about 29K tokens it is most of Llama 3.1 8B's work (section 8). Remedies: sliding windows, trained sparse attention, linear layers.
  3. Position: rotation angles past the trained length were never seen (section 5). Remedy: RoPE scaling plus a long-context training phase.
  4. Use: a model can fit and run 128K tokens and still fail to use them. RULER found that of models claiming 32K or more, "only half of them can maintain satisfactory performance at the length of 32K" ({{Hsieh et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654}}, third-party); information in the middle of a long prompt is used worse than at its ends ({{Liu et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172}}). The claimed context is a ceiling, the effective context is measured.

Measured (on the {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}} child, CPU): SmolLM2-135M, whose config allows 8,192 positions, finds a pass key 80% of the time at 8,192 tokens and never at 12,288 or 16,384 (wall 3). Wall 4 hides behind perplexity: at 4,096 tokens, evicting three quarters of its KV cache with scores computed before the question is seen drops retrieval from 97% to 0%, while H2O eviction down to 12.5% of the cache moves the loss on a new story only from 2.07 to 2.10. And a model trained with a window has no position wall: the tiny GPT with window 64 (RoPE, trained on 256 tokens) scores 2.09 on positions 1,024 to 2,047, where the same model with full attention scores 3.82.

How to check: compute the cache at the target context and concurrency (wall 1), time a prefill at that length (wall 2), read rope_scaling and max_position_embeddings (wall 3), and run a long-context benchmark at the length you need, not the length on the card (wall 4).
