Published evidence on the quality cost. The GQA paper converts existing multi-head checkpoints by continued training on "5% of original pre-training compute" and reports that "uptrained GQA achieves quality close to multi-head attention with comparable speed to MQA" ({{Ainslie et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13245}}, authors' own runs). DeepSeek reports that MLA in DeepSeek-V2 "reduces the KV cache by 93.3% and boosts the maximum generation throughput to 5.76 times", compared with its earlier dense DeepSeek 67B ({{DeepSeek-V2 abstract|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434}}, vendor; that comparison also changes the model, so it is not MLA alone). MLA is the one variant that cuts the cache below aggressive GQA while DeepSeek reports quality at or above MHA; how it works (compress, decouple the position key, absorb the up-projections at decode) is animated step by step on {{DeepSeek|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a2ae7ec6796947a235}}, against the MHA it replaced. Sliding windows do not shrink what a token keeps, only how long it is kept: Gemma 3 27B keeps 496 KiB per token by the formula (62 layers, 16 KV heads of 128), but at 128K its 52 windowed layers (window 1,024) hold only 416 MiB together and its 10 global layers 10 GiB, about 10.4 GiB against the 62 GiB the per-token figure suggests (derived).

Attention sinks. Trained models put a large share of attention on the first token whatever it is, a place to "park" attention when nothing else is relevant. Drop the first tokens from a sliding cache and quality collapses; keep a few of them and window attention works on streams of "up to 4 million tokens" ({{StreamingLLM, Xiao et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453}}). 5. Position: telling attention where each token sits

The problem. Attention by itself is blind to order: shuffle the earlier tokens and each query gets the same weighted average. Something must inject position. The answers in order of arrival:

  • Absolute, added to the embedding. Fixed sinusoids ({{Vaswani et al. 2017|https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762}}) or a learned vector per position (GPT-2, 1,024 positions). A learned table has nothing for position 1,025.
  • RoPE (rotary position embedding) ({{Su et al., RoFormer|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cfa5e9f54459720098}}): split each query and key into pairs of numbers and rotate pair i by the angle position × θi, with θi = base−2i/dh. Rotations compose, so the query-key dot product depends only on the distance between the two tokens. No parameters; the near-universal default. The base sets the slowest rotation: 10,000 in the original, 500,000 in Llama 3.1 (rope_theta), larger for longer context.
  • ALiBi: no rotation, a penalty on each score proportional to distance ({{Press et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}), built to extrapolate.
  • NoPE: no position signal at all; a causal decoder can still infer order from the mask. Used on some layers of recent models (one in four in SmolLM3, {{config|https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM3-3B/blob/main/config.json}}).

Past the trained length. A model trained on 8K tokens has never seen the rotation angles of position 100,000 in its slow pairs, and quality collapses there. The fixes stretch the angles back into the trained range: position interpolation ({{Chen et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595}}), base scaling, and YaRN, which stretches only the slow pairs ({{Peng et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}), each followed by a short training phase at the longer length. The rule ships in the config: Llama 3.1 has "rope_scaling": {"rope_type": "llama3", "factor": 8.0, "original_max_position_embeddings": 8192}; gpt-oss uses YaRN with factor 32 from 4,096 to 131,072 ({{config|https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-120b/blob/main/config.json}}). Drop that field when copying a config and the model still loads, still answers short prompts well, and degrades past the original length.
