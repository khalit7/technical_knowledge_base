6. The feed-forward network and mixture of experts

After attention has gathered information from other positions, the FFN transforms each position on its own: expand, apply a nonlinearity, project back. It holds most of the parameters (section 3) and behaves like a key-value memory where many stored facts are found ({{Geva et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14913}}). Current models use SwiGLU, out = Wdown( SiLU(Wgatex) × Wupx ), better at equal compute than the original ReLU MLP of width 4d ({{Shazeer 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05202}}); its three matrices shrink the width to 8d/3 so they cost what two did (Llama 3.1 8B: 14,336 = 3.5d; the original 2,048 for d = 512).

Mixture of experts (MoE) replaces the one FFN by E smaller FFNs (experts) and a router, a linear layer that scores every expert for each token; the token goes to its top k experts and their outputs are summed, weighted by the router's scores. DeepSeek-V3 has 256 routed experts plus 1 shared expert that every token uses, 8 routed per token: in one of its MoE layers the experts hold 11.3 billion weights and a token uses 0.40 billion of them, 3.5% (derived from its {{config|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/blob/main/config.json}}: experts of 3 × 7,168 × 2,048); the whole model is 671B total and 37B active ({{DeepSeek-V3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437}}, vendor).

The distinction that crosses pages: total and active parameters bill different things. Total parameters set the memory floor (every expert must be resident); active parameters set FLOPs per token. At large decode batches nearly every expert is touched in each step, so the weight bytes read per step approach the total, not the active count; and MoE does not help a small device where every expert must fit in memory. {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}} (section 1) builds a model's dense twin at equal active parameters and steps through what the experts change; the {{Config calculator|#t-calc}} gives both counts for any config.

Expert size, balancing and what the tiny GPT measured

The trend is from few large experts (Mixtral 8x7B: 8 experts, top 2, {{Mixtral|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba72af0ac91ddc6b3}}) to many small ones (Qwen3-235B-A22B: 128 experts, top 8, no shared expert, {{Qwen3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388}}). Experts must stay busy in equal measure: the classic fix is an auxiliary loss that rewards balanced routing ({{Switch Transformers|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a8b541c9babbf40c94}}); DeepSeek-V3 instead nudges a per-expert bias after each step and reports this "auxiliary-loss-free" scheme beats the loss (vendor). Spread over GPUs, an MoE needs expert parallelism and its all-to-all traffic.

On the tiny GPT at equal active FFN, only the fine-grained, shared-expert layout clearly beats dense (2.210 against 2.278); without balancing the router collapses; tokens over an expert's capacity are dropped. The runs, the seed spread and the capacity arithmetic are sections 2 to 6 of {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}} measured.

7. Normalisation, residuals and stability

A normalisation layer rescales a vector to a standard size so activations and gradients neither grow nor shrink layer by layer. The 2017 Transformer used post-norm, x′ = Norm(x + F(x)), which normalises the residual stream itself; deep post-norm models need a careful learning-rate warmup to train at all. Pre-norm, x′ = x + F(Norm(x)), normalises only the copy each sublayer reads and leaves an untouched identity path; pre-norm can drop the warmup ({{Xiong et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04745}}, their claim; every run here uses a 100-step warmup). Its cost is a residual stream whose size grows with depth, since every layer only adds. On the tiny GPT at the default learning rate, post-norm never leaves the unigram plateau (it ends at 5.943, the loss of predicting each token by its frequency) while pre-norm reaches 2.281 measured; section 2 of {{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}} draws both residual streams.

  • RMSNorm ({{Zhang and Sennrich 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07467}}) divides by the root mean square and drops LayerNorm's mean subtraction and bias: one reduction instead of two, no measured quality loss; universal in current models.
  • QK-norm: RMSNorm on queries and keys before the dot product, so attention scores cannot grow without bound, a known source of loss spikes ({{Dehghani et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05442}}; used in {{OLMo 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fb9857fb956165ae1c}}, Gemma 3 and Qwen3).
  • Norms after the sublayer: OLMo 2 normalises each sublayer's output before adding it, x + Norm(F(x)); Gemma 3 normalises both before and after ({{Gemma 3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786}}).

How to check a run. A loss spike is a sudden jump in training loss; log the global gradient norm, the largest attention logit and the per-layer residual norms. A spike preceded by growing attention logits points at QK-norm or a lower learning rate; a single bad batch points at data. Trained models also grow a few massive activations, single residual dimensions thousands of times larger than the rest ({{Sun et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17762}}), tied to the attention sinks of section 4, which matter for low-precision training and quantisation.

Measured runs and newer variants
  • Placements and fixes on the tiny GPT. Post-norm with a 500-step warmup is the best of eight placement runs (2.138), and QK-norm is the fix that saves a run at ten times the default rate (2.432 against 4.295); the runs, the gradient reaching each block and the stream under each placement are sections 2 and 4 of {{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}} measured.
  • Massive activations. In SmolLM2-135M dimension 507 of the first token's residual stream reaches 20,156 at layer 12 and stays above 4,047 to the last block, while the median entry at that depth is about 1.6, a ratio near 13,000 to 1; Qwen3-0.6B shows the same thing, dimension 35 of the first token at 6,160 in layer 26, about 1,100 times its median. The tiny GPT, without a sink, has none: its largest entry after training is 36 measured.
  • Newer variants, read from configs and code (transformers v5.20.1): Gemma 4 multiplies by a plain RMSNorm gain where Gemma 1 to 3 used 1 + gain (modeling_gemma4.py line 222); MiniMax-M1 keeps post-norm with DeepNorm's residual scale, α = (2 × 80)1/4 ≈ 3.5566 for its 80 layers (layernorm_*_alpha in its config); DeepSeek-V4 widens the residual stream into 4 streams mixed by a doubly stochastic matrix, manifold-constrained hyper-connections (mHC, {{arXiv 2512.24880|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24880}}), with pre-norm RMSNorm inside.
