8. Diffusion language models

A decoder writes 48 tokens in 48 forwards, each waiting for the last. An image diffusion model starts from noise and refines the whole picture at once over a fixed number of steps ({{Diffusion from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9bf0d94e57ce9164}} teaches it for pixels). A diffusion language model does the same for text with masking as the noise: start from a row of [MASK] tokens and fill in several per forward, in any order. Here is ours (the reference tiny GPT adapted into one, below) writing the same 48-token continuation of the running sequence's opening that the causal model writes token by token. "Token positions processed" counts what each forward reads: the causal model with a KV cache processes each new token once after a prefill of the prompt; every diffusion step reads the prompt and all 48 answer slots, because nothing can be cached between steps.

How it works: masked diffusion from zero

  1. Forward process (the noise). Pick a time t between 0 and 1 and hide each token independently with probability t: clean at t = 0, all [MASK] at t = 1.
  2. The model is a bidirectional transformer (an encoder) that reads the partly masked sequence and predicts the original token at every masked position.
  3. The loss (LLaDA, {{Nie et al., arXiv 2502.09992|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992}}): cross-entropy on the masked positions only, each weighted by 1/t, averaged over the sequence length. The weight makes the sum an upper bound on the negative log-likelihood; MDLM ({{Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524}}) calls it "a mixture of classical masked language modeling losses". Fix t = 0.15 and you have BERT; draw t at random and the model learns every stage from nearly clean to fully masked.
  4. Sampling (the reverse process), what the figure steps through: start with the prompt followed by L masks; each step, predict every masked position, commit some and leave the rest masked. LLaDA commits the most confident ones (low-confidence remasking). With L tokens and K steps it commits L/K tokens per forward.

Why 1/t: at rate t the expected number of masked tokens is tL; dividing by t makes every rate contribute a comparable per-sequence term, and the result bounds the likelihood, so training it is maximum-likelihood training of a generative model rather than a fixed-rate MLM. The bound is not a perplexity: our adapted model's and the causal model's 2.28 are not the same kind of number.

Measured: the reference tiny GPT turned into a diffusion LM

Adapting a trained AR model is how DiffuLLaMA ({{Gong et al., ICLR 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17891}}: GPT-2 and LLaMA from 127M to 7B, under 200B tokens) and Dream 7B ({{HKU blog, April 2025|https://hkunlp.github.io/blog/2025/dream/}}: "initialized with weights from Qwen2.5 7B", 580B tokens) were built; LLaDA 8B was trained from scratch on 2.3T tokens with 0.13 million H800 GPU hours. We added a [MASK] token to the reference checkpoint's embedding, switched the mask to bidirectional and trained with the LLaDA loss for steps of 16 windows ( at learning rate 1e-3, then the rest at 5e-4; src/lab/train_diffusion.py, CPU, minutes in all). Committing more tokens per forward from the same, partly masked context lowers quality. Random remasking instead of confidence order is clearly worse at 48 steps ( against ), as in LLaDA's own ablation, but at 12 steps it is better here ( against ): committing the 4 most confident slots at once tends to pick the same easy pieces in neighbouring places, which random order avoids. LLaDA's table is at one step per token; this toy does not test it at few steps. The tiny model is small and briefly adapted: read the ordering, not the gap to AR.

Real bad output: parallel commits do not coordinate

The causal model continues prompt 7 with "". The diffusion model at 12 steps writes "". Our tokenizer splits the name Roxy into pieces (R, o, x, y), and each step predicts every masked slot independently given the current context, so slots filled in the same step cannot agree: the name comes out as fragments ("R", "Ro", "Roy") and confident common pieces land side by side ("named named"). This conditional-independence cost grows with tokens per step, which is why fewer steps lose quality and why production systems commit in small blocks with confidence thresholds.

What it costs

Every diffusion step is a full bidirectional forward over prompt plus answer, and nothing can be cached between steps because every position's keys change as tokens are committed ("LLaDA is incompatible with KV caching"). So diffusion trades more FLOPs for fewer sequential forwards: worth it when decoding is memory-bound (one user, small batch), not when a server is already compute-bound at large batch. For the 48-token continuation of an -token prompt, 12 diffusion steps process token positions against the AR decoder's (derived; the counter in the figure). Block diffusion ({{Arriola et al., ICLR 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09573}}) fixes the caching problem: generate blocks left to right, diffuse inside each block, cache the finished ones. DiffusionGemma uses the same block-AR idea; its {{paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d818390bfd7969757211e}} runs that sampler on its own toy.

Published results

  • LLaDA (vendor, arXiv v3, 2025-10-18): 8B "competitive with strong LLMs like LLaMA3 8B in in-context learning"; low-confidence remasking beats random on every benchmark of its Table 9, by 12.9 (BBH) to 48.7 (GSM8K) points; on reversal poem completion LLaDA 8B Instruct scores 51.8 forward and 45.6 reversed against GPT-4o's 82.7 and 34.3: an order-free model suffers less from the reversal curse. Throughput 1.5x and 1.8x LLaMA3 8B Base on GSM8K and Math at comparable quality.
  • Mercury (Inception Labs, {{arXiv 2506.17298|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17298}}, June 2025): Mercury Coder Mini 1,109 and Small 737 tokens/s on H100s, independent (Artificial Analysis, as cited by the authors). The current line, Mercury 3 (19 October 2026), is API-only with a 1M-token context (Mercury 2.5: 260K), 1,068 tokens/s on NVIDIA GPUs and $0.20 / $0.70 per million input and output tokens (vendor, {{Inception|https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/introducing-mercury-3}}); Inception says it beats GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 "across a range of benchmarks" but published no scores, and no independent index listed it at release: a speed figure with no quality figure beside it.
  • Gemini Diffusion (Google DeepMind, {{model page|https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-diffusion/}}, announced 2025-05-20, an experimental demo): vendor 1,479 tokens/s excluding 0.84 s overhead; level with Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite on code, well behind on GPQA Diamond (40.4 against 56.5).
  • DiffusionGemma ({{paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d818390bfd7969757211e}}, weights 2026-06-09, report August 2026): Gemma 4 26B A4B warm-started from AR weights with under 10% of the AR token budget; 19.74 tokens per forward on average, 1,479 tokens/s on one H100 at one request (vendor; 7.2x plain AR, 4.9x Gemma 4 with MTP at 1.40 tokens per forward), and a quality tax: 73.2 against 82.3 on GPQA-Diamond. The paper page puts the crossover where batched AR serving wins at about 32 concurrent users, so a tokens-per-second figure means nothing without its batch size. (The 1,479 matches Gemini Diffusion's figure digit for digit; both are as printed by their sources.)

The adaptation run

Held-out loss bound on 64 windows with a fixed masking draw, every 100 steps of train_diffusion.py --shift: the reference checkpoint plus one [MASK] embedding row, bidirectional mask, LLaDA loss, AdamW, 16 windows of 256 per step, CPU: steps at lr 1e-3 (50 warmup steps, cosine decay), then 3,000 more at 5e-4 on new windows (--resume). The page's diffusion results use the final model; the 1,500-step model's are in src/lab/out/diffusion_eval.json.
