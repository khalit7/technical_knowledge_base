10. Choosing

  • You need to generate open-ended text, follow instructions or reason: a decoder-only model. If it ships MTP weights, use them as the draft; if you pretrain one yourself at billions of parameters, a depth-1 sequential MTP loss is a cheap addition (one block, λ around 0.1 to 0.3) that you can drop at inference.
  • You need vectors or labels for a lot of text, cheaply: an encoder (ModernBERT-class, 150M to 400M), fine-tuned for the task. At equal size and data it beats a decoder used as an embedder (Ettin).
  • The input is long, the output short and tied to it (translation, summarisation, speech): consider an encoder-decoder, ideally adapted from a decoder you trust (T5Gemma 2); an unbalanced large encoder with a small decoder buys quality at small-decoder latency.
  • One user, latency first, quality tax acceptable: a block-diffusion LM. At high concurrency a batched AR server with speculative decoding is the better use of the GPU.

Common mistakes, with the real symptoms

Running a causal checkpoint with a different mask
Held-out loss of the reference tiny GPT goes from to with a bidirectional mask and with a 128-token prefix (section 1). A prefix LM or encoder has to be trained or adapted as one.
Forgetting the causal mask while training on next tokens
Training loss nats after 150 steps, then greedy output "" (section 3). With F.scaled_dot_product_attention, is_causal defaults to False.
Adapting an AR model to diffusion without the shift
Held-out bound instead of after 100 steps (section 8): the adapted model spends its budget relearning which position predicts which token.
Replacing every masked token with [MASK]
The model never sees [MASK] again at fine-tuning time, so the representation it learned at scored positions does not match what it gets. BERT's 80/10/10 rule exists for this; transformers' collator keeps it as the default (mask_replace_prob=0.8, random_replace_prob=0.1).
Counting the MTP layer in the model size, or expecting it to load
DeepSeek-V3 is 671.0B in the paper and 684.5B on the Hub (13.46B MTP layer with its own embedding and head copies, plus 41.5M FP8 scales). transformers 5.20.1's DeepSeek-V3 class drops model.layers.61.* at load, so a "671B" model in memory is correct and the MTP draft is not there.
Judging an extra objective at equal tokens instead of equal compute
Our MTP run beat the reference by nats at equal tokens; a baseline given the same training FLOPs beat the MTP run by (section 7). An auxiliary module that adds compute must be compared at equal compute.
Reading a diffusion LM's loss as a perplexity
The 1/t-weighted loss is an upper bound on the negative log-likelihood, not the likelihood itself: our adapted model's and the causal model's 2.28 are not the same kind of number. Compare generations with one judge, as section 8 does, or compute the bound for both.
Quoting a diffusion model's tokens per second without the batch size
The 1,479 tokens/s of DiffusionGemma is at one request per GPU; the paper page's crossover, past which batched AR serving wins, is about 32 users. Diffusion spends extra FLOPs to save sequential steps, which only pays while decoding is memory-bound.
Sampling with an MTP draft and accepting by equality
Exact-match acceptance is correct only for greedy decoding (our runs: output identical to plain greedy, ). At temperature above 0 the speculative acceptance rule (accept with probability min(1, p/q), resample the remainder) is needed to keep the model's distribution; see {{Speculative decoding|n:3f35c17b0d0d81e086fde85940ebac1c}}.

Interview questions

Why did decoder-only models win over encoder-decoders?
One objective that makes every token a training example and every task a prompt; the strongest zero-shot generalisation after unsupervised pretraining (Wang et al. 2022); one stack, one cache, simple batching and prefix caching. Encoder-decoders still win input-heavy tasks at equal compute (T5 Table 2).
Why can't you generate well with BERT?
It was trained to fill a few blanks given almost all of the context; generation starts with no right context at all and needs many tokens committed in a sensible order. A masked diffusion model is the same architecture trained at every masking rate, which is exactly what fixes this.
An encoder-decoder has twice the parameters of a decoder. Does it cost twice as much to run?
No: each token passes through one stack, so at equal total length the FLOPs are about the same (T5 Table 2: 2P parameters, M FLOPs for both). Memory for weights doubles; the decoder caches its own KV plus cross-attention KV computed once from the encoder.
What does DeepSeek-V3's MTP module see, and why does that matter for drafting?
The main model's last hidden state at i and the embedding of the true token i+1; one block and the shared head predict i+2. Knowing i+1 makes its guess much better than a parallel head's (measured here: top-1 against ), so as a draft it is accepted often (85 to 90% in DeepSeek's report) and gives up to 2 tokens per forward.
With a depth-1 draft accepted with probability a, how many tokens per forward?
1 + a for one draft token per forward (each forward always yields the verified token, plus the draft when accepted); DeepSeek's 0.85 to 0.9 gives 1.85 to 1.9, close to their reported 1.8x TPS. With chained depth-k drafts accepted independently, 1 + a + a² + ... + ak.
Why is the masked diffusion loss weighted by 1/t?
At rate t the expected number of masked tokens is tL; dividing by t makes every rate contribute a comparable per-sequence term, and the result is an upper bound on the negative log-likelihood (LLaDA, MDLM), so training it is maximum-likelihood training of a generative model rather than a fixed-rate MLM.
Design: classify 50 million support tickets a day and draft a reply for the 1% that need a human.
A fine-tuned encoder (ModernBERT-base class, about 150M parameters) for the classification: one forward per ticket, batched on CPUs or one small GPU, no generation. A decoder-only model only for the 500k drafts, with MTP or a draft model for latency, prompted with the classifier's label and retrieved replies (an encoder again, as the embedder). Using the decoder for both would cost hundreds of times more per ticket for worse classification at equal size (Ettin).
Design: code completion in an editor, one user, under 100 ms for 30 tokens on one GPU.
Decode is memory-bound at batch 1, so cut sequential forwards. Options: an AR model with its MTP heads or an EAGLE-style draft (1.5 to 3x, exact distribution, easy to serve); a block-diffusion model (DiffusionGemma reports about 20 tokens per forward at one request, with a quality tax); or a smaller model. Measure acceptance on real completions first: code is repetitive, so drafts are accepted often. Revisit at high concurrency, where batching beats both.
Design: you are pretraining a 30B decoder. Would you add MTP, and how would you know it helped?
Yes, depth 1, sequential, λ about 0.3 then 0.1, shared embedding and head: about one extra block of compute per step and no inference cost. Know it helped by an equal-compute ablation (not equal tokens: the module adds FLOPs) at two scales on code, maths and knowledge benchmarks, several seeds or at least the noise of reruns, and the draft acceptance rate as a bonus. Our tiny run shows why equal compute matters: the module added FLOPs per step.
