5. Encoder-decoder: T5 to T5Gemma 2

A bidirectional encoder reads the input once; a causal decoder writes the output, and in every block a cross-attention layer lets each output position read every encoder vector (the encoder-decoder view of the mask figure in section 1). The input can be long and fully bidirectional while generation stays causal.

Twice the parameters, the same FLOPs. T5 compared architectures at equal compute (Table 2 of {{T5|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}): a 12 + 12 layer encoder-decoder has twice the parameters (2P) of a 12-layer decoder yet the same FLOPs (M) over an input plus target of the same total length, because each token passes through only one 12-layer stack:

Published, T5 Table 2 (arXiv 1910.10683; values as printed in the T5 page's tables.json), 220M-class models, one run each; the T5 page puts the paper's run-to-run noise at a fraction of these gaps. With fine-tuning on understanding-heavy benchmarks, bidirectional reading of the input was worth about 7 to 8 GLUE points over a causal decoder in 2019.

Why decoders won anyway. Wang et al. 2022 ({{arXiv 2204.05832|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05832}}, models over 5B parameters on 170B tokens) found that after purely unsupervised pretraining causal decoders have the strongest zero-shot generalisation, while non-causal MLM models win only after multitask fine-tuning. A general assistant answers anything zero-shot and writes long outputs from short prompts, and one stack makes caching, batching and serving simpler.

Where encoder-decoders still win: long input, short output tied to it: translation, summarisation, speech recognition (Whisper: "We chose an encoder-decoder Transformer", {{Radford et al. 2022, section 2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356}}). T5Gemma and T5Gemma 2 ({{paper page|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}) build encoder-decoders by copying a pretrained decoder's weights into both stacks and continuing with UL2 or PrefixLM: after instruction tuning the 2B-2B beats Gemma 2 2B by 7.1 points (46.1 against 39.0); the unbalanced 9B-2B decodes at about 1.05x Gemma 2 2B's latency with much higher accuracy on GSM8K; T5Gemma 2's 4B-4B scores 81.7 on RULER 32K against Gemma 3 4B's 66.8 although pretrained at 16K (57.6 against 51.7 at 128K). The paper page's verdict: the direction is believable, the sizes are single runs from models with about twice the parameters.

6. Prefix LM and mixtures of objectives (UL2)

A prefix LM is a decoder with the prefix mask of section 1: prompt read bidirectionally, continuation causally, only the continuation scored. It buys most of the encoder-decoder's gain with one stack (T5 Table 2: 81.82 against 83.28 GLUE; the causal LM 74.70). The price is at inference: the prompt's keys and values depend on the whole prompt, so a prompt cannot be extended without recomputing its cache, which breaks the multi-turn and prefix-caching tricks serving relies on ({{Batching, KV cache and scheduling|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}). And it has to be trained as one: our causal checkpoint run with a 128-token prefix got worse even on the scored suffix (section 1). A fine-tuning-time variant keeps the causal model and adds a bidirectional read: {{Bitune|n:3ce5c17b0d0d8173af14ed0caa19d6db}} reads the prompt twice, causally and bidirectionally with an adapter each, mixes the two sets of keys and values per block with learned weights and still generates causally (+0.1 to +4.0 points over LoRA on five 2B to 8B models, for about 3 times the fine-tuning time).

UL2 ({{Tay et al., arXiv 2205.05131|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05131}}) mixes three denoisers in one run, each announced by a mode token: R (regular span corruption, mean spans 3 and 8 at 15%), S (sequential: one span covering the last quarter of the input, which is prefix LM), X (extreme: spans of 64 tokens or corruption at 50%). Its 20B model reported beating 175B GPT-3 on zero-shot SuperGLUE (vendor, 2022). UL2 survives as the adaptation objective of T5Gemma.
