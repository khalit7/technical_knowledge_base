4. Encoders: BERT to ModernBERT, and what they are for now

The problem they solve. Many jobs never generate text: turn a query and a million documents into vectors and find the nearest ones, label a support ticket, score whether a passage answers a question. For those, the best representation of each token uses the words on both sides of it, and the model needs one forward pass, not a decoding loop.

How. An encoder is the same block stack with a bidirectional mask, trained with MLM. Its output is one vector per token, each conditioned on the whole input. A task head reads them: a pooled vector (the first token's, or the mean) for classification and {{embedding models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81508f99e1fa78c2bb41}}; a relevance score over a concatenated query and document for a cross-encoder {{reranker|n:3f85c17b0d0d813fba53fc883f8a2c8f}}; per-token labels for tagging.

Measured here: both sides win, once the model has learned to use them. The masked diffusion model of section 8 is, at a fixed rate, an MLM encoder adapted from the reference tiny GPT. We hid a fixed random 15%, 50% or 90% of 16 held-out windows and asked it to recover them in one bidirectional pass; the causal reference predicted the same positions from the true left context (it is allowed every real token before the blank, which the encoder is not):

At 15% hidden, where the encoder sees nearly all of both sides, it now beats the causal model clearly; after the first adaptation steps it did not ( against the causal ): turning right-hand context into accuracy has to be learned, and the encoder gets loss terms on only a share of positions. The published gap at full pretraining points the same way (BERT Table 5 below: 87.9 against 77.8 F1 on SQuAD for the same model trained left to right). At 90% hidden the order reverses: the encoder sees almost nothing while the causal model still sees the true past. That is why MLM masks a small share, and why a diffusion LM's first steps, with almost everything masked, are its least certain.

The lineage, in four steps

  • BERT (2018): MLM at 15% plus next sentence prediction; the BERT page's verdict treats NSP as unproven (one run, reversed by RoBERTa) and the bidirectional gain as real: 87.9 against 77.8 F1 on SQuAD dev for the same model trained left to right (BERT Table 5, {{BERT paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e5ad9bd09cbf18ad7c}}).
  • RoBERTa ({{Liu et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692}}): dropped NSP, more data and longer training, dynamic masking.
  • ModernBERT ({{Warner et al., arXiv 2412.13663|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13663}}, December 2024): the decoder era's parts brought back into an encoder: RoPE, GeGLU, pre-norm LayerNorm without biases, alternating attention (every third layer global, the others a 128-token sliding window, RoPE theta 160,000 on global layers and 10,000 on local ones), MLM at 30% with no NSP, 2 trillion training tokens at native context 8,192; base 22 layers and about 149M parameters, large 28 layers and about 395M. vendor: about twice DeBERTaV3's speed on short inputs (about 148k against 70k tokens/s at 512 tokens on an RTX 4090). transformers 5.20.1 builds the pattern from global_attn_every_n_layers (default 3) and halves local_attention (128) into a 64-token window on each side (configuration_modernbert.py lines and ).
  • Ettin ({{Seq vs Seq paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81139cffea35080afe2b}}, July 2025): encoders and decoders trained in pairs on the same data with the same recipe from 17M to 1B. Encoders win classification and retrieval, decoders win generation, at every size; 50B tokens of continued training on the other objective does not close either gap. The 400M encoder reaches 91.3 on MNLI against the 1B decoder's 89.9.

Cost and when it matters. An encoder forward costs about 2N FLOPs per token (N parameters), like a decoder's prefill, with no KV cache to hold afterwards; at 149M parameters it embeds a document chunk in one pass on a CPU. The comparison that matters in 2026 is a small encoder against a decoder used as an embedder: Ettin's result says that at matched size and data the encoder is the better embedder and classifier, and size buys the decoder its lead back only at large ratios (the paper page puts the largest ratio a native model wins at 2.7x, against the abstract's "10x").

How to check a model is an encoder. Its config's architectures names a ...ForMaskedLM or ...Model class rather than ...ForCausalLM; its attention class sets is_causal = False; and its tokenizer has a [MASK] (or <mask>) token. Generating from it with model.generate either fails or produces filler.

5. Encoder-decoder: T5 to T5Gemma 2

The idea. Split the work: a bidirectional encoder reads the input once; a causal decoder writes the output, and in every block a cross-attention layer lets each output position read every encoder vector. Inputs and outputs live in separate stacks, so the input can be long and fully bidirectional while generation stays causal.

The cost, from T5's own table. T5 compared architectures at equal compute (Table 2 of {{T5|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}). An encoder-decoder with 12 + 12 layers has twice the parameters (2P) of a 12-layer decoder, yet the same FLOPs (M) over an input plus target of the same total length, because each token passes through only one 12-layer stack:

With fine-tuning on these understanding-heavy benchmarks, bidirectional reading of the input was worth about 7 to 8 GLUE points over a causal decoder in 2019.

Why decoders won anyway. Wang et al. 2022 ({{arXiv 2204.05832|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05832}}, models over 5B parameters trained on 170B tokens) found that after purely unsupervised pretraining, causal decoders trained with next-token prediction have the strongest zero-shot generalisation, while non-causal models trained with MLM win only after multitask fine-tuning. For a general assistant that answers anything zero-shot, and that must generate long outputs from short prompts, the causal decoder is the better default; its single stack also makes caching, batching and serving simpler.

Where encoder-decoders still win. When the input is long and the output is short or tightly tied to it: translation, summarisation, speech recognition (Whisper: "We chose an encoder-decoder Transformer", {{Radford et al. 2022, section 2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356}}). The encoder runs once; each decoding step then reads the input through cross-attention keys and values computed once.

T5Gemma and T5Gemma 2 ({{paper page|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}) build encoder-decoders by copying a pretrained decoder's weights into both stacks and continuing training with UL2 or PrefixLM. After instruction tuning the 2B-2B beats Gemma 2 2B by 7.1 points (46.1 against 39.0), the unbalanced 9B-2B decodes at about 1.05x Gemma 2 2B's latency with much higher accuracy on GSM8K, and T5Gemma 2's 4B-4B scores 81.7 on RULER 32K against Gemma 3 4B's 66.8 although pretrained at 16K (the lead narrows to 57.6 against 51.7 at 128K). The paper page's verdict: the direction is believable, the sizes are single runs from models with about twice the parameters.

6. Prefix LM and mixtures of objectives (UL2)

A prefix LM is a decoder-only model with the prefix mask of section 1: the prompt is read bidirectionally, the continuation causally, and only the continuation is scored. It buys most of the encoder-decoder's gain with one stack (T5 Table 2: 81.82 against 83.28 GLUE, against the causal LM's 74.70). The price shows at inference: the prompt's keys and values depend on the whole prompt, so a prompt cannot be extended without recomputing its cache, which breaks the multi-turn and prefix-caching tricks that serving systems rely on ({{Batching, KV cache and scheduling|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}).

UL2 ({{Tay et al., arXiv 2205.05131|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05131}}) mixes three denoisers in one pretraining run, each announced by a mode token: R (regular span corruption, mean spans 3 and 8 at 15%), S (sequential: one span covering the last quarter of the input, which is prefix LM), X (extreme: spans of 64 tokens or corruption at 50%). Its 20B model reported beating 175B GPT-3 on zero-shot SuperGLUE (vendor, 2022). UL2 survives as the adaptation objective of T5Gemma.

Check it. The {{Mask lab|#t-mask}} shows the prefix mask; on our causal checkpoint, switching to a 128-token prefix at inference raised the loss of the scored suffix from to (section 1): a prefix LM has to be trained as one.
