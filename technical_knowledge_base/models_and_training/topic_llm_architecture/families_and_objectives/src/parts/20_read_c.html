How this happens in real code
Writing your own attention with F.scaled_dot_product_attention(q, k, v) and forgetting is_causal=True (the default is False); passing a padding mask and assuming it also makes attention causal; or loading a decoder checkpoint into an encoder class. The symptom is always the same: a training loss far below anything plausible for the data (here  nats per token on stories whose honest loss is about 2.3), then nonsense at generation. A loss that is too good is a bug until proven otherwise.







4. Encoders: both sides of every token


Many jobs never generate text: embed a query and a million documents and find the nearest, label a ticket, score whether a passage answers a question. For those the best representation of each token uses the words on both sides, in one forward pass. An encoder is the same stack with a bidirectional mask, trained with MLM; a task head reads its per-token vectors: a pooled vector for classification and {{embedding models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81508f99e1fa78c2bb41}}, a relevance score over a concatenated query and document for a cross-encoder {{reranker|n:3f85c17b0d0d813fba53fc883f8a2c8f}}, per-token labels for tagging.


Measured: both sides win, once the model has learned to use them. The masked diffusion model of section 8 is, at a fixed rate, an MLM encoder adapted from the reference tiny GPT. We hid a fixed random 15%, 50% or 90% of 16 held-out windows and asked it to recover them in one bidirectional pass; the causal reference predicted the same positions from the true left context (every real token before the blank, which the encoder is not given). Predict: at 90% hidden, which wins?


Both sides beat the left side only when most of both sides is visible


Top-1 accuracy on the hidden positions, measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), src/lab/diff_eval.py, after  adaptation steps.


At 15% hidden the encoder beats the causal model clearly; at 90% the order reverses, because the encoder sees almost nothing while the causal model still sees the true past. That is why MLM masks a small share, and why a diffusion LM's first steps, with almost everything masked, are its least certain. Turning right-hand context into accuracy has to be learned: after the first  adaptation steps the encoder did not yet win at 15% ( against the causal ). At full pretraining the gap is the same sign: BERT Table 5 gives 87.9 against 77.8 F1 on SQuAD dev for the same model trained left to right.


What changed since BERT. RoBERTa dropped next-sentence prediction (which the {{BERT paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e5ad9bd09cbf18ad7c}} treats as unproven: one run, reversed by RoBERTa) and trained longer on more data with dynamic masking. ModernBERT (December 2024) brought the decoder era's parts into an encoder: RoPE, GeGLU, alternating global and 128-token sliding-window layers, MLM at 30%, 2 trillion tokens at native context 8,192; base about 149M parameters, large about 395M. Ettin ({{Seq vs Seq paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81139cffea35080afe2b}}, July 2025) trained encoders and decoders in pairs on the same data and recipe from 17M to 1B: encoders win classification and retrieval, decoders win generation, at every size, and 50B tokens of continued training on the other objective closes neither gap (the 400M encoder reaches 91.3 on MNLI against the 1B decoder's 89.9).


Cost and how to check. An encoder forward costs about 2N FLOPs per token (N parameters), like a decoder's prefill, with no KV cache afterwards; at 149M parameters it embeds a chunk in one pass on a CPU. Size buys a decoder-as-embedder its lead back only at large ratios (the Ettin paper page puts the largest ratio a native model wins at 2.7x, against the abstract's "10x"). An encoder's config names a ...ForMaskedLM or ...Model class rather than ...ForCausalLM, its attention sets is_causal = False, and its tokenizer has a [MASK] (or <mask>) token; model.generate either fails or produces filler.
