3. Collapse and the three ways to balance

The problem. An expert chosen slightly more often gets more gradient, improves, and is chosen more often still; the others starve and, since unchosen experts get no gradient (section 2), never show that they were worth reviving. Two things go wrong at once. Quality: the model's effective size shrinks to the experts that survived. Systems: with experts on different GPUs the busiest one sets the step time, and with fixed buffers its overflow is dropped (section 4).

Fix 1: an auxiliary load-balancing loss (Switch, 2021)

ℒaux = α · E · ∑i=1E fi Pi
fi
fraction of the batch's tokens dispatched to expert i (a count: no gradient)
Pi
mean router probability given to expert i over the batch (differentiable)
α
coefficient: Switch used 10−2, after a sweep from 10−1 to 10−5 "found 10−2 balanced load quickly without interfering with training loss" ({{Switch|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a8b541c9babbf40c94}}, eq. 4 to 6)
E
the number of experts: under uniform routing ∑ fiPi = 1/E, so the factor keeps the loss at α whatever E is

The product is smallest when both vectors are uniform. Because f has no gradient, the loss does not move tokens; it lowers the router probability of experts that are both popular and confident, and the top-k then follows. Worked example: E = 4 collapsed onto expert 1, f = (1, 0, 0, 0), P = (0.7, 0.1, 0.1, 0.1): ℒ = α × 4 × 0.7 = 2.8α, against α when uniform. Its cost is that it is a second objective: wherever a token genuinely belongs with one expert, the balancing gradient pushes the other way. The tiny GPT uses this loss with α = 0.01, f counted over the n · k assignments ([[ref:tg_aux]], added to the loss at [[ref:tg_aux_add]]).

A variant: balance devices, not experts. DeepSeekMoE kept a small expert-level loss "to mitigate the risk of routing collapse" and added a larger device-level one, the same f · P product over groups of experts that share a device, because "it becomes unnecessary to enforce strict balance constraints at the expert level ... our primary objective is to ensure balanced computation across the devices" ({{DeepSeekMoE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066}}, eq. 15 to 17). DeepSeek-V2 used three such losses, expert-level, device-level and communication balance, with coefficients 0.003, 0.05 and 0.02 ({{DeepSeek-V2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434}}, sections 2.2.3 and 3.1.2); V3 replaced them with the bias below.

Checked: transformers' version is not the per-layer Switch loss

load_balancing_loss_func (Qwen3-MoE, Mixtral, and the models that copy them, [[ref:q3_lbl_def]]) adds the counts and probabilities of all layers together before multiplying ([[ref:q3_lbl_count]] to [[ref:q3_lbl_out]]), and divides counts by tokens, not by tokens × k. Measured on hand-made logits (8 experts, top 2, src/code/hf_aux_check.py): perfectly balanced routing returns [[f:hf_bal|f2]], not 1; one layer collapsed onto two experts returns [[f:hf_one|f2]]; two layers each collapsed but onto different experts return [[f:hf_two_diff|f2]], half as bad, because pooled over layers the traffic looks spread. The per-layer Switch loss gives [[f:sw_one|f2]] for every collapsed layer. And the loss only exists if you ask for it: output_router_logits defaults to False ([[ref:q3_cfg_orl]]) and the released Qwen3, Mixtral, gpt-oss and Llama 4 configs keep it off, so a plain fine-tune with Trainer adds no balancing loss at all ([[ref:q3_aux_gate]]).

Fix 2: the router z-loss (ST-MoE, 2022), which balances nothing

Often listed with balancing, it solves a different problem: router logits that grow large, where bfloat16 roundoff in the exponential causes loss spikes. ℒz = (1/B) ∑tokens (log ∑j exj)2, coefficient cz = 0.001 "based on the best model quality after pre-training with a hyperparameter sweep". In ST-MoE's Table 4 the baseline was stable in 4 of 6 runs, update clipping in 3 of 3 at a large quality cost (−4.206 against −1.755), and the z-loss in 3 of 3 with a slight gain (−1.741) ({{ST-MoE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08906}}, section 3.3). Switch had already computed the router in float32 inside a bfloat16 model ("selective precision"); transformers' DeepSeek-V3 router still does ([[ref:ds_fp32]]).

Fix 3: a per-expert bias and no loss (DeepSeek, 2024)

Add a bias bi to expert i's score for the top-k choice only; the gate weight uses the score without it. After each step, lower the bias of overloaded experts and raise it for underloaded ones:

select by si + bi;   weight by si;   bi ← bi + γ · sign(c̄ − ci)
ci
tokens expert i received in the step; c̄ their mean over experts
γ
"bias update speed": 0.001 for DeepSeek-V3's first 14.3T tokens and 0 for the last 500B ({{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}, sections 2.1.2 and 4.2)

The bias never enters the output, so it never enters the gradient: balancing moves the decision boundary instead of adding a competing objective. V3 still kept a sequence-wise loss of the Switch form with α = 0.0001, "just to avoid extreme imbalance within any single sequence", and "does not drop any tokens during training". The published evidence is DeepSeek's own (vendor): on 1B and 3B MoE models, validation perplexity 9.56 to 9.50 and 7.97 to 7.92 with MaxVio 0.72 to 0.04 and 0.52 to 0.04 ({{Wang et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664}}, Table 2), and in V3's Table 5 better scores "on most" benchmarks: the large model's MMLU went the other way (68.3 to 67.2). The tiny GPT's version is [[ref:tg_sel]] and [[ref:tg_bias]] (γ = 0.001, every training step).

Checked: in transformers the bias is only loaded, never updated

DeepSeek-V3's router in transformers 5.20.1 stores e_score_correction_bias as a buffer ([[ref:ds_bias_buf]]), adds it at selection ([[ref:ds_choice]]) and keeps it in float32 ([[ref:ds_keep32]]), but no line of modeling_deepseek_v3.py updates it and the file computes no auxiliary loss. The same holds for GLM-4.5 ([[ref:glm_sigmoid]]), Nemotron-H ([[ref:nh_sigmoid]]) and DeepSeek-V4 ([[ref:v4_topk]]). Fine-tuning one of these with plain transformers therefore trains with the pretraining bias frozen and no balancing at all; the training loop has to implement the update itself, as the tiny GPT does.

Over which tokens is balance measured?

A balancing loss computed per micro-batch is close to a per-sequence constraint: a micro-batch holds a few sequences, often from one domain, so even a pure code sequence is pushed to spread over every expert. Qwen's "global-batch" loss synchronises fi across data-parallel ranks so balance is demanded only over the whole, diverse batch; it reports "approximately 0.1 in pre-training PPL and 2 in benchmark scores" better, and domain specialisation that "only clearly emerges when trained with the global-batch LBL" (up to 42.8B parameters and 400B tokens; {{Qiu et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11873}}, vendor, Alibaba). Qwen3 adopted it ({{Qwen3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388}}). DeepSeek reached the same conclusion from the other side: its bias balances per batch, and a batch-wise auxiliary loss matched it (validation loss 2.258 sequence-wise, 2.253 bias, 2.253 batch-wise at 1B; 2.085, 2.080, 2.080 at 3B; {{V3 report|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} section 4.5.3), with two named risks: imbalance "within certain sequences or small batches" and "domain-shift-induced load imbalance during inference".
