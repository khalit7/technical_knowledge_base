3. Collapse, and the two ways to balance

An expert chosen slightly more often gets more gradient, improves, and is chosen more often still; the others starve and, getting no gradient (section 2), never show they were worth reviving. This is routing collapse. It costs quality (the model shrinks to the experts that survived) and time (with experts on different GPUs the busiest one sets the step, and with fixed buffers its overflow is dropped). The tiny GPT's training log records every 10 steps how many of the batch's 16,384 assignments (8,192 tokens × 2) went to each expert; replay it. Predict first: with no balancing at all, how much of a layer's traffic does the busiest expert end up with?

Fix 1: an auxiliary load-balancing loss (Switch, 2021)

Worked example first: E = 4 collapsed onto expert 1, so the dispatch fractions are f = (1, 0, 0, 0) and the mean router probabilities P = (0.7, 0.1, 0.1, 0.1). The loss below is 4 × 0.7 = 2.8 times its coefficient, against 1 times when routing is uniform.

ℒaux = α · E · ∑i=1E fi Pi
fi
fraction of the batch's tokens dispatched to expert i (a count: no gradient)
Pi
mean router probability given to expert i over the batch (differentiable)
α
coefficient: Switch used 10−2 after a sweep from 10−1 to 10−5 "found 10−2 balanced load quickly without interfering with training loss" ({{Switch|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a8b541c9babbf40c94}}, eq. 4 to 6)
E
the number of experts: under uniform routing ∑ fiPi = 1/E, so the factor keeps the loss at α whatever E is

Because f has no gradient, the loss does not move tokens; it lowers the router probability of experts that are both popular and confident, and the top-k follows. Its cost is that it is a second objective: wherever a token genuinely belongs with one expert, the balancing gradient pushes the other way. The tiny GPT uses it with α = 0.01, f counted over the n · k assignments ([[ref:tg_aux]], added to the loss at [[ref:tg_aux_add]]).

Fix 2: a per-expert bias and no loss (DeepSeek, 2024)

Add a bias bi to expert i's score for the top-k choice only; the gate weight uses the score without it. After each step, lower the bias of overloaded experts and raise it for underloaded ones:

select by si + bi;   weight by si;   bi ← bi + γ · sign(c̄ − ci)
ci
tokens expert i received in the step; c̄ their mean over experts
γ
"bias update speed": 0.001 for DeepSeek-V3's first 14.3T tokens and 0 for the last 500B ({{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}, sections 2.1.2 and 4.2)

The bias never enters the output, so it never enters the gradient: balancing moves the decision boundary instead of adding a competing objective. V3 still kept a sequence-wise loss of the Switch form with α = 0.0001, "just to avoid extreme imbalance within any single sequence", and "does not drop any tokens during training". The tiny GPT's version is [[ref:tg_sel]] and [[ref:tg_bias]] (γ = 0.001, every training step).

Over which tokens is balance measured? A loss computed per micro-batch is close to a per-sequence constraint: even a pure code sequence is pushed to spread over every expert. Qwen's global-batch loss synchronises fi across data-parallel ranks, so balance is demanded only over the whole, diverse batch; Qwen3 adopted it ({{Qwen3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388}}). DeepSeek's bias balances per batch too. The cost of batch-level balance is imbalance inside one sequence or a small batch, which comes back at serving time (section 8).

The router z-loss balances nothing. Often listed with balancing, it bounds router logits that grow until bfloat16 roundoff in the exponential causes loss spikes: ℒz = (1/B) ∑tokens (log ∑j exj)2, coefficient cz = 0.001 ({{ST-MoE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08906}}, section 3.3). The same concern is why Switch computed the router in float32 inside a bfloat16 model ("selective precision"), and transformers' DeepSeek-V3 router still does ([[ref:ds_fp32]]).

Checked: fine-tuning with transformers balances nothing by default

Qwen3-MoE, Mixtral, gpt-oss and Llama 4 compute their auxiliary loss only when output_router_logits=True, and their configs ship with it off. DeepSeek-V3, GLM-4.5, Nemotron-H and DeepSeek-V4 load the bias but never update it. A plain fine-tune therefore runs with no balancing at all; log per-expert counts, set the flag, or implement the bias update (details below).

The evidence for the bias and for global-batch balance, device-level losses, and what transformers actually computes (depth, checked)

Bias against loss, published. DeepSeek's own evidence (vendor): on 1B and 3B MoE models, validation perplexity 9.56 to 9.50 and 7.97 to 7.92 with MaxVio 0.72 to 0.04 and 0.52 to 0.04 ({{Wang et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664}}, Table 2); in V3's Table 5 better scores "on most" benchmarks, though the large model's MMLU went the other way (68.3 to 67.2).

Global-batch balance, published. Qwen reports "approximately 0.1 in pre-training PPL and 2 in benchmark scores" better, and domain specialisation that "only clearly emerges when trained with the global-batch LBL" (up to 42.8B parameters and 400B tokens; {{Qiu et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11873}}, vendor, Alibaba), at under 3% extra latency, because the synchronised statistic is one number per expert; {{Alibaba: Qwen|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fd8c36ce06672bb235#s-moe}} has a toy in which the same routing costs four times the minimum per micro-batch and nothing over the global batch. DeepSeek reached the same conclusion from the other side: a batch-wise auxiliary loss matched its bias (validation loss 2.258 sequence-wise, 2.253 bias, 2.253 batch-wise at 1B; 2.085, 2.080, 2.080 at 3B; {{V3 report|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} section 4.5.3), with two named risks: imbalance "within certain sequences or small batches" and "domain-shift-induced load imbalance during inference".

Balance devices, not experts. DeepSeekMoE kept a small expert-level loss "to mitigate the risk of routing collapse" and added a larger device-level one, the same f · P product over groups of experts that share a device, because "it becomes unnecessary to enforce strict balance constraints at the expert level ... our primary objective is to ensure balanced computation across the devices" ({{DeepSeekMoE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06066}}, eq. 15 to 17). DeepSeek-V2 used three such losses, expert-level, device-level and communication balance, with coefficients 0.003, 0.05 and 0.02 ({{DeepSeek-V2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434}}, sections 2.2.3 and 3.1.2); V3 replaced them with the bias.

transformers' version is not the per-layer Switch loss. load_balancing_loss_func (Qwen3-MoE, Mixtral and the models that copy them, [[ref:q3_lbl_def]]) adds the counts and probabilities of all layers together before multiplying ([[ref:q3_lbl_count]] to [[ref:q3_lbl_out]]), and divides counts by tokens, not by tokens × k. Measured on hand-made logits (8 experts, top 2, src/code/hf_aux_check.py): perfectly balanced routing returns [[f:hf_bal|f2]], not 1; one layer collapsed onto two experts returns [[f:hf_one|f2]]; two layers each collapsed but onto different experts return [[f:hf_two_diff|f2]], half as bad, because pooled over layers the traffic looks spread. The per-layer Switch loss gives [[f:sw_one|f2]] for every collapsed layer. A dashboard alarm set at "aux loss above 1" fires on a perfectly balanced model with k = 2. And the loss only exists if asked for: output_router_logits defaults to False ([[ref:q3_cfg_orl]]) and the released Qwen3, Mixtral, gpt-oss and Llama 4 configs keep it off, so a plain fine-tune with Trainer adds no balancing loss ([[ref:q3_aux_gate]]): a tiny Qwen3-MoE returns aux_loss = [[f:hfc_aux_none|s]] by default and [[f:hfc_aux_on|f2]] with the flag (about k = 2, perfect balance under this formula).

In transformers the bias is only loaded, never updated. DeepSeek-V3's router in transformers 5.20.1 stores e_score_correction_bias as a buffer ([[ref:ds_bias_buf]]), adds it at selection ([[ref:ds_choice]]) and keeps it in float32 ([[ref:ds_keep32]]), but no line of modeling_deepseek_v3.py updates it and the file computes no auxiliary loss; three AdamW steps on a tiny DeepSeek-V3 left a non-zero e_score_correction_bias bit for bit unchanged (it is a buffer, not a parameter). The same holds for GLM-4.5 ([[ref:glm_sigmoid]]), Nemotron-H ([[ref:nh_sigmoid]]) and DeepSeek-V4 ([[ref:v4_topk]]). The training loop has to implement the update itself, as the tiny GPT does. (transformers 5.20.1, torch 2.14.1, CPU; src/code/hf_checks.py, hf_aux_check.py.)

z-loss evidence. cz = 0.001 was chosen "based on the best model quality after pre-training with a hyperparameter sweep". In ST-MoE's Table 4 the baseline was stable in 4 of 6 runs, update clipping in 3 of 3 at a large quality cost (−4.206 against −1.755), and the z-loss in 3 of 3 with a slight gain (−1.741).
