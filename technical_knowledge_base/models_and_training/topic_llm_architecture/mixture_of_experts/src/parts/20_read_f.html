6. Dense against MoE at equal active compute, measured

Nine runs of the root's tiny GPT, 1,500 steps of 32 × 256 tokens (12.3M TinyStories tokens) on the same seeded batches, every MoE run at the dense model's active FFN size; held-out loss on the same 20 validation batches throughout.

More stored parameters at the same compute lower the loss; only the fine-grained layout clears the seed noise

measured on Apple M1 Pro (MPS), fp32, torch 2.14.1, shared machine at low priority. Tokens per second are rough (other jobs ran beside these) and say nothing about a GPU.

[[s6_meas]]

What transfers. The direction (more stored parameters at equal active compute lower the loss), that balancing costs little at this scale, and the mechanics (collapse without balancing, drops at small batch, the shared expert's role) transfer. The size of the gap does not: published comparisons at billion scale put an MoE's benefit at roughly the quality of a dense model a few times its active size (DeepSeekMoE 16B "comparable" to LLaMA2 7B "with only about 40% of computations", vendor), and the tiny GPT trains on 12M tokens, where the dense model is far from saturated. Wall-clock speed does not transfer at all.

Protocol: optimiser, the batched forward, and why (depth)

AdamW at lr 3e-3 with cosine decay, fp32 on MPS. The dense reference is the root's ref_baseline_s1500 run. MoE runs go through src/runs/fast_moe.py, the root's train.py with the tiny GPT's per-expert loop replaced by one batched einsum over the experts that received tokens: the same choices, drops, bias updates and gradients (checked on CPU: logits within 2e-6, gradients within 1.2e-6, the same parameters left without gradient), because the loop forces a GPU sync per expert per layer and ran far too slowly on MPS. One seed each except where marked; the root's README notes that MPS reruns differ in the third decimal, and the seed-1 pair gives the spread to read gaps against. Held-out loss is recomputed on CPU from the final checkpoint (20 fixed validation batches, 163,840 tokens); the training log's own final value agrees to the third decimal. The tiny GPT's MoE loops over experts in Python, which a real kernel does not.
