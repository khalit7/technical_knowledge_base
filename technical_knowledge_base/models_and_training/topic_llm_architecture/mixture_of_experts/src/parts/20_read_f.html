7. Expert parallelism and the all-to-all

The problem. DeepSeek-V3's experts are 656.6B of its 671B parameters (98%, derived from its config: 58 layers × 257 experts × 44.04M); no GPU holds them, so they must be spread out, and then every token has to travel to the GPUs that hold its experts.

How it works. Expert parallelism (EP) gives each of W ranks E/W experts of every MoE layer; attention and the other dense parts are replicated or split another way. Each MoE layer then does, on every rank:

  1. route locally: each rank holds a copy of the tiny router and computes top-k for its own tokens: (T, d) → indices (T, k);
  2. exchange counts: a small all-to-all of W integers, so each rank knows how many token copies it will receive;
  3. dispatch: an all-to-all sends each token's hidden state (d numbers) to the rank of each chosen expert: T · k rows leave every rank;
  4. compute: each rank runs its experts on the rows it received (a grouped GEMM on GPUs);
  5. combine: the reverse all-to-all brings outputs home, where they are weighted by the gates and summed; shared experts run at home with no communication.

What it costs. Per token per MoE layer, k · d numbers go out and k · d come back (fewer if the expert is local). DeepSeek-V3 at k = 8, d = 7,168 in BF16: 8 × 7,168 × 2 B = 112 KiB each way per token per layer, 58 layers, forward; the backward pass repeats both. Unlike a gradient all-reduce it cannot be overlapped with the next layer's compute without tricks, every rank talks to every rank, and the slowest rank sets the pace: load imbalance becomes idle GPUs. How the bytes meet the network (NVLink inside a node, InfiniBand across, forwarding through NVLink, why the all-to-all crosses the spine) is owned by {{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}} (All-to-all and MoE, Collective simulator); how EP composes with data, tensor, pipeline and context parallelism in one training step is owned by {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}} (Expert section and Layout calculator).

What the MoE design does about it. The router itself is the lever. DeepSeek-V3 limits each token to experts on at most 4 nodes (the group-limited top-k of section 2) because "NVLink offers a bandwidth of 160 GB/s, roughly 3.2 times that of IB (50 GB/s)", and notes it "can scale up this number to a maximum of 13 experts (4 nodes × 3.2 experts/node) while preserving the same communication cost" ({{V3 report|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}, section 3.2.2). Its training used 64-way EP over 8 nodes, its {{DualPipe|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DualPipe}} schedule overlaps the all-to-alls of one micro-batch with the compute of another, and its kernels are open as {{DeepEP|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepEP}} (dispatch and combine over NVLink and RDMA, with a low-latency mode for decoding). Latent experts (section 5) shrink what is sent.
