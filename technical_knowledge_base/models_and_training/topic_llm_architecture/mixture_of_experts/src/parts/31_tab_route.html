Router choices recorded from the trained tiny-GPT MoE models on the root's 256-token running sequence ("Once upon a time, in a big forest, there lived a rhinoceros named Roxy..."), never seen in training. Every number on this tab comes from one forward pass of a real checkpoint (src/analysis/analyse.py, measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU)).

The same 8 tokens through the router, with and without balancing

Tokens 1 to 8 of the running sequence, one MoE layer, eight experts, top 2. The three models were trained identically except for how they balance. Step through it: scores, choices, dispatch, the load each expert gets, what a capacity factor of 1.25 would drop from this small batch, and how outputs are combined. Switching model or layer resets the steps; everything is drawn on the same scale.

Capacity on this 8-token batch: ⌈1.25 × 8 × 2 / 8⌉ = 3 slots per expert (the tiny GPT rounds up, [[ref:tg_cap]]). The trained models themselves ran dropless except the capacity-factor run; drops here show what the rule would do to these 8 tokens. Gate weights are the renormalised scores of the two chosen experts.

The whole running sequence, coloured by its first-choice expert

Each piece of text is coloured by the expert the router ranked first for it in the chosen layer (Mixtral's Figure 8 does the same for its real model). Tap a piece to see both experts and their weights in every layer.

What each expert takes, on held-out text

For the chosen model and layer: the tokens each expert most often receives as first choice over 5 held-out batches (40,960 tokens), with the share of that token's occurrences that went there. Determinism is the fraction of tokens whose first-choice expert is the one most common for their token id: 1 would mean routing is a lookup on the token id alone, 1/8 would mean it ignores it.
