1. Total and active: more weights, the same compute per token

In a dense transformer every token multiplies through every weight, about 2 FLOPs per parameter per token, and the FFN holds most of them: one tiny-GPT block has 262,144 attention weights (four 256 × 256 projections) and 524,288 FFN weights (256 × 1,024 up, 1,024 × 256 down), two thirds of the block. MoE splits that FFN into experts. The root's tiny GPT (d = 256, 6 blocks, GELU) has MoE presets that keep the active FFN exactly the dense size, so comparisons on this page are at equal compute (presets in the root's src/data/configs.py):

Per layer, moe8top2 stores 8 × (2 × 256 × 512) = 2,097,152 expert weights plus a 256 × 8 = 2,048-weight router; each expert is half the dense FFN, so a token's 2 experts (524,288) cost what the dense FFN costs. moe16top3_shared: 17 experts of 2 × 256 × 256 = 131,072 plus a 4,096-weight router; a token uses 4 of them. Drawn to scale: compute stays at 16 squares while storage grows fourfold

The same move on a real model, step by step: build the dense twin (one FFN as wide as the k routed experts plus the shared ones a token uses), then add the other experts and watch which of the five numbers move. Predict before stepping: which grows, FLOPs per token or weight memory, and what happens to the bytes a decode step reads when 64 sequences share the step?

Adding experts moves memory, not compute, until the batch grows

For a dense model the MoE version splits each FFN into 8 experts with 2 active (the Mixtral pattern). Moved here from the root's Config calculator on 2026-10-23.

y = h + ∑i ∈ TopK(s(h)) gi(h) · Ei(h) + ∑j Sj(h)
h
the token's hidden state entering the FFN sub-layer (after the norm), d numbers; the leading h is the residual connection
s(h)
router scores, one per expert, from logits Wr h (Wr is E × d)
TopK
the k experts with the highest scores (section 3 adds a bias to this choice, section 2 a group limit)
gi(h)
gate weight of chosen expert i, usually its score renormalised over the k chosen
Ei, Sj
routed expert i and shared expert j: ordinary FFNs, narrower than the dense one

What it costs, and when it pays. Compute per token stays at the active count plus the router, 2 × d × E FLOPs (4,096 per token per layer for moe8top2 against 1,048,576 for its two experts, 0.4%). Memory is priced on the total, because any token may pick any expert. MoE pays when parameters are cheap to store but compute per token is the bottleneck: pretraining at a fixed FLOP budget, and serving at large batch. It pays least on one memory-bound GPU at small batch (section 8).

How to check it. Count both numbers from a config with the {{Dense vs MoE calculator|#t-calc}}; on a live PyTorch model, sum p.numel() over one layer's experts and multiply by k/E. Mixtral 8x7B is 46.7B total and 12.9B active, not 56B, because only the FFNs are copied eight times ({{Mistral, 2023-12-11|https://mistral.ai/news/mixtral-of-experts}}, vendor); DeepSeek-V3 is 671B and 37B ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437}}, vendor); both are rebuilt from their configs in section 9.
