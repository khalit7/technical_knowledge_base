Beyond subwords

Tokenizer-free and byte-level research keeps resurfacing but has not displaced subword BPE in production. Raw bytes remove the tokenizer and its failure modes but make sequences about 4 times longer (ByT5). Patches fix the length: MegaByte groups bytes in fixed patches of 8, and the Byte Latent Transformer (BLT, Meta, December 2024) starts a new patch where a small byte model finds the next byte hard to predict, so predictable stretches become long patches and the large model takes fewer steps: it "matches training flop-controlled performance of Llama 3 while using up to 50% fewer flops at inference". SuperBPE (2025) goes the other way and lets tokens cross spaces ("by the way" as one token), up to 33% fewer tokens than BPE at 200K entries.

ByT5, MegaByte, BLT and SuperBPE in their own words (sources and numbers)
  • ByT5 (2021) feeds raw UTF-8 bytes to a T5: "a vocabulary of 256 possible byte values. An additional 3 IDs are reserved for special tokens". Sequences are about 4 times longer than SentencePiece's ("4.1 bytes per SentencePiece token" on mC4); byte models "are significantly more robust to noise and perform better on tasks that are sensitive to spelling", at "+33% pre-training time" and inference up to 10 times slower ({{ByT5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13626}}).
  • MegaByte (2023) "segments sequences into patches and uses a local submodel within patches and a global model between patches", with fixed patches of 8 bytes for text ({{MegaByte|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07185}}).
  • Byte Latent Transformer, BLT (Meta, December 2024) makes the patches dynamic: a small byte-level model predicts the next byte, and a new patch starts where its entropy is high, so predictable stretches become long patches and the large model takes fewer steps. It "matches training flop-controlled performance of Llama 3 while using up to 50% fewer flops at inference"; "The bpe tokenizers of Llama 2 and 3 have an average token size of 3.7 and 4.4 bytes. In contrast, BLT can achieve similar scaling trends with an average patch size of 6 and even 8 bytes" ({{BLT section 5.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09871}}).
  • SuperBPE (2025) goes the other way and lets tokens cross spaces in a second training stage ("superword" tokens such as "by the way"): with a 200K vocabulary it "encodes a fixed piece of text with up to 33% fewer tokens on average than BPE", and an 8B model trained with it scores "+4.0% absolute improvement over the BPE baseline across 30 downstream tasks" with "27% less compute at inference time" ({{SuperBPE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13423}}). The old page said frontier labs are experimenting with SuperBPE-style tokens; that is unconfirmed: no released frontier tokenizer measured here has superword tokens.

Training your own

Use the Hugging Face {{tokenizers|https://github.com/huggingface/tokenizers}} library or {{SentencePiece|https://github.com/google/sentencepiece}} on a corpus that matches your target distribution: the vocabulary is frozen with the model, so the languages, scripts, code and number formats you care about must be in the tokenizer's training data. Decide the pre-tokenization rules (digits, whitespace runs, code) before training, reserve special tokens for your chat template, and measure fertility on held-out text in every language you will serve. Where the training corpus comes from and how it is filtered is the subject of {{Topic: data-curation-and-datasets|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}}. A model fine-tuned on a new domain or language with an old tokenizer pays the old tokenizer's rate on every token: the running example's TinyStories BPE needs 64 tokens for a 91-character Python function and 3 tokens per Chinese character (one per byte), where Llama 3's needs 28 and about 1, and its rarely used entries may be glitch tokens. Changing the tokenizer of a trained model means new embedding rows that start untrained, so extending a vocabulary (Llama 3 added 28K entries to tiktoken's 100K before training, not after) is far easier than swapping one.

Interview rehearsal: three design questions (optional)
Why does gpt-oss-20b's vocabulary matter more than Llama 3.1 8B's, although its table is a smaller share of total parameters?
Total parameters do not run per token; active ones do. gpt-oss-20b activates 4 of 32 experts, so its 201,088 × 2,880 untied tables are 28% of the active parameters and its output layer 16% of the decode FLOPs per token, against 13% and 7% for dense Llama 3.1 8B (root Config calculator numbers).
What is a glitch token and how would you find them in a model you are given?
A vocabulary entry the model almost never saw in training, so its embedding is untrained. Find candidates from the weights: rows that cluster together or have unusual norms (here 132 unseen entries had cosine similarity 0.996 with each other and norms 2.5 times the median), check frequency in a training sample, then confirm by prompting the model to repeat the token.
Design: you are pretraining a 3B model that must serve English, Hindi, Japanese and Python. How do you choose the tokenizer?
Train byte-level BPE (no holes) on a sample matching the target mix, oversampling the smaller languages so they get merges; pick digit handling (single digits, or groups of three from the right) and whitespace-run tokens for code before training; reserve chat and tool special tokens up front. Size: measure characters per token on held-out text per language at, say, 64K, 128K and 256K, and weigh tokens saved against the table's cost (at width 3,072 a 256K untied vocabulary is 1.6B parameters, half the model, so tie the embeddings or stay near 128K: SmolLM3 3B ties a 128,256-entry table, root map config). Check the result: fertility per language within about 1.5 times English, no unseen entries in a training sample, round-trip decode exact on every test string.
