Numbers and code

The problem. BPE learns pieces from frequency, so without a rule it cuts numbers wherever the training data happened to make merges: GPT-2 has " 2024" as one token but cuts 12345678 as 123|45|678, so the same digit can sit at different positions inside different tokens, and the model has to learn place value separately for every way a number can be cut. Every tokenizer since has added a digit rule to the pre-tokenizer, and they disagree: groups of up to three from the left (GPT-4's cl100k, GPT-4o's o200k, Llama 3 and 4, DeepSeek-V3 and V4, MiniMax, OLMo 2 and Olmo 3, Kimi K3: 123|456|78) or every digit alone (LLaMA 1 and 2, Gemma 1 to 4, Qwen3, Qwen3.5, Nemotron 3 Super, SmolLM2: 1|2|3|4|5|6|7|8).

Read from each tokenizer's pattern and checked by encoding 12345678 with the real files (src/tok2026/analyze.py). The running example's TinyStories BPE learned no merges between digits at all (numbers are rare in the stories), so it cuts every digit too, by accident rather than by rule; GLM-5 is the odd one out: its pattern allows three digits but its vocabulary has only 177 of the 1,000 three-digit tokens, so 12345678 becomes 123|45|6|78 (the pattern allows a chunk the vocabulary does not have).

Why left-to-right chunks hurt. Addition works from the right. With chunks of three from the left, 12345 becomes 123|45 and 6789 becomes 678|9: the units digits of the two numbers sit in different positions of different tokens, and the answer's chunks do not line up with either. Writing the number with thousands separators (12,345) forces chunks of three from the right, aligned with place value. {{Singh and Strouse 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14903}} showed on GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 that right-to-left tokenization "enforced by comma separating numbers at inference time" "leads to largely improved performance", and that the gap shrinks with model size published.

What transfers: the direction of the effect and that it comes from the tokenizer (the problems and the model are identical between the two columns). What does not: the absolute accuracies, which are those of 1 to 2 billion parameter models on few-shot addition.

So on OLMo 2 1B: 3% exact plain against 19% with commas, digit accuracy 39% against 71%, with nothing changed but how the numbers are written. Single-digit tokenizers (Qwen, Gemma) avoid the misalignment at the price of longer numbers.

Code. Code is mostly whitespace, punctuation and identifiers, and a tokenizer trained on prose wastes tokens on it. The running example's tokenizer is the extreme case: it never saw code, so the 91-character Python function below costs 64 tokens (" total" is to|t|al, an indent is four single spaces) against 45 for GPT-2 and 28 for Llama 3, whose tokenizer has tokens for runs of spaces and for ):\n. Gemma keeps an 8-space indent as one token; byte-level tokenizers of the Llama 3 family attach the last space of an indent to the next word (' ', ' return').
