The language tax

Fertility is tokens per word, and the related premium is how many tokens a language needs for the same content relative to English: "The ratio |t(sA)| / |t(sB)| is the premium for A relative to B", measured on translations of the same sentences ({{Petrov et al. 2023 section 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15425}}). It varies wildly: GPT-4's tokenizer "uses about 1.6 times more tokens to encode the same text in Italian as it does in English, 2.6 times for Bulgarian and 3 times for Arabic. For Shan ... that difference can be as high as 15 times." A premium is both a cost (APIs bill per token, latency grows with length, less content fits in the context window) and a quality issue (the model sees each word as more, less meaningful pieces). Byte-level is not neutral either: "byte-level representation of the same text is over 4 times longer for Burmese or Tibetan than Chinese."

The tax depends on the tokenizer more than on the language: Tamil falls from 15.6 times English to 2.0

Premium against English for six languages, four generations of tokenizer, over the whole FLORES-200 dev and devtest (2,009 sentences per language). Try Tamil: GPT-2 15.6 times, GPT-4o 2.0.

Defaults reproduce Petrov et al.'s published token counts exactly, independently: 204 of 204 languages for GPT-2's r50k, GPT-4's cl100k, Llama 2, ByT5's bytes and UTF-32 characters, from the FLORES-200 release with the paper's own construction ({{their code and table|https://github.com/AleksandarPetrov/tokenization-fairness}}). The other columns extend it to newer tokenizers.

Across all 204 languages, the median premium is 2.26 with GPT-4's cl100k and 1.75 with GPT-4o's o200k; 65 languages pay more than 3 times English with cl100k, 15 with o200k and 8 with Gemma 3. So "low-resource languages pay 3 to 5 times more" is true for some tokenizers and some languages, and the size of the tax depends on the tokenizer far more than on the language: Tamil drops from 7.65 with Llama 3 to 1.46 with Gemma 3. OpenAI's GPT-4o launch listed the gain for 20 languages (Gujarati "4.4x fewer tokens (from 145 to 33)"); the {{Same text, ten tokenizers|#t-lang}} tab recomputes all 20 sentences (18 reproduce exactly) and lists every one of the 204 languages. So never compare token counts or per-token prices across models as if a token were a fixed amount of text: compare per character or per word of your own text. When choosing a tokenizer for domain adaptation, measure the fertility on your own text: a tokenizer trained on a different distribution can make the same budget of tokens carry much less of your data.
