6Generalised advantage estimation: λ is the bias-variance dial

Worked example (γ = 1): a three-step episode with rewards 0, 0, 1 and critic values V(s0) = 0.5, V(s1) = 0.6, V(s2) = 0.8. The TD errors are δ0 = 0.1, δ1 = 0.2, δ2 = 1 − 0.8 = 0.2. The first action's advantage is 0.1 if we trust the critic after one step (λ = 0); 0.5 if we use the whole return, 1 minus V(s0) = 0.5 (λ = 1); and 0.1 + 0.95 × 0.2 + 0.952 × 0.2 = 0.4705 at λ = 0.95 derived. Best results at intermediate values: γ ∈ [0.96, 0.99] and λ ∈ [0.92, 0.99] on cart-pole, γ ∈ [0.99, 0.995] and λ ∈ [0.96, 0.99] on the 3D biped. 7A3C and A2C: parallel actors instead of replay

On-policy methods cannot use a replay buffer (old data comes from old policies), yet consecutive transitions of one trajectory are highly correlated, which is what made naive deep RL unstable. A3C, asynchronous advantage actor-critic ({{Mnih et al. 2016|https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01783}}), decorrelates the data with many actor-learners, each with its own copy of the environment, exploring different states at the same moment and applying their gradients to shared weights asynchronously. Each uses an n-step return minus V as its advantage, plus an entropy bonus: "adding the entropy of the policy π to the objective function improved exploration by discouraging premature convergence to suboptimal deterministic policies" (weight β = 0.01 on Atari). It "surpasses the current state-of-the-art on the Atari domain while training for half the time on a single multi-core CPU instead of a GPU", with 16 actor-learner threads.

A3C applies each worker's gradient whenever it is ready; A2C waits for all of them and takes one batched step

Four workers, time to the right illustrative timings. Bars are n-step rollouts labelled with the version of the shared weights θ they were computed on; a tick is a gradient applied, making the version written beside it. In A3C, worker 3's second gradient was computed on θ version 4 but is applied as version 9: four other updates landed while it rolled out (stale). In A2C the faster workers idle (hatched) at the barrier, then one update averages all four.

A2C is the synchronous version. OpenAI's Baselines release found "Our synchronous A2C implementation performs better than our asynchronous implementations; we have not seen any evidence that the noise introduced by asynchrony provides any performance benefit", and that it "is more cost-effective than A3C when using single-GPU machines, and is faster than a CPU-only A3C implementation when using larger policies" ({{OpenAI Baselines: ACKTR and A2C, 2017|https://web.archive.org/web/20190213165907/https://blog.openai.com/baselines-acktr-a2c/}}, Wayback copy). The parallel actors helped; the asynchrony did not (one of the root Method atlas's corrections). A batch of rollouts from parallel environments, then one update, is the shape of PPO and of every LLM RL loop since.

8Natural gradients and TRPO: measure the step in policy space

Why the step size is dangerous. The policy gradient is only valid near the policy that collected the data. In supervised learning a too-large step costs one bad update; in RL the next batch is sampled from the broken policy, so the data itself degrades and training may never recover. Two goals follow: the largest step that is still safe, and several gradient steps on one expensive batch of rollouts.

The step is measured in the wrong units. Take two actions with logit difference z, so p = σ(z). From p = 0.5, a step Δz = 1 moves p to 0.731, a KL divergence of 0.120; from p = 0.99 the same step moves it to 0.996, a KL of 0.0037, 33 times less derived. Gradient ascent is not covariant: reparameterise the policy and its steps change. The fix is to measure distance by how much the action distribution changes. To second order, KL(πθ ‖ πθ+Δ) ≈ ½ Δ⊤FΔ, where F = 𝔼[∇ log π ∇ log π⊤] is the Fisher information matrix (for two actions, F = p(1 − p): 0.25 at p = 0.5, 0.0099 at p = 0.99).

TRPO, trust region policy optimisation ({{Schulman et al. 2015|https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05477}}), turns this into an algorithm. With the importance ratio rt(θ) = πθ(at | st) / πθold(at | st), maximising the surrogate 𝔼t[rt(θ) Ât] has the same gradient as the policy gradient at θ = θold, and the ratio corrects for the drift as θ moves (the importance-sampling correction of {{Model-free prediction and control|n:3c65c17b0d0d81898a0cd4c05c744969}}, applied to a slightly stale policy). Theory bounds how far the surrogate can mislead, but its penalty is far too conservative, so TRPO uses a hard constraint:

δ
the trust region: "We used δ = 0.01 for all experiments"
the solver
a natural-gradient direction by conjugate gradient using Fisher-vector products (no matrix is ever formed; "k = 10" iterations "to be quite effective"), then a line search that shrinks the step "until the objective improves. Without this line search, the algorithm occasionally computes large steps that cause a catastrophic degradation" (Appendix C)

It works, but the second-order machinery is heavy, and TRPO is "not compatible with architectures that include noise (such as dropout) or parameter sharing (between the policy and value function, or with auxiliary tasks)" ({{PPO, §1|https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347}}). PPO keeps the idea and drops the machinery.

Kakade's covariance argument, in his words under 1 min

"the standard gradient descent rule is non-covariant ... we present a covariant gradient by defining a metric based on the underlying structure of the policy"; and "the natural gradient is moving toward choosing a greedy optimal action rather than just a better action." He also notes that the averaged F is not fully invariant, because of the weighting by the state distribution.

TRPO's monotonic improvement bound, and its two sampling schemes 1 min

From its Theorem 1 (stated with total variation) the paper derives η(π̃) ≥ Lπ(π̃) − C · maxs KL(π ‖ π̃), with C = 4εγ / (1 − γ)2 and ε = max |Aπ(s, a)| (Eq. 9), so maximising the penalised surrogate improves the true return monotonically. The penalty coefficient that theory gives is far too conservative in practice, hence the constraint.

It tried two ways of collecting data, "single path" (ordinary rollouts) and "vine" (branching rollouts from states along a trunk, which needs a resettable simulator), and learned "simulated robotic swimming, hopping, and walking gaits; and playing Atari games using images of the screen as input".
