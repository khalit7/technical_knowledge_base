4Baselines: same mean, less variance

Subtract from the return any baseline b(s) that may depend on the state but not on the action. The gradient is unchanged in expectation, because the subtracted term averages to zero:

𝔼a~π[ ∇ log π(a | s) b(s) ] = b(s) Σa ∇π(a | s) = b(s) ∇ Σa π(a | s) = b(s) ∇1 = 0
b(s)
"can be any function, even a random variable, as long as it does not vary with a" ({{Sutton and Barto §13.4|http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=351}}); a baseline that depends on the action taken breaks the second equality and biases the gradient

The natural choice is b(s) = Vπ(s), which turns the weight into the advantage Aπ(s, a) = Qπ(s, a) − Vπ(s): better-than-average actions are pushed up, worse-than-average ones down. "In some states all actions have high values and we need a high baseline to differentiate the higher valued actions from the less highly valued ones; in other states all actions will have low values and a low baseline is appropriate" (§13.4).

Worked example illustrative: one state, three actions with rewards 1, 2 and 6, a softmax with all logits 0, so each action has probability 1/3 and V = 3. The score with respect to the logits is ea − π, so the exact gradient is πk(Rk − V) = (−0.667, −0.333, 1).

  • Sample action 1 (reward 1) without a baseline: 1 × (2/3, −1/3, −1/3) = (0.667, −0.333, −0.333), which raises the worst action. With baseline 3: (1 − 3) × (2/3, −1/3, −1/3) = (−1.333, 0.667, 0.667), which lowers it.
  • Sample action 3 (reward 6): (−2, −2, 4) without a baseline, (−1, −1, 2) with it: the same direction, half the size.
  • Averaged over the three actions, both give exactly (−0.667, −0.333, 1), but the total variance (summed over components) falls from 7.56 to 1.56 derived.

Which baseline minimises the variance? For a constant b, the variance of (Ra − b)∇ log π(a) is a parabola in b with its minimum at the score-weighted mean b* = 𝔼[‖∇ log π‖2 R] / 𝔼[‖∇ log π‖2], not at V = 𝔼[R] ({{Greensmith, Bartlett and Baxter 2004|https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume5/greensmith04a/greensmith04a.pdf}}, who "show that the popular approach of using the average reward to define the baseline can be suboptimal"). When every action's score has the same length, as at the uniform policy above, the two coincide; otherwise V is close, and it is what practice uses because it can be learned.

The mean never moves with b; the variance is a parabola whose bottom b* leaves V when the policy is uneven

Exact, not sampled: mean and variance are sums over the three actions weighted by their probabilities. Try: raise the logit of action 3 to 2 and watch b* leave V.

A learned baseline. V is unknown, so it is learned alongside the policy: Sutton and Barto's "REINFORCE with baseline" fits v̂(s, w) to the returns by Monte Carlo and updates θ with Gt − v̂(St, w) ({{box on p. 330|http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=352}}). On the short corridor a one-number baseline, v̂ = w, with αθ = 2−9 and αw = 2−6, reaches the optimum within about 100 episodes, where REINFORCE at its best step size needs most of 1,000 (Figure 13.2, rerun below). The baseline also allows a 16 times larger policy step without diverging.

A baseline does not make an actor-critic
"In REINFORCE with baseline, the learned state-value function estimates the value of the first state of each state transition. This estimate sets a baseline for the subsequent return, but is made prior to the transition's action and thus cannot be used to assess that action" ({{§13.5|http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=353}}). So REINFORCE with a learned baseline, RLOO (each sample's reward minus the mean of the other samples for the same prompt; {{Kool, van Hoof and Welling 2019|https://openreview.net/forum?id=r1lgTGL5DE}}) and GRPO (the group's mean and standard deviation; {{Shao et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300}}) are REINFORCE-family policy methods, not actor-critics, as the root's Method atlas records. Their use on language models is on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}.

5Actor-critic: a critic judges each action

An actor-critic learns an actor π(a | s, θ), updated by the policy gradient, and a critic v̂(s, w), trained by TD learning, whose estimate of the next state turns one real reward into an assessment of the action just taken. {{Barto, Sutton and Anderson (1983)|https://doi.org/10.1109/TSMC.1983.6313077}} balanced a pole with an "adaptive critic element" and an "associative search element"; the root's Method atlas dates the family there.

δt = Rt+1 + γ v̂(St+1, w) − v̂(St, w);   w ← w + αw δt ∇v̂(St, w);   θ ← θ + αθ I δt ∇ log π(At | St, θ);   I ← γI
δt
the TD error, with v̂ = 0 at a terminal state. If the critic were exact, 𝔼[δt | St, At] = Qπ − Vπ = Aπ(St, At): the TD error is an unbiased sample of the advantage
I
the running γt factor; this is the one-step actor-critic of {{Sutton and Barto, p. 332|http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=354}}, fully online: each transition is used once, as it happens

The trade is {{Model-free prediction and control|n:3c65c17b0d0d81898a0cd4c05c744969}}'s Monte Carlo against TD trade, moved to policies: one real reward and the critic's estimate give low variance and an update every step, but when the critic is wrong the weight is biased. The book adds: "the bias in the gradient estimate is not due to bootstrapping as such; the actor would be biased even if the critic was learned by a Monte Carlo method." Between the two ends sit n-step and λ-returns, which section 6 turns into an advantage estimator.

When the critic is too poor. Give the one-step actor-critic the same one-number critic as the baseline, v̂(s, w) = w. Every state looks the same to it, so δ = −1 + w − w = −1 on every step except the last, where δ = −1 − w > 0 derived. The last action of every episode is right from the third state, so the critic keeps crediting right, and the policy drifts past the optimum to the 0.95 floor (−44 per episode) within a few hundred episodes, while REINFORCE with the same baseline settles at the optimum. With a critic this poor, the actor-critic's bias wins over its lower variance; a smaller actor step (2−12) delays the drift but does not stop it. A baseline only has to be a number; a critic has to tell states apart.
