## Milestones (quotes as printed)

- **TD-Gammon**, Tesauro, CACM 38(3), March 1995, https://www.bkgm.com/articles/tesauro/tdl.html. "In late 1991, version 1.0 played a total of 51 games against Robertie, Magriel, and Malcolm Davis ... and achieved a very respectable net loss of 13 points"; "Version 2.0 ... made its public debut at the 1992 World Cup of Backgammon tournament. In 38 exhibition games ... the program had a net loss of only 7 points"; "the latest version of the program (2.1) achieved near-parity to Bill Robertie in a recent 40-game test session ... an extremely narrow 1-point victory." Table 1 (image `tdl-tab1.gif`, transcribed): training games 300,000 / 800,000 / 1,500,000; results -13 pts/51 games (-0.25 ppg), -7 pts/38 games (-0.18 ppg), -1 pt/40 games (-0.02 ppg).
- **DQN 2013**, arXiv 1312.5602: "outperformed all previous RL algorithms on six of the seven games we have attempted and surpassed an expert human player on three of them"; "We trained for a total of 10 million frames".
- **DQN Nature 2015**, https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236 (DeepMind PDF): "comparable to that of a professional human games tester across the set of 49 games, achieving more than 75% of the human score on more than half of the games (29 games"; "outperforms the best existing reinforcement learning methods on 43 of the games"; "We trained for a total of 50 million frames (that is, around 38 days of game experience in total)". No median is printed.
- **AlphaGo**, Nature 529 (2016): "winning 494 out of 495 games (99.8%) against other Go programs"; "Over 5-9 October 2015 AlphaGo and Fan Hui competed in a formal five-game match. AlphaGo won the match 5 games to 0"; "30 million positions from the KGS Go Server"; RL policy "using 50 GPUs, for one day"; value network "using 50 GPUs, for one week"; distributed version "40 search threads, 1,202 CPUs and 176 GPUs".
- **AlphaGo Lee**: Nature news 15 March 2016, "The Go Files: AI computer wraps up 4-1 victory against human champion", https://www.nature.com/articles/nature.2016.19575 (title states 4-1). AlphaGo Zero paper: "defeated Lee Sedol, the winner of 18 international titles, in March 2016"; "AlphaGo Lee was distributed over many machines and used 48 TPUs".
- **Christiano et al. 2017**, arXiv 1706.03741: "providing feedback on less than 1% of our agent's interactions with the environment"; "train complex novel behaviors with about an hour of human time"; "trained using 900 queries in less than an hour"; Atari "with 5,500 queries to a human rater".
- **AlphaGo Zero**, author copy https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10045895/1/agz_unformatted_nature.pdf: "Over the course of training, 4.9 million games of self-play were generated, using 1,600 simulations for each MCTS"; "After 72 hours ... AlphaGo Zero used a single machine with 4 Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), while AlphaGo Lee was distributed over many machines and used 48 TPUs. AlphaGo Zero defeated AlphaGo Lee by 100 games to 0"; "approximately 40 days. Over the course of training, 29 million games of self-play were generated"; "AlphaGo Zero won by 89 games to 11"; Elo "5,185, compared to 4,858 for AlphaGo Master, 3,739 for AlphaGo Lee and 3,144 for AlphaGo Fan". The self-play training hardware is not stated. AlphaZero preprint footnote: "The original AlphaGo Zero paper used GPUs to train the neural networks."
- **AlphaZero**, arXiv 1712.01815v1 (5 Dec 2017): "700,000 steps (mini-batches of size 4,096) ... using 5,000 first-generation TPUs (15) to generate self-play games and 64 second-generation TPUs to train the neural networks"; "In chess, AlphaZero outperformed Stockfish after just 4 hours (300k steps)"; Table 1 rows: chess 25/25/0 and 3/47/0 (28 W, 72 D, 0 L); shogi 43/2/5 and 47/0/3 (90 W, 2 D, 8 L); Go 31/19 and 29/21 (60 W, 40 L); Stockfish "using 64 threads and a hash size of 1GB"; "one minute per move".
- **QT-Opt**, arXiv 1806.10293: "over 580k real-world grasp attempts ... 96% grasp success on unseen objects"; "over the course of several weeks across 7 robots"; "collected over the course of four months, with a total of about 800 robot hours"; CEM optimiser quoted.
- **Dactyl**, arXiv 1808.00177: "about 100 years of experience. - **ANYmal**, arXiv 1901.08652: "breaking the previous speed record of ANYmal by 25 %"; "All training sessions presented in this paper were done on a personal computer with one CPU and one GPU, and none lasted more than eleven hours"; "we train an actuator net".
- **OpenAI Five**, arXiv 1912.06680: "a single training run that ran from June 30th, 2018 to April 22nd, 2019. After ten months of training using 770±50 PFlops/s·days of compute, it defeated the Dota 2 world champions in a best-of-three match"; "In total, Five played 3,193 teams in 7,257 total games, winning 99.4%"; footnote "Human players often abandoned losing games"; "Subset of 17 heroes ... from a pool of 117 heroes"; "Rerun took 2 months and 150 ± 5 PFlops/s·days"; "820±50 total optimization compute when it was finally turned off".

## Recomputations (recompute.py)

- MuZero Table S1 per-game scores give median 2041.12%, mean 4999.24%: reproduces Table 1 exactly. Ape-X from the same table: 434.08% (printed 434.1). R2D2: 1926.73% (S1 baselines) or 1922.73% (Agent57 baselines) against printed 1920.6%; mean 4024.93% against 4024.9%.
- Agent57 App. H.4 per-game scores reproduce Agent57 Table 1 exactly for Agent57 (1933.49, 4766.25, capped 100.00), R2D2 (bandit) (2357.92, 5461.66) and MuZero (2381.51, 5661.84).
- **Finding:** Agent57's per-game table has two MuZero scores with an extra digit: Asteroids 6,785,558.64 (MuZero's own: 678,558.64) and Beam Rider 4,549,993.53 (MuZero's own: 454,993.53). Every other MuZero score and every human/random baseline matches. With MuZero's own scores the Agent57 arithmetic gives 2041.12% median and 4998.51% mean, MuZero's own numbers. Agent57's "27469%" for MuZero on Beam Rider is the mistyped value; MuZero's own score is 2744.9%. Agent57's sentence "MuZero obtains the highest uncapped mean and median" holds in its table only because of these typos: corrected, MuZero's median (2041) is below R2D2 (bandit)'s 2357.92 though still above Agent57's 1933.49.
- **Fragility of the median:** Agent57's median game is Double Dunk (human -16.4, random -18.6: a 2.2-point gap). MuZero's table prints -16.40 and -18.55. With those baselines Agent57's median becomes 1975.81% instead of 1933.49%.

## Counting conventions worth knowing

- DQN's Nature paper says it trained on "50 million frames"; Rainbow, Ape-X and others list DQN at 200M frames. The usual reading is that the Nature figure counts agent decisions, each repeated over 4 emulator frames; the Nature text does not say so explicitly (unconfirmed). Every frame figure on the tab is the one the cited table prints.
