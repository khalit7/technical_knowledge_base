Milestones and benchmarks

Thirty-odd years of reinforcement learning results, each with what was achieved, the method, the compute or experience it took where the source discloses it, and the primary source. Then the Atari benchmark the deep RL era was measured on, one printed table at a time, because the same agent's "median" changes with who ran it and how.

How the numbers here are kept honest
Every number is printed in the source linked beside it (tables and text, never values read off a curve). Each Atari number carries its protocol: which games (49 or 57), how episodes start (random no-op actions or positions from human play), and how many frames the agent trained on. Numbers from different protocols never share an axis, and a view shows one source table at a time. Values computed here from printed per-game scores are labelled derived. Methods are explained on {{Deep RL|n:3c65c17b0d0d8180b808c8c0cf8ddbe6}} (games, control, robotics) and {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}} (language). Data as of 3 October 2026.

Atari-57: one printed table at a time

The Arcade Learning Environment's 57 games are scored as human-normalised scores, 100 × (agent − random) / (human − random), so 100% is the human tester. A paper summarises the 57 values by their median and mean. Two evaluation protocols coexist: no-op starts (each episode begins with up to 30 random do-nothing actions) and human starts (episodes begin from 100 positions taken from a human's play, a harder test of generalisation). Early papers used 49 games; some capped episodes at 5 minutes, most at 30.

Atari 100k: sample efficiency

A separate benchmark, never on the same axis as the one above: 26 of the games, 100k agent steps each (400k frames, about two hours of play), introduced with SimPLe ({{Kaiser et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00374}}). Scores are human-normalised as fractions (1.0 = human). {{Agarwal et al. (2021)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264}} showed that with 3 to 10 runs the median is unreliable and that some papers compared a maximum over training against others' final scores; they proposed the interquartile mean (IQM), which BBF's table reports.
