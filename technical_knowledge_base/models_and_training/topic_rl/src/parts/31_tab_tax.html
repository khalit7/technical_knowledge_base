Taxonomy: every axis RL methods are classified on

Calling a method "model-free, off-policy, value-based" places it on three axes; there are at least fifteen more, and most confusions in RL come from treating two of them as one: off-policy as offline, online as on-policy, a reward model as a model. This tab defines each axis with its sides, examples from the {{Method atlas|#t-atlas}}, the common misconception and where the boundary blurs, and places all forty-seven atlas methods on every axis.

Definitions follow Sutton and Barto's second edition, whose section 8.13 itself organises the field as dimensions; quotes are checked against its text by the build. The placements of methods on the extra axes are derived here from each method's first paper (the atlas cites it), and are marked unconfirmed where the paper does not settle them.

Try: pick a method to see it on every axis at once; pick any two axes to place every method on that grid; then read Sutton and Barto's unified view, the two axes that separate dynamic programming, Monte Carlo and TD.

Sutton and Barto's unified view: depth and width of the update

Two of the most important dimensions, in the book's words, are the width of an update (one sampled successor, or the expectation over all of them) and its depth (bootstrap after one step, after several, or never). Dynamic programming, TD and Monte Carlo sit at three corners; the fourth, expected updates all the way down, is exhaustive search. Every atlas method that updates values or advantages is placed below.
