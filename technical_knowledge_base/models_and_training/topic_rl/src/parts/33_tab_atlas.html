Method atlas: every method on one grid

Forty-seven methods from Bellman's value iteration (1957) to SA-MRPO (August 2026), on the same nine columns: the three taxonomy axes (what it stores, whether it uses a model, whose data it learns from), what stands in for the expected return it cannot compute, what it needs, its action space, its first paper, the problem it fixed in its predecessor, and where it is used now.

Every cell names its source and the source's date. A cell is stated when the source says it (its words are quoted in the detail panel, and the build checks the quote against the source text), derived when the classification is ours from what the source describes (dotted underline), or unconfirmed. Each method links to the child page that owns it: {{Dynamic programming|n:3c65c17b0d0d81baada7e1c05ce69d01}}, {{Model-free prediction and control|n:3c65c17b0d0d81898a0cd4c05c744969}}, {{Value-based deep RL|n:3ee5c17b0d0d818689b2c0dbce434ece}}, {{Policy gradients and actor-critic|n:3ee5c17b0d0d8101a262d3620e75747c}}, {{Model-based RL and planning|n:3ee5c17b0d0d8116be5fd39e10bd94c5}}, {{Offline RL and imitation|n:3ee5c17b0d0d81d4995ec47e428de9b3}}, {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}; DPO's pipeline is on {{Alignment|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}. Exploration methods are on {{Bandits and exploration|n:3ee5c17b0d0d81acba54d70ac51c601c}}. The axes themselves are defined, with their blurred edges, in {{Taxonomy|#t-tax}}.

Try: filter with the chips or the search box, sort by clicking a column name, click any cell for its source and quote. Tick two or three methods (PPO, GRPO and DPO start ticked) to compare them side by side. The three axes as a map

Columns are whose data a method learns from, rows what it stores; the colour is the third axis, the model. The grid's filters dim what they exclude, so the map shows where a filtered set sits. Empty cells are information too: no method in the atlas is value-based and learns only from self-play.

Lineage: what each method fixed in its predecessor

Each edge is one row's "Problem it fixed" cell: it points from the method whose problem was fixed to the method that fixed it. Click a method to light up its ancestors and descendants; click an edge to read the problem, in the source's words, with its link. Time runs downward; the six lanes are the families.

Each fix lights up: two chains, step by step

The same recipe card, changed one paper at a time. The value chain starts from tabular Q-learning and ends at Rainbow; the language-model chain starts from PPO and ends at GSPO, and its counter is the number of large networks that must sit in memory. Lit cards are what this step added; struck-through cards are what it removed.
