Further reading

The Reading tab is a complete pass through RL, from the agent-environment loop to RL for language models, built on one through-line: every method estimates the expected return in the Bellman equation and differs in what stands in for the expectation. The tabs on this page let you classify, run and look up the methods; the four pages under this one each own one layer in depth. Links open in a new tab, with reading times.

On this page

{{Taxonomy|#t-tax}}

Every way of classifying an RL method in depth: model-based or model-free, value, policy or actor-critic, on-policy, off-policy or offline, Monte Carlo or bootstrapping, tabular or approximate, episodic or continuing, observability, multi-agent, reward source, exploration. Behind section 6.

{{Estimator lab|#t-lab}}

The classic tabular experiments run live in your browser, reproducing Sutton and Barto's figures: the random walk (Monte Carlo against TD, n-step), gridworld and cliff walking (SARSA against Q-learning). Behind sections 8 to 10.

{{Method atlas|#t-atlas}}

Every method on one grid: what it stores, what stands in for the expectation, model, data, action space, year and paper. The spine table at the top of the Reading tab, extended to the whole field.

{{Milestones and benchmarks|#t-miles}}

The results that made the field, from TD-Gammon to DeepSeek-R1, with sourced scores. Behind sections 1, 12, 16 and 18.

{{Dynamic programming: planning with a known model|n:3c65c17b0d0d81baada7e1c05ce69d01}}

Policy evaluation, policy improvement, policy iteration and value iteration worked by hand on a three-state corridor; the contraction proof, sweep counts and the stopping-error bound; policy against value iteration; asynchronous DP. The exact answer every other method approximates. Section 8. (17 min read · +2h 25m resources)

{{Model-free methods: Monte Carlo, TD, SARSA, Q-learning|n:3c65c17b0d0d81898a0cd4c05c744969}}

Monte Carlo and TD on one random-walk episode, batch MC against batch TD, n-step returns and TD(λ), ε-greedy and GLIE, SARSA and Q-learning on one transition, maximisation bias, cliff walking with its numbers, and DP's backups as sampled TD updates. Sections 9 and 10. (20 min read · +5h 40m resources)

{{Deep RL: from DQN to PPO to MuZero|n:3c65c17b0d0d8180b808c8c0cf8ddbe6}}

DQN and its variants (Double, dueling, Rainbow) with worked targets, REINFORCE and the baseline proof, actor-critic, GAE and A2C/A3C, TRPO to PPO and the clip on four cases, DDPG, TD3 and SAC, AlphaGo to MuZero with the AlphaZero loss. Sections 11 to 16. (25 min read · +32h 35m resources)

{{RL for LLMs: RLHF, GRPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}

Token generation as an MDP, PPO for RLHF with GAE, GRPO through the clip and the k3 estimator, RLVR, DeepSeek-R1 in detail, Dr. GRPO, DAPO, GSPO and off-policy corrections, open questions, and a checklist for a from-scratch RLVR loop. Section 18. (19 min read · +8h 40m resources)

Best resources for the topic as a whole

All optional; the Reading tab is self-sufficient.

{{Sutton and Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction (2nd ed., free PDF)|http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html}}

The reference text; chapters 1 to 3 cover sections 1 to 6 of this page (about 2h), the whole book about 14h. (about 14h)

{{David Silver's UCL course (10 lectures)|https://www.davidsilver.uk/teaching/}}

Still the best structured path through classical RL, slides plus videos; lectures 1 and 2 (about 3h) are the source of this page's framing and the student example. (about 15h)

{{OpenAI Spinning Up|https://spinningup.openai.com}}

The best practical bridge from theory to deep RL code; "Key Concepts in RL" alone is a crisp notation reference (about 15 min). (about 3h for the core pages)

{{Nathan Lambert, RLHF Book|https://rlhfbook.com}}

The reference for the LLM side: reward models, PPO and GRPO for language models, RLVR, DPO and evaluation. (about 6h)

{{Sergey Levine, Berkeley CS285: Deep Reinforcement Learning|https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse/}}

The deepest course on the deep RL layer: policy gradients, actor-critic, value methods, model-based and offline RL, with homework. (course, about 25h)

{{Hugging Face Deep RL Course|https://huggingface.co/learn/deep-rl-course/unit0/introduction}}

Hands-on units from Q-learning to PPO and RLHF, with notebooks you can run. (course, many hours; unconfirmed total)

{{Lilian Weng, "A (Long) Peek into Reinforcement Learning"|https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2018-02-19-rl-overview/}}

The best single-page compression of the classical core. (about 35 min)

{{Lilian Weng, "Policy Gradient Algorithms"|https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2018-04-08-policy-gradient/}}

Every policy-gradient method from REINFORCE to SAC, derived in one notation. (about 45 min)

{{Lilian Weng, "Exploration Strategies in Deep RL"|https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2020-06-07-exploration-drl/}}

Count-based bonuses, curiosity, RND, Go-Explore. Section 7. (about 50 min)

{{Lilian Weng, "Reward Hacking in Reinforcement Learning"|https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2024-11-28-reward-hacking/}}

From classic specification gaming to RLHF and LLM agents. Section 18. (about 1h)

{{"The 37 Implementation Details of PPO"|https://iclr-blog-track.github.io/2022/03/25/ppo-implementation-details/}}

What PPO's results actually depend on, line by line against the reference code. Section 14. (about 50 min)

{{Andrej Karpathy, "Deep Reinforcement Learning: Pong from Pixels"|https://karpathy.github.io/2016/05/31/rl/}}

A policy gradient in 130 lines of Python, explained from scratch. Section 13. (about 30 min)

{{Alex Irpan, "Deep Reinforcement Learning Doesn't Work Yet"|https://www.alexirpan.com/2018/02/14/rl-hard.html}}

The honest 2018 list of why deep RL is hard (sample efficiency, reward design, instability); most items are the open problems of this page. (about 40 min)

{{Andy Jones, "Debugging RL, Without the Agonizing Pain"|https://andyljones.com/posts/rl-debugging.html}}

Probe environments and practical checks for when an agent will not learn. (about 30 min)

{{Hugging Face, "Illustrating RLHF"|https://huggingface.co/blog/rlhf}}

The RLHF pipeline drawn step by step. Section 18. (about 20 min)
