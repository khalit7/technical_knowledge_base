D4RL results, as printed

Six tables from five papers, each shown exactly as its authors printed it. Nothing is averaged across tables, renormalised or filled in from another source: a score depends on the dataset version, the checkpoint reported, the seeds and the implementation, so numbers from two tables are not one series. The last panel puts one method on one dataset side by side across the tables, so the differences can be seen rather than spliced.

Normalised score = 100 × (score − random) / (expert − random), with D4RL's reference returns: 0 is a uniformly random policy, 100 a domain expert (a SAC agent on Gym-MuJoCo; the data-collecting controller on Maze2D; an estimate of the maximum on AntMaze and Kitchen; a BC-then-RL policy on Adroit) ({{Fu et al. 2020, §5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07219}}). Scores above 100 are possible. How the methods work is in the Reading tab (sections 8 to 12).

One method, one dataset, every table

Pick a method and a dataset. Each line is one table's printed value with its version and protocol; they are not averaged or joined. The CQL paper calls medium-replay "mixed"; its rows are matched by D4RL's own values for the same baselines.

Two printings of the same CQL runs disagree

The CQL paper's Table 1 and the D4RL paper's Table 2 both print CQL(H) on the original (v0) Gym datasets with the same baselines (taken from D4RL). On hopper-medium-expert the CQL paper prints 111.0 and D4RL 98.7; on walker2d-medium-expert the CQL paper prints 98.7 and D4RL 111.0: the two values are swapped. The CQL paper's walker2d-medium-expert row also repeats the baselines of its walker2d-mixed row (BC 11.3, BEAR 10.8, BRAC-p −0.3, BRAC-v 0.9), where D4RL prints BC 6.4, BEAR 40.1, BRAC-p 76.9, BRAC-v 81.6. Which CQL value belongs to which dataset is unconfirmed; both are shown, side by side, in the panel above.

What the tables agree on

Behaviour cloning is strong wherever the data is good (medium-expert, Adroit expert), and filtering the data helps it: in CORL's runs 10% BC lifts BC's Gym-MuJoCo average from 50.4 to 69.3. Only methods that do dynamic programming (CQL, IQL, ReBRAC) score more than a few points on AntMaze's medium and large mazes, where trajectories must be stitched. The ensemble methods are, in CORL's words, "the strongest baselines for Gym-MuJoCo and Maze2d, but they perform poorly on both AntMaze and Adroit domains" (SAC-N and EDAC score 0 on every AntMaze dataset with their last policy). And with its last policy no method reaches 10 on Adroit's human and cloned door, hammer and relocate datasets.
