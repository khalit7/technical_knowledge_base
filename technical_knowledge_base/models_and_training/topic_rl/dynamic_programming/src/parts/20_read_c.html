What the stepper shows. It looks like policy evaluation with one difference: each sweep takes a max over actions rather than an average under some π. Equivalently, each sweep is one evaluation sweep of the policy that is greedy on the current values, so value iteration is policy iteration with evaluation cut to a single sweep ({{Sutton and Barto §4.4|http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=104}}). Silver's intuition: "start with final rewards and work backwards"; it "still works with loopy, stochastic MDPs". The answer to the prediction is in frame 2: the far corner says −2 while it is six moves away, and all four of its moves tie, so the values there carry no direction yet; so the intermediate value functions need not be the value of any policy; only the limit is, and it is the optimal policy's ({{Silver, Lecture 3|https://davidstarsilver.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/lecture-3-planning-by-dynamic-programming-.pdf}}). Formally value iteration converges only in the limit, but the greedy policy becomes optimal after finitely many sweeps (Bertsekas 1987, cited in {{Sutton and Barto's notes|http://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=111}}). Between the extremes, "faster convergence is often achieved by interposing multiple policy evaluation sweeps between each policy improvement sweep" (§4.4): modified policy iteration again.

9Why it converges, and how fast

Measure the distance between two value functions by the largest difference in any state, ‖u − v‖∞ = maxs |u(s) − v(s)|, and write T for one synchronous sweep (expectation backup for a fixed π, or optimality backup). Both are γ-contractions ({{Silver, Lecture 3|https://davidstarsilver.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/lecture-3-planning-by-dynamic-programming-.pdf}}):

‖Tu − Tv‖∞ ≤ γ ‖u − v‖∞   ⇒   ‖Vk − V*‖∞ ≤ γk ‖V0 − V*‖∞   ⇒   k ≥ ln(1/ε) / ln(1/γ) sweeps cut the error by 1/ε
why
for the expectation backup the rewards cancel and what is left is γ times an average of differences, at most γ times the largest; for the optimality backup, |maxa f(a) − maxa g(a)| ≤ maxa |f(a) − g(a)| finishes the argument
then
by the contraction mapping theorem (Banach's fixed-point theorem) repeated sweeps converge to a unique fixed point (Vπ or V*) from any start, at a linear rate; the second inequality uses TV* = V*
Two runs from different starts close in at least as fast as γk: the contraction, sweep by sweep
error from zeros, ‖Vk − V*‖error from −1/(1 − γ) everywhere (−100 at γ = 1)gap between the two runsbound γk × the starting gap

Predict, then drag γ to 0.99 and to 1: what happens to the grey bound, and do the runs still converge? Russell and Norvig's 4 × 3 world (section 13 and the Sweep lab), synchronous value iteration, log scale. The gap between the runs (purple) never rises above the bound (grey), checked sweep by sweep in src/recompute.py at γ = 0.5, 0.9 and 0.99. The errors fall faster than the bound because the bound is a worst case and the 4 × 3 world's paths end within a few steps. At γ = 1 the bound is flat and useless, yet both runs still converge: termination, not contraction.

How many sweeps. With γ = 0.9, cutting the error by a factor of 1,000 takes 66 sweeps (0.966 ≈ 0.00096); with γ = 0.99 it takes 688 derived. Since ln(1/γ) ≈ 1 − γ, the sweep count grows like the effective horizon 1/(1 − γ): rewards that far away still matter, and each sweep reaches one step further.

A small change is not a small error. If one sweep changes no value by more than θ, the contraction gives ‖Vk+1 − V*‖∞ ≤ γθ/(1 − γ): with γ = 0.99 and θ = 0.001 the values can still be off by up to 0.099, about a hundred times the last change. A small error is a small loss, though: acting greedily on any V with ‖V − V*‖∞ ≤ ε loses at most 2γε/(1 − γ) of return in any state ({{Singh and Yee 1994|https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00993308}}, in the form given by {{RL Theory, lecture 3|https://rltheory.github.io/lecture-notes/planning-in-mdps/lec3/}}).

Without discounting. At γ = 1 there is no contraction, yet Figure 4.1, Silver's shortest path and the 4 × 3 world all use γ = 1 and converge. What saves them is termination: if every policy evaluated eventually reaches a terminal state (a "proper" policy), the sweeps still converge, a stochastic-shortest-path result outside this page's scope (Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis 1996). Drop that and an undiscounted evaluation of a policy that loops forever on a −1 reward runs off to −∞. γ = 1 is not wrong in itself: it is standard when every episode terminates, as in LLM RL.

The standard iteration count (depth)

Szepesvári's lecture notes give the usual form: k ≥ ln(1/(ε(1 − γ)))/(1 − γ) iterations bring value iteration within ε of V* ({{RL Theory, lecture 3|https://rltheory.github.io/lecture-notes/planning-in-mdps/lec3/}}). It follows from the bound above: with rewards in [0, 1] and V0 = 0 the starting error is at most 1/(1 − γ), and ln(1/γ) ≥ 1 − γ derived.
