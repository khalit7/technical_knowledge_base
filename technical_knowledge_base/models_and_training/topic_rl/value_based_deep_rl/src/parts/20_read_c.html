5The dueling network: value plus advantage

The {{dueling architecture (Wang et al. 2015)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06581}} changes only the head: after the shared convolutions it splits into a state-value stream V(s) and an advantage stream A(s, a), then recombines them. "For many states, it is unnecessary to estimate the value of each action choice" (in Enduro, left or right "only matters when a collision is eminent"), but bootstrapping needs a good state value everywhere; with its own stream, V(s) improves on every update, whichever action was taken. Example: V = 5 and A = (1, −1, 3), whose mean is 1.

Two ways to recombine the same streams: the ranking of actions survives, the meaning of V does not

Q(s, a) = V(s) + ( A(s, a) − (1/|𝒜|) Σa′ A(s, a′) )
|𝒜|
the number of actions
why subtract
Q = V + A alone is unidentifiable (add a constant to V, subtract it from every A, and Q is unchanged), "mirrored by poor practical performance when this equation is used directly"

Subtracting the max, Q = V + (A − maxa′ A), makes V exactly the state value: (3, 1, 5) in the example. The mean, (5, 3, 7) derived, "loses the original semantics of V and A" but "increases the stability of the optimization", because the advantages "only need to change as fast as the mean". Evidence: on a corridor task with exact Q-values the two architectures tied with 5 actions, and the dueling network's error fell faster as no-op actions were added (10, then 20). On 57 Atari games, trained with Double DQN, the dueling network ("Duel Clip") beat the same single-stream network with the same gradient clipping on 75.4% of games (43 of 57).

Training details and the full score table (under 1 min)

The combined gradient into the last convolutional layer was rescaled by 1/√2 and gradients were clipped to norm 10. Mean and median human-normalised scores, 30 no-op starts (their Table 1): Nature DQN 227.9% and 79.1%; single stream 307.3% and 117.8%; single stream with clipping 341.2% and 132.6%; dueling with clipping 373.1% and 151.5%; dueling with prioritised replay 591.9% and 172.1%. Under human starts the dueling gain over the clipped single stream is smaller (343.8% and 117.1% against 302.8% and 114.1%).

6Prioritised experience replay

Uniform replay replays every transition as often as it happened, informative or not. {{Prioritised experience replay (Schaul et al. 2015)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952}} replays transitions with a large TD error more often, as prioritised sweeping orders planning updates. Its "Blind Cliffwalk" has n states and a reward that random actions reach with probability 2−n, so the one useful transition is buried among failures; an oracle choosing what to replay learns exponentially faster than uniform replay. Predict: with the largest error 60 times the smallest, how much more often is it sampled at α = 0.6?

Settings and evidence. The proportional version samples in O(log N) with a sum-tree (each node holds the sum of its children's priorities). It beat uniform replay "on 41 out of 49 games". Median and mean, human starts (their Table 1): DQN 48% and 122%, rank-based on DQN 106% and 355% (49 games); tuned Double DQN 111% and 418%, rank-based 113% and 454%, proportional 128% and 551% (57 games).

7Multi-step targets

A one-step target moves reward information back one state per update; an n-step target uses n real rewards before bootstrapping. On a corridor with the only reward at the end, every synchronous sweep of backups carries it n states further.

With n-step targets the reward travels n states per sweep instead of one

A 12-state corridor illustrative: one action, reward 1 on leaving the last state, γ = 0.9, values start at 0; each sweep backs up every state at once with the n-step target, so a state's value turns on once it is within k·n steps of the reward after k sweeps (shade: its value γd−1, d steps from the reward). The start needs ⌈12/n⌉ sweeps derived: 12 at n = 1, 4 at n = 3.

y(n) = Σk=0n−1 γk rt+k+1 + γn maxa′ Q(st+n, a′; θ−)
n
steps of real reward; n = 1 is DQN's target, n = ∞ a Monte Carlo return

The cost: the n − 1 intermediate actions were chosen by the behaviour policy (ε-greedy, and old for replayed data), not the greedy policy whose value the target claims, so the uncorrected target is biased off-policy. Rainbow and R2D2 accept the bias (n = 3 and n = 5); Rainbow found n "a sensitive hyper-parameter", 3 and 5 both good early and 3 best by the end. {{Retrace (Munos et al. 2016)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02647}} corrects it by cutting traces with truncated importance weights; Agent57 uses a Retrace loss. Multi-step targets also bootstrap less, and in the 2018 triad study longer n clearly reduced soft divergence (§2). The bias-variance dial, tabular n-step and TD(λ) are on {{Model-free prediction and control|n:3c65c17b0d0d81898a0cd4c05c744969}}.

8Distributional RL: C51, QR-DQN and IQN

A Q-value is the mean of a random return Z(s, a). {{Bellemare, Dabney and Munos (2017)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06887}} learn the whole distribution with the distributional Bellman equation: Z(s, a) has the same distribution as R + γZ(S′, A′).

C51 puts probabilities on N fixed atoms zi = VMIN + iΔz, Δz = (VMAX − VMIN)/(N − 1); the Atari agent uses N = 51 and VMAX = −VMIN = 10, hence the name. The network outputs a softmax over atoms per action and acts greedily on the means. The target is the next state's distribution, discounted and shifted, then put back on the grid. Step through it:

The distributional target keeps the losing and surviving outcomes apart; DQN's target averages them

The pseudocode computes bj = (T̂zj − VMIN)/Δz, l = ⌊bj⌋, u = ⌈bj⌉ and adds pj(u − bj) to atom l and pj(bj − l) to atom u. When bj is a whole number, l = u and both amounts are zero, so that atom's probability vanishes. With r = 0 and γ = 1 the whole target is lost; at the defaults the two end atoms land on the grid (b = 1 and b = 10 at N = 11) and their mass disappears derived. Evidence. Across 57 games (best evaluation score during training), C51 reached 701% mean and 178% median human-normalised, against DQN 228% and 79%. The authors' reading: the learned distributions keep "the low-value, 'losing' event" separate from "the high-value, 'survival' event, rather than average them into one (unrealizable) expectation".

QR-DQN ({{Dabney et al. 2017|https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10044}}) fixes the probabilities and learns the locations: N quantiles at the midpoints τ̂i = (2i − 1)/(2N), trained by quantile regression with an asymmetric Huber loss (κ = 1). That minimises a Wasserstein distance directly, closing C51's gap between theory (Wasserstein contraction) and algorithm (a KL loss after a projection), and drops VMIN and VMAX. IQN ({{Dabney et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06923}}) samples τ uniformly from [0, 1] and feeds it to the network, so one network represents the whole quantile function; distorting how τ is sampled gives risk-sensitive policies (CVaR acts on the worst η of outcomes).

Convergence, atom counts, IQN's embedding and every published score (1 min)

For a fixed policy the distributional Bellman operator is a contraction in a maximal form of the Wasserstein metric; for control, Proposition 1 shows it "is not a contraction in any metric over distributions", although the means still converge to Q* exponentially fast.

The 51-atom network trains at about 75% of DQN's speed. On five training games more atoms always helped, and the 51-atom agent beat DQN on all five (the two-atom "Bernoulli" variant on 3 of 5). C51's comparison over 57 games (their Figure 6, a table): Double DQN 307% and 118%, dueling 373% and 151%, prioritised 434% and 124%, prioritised dueling 592% and 172%.

QR-DQN with N = 200: 915% mean and 211% median over 57 games (QR-DQN-1, best scores; QR-DQN-0 without the Huber smoothing 881% and 199%). IQN's cosine embedding is φj(τ) = ReLU(Σi=0n−1 cos(πiτ) wij + bj) with n = 64; eight samples of τ per update were enough, and K = 32 samples choose actions. IQN's Table 1 (57 games, 30 no-op starts, averaged over seeds): IQN 1019% mean and 218% median (5 seeds), QR-DQN 864% and 193% (3 seeds), Rainbow 1189% and 230% (2 seeds); under human starts IQN's median is 162% against Rainbow's 153%. Note that IQN's table prints QR-DQN at 193% median and Rainbow at 230%, while their own papers print 211% and 223%: different seeds, averaging and protocol (§12).

9NoisyNet: exploration in the weights

ε-greedy explores by occasionally pressing a random button, the same way in every state. {{NoisyNet (Fortunato et al. 2017)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10295}} replaces a linear layer y = wx + b with a noisy one whose noise scale is learned along with the weights:

y = (μw + σw ⊙ εw) x + μb + σb ⊙ εb
μ, σ
learnable means and noise scales of the weights and biases
ε
noise: independent, one Gaussian per weight, pq + q variables for p inputs and q outputs; or factorised, p + q unit Gaussians combined as εi,j = f(εi)f(εj) with f(x) = sgn(x)√|x|, used for DQN and dueling to save random-number generation. For DQN's 512-unit layer on its 3,136 convolutional outputs (7 × 7 × 64) that is 3,648 draws per resample against 1,606,144 derived

Factorised noise: 10 random numbers instead of 28 for a layer with 6 inputs and 4 outputs

NoisyNet-DQN drops ε-greedy and acts greedily on the noisy network. Because σ is learned, the network can shrink its noise where it is sure and keep it where it is not: state-dependent, temporally consistent exploration. Median human-normalised scores, no-op starts (their Table 1): DQN 83% to NoisyNet-DQN 123%; dueling 132% to 172%; A3C 80% to 94%.

The text says NoisyNet adds "at least 18 (in the case of A3C) and at most 48 (in the case of DQN) percentage points to the median". Table 1 says otherwise: the median gains are 40 points for DQN (83 to 123) and 14 for A3C (80 to 94); 48% and 18% are relative improvements (123/83 and 94/80) derived. The parent's Milestones tab records the same finding.
Resampling, initialisation and mean scores (under 1 min)

The noise is resampled before every action and held fixed across each replayed minibatch; independent noise samples are used for the online network, the target network and acting, "to avoid bias due to the correlation".

Factorised: μ uniform in [−1/√p, 1/√p], σ = σ0/√p with σ0 = 0.5; independent: μ uniform in [−√(3/p), √(3/p)], σ = 0.017. Mean scores (Table 1): DQN 319% to 379%; dueling 524% to 633%; A3C 293% to 347%.
