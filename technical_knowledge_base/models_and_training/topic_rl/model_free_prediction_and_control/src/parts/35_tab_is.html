One blackjack state, from someone else's play

Example 5.4 and Figure 5.3 ({{book p. 106|https://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=128}}); blackjack as in Example 5.1 ({{p. 93|https://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=115}})

The state: the dealer shows a 2, the player's cards sum to 13 with a usable ace (an ace counted as 11, plus a 2). The target policy π sticks only on 20 or 21. The data come from a behaviour policy b that hits or sticks at random, each with probability ½. Cards come from an infinite deck; the dealer hits below 17; win +1, draw 0, loss −1. Each episode's return G is weighted by the importance ratio ρ, the product over the player's decisions of π(A | S) / b(A | S): 2 for every decision that matches π, 0 as soon as one does not. Ordinary importance sampling averages ρG over all episodes; weighted importance sampling divides the same sum by the sum of the ratios.

    When ordinary importance sampling never settles

    Example 5.5 and Figure 5.4 ({{book p. 106 to 108|https://incompleteideas.net/book/RLbook2020.pdf#page=128}})

    One non-terminal state s and two actions. Right ends the episode with reward 0. Left returns to s with probability 0.9 (reward 0) or ends with reward +1 with probability 0.1. The target policy always goes left, so vπ(s) = 1. The behaviour policy goes left or right with probability ½. An episode that ever goes right has ρ = 0; one that goes left k times and then ends has ρ = 2k and G = 1. Every scaled return has expectation 1, yet its variance is infinite:

    E[(ρG)2] = Σk≥1 (½)k 0.9k−1 0.1 · (2k)2 = 0.2 Σj≥0 1.8j = ∞
    k
    the number of left actions in the episode (the last one ends it)
    (½)k 0.9k−1 0.1
    the probability of that episode under the behaviour policy

    The book runs to 100 million episodes; 10 million is the most this page offers on a phone. Weighted importance sampling is drawn dashed: it is exactly 1 from the first episode that follows the target policy, because only returns consistent with π get any weight, and all of them are 1.
