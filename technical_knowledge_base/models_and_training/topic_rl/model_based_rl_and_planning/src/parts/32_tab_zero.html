Self-play lab: AlphaZero on tic-tac-toe

A small, complete replication of the AlphaGo Zero / AlphaZero loop (Reading section 7), trained offline for this page and run here with the real network weights. One network f(s) = (p, v) with 18 inputs (the mover's stones and the opponent's), one hidden layer of 64 units, 9 move logits and a tanh value. Every self-play move was chosen by a 50-simulation PUCT search (c = 1.25, no rollouts), with Dirichlet noise at the root (α = 1, weight 0.25) and moves sampled in proportion to visits for the first 5 moves; the loss is AlphaZero's, (z − v)² − π⊤ log p + 10−4‖θ‖², trained with Adam on the last 8 iterations' games and their 8 board symmetries. 60 iterations of 48 games each took about half a minute on a laptop CPU (src/train_ttt.py). Weights are stored at 8 bits; everything below uses those stored weights. Exact answers come from solving the game tree (5,478 positions, 4,520 with a move to make).

2. Look inside a checkpoint: prior, value and search

Click an empty cell to play it. In each empty cell: the network's prior p (top), the search's visits N (middle), and the exact result of that move for the player to move (W win, D draw, L loss). Shading: visit share.

3. Every checkpoint at a glance

"Optimal" means the move keeps the exact game value (a win stays a win, a draw a draw). Value in the right third: v ≥ 1/3 for won positions, |v| < 1/3 for drawn ones, v ≤ −1/3 for lost ones. Games: 100 against a perfect player that picks at random among optimal moves, 50 as X and 50 as O; the agent plays its prior's top move (raw) or the most visited move of a 50-simulation search. The search columns were computed offline by src/train_ttt.py and src/recompute.py; the others are recomputed here from the stored weights.
