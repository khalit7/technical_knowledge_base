4. Samplers in depth

The problem. A trained denoiser answers one question: "at this noise level, what is the clean image?". A sampler turns those answers into a path from noise to an image. Samplers differ in three ways only: which coordinates they step in (xt, σ or λ, section 1), how many network calls each step costs and how they combine them (the solver's order), and whether they add fresh noise along the way (stochastic or deterministic). Every sampler below ran on the same checkpoint, the lab's ε-model, from the same noise; one network evaluation is the unit of cost (NFE).

One family, one knob: DDIM's η. DDIM's general update interpolates between the deterministic path and DDPM: keep a share of the predicted noise as the direction, replace the rest by fresh noise. Written out (src/runs/dz_samplers.py, the code behind every η number below):

for i, t in enumerate(ts):                       # trailing timesteps, e.g. 999, 899, ... for 10 steps
    ab = abar[t]
    ab_prev = abar[ts[i + 1]] if i + 1 < n else torch.ones_like(ab)
    eps = eps_fn(x, t)
    x0 = (x - (1 - ab).sqrt() * eps) / ab.sqrt()
    sigma = eta * ((1 - ab_prev) / (1 - ab) * (1 - ab / ab_prev)).sqrt() if i + 1 < n else 0 * ab
    x = ab_prev.sqrt() * x0 + (1 - ab_prev - sigma ** 2).clamp(min=0).sqrt() * eps
    if i + 1 < n and eta > 0:
        x = x + sigma * torch.randn(x.shape, generator=gen).to(x.device)

diffusers computes the same σ as std_dev_t = eta * variance ** (0.5) ({{scheduling_ddim.py lines 286 and 481 to 488|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_ddim.py#L481}}).

DPM-Solver++: solve the easy part exactly. In λ coordinates the ODE has a linear part, which can be integrated exactly, and a nonlinear part that is just the network's clean-image guess. DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al. 2022) integrates the first exactly and approximates the second; first order (the guess held constant over the step) is exactly DDIM, and "2M" fits a line through this step's and the previous step's guesses, a second-order step for one network call:

h, h_0 = lambda_t - lambda_s0, lambda_s0 - lambda_s1
r0 = h_0 / h
D0, D1 = m0, (1.0 / r0) * (m0 - m1)                          # m0, m1: this and the previous x0 guess
x_t = (
    (sigma_t / sigma_s0) * sample
    - (alpha_t * (torch.exp(-h) - 1.0)) * D0
    - 0.5 * (alpha_t * (torch.exp(-h) - 1.0)) * D1
)

Where the steps land. Ten steps can be spread over the trajectory in different ways, and the spacing matters as much as the solver at few steps (the lab's "leading" spacing trap is the extreme case). "Trailing" spaces timesteps evenly from 999; Karras et al.'s spacing (ρ = 7, {{scheduling_euler_discrete.py line 551|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_euler_discrete.py#L551}}) spaces σ1/7 evenly, which crowds steps at low noise. On SD 1.5's σ range, ten Karras σs are derived. The {{Sampler workbench|#t-dzsamp}} lists the exact timesteps each scheduler walked on the tiny model.

Why the stochastic sampler won here

The lab found that ancestral DDPM beats DDIM from 4 steps up on this model, the opposite of the DDIM paper's CIFAR-10 result (). The lab's explanation was that fresh noise lets later steps correct earlier errors. Three tests of that explanation:

Predict: on the exact-score toy, give the model a typical under-training error, a denoiser that hedges between bumps it cannot yet tell apart (its posterior over bumps is flattened, temperature 2). Which sampler suffers more?
DDIM. With the hedging denoiser DDIM's distance to the truth stops falling at about however many steps it takes ( at 50, at 1,000), with of samples stranded between bumps; DDPM keeps improving to with between bumps measured (gmm_toy.py, 20,000 samples). The same plateau the tiny model shows. Why: the ODE transports noise to exactly the distribution the model's denoiser implies, errors included, and a hedging denoiser implies a smeared one. The stochastic sampler re-noises and re-denoises at every step; each re-denoise at lower noise pulls the sample toward wherever the denoiser is confident, which is on a bump.
