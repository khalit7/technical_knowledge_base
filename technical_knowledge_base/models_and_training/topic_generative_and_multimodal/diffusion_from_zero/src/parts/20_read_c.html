2. Noise schedules in depth

The problem. The schedule (the list of βt, or equivalently āt) fixes two things at once: how the 1,000 training timesteps are spread over noise levels, and what the model sees at the very last one. The lab compared three schedules by their curves; this section is about the two places schedules quietly fail: the end point, and resolution.

One axis for all schedules. Write the noise level as the log signal-to-noise ratio λ = log(ā / (1 − ā)). A schedule is then just a way of spending timesteps along λ: the tiny model's linear schedule spends of its timesteps where noise dominates (SNR < 1 from t = 259), the cosine schedule half, SD 1.5's scaled-linear derived. Two schedules that cover the same λ range differ only in how densely they sample it, which is why later work (EDM, section 3) drops the timestep altogether and samples noise levels directly.

The end point: what survives "pure noise"

Predict: at SD 1.5's last training step, √ā = 0.068, so each latent value keeps 6.8% of its amplitude and its SNR is 0.0047. How visible is the image's average brightness (the mean of one latent channel over its 64 × 64 values) to the model at that step?
Clearly visible. Averaging 4,096 independent noise values shrinks the noise's standard deviation 64 times but leaves the signal's average alone, so the SNR of the average is 4,096 × 0.0047 = derived (recompute.py). That is the per-value SNR of step of 1,000, an almost clean image. So in training, even at t = 999, the model can read the image's overall brightness off its input and learns to keep it. At sampling time the start is real noise whose average is about 0, so the model faithfully keeps "medium brightness". Lin et al. (2023) traced SD's inability to make very dark or very bright images to exactly this non-zero terminal SNR.

Two fixes, one hack and one cure.

  • Offset noise (Guttenberg, Crosslabs, 2023): during training add a random constant per image and channel to the noise, k · ξ with k around 0.1. diffusers' fine-tuning script has it as --noise_offset ({{train_text_to_image.py lines 973 to 976|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/examples/text_to_image/train_text_to_image.py#L973}}, v0.41.0). The averaged noise now has variance (1 − ā)(1/n + k²), so the brightness SNR at the last step falls from to at k = 0.1 derived. It works, but it trains on a noise distribution the sampler never starts from, and the brightness signal is reduced, not removed.
  • Zero terminal SNR (Lin et al. 2023, Algorithm 1): rescale √ā so its last value is exactly 0 and its first is unchanged, then rebuild the βs. diffusers has it as rescale_betas_zero_snr=True:
alphas_bar_sqrt -= alphas_bar_sqrt_T                                        # shift so the last timestep is zero
alphas_bar_sqrt *= alphas_bar_sqrt_0 / (alphas_bar_sqrt_0 - alphas_bar_sqrt_T)  # scale so the first is unchanged
{{diffusers v0.41.0, schedulers/scheduling_ddim.py lines 103 to 137|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_ddim.py#L103}} (rescale_zero_terminal_snr).

The cost of the cure. At SNR exactly 0 the input is the noise, so an ε-predicting network has nothing to learn there, and the clean-image guess x̂0 = (xt − √(1 − ā) ε̂) / √ā divides by zero. The paper therefore pairs it with v-prediction, fine-tuning, sampling that starts at the last timestep ("trailing"), and CFG rescale (section 5). What happens if you only flip the flag on SD 1.5:

Resolution: the same schedule is gentler on bigger images

The averaging argument applies to any patch, not just the whole image: at 4 × the pixels, every 2 × 2 block's average has 4 × the SNR of a single value, so coarse structure survives more noise. A schedule tuned at 32 × 32 therefore destroys too little of a 512 × 512 image's layout at its noisiest steps. Stable Diffusion 3 does the same for flow matching with a timestep shift; that belongs to the flow-matching sibling page and the root's {{Flow lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ab6ade318917bff55}}.

3. What the network predicts, and how the loss weights each noise level

The problem. Given xt, the clean image and the noise determine each other, so the network can be trained to output either, or a mix. The choice does not change what a perfect network knows; it changes which noise levels the loss cares about and how errors are amplified at sampling time. The lab trained ε and v models and showed where each one's error lives by timestep; here is the bookkeeping behind it.

Every target is a weighting of one loss. Write every loss as a weight times the clean-image error ||x̂0 − x0||². Because ε̂ − ε = √(ā/(1 − ā)) (x0 − x̂0), the ε loss is the x0 loss times SNR; the v loss is it times SNR + 1 derived. Multiply by how often training draws each noise level and you have what the optimiser actually spends its effort on:

EDM's preconditioning, in code. Karras et al. wrap the raw network F so that its input and its target have unit variance at every σ, and so that at low noise it mostly copies its input: D(x; σ) = cskip(σ) x + cout(σ) F(cin(σ) x; cnoise(σ)). diffusers ships the same scalings for EDM-style models:

c_noise = 0.25 * torch.log(sigma)                                    # line 210
c_skip = sigma_data**2 / (sigma**2 + sigma_data**2)                   # line 236
    c_out = sigma * sigma_data / (sigma**2 + sigma_data**2) ** 0.5    # line 239 (default branch)
c_in = 1 / ((sigma**2 + self.config.sigma_data**2) ** 0.5)            # line 596
{{diffusers v0.41.0 schedulers/scheduling_edm_euler.py lines 210 and 236 to 239|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_edm_euler.py#L236}} (precondition_noise, precondition_outputs and _get_conditioning_c_in), default σdata = 0.5 (line 92).

At high σ cskip → 0 and the network predicts the image (an x0-style target, no division by a vanishing √ā); at low σ cskip → 1 and it predicts a small correction (an ε-style target). It is v-prediction's idea made exact, and the reason EDM-family models handle both ends without special cases. The same cskip and cout reappear in consistency models (section 6), where they enforce that the model returns its input at σ = 0 (diffusers' {{scheduling_lcm.py lines 565 to 566|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_lcm.py#L565}}).
