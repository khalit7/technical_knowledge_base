3. The denoiser, what it predicts, and the training loss

The network. The denoiser takes the noisy image xt, the timestep t and (here) the class label, and returns a tensor of the image's shape. The tiny model is the shared TinyUNet, parameters: a convolutional U-Net that shrinks the image from 32 to 16 to 8 pixels and grows it back, with skip connections between matching sizes and self-attention at 8 × 8; t enters as a sinusoidal embedding and the label as a learned embedding with an 11th "no label" row, both added inside every residual block. Production denoisers (SD's U-Net, diffusion transformers) are the {{latent-diffusion page|n:3fc5c17b0d0d814fbcb5e0c8df5289f3}}'s subject.

Three things it can be asked for. Given xt and t, the clean image and the noise determine each other, so the network can be trained to output either, or a mix, and the sampler converts. The choice does not change what a perfect network knows; it changes which noise levels the loss cares about and how errors are amplified at sampling time:

The training step, as it ran in the root lab (src/diff/train_ddpm.py): a random t, fresh noise, the closed form of section 1, mean squared error against the target. The label is replaced by the null class 10% of the time, so one network also learns the unconditional model that guidance needs (section 6).

def loss_fn(model, b):
    x0, y = b
    t = torch.randint(0, sched.T, (x0.shape[0],), device=dev)
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    pred = model(sched.q_sample(x0, t, eps), t.float() / sched.T, drop_labels(y, 0.1))
    loss = F.mse_loss(pred, target(x0, eps, t))     # target: eps, or v = sqrt(abar) eps - sqrt(1 - abar) x0
    return loss, {"loss": loss.item()}

Recipe: batch 128, AdamW at 5 × 10−4 after 200 warm-up steps, gradient clipping at 1, an exponential moving average (EMA) of the weights with decay 0.999 used for all sampling, steps in minutes ( steps per second on the M1 Pro's GPU) measured. Two models were trained this way, one per target: ε and v. Why predicting noise is the right loss for DDPM, from the ELBO, is on the {{DDPM paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d811481e4e36333454f7a}} and the {{math page, sections 13 and 15|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}}.

Every target is a weighting of one loss. Write every loss as a weight times the clean-image error ||x̂0 − x0||². Because ε̂ − ε = √(ā/(1 − ā)) (x0 − x̂0), the ε loss is the x0 loss times SNR, and the v loss is it times SNR + 1 derived. EDM's preconditioning and Min-SNR, in code

Karras et al. wrap the raw network F so that its input and its target have unit variance at every σ, and so that at low noise it mostly copies its input: D(x; σ) = cskip(σ) x + cout(σ) F(cin(σ) x; cnoise(σ)).

At high σ cskip → 0 and the network predicts the image (an x0-style target, no division by a vanishing √ā); at low σ cskip → 1 and it predicts a small correction (an ε-style target). It is v-prediction's idea made exact, and the reason EDM-family models handle both ends without special cases. The same cskip and cout reappear in consistency models (section 7), where they make the model return its input at σ = 0 (diffusers' {{scheduling_lcm.py lines 565 to 566|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_lcm.py#L565}}).

Training details that change samples

EMA of the weights. An exponential moving average keeps a second copy of the weights, updated as wema ← d wema + (1 − d) w after every step; with d = 0.999 it averages roughly the last 1,000 steps, and sampling uses it. Diffusion training is noisy (every step sees a random noise level), and the average sits closer to the bottom of the loss valley than any single iterate. diffusers' EMAModel defaults to d = 0.9999 with optional warm-up ({{training_utils.py line 632|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/training_utils.py#L632}}); EDM2 ({{Karras et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02696}}) stores snapshots so the EMA length can be chosen after training.

Condition dropout. Guidance needs the unconditional model, so 10% of training labels (or prompts) are replaced by a null label. SD 1.5 used 10% text dropout (the model card).

What transfers from the tiny model. Mechanisms transfer: how targets weight noise levels, what each sampler does with a model's errors, how guidance trades fidelity for variety, what each bug looks like. Absolute numbers do not: a 1M-parameter model trained for 15 minutes on 28 × 28 grey images is far less accurate than a production model, which is exactly why the deterministic sampler loses here (section 5); well-trained models favour DPM-Solver-class ODE samplers at 20 to 30 steps.
