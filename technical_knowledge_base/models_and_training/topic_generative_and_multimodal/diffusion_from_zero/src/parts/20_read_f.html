Common mistakes, with the real outputs

Each bug below was run on the tiny ε-model (DDIM, 50 steps, the lab's noise and scoring) or on SD 1.5, so the symptom is what you would actually see. The lab's own list covers three more: a v-model sampled as ε, "leading" timestep spacing at few steps, and guidance_scale ≤ 1 meaning no guidance in diffusers.

The same ten noise images, sampled correctly and with each bug

Grey fog: an σ-space scheduler started from unit noise

Cause. Euler, Heun, DPM-Solver with Karras sigmas and the other k-diffusion-style schedulers work on x̃ = xt / √ā, whose starting noise has standard deviation σmax ( for the tiny model's schedule with trailing spacing, about 14.6 for SD 1.5). A hand-written loop that skips x = x * scheduler.init_noise_sigma starts with noise times too small, and scale_model_input then shrinks it again. Check: print scheduler.init_noise_sigma; the pipeline does the multiplication at {{pipeline_stable_diffusion.py line 713|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L713}}.

Noise or blobs at every step count: the timestep in the wrong units

Cause. The network was trained with t / T in [0, 1) (the shared TinyUNet) and is fed the integer t, or a diffusers U-Net trained on integer t is fed a fraction. The time embedding then describes a noise level the network never saw. Check: compare the range of t in the training loop with the sampler's call.

Plausible but wrong: a schedule mismatch between training and sampling

Cause. Sampling with ā from a different schedule than the model was trained with (a cosine scheduler config on a linear-schedule model, or SD 1.5's scaled-linear on a model fine-tuned with something else). Each step's conversion from ε̂ to x̂0 then uses the wrong noise level. Check: diff the scheduler config against the training config (beta_schedule, beta_start, beta_end, num_train_timesteps, rescale_betas_zero_snr).

NaN or black images: zero terminal SNR switched on for an ε-model

Cause. rescale_betas_zero_snr=True makes ā at the last step exactly 0, and turning ε̂ into x̂0 divides by √ā. Zero terminal SNR is for v-prediction models fine-tuned on that schedule (section 2).

Clipping that helps pixels and breaks latents: clip_sample

Cause. diffusers' DDPMScheduler and DDIMScheduler default to clip_sample=True, clamping x̂0 to [−1, 1] at every step. That suits pixel models trained on [−1, 1] images; a latent model's latents are not bounded that way, which is why SD 1.5's scheduler config sets clip_sample false.

Blotchy samples from a model whose loss looks fine: sampling the raw weights

Cause. The training loop's own weights are noisier than their EMA; checkpoints often hold both.

"DDIM is always better than DDPM"

True for a well-trained model at 10 to 100 steps (DDIM Table 1), false for an under-trained one (section 4). A sampler ranking measured on one checkpoint does not transfer to another; re-run the sampler sweep after each training run that matters.

Interview questions

Explain how a network trained to predict noise is a score model.
Tweedie's formula: the posterior mean is E[x0|xt] = (xt + (1 − ā) ∇ log pt) / √ā. Rearranging the noise model gives ε̂ = −√(1 − ā) ∇ log pt(xt). So minimising the ε loss is denoising score matching with a per-level scale; the sampler can use the network as a score in an SDE or an ODE.
Why does SD 1.5 struggle to make very dark or very bright images, and what would you do in a fine-tune?
Its schedule leaves √ā = 0.068 at the last step; per value that is little, but the per-channel average over 4,096 values keeps an SNR of about 19, so the model learns to read brightness off its input even at t = 999, and pure noise at sampling has average 0. Fine-tune with zero terminal SNR plus v-prediction, sample from the last timestep (trailing) and use CFG rescale (Lin et al.); the cheap patch is offset noise (about 0.1) in the fine-tune.
DDPM, DDIM or DPM-Solver++: which would you ship, and when would you pick a stochastic sampler?
For a well-trained text-to-image model: DPM-Solver++ 2M (or UniPC) at 20 to 30 steps with trailing or Karras spacing, deterministic, so a seed reproduces an image. Pick a stochastic sampler (ancestral, SDE variants, EDM churn) when the model's errors dominate (under-trained, small, or out of distribution) and you can afford more steps; measure both, since the ranking depends on the checkpoint (here DDPM won).
Your guided samples are oversaturated at scale 10. Three fixes and their costs?
CFG rescale (free, φ = 0.7; slightly flatter images), a guidance interval (free, saves calls; needs the interval tuned), a lower scale (loses prompt adherence). For pixel models, dynamic thresholding. If you can train a second model, autoguidance avoids the trade-off between diversity and quality that CFG makes.
What does the ε loss weight, and what does Min-SNR change?
In clean-image units the ε loss weights each level by its SNR, so nearly clean timesteps dominate the gradient; x0 does the opposite. Min-SNR caps the weight at γ = 5, which takes effort away from easy low-noise steps and conflicting gradients; Hang et al. report 3.4 × faster convergence on ImageNet 256 (published).
How do consistency models and LCMs get to 1 to 4 steps, and what do they lose?
They train a student to map any point on a teacher's ODE trajectory straight to its end point (self-consistency along the trajectory), with cskip/cout so the map is the identity at σ = 0; sampling is one jump, optionally re-noise and jump again. They lose some diversity and fine detail, and classifier-free guidance becomes a training-time setting (LCM bakes in a guidance range; run at scale 1 to 2).
Design: serve a text-to-image model at under 1 second per 1024 × 1024 image on one GPU, starting from a base model that needs 30 guided steps.
Count calls: 30 × 2 = 60 denoiser passes plus a VAE decode. Steps: switch to DPM-Solver++ 2M at 20 to 25 steps (no training). Guidance: distil it into the model (one call per step) or guide only within an interval (fewer double calls). Then distil steps: LCM/LCM-LoRA or adversarial distillation (ADD, DMD2) to 4 steps, which is 4 calls; check prompt adherence and diversity against the teacher on a fixed prompt set (CLIPScore, a preference model, FID on a held-out set). Then per-call cost: bf16, compiled graphs, a fast VAE decoder, batching. Trade-offs to state: distillation loses some diversity and editability; keep the base model for high-quality modes.
Why does zero terminal SNR force v-prediction?
At SNR 0 the input equals the noise, so ε is trivially the input (nothing to learn) and the clean-image guess divides by √ā = 0. v = √ā ε − √(1 − ā) x0 becomes −x0 there: a well-posed target (predict the average image), and no division.
