5. Guidance in depth

Where the lab left off. Classifier-free guidance (CFG) evaluates the network with and without the condition and extrapolates: ε̂ = εu + s (εc − εu), where s is diffusers' guidance_scale (s = 1 is the plain conditional model; the CFG paper's w is s − 1). The {{Diffusion lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ab6ade318917bff55}} measured what it buys and costs: on the tiny model class accuracy rises from 77.8% at s = 1 to 96.4% at s = 5 while diversity falls; on SD 1.5 colour saturation climbs from 0.35 at s = 1 to 0.61 at s = 20 measured. Its predecessor, classifier guidance (Dhariwal and Nichol 2021), pushed along the gradient of a separate classifier trained on noisy images; CFG removed the classifier by letting the model be its own. This section is about why high scales break images and the five fixes in use.

Why high scales oversaturate. Extrapolating past the conditional prediction is not a probability-preserving operation: the guided ε̂ can imply a clean-image guess far outside the range real images occupy, and each step feeds that back in. On the tiny model the share of pixels whose guessed value lies beyond the [−1, 1] range rises from at s = 1 to at s = 15 (the lab's sweep) measured; on a photo model that is blown highlights and neon colours.

CFG rescale, in code. The guided prediction's standard deviation (per image, over all values) is set back to the conditional prediction's, which removes the overall "too much" while keeping the direction:

std_text = noise_pred_text.std(dim=list(range(1, noise_pred_text.ndim)), keepdim=True)
std_cfg = noise_cfg.std(dim=list(range(1, noise_cfg.ndim)), keepdim=True)
# rescale the results from guidance (fixes overexposure)
noise_pred_rescaled = noise_cfg * (std_text / std_cfg)
# mix with the original results from guidance by factor guidance_rescale to avoid "plain looking" images
noise_cfg = guidance_rescale * noise_pred_rescaled + (1 - guidance_rescale) * noise_cfg

Guidance interval. Kynkäänniemi et al. found guidance harmful at the noisiest steps (it collapses variety when the layout is being chosen) and nearly useless at the cleanest (the image is decided).

Autoguidance. CFG's unconditional model differs from the conditional one in what it models (all images, not this class), so extrapolating away from it changes the content, which is where the lost diversity comes from. Autoguidance extrapolates away from a worse model of the same thing, so the push is towards "better", not "more class-like".

6. Few steps: distillation

The problem. The price of an image is network calls: steps × 2 with guidance. SD 1.5 at 25 guided steps is 50 U-Net passes, s per 512 × 512 image here measured (lab). Better solvers bring 1,000 steps down to 10 to 25 (section 4), but no solver can follow a curved path with one or two straight steps: the clean-image guess at pure noise is a blurry average (section 1), and one step lands on it. Below about 8 steps you need a second model trained to jump: distillation, where a student learns to reproduce in a few steps what the teacher (the original model plus its sampler) does in many.

7. Training details that change samples

EMA of the weights. An exponential moving average (EMA) keeps a second copy of the weights, updated as wema ← d wema + (1 − d) w after every step; with decay d = 0.999 it averages roughly the last 1,000 steps. Sampling uses the EMA copy. Diffusion training is noisy (every step sees a random noise level), and the average sits closer to the bottom of the loss valley than any single iterate. diffusers' EMAModel defaults to d = 0.9999 with optional warm-up ({{training_utils.py line 632|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/training_utils.py#L632}}); EDM2 (Karras et al. 2023) stores snapshots so the EMA length can be chosen after training.

Condition dropout. Guidance needs the unconditional model, so 10% of training labels (or prompts) are replaced by a null label. SD 1.5 used 10% text dropout (the model card).

Training length.

What transfers from the tiny model. Mechanisms transfer: how targets weight noise levels, what each sampler does with a model's errors, how guidance trades fidelity for variety, what each bug looks like. Absolute numbers do not: a 1M-parameter model trained for 15 minutes on 28 × 28 grey images is far less accurate than a production model, which is exactly why the deterministic sampler lost here; well-trained models favour DPM-Solver-class ODE samplers at 20 to 30 steps.
