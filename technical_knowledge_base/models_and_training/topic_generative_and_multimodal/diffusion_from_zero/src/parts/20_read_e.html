6. Guidance: trading variety for adherence

The problem. A conditional model asked for "sandal" produces something sandal-like, but its samples drift: some are ambiguous, some look like another class. We want a knob that trades variety for adherence to the condition. Its predecessor, classifier guidance ({{Dhariwal and Nichol 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233}}), pushed along the gradient of a separate classifier trained on noisy images; classifier-free guidance (CFG, {{Ho and Salimans 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598}}) removes the classifier by letting the model be its own.

How it works. Train one network both with and without the label (the 10% dropout of section 3). At sampling, evaluate it both ways and extrapolate from the unconditional prediction past the conditional one:

ε̂ = εu + s · (εc − εu)

where εc and εu are the predictions with the label (or prompt) and with the null label (or empty prompt), and s is diffusers' guidance_scale: 0 is the unconditional model, 1 the plain conditional model, above 1 pushes further in the direction the condition points ({{pipeline_stable_diffusion.py line 1055|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L1055}}). Two traps: the pipeline runs the unconditional branch only when the scale is above 1 ({{line 764|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L764}}), so guidance_scale=0 there is the plain conditional model, not the unconditional one; and papers often write εc + w (εc − εu) with w = s − 1. The cost: two evaluations per step, run as one batch of two (line 1037). The derivation is on the {{math page, section 17|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}}.

Why high scales oversaturate. Extrapolating past the conditional prediction does not preserve a probability distribution: the guided ε̂ can imply a clean-image guess far outside the range real images occupy, and each step feeds that back in. On the tiny model the share of pixels whose guessed value lies beyond [−1, 1] rises from at s = 1 to at s = 15 measured; on SD 1.5 colour saturation climbs from at s = 1 to at 7.5 and at 20 measured (root lab): blown highlights and neon colours. Guidance interval. Kynkäänniemi et al. found guidance harmful at the noisiest steps (it collapses variety while the layout is being chosen) and nearly useless at the cleanest (the image is decided).

Autoguidance. CFG's unconditional model differs from the conditional one in what it models (all images, not this class), so extrapolating away from it changes the content, which is where the lost diversity comes from. Autoguidance extrapolates away from a worse model of the same thing, so the push is towards "better", not "more class-like".

7. Few steps: distillation

The problem. The price of an image is network calls: steps × 2 with guidance. SD 1.5 at 25 guided steps is 50 U-Net passes, s per 512 × 512 image here measured (root lab). Better solvers bring 1,000 steps down to 10 to 25 (section 5), but no solver follows a curved path with one or two straight steps: the clean-image guess at pure noise is a blurry average (section 4), and one step lands on it. Below about 8 steps you need a second model trained to jump: distillation, where a student learns to reproduce in a few steps what the teacher (the original model plus its sampler) does in many.

Seven families of students, all published: progressive distillation (halve the steps repeatedly), guidance distillation (one call for the guided prediction), consistency models and latent consistency models (map any point on the ODE path straight to its end), LCM-LoRA (the same as a LoRA), adversarial distillation (a GAN loss on top) and distribution matching (match the student's output distribution to the teacher's).
