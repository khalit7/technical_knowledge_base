3. The VAE: an encoder that outputs a distribution

The VAE's encoder outputs a mean μ and a log-variance log σ2 per latent dimension: a small Gaussian per image instead of a point. Training decodes a random draw from it and charges for its distance from N(0, I).

# ConvVAE, the topic's src/data/nets.py lines 249 to 252
def forward(self, x):
    mu, logvar = self.encode(x)
    z = mu + torch.exp(0.5 * logvar) * torch.randn_like(mu)     # the reparameterisation trick
    return self.decode(z), mu, logvar

# objectives.py lines 130 to 136
def vae_loss(model, x, y=None, beta=1.0):
    """Negative ELBO (beta = 1) per image: reconstruction + beta * KL(q(z|x) || N(0, I))."""
    logits, mu, logvar = model(x)
    rec = recon_bce(logits, x)
    kl = 0.5 * (mu.pow(2) + logvar.exp() - 1 - logvar).flatten(1).sum(1).mean()
    loss = rec + beta * kl
    return loss, {"loss": loss.item(), "rec": rec.item(), "kl": kl.item()}

Setup (src/vi_mnist/vae.py, PyTorch 2.11 on 2 CPU threads): {{MNIST|https://ossci-datasets.s3.amazonaws.com/mnist/}} handwritten digits, 60,000 for training and 10,000 for testing, binarised (pixel above 127 becomes 1); encoder 784 to 400 (ReLU) to a 2-D mean and log-variance; decoder 2 to 400 (ReLU) to 784 Bernoulli logits; Adam at 10−3, batches of 128, 30 epochs (14,070 steps, about 1.5 to 4 seconds per epoch). A 2-D latent is chosen so it can be drawn; it reconstructs worse than a larger one (the 16-D MNIST sweep in section 5).

What the curves say. Reconstruction falls fast (222.5 nats at step 100, 159 by step 700, 138 at the end on training batches). The KL first falls (7.1 to 5.4 nats by step 700) and then slowly rises to about 6.3: early on the cheapest way to raise the ELBO is to keep codes near the prior; once the decoder can use precise codes, buying information about each image in the latent pays for its KL. On the test set after 30 epochs: reconstruction 143.98 and KL 6.22, so the negative ELBO is 150.20 nats per image. The KL is the number of nats the code carries about each image (at most; it is an upper bound on that mutual information, {{Information theory for ML, section 8|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c6baf3f778a62e14e5}}): about 6.2 nats, 9 bits, enough to say which digit and roughly how it is drawn, not the 144 nats of pixel detail the decoder still has to guess.

The posterior widths are tiny. Averaged over test images, the encoder's standard deviations are 0.066 and 0.086 against the prior's 1: each image owns a small patch of the latent plane. 4. The loss in working form: two numbers, and their units

The two terms balance as the derivation intends only when both are log-probabilities of a whole image, in the same unit; change how either is summed or averaged and you have silently changed β.

loss per image = −log p(x | z)  [summed over all pixels]
+ β · KL(q(z | x) ‖ N(0, I))  [summed over all latent dimensions]
−log p(x | z)
the reconstruction term in nats for the whole image: Bernoulli cross-entropy for the tiny models here, a Gaussian (squared error) or Laplace (absolute error) likelihood elsewhere
KL
the rate: how many nats the code carries about this image on average

Reading the two numbers. The 16-number VAE ends at nats of reconstruction plus of KL per test image: nats, bits per pixel (divide by 1,024 and by ln 2) derived. The KL is the code's budget, bits, about a short sentence.

The Gaussian decoder's σ is a β. Squared error is a Gaussian decoder with fixed σ: −log p(x | z) = Σ (x − x̂)2 / (2σ2) + a constant, so plain "MSE summed + KL" fixes σ2 = 1/2 and the KL's relative weight is 2σ2 derived. On pixels in [−1, 1], σ = 0.1 makes squared error 100 times dearer than σ = 1: the code carries nats instead of and reconstructs at dB instead of , but random-code samples get worse (prior FD against ): section 5's trade-off, reached by moving a "noise level" nobody thinks of as a weight. All 16 dimensions stay active in both. Or learn σ, as the LDM autoencoder does (closed block below). measured

How to check. Log reconstruction and KL separately, in nats per image; check the reconstruction is summed over pixels (or β rescaled); count active units. A KL near zero under a fine-looking loss curve is the signature.

The LDM autoencoder's loss: a learned σ, and LPIPS once per pixel (reference code)

It learns one log-variance for its whole reconstruction term (nll_loss = rec_loss / torch.exp(self.logvar) + self.logvar, {{contperceptual.py line 53|https://github.com/CompVis/latent-diffusion/blob/a506df575647/ldm/modules/losses/contperceptual.py#L53}}), so its effective weight between reconstruction and KL moves during training. In the same function the LPIPS distance, one number per image, is added to the L1 error of every pixel before the sum (lines 48 to 51 and 57: rec_loss = rec_loss + self.perceptual_weight * p_loss, then torch.sum(...) / shape[0]), so the perceptual term counts once per pixel and channel, 786,432 times at 512 × 512.
