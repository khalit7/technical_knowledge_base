Depth: mistakes, rehearsal, what transfers

Common mistakes, with what they look like (13)
Sampling a plain autoencoder from N(0, I)
Its codes live at whatever scale training chose (standard deviation to per dimension here), so N(0, I) samples sit in one corner and decode to one blob (section 2). Print the codes' mean and std before sampling; fit a density, or use a VAE.
Reading a lower reconstruction loss as a better generator
The plain autoencoder reconstructs best and generates worst from N(0, I) (section 2). Score samples separately, with a stated sample size and floor.
Averaging the reconstruction over pixels while summing the KL
An effective β equal to the number of pixels: of 16 active units and every reconstruction the average garment (section 4). Nothing errors; the loss curve looks fine. Check KL per image in nats, and active units.
Plain MSE plus KL, without asking what σ that implies
Summed squared error plus KL is a Gaussian decoder with σ2 = 1/2, and σ is a β (section 4). Choose σ deliberately, or learn it.
Turning β up to fix blurry samples
Higher β makes reconstructions blurrier and switches dimensions off (section 5). Blur comes from the pixel loss; the cure is a perceptual or adversarial loss, or a stronger generator on top of the latent.
A powerful decoder and a KL that drops to zero
Posterior collapse: the PixelCNN decoder here ended with a KL of nats and decoded the same picture whatever the code (section 6). Run the shuffle test; use free bits or limit the decoder.
A large codebook with the default initialisation and no usage logging
At K = 1,024 the plain VQ recipe used codes on the test set (section 8). Log codes used and perplexity; restart dead codes, use EMA with k-means initialisation or normalised codes, or switch to FSQ.
Trusting a library's commitment loss without reading it
diffusers' VectorQuantizer applies β to the wrong term unless legacy=False (section 8).
The wrong scaling factor, or a forgotten shift
Each autoencoder has its own (SD 1.x 0.18215, SDXL 0.13025, FLUX.1 a shift and a scale). Missing at encode time, the denoiser sees inputs far cleaner than the timestep says; missing at decode time, colours wash out or blow up (section 11). Check that the std of normalised latents of real images is near 1, and that encode, normalise, unnormalise, decode reproduces the image.
Running an autoencoder in float16 without checking for NaNs
The SDXL VAE in float16 returned NaN values on all of the 16 test images, already in the encoder's output; the fp16-fix VAE (same architecture, rescaled internal activations, {{madebyollin/sdxl-vae-fp16-fix|https://huggingface.co/madebyollin/sdxl-vae-fp16-fix}}) did not, and SD 1.5's is fine in float16 (section 11). diffusers' force_upcast config flag exists for this. Compare one image in float32 and float16 before a batch run.
Comparing PSNR across resolutions, crops or colour spaces
Grey 28 × 28 images through the tiny tokenizers (22 to 25 dB) and 512 × 512 colour images through SD 1.5's VAE (20.6 to 37.5 dB, depending on the image) are not on one scale, and neither are papers that compute it at 256 or on luminance only. Compare tokenizers on the same images at the same resolution.
Quoting an rFID from a few hundred images
Fréchet distances are biased upwards at small N: the topic's real-against-real floor is 2.54 at 10,000 images and about 15.6 at 1,000 (root src/data/README.md). Use thousands at least, and state N.
Choosing the tokenizer by reconstruction alone
More channels or a larger vocabulary reconstruct better and can be harder to model (sections 10 and 11). Train a small generator on the candidate latents and measure generation before committing.
Interview rehearsal (design questions the sections do not pose)
Why do latent diffusion models not diffuse in pixel space?
Cost and focus: 786,432 numbers against 16,384 in SD's latent (48 times fewer), and the denoiser runs 25 to 50 times per image; the autoencoder removes imperceptible detail once, so the generator spends its capacity on composition. The price is the decoder's ceiling and a second model to train.
Design: an autoregressive text-to-image model at 512 × 512. Choose the tokenizer.
Sequence length sets cost: f = 16 gives 1,024 tokens per image (Chameleon's choice), f = 8 gives 4,096. Vocabulary: 8k to 16k VQ codes with L2-normalised low-dimensional codes and restarts, or FSQ or LFQ for larger vocabularies; train with L1 or L2 + LPIPS + patch GAN on data like the target (text-heavy if text rendering matters, since the decoder's ceiling binds). Measure rFID, LPIPS, usage and a small AR model's generation FID for two or three candidates. Trade-offs: fewer tokens are faster but lose detail; a larger vocabulary reconstructs better but is harder to predict.
Design: a latent diffusion model's outputs have mangled small text. Generator or autoencoder?
Round-trip real images with small text through the autoencoder alone. If the reconstructions already mangle it, the decoder's ceiling is the cause (SD 1.5's VAE does, section 12) and no generator can fix it: use a VAE with more channels or a text-tuned decoder (Qwen-Image's), or higher resolution. If reconstructions are fine, look at the generator (data, text encoder, guidance).
A colleague's img2img outputs come back smeared and flat, or over-contrasted, with no error. First check?
The scaling factor: latents not multiplied by the model's factor (smeared) or multiplied by another model's (over-contrasted). Print the std of the latents fed to the U-Net; it should be near 1 (section 11).
What transfers from this laptop (provenance)

Measured on Apple M1 Pro: every tiny model trained in about 2 to 5 minutes on the laptop's GPU (MPS, float32, batch 128); the pretrained autoencoders ran one at a time. What transfers: the mechanisms and their direction (loss units changing β, the scale mismatch that sinks N(0, I) sampling, the β trade-off, collapse with a strong decoder and what the fixes do, dead codes at large K and FSQ's near-full usage, perceptual losses trading PSNR for realism), and the pretrained autoencoders' reconstruction metrics and every scaling-factor effect (deterministic arithmetic on the same weights). What does not: absolute numbers of the tiny models (one seed each, 5,000 steps, a 28 × 28 dataset; FD and rFD in a small Fashion-MNIST classifier's feature space, comparable only within this page), rankings from 16 test images, and all timings.
