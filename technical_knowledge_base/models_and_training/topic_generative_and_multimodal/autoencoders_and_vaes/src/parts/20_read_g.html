11. The autoencoders inside latent diffusion, and their scaling factors

The Stable Diffusion 1.5 autoencoder (AutoencoderKL, 83,653,863 parameters counted from the loaded module) maps 512 × 512 × 3 to 64 × 64 × 4: f = 8, 4 channels (block_out_channels [128, 256, 512, 512] in the pinned config). A VAE in name only: its KL weight is 0.000001 ({{latent-diffusion autoencoder_kl_32x32x4.yaml line 11|https://github.com/CompVis/latent-diffusion/blob/a506df575647/configs/autoencoder/autoencoder_kl_32x32x4.yaml#L11}}), a million times less than section 3's β = 1. Its sharpness comes from the rest of its loss, LPIPSWithDiscriminator ({{contperceptual.py lines 48 to 83|https://github.com/CompVis/latent-diffusion/blob/a506df575647/ldm/modules/losses/contperceptual.py#L48}}): L1 + LPIPS + the tiny KL + a patch GAN from step 50,001, VQGAN's recipe with a KL in place of the codebook.

Measured on the 16 test images (float32, posterior mean): photographs come back at 26.7 to 30.9 dB, the synthetic graphics (shapes, text, chart, clock) at 32.3 to 37.5 dB, the busiest generated image (the puppy on grass) at dB. Float16 moves PSNR by at most dB and gives no NaNs. The scaling factor 0.18215

Every later autoencoder has its own recipe, because later latents are not zero-mean or not equally scaled per channel: SDXL multiplies by 0.13025; SD3 and FLUX.1 subtract a shift and then scale (FLUX.1: (z − 0.1159) × 0.3611); Qwen-Image normalises each of 16 channels with its own mean and standard deviation; FLUX.2 patchifies 2 × 2 to 128 channels and applies batch-norm running statistics; DC-AE multiplies by 0.41407. Each must be undone exactly before decoding: leaving out FLUX.1's shift moved PSNR from to dB on the 16 test images, small and silent, harmful wherever exact colours matter, as in editing. measured

4 channels against 32, same 8 × downsampling, same crop

160 × 160 crops of the 512 × 512 test images at full resolution (WebP at quality 90, a little softness shared by every tile). The other seven autoencoders: the {{Tokenizer comparator|#t-avtok}}. measured on Apple M1 Pro

The catch: richer latents are harder to model. SD3 accepts it because "predicting latents with higher d is a more difficult task, and thus models with increased capacity should be able to perform better for larger d" (its Figure 10). At smaller scale it bites: VA-VAE at f = 16 on ImageNet 256 ({{Yao et al. 2025, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01423v3}}) published goes from 16 to 32 to 64 channels and reconstruction FID improves from 0.49 to 0.26 to 0.17 while generation FID (same LightningDiT-B) worsens from 16.24 to 22.62 to 36.83; aligning the latent to DINOv2 features (their "VF loss") recovers most of it (f16d32: 15.82).

Measured: the same 16 images through nine. The 16- and 32-channel VAEs lead on mean PSNR (FLUX.1 dB, FLUX.2 , Qwen-Image , against for SD 1.5 and for SDXL); FLUX.2 is best on SSIM and LPIPS (); DC-AE, with 8 times fewer numbers than SD 1.5, reaches dB; every model's worst image is the SD-generated puppy on grass. Two surprises: SDXL's VAE, reported beating SD 1.x on COCO at 256, scores slightly below it here on all three measures (16 images: a caution, not a ranking); and in float16 it produced NaN values on all images, already in the encoder, while the fp16-fix VAE matched float32 to dB. measured on Apple M1 Pro

Other levers: learnability, batch size, a text-tuned decoder, deeper compression (depth)

Black Forest Labs describe the learnability tension for FLUX: FLUX.1's VAE reconstructs far better than SD's but is harder to learn (generation FID 10.13 against SD's 7.73 after their flow-matching training), and FLUX.2's was retrained "to achieve better learnability and higher image quality at the same time" (rFID 0.1124 against FLUX.1's 0.1761; generation FID 3.70) ({{BFL, FLUX.2 representation comparison|https://bfl.ai/research/representation-comparison}}, {{FLUX.2 announcement, 2025-11-25|https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-2}}) vendor claims, own evaluation.

SDXL kept SD's architecture and retrained it "at a larger batch-size (256 vs 9)" with an EMA of the weights, beating SD 1.x on every reconstruction metric on COCO at 256 × 256 (rFID 4.4 against 5.0; {{Podell et al. 2023, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952v1}}) published. Qwen-Image froze the Wan 2.1 encoder and fine-tuned only the decoder on text-rich images (documents, slides, posters), with reconstruction and perceptual losses but no adversarial loss ("the discriminator is unable to provide effective guidance" at that quality): text-image PSNR 36.63 against 26.77 for the Wan 2.1 decoder it started from ({{Qwen-Image report, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02324v1}}) vendor. DC-AE compresses 32 × per side with residual shortcuts and a staged high-resolution adaptation; at f = 64 on ImageNet 512 it reports rFID 0.22 where the SD-VAE design trained at the same latent shape reaches 16.84, and 19.1 × faster inference for the same generator ({{Chen et al. 2024, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10733v8}}) published. All these share the LDM family's design: a convolutional encoder and decoder (DC-AE adds attention-like blocks), a KL weight of about 10−6, L1 + LPIPS + patch GAN.
