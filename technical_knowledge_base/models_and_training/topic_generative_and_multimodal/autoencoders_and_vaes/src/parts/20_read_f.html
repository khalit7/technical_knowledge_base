9. The autoencoders inside real image generators

What they are. The latent diffusion and flow models in this table use continuous autoencoders of one family, descended from the LDM autoencoder: a convolutional encoder and decoder (DC-AE adds attention-like blocks), a KL weight so small the latent is barely regularised (about 10−6 in the LDM recipe), and VQGAN-style losses (L1 + LPIPS + patch GAN in the LDM code; Qwen-Image's decoder fine-tune dropped the GAN). and its reason for accepting the cost: "predicting latents with higher d is a more difficult task, and thus models with increased capacity should be able to perform better for larger d", confirmed for its larger models (its Figure 10). The cost is real at smaller scales. VA-VAE measured it directly at f = 16 on ImageNet 256 ({{Yao et al. 2025, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01423v3}}) published: going from 16 to 32 to 64 channels improves reconstruction FID from 0.49 to 0.26 to 0.17 but worsens generation FID (with the same LightningDiT-B generator) from 16.24 to 22.62 to 36.83; aligning the latent to DINOv2 features during autoencoder training (their "VF loss") recovers most of it (f16d32: 15.82). Black Forest Labs describe the same tension for FLUX: FLUX.1's VAE reconstructs far better than SD's but is harder to learn (generation FID 10.13 against SD's 7.73 after their flow-matching training), and FLUX.2's VAE was re-trained "to achieve better learnability and higher image quality at the same time" (rFID 0.1124 against FLUX.1's 0.1761; generation FID 3.70) ({{BFL, FLUX.2 representation comparison|https://bfl.ai/research/representation-comparison}}, {{FLUX.2 announcement, 2025-11-25|https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-2}}) vendor claims, own evaluation.

Other levers. SDXL kept SD's architecture and retrained it "at a larger batch-size (256 vs 9)" with an EMA of the weights, beating SD 1.x on every reconstruction metric on COCO at 256 × 256 (rFID 4.4 against 5.0; {{Podell et al. 2023, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952v1}}) published. Qwen-Image froze the Wan 2.1 encoder and fine-tuned only the image decoder on text-rich images (documents, slides, posters), with reconstruction and perceptual losses but no adversarial loss ("the discriminator is unable to provide effective guidance" at that quality): text-image PSNR 36.63 against 26.77 for the Wan 2.1 decoder it started from ({{Qwen-Image report, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02324v1}}) vendor. DC-AE goes the other way, compressing 32 × per side with residual shortcuts and a staged high-resolution adaptation; at f = 64 on ImageNet 512 it reports rFID 0.22 where the SD-VAE design trained at the same latent shape reaches 16.84, and 19.1 × faster inference for the same generator ({{Chen et al. 2024, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10733v8}}) published.

10. Evaluating an autoencoder or tokenizer

Fidelity to the image

PSNR: 10 log10(2552 / mean squared error), in dB; pixel-exact, so it prefers blur over plausible detail. SSIM: compares local means, contrasts and correlations in small windows, 1 is identical; still a local pixel statistic. LPIPS: distance between deep features (AlexNet or VGG layers, weighted by a model fitted to human judgements), 0 is identical; tracks what people notice better than either.

Realism of the set

rFID: the Fréchet distance between Inception features of the reconstructions and of the originals over a whole set, usually the 50,000 ImageNet validation images at 256 × 256 (LlamaGen: "r-FID, reconstruction-FID on 256×256 ImageNet 50k validation set", {{arXiv 2406.06525v1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06525v1}}). It asks whether reconstructions look like real images as a distribution, so it punishes blur that PSNR forgives. It needs thousands of images (see Common mistakes), and protocols differ: FSQ computes it against the training set rather than the validation set ({{arXiv 2309.15505v2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15505v2}}).

The tokens themselves

Codes used and perplexity (section 5); tokens or numbers per image and bits per image (section 8); encode and decode time and memory. For a continuous latent: its per-channel statistics (section 9's normalisations exist because they are not unit-scale).

What it is for

Generation FID of a generator trained on the latents, at fixed compute. The only measure of "easy to model", and the one sections 8 and 9 show can move against reconstruction.

Metrics disagree, measured. On the comparator's 16 images the three fidelity measures rank the nine autoencoders differently at the top and in the middle: PSNR puts FLUX.1 first, SSIM and LPIPS put FLUX.2 first; MoVQ is fourth by PSNR but seventh by SSIM; Qwen-Image is third by PSNR and SSIM but fourth by LPIPS, behind SD 1.5. The parent's Latent lab shows the deeper version: a Gaussian blur with exactly the SD reconstruction's PSNR looks far worse. Comparing papers. Numbers are only comparable at the same resolution, dataset, image count and metric implementation. Even the scale of LPIPS differs between papers: SDXL's Table 3 reports LPIPS of 0.88 to 0.96 and BFL's table 0.27 to 1.67, far above the 0.01 to 0.08 the standard implementation gives for nine similar autoencoders on this page's images, without saying how they were scaled; NVIDIA's Cosmos report re-ran rivals under its own protocol and got different rFIDs from the rivals' own papers (LlamaGen at f = 16: 1.657 in Cosmos's Table 8 against 2.19 in LlamaGen's). Treat cross-paper tables as rough, and re-measure the candidates you are choosing between on your own images.

How to check a tokenizer you are about to use: round-trip a few hundred of your own images, look at full-resolution crops of text, faces and fine texture, compute PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS, check float16 against float32 once, print the normalised latent's mean and standard deviation (near 0 and 1 if the recipe is right), and for discrete tokenizers count the codes used.
