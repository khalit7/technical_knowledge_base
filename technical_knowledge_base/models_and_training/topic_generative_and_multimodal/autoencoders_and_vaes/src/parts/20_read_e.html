7. VQGAN: the recipe behind nearly every image tokenizer

The problem. A VQ-VAE trained with a pixel loss blurs (section 4), and a blurry tokenizer caps every image a generator makes from its tokens. VQGAN ({{Esser, Rombach, Ommer 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09841}}) kept the VQ-VAE and changed the loss.

loss = L1 or L2 pixel loss + LPIPS perceptual loss
+ codebook and commitment losses + λ · patch-GAN loss
LPIPS
distance between the features of the two images inside a pretrained network (AlexNet or VGG), with per-layer weights fitted to human similarity judgements ({{Zhang et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03924}}); 0 means identical features
patch GAN
a small convolutional discriminator scores every patch of the image as real or reconstructed; the autoencoder is trained to fool it, so texture must look real, not merely average
λ
set adaptively each step: the ratio of the two losses' gradient norms at the decoder's last layer, so the GAN term never swamps the reconstruction (calculate_adaptive_weight, the same function in the LDM code, lines 32 to 43); the discriminator is switched on only after the autoencoder has learned to reconstruct (LDM's disc_start 50,001)

What it bought, in the paper's own table published: reconstruction FID (rFID: the Fréchet distance between the reconstructions of the ImageNet validation set and the originals, at 256 × 256) against the earlier tokenizers.

The catch at large codebooks. The 16,384-code VQGAN reconstructs better, but later papers measured that it uses only 5.9% of its codes (1,024 codes: 44%), per {{IBQ Table 1, arXiv 2412.02692v2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02692v2}} independent. Section 5's fixes are what made large codebooks work: ViT-VQGAN's ablation, at 8,192 codes, reports usage rising from 4% with 256-dimensional codes to 96% with 32-dimensional L2-normalised ones (rFID 3.68 to 1.55), and removing the normalisation at 32 dimensions drops usage to 2% ({{Yu et al. 2021, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04627v3}}) published.

At toy scale here, adding LPIPS to the 128-code tokenizer cut rFD from to at a cost of dB of PSNR, and the patch GAN added nothing measurable on top (section 4).

Relatives worth knowing. Residual quantization (RQ-VAE, {{Lee et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01941v2}}) quantizes the leftover error again with the same codebook, D times, so each position holds D codes: at 8 × 8 positions, depth 4 reaches rFID 4.73 where a one-code VQGAN at 8 × 8 is at 17.95 even with 131,072 codes; the paper: "increasing the quantization depth D is more effective ... than increasing the codebook size K" published. Neural audio codecs use the same idea (the sibling page {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}}). Multi-scale tokenizers (VAR, {{Tian et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02905v2}}) quantize the residual at growing resolutions, 1 × 1 up to 16 × 16 in its code (10 scales, 680 tokens in total, one shared codebook of 4,096), so the generator predicts coarse to fine. 1-D tokenizers (TiTok, {{Yu et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07550v1}}) drop the grid: 32 tokens for a 256 × 256 image at rFID 2.21 published.

8. Image tokenizers in production: tokens per image and the vocabulary

The trade-off. For an autoregressive model, tokens per image is sequence length, and cost grows with it (attention roughly with its square); vocabulary size sets how much each token carries (log2 K bits) and how hard the next-token prediction is. Every tokenizer below picks a point.

What the numbers say. Most autoregressive image models settled on f = 16 and 1,024 to 16,384 codes (256 tokens at 256 × 256, 1,024 at 512 × 512): halving f quadruples the sequence. Larger vocabularies reconstruct better but are harder to predict; MAGVIT-v2's Figure 1 shows generation quality first improving and then getting worse as a VQ vocabulary grows, which it offers as the reason "the vocabulary size of most language models for visual generation is around 1-8k" published, and LFQ as the way past it. Chameleon names the cost of its choice: "A core weakness of our tokenizer is in reconstructing images with a large amount of text, therefore upper bounding the capability of our models" ({{arXiv 2405.09818v2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09818v2}}) published. Janus keeps two image encoders, SigLIP for understanding and the LlamaGen VQ tokenizer for generation, and its ablation shows one VQ tokenizer for both tasks scoring far worse on understanding (POPE 60.1 against 87.0, {{arXiv 2410.13848v1, Table 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13848v1}}) published: tokens made for reconstruction are not the features made for understanding.
