1. Connectors: five ways to hand patches to a language model

The encoder gives a grid of vectors: 576 vectors of 1,024 numbers for LLaVA-1.5’s CLIP ViT-L at 336 pixels, or 1,024 vectors of 768 numbers per 512-pixel tile for SmolVLM. The language model expects tokens of its own width (4,096 numbers for a 7B Llama, 960 for SmolVLM’s, 1,024 for Qwen3.5-0.8B’s) and pays for every token it reads. A connector must make the vectors readable (in the language model’s space), few (cheap) and faithful (small text and positions survive); the five designs trade those three.

Same image, five connectors: only the merge decides how many tokens the language model pays for

Pick a design, then step: the encoder’s patch grid, what the connector does, the tokens that come out (one square per token, the same size in every design), and where the language model reads them. Grids and counts come from each model’s configuration or paper; SmolVLM’s and Qwen3.5’s are measured on the same 512 × 512 photo.

Connector parameters: LLaVA-1.5’s MLP about 21.0M (1,024 × 4,096 + 4,096 × 4,096 weights plus biases) derived; BLIP-2’s Q-Former 188M paper; SmolVLM’s one 12,288 × 960 matrix, 11.8M, and Qwen3.5’s merger (LayerNorm, then a 3,072 to 3,072 to 1,024 MLP) 12.6M, both counted from the checkpoints measured.

Projector (LLaVA). The cheapest connector to train (LLaVA’s first stage trains only it) and the most faithful, since every patch keeps its own token; but the token count is the patch count, so resolution is paid for quadratically. LLaVA-1.5 reads the encoder’s second-to-last block and drops the [CLS] token (the Vision encoders page measures why a late, not last, layer is better). Right when images are few and small.

Query transformer (BLIP-2’s Q-Former). A fixed set of learned queries (parameters, the same for every image) attend to the frozen encoder’s features: 32 output vectors of 768 against 257 × 1,024 frozen features for ViT-L/14. Thirty-two tokens whatever the image is cheap, but the paper itself calls it “an information bottleneck between the frozen image encoder and the frozen LLM”: fine text, counts and positions must squeeze through, and the Q-Former needs its own pretraining stage (section 6). It suits a frozen language model and a tight budget, not documents.

Resampler and gated cross-attention (Flamingo). A Perceiver Resampler turns however many features into 64 visual outputs; new gated cross-attention blocks between frozen language-model layers read them, each output multiplied by tanh(α) with α initialised to 0, so training starts exactly at the frozen language model. Context stays free (many images or long video cost no sequence length); the price is new layers and an architecture serving stacks support less well.

Merging neighbours (pixel shuffle, patch merger). Lay each block of neighbouring patch vectors side by side into one longer vector, then project: nothing is averaged away, and the projector learns to read several positions out of one vector. InternVL 1.5 cuts tokens to a quarter (a 448 × 448 tile becomes 256 tokens); SmolVLM merges 4 × 4 = 16 (1,024 to 64 per tile); Qwen2-VL merges 2 × 2 with an MLP, so a 224 × 224 image is (224 / 14)² / 4 = 64 tokens plus start and end markers, the paper’s “66 tokens” derived. A fixed compression with no extra stage, combined with tiling or native resolution: today’s default. SmolVLM’s paper reports that “smaller VLMs benefit from more aggressive compression (r=4)”; section 8 measures what happens when tokens are merged further without retraining.

The one controlled comparison: once the backbones can adapt, tokens in the sequence beat cross-attention

Idefics2 ({{Laurençon et al., May 2024, section 3.2 and Table 3|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.02246}}) paper: same backbones, same data, same updates; average score over its benchmarks.

ArchitectureBackbonesAvg. score
Tokens in the sequence, no resamplerfrozen51.8
Tokens in the sequence, with resamplerfrozen60.3

With frozen backbones cross-attention wins; with LoRA (full unfreezing “would yield loss divergences”) tokens in the sequence win, “even though the latter has more parameters”. That is why almost every open VLM since 2024 puts image tokens in the sequence. The same paper’s learned resampler adds 8.5 points (60.3 − 51.8) while cutting tokens “from 729 to 64”, with “no gains when using more than 64 visual tokens” (Table 4: 71.2 at 128, 71.7 at 64).

The designs in the papers’ own words, and two numbers in Idefics2’s prose that do not match its table (provenance)
  • LLaVA: first “a simple linear layer to connect image features into the word embedding space” ({{Liu et al., April 2023, section 4.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2304.08485v2}}), then in LLaVA-1.5 “a two-layer MLP” which “can improve LLaVA’s multimodal capabilities” ({{October 2023, section 3.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03744}}) paper.
  • BLIP-2 ({{Li et al., January 2023, section 3|https://arxiv.org/html/2301.12597}}) paper: “we use 32 queries where each query has a dimension of 768”; the output “(32×768) is much smaller than the size of frozen image features (e.g. 257×1024 for ViT-L/14)”; the Q-Former is initialised from BERT-base, has cross-attention “inserted every other transformer block”, and “contains 188M parameters”; a fully connected layer projects its outputs, which are “prepended to the input text embeddings” as “soft visual prompts”.
  • Flamingo ({{Alayrac et al., April 2022, sections 2.1 to 2.3|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.14198}}, checked word for word in the root lab) paper: the resampler “produces a fixed number of visual outputs (64)”; α is “a layer-specific learnable scalar initialized to 0”; each text token “attends to the visual tokens of the image that appeared just before it”.
  • InternVL 1.5 ({{section 3.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16821}}): “a simple pixel shuffle to reduce the number of visual tokens to one-quarter”, so “a 448×448 image is represented by 256 visual tokens”. Qwen2-VL ({{section 2.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12191}}): “a simple MLP layer is employed after the ViT to compress adjacent 2×2 tokens into a single token” paper.
  • Idefics2’s prose against its Table 3 derived: the text says cross-attention “performs 7 points better” (table: 66.7 − 60.3 = 6.4) and LoRA gives “a 12.9 points increase under the fully autoregressive architecture” (table: 69.5 − 60.3 = 9.2; the 0.6 for cross-attention matches). The ranking holds either way; quote the table. In its frozen setting about 15% (in the sequence) and 25% (cross-attention) of all parameters are trained.
Inside the encoder: does a VLM inherit SigLIP 2’s outlier tokens? (measured; SmolVLM’s retrained encoder has none)

The {{Vision encoders|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c99f60ceb2f02cf5ea}} page found that SigLIP 2 B/16 at 224 pixels has a few patch tokens with 15 to 22 times the median norm at fixed grid positions in every image (the “high-norm tokens” registers were invented to absorb). A VLM passes every patch to its connector, so such tokens would be a fixed tax on every image. Measured with the same statistic (the last block’s output before the final LayerNorm, each patch’s norm over the median of its view), one dot per test image measured:

  • SigLIP 2 B/16 (the root’s and the sibling’s checkpoint): at 224 pixels every image has a patch 15 to 23 times the median, at fixed positions (row, column) (0,13) and (3,13) in 16 of 16 images and (0,11) in 5 (the sibling, with its own preprocessing, lists (0,1), (0,13) and (3,13)); at 512 pixels (32 × 32 grid, position table interpolated) 18 to 24 times, at (0,30), (0,31) and (1,30) in all 16.
  • The public SigLIP base checkpoint at 512, the shape SmolVLM’s encoder starts from: milder fixed outliers, 4.5 to 6.1 times, at (2,4) in 11, (29,4) in 10, (3,4) in 9.
  • Inside SmolVLM: the largest ratio over all 272 views (16 images × 17) is 5.3; only 6 patches exceed 5, at 6 distinct positions; after the final LayerNorm those patches are 0.66 to 0.69 times the median, and after the projector no token exceeds 3.6 times.
  • Qwen3.5’s encoder (12 blocks of width 768, interpolated position table plus 2-D rotary positions; the Qwen3-VL report says its towers start from SigLIP-2, the Qwen3.5 card does not say): largest ratio 3.1 to 19.7 per image, 0 to 276 of 1,024 patches above 5, mostly on synthetic images with large flat areas, at positions that follow the content; only (1,1) repeats in more than half the images (11 of 16).

Outliers are a property of how an encoder was trained, not of vision transformers as such. A connector that merges neighbours carries them into tokens, so check your own encoder with this one statistic before adding registers or dropping tokens by norm. The {{What the model saw|#t-vmsaw}} tab shades every token by this ratio.
