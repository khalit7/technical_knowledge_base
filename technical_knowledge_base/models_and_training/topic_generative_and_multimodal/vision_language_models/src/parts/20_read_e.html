6. Training recipes: stages, frozen parts, data

The problem. A VLM is built from two pretrained parts that have never met, joined by a randomly initialised connector. Training everything at once lets the random connector send noise into a good language model; training too little leaves the image tokens unreadable. Every recipe answers three questions: which parts train in which stage, on what data, and how long. The root lab explains LLaVA’s two stages (align the projector with everything frozen, then instruction-tune projector and language model); the recipes since then differ in what they unfreeze and how much data they use paper:

The pattern. A short alignment stage with only the connector training survives everywhere (LLaVA, Qwen3-VL’s S0); after it, recent recipes train everything, the encoder included, on a trillion tokens or more, with text-only data mixed in so the language model does not forget. Image-caption pairs teach what is in an image; interleaved web pages teach several images in context; OCR, chart, document and grounding data teach reading and pointing; instruction and conversation data teach answering. The loss is on text tokens only: the model never predicts image tokens (that is the subject of the sibling page Native multimodal and any-to-any models).

6a. Was SmolVLM’s encoder trained inside the VLM? measured

The SmolVLM paper names its encoder (“SigLIP-B/16”) but the passages read do not say whether it was frozen. The weights can say. SmolVLM’s vision tower has the same shape as Google’s public google/siglip-base-patch16-512 (12 blocks, width 768, 1,024 positions; the public checkpoint adds a pooling head a VLM does not use), so each tensor can be compared directly: the relative change ‖WSmolVLM − WSigLIP‖ / ‖WSigLIP‖, averaged over each block weighted by size.

Why freeze or not. Freezing the encoder saves memory (no optimizer state for it) and protects features learned from billions of pairs; training it lets the encoder learn what the language model needs (small text, positions, the processor’s tiles). Idefics2 found that adapting the backbones (with LoRA) beat freezing them by 9.2 points in its own table (section 1), and the recipes above that train the encoder do so after an alignment stage, usually at a lower learning rate.
