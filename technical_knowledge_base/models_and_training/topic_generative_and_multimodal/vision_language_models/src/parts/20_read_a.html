The idea in one screen

A vision-language model (VLM) cuts an image into patches, encodes each patch as a vector, and a small connector turns those vectors into image tokens of the language model’s width that sit in the sequence like words. Every design choice is about those tokens: how many, cut from which pixels, at which positions, and trained to mean what; each one costs context, cache and time like any other token.

One 512 × 512 photo becomes 1,088 image tokens in SmolVLM and 256 in Qwen3.5

The same photo through two small open VLMs, every number measured on the real processors and models (2026-10-17) measured. SmolVLM tiles and merges 4 × 4 patches into one token; Qwen3.5 keeps the image at its own size, merges 2 × 2 and gives each token a row and a column. The encoder box is the {{Vision encoders|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c99f60ceb2f02cf5ea}} page’s subject; everything else is this page’s.

You need: what a vision transformer, CLIP and SigLIP produce ({{Vision encoders|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c99f60ceb2f02cf5ea}}), how a decoder reads a sequence and why it caches keys and values ({{The transformer from zero|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8174980ddde4a20c7b3c}}), and RoPE ({{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}). Besides SmolVLM, the small VLM the whole topic measures, this page runs a second model, Qwen3.5-0.8B (Apache 2.0, 1.75 GB in bfloat16), chosen because it differs from SmolVLM exactly where this page teaches: native resolution instead of tiles, 3-D positions instead of 1-D, trained to output boxes and read video, and a hybrid language model whose cache grows in only 6 of 24 layers.

Patch
a small square of pixels (14 or 16 on a side) that the encoder turns into one vector.
Connector
the trained module between encoder and language model: a projector, a set of learned queries, or a merge of neighbouring patches.
Image token
one vector of the language model’s width that stands for part of the image; it takes a slot in the sequence (or, in cross-attention designs, is read from outside it).
Tile, AnyRes
cutting a large image into fixed-size squares the encoder was trained on, plus a shrunken whole view.
Native resolution
feeding the image at (nearly) its own size, so the token count follows the pixel count.
M-RoPE
rotary positions with separate time, row and column parts, so an image token knows where it sat.
Grounding
answering with a place in the image, usually a bounding box written as numbers in the text.

Labels: measured = run for this page on an Apple M1 Pro (PyTorch MPS, bfloat16, greedy decoding, 2026-10-17); paper = stated by the cited authors; vendor = a model card or blog; derived = arithmetic on these, formula shown. Two small models on synthetic images are a probe, not a benchmark: one question is several percentage points.
