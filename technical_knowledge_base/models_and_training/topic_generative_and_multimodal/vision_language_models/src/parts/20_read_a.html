0. In one screen

A vision-language model (VLM) is a language model that has been given eyes. A vision encoder (a vision transformer, see the sibling page {{Vision encoders|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c99f60ceb2f02cf5ea}}) turns an image into one vector per small square of pixels, a patch; a connector turns those vectors into a few hundred or a few thousand image tokens of the language model’s width; and the language model reads them, either as part of its input sequence or through extra attention layers. Everything on this page is a choice inside that path: how many tokens, cut from which pixels, at which positions, and trained to mean what.

The root page’s {{VLM lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ab6ade318917bff55}} follows one image through SmolVLM-500M step by step, counts its tokens, shows the exact prompt, and scores 30 questions and a yes/no probe. This page assumes that walk-through and goes further: the other connector designs, native resolution and 3-D positions, text and boxes and video, training recipes, hallucination, and cost. It runs a second model to make each of those concrete: Qwen3.5-0.8B (Apache 2.0, 1.75 GB in bfloat16), chosen because it differs from SmolVLM in exactly the places this page teaches (native resolution instead of tiles, 3-D positions instead of 1-D, trained to output boxes and read video, and a hybrid language model whose cache does not grow in most layers). Both run on an Apple M1 Pro laptop GPU, one at a time, in bfloat16.
