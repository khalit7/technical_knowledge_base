Mistakes that ran here, and two design questions

Real errors and outputs from this page’s runs, each with what to do instead; open the ones that match your work.

Calling the image processor instead of the processor (InternVL)
The InternVL3-1B-hf repository ships "crop_to_patches": false in its image-processor config; the InternVLProcessor overrides it to True (models/internvl/processing_internvl.py line 34, v5.20.1). Call the image processor alone and dynamic tiling silently disappears: a 1,920 × 1,080 screenshot gets 1 view (256 tokens) from the image processor alone and 9 views (2,304 tokens) through the processor; an A4 page at 150 dpi, 1 against 7 measured with the processors. Always build inputs with the model’s processor and count the tokens it produced.
Drawing boxes in the wrong coordinate system
Qwen2-VL and Qwen3-VL write boxes on a 0 to 1,000 scale; Qwen2.5-VL writes pixels of the resized image (section 4). On this page’s 24 boxes, reading Qwen3.5’s numbers as 0 to 1,000 (the convention its Qwen3-VL predecessor documents) gives mean IoU 0.06 instead of 0.49: every box lands in the wrong place and nothing raises an error. Verify the convention by measurement for each checkpoint.
Passing video frames without their frame rate
Qwen3.5’s video processor needs the clip’s frame rate to sample at 2 frames per second. Without metadata transformers warns, verbatim: “Asked to sample `fps` frames per second but no video metadata was provided which is required when sampling with `fps`. Defaulting to `fps=24`.” On this page’s 16-frame, 4-second clip that made the processor treat it as 0.67 seconds: 2 temporal patches (4 frames kept of 16) and a first timestamp of <0.1 seconds>, against 4 patches spread over 0.2 to 3.5 seconds with video_metadata set measured with the processor. Pass the real frame rate and duration.
Writing the prompt by hand
The processor expands one placeholder per image into hundreds of image tokens; without the placeholder there is nowhere to put the image. SmolVLM’s processor refuses, verbatim: “ValueError: The total number of <image> tokens in the prompts should be the same as the number of images passed. Found [0] <image> tokens and [0, 1] images per sample.” Qwen3.5’s processor accepts the same mistake without a word and returns a prompt with no image tokens; the model then fails at the first forward, verbatim: “ValueError: Image features and image tokens do not match, tokens: 0, features: 256” measured. Use apply_chat_template with an image entry, then the processor.
Expecting a training-free token cut to be free
Here random pruning to half the image tokens cost SmolVLM 6 of 30 answers, attention-ranked pruning 10, and dropping the 64-token global view alone 11 (section 8a). Methods that report no loss (FastV) were measured on larger models and on benchmarks; measure on your own questions before shipping a pruning rate.
Scoring yes/no answers by parsing text only
A parsed “yes” hides how close the call was, and a model that answers neither yes nor no gets miscounted. Read the probability on yes and no tokens at the first answer position (section 7): the yes rate on absent objects, its confidence, and the share of probability on neither are all visible.
Comparing models at their default settings and calling it a model comparison
At defaults SmolVLM spends 1,088 tokens on a 512-pixel square and Qwen3.5 spends 256; on a 1,024-pixel page, 1,088 and 1,024; on a 64-pixel icon, 1,088 and 64 (section 2, measured). Compare at matched token budgets as well as at defaults, and say which.
Running a model with a decoding setting it was not tuned for, without saying so
Qwen3.5-0.8B’s card recommends sampling for vision tasks (“temperature=0.7, top_p=0.80, top_k=20, min_p=0.0, presence_penalty=1.5”) vendor; this page decodes greedily, so every answer is reproducible but may differ from the vendor’s setting. Report the decoding with every score.
Design: a VLM that extracts fields and their locations from invoices (interview rehearsal)
Native-resolution or tiled encoder sized so the smallest printed text has several pixels per stroke after resizing (test legibility first, section 3); count tokens per page and cap them; a model trained for grounding, with its coordinate convention verified on non-square pages; training data of documents with boxes, plus negatives (“field absent”) against hallucinated fields; evaluate exact match per field, box IoU, and the absent-field false-positive rate; cache the page encoding so several questions about one invoice reuse it.
Design: question answering over 10-minute videos on one GPU (interview rehearsal)
Sample 1 to 2 frames per second, merge frame pairs and 2 × 2 patches, and cap tokens per video (Qwen2-VL trained with 16,384); add timestamps so answers can cite times. Know what your processor does to long inputs: at 320 × 240 and 2 frames per second a short clip costs 80 tokens per second, but for 10 minutes Qwen3.5’s processor keeps at most 768 frames and shrinks each to 192 × 128, so the whole video becomes 9,216 tokens (measured with the processor), which may be too blurry to answer. Choose fps and resolution per question type, prune near-duplicate frames or tokens (vLLM’s video pruning), cache the encoded video across questions, and measure accuracy against tokens.
