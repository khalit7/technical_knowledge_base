10. Flow against diffusion, and every model at equal cost

The question. Same data, same 1,040,353-parameter U-Net, same scorer, same starting noise: the root's diffusion model with three samplers, the flow model (1-RF) with Euler and midpoint, its reflows and the one-step students. Which gives the best sample per network evaluation? (One evaluation is one forward pass of the same network, so equal NFE is equal time.)

Flow with Euler beats every diffusion sampler at every budget from 1 to 50 evaluations; only DDPM with hundreds of steps wins

FD, 1,000 samples (100 per class), noise seed 11; real images . Diffusion rows: the root Diffusion lab's recorded runs; 1-RF and 2-RF: the root's; 3-RF, students and the SDE row: this page measured on Apple M1 Pro (MPS), 2026-10-17.

What it shows. At 4 evaluations flow with Euler scores FD 158.3 against 478.9 for DDIM and 300.3 for DDPM; at 10, 64.4 against 181.3, 130.5 and DPM-Solver++'s 115.1; at 20, 49.8 against 130.9, 77.9 and 101.2. Midpoint does better still (47.3 at 4 evaluations). Few steps cost diversity, not recognisability: class accuracy is above 85% for every flow run from 2 Euler steps, while diversity is 15.3 at 2 steps and 25.7 at 50 (real 28.9) measured. Why flow needs fewer steps here: the DDPM path bends by construction (straightness against for flow, measured on 50-step image paths), its linear schedule is nearly pure noise for most of its range (section 6), and x̂0 from a noise prediction divides by a tiny √ā at the noisy end, while a velocity stays bounded.

Where diffusion still wins. With many steps the stochastic DDPM sampler reaches FD 34.9 at 1,000 steps (39.7 at 250), below every deterministic flow run (best 43.5, Heun with a last Euler step at 19 evaluations). Solving the flow ODE more exactly does not help (Euler at 250 steps 53.0, midpoint at 25 steps 53.1): fresh noise corrects an imperfect model, and section 12 gives the flow model that noise.

Same ten noises, two models, one evaluation budget at a time

Predict, then step: at 4 evaluations, which side looks like clothing? Step through 1, 2, 4, 10, 20 and 50 evaluations. At 50 the two models turn the same noise into very similar garments although they were trained separately with different objectives (observed on these ten, not measured further): both learned a deterministic map from noise to data, and on the same data and network the maps come out close.

Guidance carries over unchanged. Trained with 10% of labels dropped, the model gives a conditional velocity vc and an unconditional vu, and the sampler uses vu + s (vc − vu): two evaluations per step. On the tiny flow model at 50 Euler steps, FD unguided and at s = 1.5, the best score of either model measured by the root.
