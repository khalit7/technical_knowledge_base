6. Reflow, round after round

The problem. Sections 2 and 5: the exact field of random pairs bends, and better pairing helps little on images. Reflow (Liu, Gong and Liu, arXiv 2209.03003, 2022) changes the pairing after training instead: push many noises z through the trained flow to get X1 = flow(z), then train again on the fixed pairs (z, X1). Paths of an ODE "cannot cross each other" (their words), so the new pairs do not cross, and the field that averages them is close to each pair's straight line. The root's Flow lab ran one reflow (giving 2-RF). What do a second and third reflow buy?

Names. Liu et al. call the first model the 1-rectified flow (1-RF), the model retrained on its pairs 2-RF, and so on. The root's "reflow" model is 2-RF; this page adds 3-RF on images and 3-RF and 4-RF on the toy, each trained exactly like the round before (the root's recipe: toy 12,000 steps on 20,000 pairs from a 256-step Heun solve; images 2,000 steps from the previous round's weights on 30,000 pairs from a 25-step midpoint solve).

7. Distillation to one step

The problem. Even a rectified flow is integrated in steps. Distillation trains a student network T(z) to output the teacher's ODE end point X1 = flow(z) directly, one call per sample. The question that reflow answers: distil which teacher?

How it works. Liu et al.'s recipe: generate (z, X1) pairs with the k-RF and regress T(z) onto X1 (for one step they "replace the L2 loss function with the LPIPS similarity", a perceptual distance). Here the student is the teacher's own network evaluated at t = 0, initialised from the teacher, with a squared error: z + v(z, 0) → X1, on the same pairs the next reflow round uses.

None of the tiny students reaches the multi-step teachers (FD for 1-RF at 50 steps): these students had 1,500 steps of an L2 loss on 30,000 pairs, where Liu et al. used LPIPS and far longer training (not measured further here).

Published one-step and few-step methods for flows (each labelled, dated; consistency models, progressive and adversarial distillation of diffusion models belong to the sibling page Diffusion from zero):

  • InstaFlow (Liu et al., arXiv 2309.06380, Sep 2023): reflow then distillation on Stable Diffusion; one step, FID 23.3 on MS COCO 2017-5k, 0.09 s per image, 199 A100 GPU-days of training (abstract) published.
  • Latent adversarial diffusion distillation, LADD (Sauer et al., arXiv 2403.12015, Mar 2024): applied to SD3 (8B) to get SD3-Turbo, matching strong generators "using only four unguided sampling steps"; FLUX.1 [schnell] (12B rectified flow, "1 to 4 steps") is distilled with it per its model card published.
  • Shortcut models (Frans et al., arXiv 2410.12557, Oct 2024): one network conditioned on the step size as well as the time, trained in one phase, so the same model samples in 1 or many steps published.
  • MeanFlow (Geng et al., arXiv 2505.13447, May 2025): learns the average velocity over an interval instead of the instantaneous one; "FID of 3.43 with a single function evaluation (1-NFE) on ImageNet 256x256 trained from scratch", with "no pre-training, distillation, or curriculum learning" published.
  • Guidance distillation: FLUX.1 [dev]'s transformer takes the guidance scale as an input (diffusers v0.41.0, pipelines/flux/pipeline_flux.py line 847, if self.transformer.config.guidance_embeds:), so one call replaces classifier-free guidance's two.

The cost. A pair pass plus a student training; and the student can be no better than its teacher's map. When it matters: latency-bound products (one call instead of 20 to 50, and the VAE decode then dominates). How to check: student against teacher at the teacher's best step count on the same metric, plus diversity (distilled students often lose variety).
