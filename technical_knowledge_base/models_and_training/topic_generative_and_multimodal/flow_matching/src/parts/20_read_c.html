4. The perfect model only copies its training set

The problem. A perfectly trained network outputs the exact marginal field. For real training data the distribution the loss sees is the training set itself: 55,000 Fashion-MNIST images, each a single point. Its exact field has a closed form too (xt given image j is Gaussian around t · imagej with spread 1 − t, the weights are a softmax over images, u = (E[x1 | x] − x)/(1 − t)). Published: the same for diffusion, and where the novelty comes from

The same for diffusion: denoising score matching "has a closed-form optimal solution that can only generate training data replicating samples", and memorisation "tend[s] to occur on smaller-sized datasets" (Gu et al., arXiv 2310.02664, Oct 2023). Kamb and Ganguli (arXiv 2412.20292, Dec 2024) explain the novelty: convolutional networks are local and translation-equivariant, so they cannot implement the global optimum and instead stitch "local training set patches"; their patch-mosaic model predicts trained models' outputs with median r² 0.94 on Fashion-MNIST published.

When it matters. Memorisation and privacy (a model that fits a small fine-tuning set too well reproduces it), and evaluation: FID cannot see copying (metrics are on {{Evaluating generative models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81c890f4f62543312faa}}). How to check: nearest-training-neighbour distances of samples against those of held-out real images, as above.

5. Sampling is solving an ODE: solvers and step grids at equal cost

The problem. Turning the velocity into a sample means integrating dx/dt = vθ(x, t) from 0 to 1 in finite steps, and every step costs network evaluations (NFE, each a full forward pass: the real cost). Which solver gives the best sample per evaluation?

How it works. Euler: move along the current velocity for a step h; one evaluation; error shrinks in proportion to h (first order). Heun: take the Euler step as a guess, evaluate the velocity there too, step with the average of the two slopes. Midpoint: evaluate at the half step and use that slope. Both cost two evaluations a step and their error shrinks with h² (second order).

At equal evaluations midpoint wins; plain Heun loses to Euler until its last step is made Euler

The trained image model: RMS pixel difference from a fine answer (midpoint, 500 steps, which never evaluates t = 1), 200 images, both axes logarithmic, so the slopes are the solvers' orders measured by the root on Apple M1 Pro (MPS). At evaluations: Euler , plain Heun , midpoint , Heun with a last Euler step (9 evaluations). Even midpoint's error stops falling past 50 evaluations ( at 50, at 100): finer steps evaluate closer to t = 1, where the learned field is rough.

On samples. FD at 4 evaluations: midpoint , Euler , plain Heun ; one midpoint step (2 evaluations) against for two Euler steps measured. The culprit is t = 1. Heun's last corrector evaluates the network on a clean image, whose source noise is independent of it, so the best squared-error answer is v = x1 − E[x0] = the input itself, not any path's slope: the network's output at t = 1 has cosine with its input and with the true x1 − x0 ( at t = 0.9) measured on 1,000 validation images. Making the last step plain Euler, as diffusers' HeunDiscreteScheduler does (v0.41.0, scheduling_heun_discrete.py lines 364 and 367; n steps cost 2n − 1 evaluations), recovers it: at 5 evaluations, at 9.

The grid is half the solver. Where the steps go matters as much as how each is taken (section 6 shows DDIM is Euler on a warped grid). Production samplers keep Euler and move the grid: the resolution shift (section 11), and Movie Gen's linear-quadratic grid, which "follows the first 25 steps of an N-step linear schedule and then approximates the remaining N − 25 steps with 25 quadratically placed steps", 50 steps "emulating 250 linear steps" (arXiv 2410.13720, section 3.4.2) published. Multistep solvers reuse past evaluations, so they get second order at one evaluation per step: Wan 2.1's diffusers repository ships UniPCMultistepScheduler with prediction_type: flow_prediction, flow_shift: 3.0, order 2 (config at revision 0fad780a534b, read 2026-10-17), a diffusion solver reused on a flow model, as Sana does with DPM-Solver.
