4. Choosing the path: straight, cosine, or diffusion's

The problem. "Flow matching" usually means the straight path at = t, bt = 1 − t, but the loss works for any Gaussian path xt = at x1 + bt x0 (Lipman et al. 2023 and Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden's stochastic interpolants, both 2022 to 2023). Diffusion's paths are variance preserving (VP: a² + b² = 1). Which path is best, and why?

Three paths, all exact. Because the toy's field is exact for any path, the comparison below has no learning error at all: straight (a = t, b = 1 − t); cosine VP (a = sin(πt/2), b = cos(πt/2), what SiT calls GVP); and the continuous DDPM schedule (Song et al.'s VP SDE with β from 0.1 to 20, the schedule behind DDPM's linear betas).

Published, at ImageNet scale. SiT (Ma et al., arXiv 2401.08740, Jan 2024) kept DiT's architecture and changed one ingredient at a time, SiT-B, ImageNet 256, 400K steps, FID (lower is better) published: DDPM-style noise prediction 44.2; continuous-time score 43.6; velocity on the VP path 39.8; velocity on the straight path 34.8; velocity on the cosine (GVP) path 34.6 (their Tables 2 to 4). They attribute the gain to shorter, less curved paths and note that the VP path's σ̇ "becomes singular" near the data, which makes the velocity hard to learn there. The ranking matches the toy: cosine and straight close together, the DDPM schedule well behind.

Cost. None at training time: the path is two scalar functions. When it matters: when porting a sampler between models (its time grid assumes a path) and when reading ablations (a "flow against diffusion" result usually changes path, prediction target and time sampling at once). How to check: straightness of many-step trajectories, and sample quality at 2 to 8 steps under each path at equal compute.

Go deeper: the root's Flow lab, section 6 ("One family") shows the conversion formulas between velocity, noise and clean-image predictions; the {{Math page, sections 15 and 16|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}} derive the probability-flow ODE and DDIM.

5. Couplings: which noise is paired with which image

The problem. A coupling is the rule that pairs each noise with an image during training. Plain flow matching uses the independent coupling: draw both at random. Random pairs cross each other everywhere, and section 1 showed that crossing lines are what the average bends around. Can a better pairing straighten the learned flow without a second training stage?

How it works. Minibatch optimal transport (OT; Tong et al., arXiv 2302.00482, and Pooladian et al., "Multisample Flow Matching", arXiv 2304.14772, both 2023): inside each batch of B noises and B images, re-pair them to minimise the total squared distance (the Hungarian algorithm), then train as usual. The pairs are still noise-and-data, so the marginals are unchanged; only which line each point is on changes.

When it matters: low-dimensional or strongly clustered data, and latent spaces where the data are much less spread than the noise.
