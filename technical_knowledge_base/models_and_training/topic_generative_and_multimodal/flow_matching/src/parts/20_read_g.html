10. Solvers, step grids and step counts

The root's Flow lab (section 3) measured Euler, Heun and midpoint on the tiny model at equal network evaluations (NFE, the real cost), found plain Heun failing because its last correction evaluates the net at t = 1, and fixed it with diffusers' last-step-Euler rule. Three additions from this page:

  • The grid is half the solver. Section 4 showed DDIM is Euler on a warped grid and that the same curves sampled on two schedules give against at 4 steps. Production samplers keep Euler and move the grid: the resolution shift (section 8), Qwen-Image's and LTX-Video's shift_terminal (the shifted grid is stretched so that its last noise level before the final step to clean data is 0.02 or 0.1; stretch_shift_to_terminal, line 279), and Movie Gen's linear-quadratic grid, which "follows the first 25 steps of an N-step linear schedule and then approximates the remaining N − 25 steps with 25 quadratically placed steps", 50 steps "emulating 250 linear steps" (arXiv 2410.13720, section 3.4.2) published.
  • Multistep solvers on flows. Wan 2.1's diffusers repository ships UniPCMultistepScheduler with prediction_type: flow_prediction, use_flow_sigmas: true, flow_shift: 3.0, order 2 (scheduler config at revision 0fad780a534b, read 2026-10-17): a diffusion solver reused on a flow model, as Sana does with DPM-Solver (the root's Flow lab, section 8). Multistep solvers reuse past evaluations, so they get second order at one NFE per step.
  • How many steps. Exact-field toy: 4 Euler steps already put on a blob ( converged); images: the root's 1-RF needs 10 to 20 Euler steps, its midpoint solver 4 NFE. Released examples: FLUX.1 [schnell] "1 to 4 steps" (card); FLUX 3 Image Klein 50 steps for the base and 16 for the distilled variant (vendor statement, the root's Model map); Movie Gen 50 (above).

The pattern. Since SD3 every major open image and video generator trains a transformer with flow matching on the straight path and logit-normal t, then samples with Euler (or a multistep solver) on a shifted grid whose shift grows with resolution or with fewer steps. What differs is the shift rule (fixed, or linear in token count), whether the grid stops short of the data end, and the distillation used for few-step variants.
