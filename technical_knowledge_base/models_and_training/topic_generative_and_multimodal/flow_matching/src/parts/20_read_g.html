11. Which t to train on: timestep sampling and the resolution shift

The problem. Each training step draws one t per example. Uniform t spends equal effort on every noise level, but section 2 showed the levels differ: near pure noise the target is mostly unpredictable, near clean data the input gives the answer away, and the middle is where the network has the most to learn. Which distribution of t gives the best model for a fixed budget?

How it works. Draw u uniform in [0, 1] and map it. SD3 (Esser et al., arXiv 2403.03206, section 3.1) compared, in its own convention (σ = 1 − t: 0 data, 1 noise): logit-normal lognorm(m, s), the logistic of a normal with mean m and scale s (m > 0 leans towards noise); mode sampling fmode(u; s) = 1 − u − s (cos²(πu/2) − 1 + u), which keeps some weight at the ends; and CosMap 1 − 1/(tan(πu/2) + 1).

Logit-normal(0, 1) puts most training where the loss floor is falling: the middle of the path

SD3 trained 61 variants on ImageNet and CC12M; ranked over two datasets, EMA and plain weights and several step counts, "rf/lognorm(0.00, 1.00) consistently achieves a good rank": first overall (rank 1.54), plain uniform rectified flow 5.67, classic eps/linear diffusion 2.88 (Table 1). At 25 steps on ImageNet, FID 45.78 for lognorm(0, 1) against 49.70 for uniform (Table 2) published. Logit-normal(0, 1) became the default: Movie Gen (arXiv 2410.13720: "zero mean and unit standard deviation"), HunyuanVideo (2412.03603) and Wan (2503.20314) all state logit-normal t published.

The resolution shift. A bigger image has more pixels to average over, so the same noise level destroys less of it: at a given t a 1024-pixel image is "less noisy" than a 256-pixel one. SD3 shifts the time to compensate, tm = √(m/n) tn / (1 + (√(m/n) − 1) tn) on the noise level (section 5.3.2, eq. 23), with "a shift value of α = 3.0 both during training and sampling at resolution 1024×1024" published. diffusers implements a fixed shift (v0.41.0, scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py line 351) and, with use_dynamic_shifting, math.exp(mu) / (math.exp(mu) + (1 / t - 1) ** sigma) (lines 646 to 647) with μ linear in the number of image tokens (calculate_shift, pipelines/flux/pipeline_flux.py lines 73 to 83). With sigma = 1 the two are one formula with shift = eμ derived: FLUX's defaults (μ from 0.5 at 256 tokens to 1.15 at 4,096) give shift 1.65 at 512 × 512 and 3.16 at 1024 × 1024, beside SD3's 3.0.

The shift pushes a few-step grid towards the noise end, more so at higher resolution

Predict, then drag: at 4 steps and 1024 px with the FLUX.1 defaults, how many evaluations fall in the noisier half? Then halve the resolution.

When it matters: training from scratch (logit-normal(0, 1) unless you have a reason), fine-tuning at a new resolution (the shift should change with it), and sampling with few steps (HunyuanVideo: "we set a larger shifting factor s for a lower inference step"). On the 28 × 28 model no shift was best (the root measured FD 158.3 at 4 steps unshifted against 294.6 with shift 1/3 and 242.4 with shift 3): a small image has nothing to gain. How to check: per-t validation loss against a reference run, and samples at your production step count.
