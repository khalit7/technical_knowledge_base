12. Common mistakes, with what they look like

The root's Flow lab, section 9, reproduced the two convention bugs (reversed time, t × 1000) as images. These are the next layer.

Reading the training loss as progress
On the toy, of the converged loss is the irreducible spread of the targets (section 1). A loss curve that has flattened says nothing about whether samples are good, and two objectives (paths, couplings, t distributions) have different floors, so their losses cannot be compared. Score samples instead.
"Train longer" to fix blurry few-step samples
The exact field itself scores at 2 steps (section 2). Change the pairing (reflow), the grid (shift) or distil.
Calling set_timesteps(n) yourself instead of passing the pipeline's grid
diffusers' flow scheduler, given only a step count, spaces its grid down to the smallest training noise level, so with 4 steps the last step starts at noise level about 0.003 and does nothing; the FLUX, FLUX.2 and Qwen-Image pipelines pass sigmas = np.linspace(1.0, 1 / num_inference_steps, num_inference_steps) instead (pipeline_flux.py line 823). Measured with FLUX.2 [klein]'s real config at 1024 px (src/runs/fm_sched_demo.py, diffusers 0.41.0):
The scheduler's own grid gives three useful steps; the pipeline's gives four. The fixed and dynamic forms of the shift agree inside the grid (0.8635 against 0.8639) and differ only in where they put the smallest level.


A dynamic-shift scheduler without μ
Using a FLUX.2, Qwen-Image or LTX-Video scheduler config in your own loop: ValueError: `mu` must be passed when `use_dynamic_shifting` is set to be `True` (line 310, the real message above). Compute μ from your image's token count with calculate_shift and the config's base and max values; a fixed shift of eμ is equivalent inside the grid.


Copying a shift across resolutions or step counts
SD3's 3.0 is for 1024 × 1024; FLUX's rule gives 1.65 at 256 tokens and 3.16 at 4,096; HunyuanVideo raises the shift when it lowers the step count. Recompute it for your resolution (the explorer tab) and sweep it at your step budget.

One more reflow round "to be safe"
Each round fits the last round's errors: the toy's fine-solve quality drops round by round, and the images' 50-step FD and diversity drop too (section 6). Liu et al.: "It is not recommended to apply too many reflow steps". Stop when few-step quality stops improving, then distil.

13. Interview questions


What does a perfectly trained flow-matching network output?
The marginal velocity u(x, t) = E[x1 − x0 | xt = x]: the posterior-weighted average of the straight-line velocities of every (noise, data) pair whose line passes through x at time t. Following it carries the noise distribution onto the data distribution.


Why can we train on single pairs if the target is the marginal field?
The conditional loss and the marginal loss differ by a constant independent of the network (Lipman et al., Theorem 2), so their gradients are equal. The constant is the spread of the targets around their average, which on the toy is about 99% of the converged loss.


Each training path is straight; why does one Euler step give a blur?
At t = 0 the field averages over every possible endpoint, so one step lands on the mean of the data (exactly the centre of the toy's ring; a class-average blur on images). This is a property of the exact field, not a training failure.


If the network learned the loss's exact optimum on its training set, what would it generate?
Copies of training images: the empirical field's softmax collapses onto one image. Measured here, 100% copies at 50 steps. Trained networks generalise because their inductive biases (locality, equivariance) stop them reaching that optimum.


What is the difference between a path, a coupling and a time distribution?
Path: how a pair is interpolated (at, bt). Coupling: which noise is paired with which image (independent, minibatch OT, reflow pairs). Time distribution: which t the loss is evaluated at (uniform, logit-normal). They are independent knobs; diffusion and flow matching differ in all three by default.


Why does reflow straighten paths, and what does it cost?
The new pairs come from an ODE, whose paths cannot cross, so the averaged field is close to each pair's straight line. Cost: a pair-generation pass and a second training, and each round inherits the previous model's errors (many-step quality and diversity fall).


Why does minibatch OT help a 2-D toy far more than an image model?
In high dimensions distances between random noise and images concentrate, so re-pairing a few thousand candidates barely shortens the trip: 5% here against 69% in 2-D.


Why logit-normal t, and why a resolution shift?
Logit-normal spends training on the middle noise levels, where there is the most to learn; SD3's 61-variant study ranked lognorm(0, 1) first. The shift moves noise levels towards noise for bigger images, which need more noise to lose the same structure (SD3 eq. 23; shift 3.0 at 1024 px; FLUX's eμ rule).


ODE or SDE sampling for a flow model?
Same marginals for any noise level ε: dx = (v + ε/2 score) dt + √ε dW with score = −(x − t v)/(1 − t). With a perfect field noise only costs steps; with an imperfect one it corrects accumulated error, given enough steps and noise that fades out at the data end (SiT; on the tiny model here FD 35.7 at 250 steps against 53.0 for the ODE). Few steps: ODE.


Design: you must train a text-to-image model to be served at 4 steps, 1024 px. Plan the generative side.
Latent space from an existing VAE; a transformer trained with flow matching on the straight path, logit-normal(0, 1) t, the resolution shift at 1024 px (about 3), 10% caption dropout for guidance; sample a 50-step teacher with Euler or a multistep solver on the pipeline grid. For 4 steps: either reflow once and distil (InstaFlow route), or adversarial distillation (LADD, as FLUX.1 [schnell]), or a step-conditioned model (shortcut, MeanFlow); distil guidance into the model (FLUX.1 [dev]) so each step is one call. Evaluate at 4 steps against the teacher at 50 on FID (with large N), a text-alignment score and a diversity measure; check nearest-neighbour copies. Cost to watch: the pair pass or adversarial training, and the VAE decode once denoising is 4 calls.
