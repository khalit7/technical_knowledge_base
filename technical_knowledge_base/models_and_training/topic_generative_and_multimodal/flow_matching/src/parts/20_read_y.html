Mixing conventions: time direction, t × 1000, the sign of v
diffusers' flow scheduler counts the other way. FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler (v0.41.0, schedulers/scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py) noises with sample = sigma * noise + (1.0 - sigma) * sample (line 234), so its model output is noise − data, the negative of this page's v; it steps with dt = sigma_next - sigma (line 504, negative) and prev_sample = sample + dt * model_output (line 511), and passes the network sigmas * num_train_timesteps, σ × 1000 (line 367). Same maths, opposite sign and a factor of 1,000 on t. What each mix-up does to the tiny model (Euler, 50 steps, same noise):
correct
time reversed (integrating from t = 1 down to 0)
t × 1000 passed to a net trained on t in [0, 1]

Porting a checkpoint between the conventions needs both the time flip and the sign flip. A quick test: the 1-step output should be a recognisable blur of the class; if it is not, the convention is wrong, not the model.
Reading the training loss as progress
On the toy of the converged loss is the irreducible spread of the targets (section 2). A flat loss says nothing about samples, and objectives with different paths, couplings or t distributions have different floors, so their losses cannot be compared. Score samples (FD at a fixed sample size, accuracy, diversity) at several step counts.
"Train longer" to fix blurry few-step samples
The exact field itself scores at 2 steps (section 3). Change the pairing (reflow), the grid (shift) or distil.
Comparing solvers at equal steps, or evaluating the net at t = 1
Heun and midpoint cost two evaluations per step: compare at equal NFE. A solver that evaluates the network exactly at t = 1 (plain Heun's last corrector) uses a slope the model cannot know; on the image model that made Heun worse than Euler (section 5). End with an Euler step, or use a solver that never touches the end point (midpoint).
Calling set_timesteps(n) yourself instead of passing the pipeline's grid
diffusers' flow scheduler, given only a step count, spaces its grid down to the smallest training noise level, so with 4 steps the last step starts at noise level about 0.003 and does nothing; the FLUX, FLUX.2 and Qwen-Image pipelines pass sigmas = np.linspace(1.0, 1 / num_inference_steps, num_inference_steps) instead (pipeline_flux.py line 823). The scheduler's own grid gives three useful steps; the pipeline's gives four. The fixed and dynamic forms of the shift agree inside the grid (0.8635 against 0.8639) and differ only in where they put the smallest level.


A dynamic-shift scheduler without μ
Using a FLUX.2, Qwen-Image or LTX-Video scheduler config in your own loop: ValueError: `mu` must be passed when `use_dynamic_shifting` is set to be `True` (line 310, the real message above). Compute μ from your image's token count with calculate_shift and the config's base and max values; a fixed shift of eμ is equivalent inside the grid.


Copying a shift or a guidance scale across resolutions, models or step counts
SD3's 3.0 is for 1024 × 1024; FLUX's rule gives 1.65 at 256 tokens and 3.16 at 4,096; HunyuanVideo raises the shift when it lowers the step count; on the 28 × 28 model no shift was best. Guidance scales differ the same way (best s = 1.5 on the tiny flow model, section 10). Recompute the shift for your resolution (section 11) and sweep both at your step budget on a few hundred samples.

One more reflow round "to be safe"
Each round fits the last round's errors: the toy's fine-solve quality and the images' 50-step FD and diversity drop round by round (section 8). Liu et al.: "It is not recommended to apply too many reflow steps". Stop when few-step quality stops improving, then distil.


Logit-normal t, then sampling from the least trained point
Every sampler's first evaluation is at pure noise, which logit-normal(0, 1) almost never trains: the toy's error there rose from  to  (section 11). SD3 still ranked it first at 5 steps, so this is usually harmless, but it is the first suspect if 1- or 2-step samples get worse after switching to logit-normal.


Reading DDIM-against-flow results as path-against-path
A VP schedule with a uniform grid and the cosine schedule trace the same curves but score  against  at 4 steps (section 6). Before crediting the path, check the grid.


Stochastic sampling with constant noise to the very end
The score of a flow model is −(x − t v)/(1 − t): near the data end it multiplies the network's errors. Constant ε = 0.5 turned FD  into  at 50 steps (section 12). Let the noise go to zero at the data end, and use enough steps.


Expecting reflow to be free
The pairs inherit the first model's mistakes, so full-solve quality drops (Liu et al.'s CIFAR-10 RK45 FID 2.58 to 3.36; the toy and the images in section 8). Measure both ends: few-step and many-step.


Trusting a model whose samples are suspiciously sharp
The exact optimum of the loss copies the training set (section 4). On a small fine-tuning set, check nearest-training-neighbour distances against held-out images before trusting a low FID.







Interview rehearsal (optional)


What does a flow-matching model predict, what is its loss, and why is it called simulation-free?
A velocity vθ(xt, t). Training: xt = (1 − t) noise + t data, loss ‖vθ − (data − noise)‖², t uniform or logit-normal. Sampling: integrate dx/dt = v from noise to data. Simulation-free: training never solves the ODE; each step builds xt in closed form and does one regression, where older continuous normalising flows simulated and backpropagated through the ODE.


What does a perfectly trained flow-matching network output, and why can we train on single pairs?
The marginal velocity u(x, t) = E[x1 − x0 | xt = x], the posterior-weighted average of the line velocities through x. The per-pair and marginal losses differ by a network-independent constant (Lipman et al., Theorem 2), so their gradients are equal; the constant is the targets' spread, about 99% of the converged loss on the toy.


Each training path is straight; why does one Euler step give a blur, and what would the exact optimum generate?
At t = 0 the field averages over every possible end point, so one step lands on the data mean (the centre of the toy's ring; a class-average blur on images): a property of the exact field, not a training failure. On a finite training set the exact optimum generates only copies of training images (100% at 50 steps here); networks generalise because their inductive biases stop them reaching it.


Path, coupling, time distribution: what is each, and how do flow matching and diffusion differ?
Path: how a pair is interpolated (at, bt). Coupling: which noise is paired with which data point (independent, minibatch OT, reflow pairs). Time distribution: which t the loss is evaluated at. Diffusion and flow matching are one family (Gaussian paths, a network readable as noise, data or velocity prediction, a deterministic ODE); they differ in path, output parameterisation (hence loss weighting) and t distribution by default.


Why does reflow straighten paths, what does it cost, and why does minibatch OT help a toy far more than images?
Reflow's pairs come from an ODE, whose paths cannot cross, so the averaged field is close to each pair's straight line. Cost: a pair-generation pass and a second training; each round inherits the previous model's errors (many-step quality and diversity fall). Minibatch OT: in high dimensions distances between random noise and images concentrate, so re-pairing a few thousand candidates barely shortens the trip (5% here against 69% in 2-D).


Euler or Heun? ODE or SDE?
Compare at equal evaluations. Second-order error falls with h², but the treatment of the data end matters as much: on the image model midpoint won at every budget while plain Heun lost to Euler until its last step was made Euler. SDE: same marginals for any ε; with a perfect field noise only costs steps, with an imperfect one it corrects accumulated error given enough steps and noise that fades at the data end (FD 35.7 at 250 steps against 53.0 for the ODE here). Few steps: ODE.


Why logit-normal t, and why a resolution shift?
Logit-normal spends training on the middle noise levels, where there is the most to learn; SD3's 61-variant study ranked lognorm(0, 1) first. The shift moves noise levels towards noise for bigger images, which need more noise to lose the same structure (SD3 eq. 23; 3.0 at 1024 px; FLUX's eμ rule).


Design: a text-to-image model served at 4 steps, 1024 px. Plan the generative side.
Latent space from an existing VAE; a transformer trained with flow matching on the straight path, logit-normal(0, 1) t, the resolution shift at 1024 px (about 3), 10% caption dropout for guidance; a 50-step teacher with Euler or a multistep solver on the pipeline grid. For 4 steps: reflow once and distil (InstaFlow route), adversarial distillation (LADD, as FLUX.1 [schnell]), or a step-conditioned model (shortcut, MeanFlow); distil guidance in (FLUX.1 [dev]) so each step is one call. Evaluate at 4 steps against the teacher at 50 on FID with large N, a text-alignment score and diversity, and check nearest-neighbour copies. Cost to watch: the pair pass or adversarial training, and the VAE decode once denoising is 4 calls.
