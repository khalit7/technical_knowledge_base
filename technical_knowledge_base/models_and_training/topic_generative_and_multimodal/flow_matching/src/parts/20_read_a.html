The idea in one screen

Flow matching trains a network to output the straight-line velocity from a noise sample to a data sample; because many such lines pass through every point, what it actually learns is their average, the marginal velocity field, whose paths bend, and every few-step method is a way to make that field easier to follow.

Fig. 1 is the page's 2-D toy in space-time: each upright glass plate is the same plane at one time, t = 0 (noise) on the left to t = 1 (data) on the right. Every point and path is computed in your browser by the page's own engine (the exact field and the trained networks' int8 weights).

  1. Two clouds. Noise x0 is easy to sample (a standard Gaussian, teal, on the t = 0 plate); data x1 is not (the toy's ring of 8 blobs, orange, at t = 1). Flow matching looks for a velocity that carries the first cloud onto the second.
  2. Train on one straight line per pair. Pair each noise with a random data point. Training picks a t, the point xt = (1 − t) x0 + t x1 on their line, and asks the network, given xt and t, for this line's constant velocity x1 − x0. One call, one squared error. The bold pair is section 1's illustrative example.
  3. Many lines cross at every point. Through one point x at t = 0.5 pass lines from many pairs, drawn as strong as their posterior weight (here mostly two neighbouring blobs). The network sees only xt and t, so a squared error makes it output their weighted average: the marginal field u (section 2).
  4. Sample by following u. Solving dx/dt = u from noise reaches the data: 40 paths of the exact field (48 Euler steps) land on the blobs, along bent paths, because each point's average direction turns as it commits to one blob.
  5. One big step lands on the mean. One Euler step follows the first arrow and lands on the average of the data, where there is none: at t = 0 the field is "the average data point minus where you are", so every point goes to the ring's empty centre (section 3; on images, the blur of section 1).
  6. Reflow makes the path straight enough for one step. Retraining on the flow's own (noise, output) pairs (section 8) gives the root's 2-RF network, whose paths are straight: one Euler step lands on a blob for of 2,000 noises (and for the count shown of these 40).

Regression with a squared error (it predicts the conditional mean of its target) and Gaussian noise; the page builds the rest. New terms

  • Velocity field v(x, t): an arrow for every point and time; sampling follows it from t = 0 (noise) to t = 1 (data).
  • Conditional / marginal field: one pair's line velocity / the average over every pair through x.
  • Path (at, bt): how a pair is mixed, xt = at x1 + bt x0.
  • Coupling: which noise is paired with which image in training.
  • k-RF, reflow: the k-th rectified flow, trained on the (k−1)-th flow's own (noise, output) pairs.
  • NFE: network evaluations per sample, the real cost. Straightness: straight-line distance over path length (1 = straight).
  • Shift: a warp of the time grid that moves steps towards the noise end.

Running example. The root topic's 2-D toy (noise in the plane; data a ring of 8 Gaussian blobs, radius 3, spread 0.25; small 3 → 48 → 48 → 48 → 2 networks, parameters) and its Fashion-MNIST model (a -parameter U-Net trained steps in s on an Apple M1 Pro). Image scores are the root's: FD, the Fréchet distance between 1,000 samples and the test set in a Fashion-MNIST classifier's feature space (lower is better; 1,000 real images score ); class accuracy; and diversity, the mean feature distance between same-class samples (real ). On the toy, "on a blob" means within 0.75 (3 spreads) of a centre.
