The idea. Making images and reading them are two pipelines built from the same few parts: a generator turns random noise into a small latent through tens of denoising passes and decodes it to pixels once; a reader cuts an image into patch tokens that a language model attends to like words; a native multimodal model runs both inside one transformer.

Shared parts tie the lanes together: CLIP’s text tower is Stable Diffusion 1.5’s text encoder and its image tower was the first open VLMs’ encoder; the autoencoder that compresses for a generator also tokenises images for a native model; the transformer of the bottom lane became the denoiser of the top one (DiT).

You need the transformer (attention, a block, a language model’s next-token loss): {{The transformer from zero|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8174980ddde4a20c7b3c}} if it is rusty. The derivations behind the losses (ELBO, scores, guidance, flow matching) are on {{Variational inference and generative-model maths|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}}; nothing here needs them.

New terms on this page (each defined again where it is used):
multimodal
takes in or produces more than one kind of data (images and text, video and audio)
latent
a compressed version of the image (Stable Diffusion: 64 × 64 × 4 numbers for 512 × 512 pixels) that the generator works on
denoiser, pass
the network that removes a little noise; one run of it is a pass, and an image costs tens of them
guidance (CFG)
running the denoiser with and without the prompt and pushing past the difference; two passes per step
patch token
one square of pixels turned into one vector by a vision transformer
connector
the small network that maps encoder vectors to a language model’s width
early fusion
images enter one transformer as tokens beside text, from the first layer
FID
a distance between the features of real and generated images; lower is better

The running example. Every small model in the topic trains on Fashion-MNIST: 70,000 grey 28 × 28 images of clothing in 10 classes (MIT licence, Zalando), padded to 32 × 32; clothing shows blur, dropped modes and class confusions by eye, and a 1M-parameter U-Net learns it in minutes on a laptop GPU. Sixteen fixed test images are used for every before/after. The pretrained models, pinned to exact revisions, are Stable Diffusion v1.5 (CreativeML OpenRAIL-M), CLIP ViT-B/32, SigLIP 2 base and the VLM SmolVLM-500M-Instruct (both Apache 2.0), run on sixteen colour test images with known answers (photographs in the public domain or CC0, synthetic scenes, Stable Diffusion outputs). 1. Five ways to generate, three ways to understand

An image of 28 × 28 grey pixels is a point in a 784-dimensional space; almost every point is static, and the images that look like clothing fill a tiny, oddly shaped region of it. We never get a formula for that region (the data distribution p(x)), only examples. A generative model is a recipe for drawing new points from it; a conditional one draws from p(x | c), with c a class or a prompt. The five families differ mostly in network calls per sample and training signal:

Autoregressive: p(x) = ∏ p(xi | x<i), a language model’s chain rule; on raw pixels the samples were poor (Gated PixelCNN: FID 65.93 on CIFAR-10, {{DDPM Table 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2006.11239v2#S4.T1}}), on learned image tokens far better (Parti treats text-to-image “as a sequence-to-sequence modeling problem” at 20B parameters, {{Yu et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10789}}). VAE ({{Kingma and Welling, Dec 2013|https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114}}): section 2. GAN ({{Goodfellow et al., June 2014|https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661}}): a generator and a discriminator, “the training procedure for G is to maximize the probability of D making a mistake”; sharp in one call, no likelihood, and mode collapse (a few outputs that fool the judge); no child page teaches GANs, because in 2026 they survive only as a loss inside other models. Diffusion ({{Ho et al., June 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239}}) reached FID 3.17 on CIFAR-10, the best unconditional score in its table ({{DDPM page|n:3c65c17b0d0d811481e4e36333454f7a}}). Flow matching: {{Lipman et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747}}; rectified flow, {{Liu et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003}}. Section 3 compares the last two.

Understanding, not generating. A vision encoder turns an image into vectors (ViT, CLIP, SigLIP). A vision-language model (VLM) feeds those vectors to a language model, which answers in text. A native multimodal model puts text and image tokens into one transformer, which can read and write both.

Three families make the same pullover: count the network calls

An autoregressive model reveals 49 tokens, one call each; a diffusion sampler takes 50 steps; a flow sampler takes 8 Euler steps. derived Autoregressive: 1,024 calls, one per token, each waiting for the last (Chameleon encodes “a 512×512 image into 1024 discrete tokens from a codebook of size 8192”, {{Chameleon Team 2024|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v1}}). Diffusion: 30 steps × 2 (conditional and unconditional pass) = 60 calls, and diffusers runs the two passes of a step as one batch of 2 (torch.cat([latents] * 2), {{pipeline_stable_diffusion.py line 1037, v0.41.0|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L1037}}), so 30 sequential rounds; 1,024 / 60 ≈ 17. But each autoregressive call reuses a cache and processes one new token, while each diffusion call processes the whole image: counting calls alone is not counting compute (section 10).
