A generative model learns what its training data looks like well enough to produce new examples of it: new images, video, sound or text that could plausibly have come from the same source. A multimodal model takes in or produces more than one kind of data (images and text, video and audio). This topic covers both, taught from zero: how images are compressed into latents, how diffusion and flow models turn random noise into an image, how a model "sees" an image as a sequence of patches, and how a language model is taught to read those patches. Reading time about 30 minutes (measured from the words outside widgets at 200 a minute; the folded answers add about 10).

Understanding, not generating. A vision encoder turns an image into vectors (ViT, CLIP, SigLIP). A vision-language model (VLM) feeds those vectors to a language model, which answers in text. A native multimodal model puts text and image tokens into one transformer, which can read and write both.

What ties them together. Modern generators work in a compressed latent (section 2), read images as patches with a transformer (sections 4, 5), and get text through an encoder trained on image-caption pairs (section 5).
