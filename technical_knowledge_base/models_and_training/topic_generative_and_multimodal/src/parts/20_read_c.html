5. Vision encoders: how a model sees

A Vision Transformer cuts the image into square patches, maps each to a vector with one learned matrix and adds a position vector: a 224 × 224 image in patches of 16 is 14 × 14 = 196 tokens derived, and from then on the image is a sequence like a sentence ({{ViT page|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa231cfcde6c1954d}}). What the encoder learns depends on its training signal: labels, the pixels themselves (MAE, DINO), or captions. Caption training is what links images to words: CLIP scores every image against every caption in a batch with a softmax ({{Radford et al., Feb 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020}}); SigLIP scores each pair on its own with a sigmoid, so it needs no batch-wide sum and shards more cheaply ({{Zhai et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15343}}). The shared space gives zero-shot classification: write one sentence per class and pick the nearest. On the 10,000 Fashion-MNIST test images CLIP B/32 reaches 66.7% at best and SigLIP 2 74.5% (chance 10%); a linear probe on 16 labelled images per class reaches 79.0% and 84.0%; SigLIP 2 drops to about 22% if class names keep their capitals measured ({{Zero-shot lab|#t-vision}}). The embedding keeps what helps match captions and loses much else: a written word can override the object, and counting, left and right and attribute binding are close to guesses.

Most open VLMs follow LLaVA ({{Liu et al., Apr 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485}}): the projected patch vectors replace a placeholder in the prompt and are attended to like words; the alternative, cross-attention from a frozen language model to the image (Flamingo), keeps images out of the context. An image costs tokens: LLaVA-1.5’s CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 pixels gives (336 / 14)2 = 576 derived; SmolVLM-500M gives 1,088 for any square image (16 tiles plus one whole view, 64 tokens each after a pixel shuffle) measured ({{VLM lab|#t-vlm}}). More tokens read smaller text and cost context and time quadratically, which is why models tile, run at native resolution or merge neighbouring patches. Training aligns the connector first with the rest frozen, then tunes more of the model on conversations about images; the signature failure is object hallucination, naming what is not there because the language model’s habits outvote weak visual evidence.

8. Video and audio: the same four moves

Video is latent diffusion or flow with a time axis: a causal VAE compresses space and time, and a transformer denoises space-time patches (Sora is “trained on and subsequently generates videos within this compressed latent space”, {{OpenAI, Feb 2024, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/}} vendor; Movie Gen is a 30B-parameter transformer trained with flow matching, {{Polyak et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13720}}). Attention grows with the square of the patch count, so duration is the expensive axis, and the hard failures are temporal: identities that change and objects that vanish between frames. Audio takes two routes: a neural audio codec turns the waveform into parallel streams of discrete tokens that a language model predicts (AudioLM, MusicGen), or diffusion and flow generate a continuous latent or spectrogram (Stable Audio, flow-matching speech).
