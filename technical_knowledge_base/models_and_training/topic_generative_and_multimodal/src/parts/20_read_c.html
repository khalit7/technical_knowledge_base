2. Latents: generate something smaller than the image

The problem. Pixels are expensive and redundant. A 512 × 512 colour image is 786,432 numbers, most of them describing texture the eye barely registers, and a diffusion model pays for every one of them at every step. The fix that every modern image and video generator uses is to generate a compressed version instead, a latent, and turn it back into pixels once at the end.

Predict: Stable Diffusion v1 works on a 64 × 64 grid with 4 channels for a 512 × 512 RGB image. How many times fewer numbers does each denoising step handle?
derived 512 × 512 × 3 = 786,432 against 64 × 64 × 4 = 16,384: 48 times. The spatial grid shrinks 64 times (8 per side, the "downsampling-factor 8 autoencoder" of the {{Stable Diffusion README|https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion}}), but each position keeps 4 channels instead of 3. The LDM paper measured at least 2.7 times faster training and sampling at a milder 4 per side ({{LDM page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}, Table 6).

Autoencoder. An encoder network maps the image to a small code (the bottleneck); a decoder maps the code back; training minimises the reconstruction loss, the difference between input and output. A plain autoencoder compresses well but does not generate: its codes land wherever training put them, so a random code falls in a gap and decodes to nonsense. VAE. The variational autoencoder's encoder outputs a mean and a spread for each code dimension, training samples the code from that range (the reparameterisation trick, z = μ + σ · ε, lets gradients pass through the sampling), and a KL penalty pulls every image's range towards a standard Gaussian. The space fills in, so a random Gaussian code decodes to something plausible. The price is blur: with a squared-error reconstruction loss, the best guess for detail the code cannot pin down is the average of the possibilities, and an average of sharp images is a blurry one. A weight β on the KL term trades the two: more β, a smoother space and blurrier pictures. TODO (Latent lab): the beta sweep on the running example (reconstruction error and sample quality at each beta).

VQ-VAE and image tokens. Replace the continuous code by the nearest entry of a learned codebook ({{van den Oord et al. 2017|https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00937}}): the image becomes a grid of integers, image tokens, which an autoregressive transformer can predict like words. Two terms keep encoder and codebook together (a codebook loss and a commitment loss), and the classic failure is codebook collapse, where most entries are never used.

The Stable Diffusion autoencoder is a VAE in name, but its KL weight is about 10−6 and it is trained with a perceptual loss (distance between deep-network features, LPIPS) plus a patch adversarial loss (a small GAN judge on image patches), which together avoid the blur of pixel losses ({{LDM page, Stage 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}). Its latents are not unit-variance, so every Stable Diffusion v1 pipeline multiplies them by a scaling factor of 0.18215 before diffusion and divides by it before decoding ({{v1 config|https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion/blob/main/configs/stable-diffusion/v1-inference.yaml}}); later models use 16 channels and their own factors (FLUX: 0.3611, {{FLUX code|https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux/blob/main/src/flux/util.py}}). Forget the factor and the denoiser sees a signal about 5.5 times too large (1 / 0.18215, derived), so its noise schedule no longer matches. 3. Diffusion and flow matching, side by side

Diffusion in four sentences. The forward process mixes an image x0 with Gaussian noise ε: xt = √āt x0 + √(1 − āt) ε, where āt falls from 1 to almost 0 as the timestep t runs from 0 to T (1,000 in DDPM); the noise schedule is the rule for how fast it falls, and the signal-to-noise ratio āt / (1 − āt) is the cleanest way to compare schedules. Training picks an image, a random t and random noise, and asks a network, the denoiser, to predict the noise from xt: one squared-error loss. Sampling starts from pure noise and repeatedly subtracts the predicted noise, a little at a time; the rule for each step is the sampler. The denoiser can equally be asked for the clean image or for a mix of the two called v ({{Salimans and Ho 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00512}}); the choice changes which timesteps the loss emphasises, not what the model can learn.

Samplers and steps. DDPM's own sampler adds fresh noise at every step and needs hundreds to a thousand. DDIM ({{Song, Meng and Ermon 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502}}) makes sampling deterministic and works in 10 to 50; higher-order ODE solvers such as DPM-Solver ({{Lu et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00927}}) cut that further. Fewer steps means fewer network calls, and too few means blurred or broken samples. Classifier-free guidance (CFG, {{Ho and Salimans 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598}}) makes the model follow its prompt harder. Train with the prompt dropped some of the time, so one network predicts both with and without it; at sampling, run both and push past the conditional prediction: ε = εuncond + w (εcond − εuncond). It doubles the calls per step, and large w buys prompt fidelity at the cost of variety and, at the extreme, over-saturated colours. Hugging Face diffusers' Stable Diffusion pipeline defaults to num_inference_steps = 50 and guidance_scale = 7.5 ({{pipeline_stable_diffusion.py lines 785 and 788, v0.41.0|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L785}}), and applies the formula above at line 1055; the {{math page, section 17|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}} derives it and shows the diversity loss on a toy. Flow matching in three sentences. Pair each image with a noise sample and draw the straight line between them, xt = (1 − t) x0 + t ε; train a network to predict the direction of that line, ε − x0 (the velocity), from xt and t, with a squared-error loss. To generate, start at noise and step against the predicted velocity with an ODE solver (Euler: move by velocity × step size). If the learned paths were exactly straight, one step would land on the image; they bend because one noise sample could lead to many images, and reflow (retraining on the model's own noise-image pairs, {{Liu, Gong and Liu 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003}}) straightens them.

They are one family. Flow matching "is compatible with a general family of Gaussian probability paths", "which subsumes existing diffusion paths as specific instances", and straight paths "are more efficient than diffusion paths" ({{Lipman et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747}}); a trained diffusion model also defines an ODE, the probability-flow ODE that DDIM and the solvers integrate ({{Song et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456}}). So the choice is mostly the path, the prediction target and how training weights the noise levels; Stable Diffusion 3 changed the last, biasing training noise "towards perceptually relevant scales" ({{Esser et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206}}).

Few-step generation. Distillation trains a student to jump several steps at once: progressive distillation halves the step count repeatedly ({{Salimans and Ho 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00512}}), consistency models map any point on the path to its end ({{Song et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469}}), and adversarial diffusion distillation samples "in just 1-4 steps" ({{Sauer et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17042}}), as FLUX.1 [schnell] does vendor ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell}}). The usual price is some variety and fine detail. Conditioning. In cross-attention, the image positions are the queries and the text tokens are the keys and values, so each region of the latent can look up the words that concern it (the {{LDM page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}} covers this, and the later add-ons built on it: ControlNet, IP-Adapter, LoRA fine-tuning). The text encoder matters more than its size suggests: Imagen found that "increasing the size of the language model in Imagen boosts both sample fidelity and image-text alignment" much more than enlarging the diffusion model ({{Saharia et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11487}}), which is why Imagen conditions on a frozen text-only language model (T5) rather than on a contrastive text tower. Stable Diffusion 3 credits its joint text-image transformer (below) with better text comprehension and "typography", the ability to spell words inside the image, a long-standing weak point of earlier models.

From U-Net to transformer. The U-Net is a convolutional network that shrinks the latent through several resolutions and grows it back, with skip connections between matching levels. A diffusion transformer (DiT, {{Peebles and Xie, Dec 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748}}) cuts the latent into patches and runs a plain transformer; more compute per step "consistently" gave lower FID, reaching 2.27 on ImageNet 256 × 256 (a 32 × 32 × 4 latent in patches of 2 is 256 tokens, derived). Stable Diffusion 3's MMDiT puts text and image tokens in one sequence with "separate weights for the two modalities" ({{Esser et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206}}); FLUX.1 is "a 12 billion parameter rectified flow transformer" ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell}}).

What one image costs. Text encoder once (twice with guidance: diffusers also encodes the empty or negative prompt, {{line 454|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L454}}), denoiser once per step and twice with guidance (run as one batch of two, line 1037), decoder once (line 1085, after dividing by the scaling factor). With 50 steps and guidance, that is 100 denoiser evaluations against one decode, so the denoiser is almost the whole bill, and steps × (1 or 2) is the number to cut.
