2. Latents: generate something smaller than the image

A 512 × 512 colour image is 786,432 numbers; Stable Diffusion v1 generates a 64 × 64 × 4 latent instead, 16,384 numbers, 48 times fewer derived, and the denoiser pays for every number at every step. An autoencoder makes the latent: an encoder squeezes the image, a decoder rebuilds it, trained on the difference. Three kinds matter, and they differ in what you can do with the code:

KindThe codeCan you sample a new image from it?Used by
Plain autoencoderAny vector the training put thereNo: a random code falls in a gap and decodes to nonsenseCompression only
VAEA distribution pulled towards a standard Gaussian by a KL penaltyYes, but blurry: the decoder averages what the code cannot pin downThe compressor of latent diffusion (with the KL weight near zero)
VQ-VAE and its successorsThe nearest entries of a learned codebook: a grid of integersYes, with a second model that predicts the integers like wordsAutoregressive and native multimodal models

The autoencoder of a latent diffusion model is a compressor, not a generator: Stable Diffusion’s weights its KL term at about 10−6 and adds perceptual and adversarial losses for sharp texture, and its latents are rescaled by a scaling factor (0.18215 for Stable Diffusion v1, 0.3611 for FLUX’s 16 channels) so the denoiser sees unit-scale numbers.

3. Diffusion and flow matching, side by side

Diffusion. Mix an image with Gaussian noise in closed form, xt = √āt x0 + √(1 − āt) ε, train a denoiser to predict the noise from xt with a squared error, and generate by starting from pure noise and removing the predicted noise a little per step; the rule per step is the sampler. Flow matching. Draw the straight line from an image to a noise sample, xt = (1 − t) x0 + t ε, train the network to predict its direction ε − x0 (the velocity), and generate by stepping against the predicted velocity with an ODE solver. Classifier-free guidance works the same in both: run the network with and without the prompt and push past the difference, ε = εuncond + w (εcond − εuncond), which doubles the passes per step.

They are one family. Flow matching “is compatible with a general family of Gaussian probability paths”, “which subsumes existing diffusion paths as specific instances” ({{Lipman et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747}}), and a trained diffusion model also defines an ODE, the probability-flow ODE that DDIM and the solvers integrate ({{Song et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13456}}). So the choice is the path, the prediction target and how training weights the noise levels; straighter paths survive larger steps. On the topic’s tiny model, same network, data and training steps, flow with Euler scored FD 158.3 at 4 steps against DDIM’s 478.9 and beat every diffusion sampler from 1 to 50 steps, yet DDPM at 1,000 steps (34.9) still beat any unguided flow sampler measured: fewer steps, not a better end point. Cutting to 1 to 4 steps is distillation, owned by {{Latent diffusion and DiTs|n:3fc5c17b0d0d814fbcb5e0c8df5289f3}} section 10 (reflow and one-step distillation of a flow: {{Flow matching|n:3fc5c17b0d0d8136a1efc73aaa446ceb}} sections 8 and 9).

Stable Diffusion v1.5, the cleanest example. Parameters counted from the pinned weights measured, matching the {{CompVis README|https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion}}’s “860M UNet and 123M text encoder”; 77 tokens is the context length of CLIP’s tokenizer.

What one image costs. Text encoder once (twice with guidance, for the empty prompt), denoiser once per step and twice with guidance, decoder once. With 50 steps and guidance that is 100 denoiser passes against one decode, so the denoiser is almost the whole bill and steps × (1 or 2) is the number to cut. Measured: Stable Diffusion v1.5, 512 × 512, 25 guided DPM-Solver steps, fp16 with attention slicing: 15.75 s warm, 0.610 s per guided step against 0.319 s for one pass, decode 0.488 s, text encoder 0.025 s, peak 4,452 MiB measured on Apple M1 Pro. A datacenter GPU is many times faster per pass; the pass count is the same.

Since 2022 the denoiser became a transformer over latent patches (DiT: more compute per step “consistently” lowered FID, to 2.27 on ImageNet 256 × 256, {{Peebles and Xie, Dec 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748}}), text and image tokens moved into one attention (MMDiT in Stable Diffusion 3, {{Esser et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03206}}; double and single stream blocks in FLUX.1, “a 12 billion parameter rectified flow transformer”, {{model card|https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell}}), and the text encoder grew from CLIP to T5 and to whole language models, because a larger text encoder “boosts both sample fidelity and image-text alignment” more than a larger denoiser ({{Imagen, Saharia et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11487}}).
