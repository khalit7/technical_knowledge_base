9. Evaluation: what the numbers measure and how they mislead

FID (Fréchet Inception Distance, {{Heusel et al. 2017|https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08500}}) is the standard score for generated images. Pass a set of real images and a set of generated ones through Inception-v3, an ImageNet classifier, and keep one 2,048-number feature vector per image; fit a Gaussian (mean and covariance) to each set; report the distance between the two Gaussians, ‖μr − μg‖2 + Tr(Σr + Σg − 2(ΣrΣg)1/2). Lower is better, and it penalises both bad images and too little variety. Its pitfalls are well documented:

  • Sample count. The estimate is biased upwards at small counts ("inconsistent results when varying the sample size", {{Jayasumana et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09603}}), so compare only at equal counts (50,000 by convention). FID of real images against real images at several sample counts is measured in the setup (fid_floor.json). The child page {{Evaluating generative models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81c890f4f62543312faa}} repeats this for every metric at six sample counts (its own draws, real against real: FID 2.538 at 10,000; 15.9, 27.8 and 44.9 at 1,000, 500 and 250 measured).
  • Preprocessing. Resizing and JPEG compression "can induce large variations"; aliased resizing corrupts the features, and "FID can actually improve if the generated images are also subsequently compressed" ({{Parmar, Zhang and Zhu 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11222}}, the clean-fid paper).
  • ImageNet leakage. Inception's features are so tied to ImageNet classes that matching the class histogram of the real set lowers FID "without any real gain in image quality" ({{Kynkäänniemi et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06026}}).
  • Wrong features for modern content. For text-to-image models FID "contradicts human raters"; CMMD, a kernel distance on CLIP embeddings, is one proposed fix ({{Jayasumana et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09603}}).
  • One number for two things. Precision (are samples realistic?) and recall (is the data's variety covered?) separate fidelity from diversity ({{Kynkäänniemi et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06991}}). Papers rarely give FID error bars; differences under about 0.5 between single runs are not evidence ({{LDM page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}).

Prompt alignment. CLIPScore is the CLIP similarity between an image and its prompt ({{Hessel et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08718}}); it inherits CLIP's blindness to counts and relations (section 5). GenEval instead runs an object detector to check "object co-occurrence, position, count, and color" against the prompt ({{Ghosh et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11513}}). The kind of failure it catches: Stable Diffusion v1.5 asked for "an astronaut riding a horse on the moon" drew the astronaut on the moon and no horse measured (setup smoke test, seed 5), an image a global similarity score can still rate as close to its prompt. Human preference remains the final judge: pairwise votes on text-to-image and vision arenas, with the same caveats as text arenas (style, self-selected prompts, vendors testing private variants), are covered on {{Human preference and arenas|n:3ef5c17b0d0d814db49ff3b5c9fc3034}}.

VLMs are scored on question-answering benchmarks (MMMU, MathVista, ChartQA, DocVQA and others, on {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}). Many questions can be answered without the image ({{Chen et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20330}}), so report a text-only baseline beside every score, and probe absent objects directly (section 6).

Predict: two teams report FID 9.1 and 8.4 for their models. What do you need to know before believing the second is better?
All of them. Sample count biases the score, resizing and compression "can induce large variations" (clean-fid), the reference set (training or held-out images, how many) changes it, and two implementations differ (the LDM paper reports 7.76 against 7.77 for the same samples). A 0.7 gap between single runs computed differently says nothing; recompute both with one tool on the same reference set, and look at precision and recall and at images.
