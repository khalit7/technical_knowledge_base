Depth: mistakes that cross pages, facts kept from the labs, two design questions

Mistakes that cross pages (one line each, with the page that shows it)
  • Counting steps instead of passes. Guidance doubles the passes per step and some solvers make two per step (Heun’s method, as in {{EDM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00364}}): compare samplers and models by passes and by time on the same hardware, not by the step count in a config (section 10).
  • Decoding with the wrong latent scale. Every autoencoder has its own scaling factor and the pinned Stable Diffusion v1.5 vae/config.json has no scaling_factor entry, so diffusers falls back to its class default: {{Autoencoders and VAEs|n:3c65c17b0d0d814da93bdb159b7c1e11}} section 11.
  • Mixing up the two guidance-scale conventions, and a scheduler that does not match how the model was trained (prediction target, direction of time, schedule): {{Diffusion from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9bf0d94e57ce9164}} sections 5, 6 and 8; for flows, {{Flow matching|n:3fc5c17b0d0d8136a1efc73aaa446ceb}} section 14.
  • Comparing FIDs computed at different sample counts or with different code, and reading CLIPScore as image quality: {{Evaluating generative models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81c890f4f62543312faa}} sections 3, 5 and 6.
  • Judging a VLM by its captions, and shrinking the image until its text is unreadable: {{Vision-language models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81adbb7be5bc41c4d9d0}} sections 3 and 7.
Kept from the retired labs: facts no child page carries yet

The Latent, Diffusion, Flow and Vision labs were retired on 2026-10-24 because their children now teach the same mechanisms; these few facts are not yet on the owning child and stay here until it absorbs them.

  • Vision encoders. A convolutional network (CNN) slides small filters (3 × 3 pixels, say) over the image, so each layer only mixes neighbours and the same filter is used everywhere: that inductive bias makes it learn from little data and limits what one layer sees; a stack of L such layers (stride 1) sees a window of only 2L + 1 pixels derived. From scratch, a CNN’s inductive bias wins on small data; in practice you start from a pretrained encoder. Raw cosines do not compare across models: CLIP’s matching pairs sit around 0.3 and SigLIP 2’s around 0.1 to 0.2, so thresholds for retrieval or filtering must be calibrated per model. The CAT sticker raises CLIP’s cat score (0.2926 against 0.2846 plain) measured. Temperature: too high (small scale) leaves the loss near ln N with weak gradients, too low lets only the hardest negatives matter and destabilises training (CLIP clipped its scale at 100).
  • Vision-language models. Flamingo’s largest model builds on the 70B Chinchilla language model and is called Flamingo-80B; DeepMind’s announcement calls it “an 80B parameter VLM”.
  • Flow matching. Training the topic’s flow model took 893.3 s (4.48 steps/s) against the DDPM’s 906.7 s measured: the objective costs nothing extra. diffusers implements the resolution shift as sigmas = self.shift * sigmas / (1 + (self.shift - 1) * sigmas) (FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler, v0.41.0, line 351).
  • Diffusion from zero. DPM-Solver++ (2M) is the diffusers default for Stable Diffusion 1.5 (scheduler line 227, v0.41.0); it reuses the previous step’s prediction to take second-order steps at no extra network cost.
  • Autoencoders and VAEs. On the 128-code VQ-VAE’s token map a test image takes 28 to 43 distinct codes for its 64 positions, and the same blank corner gets the same few codes across images (117, 72, 26 recur) measured.
  • Evaluating generative models. FID is biased upwards at small counts even with nothing wrong. Two samples drawn from the same distribution still get a positive FID, and it shrinks as the count grows: 
    import numpy as np
from scipy import linalg

def fid(a, b):  # the Frechet distance FID uses, on any features
    m1, m2 = a.mean(0), b.mean(0)
    s1, s2 = np.cov(a, rowvar=False), np.cov(b, rowvar=False)
    covmean = linalg.sqrtm(s1 @ s2).real
    return float(((m1 - m2) ** 2).sum() + np.trace(s1 + s2 - 2 * covmean))

d = 64                                   # Inception features have 2,048 dimensions; 64 keeps this fast
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
A = rng.normal(size=(d, d)) / np.sqrt(d)
cov = A @ A.T + 0.1 * np.eye(d)          # one fixed "true" feature distribution
L = np.linalg.cholesky(cov)
draw = lambda n: rng.normal(size=(n, d)) @ L.T

for n in [500, 2000, 10000, 50000]:
    scores = [fid(draw(n), draw(n)) for _ in range(3)]
    print(f"n = {n:>6}: FID between two samples of the SAME distribution = {np.mean(scores):.4f} (3 draws: {', '.join(f'{s:.4f}' for s in scores)})")
    n =    500: FID between two samples of the SAME distribution = 3.3324 (3 draws: 3.3952, 3.3297, 3.2723)
n =   2000: FID between two samples of the SAME distribution = 0.8557 (3 draws: 0.8410, 0.8715, 0.8546)
n =  10000: FID between two samples of the SAME distribution = 0.1669 (3 draws: 0.1666, 0.1617, 0.1726)
n =  50000: FID between two samples of the SAME distribution = 0.0331 (3 draws: 0.0338, 0.0334, 0.0322)
    measured on Apple M1 Pro CPU (numpy 2.5.4, scipy 1.18.1; src/read/fid_bias.py). The true distance is 0; the estimate falls roughly as 1 / n, and with 2,048 Inception dimensions instead of 64 the bias is far larger. So "FID 3.3" at 500 samples and "FID 0.03" at 50,000 can be the same model: compare only at equal n, and prefer 50,000.
Design: a text-to-image system that returns an image in under 1 second.
Budget the passes: time one denoiser pass at the target resolution on the target GPU and multiply. 50 steps with guidance is 100 passes, so unless a pass takes under 10 ms it cannot meet 1 s, before the text encoder and decoder. Use a model distilled to 1 to 4 steps with guidance folded in (adversarial or consistency distillation), bf16 or fp8 weights, a compiled graph, the text encoder output cached for repeated prompts, and a fast decoder (or a tiny distilled decoder for previews). Generate at 512 to 768 and upscale if needed; batch requests per GPU; keep a few-step preview then refine path for quality. Measure the latency distribution, and check quality against the slow model with preference tests, because distillation loses variety.
Design: a multimodal LLM should also generate images. Autoregressive tokens or diffusion?
Discrete tokens reuse the language-model stack, loss and serving (Chameleon, and, per its system card, GPT-4o image generation), but cost one pass per token and lose detail in the tokenizer. Diffusion or flow on continuous latents gives better images per FLOP and runs in tens of passes, but needs a second objective and sampler (Transfusion puts both in one transformer; others attach a separate diffusion decoder). Choose by what you must share (one context, editing in conversation), the latency budget and the quality bar.
