The same questions on the pinned SD v1.5 (fp16 on the M1 Pro's GPU, attention slicing on, 512 × 512, guidance 7.5, the prompt and seed of section 6). Its denoiser works on a 4 × 64 × 64 latent rather than pixels (the {{Latent lab|#t-latent}} covers the autoencoder), but the samplers are the same code paths as on the tiny model.

Steps, and where the sampler starts

The timestep-spacing trap. SD 1.5's scheduler config has steps_offset 1 and no timestep_spacing, so DDIM falls back to its default "leading" ({{scheduling_ddim.py lines 365 to 370|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_ddim.py#L365}}): with n steps it uses timesteps 0, 1000/n, 2000/n, … plus 1, so its first step is never t = 999. With 1 step it asks the model to denoise pure noise as if it were step , an almost clean image, and the result is noise. "trailing" starts at 999 (lines 371 to 376), the fix {{Lin et al. (2023)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08891}} recommend; DPM-Solver's default, "linspace", also starts at 999. In the gallery, DDIM with leading spacing at 4 steps (timesteps 751, 501, 251, 1) gives a pale, foggy scene with no lighthouse, while trailing spacing at 4 steps (999, 749, 499, 249) gives a dark but recognisable silhouette with its lighthouse: same model, same noise, same number of network calls. At 25 or 50 steps the difference is small; at 1 to 4 steps, the schedule your sampler walks matters as much as the model.

Where the seconds go: one 512 × 512 image, measured piece by piece

What transfers to a datacenter GPU. The counts transfer: steps × (2 with guidance) U-Net passes, one decode, and the denoiser dominating. The seconds do not: an M1 Pro's GPU in fp16 with attention slicing (chosen here to keep memory near 4.5 GB) is many times slower per pass than a datacenter GPU, and the ratio of a batch-2 pass to a batch-1 pass depends on how much spare compute the device has, which is why guidance costs here close to twice the time per step but can cost less on a large GPU at batch 1.

9. Interview questions

Why can diffusion training sample any timestep without running the earlier ones?

Because the forward process is a chain of Gaussians, xt given x0 is Gaussian in closed form: xt = √āt x0 + √(1 − āt) ε. One line makes the training input for any t.

What is the difference between DDPM and DDIM sampling, and can you use DDIM on a DDPM-trained model?

Same training; DDIM drops the fresh noise and follows the deterministic probability-flow path, so it works with 10 to 50 steps and the same noise always gives the same image. Yes, no retraining: the samplers in section 5 all run on one checkpoint. Which wins depends on the model: on this under-trained one DDPM beat DDIM at every step count from 4 up, because its fresh noise corrects the model's errors.

ε, x0 or v: which would you predict, and why?

They are interchangeable given xt; what differs is the loss weighting across noise levels and the numerical behaviour at high noise. ε is the DDPM default and fine at many steps; v avoids dividing by √ā near pure noise, which matters for few-step sampling, distillation and zero-terminal-SNR schedules (section 5's one-step comparison).

What does classifier-free guidance cost, and what does it buy?

Two denoiser evaluations per step (usually one batch of two) and 10 to 20% of training steps with the condition dropped. It buys adherence to the condition and cleaner samples at the cost of diversity; too high a scale oversaturates. Here, on the tiny model, class accuracy rose from at s = 1 to at s = 3 while diversity fell from to .

Your 4-step samples are noise but 50-step samples are fine. What do you check first?

The timesteps the scheduler actually walks (scheduler.timesteps): with "leading" spacing the first step is far below the last training step. Then the prediction type and whether the model was trained for few steps at all.

How would you cut a text-to-image model's latency by 10 times?

Count network passes: steps × 2 with guidance. Use a better solver (50 to 20 to 25 steps), distil guidance into the model (one pass per step), then distil steps (consistency or adversarial distillation to 1 to 4), and only then shave the per-pass time (lower precision, compilation, a smaller denoiser or latent).
