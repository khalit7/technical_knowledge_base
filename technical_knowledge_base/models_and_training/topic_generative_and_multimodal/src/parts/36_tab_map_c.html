Which model for which job

Pick a job, a licence need and your hardware. The picker filters the table and ranks what is left: current models first, then newer. Memory is estimated as weights only: parameters times bytes per parameter (2 for bfloat16, 1 for 8-bit, 0.5 for 4-bit), against 75% of the memory you choose, because activations, the text encoder and the VAE need the rest. Treat it as a first filter, not a guarantee.

Reading a model licence in one minute

"Open" covers four different things, and the difference decides whether you can ship. Open source in the strict sense means the weights, code and (ideally) data are under an OSI-approved licence such as Apache 2.0 or MIT. Open weights means you can download the weights but the licence may restrict use. Read four clauses:

  • Field of use: "non-commercial" or "research only" (FLUX.1 [dev], FLUX.2 [dev], Qwen-Image-2.1, Ideogram 4, MusicGen, F5-TTS, XTTS-v2).
  • Revenue or user thresholds: free until US$1M annual revenue (Stability AI Community License), US$5M (FLUX Kommunity), US$10M (LTX-2.x), or 700 million monthly users (Llama 4), or 1 million visits a month (CogVideoX).
  • Territory: the Tencent Hunyuan licence "does not apply in the European Union, United Kingdom and South Korea"; Llama 3.2 and Llama 4 withhold multimodal rights from EU-domiciled companies.
  • Use restrictions and naming: OpenRAIL licences list banned uses; DINOv3 bans military uses and asks for "Built with DINOv3".
Same family, different licence
Licences change between versions and sizes: Qwen-Image is Apache 2.0, Qwen-Image-2.1 is research-only; FLUX.2 [klein] 4B is Apache 2.0, the 9B is non-commercial; Gemma 3 used Google's own terms, Gemma 4 is Apache 2.0; DINOv2 is Apache 2.0, DINOv3 has its own licence. Check the licence of the exact checkpoint, not the family. This page summarises; it is not legal advice.
