6. One family: flow, diffusion and the probability-flow ODE

The idea. Both models train on the same kind of input: a scaled image plus scaled Gaussian noise, xt = at · image + bt · noise. The DDPM schedule uses a = √āt, b = √(1 − āt) (variance preserving); the straight path uses a = t, b = 1 − t. Any such model can be read as predicting the noise, the clean image or the velocity, because given xt each determines the others. With this lab's convention (t = 0 noise, derived from xt = (1 − t) x0 + t x1 and v = x1 − x0):

x̂1 = xt + (1 − t) vθ,   x̂0 (the noise) = xt − t vθ

Every diffusion model also has a deterministic ODE with the same marginals at every t, the probability-flow ODE (Song et al. 2021), and DDIM is a way to solve it. So "flow matching against diffusion" is not two kinds of model but three separable choices: the path (schedule), what the network outputs (which sets how the loss weighs each noise level) and how t is drawn in training. The blog Diffusion Meets Flow Matching: Two Sides of the Same Coin (diffusionflow.github.io; its acknowledgements thank colleagues at Google DeepMind; authors and date not shown on the page, read 2026-10-17) puts it as "diffusion models and Gaussian flow matching are the same", and shows that DDIM on the flow schedule is exactly the flow Euler step ("Diffusion with DDIM sampler == Flow matching sampler (Euler)"). The Diffusion lab saw the same identity from the other side: diffusers' Euler sampler and DDIM gave the same FD within 0.2 on its model.

The convention trap, at the pinned version. diffusers' flow scheduler counts the other way: σ = 1 is noise. FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler (diffusers v0.41.0, schedulers/scheduling_flow_match_euler_discrete.py) noises with sample = sigma * noise + (1.0 - sigma) * sample (line 234), so its model output is noise − image (the negative of this lab's v); it steps with dt = sigma_next - sigma (line 504, negative) and prev_sample = sample + dt * model_output (line 511), and the timestep it passes the network is sigmas * num_train_timesteps, σ × 1000 (line 367). Same maths, opposite sign and a factor of 1,000 on t: section 9 shows what each mix-up does.

Timestep shift. Higher resolutions need more noise to destroy the same structure, so SD3 shifts the schedule towards noise: tm = √(m/n) tn / (1 + (√(m/n) − 1) tn) on the noise level, with "a shift value of α = 3.0 both during training and sampling at resolution 1024×1024" (Esser et al. 2024, section 5.3.2, eq. 23, published); diffusers implements it as sigmas = self.shift * sigmas / (1 + (self.shift - 1) * sigmas) (line 351), and Z-Image-Turbo ships shift 3 (the {{Model map|#t-map}}). Which t to train on. Uniform t is the default here. SD3 compared training-time distributions over t and found logit-normal sampling (draw u from a normal with mean m and scale s, take the logistic of it) with m = 0, s = 1 "consistently achieves a good rank" across their settings (section 5.1.1 and Table 1, published): it spends more training on the middle of the path, where the per-t loss is neither trivially easy nor pure guesswork. Not run here: it needs a second 15-minute training to show a difference smaller than the noise of a 1,000-sample FD.

7. Guidance works the same way

Classifier-free guidance needs nothing new: the model was trained with 10% of labels dropped, so it gives a conditional velocity vc and an unconditional vu, and the sampler uses vu + s (vc − vu) (diffusers' convention: s = 1 is the plain conditional model, s = 0 unconditional; SD3's pipeline applies the same line to its flow transformer's output, pipelines/stable_diffusion_3/pipeline_stable_diffusion_3.py line 1083 at v0.41.0). Two evaluations per step, so a guided 50-step sample costs 100 evaluations.

Choosing, in one paragraph. For a new image or video generator trained from scratch, flow matching on straight paths is the default: one line of loss, no schedule to tune beyond a shift, good few-step behaviour, and it composes with every trick diffusion has (guidance, latent spaces, distillation, higher-order or multistep solvers). Diffusion with ε prediction remains the right reading for the large body of existing checkpoints and tools (SD 1.5, SDXL). Neither choice fixes cost by itself: steps do, and step counts of 1 to 4 come from distillation, not from the objective. Autoregressive generation is the other option (the {{Reading tab|#t-read}} compares it).
