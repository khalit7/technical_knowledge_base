3. Sampling is solving an ODE: Euler, Heun, midpoint

The problem. The network gives a velocity at any (x, t); turning that into a sample means integrating dx/dt = vθ(x, t) from t = 0 to 1. A computer does it in finite steps, and every step costs one or more network evaluations (NFE, the number of function evaluations, is the real cost: each is a full forward pass).

How it works. Euler: move along the current velocity for a step of size h; one evaluation per step; its error shrinks in proportion to h (first order). Heun: take the Euler step as a guess, evaluate the velocity there too, and step with the average of the two slopes. Midpoint: evaluate at the half step and use that slope. Both cost two evaluations a step and their error shrinks with h² (second order). Adaptive solvers such as dopri5 pick h themselves (Meta's library defaults to Euler and lists dopri5, midpoint and heun3 from torchdiffeq: flow_matching/solver/ode_solver.py lines 34 and 70). Why flow needs fewer steps here. Three reasons, measured or derived on this pair of models. (1) Path shape. Measured over 50 steps on 200 images, the flow model's paths have straightness  (distance over path length; 1 = straight) against  for DDIM on the diffusion model. Even with a perfect denoiser, the DDPM path xt = √ā x0 + √(1 − ā) ε traces an arc (the two coefficients stay on a unit circle), whose chord over length is about 2√2/π ≈ 0.90 for a quarter circle (derived); the straight path has no such built-in bend, and in 1,024 dimensions the crossing of random pairs bends it only a little. (2) Where the steps fall. DDIM on the linear DDPM schedule spends its steps evenly in timestep, but most of that schedule is nearly pure noise (signal below noise from t = 259 of 1,000, the {{Diffusion lab, section 2|#t-diff}}), so few steps leave few for the part that shapes the image; flow's uniform t spends half its steps where the image is more than half formed. (3) The prediction. Near pure noise, recovering x̂0 from a noise prediction divides by √āt (0.0064 at the last timestep), which amplifies errors, while a velocity stays bounded: one DDIM step lands  (RMS per pixel) from the 50-step answer, one flow Euler step . 5. Straighter paths: reflow and distillation


The problem. One-step generation needs paths so straight that a single Euler step follows them. Section 2 showed why plain flow matching does not give that: random pairs cross, and the averaged field bends.


How it works. Reflow (Liu et al. 2022): sample many noises z, solve the trained flow's ODE to get X1 = flow(z), and train again with the same loss on the fixed pairs (z, X1) instead of random pairs. Paths of an ODE never cross, so the new pairs never cross, and the new averaged field is (nearly) the straight line for each pair. Repeating it gives the 3-rectified flow and so on. Distillation then trains a student to jump from z to X1 in one evaluation. The pairs are fixed and deterministic, which is why the toy's reflow loss falls to  against  for random pairs (that floor is the variance of the random target).

Published, at scale. Liu et al.'s CIFAR-10 table (unconditional, Table 1, published): one Euler step gives FID 378 for the 1-rectified flow and 12.21 after one reflow (8.15 after two); adding distillation, 6.18 and 4.85. With the adaptive RK45 solver the order flips: 2.58 (127 evaluations) for the 1-rectified flow against 3.36 (110) after reflow. Reflow buys few-step quality and costs a little full-solve quality, because the pairs carry the first model's errors (on the toy:  on a mode at 64 steps before reflow,  after). InstaFlow (Liu et al. 2023) applies reflow then distillation to Stable Diffusion: one step, FID 23.3 on MS COCO 2017-5k, 13.1 on MS COCO 2014-30k in 0.09 s, for 199 A100 GPU-days of training (abstract, published). Minibatch OT pairing (Tong et al. 2023) straightens the paths during the first training instead (the toy's OT pairs model); in high dimensions a batch of a few hundred images is a weak approximation of the full transport plan, so it helps less there than on a 2-D toy.


When it matters. Reflow and distillation are what let flow models sample in 1 to 4 steps: FLUX.1 [schnell] is "a 12 billion parameter rectified flow transformer" that generates in "1 to 4 steps", distilled with latent adversarial diffusion distillation (model card, published). For a model you train yourself, reflow costs a pair-generation pass plus a second training; check it by FD at 1, 2 and 4 steps before and after, and at many steps to see what it cost.
