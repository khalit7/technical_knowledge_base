8. The Stable Diffusion autoencoder

What it is. The autoencoder of Stable Diffusion 1.5 (AutoencoderKL in diffusers, 83,653,863 parameters, counted from the loaded module) maps 512 × 512 × 3 to 64 × 64 × 4: a downsampling factor f = 8 and 4 channels (latent_channels 4 and block_out_channels [128, 256, 512, 512] in the pinned vae/config.json) published config. It is called a VAE, but barely: its KL weight in the original training config is 0.000001 ({{latent-diffusion configs/autoencoder/autoencoder_kl_32x32x4.yaml line 11|https://github.com/CompVis/latent-diffusion/blob/a506df575647/configs/autoencoder/autoencoder_kl_32x32x4.yaml#L11}}), a million times less than the beta 1 of section 4, so its codes are not pulled to N(0, I). What makes it sharp is the rest of the loss.

Its loss, LPIPSWithDiscriminator ({{ldm/modules/losses/contperceptual.py lines 48 to 83|https://github.com/CompVis/latent-diffusion/blob/a506df575647/ldm/modules/losses/contperceptual.py#L48}}): an L1 pixel loss plus a perceptual loss, LPIPS, the distance between the two images' features inside a pretrained network ({{Zhang et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.03924}}), plus a tiny KL, plus, from step 50,001 (disc_start), an adversarial loss from a patch discriminator, a small network trained to tell real image patches from reconstructed ones, weighted adaptively so its gradient matches the reconstruction gradient's size (calculate_adaptive_weight, lines 32 to 42). Pixel losses reward the average of the plausible images, which is blurry; the perceptual and adversarial terms reward texture that looks right even when it is not pixel-exact. The recipe is VQGAN's ({{Esser et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09841}}) with a KL in place of the codebook ({{LDM paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}, Stage 1).

The posterior's own σ is about 0.00005 to 0.001 against a latent std of 4 to 7: with a KL weight of 10−6 the "variational" part is negligible, and the encoder is in practice deterministic.

9. The scaling factor 0.18215

The problem. A diffusion model adds noise of standard deviation about 1 to its input, on a fixed schedule. The schedule assumes the input itself has a scale near 1; the SD autoencoder's raw latents do not, because its tiny KL weight never asked for it. The LDM authors divide the latent by its standard deviation measured on the first training batch (scale_by_std, {{ldm/models/diffusion/ddpm.py line 489|https://github.com/CompVis/latent-diffusion/blob/a506df575647/ldm/models/diffusion/ddpm.py#L489}}: 1. / z.flatten().std()), and SD 1.x froze the result as scale_factor: 0.18215. Without it, the LDM paper says, the SNR is "very high" and the model "allocates a lot of semantic detail early on" ({{LDM paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}, D.1).

Where it lives in code. The pinned SD 1.5 vae/config.json has no scaling_factor key; diffusers falls back to the class default (scaling_factor: float = 0.18215, {{autoencoder_kl.py line 90, v0.41.0|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/autoencoders/autoencoder_kl.py#L90}}). Pipelines multiply after encoding ({{img2img line 771|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion_img2img.py#L771}}) and divide before decoding (text-to-image line 1085). Other autoencoders have other factors: SDXL's 0.13025, and 16-channel ones with a shift as well (the {{Model map|#t-map}} lists them).

10. Common mistakes, checks and interview questions

Sampling a plain autoencoder from N(0, I)
Its codes live at whatever scale training chose (standard deviation 5 to 9.5 per dimension here), so N(0, I) samples sit in one corner and decode to one blob (section 3). Check: print the mean and std of the codes of real images before sampling; fit a density to them, or use a VAE.
Reading a lower reconstruction loss as a better generator
The plain autoencoder reconstructs best and generates worst from N(0, I). Reconstruction and generation are different measurements; score samples separately (FD with a stated sample size and floor).
Turning beta up to "fix" blurry samples
Higher beta makes reconstructions blurrier and switches dimensions off (section 6). Blur comes from the pixel loss; the cure is a perceptual or adversarial loss, or a stronger generator on top of the latent.
Ignoring codebook usage in a VQ model
A codebook of 128 with a fraction in use is a smaller codebook that cost the full size. Log codes used and perplexity during training (section 7); restart dead codes or initialise from encoder outputs.
Trusting a library's commitment loss without reading it
diffusers' VectorQuantizer applies β to the wrong term unless legacy=False (vae.py lines 574 to 576). Read the loss you train with.
Using the wrong scaling factor, or none
Each autoencoder has its own (SD 1.x 0.18215, SDXL 0.13025, some with a shift too). Missing at encode time, the U-Net sees inputs far cleaner than the timestep says (section 9); missing at decode time, colours wash out or blow up. Check: the std of scaled latents of real images should be near 1, and a round trip encode, scale, unscale, decode must reproduce the image.
Measuring an autoencoder only by PSNR
A blur with the same PSNR as the SD reconstruction looks far worse (section 8). Report a perceptual measure (LPIPS, or at least SSIM) and look at crops at full resolution, including text.
Running a VAE decoder in float16 without checking
SD 1.5's decoder is fine in float16 here (table in section 8), but some autoencoders overflow in half precision (the SDXL VAE is the known case, which is why a separate {{fp16-fixed SDXL VAE|https://huggingface.co/madebyollin/sdxl-vae-fp16-fix}} exists). Check for NaNs and compare with float32 once.

Interview questions

Why do latent diffusion models not diffuse in pixel space?
Cost and focus: a 512 × 512 image has 786,432 numbers against 16,384 in SD's latent (48 times fewer), and the denoiser runs 25 to 50 times per image; the autoencoder removes imperceptible detail once, so the generator spends its capacity on composition. The price is the decoder's ceiling and a second model to train.
What does the reparameterisation trick do, and why is it needed?
It writes the sample as z = μ + σε with ε drawn outside the network, so z is a differentiable function of the encoder's outputs and backpropagation reaches μ and σ. Sampling z directly from N(μ, σ2) has no gradient path.
Why are VAE samples blurry, and what fixes it?
A per-pixel loss is minimised by the average of the images consistent with the code, and averages are soft; the KL term adds noise to the code, which widens that set. Fixes: perceptual and adversarial losses (as in the SD autoencoder and VQGAN), or a powerful prior over the latent (diffusion, autoregressive).
What is codebook collapse and how do you prevent it?
Codes that are never nearest to any encoder output receive no gradient and stay unused, shrinking the vocabulary. Prevent with data-dependent initialisation, EMA codebook updates, dead-code restarts, or low-dimensional normalised codes; monitor codes used and perplexity.
What is the 0.18215 in Stable Diffusion, and what breaks without it?
One over the standard deviation of SD's raw latents, measured by the LDM authors on a training batch, so latents enter the diffusion process at unit scale. Without it the latents are about 5.5 times too large: at every timestep the signal-to-noise ratio is about 30 times higher than the schedule assumes, and decoding without dividing gives washed-out images.
Design: you need a tokenizer for an autoregressive image model. What do you choose and measure?
A VQ (or lookup-free) tokenizer with a 2-D grid of tokens; pick the downsampling factor (tokens per image sets sequence length and cost) and codebook size (bits per token), train with perceptual and adversarial losses, and measure reconstruction (PSNR, LPIPS, rFID), codebook usage and perplexity, and how well a small prior models the tokens. Trade-off: fewer tokens are cheaper to generate but reconstruct worse.
A colleague's img2img outputs come back smeared and low-contrast, or over-contrasted, with no error. First check?
The VAE scaling factor: encoded latents not multiplied by the model's factor (smeared, section 9) or multiplied by another model's (over-contrasted). Print the std of the latents fed to the U-Net; it should be near 1.
