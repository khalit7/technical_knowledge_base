4. Sampling: walking back from noise

The problem. The trained network only answers "given this noisy image at step t, what was the noise?". A sampler turns that answer into a path from pure noise to an image: a rule for where to go next and how many steps to take. Every sampler below uses the same trained weights; only the rule changes.

  • DDPM, ancestral ({{Ho et al. 2020|n:3c65c17b0d0d811481e4e36333454f7a}}): estimate the clean image x̂0, move to the average of where xt could have come from given x̂0 (the posterior mean), then add fresh noise. Built for the 1,000 small steps it was trained on; the fresh noise means the same start gives a different image each run.
  • DDIM ({{Song, Meng and Ermon 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02502}}): the same trained model, but no fresh noise: keep the predicted noise as the direction and jump straight to the previous noise level, xprev = √āprev x̂0 + √(1 − āprev) ε̂. Deterministic (same noise, same image) and valid for any number of steps, because it follows the probability-flow ODE that the {{math page, section 16|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}} derives.
  • DPM-Solver++ and Euler ({{Lu et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01095}}; {{Karras et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00364}}): treat that ODE as an ODE and integrate it with better numerical methods. DPM-Solver++ (2M, the diffusers default, {{line 227|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_dpmsolver_multistep.py#L227}}) reuses the previous step's prediction to take second-order steps at no extra network cost; Euler is the first-order method in the noise-level parametrisation.

DDIM, written out (src/diff/samplers.py, the code that made the animation below). Run on the trained model for 10 steps from the same 16 noise images, it matches diffusers' DDIMScheduler (trailing spacing) to (largest absolute difference in any pixel, float32 on MPS):

def ddim(net, abar, x, y, n, s=1.0, pred="eps", trace=None):
    ts = trailing(n)                                   # e.g. n = 4: 999, 749, 499, 249
    for i, t in enumerate(ts):
        ab = abar[t]
        ab_prev = abar[ts[i + 1]] if i + 1 < n else torch.ones_like(ab)
        eps, x0 = to_eps_x0(guided(net, x, t, y, s), x, ab, pred)
        x = ab_prev.sqrt() * x0 + (1 - ab_prev).sqrt() * eps       # jump to the previous noise level
    return x

diffusers v0.41.0 does the same in {{scheduling_ddim.py lines 456 and 491|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_ddim.py#L456}}; the DDPM step's posterior mean and variance are {{scheduling_ddpm.py lines 534 to 535 and 388|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/schedulers/scheduling_ddpm.py#L534}}, and the hand-written ddpm() beside ddim() follows them.

What to notice. Switch to the model's guess at 20 steps: over the first third of the steps x̂0 turns from amplified noise into a rough, noisy outline of the final garment, and the remaining steps clean it up and add edges and texture; the 20-step and 50-step results are almost the same image, because DDIM follows one deterministic path from a given noise. Coarse structure is decided at high noise, detail at low noise, which is why skipping steps late hurts less than skipping them early. (At the very first step this noise-predicting model's guess is still mostly amplified noise, for the reason section 3 gives; a v-model's first guess is the blurred average, section 5.) The DDPM mode starts from the same noise but ends somewhere else: its fresh noise at every step makes it a different sample.

5. Steps against quality against time

The cost. Every step is one network evaluation (two with guidance), so the step count is the price of an image. Here one evaluation of the tiny U-Net takes ms for one image and ms for a batch of 1,000 on the M1 Pro's GPU; 1,000 DDPM steps for 1,000 images took s, DDIM at 20 steps s.

Predict, then reveal. Same model, same noise, 10 steps each. Which scores better: DDPM's ancestral sampler or DDIM?
Here, DDPM: FD against for DDIM, class accuracy against measured. The textbook answer is DDIM: the DDIM paper reports its deterministic sampler far ahead at 10 to 100 steps on CIFAR-10, and that is why it exists. The difference is the model. Ours trained for minutes and its noise predictions are still wrong in ways that pile up along a deterministic path; DDPM's fresh noise at every step lets later steps correct earlier mistakes ({{Karras et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00364}}, section 4, make this argument and add a tuned amount of noise to their own sampler). DDIM stops improving at by 250 steps; DDPM keeps going to at 1,000. A sampler ranking measured on one model does not transfer to another.

6. Classifier-free guidance

The problem. A conditional model asked for "sandal" produces something sandal-like, but its samples drift: some are ambiguous, some look like another class. We want a knob that trades variety for adherence to the condition, without training a separate classifier ({{Ho and Salimans 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12598}}).

How it works. Train one network both with and without the label (the 10% dropout in section 3). At sampling, evaluate it both ways and extrapolate from the unconditional prediction past the conditional one:

ε̂ = εu + s · (εc − εu)
εc, εu
the prediction with the label (or prompt) and with the null label (or empty prompt)
s
the guidance scale: 0 is the unconditional model, 1 the plain conditional model, above 1 pushes further in the direction the condition points

This is diffusers' convention, {{pipeline_stable_diffusion.py line 1055|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L1055}}. Two traps: the pipeline only runs the unconditional branch when the scale is above 1 ({{line 764|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion/pipeline_stable_diffusion.py#L764}}), so guidance_scale=0 there is the plain conditional model, not the unconditional one; and papers often write the same formula as εc + w (εc − εu), where w = s − 1 (the shared objectives.py does). The cost: two evaluations per step, run as one batch of two (line 1037). The derivation, and a toy showing the lost diversity, are on the {{math page, section 17|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}}.
