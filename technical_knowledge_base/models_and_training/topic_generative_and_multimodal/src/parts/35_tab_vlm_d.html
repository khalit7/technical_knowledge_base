8. Mistakes that ran here, and two design questions

Assuming a small image costs few tokens
SmolVLM upscales to its tile grid: a 64-pixel icon costs the same 1,088 image tokens as a 3,000-pixel photo (section 3). Set size={"longest_edge": N*512} or turn splitting off for small or simple images; measure with the processor, not from the file size.
Writing the prompt by hand
The placeholders, tile tags and wrappers must match what the model was trained on and the number of image vectors exactly. Use processor.apply_chat_template and then the processor on text and image together; never paste <image> yourself.
Raising resolution past what the model was trained for
More tiles are not always better: past 6 × 6 SmolVLM’s tile tags stop being single tokens (section 3), and any VLM degrades on token counts and layouts it never saw in training. Check the model card’s recommended settings and measure on your own questions.
Judging a VLM by its captions
Fluent descriptions hide errors: section 6’s long descriptions add details that are not there. Score with questions that have known answers, including absent objects (POPE-style) and traps, and report the yes rate on absent objects, not only accuracy.
Treating image tokens like words
Projected image vectors are far from every word embedding (section 4: norms about 30 times larger, nearest vocabulary entries meaningless). Tricks designed for text tokens (pruning by similarity to the vocabulary, reusing text-only quantisation statistics) need checking on image tokens separately.
Forgetting the aspect ratio
Processors that resize to whole tiles or to a square change the shape of the image (a 16:9 photo stretched by a third here; squashed to a square with splitting off). Thin text and circles suffer first. Pad to a tile-friendly shape yourself if geometry matters.

Two design questions

Your VLM cannot read small text in screenshots. What do you try?
More visual tokens where the text is: higher resolution or tiling (more tiles), a smaller merge factor, or cropping to the region; check the processor is not downscaling first; check the aspect ratio is preserved. Then data: OCR-heavy instruction data. Measure the token and latency cost of each.
Design: a VLM that answers questions about phone screenshots in under one second on a laptop
Small language model (0.5–2B), SigLIP-class encoder, tiles sized to the screenshot’s aspect ratio, aggressive token merging (pixel shuffle) to keep the prompt near 1,000 tokens, prefix caching of the system prompt, short answers. Here SmolVLM-500M answered a short question about one image in about 2.1 s, 1.6 s of it in the vision encoder on an M1 Pro (section 7), so the budget is the image tokens: count them, then trade tiles for latency on a held-out question set.
