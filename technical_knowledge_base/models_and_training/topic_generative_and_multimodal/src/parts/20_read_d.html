9. Evaluation: five questions, five kinds of number

No single number judges a generator. Each family of metrics answers one question and has one trap you must check before trusting it:

QuestionMetricsThe trapOwner
Do the samples look like the data, and cover it?FID, KID, precision and recall, FD-DINOv2, CMMDFID falls with the sample count even for real against real; resizing, compression and the reference set move itEvaluating, sections 2 to 5
Did it draw what the prompt asked?CLIPScore; question-based checks (TIFA, DSG, VQAScore); GenEvalCLIP cannot count or check relations, so a wrong image can score as close to its promptEvaluating, section 6
Do people prefer it?Pairwise arenas, reward models (PickScore, HPS, ImageReward)Style, self-selected prompts, vendors testing private variantsEvaluating, section 7; {{Human preference and arenas|n:3ef5c17b0d0d814db49ff3b5c9fc3034}}
Does a VLM see the image?Benchmarks with answer keys (MMMU, ChartQA, DocVQA), POPE for absent objectsMany questions are answerable without the image: report a text-only baselineEvaluating, section 8; {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}
Video and audio?FVD, FAD and per-dimension suitesThe same recipe with other feature extractors, and their blind spotsEvaluating, section 9

FID ({{Heusel et al. 2017|https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08500}}) passes real and generated images through Inception-v3, fits a Gaussian to each set of 2,048-number features and reports the distance between the Gaussians; lower is better, and it punishes both bad images and too little variety. The trap in the first row is easy to see on the running example: real training images against the real test images, nothing wrong, still score FID 2.538 at 10,000 images and 44.9 at 250 measured, so compare only at equal counts. The kind of failure a global similarity misses: Stable Diffusion v1.5 asked for “an astronaut riding a horse on the moon” drew the astronaut on the moon and no horse measured (setup smoke test, seed 5).
