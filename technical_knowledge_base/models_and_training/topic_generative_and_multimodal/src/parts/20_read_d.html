5. Vision encoders: how a model sees

Images as patches. A transformer reads a sequence, so the Vision Transformer (ViT, {{Dosovitskiy et al., Oct 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929}}) cuts the image into a grid of square patches, flattens each (a 16 × 16 RGB patch is 768 numbers), multiplies it by one learned matrix (the patch embedding) and adds a learned position embedding saying where the patch came from. A 224 × 224 image in patches of 16 is 14 × 14 = 196 tokens derived. The output is either an extra summary token prepended to the sequence ([CLS]) or an average over all patch outputs. A convolutional network builds in the assumptions that nearby pixels matter together and that a pattern means the same thing anywhere (its inductive bias); a ViT has to learn them from data, and beat the best CNNs of 2020 only after pre-training on 300M images ({{ViT page|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa231cfcde6c1954d}}).

Learning without labels. Self-supervised methods train on images alone: MAE hides most patches ("e.g., 75%") and fills them in ({{He et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377}}); DINO-style methods match a slowly updated copy of the network across crops ({{DINOv2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193}}); both are on {{Contrastive and self-supervised learning|n:3c65c17b0d0d811db6a7d02d0ee7215c}}.

Learning from captions: CLIP. CLIP ({{Radford et al., Feb 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020}}) trains an image encoder and a text encoder together on 400M web image-caption pairs. In a batch of N pairs it computes all N × N image-text similarities and applies a softmax cross-entropy along each row and column so that each image picks out its own caption and vice versa (the contrastive or InfoNCE loss), with a learned temperature that sharpens the softmax. The payoff is zero-shot classification: embed "a photo of a dog", "a photo of a cat" and so on, and pick the class whose text is nearest the image, with no task-specific training; CLIP's best model reached 76.2% on ImageNet that way ({{CLIP page|n:3c65c17b0d0d8194a85dfc5f3bf3f799}}). Its text tower became Stable Diffusion's text encoder (section 4) and its image tower the eyes of the first VLMs (section 6).

SigLIP. The softmax needs every similarity in the batch, which makes huge batches expensive to shard. SigLIP ({{Zhai et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15343}}) scores each pair independently with a sigmoid, which "does not require a global view of the pairwise similarities for normalization", works better at small batches and scales to larger ones (84.5% zero-shot ImageNet with locked image features, four TPUv4 chips, two days vendor). SigLIP-family towers sit in many open VLMs, SmolVLM's included.

What the embeddings miss. A contrastive embedding keeps what helps match captions and drops much else. CLIP reads text in images so well that a handwritten label can override the object: OpenAI called these typographic attacks, noting they need "no more technology than pen and paper" ({{OpenAI, March 2021, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/2021/https://openai.com/blog/multimodal-neurons/}}). Such models also handle word order, counting and spatial relations poorly, behaving partly like bags of words ({{Yuksekgonul et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01936}}). 6. Vision-language models: teaching a language model to read images

The recipe. A VLM bolts a vision encoder onto a pretrained language model. In LLaVA ({{Liu et al., Apr 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485}}), the pattern most open models follow, the encoder turns the image into patch vectors, a small projector (one linear layer; a two-layer MLP in LLaVA-1.5) maps each into the language model's embedding space, and these image tokens replace an image placeholder in the prompt, attended to like words. The alternative is cross-attention: Flamingo kept a pretrained vision model and a 70B language model frozen and trained new layers between them, "an 80B parameter VLM" ({{DeepMind, April 2022|https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/tackling-multiple-tasks-with-a-single-visual-language-model/}}), whose open reproduction is named for "Interleaved Cross-attention" ({{IDEFICS|https://huggingface.co/blog/idefics}}). Cross-attention keeps images out of the context window; tokens are simpler, and most open VLMs since 2023 use them.

How many tokens is an image? It is the patch count after any merging: LLaVA-1.5's CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 pixels gives (336 / 14)2 = 576 tokens per image derived, as many as a page of text. Higher resolution helps reading small text but costs tokens quadratically, so models tile a large image into crops plus a thumbnail, process images at their own resolution (Qwen2-VL's "Naive Dynamic Resolution", {{Wang et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12191}}), or merge neighbouring patch vectors before the projector (pixel shuffle, trading spatial positions for channels). Training in stages. LLaVA first trains only the projector on 595K image-caption pairs, encoder and language model frozen, so image vectors land where the language model can read them; then projector and language model together on instruction conversations that a text-only GPT-4 wrote from captions and object boxes ({{LLaVA, section 4.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2304.08485v2}}). LLaVA-1.5 used "merely 1.2M publicly available data" and "~1 day on a single 8-A100 node" ({{Liu et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744}}). Later recipes change data, resolution and what is frozen, but keep align-then-instruct.

Failure modes. The best known is object hallucination: naming things that are not in the image, especially objects that usually co-occur with what is there. POPE measures it with yes/no questions about present and absent objects ({{Li et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10355}}). Counting, small text and left-right relations are the other usual errors. 7. Native multimodal models: one transformer for both

A VLM reads images but writes only text. A native (or unified) multimodal model reads and writes both with one network. Three designs are published:

  • Everything is a token (early fusion). Chameleon ({{Chameleon Team, May 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09818}}) turns each 512 × 512 image into 1,024 tokens from a codebook of 8,192 and trains one 7B or 34B transformer on interleaved text and image tokens; generating an image is next-token prediction. Simple, but slow (section 1's drill) and capped by the tokenizer's detail.
  • Two encodings, one transformer. Janus argues that understanding and generation want different image representations and "decouple[s] visual encoding into separate pathways" while keeping "a single, unified transformer" ({{Wu et al., Oct 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13848}}).
  • Two objectives, one transformer. Transfusion "combines the language modeling loss function (next token prediction) with diffusion" in one model, up to 7B parameters on 2T tokens, with images as continuous latent patches rather than discrete tokens ({{Zhou et al., Aug 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11039}}).

The closed frontier publishes little. OpenAI's system card for GPT-4o image generation says only: "Unlike DALL·E, which operates as a diffusion model, 4o image generation is an autoregressive model natively embedded within ChatGPT" ({{OpenAI, 25 March 2025, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/20250325201732/https://cdn.openai.com/11998be9-5319-4302-bfbf-1167e093f1fb/Native_Image_Generation_System_Card.pdf}}) vendor; tokenizer, decoder and sizes are not disclosed. The traffic also runs the other way: diffusion is now used to generate text, as on {{DiffusionGemma Technical Report|n:3c65c17b0d0d818390bfd7969757211e}}.

8. Video and audio, in brief

Video is latent diffusion or flow with time added: the autoencoder compresses space and time, and a transformer denoises spacetime patches. Sora is "trained on and subsequently generates videos within this compressed latent space" ({{OpenAI, Feb 2024, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/}}) vendor; Movie Gen is a 30B-parameter transformer trained with flow matching ({{Polyak et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13720}}). Every frame adds patches and attention grows with the square of the total, so video compresses time too and costs far more than an image. The hard failures are temporal: objects changing identity or vanishing between frames, physics that does not hold.

Audio follows two routes. A neural audio codec turns the waveform into parallel streams of discrete tokens that a language model predicts: AudioLM "maps the input audio to a sequence of discrete tokens" ({{Borsos et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03143}}), MusicGen uses one "single-stage transformer LM together with efficient token interleaving patterns" ({{Copet et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05284}}). Or diffusion generates a continuous audio latent, as in Stable Audio ({{Evans et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04825}}).
