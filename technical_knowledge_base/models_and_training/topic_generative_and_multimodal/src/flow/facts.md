# Flow lab: verified facts (checked 2026-10-17)

Published (read from the source named; quotes verbatim):
- Lipman, Chen, Ben-Hamu, Nickel, Le, "Flow Matching for Generative Modeling", arXiv 2210.02747, v1 6 Oct 2022, v2 8 Feb 2023. Abstract: "a simulation-free approach for training CNFs based on regressing vector fields of fixed conditional probability paths"; "using Optimal Transport (OT) displacement interpolation to define the conditional probability paths"; "subsumes existing diffusion paths as specific instances".
- Liu, Gong, Liu, "Flow Straight and Fast: Learning to Generate and Transfer Data with Rectified Flow", arXiv 2209.03003, v1 7 Sep 2022. Abstract: "recursively applying rectification allows us to obtain a sequence of flows with increasingly straight paths"; "nearly straight flows that give high quality results even with a single Euler discretization step". Table 1 (CIFAR-10 unconditional, arXiv HTML): one Euler step FID 1-RF 378, +distill 6.18; 2-RF 12.21, +distill 4.85; 3-RF 8.15, +distill 5.21. RK45: 1-RF 2.58 (NFE 127), 2-RF 3.36 (110), 3-RF 3.96 (104). (The introduction quotes recall 0.51 for the 4.85 result; recall is not used on the page.)
- Albergo, Vanden-Eijnden, "Building Normalizing Flows with Stochastic Interpolants", arXiv 2209.15571, v1 30 Sep 2022.
- Tong et al. (8 authors), "Improving and generalizing flow-based generative models with minibatch optimal transport", arXiv 2302.00482, v1 1 Feb 2023: OT-CFM "creates simpler flows that are more stable to train and lead to faster inference".
- Esser et al., "Scaling Rectified Flow Transformers for High-Resolution Image Synthesis" (SD3), arXiv 2403.03206, 5 Mar 2024. Abstract: rectified flow "connects data and noise in a straight line"; noise sampling "biasing them towards perceptually relevant scales"; transformer that "uses separate weights for the two modalities". Section 3.1 eq. 19 logit-normal; section 5.1.1 / Table 1 rf/lognorm(0.00, 1.00) "consistently achieves a good rank"; section 5.3.2 eq. 23 shift t_m = sqrt(m/n) t_n / (1 + (sqrt(m/n) - 1) t_n), "we use a shift value of alpha = 3.0 both during training and sampling at resolution 1024x1024".
- Liu, Zhang, Ma, Peng, Liu, "InstaFlow", arXiv 2309.06380, v1 12 Sep 2023: one step, FID 23.3 MS COCO 2017-5k (22.4 with a 1.7B network), 13.1 MS COCO 2014-30k in 0.09 s, 199 A100 GPU days.
- Song et al., "Score-Based Generative Modeling through Stochastic Differential Equations", arXiv 2011.13456, v1 26 Nov 2020: "an equivalent neural ODE that samples from the same distribution as the SDE".
- Lipman et al. (10 authors), "Flow Matching Guide and Code", arXiv 2412.06264, v1 9 Dec 2024, with "a PyTorch package".
- Blog "Diffusion Meets Flow Matching: Two Sides of the Same Coin", https://diffusionflow.github.io/ (authors and date not shown; acknowledgements thank "our colleagues at Google DeepMind"): "diffusion models and Gaussian flow matching are the same"; "Diffusion with DDIM sampler == Flow matching sampler (Euler)".
- FLUX.1, Sana, Qwen-Image, Z-Image-Turbo, Wan, FLUX 3 rows: from the shared FACTS (map and rd keys), not re-checked here.
- Karras, Aittala, Aila, Laine, "Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models" (EDM), arXiv 2206.00364, v1 1 Jun 2022. Its Heun sampler's last step is first order; verified in code rather than the paper (the arXiv HTML was not available): diffusers v0.41.0 schedulers/scheduling_heun_discrete.py line 364 `self.sigmas = torch.cat([sigmas[:1], sigmas[1:-1].repeat_interleave(2), sigmas[-1:]])` and line 367 `timesteps = torch.cat([timesteps[:1], timesteps[1:].repeat_interleave(2)])`: n steps cost 2n - 1 evaluations, and the final interval (to sigma = 0) has no corrector.
