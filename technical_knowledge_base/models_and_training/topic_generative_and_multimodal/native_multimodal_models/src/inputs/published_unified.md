## 1. Chameleon (Meta FAIR, arXiv 2405.09818v2)

- Early-fusion definition: "This early-fusion approach, where all modalities are projected into a shared representational space from the start, allows for seamless reasoning and generation across modalities. However, it also presents significant technical challenges, particularly in terms of optimization stability and scaling." (v2, Section 1 Introduction)
- Abstract framing: "We present Chameleon, a family of early-fusion token-based mixed-modal models capable of understanding and generating images and text in any arbitrary sequence." (v2, Abstract)
- Contrast with late fusion: "As a fully token-based early-fusion model, Chameleon differs from late-fusion approaches like Flamingo (Alayrac et al., 2022) which encode images and text separately before combining them at a later stage. Other models like LLaVA (Liu et al., 2023a), IDEFICS (Laurençon et al., 2023), and VisualGPT (Chen et al., 2022) also maintain separate image and text encoders." (v2, Section 6 Related Work)
- Gemini contrast: "The most similar model to Chameleon is Gemini (Gemini et al., 2023), which also uses an early-fusion token-based approach. However, a key difference is that Gemini uses separate image decoders, whereas Chameleon is an end-to-end dense model without any routing components." (v2, Section 6)
- Image tokenizer, CONFIRMED 512x512 -> 1024 tokens, codebook 8192: "We train a new image tokenizer based on Gafni et al. (2022), which encodes a 512 x 512 image into 1024 discrete tokens from a codebook of size 8192. For training this tokenizer, we use only licensed images." (v2, Section 2.1 Tokenization)
- Tokenizer weakness: "A core weakness of our tokenizer is in reconstructing images with a large amount of text, therefore upper bounding the capability of our models, when it comes to heavy OCR-related tasks." (v2, Section 2.1)
- Vocabulary: "We train a new BPE tokenizer (Sennrich et al., 2016) over a subset of the training data outlined below with a vocabulary size of 65,536, which includes the 8192 image codebook tokens, using the sentencepiece library" (v2, Section 2.1)
- Training tokens, three different numbers in the paper (flag):
  - Figure 1 caption: "trained from scratch in an end-to-end fashion on ~10T tokens of interleaved mixed-modal data." (v2, Figure 1)
  - Table 1 lists Tokens = 4.4T for both Chameleon 7B and 34B (v2, Table 1, "Summary of core architecture and optimization decisions"), with Epochs 2.1.
  - Section 2.3: "We do 2.1 epochs over our full training dataset for a total of 9.2 trillion tokens seen during training." (v2, Section 2.3, Optimization)
  - Reading: 4.4T is the unique dataset, 2.1 epochs gives 9.2T seen, Figure 1 rounds to ~10T. Pre-training data first stage: "2.9 trillion text-only tokens", "1.4 billion text-image pairs, which produces 1.5 trillion text-image tokens", "400 billion tokens of interleaved text and image data" (v2, Section 2.2.1). 2.9 + 1.5 + 0.4 = 4.8T, which does not exactly equal Table 1's 4.4T (the second stage reweights data); not reconciled in the paper.
  - Intro: "we successfully train Chameleon-34B on 5x the number of tokens as Llama-2" (v2, Section 1).
- Where instability starts: "It was challenging to maintain stable training when scaling the Chameleon models above 8B parameters and 1T tokens, with instabilities often only arising very late in training." (v2, Section 2.3 Stability)
- Root cause and the "competition" / logit drift explanation: "We narrowed down the cause of the divergence to the softmax operation being problematic when training with multiple modalities of significantly varying entropy due to the translation invariant property of softmax (i.e., softmax(z)=softmax(z+c)). Because we share all weights of the model across modalities, each modality will try to "compete" with the other by increasing its norms slightly; while not problematic at the beginning of training, it manifests in divergences once we get outside the effective representation range of bf16 (In Figure 12, we show that ablations without image generation did not diverge). In a unimodal setting, this problem has also been named the logit drift problem (Wortsman et al., 2023)." (v2, Section 2.3)
- Image generation is the trigger: "Figure 10: Training loss curve with image generation disabled does not suffer from instability issues." (v2, Figure 10 caption)
- Monitoring signal: "although training divergences can manifest after as much as even 20-30% of training progress, monitoring uncontrolled growth of output norms is strongly correlated with predicting future loss divergence." (v2, Section 2.3)
- QK-Norm (needed by both sizes): "we first deviate from the Llama architecture by using query-key normalization (QK-Norm). QK-Norm directly controls the norm growth of input to the softmax by applying layer norm to the query and key vectors within the attention. In Figure 8, we show training loss curves for Chameleon-7B with and without QK-Norm, and the latter diverges after approximately 20% of a training epoch." (v2, Section 2.3)
- Dropout (7B) and norm reordering (34B): "We found that to stabilize Chameleon-7B by controlling norm growth, it was necessary to introduce dropout after the attention and feed-forward layers, in addition to QK-norm (see Figure 8). However, this recipe was not enough to stabilitize, Chameleon-34B, which required an additional re-ordering of the norms. Specifically, we use the strategy of normalization proposed in Liu et al. (2021); Liu et al. (2022), within the transformer block. The benefit of the Swin transformer normalization strategy is that it bounds the norm growth of the feedforward block, which can become additionally problematic given the multiplicate nature of the SwiGLU activation function." (v2, Section 2.3; "stabilitize" is the paper's typo)
- 34B without dropout; 7B retroactively: "we found that this type of normalization did not work well in combination with dropout and therefore, we train Chameleon-34B without dropout (Figure 12). Furthermore, we retroactively found that Chameleon-7B can also be stably trained without dropout, when using norm-reordering, but QK-norm is essential in both cases." (v2, Section 2.3)
- Dropout value: "We use a dropout of 0.1 (Srivastava et al., 2014) for Chameleon-7B for training stability, but not for Chameleon-34B (see Figure 8 and 12)." (v2, Section 2.3 Optimization)
- z-loss: "The application of QK-Norm while helping the inner softmaxes within the Transformer does not solve the problem of logit shift in the final softmax. Following Chowdhery et al. (2022); Wortsman et al. (2023), we apply z-loss regularization. Specifically, we regularize the partition function Z of the softmax function ... by adding 10^-5 log^2 Z to our loss function." (v2, Section 2.3)
- Which size needed which: "For Chameleon-7B it was important to use both dropout and z-loss to achieve stability, while Chameleon-34B only required z-loss (Figure 12)." (v2, Section 2.3)
- Summary table per size (my reading, all from the quotes above): 7B = QK-Norm + dropout 0.1 + z-loss (norm reordering optional, found later); 34B = QK-Norm + Swin-style norm reordering + z-loss, no dropout. Table 1 also marks the 34B with GQA (check mark) and the 7B without.
- Image generation evaluation: Chameleon reports NO FID or GenEval number for its own image generation; the evaluation is human preference on mixed-modal long-form responses. "Chameleon-34B substantially outperforms strong baselines like Gemini-Pro and GPT-4V (Section 4), achieving a 60.4% preference rate against Gemini-Pro and a 51.6% preference rate against GPT-4V in pairwise comparisons." (v2, Section 1)
  - Important caveat: the 60.4% / 51.6% are win rates against augmented baselines Gemini+ and GPT-4V+ (text-only API responses plus DALL-E 3 images generated from captions the models wrote): "Overall, Chameleon has win rates of 60.4% and 51.6% over Gemini+ and GPT-4V+, respectively. ... Chameleon's win rates over Gemini and GPT-4V are 69.1% and 61.7%, respectively." and "The win rate is calculated by adding 1 point for a win and 0.5 points for a tie." (v2, Section 4.3 and footnote). The intro sentence calls them "Gemini-Pro and GPT-4V", the body says Gemini+ / GPT-4V+: a text/table wording mismatch.
  - Raw split: "Compared with Gemini+, Chameleon's responses are better in 41.5% of the cases, 34.5% are tie, and 24.0% are inferior. ... than GPT-4V+, with 35.8% win, 31.6% tie, and 32.6% loss." (v2, Section 4.3)
  - Task fulfilment: "55.2% for Chameleon vs. 37.6% of Gemini+ and 44.7% of GPT-4V+." (v2, Section 4.3)
  - Raters are a third-party crowdsourcing vendor hired by the authors: the eval is the authors' own design, not an independent benchmark. The authors' own caveat: "readers should be aware of the limitations of human evaluation. First, the prompts used in the evaluation came from crowdsourcing instead of real users" (v2, Section 4.5).
  - Third-party GenEval for Chameleon 7B: 0.39, reported in Transfusion Table 9 and copied by Show-o, Janus-Pro and BAGEL tables; also COCO FID 26.74 in Transfusion Table 9 (Transfusion authors' number, i.e. Meta again, not independent of Meta).
- Release licence: Hugging Face card facebook/chameleon-7b (commit 55361b71ee529c4851ef6dbeffc58e9086a78ea7, gated "manual") metadata: license "other", license_name "chameleon-research-license", gate prompt "META CHAMELEON RESEARCH LICENSE AGREEMENT" (https://huggingface.co/api/models/facebook/chameleon-7b). GitHub README: "Use of this repository and related resources are governed by the [Chameleon Research License]" (https://github.com/facebookresearch/chameleon, README "License").
- Image generation not released: "Today, we're publicly releasing key components of our Chameleon 7B and 34B models under a research-only license. The models we're releasing today were safety tuned and support mixed-modal inputs and text-only output to be used for research purposes. ... At this time, we are not releasing the Chameleon image generation model." (Meta blog, 18 Jun 2024, https://ai.meta.com/blog/meta-fair-research-new-releases/, section "Meta Chameleon")

## 2. Transfusion (Meta, arXiv 2408.11039v1)

- One-line recipe: "Transfusion combines the language modeling loss function (next token prediction) with diffusion to train a single transformer over mixed-modality sequences." (v1, Abstract)
- Setup: "We demonstrate Transfusion by pretraining a transformer model on 50% text and 50% image data using a different objective for each modality: next token prediction for text and diffusion for images. The model is exposed to both modalities and loss functions at each training step." (v1, Section 1)
- Combined loss: "We combine the two losses by simply adding the losses computed over each modality with a balancing coefficient lambda: L_Transfusion = L_LM + lambda * L_DDPM (4)" (v1, Section 3, Training Objective). "LM loss is computed per token, while diffusion loss is computed per image, which may span multiple elements (image patches) in the sequence." (same)
- lambda value: "We set the lambda coefficient in the Transfusion objective (Equation 4) to 5 following preliminary experiments; we leave further tuning of lambda to future work." (v1, Section 4.1, Optimization). CONFIRMED lambda = 5.
- Attention mask: "Transfusion combines both attention patterns by applying causal attention to every element in the sequence, and bidirectional attention within the elements of each individual image. This allows every image patch to attend to every other patch within the same image, but only attend to text or patches of other images that appeared previously in the sequence." (v1, Section 3, Transfusion Attention). Figure 4 caption: "Expanding on the causal mask, Transfusion allows patches of the same image to condition on each other."
- Mask ablation: "we notice a significant improvement in FID when using linear encoding layers (61.3 -> 20.3)" when intra-image bidirectional attention is enabled (v1, Section 4.3.1, Table 5, 0.76B models, patch 2x2).
- How images enter: "Each image is encoded as latent patches using a VAE (see 2.3), where each patch is represented as a continuous vector; the patches are sequenced left-to-right top-to-bottom" (v1, Section 3). Two patch encoders: "(1) a simple linear layer, ... and (2) up and down blocks of a U-Net" (v1, Section 3, Model Architecture)
- VAE: "We train a 86M parameter VAE following Esser et al. (2021). We use a CNN encoder and decoder, and latent dimension 8. ... Our implementation reduces an image of 256 x 256 pixels to a 32 x 32 x 8 tensor, where each latent 8-dimensional latent pixel represents (conceptually) an 8 x 8 pixel patch in the original image" (v1, Section 4.1)
- Tokens per 256x256 image: "Depending on the compression rate of the patch encoder (see Model Architecture in 3), each image will be represented by either 1024, 256, 64, or 16 elements in the sequence." (v1, Section 4.1, footnote 11). The 7B / 2T model uses "U-Net encoding/decoding layers (2 x 2 latent pixel patches)" (v1, Section 4.4), i.e. 32x32 latent / 2x2 = 256 patches per image, matching "about 3.5B caption-image pairs at 256 patches per image" (v1, Section 4.1).
- Serving-cost claim: "adding U-Net down and up blocks to encode and decode images enables Transfusion to compress larger image patches with relatively small loss to performance, potentially decreasing the serving costs by up to 64x." (v1, Section 1)
- Headline comparison with Chameleon (controlled, same data and compute, Chameleon re-trained by the Transfusion authors, not Meta's released Chameleon):
  - Intro: "In text-to-image generation, we find that Transfusion exceeds the Chameleon approach at less than a third of the compute, as measured by both FID and CLIP scores. When controlling for FLOPs, Transfusion achieves approximately 2x lower FID scores than Chameleon models. We observe a similar trend in image-to-text generation, where Transfusion matches Chameleon at 21.8% of the FLOPs. Surprisingly, Transfusion is also more efficient at learning text-to-text prediction, achieving perplexity parity on text tasks around 50% to 60% of Chameleon's FLOPs." (v1, Section 1)
  - Section 4.2: "The difference in compute efficiency is particularly striking in image generation, where FID Transfusion achieves parity with Chameleon using 34x less compute." (v1, Section 4.2)
  - Table 3 (7B each, both trained on 0.5T tokens): Transfusion C4 PPL 7.72, Wiki PPL 4.28, Llama acc 61.5, COCO CIDEr 27.2, FID 16.8, CLIP 25.5; Chameleon 8.41, 4.69, 59.1, 18.0, 29.6, 24.3; Parity FLOP ratio 0.489, 0.526, 0.600, 0.218, 0.029, 0.319. Caption: "Parity FLOP Ratio is the relative amount of Transfusion FLOPs needed to match the results of Chameleon 7B." (v1, Table 3)
  - FLAG text/table: "less than a third of the compute" (intro) is the CLIP ratio 0.319 (the binding one of the two metrics); the FID-only ratio is 0.029, i.e. "34x less compute". Both are consistent with Table 3 but the intro and Section 4.2 quote different headline numbers.
  - Their explanation of the text gain: "One hypothesis is that this stems from the competition between text and image tokens in the output distribution; alternatively, it is possible that diffusion is more efficient at image generation and requires fewer parameters" (v1, Section 4.2)
- 7B / 2T large model: "We train from scratch a 7B transformer enhanced with U-Net down/up layers (0.27B parameters) over 2T tokens: 1T text tokens, and approximately 5 epochs of 692M images and their captions, amounting to another 1T patches/tokens." (v1, Section 1)
- 7B / 2T results, Table 9: "Transfusion (Ours) | 7.3B | 1.0T text tokens | 3.5B images | Llama Acc 66.1 | COCO FID 6.78 | GenEval 0.63" (v1, Table 9). Same table, copied/reported literature rows: Chameleon 7B FID 26.74, GenEval 0.39; DALL-E 2 FID 10.39, GenEval 0.52; SDXL GenEval 0.55; DeepFloyd FID 6.66, GenEval 0.61; SD 3 GenEval 0.68 (footnote: "SD 3 trains with synthetic caption data, which provides boosts GenEval performance"); Imagen FID 7.27; Parti FID 7.23 (reranked). Table 9 caption: "compared to similar scale models in the literature." These comparison rows are third-party numbers the authors collected ("We compare the performance of our model to reported results of other similar scale image generation models"), not re-run.
- CLIP score for the 7B/2T model: UNCONFIRMED (not reported in Table 9; CLIP 25.5 is the controlled 0.5T-token 7B in Table 3).
- Inference: "For image generation, we follow the standard of 250 diffusion steps (the model is trained on 1,000 timesteps)." (v1, Section 4.1)

## 3. Show-o (Show Lab NUS + ByteDance, arXiv 2408.12528v7)

- Opening: "We present a unified transformer, i.e., Show-o, that unifies multimodal understanding and generation." (v7, Abstract)
- Omni-attention: "we propose an omni-attention mechanism to enable Show-o to model various types of signals in distinct ways. It is a comprehensive attention mechanism with causal and full attention that adaptively mixes and changes according to the format of the input sequence. ... Specifically, Show-o model text tokens v within the sequence via causal attention. For image tokens u, Show-o processes them via full attention, allowing each token to comprehensively interact with all others. ... When given only text tokens, it degrades to causal attention (Fig. 5(c))." (v7, Section 3.2, Omni-Attention Mechanism)
- Tokenizer, CONFIRMED: "Following MAGVIT-v2 (Yu et al., 2023), we train a lookup-free quantizer using a large-scale image data. The quantizer maintains a codebook of size K=8,192 and encodes images of 256 x 256 resolution into 16 x 16 discrete tokens" (v7, Section 3.1, Image Tokenization). So 256 tokens per 256x256 image. For the 512x512 variant, 32x32 = 1024 tokens is my arithmetic (same 16x downsampling), not a quoted number.
- Embedding expansion: "We initialize Show-o with the weights of a pre-trained LLM and expand the size of the embedding layer by incorporating 8,192 new learnable embeddings for discrete image tokens." (v7, Section 3.2). Also adds QK-Norm to each attention layer (same paragraph).
- Discrete denoising: "we employ a simplified discrete denoising diffusion, similar to MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022), to model discrete image tokens instead of continuous diffusion used in existing works" (v7, Section 1) and "this work employs mask token prediction used in MaskGIT as a simplified discrete diffusion modeling to enable a more unified learning objective, i.e., predicting discrete tokens within one single transformer." (v7, Section 3, Preliminaries)
- Inference: "we use all [MASK] tokens as initial input for Show-o, in which [MASK] tokens will be iteratively replaced by the predicted image tokens within T steps." (v7, Section 3.3)
- Number of sampling steps: the paper gives no single T in the text it states; it says: "In contrast to autoregressively generating an image, Show-o requires approximately 20 times fewer sampling steps" (v7, Section 1). The official repo README (github.com/showlab/Show-o, main at commit 45a5a2de) runs 512x512 text-to-image with "guidance_scale=5 generation_timesteps=50" and 256x256 inpainting/extrapolation with "generation_timesteps=16". So 50 steps (512 demo) / 16 steps (256 editing) are repo defaults, not paper numbers. (1024 tokens / 50 steps is about 20x: consistent with the "approximately 20 times" claim, my arithmetic.)
- Model size: "Current version of Show-o is based on Phi-1.5 (1.3B) (Li et al., 2023)." (v7, Section 4.1)
- GenEval, Table 3 (v7): "Show-o (Ours) | 1.3B | 0.98 | 0.80 | 0.66 | 0.84 | 0.31 | 0.50 | 0.68" (overall 0.68); Show-o-double-dagger (CLIP-ViT input for understanding) overall 0.69.
- FLAG version disagreement: v1 Table 3 reports "Show-o (Ours) | 1.3B | 0.95 | 0.52 | 0.49 | 0.82 | 0.11 | 0.28 | 0.53" (overall 0.53, https://arxiv.org/html/2408.12528v1). Janus-Pro Table 4 copies 0.53; BAGEL Table 5 copies 0.68. v7 Appendix F says the later model continued training "on the 2.0B image-text pairs for 500K steps and then we increase the image resolution to 512 x 512 and train Show-o for an additional 500K steps", which explains the change. Cite the version.
- COCO FID: "Show-o achieves a better FID, i.e., 9.24, with only 1.3B parameters and 35M training data." (v7, Section 4.2, MSCOCO 30K)

## 4. Janus (arXiv 2410.13848v1) and Janus-Pro (arXiv 2501.17811v1), DeepSeek

- Motivation, decoupled encoding: "Prior research often relies on a single visual encoder for both tasks, such as Chameleon. However, due to the differing levels of information granularity required by multimodal understanding and generation, this approach can lead to suboptimal performance, particularly in multimodal understanding. To address this issue, we decouple visual encoding into separate pathways, while still leveraging a single, unified transformer architecture for processing." (Janus v1, Abstract)
- Architecture: "For multimodal understanding, we use the SigLIP [92] encoder to extract high-dimensional semantic features from images. These features are flattened from a 2-D grid into a 1-D sequence, and an understanding adaptor is used to map these image features into the input space of the LLM. For visual generation tasks, we use the VQ tokenizer from [73] to convert images into discrete IDs. ... a randomly initialized prediction head is used for image predictions in the visual generation task. The entire model adheres to an autoregressive framework without the need for specially designed attention masks." (Janus v1, Section 3.1)
- Components, CONFIRMED: "we utilize DeepSeek-LLM (1.3B) [5] with a maximum supported sequence length of 4096 as the base language model. For the vision encoder used in understanding tasks, we select SigLIP-Large-Patch16-384 [92]. The generation encoder has a codebook of size 16,384 and downsamples images by a factor of 16. Both the understanding adaptor and the generation adaptor are two-layer MLPs. ... All images are resized to 384 x 384 pixels." (Janus v1, Section 4.1). Janus-Pro repeats the same SigLIP, 16,384 codebook, factor-16 and 384x384 sentences (Janus-Pro v1, Section 3.1).
- 576 tokens per image: not stated as a number in either paper's text I fetched; 384/16 = 24, 24 x 24 = 576 is arithmetic from the confirmed facts. Mark as DERIVED, not quoted.
- FLAG: Janus says DeepSeek-LLM "1.3B"; Janus-Pro says "The previous version of Janus validates the effectiveness of visual encoding decoupling using a 1.5B LLM" and "Janus is validated at the 1B parameter scale" (Janus-Pro v1, Sections 1 and 2.4). Same model, three different size labels.
- Training stages (Janus): "Stage I: Training Adaptors and Image Head. ... We keep the visual encoders and the LLM frozen during this stage, allowing only the trainable parameters within the understanding adaptor, generation adaptor and image head to be updated." "Stage II: Unified Pretraining. ... We unfreeze the LLM and utilize all types of training data: pure text data, multimodal understanding data, and visual generation data. Inspired by Pixart [9], we begin by conducting simple visual generation training using ImageNet-1k". "Stage III: Supervised Fine-tuning. ... We fine-tune all parameters except the generation encoder." (Janus v1, Section 3.2)
- Janus-Pro changes: "Longer Training in Stage I", "Focused Training in Stage II: In Stage II, we drop ImageNet data and directly utilize normal text-to-image data", and Stage III data ratio "changing the proportion of multimodal data, pure text data, and text-to-image data from 7:3:10 to 5:1:4" (Janus-Pro v1, Section 2.2). Data: "approximately 90 million samples" added for understanding; "approximately 72 million samples of synthetic aesthetic data, bringing the ratio of real to synthetic data to 1:1" (Section 2.3). Scale: "we scaled the model up to 7B" (Section 2.4).
- Janus Table 5 ablation, CONFIRMED POPE 60.1 vs 87.0: Exp-A "VQ Tokenizer | Und. + Gen. | POPE 60.1 | MMB 35.0 | SEED 34.9 | MMMU 24.7 | COCO-FID 8.72"; Exp-D "SigLIP + VQ (Ours) | Und. + Gen. | 87.0 | 69.4 | 63.7 | 30.5 | 8.53". Also B (semantic tokenizer, Und.+Gen.) POPE 82.4, FID 7.11; C (semantic tokenizer, Und. only) 83.9; E (SigLIP, Und. only) 85.9; F (VQ, Gen. only) FID 8.92. (Janus v1, Table 5). Text: "there is a significant gap on understanding benchmarks between Exp-A and our model (Exp-D)" (Section 4.5)
- Janus-Pro-7B GenEval and DPG: "Janus-Pro-7B scores 0.80, outperforming Janus [46] (0.61), DALL-E 3 (0.67), and Stable Diffusion 3 Medium [11] (0.74)." (Janus-Pro v1, Section 1) and "Janus-Pro achieves a score of 84.19 on DPG-Bench, surpassing all other methods." (Section 3.3)
  - Table 4 (GenEval) row: "Janus-Pro-7B | 0.99 | 0.89 | 0.59 | 0.90 | 0.79 | 0.66 | 0.80"; Janus-Pro-1B overall 0.73; Janus 0.61.
  - Table 4 copied rows: "DALL-E 3 [2] | 0.96 | 0.87 | 0.47 | 0.83 | 0.43 | 0.45 | 0.67"; "SD3-Medium [11] | 0.99 | 0.94 | 0.72 | 0.89 | 0.33 | 0.60 | 0.74"; also Transfusion 0.63, Chameleon 0.39, Show-o 0.53 (v1 number, see Show-o flag).
  - Table 5 (DPG-Bench overall): Janus-Pro-7B 84.19 (Global 86.90, Entity 88.90, Attribute 89.40, Relation 89.32, Other 89.48); "DALL-E 3 [2] | 90.97 | 89.61 | 88.39 | 90.58 | 89.83 | 83.50"; "SD3-Medium [11] | 87.90 | 91.01 | 88.83 | 80.70 | 88.68 | 84.08"; Janus 79.68.
  - The DALL-E 3 and SD3-Medium rows are third-party numbers copied from their references [2] and [11], not re-run by DeepSeek. Janus-Pro-7B's own numbers are vendor self-reported. Note 84.19 vs 84.08 (SD3-Medium) is a 0.11-point margin.
  - BAGEL Table 5 copies the same DALL-E 3 (0.67) and SD3-Medium (0.74) GenEval rows and Janus-Pro-7B (0.80): consistent.

## 5. BAGEL (ByteDance Seed, arXiv 2505.14683v3)

- MoT quote: "we adopt a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture that employs selective activation of modality-specific parameters. Unlike some prior approaches [73, 57, 69, 18] that introduce bottleneck connectors between generation and understanding modules, our design enables long-context interaction between multimodal understanding and generation through shared self-attention operations." (v3, Section 1)
- Two experts, two encoders: "BAGEL adopts a MoT architecture comprising two transformer experts [dash] one dedicated to multimodal understanding and the other to multimodal generation. Accordingly, the model employs two separate visual encoders: an understanding-oriented encoder and a generation-oriented encoder. The two transformer experts operates on the same token sequence through the shared self-attention operation at every layer. When predicting text tokens, BAGEL follows the Next-Token-Prediction paradigm ... For visual token prediction, BAGEL adopts the Rectified Flow [45, 19, 41] method" (v3, Section 2)
- Size: "an open-source multimodal foundation model with 7B active parameters (14B total) trained on large-scale interleaved multimodal data." (v3, Section 1). CONFIRMED 7B active / 14B total.
- Backbone: "We choose Qwen2.5 LLM [93] as the initialization" and adds QK-Norm (v3, Section 2.1)

## 6. Mixture-of-Transformers (Meta + Stanford, arXiv 2411.04996v2)

- Modality-specific parameters: "MoT decouples non-embedding parameters of the model by modality---including feed-forward networks, attention matrices, and layer normalization---enabling modality-specific processing with global self-attention over the full input sequence." (v2, Abstract; "---" is in the source)
- Compute saving: "In the Chameleon 7B setting (autoregressive text-and-image generation), MoT matches the dense baseline's performance using only 55.8% of the FLOPs. When extended to include speech, MoT reaches speech performance comparable to the dense baseline with only 37.2% of the FLOPs. In the Transfusion setting, where text and image are trained with different objectives, a 7B MoT model matches the image modality performance of the dense baseline with one third of the FLOPs, and a 760M MoT model outperforms a 1.4B dense baseline across key image generation metrics. System profiling further highlights MoT's practical benefits, achieving dense baseline image quality in 47.2% of the wall-clock time and text quality in 75.6% of the wall-clock time (measured on AWS p4de.24xlarge instances with NVIDIA A100 GPUs)." (v2, Abstract)
- Per-modality detail: "MoT consistently required only 45.5% of the dense model's training steps to achieve comparable pre-training loss ... Modality-specific analysis showed MoT's particular effectiveness in the image modality, requiring only 34.8% of the dense model's training steps to match final loss" (v2, Section 6.1, Figure 5). The 55.8% is the validation-loss match point: "MoT requires only 55.8% of the training FLOPs to match the dense model's validation metrics" (same section).
- All runs are equal-FLOPs by construction: "All model implementations, built upon the dense model, maintain identical FLOPs for both training and testing" (v2, Section 5). The compute saving is in training steps to reach a loss, not per-token FLOPs.

## 9. Gemma 4 12B Unified (Google DeepMind) and Qwen3.8-27B

Hugging Face search (https://huggingface.co/api/models?search=gemma-4&author=google) lists google/gemma-4-12B and google/gemma-4-12B-it (created 2026-05-23). Card used: google/gemma-4-12B-it, commit 707f0a3b8a3c7ad586ed01e27eafbad8a27dd0f7 (lastModified 2026-07-20), not gated, license apache-2.0.

- Card, encoder-free: "This model card is for the Gemma 4 12B Unified model ... it brings native audio and vision understanding directly to local environments without the need for separate encoders. This unified approach to multimodality makes the model encoder-free" (card intro)
- Card, mechanism: "The "Unified" in Gemma 4 12B Unified refers to its encoder-free architecture. Other Gemma 4 models use dedicated encoders to process multimodal data before passing it to the LLM. Gemma 4 12B eliminates these encoders entirely, projecting raw image patches and audio waveforms directly into the LLM's embedding space through lightweight linear layers. This unified approach means all modalities flow straight into a single decoder-only transformer, reducing multimodal latency and allowing the entire model to be fine-tuned in one pass." - Card table: 12B Unified total parameters "11.95B", 48 layers, sliding window 1024, context 256K, modalities "Text, Image, Audio", Vision Encoder Parameters "-", Audio Encoder Parameters "-" (vs ~550M vision encoder for 31B, ~150M vision + ~300M audio for E2B/E4B).

- config.json (same commit, https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B-it/raw/main/config.json): architectures ["Gemma4UnifiedForConditionalGeneration"], model_type "gemma4_unified"; vision_config: "patch_size": 16, "pooling_kernel_size": 3, "model_patch_size": 48, "num_soft_tokens": 280, "mm_embed_dim": 3840, "mm_posemb_size": 1120; audio_config: "audio_samples_per_token": 640, "audio_embed_dim": 640; text_config: "use_bidirectional_attention": "vision", hidden_size 3840, num_hidden_layers 48, vocab_size 262144. - Technical report (arXiv 2607.02770v2): "Alongside improved vision and audio encoders for all model sizes, we propose a unified, encoder-free architecture for our 12B model, which ingests raw audio and image patches." (Abstract)
- TR Section 2.3 Encoder-free architecture: "Gemma 4 12B is trained from scratch based on a new, unified, and encoder-free model paradigm, replacing the separate vision and audio encoders with lightweight projection modules. For the vision modality, Gemma 4 12B takes in 48 x 48 x 3 RGB patches, but replaces the 550M vision encoder by a single large matmul (35M parameters). Spatial awareness is maintained by adding 2D coordinate-based positional embeddings directly to the patch representations before a final LayerNorm layer (Ba et al., 2016). For audio, the 305M USM-based conformer encoder is entirely discarded. Raw audio is segmented into 40ms chunks at 16kHz, resulting in 640-dimensional vectors per chunk. These are projected directly into the LLM embedding space. Since audio is a temporal sequence, it does not require additional positional encoding."
- TR, encoder models' patch/pooling (for contrast): "Both are Vision Transformers (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021, ViT) with a patch size of 16 ... We restrict the maximum number of tokens, N_max to the values 70,140,280,560 and 1120" (Section 2.1). Figure 2 caption: "Here we use patch_size=16, pooling_kernel_size=3, max_soft_tokens=10." Reading: in encoder models 16-pixel ViT patches are pooled 3x3 into one soft token covering 48x48 pixels; the 12B Unified skips the ViT and projects the 48x48x3 patch directly (consistent with config model_patch_size 48 = 16 x 3).
- TR, 12B "from scratch" plus "Gemma 4 models have frozen vision and audio encoders" (Section 1 bullet) i.e. encoders frozen for other sizes, none for 12B.
- Audio results for 12B: "Table 8: Audio performance of Gemma 4 12B model on supported languages, demonstrating that competitive audio-text performance can be achieved without a dedicated audio encoder." (TR, Table 8 caption). All Gemma numbers are vendor self-reported.
