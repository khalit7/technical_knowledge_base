6. One transformer that reads and writes: the unified models

Sections 3 to 5 built a generator over image tokens alone. A unified model puts that generator inside a language model, so one network reads interleaved text and images and writes either. The open designs differ in three choices: what an image becomes on the way in, what it becomes on the way out, and which positions may attend to which (the attention mask). The {{Fusion explorer|#t-nmfx}} draws each design's sequence and mask on the same small example.

Chameleon: everything is a token, and the training fights back

Chameleon (Meta, May 2024) is the reference early-fusion model: "This early-fusion approach, where all modalities are projected into a shared representational space from the start, allows for seamless reasoning and generation across modalities. However, it also presents significant technical challenges, particularly in terms of optimization stability and scaling" ({{arXiv 2405.09818v2, section 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v2}}). Images go through a VQ tokenizer, "a 512 x 512 image into 1024 discrete tokens from a codebook of size 8192", and those 8,192 codes sit inside one BPE vocabulary of 65,536; the 7B and 34B transformers then predict text and image tokens with the same softmax.

Why it was hard. The paper's diagnosis: with all weights shared, "each modality will try to "compete" with the other by increasing its norms slightly", harmless early but divergent "once we get outside the effective representation range of bf16"; training with image generation disabled showed no instability (Figure 10). The fixes, by size: QK-Norm (layer norm on queries and keys before the attention softmax) for both, "essential in both cases"; for the 7B also dropout 0.1, for the 34B a Swin-style reordering of the norms instead; and z-loss, a penalty of 10-5 log2 Z on the output softmax's normaliser Z, because QK-Norm "does not solve the problem of logit shift in the final softmax". The useful operational lesson: "monitoring uncontrolled growth of output norms is strongly correlated with predicting future loss divergence", even when divergence arrives "after as much as even 20-30% of training progress" (all {{section 2.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v2}}) vendor.

What it showed, and what it released. Chameleon reports no FID or GenEval of its own; its evidence is human preference on mixed text-and-image answers, 60.4% and 51.6% win rates (a tie counts half) against Gemini and GPT-4V answers augmented with DALL·E 3 images ("Gemini+" and "GPT-4V+" in the body; the introduction calls them "Gemini-Pro and GPT-4V") vendor. The released weights take images in but write only text: "At this time, we are not releasing the Chameleon image generation model" ({{Meta blog, 18 June 2024|https://ai.meta.com/blog/meta-fair-research-new-releases/}}). Licence: Chameleon Research License (research only, gated). The transformers port enforces it in code: after the output layer, logits[:, :, image_tokens] = torch.finfo(logits.dtype).min under the comment "Disallow image tokens" ({{modeling_chameleon.py lines 1066 to 1068, v5.20.1|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.20.1/src/transformers/models/chameleon/modeling_chameleon.py#L1066}}), so the released model physically cannot sample an image code; its QK-Norm is self.q_norm = ChameleonLayerNorm(...) at line 280. Its own named weakness is the tokenizer: "reconstructing images with a large amount of text" (section 2.1).

Emu3: next-token prediction only, at 4,096 tokens per image

Emu3 (BAAI, September 2024) keeps Chameleon's bet and pushes it: an 8B model with a 131,072-token context over text and SBER-MoVQGAN codes, 4,096 tokens from 32,768 codes for a 512 × 512 image, sampled with top-k 16,384 and guidance 5.0 to 5.5 ({{arXiv 2409.18869v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18869v1}}) vendor. Its GenEval win over SDXL (0.66 against 0.55) uses a GPT-4V prompt rewriter; without it Emu3 scores 0.54 (its Table 7). Emu3.5 (October 2025) adds "Discrete Diffusion Adaptation" to fill tokens in parallel, "about 20x" faster per image; its Table 16 (512 s to 22 s, or 120 s to 10 s with FlagScale, for a 4,096-token 1024 × 1024 image) gives ratios of about 23 and 12 derived ({{arXiv 2510.26583v1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26583}}).

Janus and Janus-Pro: one transformer, two image encodings

DeepSeek's Janus starts from the measurement on the sibling page Autoencoders and VAEs: tokens built to reconstruct pixels are poor features for understanding. "Due to the differing levels of information granularity required by multimodal understanding and generation, this approach can lead to suboptimal performance ... we decouple visual encoding into separate pathways" ({{arXiv 2410.13848v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13848v1}}): SigLIP-Large-Patch16-384 features to read, LlamaGen's VQ tokenizer (16,384 codes, downsampling 16, 384 × 384 images, so 576 tokens: derived) to write. Its ablation (Table 5): one VQ tokenizer for both tasks scores POPE 60.1, the two-encoder model 87.0 vendor. Janus-Pro-7B reports GenEval 0.80 and DPG-Bench 84.19, in a table whose DALL·E 3 (0.67) and SD3-Medium (0.74) rows are copied from other papers, not re-run ({{arXiv 2501.17811v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17811v1}}).

Transfusion: next token for text, diffusion for images, one set of weights

Transfusion (Meta, August 2024) keeps images continuous. "Transfusion combines the language modeling loss function (next token prediction) with diffusion to train a single transformer over mixed-modality sequences" ({{arXiv 2408.11039v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11039v1}}). An 86M-parameter VAE turns a 256 × 256 image into a 32 × 32 × 8 latent; U-Net down blocks make 2 × 2 latent patches, 256 positions per image. The loss is L = LLM + λ LDDPM with λ = 5, the language loss per text token and the diffusion loss per image. The mask is "causal attention to every element in the sequence, and bidirectional attention within the elements of each individual image"; turning on the bidirectional part alone moved FID from 61.3 to 20.3 in their ablation (Table 5, 0.76B models).

The controlled comparison. The authors retrained Chameleon-style discrete tokens and Transfusion at 7B on the same 0.5T tokens (their Table 3): FID 29.6 against 16.8, CLIP score 24.3 against 25.5, and Transfusion matched Chameleon's FID with 0.029 of the FLOPs ("34x less compute") and its CLIP score with 0.319 ("less than a third", the figure the introduction quotes) vendor. Even text got better (C4 perplexity 7.72 against 8.41), which the authors tentatively put down to "the competition between text and image tokens in the output distribution". The scaled 7B model trained on 2T tokens reaches COCO FID 6.78 and GenEval 0.63, sampling with 250 diffusion steps.

Show-o and BAGEL: the other hybrids

  • Show-o (NUS and ByteDance, 2024): 1.3B (Phi-1.5), images as MAGVIT-v2 lookup-free codes (8,192, 16 × 16 at 256 px), generated by masked prediction as in section 4, with "omni-attention": "Show-o model text tokens v within the sequence via causal attention. For image tokens u, Show-o processes them via full attention" ({{arXiv 2408.12528v7|https://arxiv.org/html/2408.12528v7}}). Its GenEval is 0.53 in v1 and 0.68 in v7 of the same paper (more training); later papers copy one or the other.
  • BAGEL (ByteDance Seed, 2025): a "Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture that employs selective activation of modality-specific parameters" with "shared self-attention operations": separate weights for understanding and generation tokens, one attention over both; SigLIP 2 to read, the FLUX VAE latent and rectified flow to write; 7B active, 14B total ({{arXiv 2505.14683v3|https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14683v3}}). GenEval 0.82, or 0.88 with an LLM prompt rewriter vendor. The parent idea, Mixture-of-Transformers, "decouples non-embedding parameters of the model by modality" and matched the dense Chameleon-7B setting at 55.8% of the FLOPs ({{arXiv 2411.04996v2|https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04996v2}}) vendor.

Encoder-free: patches straight into the language model

Every design above still has an image encoder or tokenizer trained separately. Fuyu-8B (Adept, October 2023) removed it: "there is no image encoder. Image patches are instead linearly projected into the first layer of the transformer, bypassing the embedding lookup" ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/adept/fuyu-8b}}; 30-pixel patches, CC-BY-NC). Gemma 4 12B Unified (Google; Hugging Face repository created 23 May 2026; Apache 2.0) does the same at scale for images and audio: it "takes in 48 x 48 x 3 RGB patches, but replaces the 550M vision encoder by a single large matmul (35M parameters)", with 2-D coordinate position embeddings, and is "trained from scratch" ({{Gemma 4 report, arXiv 2607.02770v2, section 2.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v2}}). Its config sets 280 soft tokens per image by default (budgets 70 to 1,120), 640 audio samples per token (40 ms at 16 kHz), and bidirectional attention among vision tokens ({{config.json at 707f0a3|https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-12B-it/blob/707f0a3b8a3c7ad586ed01e27eafbad8a27dd0f7/config.json}}) vendor. It reads images and audio; it does not write them.

"Native" and "early fusion" mean different things in different documents

Chameleon: discrete image tokens, no encoder, trained together from scratch. Llama 4: "incorporating early fusion to seamlessly integrate text and vision tokens into a unified model backbone", yet with a vision encoder "based on MetaCLIP but trained separately in conjunction with a frozen Llama model" ({{Meta, 5 April 2025|https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-4-multimodal-intelligence/}}): early means the vision tokens join from pretraining onward. Qwen3.8-27B calls itself "a native vision-language model", and its card also says "Causal Language Model with Vision Encoder" (a 27-layer ViT in its config): native is a training claim. Gemma 4 12B Unified and Fuyu: no encoder at all. When a model card says native, read the config.
