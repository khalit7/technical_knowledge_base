Masks follow the papers and configs quoted below (Gemma 4 12B Unified's config: "use_bidirectional_attention": "vision"); the input image's loss differs by recipe (Chameleon trains on all tokens, LLaVA-style VLMs on the answer only), so the letters show the common case.

Chameleon: everything is a token, and the training fights back

Chameleon (Meta, May 2024) is the reference early-fusion model: the shared space "allows for seamless reasoning and generation across modalities. However, it also presents significant technical challenges, particularly in terms of optimization stability and scaling" ({{arXiv 2405.09818v2, section 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v2}}). A VQ tokenizer turns "a 512 x 512 image into 1024 discrete tokens from a codebook of size 8192", and those 8,192 codes sit inside one BPE vocabulary of 65,536; the 7B and 34B transformers predict text and image tokens with the same softmax.

Why it was hard: the modalities compete. With all weights shared, "each modality will try to "compete" with the other by increasing its norms slightly", harmless early but divergent "once we get outside the effective representation range of bf16"; with image generation disabled training showed no instability (Figure 10). The fixes: QK-Norm (layer norm on queries and keys before the attention softmax) for both sizes, "essential in both cases"; dropout 0.1 for the 7B, a Swin-style reordering of the norms for the 34B; and z-loss, 10-5 log2 Z on the output softmax's normaliser Z, because QK-Norm "does not solve the problem of logit shift in the final softmax". The operational lesson: "monitoring uncontrolled growth of output norms is strongly correlated with predicting future loss divergence", even when divergence arrives "after as much as even 20-30% of training progress" (all {{section 2.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v2}}) vendor. How QK-norm and z-loss work in any transformer is on {{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}}.

Emu3 (BAAI, September 2024) pushed the same bet to an 8B model writing 4,096 codes per 512 × 512 image, and Emu3.5 (October 2025) fills those codes in parallel rounds for "about 20x" faster images.

Chameleon's evidence, what Meta released, and Emu3's numbers (provenance)

What it showed, and what it released. No FID or GenEval of its own: its evidence is human preference on mixed text-and-image answers, 60.4% and 51.6% win rates (a tie counts half) against Gemini and GPT-4V answers augmented with DALL·E 3 images ("Gemini+" and "GPT-4V+" in the body; the introduction calls them "Gemini-Pro and GPT-4V") vendor. The released weights take images in but write only text: "At this time, we are not releasing the Chameleon image generation model" ({{Meta blog, 18 June 2024|https://ai.meta.com/blog/meta-fair-research-new-releases/}}); licence Chameleon Research License (research only, gated). The transformers port enforces it in code: after the output layer, logits[:, :, image_tokens] = torch.finfo(logits.dtype).min under the comment "Disallow image tokens" ({{modeling_chameleon.py lines 1066 to 1068, v5.20.1|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.20.1/src/transformers/models/chameleon/modeling_chameleon.py#L1066}}); its QK-Norm is self.q_norm = ChameleonLayerNorm(...) at line 280. Its own named weakness is the tokenizer: "reconstructing images with a large amount of text" (section 2.1).

Emu3 (BAAI, September 2024) keeps the bet and pushes it: an 8B model with a 131,072-token context, 4,096 tokens from 32,768 SBER-MoVQGAN codes for a 512 × 512 image, sampled with top-k 16,384 and guidance 5.0 to 5.5 ({{arXiv 2409.18869v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18869v1}}) vendor. Its GenEval win over SDXL (0.66 against 0.55) uses a GPT-4V prompt rewriter; without it Emu3 scores 0.54 (its Table 7). Emu3.5 (October 2025) fills tokens in parallel for "about 20x" faster images; its Table 16 (512 s to 22 s, or 120 s to 10 s with FlagScale, for a 4,096-token 1024 × 1024 image) gives ratios of about 23 and 12 derived ({{arXiv 2510.26583v1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26583}}).

Janus: one transformer, two image encodings

DeepSeek's Janus starts from the section 2 measurement: "due to the differing levels of information granularity required by multimodal understanding and generation, this approach can lead to suboptimal performance ... we decouple visual encoding into separate pathways" ({{arXiv 2410.13848v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13848v1}}): SigLIP-Large-Patch16-384 features to read, LlamaGen's VQ tokenizer (16,384 codes, downsampling 16, 384 × 384 images, so 576 tokens: derived) to write.

Transfusion: next token for text, diffusion for images, one set of weights

Transfusion (Meta, August 2024) keeps images continuous: it "combines the language modeling loss function (next token prediction) with diffusion to train a single transformer over mixed-modality sequences" ({{arXiv 2408.11039v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11039v1}}). An 86M-parameter VAE turns a 256 × 256 image into a 32 × 32 × 8 latent; U-Net down blocks make 2 × 2 latent patches, 256 positions per image. The loss is L = LLM + λ LDDPM with λ = 5 (section 7). The mask is "causal attention to every element in the sequence, and bidirectional attention within the elements of each individual image"; turning on the bidirectional part alone moved FID from 61.3 to 20.3 in their ablation (Table 5, 0.76B models).

Show-o and BAGEL: the other hybrids

  • Show-o (NUS and ByteDance, 2024): 1.3B (Phi-1.5), images as MAGVIT-v2 lookup-free codes (8,192, 16 × 16 at 256 px), written by masked prediction as in section 4, with "omni-attention": "Show-o model text tokens v within the sequence via causal attention. For image tokens u, Show-o processes them via full attention" ({{arXiv 2408.12528v7|https://arxiv.org/html/2408.12528v7}}).
  • BAGEL (ByteDance Seed, 2025): a "Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture that employs selective activation of modality-specific parameters" with "shared self-attention operations": separate weights for understanding and generation tokens, one attention over both; SigLIP 2 to read, the FLUX VAE latent and rectified flow to write; 7B active, 14B total ({{arXiv 2505.14683v3|https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14683v3}}). Why separate weights pay is section 7.

Encoder-free: patches straight into the language model

Every design above still has an image encoder or tokenizer trained separately. Fuyu-8B (Adept, October 2023) removed it: "there is no image encoder. Image patches are instead linearly projected into the first layer of the transformer, bypassing the embedding lookup" ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/adept/fuyu-8b}}; 30-pixel patches, CC-BY-NC). Gemma 4 12B Unified (Google; Hugging Face repository created 23 May 2026; Apache 2.0) does the same at scale for images and audio: it "takes in 48 x 48 x 3 RGB patches, but replaces the 550M vision encoder by a single large matmul (35M parameters)", with 2-D coordinate position embeddings, and is "trained from scratch" ({{Gemma 4 report, arXiv 2607.02770v2, section 2.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v2}}). It reads images and audio; it does not write them.

  • BAGEL: GenEval 0.82, or 0.88 with an LLM prompt rewriter vendor; like for like with Janus-Pro-7B that is 0.82 against 0.80.

"Native" and "early fusion" mean different things in different documents

Chameleon: discrete image tokens, no encoder, trained together from scratch. Llama 4: "incorporating early fusion to seamlessly integrate text and vision tokens into a unified model backbone", yet with a vision encoder "based on MetaCLIP but trained separately in conjunction with a frozen Llama model" ({{Meta, 5 April 2025|https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-4-multimodal-intelligence/}}): early means the vision tokens join from pretraining onward. Qwen3.8-27B calls itself "a native vision-language model", and its card also says "Causal Language Model with Vision Encoder" (a 27-layer ViT in its config): native is a training claim. Gemma 4 12B Unified and Fuyu: no encoder at all. When a model card says native, read the config.
