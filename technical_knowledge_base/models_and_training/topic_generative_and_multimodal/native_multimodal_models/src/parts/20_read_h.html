9. Speech in and out: from a three-model cascade to one model per audio frame

A voice assistant built as a cascade runs speech recognition, a language model and text-to-speech in a row. OpenAI's description of its own pre-GPT-4o Voice Mode is exactly this: "a pipeline of three separate models", with average latencies "of 2.8 seconds (GPT-3.5) and 5.4 seconds (GPT-4)", and the language model "loses a lot of information": "it can't directly observe tone, multiple speakers, or background noises, and it can't output laughter, singing, or express emotion" ({{Hello GPT-4o, 13 May 2024, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/20240515000000id_/https://openai.com/index/hello-gpt-4o/}}) vendor.

Moshi's layout, drawn for 6 frames (480 ms): one model takes the user's audio tokens in and writes its own text and audio tokens in the same 80 ms step, so nothing passes through a text hand-off and tone survives in both directions. How the 17 streams are laid out (inner-monologue text, the temporal and depth transformers, the one-frame acoustic delay) is taught on {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}}, section 8, which owns the codec and the streams. lab

How native speech works. A neural audio codec cuts the waveform into frames and quantizes each frame with several codebooks in a chain (residual vector quantization); the codecs themselves are {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}}'s, and here only the rates matter. Two open designs:

  • Moshi (Kyutai, 2024): one full-duplex model on the Mimi codec at 12.5 frames a second (8 codebooks, 1.1 kbps), one big-model step per 80 ms frame; "a theoretical latency of 160ms, 200ms in practice" ({{arXiv 2410.00037v2|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037v2}}) lab. Its streams, inner monologue and latency budget: {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}}, section 8.
  • Qwen2.5-Omni and Qwen3-Omni (Alibaba, open weights): a Thinker (the language model) and a Talker that turns the Thinker's output into speech codes, "end-to-end jointly trained" ({{Qwen2.5-Omni, arXiv 2503.20215v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20215v1}}). In Qwen3-Omni both are mixtures of experts (Thinker 30B with 3B active, Talker 3B with 0.3B active), input and output audio run at 12.5 Hz, the Talker predicts the first codebook and a small multi-token module the residual ones, a causal ConvNet replaces diffusion for code-to-waveform, and the "theoretical end-to-end first-packet latency" is 234 ms for audio, 547 ms for video, 1,172 ms at 6 concurrent requests ({{arXiv 2509.17765v1, Tables 1 and 2|https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17765v1}}) vendor.
AnyGPT, cascade latency sources, and the Qwen omni licences (depth)
  • AnyGPT (Fudan, 2024): the purest "everything is a token" design. Speech through SpeechTokenizer (8 codebooks of 1,024 at 50 Hz; the language model predicts only the first, semantic one), images through SEED (32 tokens from 8,192 codes, decoded by an unCLIP Stable Diffusion), music through Encodec (4 codebooks of 2,048 at 50 Hz, 200 tokens a second), all on an unchanged LLaMA-2 7B: "without any alterations to the current large language model (LLM) architecture" ({{arXiv 2402.12226v3|https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12226v3}}). Its Table 1 gives the music vocabulary as 4,096 while its text says 8,192: the paper disagrees with itself.
  • Cascade latency, other sources: Kyutai's Moshi paper puts it at "several seconds", against "a few hundred milliseconds" in human conversation ({{Défossez et al. 2024, section 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037v2}}) lab; no primary source checked here gives a component-by-component breakdown of a cascade (unconfirmed).
  • Qwen licences: Apache 2.0 for the Qwen3-Omni 30B-A3B models; Qwen2.5-Omni-3B is under a research licence while the 7B is Apache 2.0 (model cards, 2026-10-17).
Predict: a native speech model must produce 10 seconds of audio with Mimi tokens. If every codebook token were generated by the big model one at a time, how many big-model steps is that, and what time budget does each step get for real time?
derived 10 s × 12.5 frames × 8 codebooks = 1,000 tokens, so 1,000 sequential steps with 10 ms each, a hard budget for a 7B model. Moshi's layout runs the big model once per frame (125 steps, 80 ms each) and leaves the 8 codebooks of a frame to a small depth transformer: the same tokens, an eighth of the big steps. Try both layouts in {{Token economics|#t-nmtok}}, part 3.

When it matters. Native speech wins when the turn-taking delay or the voice itself is the product (tone, interruptions, laughter). A cascade wins when you must swap parts (a better recogniser, a new voice), audit the text that was said, or reuse a strong text model without retraining it on audio. Safety has to be rebuilt for the audio channel: OpenAI allows "only the preset voices we created in collaboration with voice actors" and runs "a standalone output classifier" in streaming fashion that blocks output whose speaker does not match the preset, after observing "rare instances where the model would unintentionally generate an output emulating the user's voice" ({{GPT-4o system card, section 3.3.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21276v1}}) vendor.
