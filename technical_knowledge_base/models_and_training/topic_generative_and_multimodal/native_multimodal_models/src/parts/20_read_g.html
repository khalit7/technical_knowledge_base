What the API meters. Google's API documentation states the per-modality prices in tokens: an image up to 384 px is 258 tokens, larger images are cut into 768 × 768 tiles of 258 tokens each, audio is 32 tokens a second and video 263 tokens a second at one frame a second ({{Gemini API token docs, updated 2026-10-13|https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/tokens}}) vendor. How to read these claims

"Autoregressive" for GPT-4o images says the image is produced token by token; it does not say there is no diffusion decoder after the tokens, and the document gives no way to tell. "Natively multimodal" for Gemini says the model was trained on all modalities from the start; it does not say images go in as discrete tokens (the report credits Flamingo, CoCa and PaLI for its visual encoding, all continuous encoders). Latency figures (GPT-4o 232 and 320 ms, Moshi 160 and 200 ms, Qwen3-Omni 234 ms) are each self-reported under different conditions, and cannot be ranked against one another.

8. Speech in and out: from a three-model cascade to one model per audio frame

The problem. A voice assistant built as a cascade runs three models in a row: speech recognition (audio to text), a language model (text to text), text-to-speech (text to audio). OpenAI's description of its own pre-GPT-4o Voice Mode is exactly this: "a pipeline of three separate models", with average latencies "of 2.8 seconds (GPT-3.5) and 5.4 seconds (GPT-4)", and the language model "loses a lot of information": "it can't directly observe tone, multiple speakers, or background noises, and it can't output laughter, singing, or express emotion" ({{Hello GPT-4o, 13 May 2024, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/20240515000000id_/https://openai.com/index/hello-gpt-4o/}}) vendor. Kyutai's Moshi paper puts the cascade's latency at "several seconds", against "a few hundred milliseconds" in human conversation ({{Défossez et al. 2024, section 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037v2}}) lab; no primary source checked here gives a component-by-component breakdown of a cascade (unconfirmed).

How native speech works. Audio becomes tokens with a neural audio codec: an encoder cuts the waveform into frames and quantizes each frame with several small codebooks in a chain (residual vector quantization); a decoder turns codes back into sound. The codecs, their bitrates and a measured round trip are on the sibling page {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}}; here only the rates matter. Three open designs:

  • Moshi (Kyutai, 2024): Mimi codec at 12.5 frames a second, "Q = 8 quantizers, each with a codebook size of N A = 2048", 1.1 kbps; one 7B "temporal" step per 80 ms frame, a small depth transformer for the 8 codebooks inside the frame, text predicted first as an "Inner Monologue", and both speakers modelled at once (full duplex). Latency: "a theoretical latency of 160ms, 200ms in practice", the 160 being one 80 ms frame plus one 80 ms acoustic delay ({{arXiv 2410.00037v2|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00037v2}}) lab.
  • Qwen2.5-Omni and Qwen3-Omni (Alibaba, open weights): a Thinker (the language model) and a Talker that turns the Thinker's output into speech codes, "end-to-end jointly trained" ({{Qwen2.5-Omni, arXiv 2503.20215v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20215v1}}). In Qwen3-Omni both are mixtures of experts (Thinker 30B with 3B active, Talker 3B with 0.3B active), input and output audio run at 12.5 Hz, the Talker predicts the first codebook and a small multi-token module the residual ones, a causal ConvNet replaces diffusion for code-to-waveform, and the "theoretical end-to-end first-packet latency" is 234 ms for audio, 547 ms for video, 1,172 ms at 6 concurrent requests ({{arXiv 2509.17765v1, Tables 1 and 2|https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17765v1}}) vendor. Licence Apache 2.0 for the 30B-A3B models; Qwen2.5-Omni-3B is under a research licence while the 7B is Apache 2.0 (model cards, 2026-10-17).
  • AnyGPT (Fudan, 2024): the purest "everything is a token" design. Speech through SpeechTokenizer (8 codebooks of 1,024 at 50 Hz; the language model predicts only the first, semantic one), images through SEED (32 tokens from 8,192 codes, decoded by an unCLIP Stable Diffusion), music through Encodec (4 codebooks of 2,048 at 50 Hz, 200 tokens a second), all on an unchanged LLaMA-2 7B: "without any alterations to the current large language model (LLM) architecture" ({{arXiv 2402.12226v3|https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12226v3}}). Its Table 1 gives the music vocabulary as 4,096 while its text says 8,192: the paper disagrees with itself.
Predict: a native speech model must produce 10 seconds of audio with Mimi tokens. If every codebook token were generated by the big model one at a time, how many big-model steps is that, and what time budget does each step get for real time?
derived 10 s × 12.5 frames × 8 codebooks = 1,000 tokens, so 1,000 sequential steps with 10 ms each, a hard budget for a 7B model. Moshi's layout runs the big model once per frame (125 steps, 80 ms each) and leaves the 8 codebooks of a frame to a small depth transformer: the same tokens, an eighth of the big steps. Try both layouts in {{Token economics|#t-nmtok}}, section 3.

When it matters. Native speech wins when the turn-taking delay or the voice itself is the product (tone, interruptions, laughter). A cascade wins when you must swap parts (a better recogniser, a new voice), audit the text that was said, or reuse a strong text model without retraining it on audio. Safety has to be rebuilt for the audio channel: OpenAI allows "only the preset voices we created in collaboration with voice actors" and runs "a standalone output classifier" in streaming fashion that blocks output whose speaker does not match the preset, after observing "rare instances where the model would unintentionally generate an output emulating the user's voice" ({{GPT-4o system card, section 3.3.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21276v1}}) vendor.
