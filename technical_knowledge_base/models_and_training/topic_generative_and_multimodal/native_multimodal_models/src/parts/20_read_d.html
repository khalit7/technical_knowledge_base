4. Faster than one token per call

One call per token means 1,024 sequential calls for a Chameleon image and 4,096 for Emu3; each is cheap with a KV cache, but they cannot run in parallel. Four published ways around it, each trading sequential calls for more work per call:

The four methods in detail, with their sources (published numbers)
  • Masked parallel decoding (MaskGIT, 2022). A bidirectional transformer learns to predict randomly masked codes from the visible ones (mask ratio from a cosine schedule). To generate, start fully masked; each round predict every masked code, keep the most confident, re-mask the rest: 8 rounds instead of 256 calls. ImageNet 256 FID 6.18 against 15.78 for the autoregressive VQGAN and "up to 64x" faster decoding ({{arXiv 2202.04200v1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04200}}) vendor; Muse scaled it to text-to-image at 0.5 s per 256 px image on TPUv4 against 6.4 s for Parti-3B ({{arXiv 2301.00704v1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00704}}). Show-o writes its images this way (section 6).
  • Next-scale prediction (VAR, 2024). Predict a 1 × 1 token map, then 2 × 2, up to 16 × 16, each scale in one parallel step: 10 steps. FID 1.92 on ImageNet 256 (1.73 with rejection sampling) against DiT-XL/2's 2.27, claimed 45 times faster ({{arXiv 2404.02905v2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02905}}) vendor.
  • Continuous tokens with a diffusion head (MAR, 2024). Drop the codebook: a small diffusion model, conditioned on the transformer's output, writes each continuous latent token. FID 1.55 with guidance (2.35 without), under 0.3 s per image on one A100 at batch 256 ({{arXiv 2406.11838v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11838}}) vendor. This removes the tokenizer ceiling of section 5.
  • Serving tricks. LlamaGen keeps raster order and serves it with vLLM: 3.3 to 4.1 times faster in its Table 7 (26.38 s to 6.36 s for a batch of 16 with its XXL model) vendor. Emu3.5 adapts the model to fill codes in parallel ("Discrete Diffusion Adaptation") for "about 20x" faster images.

5. Autoregressive against diffusion and flow, at equal training compute

The setup. The topic's diffusion run trained a 1.04M-parameter TinyUNet as a DDPM for 4,000 steps at batch 128, and its flow run trained the same network with flow matching (root Diffusion and Flow lab recordings). One TinyUNet forward pass on one image costs MFLOP (counted with PyTorch's FlopCounterMode); one pass of the token transformer over 64 tokens costs MFLOP (linear layers counted, attention added by formula because the counter skips the fused attention kernel). So the transformer trains for 4,000 × / = steps: the same training FLOPs, the same images in the same minibatch order.

At scale. On ImageNet 256 the families have traded places for years: LlamaGen-3B FID 2.18 with guidance s = 1.65 (9.38 without), VAR 1.92, MAR 1.55, DiT-XL/2 2.27, each reported by its own authors with its own tokenizer or VAE ({{LlamaGen|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06525}}, {{VAR|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02905}}, {{MAR|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11838}}) vendor. For text-to-image inside a unified model, Transfusion's controlled comparison (section 6) found continuous latents with a diffusion loss far more compute-efficient than discrete tokens, for the reason seen here: a discrete tokenizer throws information away before the generator starts.
