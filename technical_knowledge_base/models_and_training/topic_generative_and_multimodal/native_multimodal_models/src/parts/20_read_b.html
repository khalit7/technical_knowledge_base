1. The words: modality, fusion, native, unified, any-to-any

A modality is a kind of signal: text, images, audio, video. A model is multimodal when it takes more than one kind. The question that separates designs is where and when the modalities meet, called fusion:

  • Late fusion: each modality is first processed by its own network, trained separately, and only the results are combined. A vision-language model (VLM) such as LLaVA is late fusion in this sense: a pretrained image encoder makes vectors, a small projector maps them into a pretrained language model. Chameleon's authors put it this way: late-fusion approaches "encode images and text separately before combining them at a later stage" ({{arXiv 2405.09818v2, section 6|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v2}}).
  • Early fusion: the modalities share one network from the first layer and from the start of training: "all modalities are projected into a shared representational space from the start" (same paper, section 1).
  • Native multimodal: a vendor word for "trained on several modalities from pretraining onward". It says nothing exact about the architecture; section 6 shows four models that all use it and mean different things.
  • Unified: one model both understands and generates a modality (reads images and also draws them).
  • Any-to-any: any combination of modalities in, any combination out (text, image, speech). GPT-4o's system card is the best-known claim: it "accepts as input any combination of text, audio, image, and video and generates any combination of text, audio, and image outputs" ({{arXiv 2410.21276v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21276v1}}) vendor.

What the animation shows. In the pipeline, the photo reaches the generator only as the sentence the VLM wrote: every detail the sentence leaves out (the exact cut, the cuffs, the pose) is gone, and the generator draws a new pullover. In the early-fusion model the photo's codes sit in the same sequence the output is written into, so every output token can look at every input token. The price is in the second counter: 1,027 sequential calls against 32, most of them the 1,024 image codes against 25 batched guidance steps. Sections 3 to 5 measure that price on the running example; section 9 turns it into a design rule.
