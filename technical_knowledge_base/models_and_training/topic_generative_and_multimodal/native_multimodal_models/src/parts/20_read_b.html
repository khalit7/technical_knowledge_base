1. Early against late fusion: where the modalities meet

A modality is a kind of signal (text, images, audio, video). The question that separates multimodal designs is where and when the modalities meet, called fusion. Late fusion approaches "encode images and text separately before combining them at a later stage": a VLM such as LLaVA is late fusion, a pretrained image encoder plus a small projector into a pretrained language model. Early fusion means "all modalities are projected into a shared representational space from the start" (both from Chameleon, {{arXiv 2405.09818v2, sections 6 and 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v2}}). Any-to-any is the far end; GPT-4o's system card is the best-known claim: it "accepts as input any combination of text, audio, image, and video and generates any combination of text, audio, and image outputs" ({{arXiv 2410.21276v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21276v1}}) vendor. Native is a training claim, not an architecture: section 6 shows four models that use the word and mean different things.
