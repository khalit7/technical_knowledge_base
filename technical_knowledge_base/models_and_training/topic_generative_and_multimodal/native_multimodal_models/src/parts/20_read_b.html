1. Early against late fusion: where the modalities meet

A modality is a kind of signal (text, images, audio, video). The question that separates multimodal designs is where and when the modalities meet, called fusion. Late fusion approaches "encode images and text separately before combining them at a later stage": a VLM such as LLaVA is late fusion, a pretrained image encoder plus a small projector into a pretrained language model. Early fusion means "all modalities are projected into a shared representational space from the start" (both from Chameleon, {{arXiv 2405.09818v2, sections 6 and 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09818v2}}). Any-to-any is the far end; GPT-4o's system card is the best-known claim: it "accepts as input any combination of text, audio, image, and video and generates any combination of text, audio, and image outputs" ({{arXiv 2410.21276v1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21276v1}}) vendor. Native is a training claim, not an architecture: section 6 shows four models that use the word and mean different things.

Predict before you step: the request "here is a photo of a pullover, draw it in red" runs through both designs in Fig. 1. Which needs more sequential network calls, and which generator ever sees your photo? The pipeline's generator only ever sees one sentence; the early-fusion model pays for keeping the photo with 1,027 sequential calls against 32.

  1. A photo and an instruction. The user sends a 512 × 512 photo of a pullover (drawn here as a Fashion-MNIST test pullover) and the words "draw it in red".
  2. The VLM's vision encoder reads the photo. A separately trained encoder (SigLIP in SmolVLM) turns it into continuous vectors and a projector maps them to the language model's width: 1,088 soft tokens for one image in SmolVLM-500M (1,092 positions with the words). They are inputs only: no vocabulary entry exists that the model could ever output for them.
  3. One prefill pass over the whole prompt. Image vectors and words go through the language model in one forward pass under a causal mask, counted with the encoder as one call: it costs compute, not sequential steps.
  4. It can only write text: a prompt, one call per word piece. An illustrative "a red knit pullover", 4 sequential calls (5 so far).
  5. Only the sentence crosses to a separate model. Stable Diffusion 1.5's CLIP text encoder reads the prompt (6 calls). The photo itself does not cross: the generator receives about a dozen tokens of text.
  6. 25 denoising calls. The generator starts from noise and runs 25 guided DPM-Solver steps of an 860M U-Net over a 64 × 64 × 4 latent; guidance's conditional and unconditional passes are batched, so 25 sequential calls (31 so far).
  7. A red pullover, but not yours. The VAE decodes the latent (32 calls in all). The output is plausible, and any detail the sentence left out (the exact cut, the cuffs, the pose) is gone. Drawn here as a stand-in: a pullover the page's tiny model wrote from its class label alone, never having seen the photo.
  1. The same photo and instruction. This model has one vocabulary of 65,536 entries, 8,192 of which are image codes (Chameleon).
  2. The image tokenizer turns the photo into codes. A VQ tokenizer maps 512 × 512 pixels to a 32 × 32 grid of integers, 1,024 discrete tokens, which join the words in one stream (1,028 positions). They are ordinary vocabulary entries, the same kind of thing the model can output.
  3. One prefill pass over codes and words. One transformer, one causal mask, one softmax over the shared vocabulary (1 call, with the tokenizer). Every later position can attend to every image code.
  4. A start-of-image marker, then code 1. The model predicts the marker, then the first image code from the softmax restricted to image codes: one call each (3 so far).
  5. Codes 2 to 1,024, one call each. Raster order, left to right, top to bottom; each new code can read every input code (the faint streams), so it can copy or change it: a red version of the same shape. 1,026 sequential calls so far, each cheap thanks to the KV cache, with 2,053 positions in context.
  6. Your pullover, in red, capped by the tokenizer. The tokenizer's decoder turns the 1,024 codes back into pixels (1,027 calls; the recolouring is illustrative). The output can keep the input's exact shape, but its sharpness is capped by what 1,024 codes from 8,192 can reconstruct ("a core weakness of our tokenizer is in reconstructing images with a large amount of text", Chameleon).

In the pipeline every detail the sentence leaves out is gone and the generator draws a new pullover; in the early-fusion model every output code can attend to every input code. Token counts are real for the models named: SmolVLM-500M's 1,088 soft tokens are measured on {{Vision-language models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81adbb7be5bc41c4d9d0}}; Chameleon turns a 512 × 512 image into 1,024 codes (its paper); the generator is the topic's Stable Diffusion 1.5 at its running-example default of 25 guided DPM-Solver steps. The chips show 4 of each image's positions; the prompt the VLM writes is illustrative. Sections 3 to 5 measure the price of 1,024 calls; section 10 turns it into a design rule.
