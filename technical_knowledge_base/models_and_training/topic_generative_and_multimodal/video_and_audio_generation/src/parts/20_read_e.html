7. Neural audio codecs and residual vector quantisation

What a neural codec is. An autoencoder for sound with a discrete bottleneck. A convolutional encoder strides through the waveform (EnCodec: 320 samples per step, so 24,000 / 320 = 75 frames a second, each a 128-number vector); a quantiser replaces each vector with codebook indices; a decoder turns the indices back into a waveform. It is trained with reconstruction losses on waveform and mel spectrograms plus adversarial losses from discriminators that look at the spectrogram at several scales, which is what makes it sound natural rather than merely close (SoundStream 2021, {{EnCodec 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13438}}, DAC 2023, Mimi 2024).

Why residual quantisation. One codebook of 1,024 gives each frame log2(1,024) = 10 bits; spending 80 bits a frame (6 kbps) with one codebook would need 280 entries. Residual vector quantisation (RVQ) chains several small codebooks: the first quantises the vector, the second quantises what the first missed, and so on, so 8 codebooks of 1,024 give 80 bits with 8,192 stored vectors. Bits per codebook per second in EnCodec's code: bw_per_q = math.log2(self.codebook_size) * self.frame_rate = 10 × 75 = 750 bit/s, so 1.5 kbps is 2 codebooks and 24 kbps is 32. The image version (RQ-VAE) and VQ training tricks are on {{Autoencoders and VAEs|n:3c65c17b0d0d814da93bdb159b7c1e11}}.

Bitrate scalability for free. During training the number of codebooks is drawn at random per example (quantiser dropout, introduced with {{SoundStream|https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03312}}), so the decoder learns to work from any prefix of the chain. That is why one model serves 1.5 to 24 kbps, and why a token generator can predict the first codebooks first: they carry the most.

Semantic tokens. A codec trained only to reconstruct sound has no reason to put what is said into its first codebook rather than the voice's timbre or the room. Mimi (Kyutai's codec for Moshi) runs at only 12.5 frames a second with codebooks of 2,048 and distils a speech model into its first codebook: "we distill semantic information from a self-supervised model (WavLM ...) into the first level of the RVQ", using "a plain VQ and ... an RVQ with 7 levels in parallel" ({{Défossez et al. 2024, Sec 3.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037}}). At 8 codebooks that is 1.1 kbps and 100 tokens a second, rated MUSHRA 81.0 against 74.3 for SpeechTokenizer at 4 kbps (Table 4) vendor.
