The idea in one screen

A video or audio generator is an image generator with a time axis, and almost every design choice exists to make that axis affordable: a causal video VAE folds 4 frames into one latent frame so that a 5 s clip becomes about 33,000 space-time tokens for a flow-matching transformer, and a neural codec turns each second of sound into 100 to 600 discrete tokens for a language model, or into a short continuous latent for flow.

Same four moves for both media: compress, cut into tokens, generate, decode

Causal video VAE
a VAE with 3-D convolutions that only look backwards in time; frame 1 alone, then each group of 4 (or 8) frames becomes one latent frame.
Space-time patch
one token: a small block of the latent across space and time (1 × 2 × 2 latent cells in Wan).
Full against factorised attention
every token attends to every token in the clip, against attention within a frame followed by attention across frames at one position.
Exposure bias, drift
a model trained on real frames conditions on its own imperfect ones when extending a video, and errors pile up.
Mel spectrogram
sound as an image: log power in frequency bands spaced like human hearing, one column every 10 to 20 ms.
Vocoder
a network that turns a spectrogram back into a waveform (it must invent the phase).
Neural codec, RVQ
an autoencoder for sound whose bottleneck is a chain of small codebooks, each quantising what the previous ones missed (residual vector quantisation).
Quantiser dropout
training with a random number of codebooks, so any prefix of the chain decodes (one model, many bitrates).
Semantic tokens
codes that carry what is said rather than how it sounds (Mimi distils a speech model into its first codebook).
Delay pattern
codebook k shifted k − 1 steps, so one transformer emits every codebook of a frame in order, one step per frame.

The running examples. Video: the topic's public-domain test image p01_cat (scikit-image's "chelsea", CC0) turned into two 17-frame 256 × 256 clips by sliding a crop across it, slowly (2 pixels per frame) and fast (14 pixels per frame), sent through Wan 2.1's video VAE (126.9M parameters, Apache 2.0) on the CPU, plus the configs of seven open video denoisers. Audio: one LibriSpeech sentence ("I hope he doesn't work too hard said Shaggy", 3.67 s, utterance 1272-135031-0008, CC BY 4.0) and a 3.5 s synthesised music phrase, through three open neural codecs, EnCodec 24 kHz, DAC 44.1 kHz and Mimi, at every number of codebooks. Everything measured ran on an Apple M1 Pro CPU; files and scripts are in this page's src/.
