5. Keeping a video consistent, and making it longer

The problem. Inside one clip, full attention keeps things consistent: every frame sees every other. The failures are temporal: identity drift (a face or a shirt slowly changing), objects that appear or vanish, physics that does not hold. Sora's own report: it "does not accurately model the physics of many basic interactions, like glass shattering", and shows "incoherencies that develop in long duration samples or spontaneous appearances of objects" ({{OpenAI, Feb 2024, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/20250105124831/https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/}}) vendor. Independent physics tests agree: on Physics-IQ "the best model scoring 29.5%" where real videos score 100 ({{Motamed et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09038}}) independent; on VideoPhy the best model, CogVideoX-5B, follows both the caption and physical laws in 39.6% of cases ({{Bansal et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03520}}) independent.

Why length is hard. Models train on clips of 5 to 16 s because tokens, and attention with their square, grow with duration (section 4). A longer video needs a bigger window (Movie Gen trained to 16 s; LTX-2 reaches 20 s with audio) or generation in pieces, each conditioned on the frames before. That works like a language model writing text, with the same weakness: the model trained on clean real frames now conditions on its own imperfect ones, errors accumulate, and quality drifts (exposure bias; FramePack calls the result "drifting").

Three noise schedules over a long video: the rising-noise one streams and can still correct course

Each row is one denoising step, each column one chunk of frames; darker means noisier. Illustration of the published schemes (no run here).

  • Per-frame noise. Diffusion Forcing gives every frame its own noise level during training, so at sampling time later frames can be noisier than earlier ones; it rolls out "with lengths past the training horizon, where baselines diverge" ({{Chen et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01392}}). MAGI-1 generates "in chunks of 24 frames, where each chunk attends to all previously denoised chunks", denoising several chunks at once at rising noise levels ({{Sand AI 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13211}}).
  • Causal students. CausVid distils a bidirectional model into a few-step causal one that streams with a KV cache: "an initial latency of only 1.3 seconds, after which frames are generated continuously ... at approximately 9.4 FPS" ({{Yin et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07772}}) vendor. Self Forcing trains on its own rollouts to close the train-test gap: "real-time video generation at 17 FPS with sub-second latency on a single H100 GPU"; in its Table 1 the many-step Wan2.1 1.3B it starts from runs at 0.78 FPS with 103 s latency ({{Huang et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08009}}) vendor.
  • Compressed context. FramePack encodes older frames with fewer tokens so the context stays bounded however long the video, and samples in an order that limits drift ({{Zhang and Agrawala 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12626}}).

How to check. Generate past the training length and plot a per-frame quality score and an identity score (similarity of a face or object embedding to frame 1) against time: drift shows as a slope. VBench-Long and the "dynamic degree" dimension exist because a frozen video scores well on many per-frame metrics (section 9).

6. Sound as data: waveforms and spectrograms

The problem. Audio is a waveform: air pressure measured tens of thousands of times a second (the sample rate: 16 kHz for speech recognition, 24 kHz for most speech generation, 44.1 or 48 kHz for music). The Shaggy sentence is 3.67 s, so 58,720 samples at 16 kHz or 88,080 at 24 kHz; ten seconds of speech is 240,000 numbers, against a few dozen text tokens for the same words. Nothing can attend over that directly.

The spectrogram. Cut the waveform into short overlapping windows, take each window's Fourier transform, keep the magnitude, pool the frequencies into bands spaced like human hearing (the mel scale: fine at low frequencies, coarse at high), and take the log. Whisper: "an 80-channel log-magnitude Mel spectrogram representation is computed on 25-millisecond windows with a stride of 10 milliseconds" ({{Radford et al. 2022, Sec 2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356}}); large-v3 uses 128 bins. The result is an image, 80 numbers per 10 ms, about 30 times shorter than the 16 kHz waveform.

The same 3.67 s as 88,080 samples and as an image of frequency against time

Top: the waveform at 24 kHz drawn as its minimum and maximum in every 10 ms. Below: 64 mel bands from 0 to 12 kHz (bottom to top) at 20 ms steps, brightness = log power over 70 dB, computed on this page's data (codec/make_assets.py). The source is a 16 kHz recording, so it holds nothing above 8 kHz (the top eighth of the bands holds only faint energy leaked by the resampler, 37 dB below the total).

The catch: phase. A magnitude spectrogram throws away each frequency's phase, so it cannot be played back directly. A vocoder turns a spectrogram back into a waveform; HiFi-GAN "uses a mel-spectrogram as input and upsamples it through transposed convolutions until the length of the output sequence matches the temporal resolution of raw waveforms", reaching MOS 4.36 against 4.45 for the real recordings, "167.9 times faster than real-time on a single V100 GPU" ({{Kong et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05646}}) vendor. Because phase is free, a vocoder's output can sound right while matching the original waveform poorly sample by sample, which is why waveform error (SNR) is a poor quality measure for generated audio (section 7).

From spectrograms to today's two representations. Text-to-speech from 2017 to 2022 predicted a mel spectrogram and called a vocoder (Tacotron 2: 80 mel channels, 12.5 ms hop, WaveNet vocoder). Today two representations dominate: discrete codec tokens (section 7), which turn audio into a language-model problem, and continuous latents from an audio VAE (Stable Audio Open: 64 numbers per frame at 21.5 frames a second for 44.1 kHz stereo, {{Evans et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14358}}), which turn it into a diffusion problem, exactly like an image VAE.
