2. Attention across space and time

The problem. Each token must see the tokens it depends on: the rest of its frame (to draw a coherent picture) and the same place in other frames (to move smoothly). Full 3-D attention lets every token attend to every other token in the clip, at a cost that grows with the square of the token count N. For Wan 2.1 1.3B's default clip, N = 32,760, so one attention layer forms N2 = 1.07 billion query-key pairs per head.

The older answer: factorise. Factorised attention splits one layer into spatial attention (each token attends only within its own latent frame) followed by temporal attention (each token attends only to the same position in the other frames). Video Diffusion Models did this inside a U-Net: "we insert a temporal attention block that performs attention over the first axis" with the spatial axes treated as batch ({{Ho et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458}}). Latte compared four ways of splitting the two inside a video DiT; alternating spatial and temporal blocks "performs the best" (a plot, not a table: {{Ma et al. 2024, Fig 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048}}). With f latent frames of s tokens each, the pairs fall from (fs)2 to fs2 + sf2: about 21 times fewer for Wan's default clip.

The current answer: full attention. Every open model from 2024 on uses full 3-D attention (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Mochi, LTX-Video, Wan, Movie Gen). CogVideoX's argument: separated attention "requires extensive implicit transmission of visual information" ({{Yang et al. 2024, Sec 2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072}}): an object that moves diagonally between frames is in a different spatial position, so the temporal layer (same position only) cannot follow it directly. Full attention became affordable because the VAE took most of the tokens out (section 1) and because FlashAttention-style kernels never store the N2 matrix. The price is compute, and section 4 shows attention becomes the largest term.

A latent clip of 6 frames of 6 × 10 tokens (illustrative size). The highlighted query token sits on the moving disc; shaded tokens are the keys it attends to in each layer. Counters use Wan 2.1 1.3B's real default clip (21 latent frames of 30 × 52 tokens).

Positions in 3-D. Each token needs to know its frame, row and column. Most of these models use 3-D RoPE: the head's channels are split into three groups, each rotated by one coordinate. From the configs and code: HunyuanVideo splits a 128-wide head 16 / 56 / 56 (time / height / width, rope_axes_dim), Wan 44 / 42 / 42 (h_dim = w_dim = 2 * (attention_head_dim // 6), {{transformer_wan.py:368|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/transformers/transformer_wan.py#L368}}). How the ids are built, and why rotation gives relative positions, is on the sibling page Latent diffusion and diffusion transformers (section 7) and the {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}} page. Movie Gen is the exception: "factorized learnable positional embedding" ({{Polyak et al. 2024, Sec 3.1.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13720}}).

Text in. Two designs, both inherited from image models: cross-attention to the text encoder's output in every block (Wan with umT5, LTX-Video with T5; LTX reports cross-attention beat joint attention for them, Sec 2.3) or joint attention over text and video tokens together, MMDiT-style (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo's dual-stream then single-stream blocks, Mochi's asymmetric streams with a visual stream "nearly 4 times" the text stream's parameters, {{Mochi card|https://huggingface.co/genmo/mochi-1-preview}}). The text encoders are getting bigger: T5 (CogVideoX, LTX, Mochi), a multimodal LLM (HunyuanVideo), Qwen2.5-VL (HunyuanVideo 1.5), Gemma 3 12B (LTX-2).

Go deeper (optional): DiT, adaLN-Zero, MMDiT and double and single stream blocks on the sibling page Latent diffusion and diffusion transformers; attention kernels on {{Writing kernels: ... FlashAttention|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b9ab89c1bb7238cb6c}}.

3. The video models, from what is published

Below, every open model whose diffusers config could be read, plus Movie Gen (paper only), with numbers from configs, pipeline defaults and papers; then what the closed models disclose. Licences, release dates and current status for all of them, and for Kling, Seedance, Veo, Sora 2, FLUX 3 and Gemini Omni, are on the root's {{Model map|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ab6ade318917bff55}} tab, not repeated here.

Parameters: counted from each config on the meta device (diffusers 0.41.0, no weights), except Movie Gen (stated). Default clip: diffusers v0.41.0 pipeline defaults or the model card; tokens from the formula in section 1 (Movie Gen's 8 × 8 × 8 VAE has no separate first frame). Training data and win rates: the papers' own claims vendor.

  • Movie Gen (Meta, 2024; weights not released) is the most detailed public recipe: a 30B transformer trained with flow matching, "a maximum context length of 73K video tokens, corresponding to a generated video of 16 seconds at 16 frames-per-second" ({{abstract|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13720}}); O(100M) video-text pairs and O(1B) images; training on "up to 6,144 H100 GPUs"; stages of 256 px text-to-image, then 256 px and 768 px joint image and video. Its human evaluation reports a net win rate (wins minus losses) on overall quality of 8.23% against Sora, "within 1-2" standard deviations of ±5.07, and 3.87% against Kling 1.5, "on par" (Table 6) vendor.
  • CogVideoX (Zhipu, 2024): a causal 3-D VAE, v-prediction with zero terminal SNR, an "expert transformer" with separate adaptive LayerNorms for text and video tokens; about 35M clips and 2B images. On an H800 the 5B model takes 113 s for 6 s at 480 × 720 with 50 steps (Table 7) vendor.
  • HunyuanVideo (Tencent, 2024): 13B, dual-stream then single-stream blocks like FLUX, a multimodal LLM as text encoder; its VAE reaches 35.39 dB PSNR on the MCL-JCV video set against 33.22 for CogVideoX 1.5's (Table 1) vendor.
  • LTX-Video (Lightricks, 2024): compression first. A 32 × 32 × 8 VAE with 128 channels ("1:192", "a pixels-to-tokens ratio of 1:8192") leaves so few tokens that a 1.9B model with full attention runs "faster-than-real-time": "2 seconds to generate 121 frames at 768x512 pixels and 20 diffusion steps on an Nvidia H100 GPU" ({{Sec 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00103}}) vendor. The price of that compression is fine detail, so its decoder also performs the last denoising step.
  • Wan 2.1 and 2.2 (Alibaba, 2025): 1.3B and 14B DiTs with cross-attention to umT5, the 1.3B "requiring only 8.19 GB VRAM"; VBench total 86.22% for 14B against 84.28% for Sora (Table 4) vendor. Wan 2.2 splits denoising between two 14B experts, "a high-noise expert for the early stages ... and a low-noise expert for the later stages" (27B total, 14B active per step), and adds a 5B model on a 4 × 16 × 16 VAE ({{card|https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-T2V-A14B}}).
  • HunyuanVideo 1.5 (2025): 8.3B, a 16 × 16 × 4 VAE with 32 channels and no extra patching, sparse "SSTA" attention 1.87 times faster than FlashAttention-3 end to end for 10 s at 720p (card) vendor; about 800M video segments. LTX-2 (2026) generates video and synchronised audio in one model; its own paper gives 14B + 5B in the abstract, 13B + 3B in the conclusion and 19B in Table 1 (the checkpoints are named 19b) ({{arXiv 2601.03233|https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03233}}).
  • Closed models disclose almost nothing. Sora: "Model and implementation details are not included in this report"; it "is a diffusion transformer" trained to predict "clean" patches, on re-captioned videos at native aspect ratios, generating up to a minute. Veo 3's model card: "latent diffusion ... applied to the temporal audio latents, and the spatio-temporal video latents", trained on TPUs with JAX ({{model card|https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Veo-3-Model-Card.pdf}}) vendor; the Gemini API serves 8 s at 24 fps, 720p or 1080p.

Image to video, audio with video. Image-to-video is the same model with the first frame given: the causal VAE encodes it alone (section 1), and its latent replaces or conditions the first latent frame (LTX-Video gives conditioning tokens a small timestep, Sec 2.4). Joint audio and video (Veo 3, LTX-2, Sora 2) adds an audio latent stream with its own 1-D positions and cross-attention between the streams; LTX-2 uses only the time component of RoPE in that cross-attention so sound aligns with frames (Sec 3.1.1) vendor.
