Common mistakes

A frame count that is not 4k + 1 (or 8k + 1)
A causal video VAE needs the first frame plus whole groups. diffusers does not fail, it changes your request: . Ask for 80 frames from Wan and you get 81; from LTX-Video (8 per group) the rule is 8k + 1. Check the frame count of the output, and pick 4k + 1 yourself.
A size the latent grid cannot divide
Height and width must be multiples of the VAE factor times the patch: (Wan, 8 × 2), and 32 for LTX-Video. 720p is 1280 × 720 for Wan 2.1 (720 / 16 = 45) but 1280 × 704 for Wan 2.2's 5B model, whose VAE plus patch divides by 32.

Judging a codec or vocoder by waveform error
Measured: EnCodec at 24 kbps has a lower SNR than Opus at 12 kbps on the same sentence but a closer spectrogram, and its own listening test ranks it far above (section 7). Adversarially trained decoders do not reproduce phase. Use mel distance for quick checks, MUSHRA or MOS for decisions.
Counting cost in frames or parameters instead of tokens
Wan 2.2's 5B model makes 720p clips with fewer tokens (27,280 for 121 frames) than Wan 2.1's 1.3B makes at 480p and 81 frames (32,760), because its VAE plus patch is 32 × 32 in space. And attention is about two thirds of the compute at these sizes, so doubling the duration roughly quadruples it. Count tokens with the formula in section 1, then FLOPs (section 4).
Trusting a headline compression ratio
Mochi's card says its VAE "causally compresses videos to a 128x smaller size, with an 8x8 spatial and a 6x temporal compression to a 12-channel latent space"; those factors give 3 × 8 × 8 × 6 / 12 = 96, not 128 (the card does not show its arithmetic). LTX-Video's "1:192" and Wan 2.2's "64" do reproduce (section 1). Recompute from the factors.
Comparing FVD across settings, or trusting it alone
FVD depends on frame count, resolution, the feature network and the number of samples, and it rewards good single frames over motion: a frozen video can score well (section 9). Compare only at identical settings, add a motion measure (VBench's dynamic degree), and run a human or arena comparison for decisions.
Building on a closed model as if it will stay
Sora's app closed in April 2026 and its API was scheduled to end in September 2026 (secondary reports; the root's Model map tracks status). Keep an open fallback and your own evaluation set, so switching is a measured decision.

Interview questions

Why compress time in the VAE rather than just using bigger patches in the transformer?
Both cut tokens, but the VAE is trained to reconstruct, so it learns what to keep (a moving edge) and what to drop (a static background repeated over 4 frames); a bigger patch is a fixed linear projection that loses detail uniformly. Latte found tubelet-style patches "notably" worse than per-frame patches; LTX-Video moved patching into the VAE for the same reason.
Full 3-D attention or factorised spatial and temporal attention?
Factorised is about 20 times cheaper at Wan's default size but a token can only see its own frame or its own position over time, so diagonal motion must be relayed through layers. Full attention sees everything and is what every 2024 to 2026 open model uses; it is affordable because the VAE leaves tens of thousands of tokens, not hundreds of thousands, and FlashAttention avoids storing N2. Above that, sparse or sliding-tile attention (HunyuanVideo 1.5) is the middle ground.
Why is the first frame encoded on its own?
So that an image is a valid 1-frame video (joint image and video training, and image-to-video conditioning on a clean first latent), and so the encoder can be causal: each later group of 4 frames is encoded with a cache of features from earlier frames only. Hence 4k + 1 frames.
How many tokens is 10 s of 720p video for Wan 2.1 14B, and what does it do to attention?
161 frames at 16 fps become 1 + 160 / 4 = 41 latent frames of 90 × 160, patched 2 × 2: 41 × 45 × 80 = 147,600 tokens, against 75,600 for 5 s. Tokens about double, attention FLOPs about quadruple (3.8 times).
Why residual VQ rather than one big codebook?
Bits add up across stages while storage adds linearly: 8 codebooks of 1,024 give 80 bits per frame with 8,192 vectors, where one codebook would need 280. Quantizer dropout makes every prefix decodable, so one model gives many bitrates and a generator can predict coarse codebooks first.
Codec language model or flow matching for text-to-speech?
Tokens and a language model when you need streaming, voice cloning by prompt, or one model shared with text (dialogue agents, any-to-any). Flow or diffusion over mel or latents when you can generate the whole utterance at once and want speed and robustness (F5-TTS: RTF 0.15, no aligner). The deciding factors are usually latency, length control and how the model is served.
Why can a codec with worse SNR sound better?
SNR compares waveforms sample by sample, so any phase shift counts as error; hearing is mostly insensitive to phase. Neural codecs are trained with spectral and adversarial losses that reward sounding right, so their waveforms differ while their spectrograms match.
Design question: a text-to-video API must return 5 s of 720p in under a minute on one datacenter GPU. Sketch the model and the arithmetic.
Work backwards from tokens. At Wan 2.1 14B's design (8 × 8 × 4 VAE, 2 × 2 patches) 5 s at 720p is 75,600 tokens and about 6,800 TFLOP per pass, so 50 steps with guidance is about 680,000 TFLOP: far beyond a minute on any single GPU. Levers in order of size: a stronger VAE (32 × 32 in space, as Wan 2.2 5B or LTX: 4 to 10 times fewer tokens, quadratic gain in attention); a smaller denoiser (2 to 5B); step and guidance distillation (50 × 2 passes down to 4 to 8 single passes); sparse attention; generate at 480p and upsample. Verify with the calculator, then measure: a published check is LTX-Video's 2 s for 121 frames at 768 × 512 on an H100 with a 1.9B model and 1:8192 compression.
Design question: a real-time voice agent with sub-300 ms response. What audio representation and model?
A causal codec with a low frame rate and semantic first codebook (Mimi: 12.5 Hz, 80 ms frames, 8 codebooks), a model that runs once per frame and generates the codebooks within the frame with a small depth model, full duplex so it can listen while speaking, and text tokens predicted alongside audio for content. Moshi's budget: 80 ms frame plus 80 ms acoustic delay = 160 ms theoretical, 200 ms measured on an L4. A cascade (ASR, LLM, TTS) is simpler to build but adds each stage's latency and loses tone.
How would you get a 1-minute video from a model trained on 5 s clips?
Extend in chunks conditioned on previous frames, and fight drift: train with per-frame noise levels (Diffusion Forcing) or on your own rollouts (Self Forcing), compress old context (FramePack), keep identity anchors (a reference image or keyframes generated first and filled in between). Check with a per-frame identity score over time.

What was measured here, and what transfers

Measured on Apple M1 Pro, all on the CPU (float32, 4 threads, no GPU lock needed): the three codecs at every depth on two clips, the Opus baselines (libopus through PyAV), Wan 2.1's VAE on two 17-frame clips, the FLOP count of Wan 2.1 1.3B on meta tensors, and parameter counts of seven configs. Transfers exactly: codes, reconstructions and their spectral errors and SNRs (same weights, float32; a GPU may differ in the last digits), PSNR per frame, parameter and FLOP counts, token counts. Does not transfer: the timings (a laptop CPU, one clip at a time). The listening players use 32 kbps MP3 copies (the page has no network and must stay small), which add their own coding on top; every number was computed on the decoders' raw output. Not run here: any video denoiser (the smallest, Wan 2.1 1.3B, generates one 480p clip in minutes on a large GPU and would teach less than the arithmetic), and any speech or music generator.
