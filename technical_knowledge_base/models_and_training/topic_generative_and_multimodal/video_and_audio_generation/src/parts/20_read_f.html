6. Sound as data: waveforms and spectrograms

The problem. Audio is a waveform: air pressure measured tens of thousands of times a second (the sample rate: 16 kHz for speech recognition, 24 kHz for most speech generation, 44.1 or 48 kHz for music). The Shaggy sentence is 3.67 s, so 58,720 samples at 16 kHz or 88,080 at 24 kHz: ten seconds of speech is 240,000 numbers, against a few dozen text tokens for the same words. Nothing can attend over that directly, so audio models first shorten the sequence.

The spectrogram. Cut the waveform into short overlapping windows (25 ms in Whisper, every 10 ms), take each window's Fourier transform, keep the magnitude, pool the frequencies into bands spaced like human hearing (the mel scale: fine at low frequencies, coarse at high), and take the log. Whisper: "an 80-channel log-magnitude Mel spectrogram representation is computed on 25-millisecond windows with a stride of 10 milliseconds" ({{Radford et al. 2022, Sec 2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356}}); large-v3 uses 128 bins. The result is an image, 80 numbers per 10 ms, about 30 times shorter than the 16 kHz waveform.

The running example as a waveform and as a log-mel spectrogram

64 mel bands from 0 to 12 kHz (bottom to top) at 20 ms steps, brightness = log power over 70 dB, computed on this page's data (codec/make_assets.py). The source is a 16 kHz recording, so it holds nothing above 8 kHz (the top eighth of the bands holds only faint energy leaked by the resampler, 37 dB below the total). Above it, the waveform at 24 kHz drawn as its minimum and maximum in every 10 ms.

The catch: phase. A magnitude spectrogram throws away the phase of each frequency, so it cannot be played back directly. A vocoder is a network that turns a spectrogram back into a waveform; HiFi-GAN "uses a mel-spectrogram as input and upsamples it through transposed convolutions until the length of the output sequence matches the temporal resolution of raw waveforms", reaching MOS 4.36 against 4.45 for the real recordings, "167.9 times faster than real-time on a single V100 GPU" ({{Kong et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05646}}) vendor. Because phase is free, the vocoder's output can sound right while matching the original waveform poorly sample by sample, which is why waveform error (SNR) is a poor quality measure for generated audio (section 7).

Two ways to go further. Text-to-speech systems from 2017 to 2022 predicted a mel spectrogram and called a vocoder (Tacotron 2: 80 mel channels, 12.5 ms hop, WaveNet vocoder). Today two representations dominate: discrete codec tokens (section 7), which turn audio into a language-model problem, and continuous latents from an audio VAE (Stable Audio Open: 64 numbers per frame at 21.5 frames a second for 44.1 kHz stereo, {{Evans et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14358}}), which turn it into a diffusion problem, exactly like an image VAE.

7. Neural audio codecs and residual vector quantization

What a neural codec is. An autoencoder for sound with a discrete bottleneck. A convolutional encoder strides through the waveform (EnCodec: 320 samples per step, so 24,000 / 320 = 75 frames a second, each a 128-number vector); a quantizer replaces each vector with codebook indices; a decoder turns the indices back into a waveform. It is trained with reconstruction losses on waveform and mel spectrograms plus adversarial losses from discriminators that look at the spectrogram at several scales, which is what makes it sound natural rather than merely close (SoundStream 2021, {{EnCodec 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13438}}, DAC 2023, Mimi 2024).

Why residual quantization. With one codebook, each frame gets log2(1,024) = 10 bits. To spend 80 bits a frame (6 kbps) with one codebook you would need 280 entries. Residual vector quantization (RVQ) uses several small codebooks in a chain: the first codebook quantizes the vector, the second quantizes what the first missed, and so on, so 8 codebooks of 1,024 give 80 bits with 8,192 stored vectors. The image version (RQ-VAE) and the general VQ training tricks are on the sibling page Autoencoders and VAEs; here is the loop as transformers v5.20.1 runs it for EnCodec:

Bitrate scalability for free. During training the number of codebooks is drawn at random per example (quantizer dropout, introduced with {{SoundStream|https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03312}}), so the decoder learns to work from any prefix of the chain. That is why one model serves 1.5 to 24 kbps, and why a token generator can predict the first codebooks first: they carry the most.

Semantic tokens. A codec trained only to reconstruct sound has no reason to put what is said into its first codebook rather than, say, the voice's timbre or the room. Mimi (Kyutai's codec for Moshi) runs at only 12.5 frames a second with codebooks of 2,048 and distils a speech model into its first codebook: "we distill semantic information from a self-supervised model (WavLM ...) into the first level of the RVQ", using "a plain VQ and ... an RVQ with 7 levels in parallel" ({{Défossez et al. 2024, Sec 3.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037}}). At 8 codebooks that is 1.1 kbps and 100 tokens a second, rated MUSHRA 81.0 against 74.3 for SpeechTokenizer at 4 kbps (Table 4) vendor.
