8. Generating audio: token language models and flow

Route 1: a language model over codec tokens. Once audio is tokens, generating it is next-token prediction, and everything known about language models applies (prompts, sampling temperature, KV caches, streaming). The difficulty is that each frame has several codebooks, so the question is in what order to predict them.

  • AudioLM (Google, 2022) used a hierarchy: first semantic tokens (k-means clusters of a speech model's features at 25 a second) for long-range structure, then the first 4 SoundStream codebooks, then the remaining 8, each by its own 0.3B decoder; listeners told its continuations from real speech 51.2% of the time, "not statistically significantly different" from chance ({{Borsos et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03143}}) vendor.
  • VALL-E (Microsoft, 2023) made text-to-speech a language-model task: phonemes plus a "3-second enrolled recording" of a voice in, EnCodec tokens out (8 codebooks at 6 kbps). The first codebook is predicted autoregressively, codebooks 2 to 8 by a second, non-autoregressive model, all trained on 60K hours of audiobooks ({{Wang et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02111}}).
  • MusicGen (Meta, 2023) predicts all 4 codebooks with one transformer using a delay pattern: codebook k is shifted k − 1 steps, so each step emits one token per codebook and still sees the coarser codebooks of the same frame from earlier steps. "This translates 30 seconds of audio into 1500 autoregressive steps" (50 frames a second; {{Copet et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05284}}); trained on 20K hours of licensed music; weights CC-BY-NC.

Three orders for 4 codebooks: the delay pattern keeps one step per frame and still predicts coarse before fine

Moshi (Kyutai, 2024) is full duplex: it "always listens and always generates sound", modelling its own Mimi tokens and the user's as parallel streams plus a text stream it writes as an "inner monologue", 17 tokens per 80 ms step (1 text + 8 + 8). A 7B temporal transformer runs once per step and a small depth transformer generates the codebooks within the step; "a theoretical latency of 160ms, 200ms in practice" ({{Défossez et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00037}}) vendor. Mimi's 12.5 frames a second are what make one big forward pass per 80 ms affordable.

Route 2: flow or diffusion over continuous audio. The image recipe, applied to a spectrogram or an audio latent. F5-TTS trains a DiT with flow matching to fill in masked mel frames; the text is "simply padded with filler tokens to the same length as input speech", with no duration model or aligner; 336M parameters, about 95K hours of speech, "an inference RTF of 0.15" (real-time factor: 10 s of speech in 1.5 s, on an RTX 3090 at 16 steps) ({{Chen et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06885}}) vendor. Stable Audio Open generates "variable-length (up to 47s) stereo audio at 44.1kHz" with a 156M VAE, a 109M T5 text encoder and a 1,057M DiT, trained on Creative Commons recordings; it cannot produce intelligible speech or lyrics ({{Evans et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14358}}).

F5-TTS needs no aligner: text padded to the length of the audio, the missing frames filled by flow

Training: a random span of the mel frames is masked and the model learns the flow that fills it, seeing the rest of the audio and the whole padded text. Inference: the prompt recording is the unmasked part, its transcript plus the new text is padded with filler tokens, and the total length must be chosen before sampling (the "fixed or estimated length" weakness in the table below). Small and fast. Not every speech model is large: Kokoro-82M (StyleTTS 2 with an ISTFTNet decoder, Apache 2.0) and Chatterbox Turbo (350M, MIT; the narration engine of this knowledge base's explainer videos) run comfortably on a laptop.
