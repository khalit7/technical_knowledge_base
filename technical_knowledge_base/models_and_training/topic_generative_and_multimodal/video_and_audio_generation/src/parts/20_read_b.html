Video and audio generators are image generators with time added. A video model compresses a clip into a small latent (a grid of numbers in space and time), cuts it into space-time patches that become transformer tokens, and denoises them with diffusion or flow matching, exactly as an image model does with an image latent. An audio model first turns a waveform into something short enough to model: a spectrogram (a picture of frequency against time) or the discrete tokens of a neural audio codec, then either predicts those tokens one step at a time like a language model or generates a continuous audio latent with diffusion or flow. This page explains both, with the arithmetic that decides what is affordable. Reading time about 40 minutes (words outside widgets at 200 a minute; the folded answers add about 5).

The running examples. For video, the topic's public-domain test image p01_cat (scikit-image's "chelsea", CC0) turned into two 17-frame 256 × 256 clips by sliding a crop across it, slowly (2 pixels per frame) and fast (14 pixels per frame), and sent through Wan 2.1's video VAE (126.9M parameters, Apache 2.0) on the CPU; plus the configs of seven open video denoisers. For audio, one LibriSpeech sentence ("I hope he doesn't work too hard said Shaggy", 3.67 s, utterance 1272-135031-0008, CC BY 4.0) and a 3.5 s synthesised music phrase made on this page, through three open neural codecs: EnCodec 24 kHz, DAC 44.1 kHz and Mimi, at every number of codebooks. Everything measured here ran on an Apple M1 Pro CPU (no GPU needed); the files and scripts are in this page's src/.
