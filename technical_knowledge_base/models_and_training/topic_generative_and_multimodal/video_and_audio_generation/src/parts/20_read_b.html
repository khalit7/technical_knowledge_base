1. Video as space-time latents

The problem. Wan 2.1's default clip (480 × 832 pixels, 81 frames, about 5 s at 16 frames per second) is 97 million numbers, and neighbouring frames are almost the same picture. An image VAE applied to every frame (8 × 8 smaller in space) still leaves 81 latent frames; with 2 × 2 patches that is 81 × 30 × 52 = 126,360 tokens, most of them repeating the frame before. Attention cost grows with the square of the token count, so redundancy across time is the first thing to remove.

A causal video VAE. The image VAE ({{Autoencoders and VAEs|n:3c65c17b0d0d814da93bdb159b7c1e11}}) with 3-D convolutions, so it also shrinks the time axis: every open model on this page compresses time by 4 (or 6, or 8) as well as space by 8 (or 16, or 32). Two details recur:

  • Causal in time. The encoder's convolutions only look at the current and earlier frames (all the padding goes at the start of the time axis, {{CogVideoX, Sec 2.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072}}), so the latent of frame group t never depends on later frames. One model then encodes an image (a 1-frame video), and a decoder can stream.
  • The first frame alone. Wan's VAE encodes frame 1 on its own and then each further group of 4 frames into one latent frame, carrying a cache of features from earlier groups. So a clip must have 4k + 1 frames, and 81 frames become 1 + 80 / 4 = 21 latent frames. Wan's paper: "the first frame is only spatially compressed to better handle the image data" ({{Wan 2025, Sec 4.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20314}}).

Then patches. The denoiser cuts the latent into space-time patches: Wan, HunyuanVideo and Movie Gen use 1 × 2 × 2 (one latent frame, 2 × 2 latent pixels), so each token stands for 4 × 16 × 16 = 1,024 pixels across 4 frames. Sora's report: "spacetime patches which act as transformer tokens. This scheme works for images too since images are just videos with a single frame" ({{OpenAI, Feb 2024, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/20250105124831/https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/}}) vendor. With time compression ct, space compression cs and patch size p, a clip of F frames at H × W becomes


tokens = (1 + (F − 1) / ct) × H / (cs p) × W / (cs p)


For Wan 2.1's default: 21 × 30 × 52 = 32,760, about a quarter of the per-frame count. The pipeline computes the first factor the same way (num_latent_frames = (num_frames - 1) // self.vae_scale_factor_temporal + 1, {{pipeline_wan.py:341|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/wan/pipeline_wan.py#L341}}).

What temporal compression costs, measured. Both 17-frame clips through Wan 2.1's VAE become a latent of 16 × 5 × 32 × 32 (5 = 1 + 16 / 4), 48 times fewer numbers, then are decoded and compared frame by frame with PSNR (peak signal-to-noise ratio in decibels; higher is closer, every 6 dB halves the typical error).

2. Attention across space and time


The problem. Each token must see the rest of its frame (to draw a coherent picture) and the same object in other frames (to move smoothly). Full 3-D attention lets every token attend to every other token in the clip, at a cost that grows with the square of the token count N: for Wan 2.1 1.3B's default clip, N = 32,760, so one layer forms N2 = 1.07 billion query-key pairs per head.


The older answer: factorise. Factorised attention splits one layer into spatial attention (within its own latent frame) followed by temporal attention (the same position in the other frames). Video Diffusion Models did this inside a U-Net: "we insert a temporal attention block that performs attention over the first axis" with the spatial axes treated as batch ({{Ho et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458}}); Latte compared four ways of splitting the two inside a video DiT, and alternating spatial and temporal blocks "performs the best" (a plot, not a table: {{Ma et al. 2024, Fig 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048}}). With f latent frames of s tokens, the pairs fall from (fs)2 to fs2 + sf2: about 21 times fewer for Wan's default clip.


The current answer: full attention. Every open model from 2024 on uses it (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Mochi, LTX-Video, Wan, Movie Gen). CogVideoX's argument: separated attention "requires extensive implicit transmission of visual information" ({{Yang et al. 2024, Sec 2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072}}): an object moving diagonally is at a different position in the next frame, so a temporal layer that looks only at the same position cannot follow it directly. Full attention became affordable because the VAE took most of the tokens out (section 1) and FlashAttention-style kernels never store the N2 matrix; the price is compute, and section 4 shows attention becomes the largest term.
