3. Conditioning: text, a first frame, sound

A video denoiser takes three kinds of condition, and each enters at a different place in the sequence.

Text enters through attention, an image as latent frame 0, sound as a second stream aligned in time

Wan 2.1 1.3B's default clip: 21 latent frames (6 drawn), 512 umT5 text tokens; the audio row follows LTX-2's design. Structure from the papers and configs named below; sizes not to scale.

  • Text. Two designs, both from image models. Cross-attention to the text encoder's output in every block (Wan with umT5, LTX-Video with T5; LTX reports cross-attention beat joint attention for them, Sec 2.3), or joint attention over text and video tokens together, MMDiT-style (CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo's dual-stream then single-stream blocks, Mochi's asymmetric streams with a visual stream "nearly 4 times" the text stream's parameters, {{Mochi card|https://huggingface.co/genmo/mochi-1-preview}}). The text encoders keep growing: T5 (CogVideoX, LTX, Mochi), a multimodal LLM (HunyuanVideo), Qwen2.5-VL (HunyuanVideo 1.5), Gemma 3 12B (LTX-2).
  • Image to video is the same model with the first frame given: the causal VAE encodes it alone (section 1), and its latent replaces or conditions the first latent frame (LTX-Video gives conditioning tokens a small timestep, Sec 2.4). This is the second reason the first frame is encoded on its own.
  • Audio with video (Veo 3, LTX-2, Sora 2) adds an audio latent stream with its own 1-D positions and cross-attention between the streams; LTX-2 uses only the time component of RoPE in that cross-attention so sound aligns with frames (Sec 3.1.1) vendor.

The levers the open models pulled. All of them are a latent transformer with full attention and flow matching (CogVideoX: diffusion with v-prediction); they differ in where they spend tokens and parameters.

  • Compress harder (LTX-Video, 2024): a 32 × 32 × 8 VAE with 128 channels ("1:192", "a pixels-to-tokens ratio of 1:8192") leaves so few tokens that a 1.9B model runs "faster-than-real-time": "2 seconds to generate 121 frames at 768x512 pixels and 20 diffusion steps on an Nvidia H100 GPU" ({{Sec 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00103}}) vendor. The price is fine detail, so its decoder also performs the last denoising step.
  • Split denoising by noise level (Wan 2.2, 2025): two 14B experts, "a high-noise expert for the early stages ... and a low-noise expert for the later stages" (27B total, 14B active per step), plus a 5B model on a 4 × 16 × 16 VAE ({{card|https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-T2V-A14B}}).
  • Sparse attention (HunyuanVideo 1.5, 2025): 8.3B, a 16 × 16 × 4 VAE with 32 channels and no extra patching, sparse "SSTA" attention 1.87 times faster than FlashAttention-3 end to end for 10 s at 720p (card) vendor.
  • Scale the context (Movie Gen, Meta, 2024, weights not released): a 30B flow-matching transformer with "a maximum context length of 73K video tokens, corresponding to a generated video of 16 seconds at 16 frames-per-second" ({{abstract|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13720}}), the most detailed public recipe.

The arc in one line: factorised U-Nets (2022) to diffusion transformers on space-time patches (2024) to flow-matching transformers that also make sound (2025 to 2026). Closed models disclose almost nothing: Sora's report says "Model and implementation details are not included in this report"; Veo 3's model card says "latent diffusion ... applied to the temporal audio latents, and the spatio-temporal video latents", trained on TPUs with JAX ({{model card|https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Veo-3-Model-Card.pdf}}) vendor.

Per-model notes: data, speed and win-rate claims (vendor figures)
  • Movie Gen: O(100M) video-text pairs and O(1B) images; training on "up to 6,144 H100 GPUs"; stages of 256 px text-to-image, then 256 px and 768 px joint image and video. Its human evaluation reports a net win rate (wins minus losses) on overall quality of 8.23% against Sora, "within 1-2" standard deviations of ±5.07, and 3.87% against Kling 1.5, "on par" (Table 6) vendor.
  • CogVideoX (Zhipu, 2024): a causal 3-D VAE, v-prediction with zero terminal SNR, an "expert transformer" with separate adaptive LayerNorms for text and video tokens; about 35M clips and 2B images. On an H800 the 5B model takes 113 s for 6 s at 480 × 720 with 50 steps (Table 7) vendor.
  • HunyuanVideo (Tencent, 2024): 13B, dual-stream then single-stream blocks like FLUX, a multimodal LLM as text encoder; its VAE reaches 35.39 dB PSNR on the MCL-JCV video set against 33.22 for CogVideoX 1.5's (Table 1) vendor. HunyuanVideo 1.5 trained on about 800M video segments.
  • Wan 2.1 (Alibaba, 2025): 1.3B and 14B DiTs with cross-attention to umT5, the 1.3B "requiring only 8.19 GB VRAM"; VBench total 86.22% for 14B against 84.28% for Sora (Table 4) vendor.
  • LTX-2 (2026) generates video and synchronised audio in one model; its own paper gives 14B + 5B in the abstract, 13B + 3B in the conclusion and 19B in Table 1 (the checkpoints are named 19b) ({{arXiv 2601.03233|https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03233}}).
  • Sora: it "is a diffusion transformer" trained to predict "clean" patches, on re-captioned videos at native aspect ratios, generating up to a minute vendor. Veo 3 through the Gemini API serves 8 s at 24 fps, 720p or 1080p.

4. What one clip costs

The arithmetic. One denoiser pass over N video tokens costs about

FLOPs per pass ≈ 2 P N + 4 L N2 D

where P is the parameter count (every weight used once per token: one multiply and one add), L the number of blocks and D the attention width (query-key scores plus the weighted sum of values, each N2D multiply-adds per layer). A clip costs that times the sampling steps times the passes per step (2 with classifier-free guidance). The first term grows with the clip's length, the second with its square. The image version, fitted to eleven image denoisers, is on {{Latent diffusion and diffusion transformers|n:3fc5c17b0d0d814fbcb5e0c8df5289f3}} (section 9).

Where else the time goes, and the levers. The text encoder runs once and the VAE decoder once per clip, but a video decoder is heavy: in the measured run of section 1, decoding 17 small frames took longer than encoding them. The published speed-ups attack each factor: fewer steps by distillation (CausVid distils 50 steps to 4; HunyuanVideo 1.5 ships 8 or 12 step variants), fewer tokens by a stronger VAE (LTX-Video, Wan 2.2's 5B model), cheaper attention by sparsity (HunyuanVideo 1.5's SSTA, 1.87 times end to end, vendor), and lower resolution plus a separate upsampler (Movie Gen to 1080p, HunyuanVideo 1.5's few-step super-resolution).
