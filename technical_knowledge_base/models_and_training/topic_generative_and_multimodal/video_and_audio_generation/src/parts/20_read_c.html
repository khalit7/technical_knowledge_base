1. Video as space-time patches

The problem. A video is a stack of images, and neighbouring frames are almost the same picture. Wan 2.1's default clip (480 × 832 pixels, 81 frames, about 5 s at 16 frames per second) is 97 million numbers. An image VAE applied to every frame separately (8 × 8 smaller in space) still leaves 81 latent frames, and with 2 × 2 patches that is 81 × 30 × 52 = 126,360 tokens, most of them repeating the frame before. Attention cost grows with the square of the token count, so redundancy across time is the first thing to remove.

How it works: a causal video VAE. A video VAE is the image VAE (sibling page Autoencoders and VAEs) with 3-D convolutions, so it also shrinks the time axis. Every open model on this page compresses time by 4 (or 6, or 8) as well as space by 8 (or 16, or 32). Two details recur:

  • Causal in time. The encoder's convolutions only look at the current and earlier frames (all the padding goes at the start of the time axis, {{CogVideoX, Sec 2.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072}}), so the latent of frame group t never depends on later frames. That lets one model encode an image (a 1-frame video) and lets a decoder stream.
  • The first frame alone. Wan's VAE encodes frame 1 on its own and then each further group of 4 frames into one latent frame, carrying a cache of features from earlier groups. So a clip must have 4k + 1 frames, and 81 frames become 1 + 80 / 4 = 21 latent frames. Wan's paper: "the first frame is only spatially compressed to better handle the image data" ({{Wan 2025, Sec 4.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20314}}).


diffusers v0.41.0 models/autoencoders/autoencoder_kl_wan.py, lines : the encode loop. The first call sees only frame 0; each later call sees 4 frames plus the cached features of everything before.



Then patches. The denoiser cuts the latent into space-time patches: Wan, HunyuanVideo and Movie Gen use 1 × 2 × 2 (one latent frame, 2 × 2 latent pixels), so each token stands for 4 × 16 × 16 = 1,024 pixels across 4 frames. Sora's report put it as "spacetime patches which act as transformer tokens. This scheme works for images too since images are just videos with a single frame" ({{OpenAI, Feb 2024, archived|https://web.archive.org/web/20250105124831/https://openai.com/index/video-generation-models-as-world-simulators/}}) vendor. From the configs, the token count of a clip of F frames at H × W is


tokens = (1 + (F − 1) / ct) × H / (cs p) × W / (cs p)


with time compression ct, space compression cs and patch size p. For Wan 2.1's default: 21 × 30 × 52 = 32,760, about a quarter of the per-frame count. The source computes the first factor the same way (num_latent_frames = (num_frames - 1) // self.vae_scale_factor_temporal + 1, {{pipeline_wan.py:341|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/wan/pipeline_wan.py#L341}}).
