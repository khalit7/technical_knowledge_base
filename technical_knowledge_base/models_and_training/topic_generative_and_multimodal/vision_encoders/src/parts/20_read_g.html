Predict: encoder A beats encoder B by 5 points of ImageNet zero-shot accuracy. Does A have better image features?
Not necessarily. Zero-shot accuracy is a property of the image tower, the text tower, the prompts and the preprocessing together. In the Vision lab one silent preprocessing difference (capital letters) moved SigLIP 2 by 50 points on the same image features, and a 16-label linear probe beat every prompt set for both models. To compare image features, use a probe or kNN with the same protocol; to compare zero-shot systems, report the prompts.

How to read a paper's encoder table. Check the resolution (a "B/16" at 384 sees 2.9 times the tokens of one at 224), whether numbers are zero-shot, probe or fine-tuned (MAE ViT-B: 68.0% linear, 83.6% fine-tuned), whether the probe ran on the [CLS], the mean or several layers, and who ran it (DINOv2's Table 4 re-ran every baseline "with our code", which is why its CLIP ViT-L/14 linear score is its own measurement, not OpenAI's). And check what the training data could have seen: DFN, the closest rival in SigLIP 2's table, "uses a network fine-tuned on ImageNet, COCO, and Flickr (i.e. the main benchmarks in Table 1) as a filter" (SigLIP 2, §3.1) published.

Predict: you have 5,000 product photos per category and need pixel-accurate masks of the product with a frozen backbone and a linear head. Which pre-training signal do you want?
Self-distillation with masked patches. Its loss is applied to patch tokens, so they carry semantics at each location: 47.3 ADE20K mIoU linear for DINOv2 B/14 against 39.3 for OpenCLIP ViT-G/14 and 33.3 for MAE ViT-H/14 (DINOv2 Table 10) published. MAE's tokens hold pixel detail but need a trained non-linear head to become semantic; CLIP's are shaped for one pooled vector. Common mistakes, with the real symptoms

Feeding a new resolution without interpolating positions
CLIP raises ValueError: Input image size (336*336) doesn't match model (224*224). and SigLIP 2 RuntimeError: The size of tensor a (441) must match the size of tensor b (196) at non-singleton dimension 1 measured. The fix (interpolate_pos_encoding=True) runs, but moves the embedding (cosine 0.91 to the 224-pixel one for CLIP at 448); fine-tune briefly at the new size if accuracy matters.
Reading MAE patch tokens in the wrong order
transformers' ViTMAEModel always draws a random patch shuffle, even with mask_ratio=0: on one test image only of 196 patches stayed in place and patch 0's output had cosine with the unshuffled run, while the mean over patches was identical () measured. Pooled features are fine; per-patch maps are scrambled. Pass noise=torch.arange(L) (or use ids_restore) to keep the order; src/runs/mae_shuffle.py.
Trusting a FLOP count that skips attention
PyTorch's FlopCounterMode does not count the fused scaled_dot_product_attention kernel, so a counted ViT-B/16 comes out at 16.85 GMACs instead of 17.56, and DINOv2 B/14 at 518 at 117.0 instead of 151.6 measured. Two timm cards use different conventions. State whether the N² terms are in.
Using the last layer or the pooled output by default
For CLIP B/32, block 's mean patch token probes at against for the pooled embedding (section 3). For a frozen-feature task, try a few layers; VLMs read the second-to-last.
Assuming base-size models have no high-norm tokens
SigLIP 2 B/16 has three fixed-position patches with 15 to 23 times the median norm in every image (section 1). They distort attention maps, patch similarities and anything that averages patches. Look at token norms before building dense features on a model; prefer a registers variant where one exists.
Assuming every processor sees the whole image
CLIP's processor resizes the short side and crops the centre square: a 1920 × 1080 frame loses 44% of its area. SigLIP 2's fixed-resolution processor squashes it instead (aspect distorted by 78%). Run the {{Tokens and cost|#t-vecalc}} tab on your image sizes.
Comparing cosines across models, or SigLIP text without lowercasing
Both are in the Vision lab's mistakes: CLIP's matching pairs sit near 0.3, SigLIP 2's near 0.2, so thresholds are per model; SigLIP 2 needs lowercased text (70.2% against 22.3% zero-shot on Fashion-MNIST with one template).

Interview questions

Why does a ViT need position embeddings, and what happens to a learned table at a new resolution?
Attention is permutation-invariant, so without them the model sees a bag of patches. A learned table has one row per training slot; at a new resolution it must be resized (bicubically, in transformers) and the model then runs on positions it never trained on, so outputs drift and a short fine-tune at the new size is the usual fix. Fixed sine-cosine tables, 2-D RoPE and NaFlex-style training avoid the problem.
Doubling the input side of a ViT-B/16: how do tokens and compute change?
Tokens grow 4 times; compute about 4.5 times at 224 to 448, because the projections and MLP (linear in tokens) dominate and only the attention-score term grows 16 times. At several thousand tokens the quadratic term takes over, which is why high-resolution VLM encoders use windowed attention.
What are registers and why do they help?
Extra learned tokens appended to the sequence that carry no patch. Large ViTs otherwise hijack a few low-information patches (about 2% of tokens, roughly 10 times the norm) as scratch space for global computation, which spoils attention and dense maps. With registers the outliers vanish and dense probes improve slightly (DINOv2 ViT-L ADE20K 46.6 to 47.9 mIoU in the paper).
Contrastive, masked-autoencoder or self-distillation pre-training: what does each give you?
Contrastive with captions: a pooled vector aligned with text (zero-shot, text search, VLM backbones) but weaker patch tokens. MAE: strong initialisation for fine-tuning, weak frozen features (68.0% linear for ViT-B on ImageNet). Self-distillation with masking (DINOv2): the best frozen global and dense features, no text.
Why did data curation matter more than the loss for CLIP-style models?
Web pairs are noisy and long-tailed. Filtering by an existing CLIP score (LAION), better filters (DataComp: +6.9 points at fixed compute) and balancing over concepts (MetaCLIP: 65.5% against 57.4% raw at fixed steps for ViT-B/32) each moved zero-shot accuracy by several points at fixed compute, while the SigLIP paper found "performance for both sigmoid and softmax saturate at around 32 k batch size": the sigmoid mainly cut the cost of large batches and helped small ones.
Design: an image search over 10 million product photos, by text and by example image.
Embed every image once with a caption-trained encoder (SigLIP 2 at a resolution matched to product detail; NaFlex if aspect ratios vary), store unit vectors in an approximate-nearest-neighbour index, embed queries with the same model's text tower (lowercased, padded as trained). For "more like this", consider a second index of DINOv2 vectors, which group visually similar items better. Evaluate on a labelled sample with recall@K for paired queries and precision@K for category queries; watch for typographic hits (product text in the photo) and calibrate any score threshold per model. Cost: one encoder pass per image (about 17 GMACs at B/16, 224) offline, one text pass per query online.
Design: choosing the vision encoder for a VLM that must read documents and charts.
Text in images needs resolution: count tokens per page at the encoder's patch size and the projector's merge factor, and prefer a native-resolution encoder (NaFlex SigLIP 2, or a Qwen2.5-VL-style ViT with 2-D RoPE and windowed attention) over a fixed 224 or 336 square that would squash or crop the page. Check high-norm tokens, pick the feature layer (second-to-last is the default), and evaluate the encoder inside the VLM on OCR-heavy benchmarks, since frozen-feature probes do not measure reading.
