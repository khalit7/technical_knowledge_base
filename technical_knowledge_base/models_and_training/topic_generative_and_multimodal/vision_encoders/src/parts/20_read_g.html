7. Evaluating and choosing an encoder

Zero-shot classification is what a text tower buys: embed one sentence per class ("a photo of a sneaker.") and pick the class whose sentence is nearest to the image; the ten text vectors are the classifier's weights. On Fashion-MNIST CLIP B/32 lands between 61.3% and 66.7% over ten prompt sets and SigLIP 2 B/16 between 20.0% and 74.5%, and a linear probe with 16 labels per class beats every prompt set for both (79.0% and 84.0%) measured (root Vision lab). SigLIP 2's 20% is a preprocessing bug: its reference code lowercases text and the transformers processor alone does not; with one template it scores 22.3% on the class names as written ("T-shirt/top", "Trouser") and 70.2% lowercased.

Feeding SigLIP text: case and padding (depth)

CLIP's tokenizer lowercases on its own, so code written for CLIP silently passes capitals to SigLIP 2. SigLIP was also trained on text padded to a fixed 64 tokens (padding="max_length", max_length=64 in the processor call); padding to the longest in the batch, as CLIP code does, changes its text embeddings. The root's loaders and this page pass both explicitly.

Predict: encoder A beats encoder B by 5 points of ImageNet zero-shot accuracy. Does A have better image features?
Not necessarily. Zero-shot accuracy is a property of the image tower, the text tower, the prompts and the preprocessing together: capital letters alone moved SigLIP 2 by 50 points on the same image features. To compare image features, use a probe or kNN with the same protocol; to compare zero-shot systems, report the prompts.
How to read a paper's encoder table (depth)

Check the resolution (a "B/16" at 384 sees 2.9 times the tokens of one at 224), whether numbers are zero-shot, probe or fine-tuned (MAE ViT-B: 68.0% linear, 83.6% fine-tuned), whether the probe ran on the [CLS], the mean or several layers, and who ran it (DINOv2's Table 4 re-ran every baseline "with our code", which is why its CLIP ViT-L/14 linear score is its own measurement, not OpenAI's). And check what the training data could have seen: DFN, the closest rival in SigLIP 2's table, "uses a network fine-tuned on ImageNet, COCO, and Flickr (i.e. the main benchmarks in Table 1) as a filter" (SigLIP 2, §3.1) published. Cosines are not comparable across models either: CLIP's matching pairs sit near 0.3, SigLIP 2's near 0.2, so any threshold is per model.

Predict: you have 5,000 product photos per category and need pixel-accurate masks of the product with a frozen backbone and a linear head. Which pre-training signal do you want?
Self-distillation with masked patches. Its loss is applied to patch tokens, so they carry semantics at each location: 47.3 ADE20K mIoU linear for DINOv2 B/14 against 39.3 for OpenCLIP ViT-G/14 and 33.3 for MAE ViT-H/14 (DINOv2 Table 10) published. MAE's tokens hold pixel detail but need a trained non-linear head to become semantic; CLIP's are shaped for one pooled vector. In the dense-feature stepper, DINOv2 is the one whose colours follow object parts.

Depth: design questions

Design: an image search over 10 million product photos, by text and by example image.
Embed every image once with a caption-trained encoder (SigLIP 2 at a resolution matched to product detail; NaFlex if aspect ratios vary), store unit vectors in an approximate-nearest-neighbour index, embed queries with the same model's text tower (lowercased, padded as trained). For "more like this", consider a second index of DINOv2 vectors, which group visually similar items better. Evaluate on a labelled sample with recall@K for paired queries and precision@K for category queries; watch for typographic hits (product text in the photo) and calibrate any score threshold per model. Cost: one encoder pass per image (about 17 GMACs at B/16, 224) offline, one text pass per query online.
Design: choosing the vision encoder for a VLM that must read documents and charts.
Text in images needs resolution: count tokens per page at the encoder's patch size and the projector's merge factor, and prefer a native-resolution encoder (NaFlex SigLIP 2, or a Qwen2.5-VL-style ViT with 2-D RoPE and windowed attention) over a fixed 224 or 336 square that would squash or crop the page. Check high-norm tokens, pick the feature layer (second-to-last is the default), and evaluate the encoder inside the VLM on OCR-heavy benchmarks, since frozen-feature probes do not measure reading.
Zero-shot accuracy is poor on your domain. What do you try, in order?
Check preprocessing (case, padding, crop); try class wordings and templates and an ensemble; measure a linear probe on a few labels per class (if it is much higher, the features are fine and you need labels or prompt tuning); if the probe is also poor, fine-tune or pick an encoder trained on closer data.
