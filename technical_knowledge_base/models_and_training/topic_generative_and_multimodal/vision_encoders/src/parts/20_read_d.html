2. Positions, and what happens when the resolution changes

Attention is permutation-invariant: without positions the model sees a bag of patches. A learned position table has one row per grid slot of the training resolution. CLIP B/32 has 49 rows for its 7 × 7 grid; give it a 336-pixel image (10 × 10 = 100 patches) and there are no rows for the extra slots. Both libraries refuse, with these exact errors measured:

CLIP ViT-B/32, 336 x 336 input:
ValueError: Input image size (336*336) doesn't match model (224*224).

SigLIP 2 B/16, 336 x 336 input:
RuntimeError: The size of tensor a (441) must match the size of tensor b (196) at non-singleton dimension 1

Pass interpolate_pos_encoding=True and transformers reshapes the table into its 2-D grid, resizes it bicubically to the new grid and flattens it back (interpolate_pos_encoding, models/clip/modeling_clip.py:161, mode="bicubic" at :194; the same code in SigLIP at modeling_siglip.py:137 and DINOv2 at modeling_dinov2.py:87). The cost. It runs, on positions the network never trained on. On the 16 colour test images, CLIP's image embedding at 448 pixels keeps a cosine of only on average (as low as ) with the same image at 224; SigLIP 2's keeps . Caption matching did not change on these easy pairs (CLIP still 15 of 16, SigLIP 2 still 16 of 16) measured. The published fix is to train briefly at the new size, as SigLIP 2 did for its fixed-resolution checkpoints (resumed at 95% of training with the table resized, Tschannen et al. 2025, §2.4.1) published.

Designs that avoid the table. MAE's fixed sine-cosine table can be computed for any grid. FlexiViT randomises the patch size during training so one set of weights works "across a wide range of patch sizes" (Beyer et al. 2022). NaViT packs several images of different sizes and aspect ratios into one sequence and trains on "inputs of arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios" (Dehghani et al. 2023); SigLIP 2's NaFlex variant combines the two, resizing each image so both sides are multiples of the patch size with the aspect ratio kept as closely as possible within a token budget (256 by default). 2-D RoPE (rotary position embedding along rows and columns, {{Positional encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}) puts position into the attention product instead of a table, so any grid works: Qwen2.5-VL's encoder uses it (Qwen2.5-VL report, §2.1.1), and DINOv3 replaced DINOv2's learnable table with RoPE (DINOv3, Table 2).

What the processor does to a real image decides both what the encoder sees and how many tokens it costs. 3. High-norm tokens and registers

Large ViTs learn to dump global information into a few patch tokens in uninformative background areas. Darcet et al. (2023) found these "tokens with roughly 10x higher norm at the output", "around 2%" of the sequence, holding "less information about their original position in the image or the original pixels in their patch" published. They spoil attention maps and dense features. The fix: append a few learned register tokens that carry no image content, so the model has somewhere else to compute; "one register is sufficient to remove artefacts", and they kept 4.

Where the paper found them, and what registers bought (depth)

In DINOv2 the outliers "appear with models larger than and including ViT-Large", and after a third of training. They are not specific to DINOv2: the label-trained DeiT-III B and the caption-trained OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 the authors trained also had outliers, and registers removed them in all three (their Fig. 7). With registers, their DINOv2 ViT-L (ImageNet-22k) rose in linear ADE20K segmentation from 46.6 to 47.9 mIoU and on ImageNet from 84.3% to 84.8% (their Table 2a) published. So the paper's ViT-Large threshold is about DINOv2; a base-size caption-trained model having outliers (below) fits it.

In SigLIP 2 B/16 the same three grid slots (row 0 column 1, row 0 column 13, row 3 column 13) have 15 to 23 times the median patch norm in all 16 images, wherever the object is (a fourth, row 0 column 11, joins them in the five flat synthetic images): three bright fixed slots in every image. Two of them, (0, 1) and (3, 13), are among the four slots where SigLIP 2's attention-pooling head puts 30 to 45% of its weight: the pooled vector reads from the outliers. DINOv2 B has milder ones (up to 4.7 times the median) in 8 of the 16 images, at varying places; DINOv2 B with registers, CLIP B/32 and MAE B have none (at most 1.5 times). Look at token norms before building dense features on a model, and prefer a registers variant where one exists.
