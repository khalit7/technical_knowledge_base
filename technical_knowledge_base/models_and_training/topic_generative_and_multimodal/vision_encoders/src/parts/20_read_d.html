From many tokens to one vector: pooling

The blocks return one vector per token; retrieval and classification want one per image. Three ways are in use, and the choice decides what a "frozen feature" is:

  • [CLS] token. A learned extra token joins the sequence and its final state is the summary. CLIP takes it, applies a LayerNorm, then a linear projection into the space shared with text (pooled_output = last_hidden_state[:, 0, :], models/clip/modeling_clip.py:641, then visual_projection at :941, transformers v5.20.1).
  • Attention pooling (MAP). SigLIP has no [CLS]; a learned query vector (self.probe) attends over all patch tokens once, followed by an MLP (SiglipMultiheadAttentionPoolingHead, models/siglip/modeling_siglip.py:622). The Vision lab found that this head puts 30 to 45% of its weight on the same four slots in every image.
  • Mean of patch tokens. Order-free and parameter-free. MAE's loss is on patch pixels only; its [CLS] is "an auxiliary dummy token" kept so a classifier can be trained on it later, and its paper says MAE "works similarly well without this token (with average pooling)" (He et al. 2021, appendix A.1). On Fashion-MNIST here the two are not alike: MAE's final [CLS] probes at and its mean patch token at (500 labels per class) measured; for DINOv2 the two are within half a point. Where you read matters too: MAE's mean patch token taken before the final LayerNorm probes at 85.2% even with 200 labels per class (section 3's layer chart), against 80.6% after it with 500 labels per class on all 10,000 test images (different splits, same probe). Which readout to use is an empirical question, per model and task. DINOv2's own linear evaluation concatenates [CLS] and the mean.

A vision-language model (VLM) pools nothing: it passes every patch token, usually from the second-to-last block, to the language model. LLaVA-1.5's config says "vision_feature_layer": -2 and its default strategy drops the [CLS] token (selected_image_feature[:, 1:], models/llava/modeling_llava.py:166-168). Section 3 measures why a middle or late layer can beat the last.

The cost. It runs, but the network was never trained on the new positions. The published recipe is to train briefly at the new size: SigLIP 2's fixed-resolution checkpoints were made by resuming at 95% of training, resizing "the positional embedding to the target sequences length" and continuing "at the target resolution with all losses" (Tschannen et al. 2025, §2.4.1) published.


The alternatives, in the order they appeared. MAE's fixed sine-cosine table can be computed for any grid. FlexiViT randomises the patch size during training so one set of weights works "across a wide range of patch sizes" (Beyer et al. 2022). NaViT packs several images of different sizes and aspect ratios into one sequence and trains on "inputs of arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios" (Dehghani et al. 2023); SigLIP 2's NaFlex variant combines the two, resizing each image so both sides are multiples of the patch size with the aspect ratio kept as closely as possible within a token budget (256 by default). 2-D RoPE (rotary position embedding applied along rows and columns) puts position into the attention product instead of a table, so any grid works: Qwen2.5-VL's encoder uses it (Qwen2.5-VL report, §2.1.1), and DINOv3 replaced DINOv2's learnable table with RoPE (DINOv3, Table 2). High-norm tokens and registers


The problem. Large ViTs learn to dump global information into a few patch tokens in uninformative background areas. Darcet et al. (2023) found these "tokens with roughly 10x higher norm at the output", "around 2%" of the sequence, that hold "less information about their original position in the image or the original pixels in their patch"; in DINOv2 they "appear with models larger than and including ViT-Large", and after a third of training published. They spoil attention maps and dense features. The fix: append a few learned register tokens that carry no image content, so the model has somewhere else to compute; "one register is sufficient to remove artefacts", and they kept 4. Their method is not specific to DINOv2: the label-trained DeiT-III B and the caption-trained OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 they trained also had outliers, and registers removed them in all three (their Fig. 7). With registers, their DINOv2 ViT-L (trained on ImageNet-22k) rose in linear ADE20K segmentation from 46.6 to 47.9 mIoU and ImageNet from 84.3% to 84.8% (their Table 2a) published.

SigLIP 2 strongly, DINOv2 B mildly. In SigLIP 2 B/16 the same three grid slots (row 0 column 1, row 0 column 13, row 3 column 13) have 15 to 23 times the median patch norm in all 16 images, wherever the object is (a fourth, row 0 column 11, joins them in the five flat synthetic images): fixed-position outliers, not the content-driven kind the paper describes. Two of the three, (0, 1) and (3, 13), are among the four slots where the Vision lab found SigLIP 2's attention-pooling head puts 30 to 45% of its weight: the pooled vector reads from the outliers. DINOv2 B has milder ones (up to 4.7 times the median) in 8 of the 16 images, at varying places. DINOv2 B with registers, CLIP B/32 and MAE B have none (at most 1.5 times) measured. The paper's ViT-Large threshold is about DINOv2; its own OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 had outliers too, so a base-size caption-trained model having them fits the paper.
