0. In one screen

A vision encoder turns an image into vectors that other models can use: one vector for search and classification, or one per patch for segmentation and for vision-language models. Almost every current encoder is the same vision transformer; what differs is what it was trained to do, and that decides what its vectors are good for.

Captions (CLIP, SigLIP)

Match each image to its web caption. Gives a text tower (zero-shot, text search) and language-aligned tokens, the default VLM backbone. Patch tokens are weaker; data curation drove most of the progress.

Images alone (DINOv2, DINOv3)

Match views of one image, including masked ones. Best frozen features, global and per patch; no text. Built to be used frozen.

Pixel reconstruction (MAE)

Redraw hidden patches. A strong starting point for fine-tuning; frozen vectors are the weakest of the three for kNN and search.

Measured here measured, on Fashion-MNIST (frozen features, 10,000 test images): linear probe with 16 labels per class, CLIP B/32 , SigLIP 2 B/16 , DINOv2 B/14 , MAE B/16 ([CLS]); nearest-neighbour search mAP , , , . Token cost: an R × R image in P-pixel patches is (R/P)² tokens, and compute grows almost linearly with tokens until a few thousand. Three things that break silently: a learned position table at a new resolution, high-norm "register" tokens (SigLIP 2 B/16 has three in every image), and processors that crop or squash.

Every number marked measured was computed for this page on an Apple M1 Pro CPU (float32, 4 threads) on 2026-10-17; the shapes, accuracies and cosines are properties of the released weights and transfer to any hardware up to float rounding; no timing on this page is meant to transfer.
