1. From pixels to tokens

A 224 × 224 colour image is 150,528 numbers; as one token per pixel it would be 50,176 tokens and about 2.5 billion attention scores per head per layer derived: 224² = 50,176; 50,176². The vision transformer (ViT, Dosovitskiy et al. 2020, "An Image is Worth 16x16 Words") groups pixels into square patches instead. Step through it on a real image, and switch the patch size to see the three grids used on this page:

One image becomes a sequence of tokens: smaller patches, many more tokens

The image is the root's test image p01 at the model's 224-pixel input. Token colours are illustrative; shapes and counts are the models' own (configs and the loaded modules) measured.

The patch embedding is one matrix. Libraries write it as a 2-D convolution with kernel and stride equal to the patch size, which is the same arithmetic: cutting with unfold and multiplying by the convolution's weights reshaped into a matrix differs from the model's convolution by 0.0 for CLIP and 6.9e-06 for SigLIP 2 measured. Nothing convolutional happens across patches, so unlike a CNN the ViT must learn locality from data, which is why it beat 2020's best CNNs only after pre-training on hundreds of millions of images ({{ViT page|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa231cfcde6c1954d}}).

The check, and where pooling lives in the code (depth)

The patch-embedding check (root's src/vision/analyse.py) cuts the root's images into patches with unfold, multiplies by the convolution's own weights reshaped into a matrix, and compares with the model's convolution: CLIP's is a 768 × 3,072 matrix, no bias, 2,359,296 parameters (difference 0.0); SigLIP 2's is 768 × 768 plus a bias, 590,592 parameters (6.9e-06, float32 rounding) measured.

Pooling in transformers v5.20.1: CLIP takes pooled_output = last_hidden_state[:, 0, :] (models/clip/modeling_clip.py:641), applies a LayerNorm, then visual_projection (:941) into the space shared with text. SigLIP's head is SiglipMultiheadAttentionPoolingHead (models/siglip/modeling_siglip.py:622), a learned query self.probe that attends once over all patch tokens, then an MLP. LLaVA-1.5 drops the [CLS] token with selected_image_feature[:, 1:] (models/llava/modeling_llava.py:166-168). MAE's [CLS] is "an auxiliary dummy token" and its paper says MAE "works similarly well without this token (with average pooling)" (He et al. 2021, appendix A.1); DINOv2's own linear evaluation concatenates [CLS] and the mean.

What a token costs

Fine detail (small text, thin lines, many small objects) needs more pixels per object, and every extra patch is another token every block must process. An R × R image in P × P patches gives N = (R / P)² tokens. Each block then does four D × D projections per token (queries, keys, values, output), two N × N products for attention (scores, then the weighted sum), and two MLP matrices of D × F per token:

MACs per image ≈ 3P²·D·N + L · ( 4ND² + 2N²D + 2NDF )
N
tokens: (R/P)² patches plus any [CLS] or register tokens
D, F, L
width, MLP hidden width, number of blocks (768, 3,072, 12 for every base model here)
MACs
multiply-accumulates; FLOPs are about twice as many
Predict: a ViT-B/16 goes from 224 × 224 to 448 × 448 pixels. How much more compute per image?
About 4.5×: 197 tokens become 785, four times as many, so the projections and MLP cost four times as much; the attention scores cost sixteen times as much, but they were only 4% of the total. The formula gives 17.56 to 78.49 GMACs, a factor of 4.47 derived.

Only the 2N²D term grows with the square of the token count, and at 224 pixels it is small: 4% of a ViT-B/16 and 23% of DINOv2 B/14 at its 518-pixel training size derived. Most of the cost is linear in tokens, so token count is the number to watch; quadratic attention dominates only at thousands of tokens, which is why high-resolution VLM encoders use windowed attention. The {{Tokens and cost|#t-vecalc}} tab computes the split for any configuration.

Counting FLOPs: one formula, a FLOP counter and two published cards that disagree (depth)

The formula agrees exactly with PyTorch's FLOP counter (torch.utils.flop_counter.FlopCounterMode) on transformers models built from the configs, but only when attention runs as plain matrix multiplies: with PyTorch's fused attention kernel (scaled_dot_product_attention, the default) the counter silently skips the 2N²D term measured, so a counted ViT-B/16 comes out at 16.85 GMACs instead of 17.56, and DINOv2 B/14 at 518 at 117.0 instead of 151.6. Published counts disagree for the same reason: the timm model card for ViT-B/16 at 224 says 16.9 GMACs, which matches the count without attention scores (16.85; with them, 17.56); the card for DINOv2 B/14 at 518 says 151.7, which matches the count with them (151.57; without, 116.98) derived. Two cards from one library, two accountings: state whether the N² terms are in.

From many tokens to one vector: pooling

Search and classification want one vector per image (the stepper's last step shows the three ways):

  • [CLS] token. A learned extra token joins the sequence and its final state is the summary; CLIP then applies a LayerNorm and a projection into the space shared with text.
  • Attention pooling (MAP). SigLIP has no [CLS]; a learned query vector attends over all patch tokens once, followed by an MLP. The root's Vision lab found that this head puts 30 to 45% of its weight on the same four slots in every image (section 3 explains why).
  • Mean of patch tokens. Order-free and parameter-free. The choice matters: MAE's final [CLS] probes at on Fashion-MNIST and its mean patch token at (500 labels per class) measured, while for DINOv2 they are within half a point; and MAE's mean taken before the final LayerNorm probes at 85.2% with only 200 labels per class (section 6), against 80.6% after it. Which readout to use is an empirical question, per model and task.

A vision-language model (VLM) pools nothing: it passes every patch token, usually from the second-to-last block (LLaVA-1.5's config says "vision_feature_layer": -2), to the language model.

A trap: transformers' MAE shuffles its patch tokens even with no masking
ViTMAEModel always draws a random patch shuffle, even with mask_ratio=0: on one test image only of 196 patches stayed in place and patch 0's output had cosine with the unshuffled run, while the mean over patches was identical () measured. Pooled features are fine; per-patch maps are scrambled. Pass noise=torch.arange(L) (or use ids_restore) to keep the order; src/runs/mae_shuffle.py.
