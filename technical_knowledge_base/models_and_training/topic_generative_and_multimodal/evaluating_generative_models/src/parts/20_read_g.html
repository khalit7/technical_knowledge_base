8. Vision-language models: benchmarks with answer keys

A VLM answers questions about images, so it is graded like a language model, against known answers. The benchmarks are named on {{Vision-language models: how images become tokens|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81adbb7be5bc41c4d9d0}} and catalogued with current scores on {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}; the statistics of grading (error bars, paired tests) are on {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}. Two habits carry over to every VLM score: report a text-only (blind) baseline beside it (GeminiPro scores 42.9% on MMMU with no image at all, random 22.1%), and report the yes-rate beside any yes/no accuracy (a model that always says yes gets F1 66.67 on POPE). The sibling page ran a POPE-style probe on SmolVLM-500M (69 of 72 right, yes to 2 of 47 absent objects, both adversarial: "a dog" on the cat with a DOG sticker); that page owns the probe.

What each image benchmark guards against, and the number that shows why (POPE's yes-bias, MMBench's CircularEval, MMStar's questions that need the image, OCRBench's five parts), is owned by {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a#s-halluc}}.

9. Video and audio: the same recipe, other features

FVD ({{Unterthiner et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01717}}) is the Fréchet distance on features of I3D, an action-recognition network trained on Kinetics. It inherits FID's sample-count and preprocessing traps and adds one: {{Ge et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12391}} found that FVD "increases only slightly with large temporal corruption" and that picking motionless samples can "drastically decrease FVD without improving the temporal quality": it mostly scores frames, not motion. Movie Gen's authors state that FVD and IS "do not correlate with human evaluation scores" and use human comparisons instead; VBench ({{Huang et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17982}}) splits video quality into 16 dimensions, each with its own prompts and evaluator. FAD ({{Kilgour et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08466}}) is the Fréchet distance on VGGish audio embeddings; on artificial distortions it correlated 0.52 with human ratings, against 0.39 for the signal-to-distortion ratio. The models these score are on {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}}.
