The idea in one screen

A generator has no right answer to compare against, so it is judged by comparing two sets of images in a feature space: every image metric is a choice of features, a distance between the two clouds of vectors, and a sample count; text-to-image adds a check against the prompt, and in the end people decide.

Floor: what real images score against other real images at the same N, the value that means "nothing is wrong".
MMD: maximum mean discrepancy, a distance between two sets built from a similarity (kernel) between points.
Precision / recall: share of samples that look real; share of the real data the samples cover.

The running example: eight sets, one reference

Everything measured here uses the topic's {{data setup|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ab6ade318917bff55}}: the 10,000 Fashion-MNIST test images are the reference, and eight sets of 10,000 images, from perfect to bad, are scored against them. They come from checkpoints the topic's earlier runs and sibling pages already trained; nothing was trained for this page.

Each strip: the first image of each class, T-shirt to ankle boot (the VAE ignores labels, so its strip is ten random draws; the "half" set only has the first five classes). measured on Apple M1 Pro (MPS); sampling times in the {{Metric comparator|#t-evcmp}} tab.

1. What a metric must do

A classifier is scored image by image. A generator draws a new image from noise, and there is no "correct" image for that noise; the question is whether its output distribution matches the data distribution. That splits into fidelity (does each image look real? a blurry VAE sample fails), coverage (does it make everything the data has, in the right proportions? a model that only draws trousers fails), alignment (does the image match its class label or prompt?) and preference (would a person choose it? which mixes all three with taste). One number cannot answer all four.

Images are compared in a feature space because raw pixels are a bad one: shift a shirt by two pixels and almost every pixel changes. The network decides what "similar" means: Inception-v3 was trained to name 1,000 ImageNet classes, DINOv2 learned without labels, CLIP learned from captions. That is why the same eight sets are ranked differently by different metrics (section 4).

The test every metric must pass first
Score real images against other real images. The result is the metric's floor at that sample count. For FID on this data the floor is at 10,000 images and about at 1,000 (the topic setup's measurement, reproduced in section 3). A model's score means something only next to its floor.
