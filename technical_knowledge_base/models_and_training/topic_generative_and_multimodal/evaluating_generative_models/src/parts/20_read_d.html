4. The alternatives: change the distance, or change the features

Every fix to FID changes one box of the hero: the distance (KID, precision and recall, CMMD's kernel) to drop the Gaussian assumption or the bias, or the features (FD-DINOv2, CMMD's CLIP) to stop seeing the world as ImageNet classes. All were run on the same eight sets; the {{Metric comparator|#t-evcmp}} tab has every value at every N.

KID: an unbiased kernel distance on the same features

MMD compares two sets through a kernel k(x, y), a similarity between two feature vectors: MMD² = mean k within real + mean k within generated − 2 mean k across; identical clouds give 0. KID ({{Bińkowski et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01401}}) uses Inception features and the cubic kernel k(x, y) = (x·y / d + 1)³, and drops the k(x, x) terms from the within-set means. That makes it unbiased: its average over draws is the true value at any N, so the floor sits at 0 and only the error bar shrinks with N. The KID paper's example, CIFAR-10 train against test: "at n=2 000, when the KID estimator is essentially always 0, the FID estimator is still quite large" (section 4). torch-fidelity computes it on 100 random subsets of 1,000 images per side (defaults.py lines 20 and 21, v0.4.0) and reports mean and standard deviation; the score is usually printed times 1,000.

Precision and recall: two numbers for the two failures

{{Kynkäänniemi et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06991}} estimate each set's region of feature space as a union of balls, one around every sample, with radius equal to its distance to its k-th nearest neighbour (k = 3). Precision is the share of generated samples inside the real region (realistic?); recall is the share of real samples inside the generated region (covered?). The paper used VGG-16 features and 50,000 images per set; here, Inception and DINOv2 features at 10,000.

These are the two failures FID merged in section 2. Their weakness: one real outlier swells the real region, and fakes clustered near it count as precise; {{Naeem et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09797}} proposed density and coverage to fix that.

FD-DINOv2: the same formula on better features

{{Stein et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04675}} ran what they call the largest human study of generative-model realism so far and found "no strong correlation between human evaluation and any common metric computed in the Inception representation space outside of the simplistic CIFAR10 dataset"; replacing Inception-V3 with DINOv2 ViT-L/14 (self-supervised, 142M images without labels) "recovers the correlation". Diffusion models fooled their raters more often yet were ranked worse than GANs by FID. EDM2 ({{Karras et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02696}}) reports both, FID 1.81 and FDDINOv2 33.09 for EDM2-XXL with guidance on ImageNet-512 (Tables 2 and 5), and the two want different settings (best guidance 1.4 for FID, 1.9 for FDDINOv2), so they tuned each separately. FD values live on DINOv2's scale: an FDDINOv2 of 33 is not "worse" than an FID of 1.81 from the same model.

CMMD: a kernel distance on CLIP features

{{Jayasumana et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09603}} ("Rethinking FID") replace both parts: L2-normalised CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 px embeddings, and MMD with a Gaussian kernel exp(−‖x − y‖² / 2σ²), σ = 10, times 1,000. FID needed "more than 20,000 images" to settle on COCO-30K subsets while "CMMD provides consistent estimates even with small image sets"; on PartiPrompts raters preferred a fully trained Muse model 92.5% to 6.9% over an early-stopped one while FID preferred the early-stopped one, and CMMD agreed with the raters.

Correction: the CMMD paper and its code disagree
The paper presents the unbiased estimator (its Eq. 4 leaves out the diagonal k(x, x) terms). The official code averages over the full matrix, diagonal included, and its docstring says it "implements the minimum-variance/biased version of the estimator" (google-research cmmd/distance.py, read 2026-10-17). Biased estimates carry a floor that falls with N: the real set scores at 250 and at 10,000 here (CLIP B/32). And with the code's σ = 10 on unit vectors, CMMD ≈ 10 · ‖μg − μr‖²: it mostly compares the two mean embeddings.
Why CMMD reduces to a distance between means, and the code lines

On unit vectors ‖x − y‖² is at most 4, so with σ = 10 the kernel never leaves the range exp(−0.02) to 1, where it is almost a straight line: exp(−d²/200) ≈ 1 − d²/200. Put that into the MMD and every term cancels except the means, so CMMD ≈ 1,000 · ‖μg − μr‖² / σ² = 10 · ‖μg − μr‖². Checked at 10,000: .

Code: k_xx = jnp.mean(jnp.exp(-gamma * (...))) over the full matrix (cmmd/distance.py, last changed in commit 5b09c22: docstring line 35, the means on lines 59 to 90, _SIGMA = 10 and _SCALE = 1000 on lines 25 and 28); the embeddings are L2-normalised by the scenic CLIP model it calls.
