4. The alternatives: change the distance, or change the features

Every fix to FID changes one of its parts. Change the distance (KID, CMMD's kernel, precision and recall) to drop the Gaussian assumption or the bias; change the features (FD-DINOv2, CMMD's CLIP) to stop seeing the world as ImageNet classes. All five were run on the same eight sets; the {{Metric comparator|#t-evcmp}} tab has every value at every N.

KID: an unbiased kernel distance on the same features

MMD (maximum mean discrepancy) compares two sets through a kernel k(x, y), a similarity between two feature vectors: MMD² = mean k within real + mean k within generated − 2 mean k across. If the clouds are identical, the within and across similarities balance and it is 0. KID ({{Bińkowski et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01401}}) uses Inception features and the cubic kernel k(x, y) = (x·y / d + 1)³, and drops the k(x, x) terms from the within-set means. That makes the estimate unbiased: its average over many draws is the true value at any N, so the real-vs-real floor sits at 0 and only the error bar shrinks with N. The KID paper's own example: on CIFAR-10 train against test, "at n=2 000, when the KID estimator is essentially always 0, the FID estimator is still quite large" (section 4). torch-fidelity computes it on 100 random subsets of 1,000 images per side (defaults.py lines 20 and 21, v0.4.0) and reports their mean and standard deviation; the score is usually printed times 1,000.

The cost is noise: one draw at small N can even come out negative (an unbiased estimate of a value near zero has to). Report the standard deviation across subsets, as torch-fidelity does, and compare models with the same subset size.

Precision and recall: two numbers for two questions

{{Kynkäänniemi et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06991}} estimate each set's region of feature space as a union of balls, one around every sample, with radius equal to its distance to its k-th nearest neighbour (k = 3). Precision is the share of generated samples that fall inside the real region (are the images realistic?); recall is the share of real samples inside the generated region (does the model cover the data?). The paper used VGG-16 features and 50,000 images per set; here, Inception and DINOv2 features at 10,000.

These are the two failures FID merged in section 2, now apart. Precision and recall have their own weakness: one real outlier swells the real region, and fakes clustered near it count as precise; {{Naeem et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09797}} proposed density and coverage to fix that.

FD-DINOv2: the same formula on better features

{{Stein et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04675}} ran what they call the largest human study of generative-model realism so far and found "no strong correlation between human evaluation and any common metric computed in the Inception representation space outside of the simplistic CIFAR10 dataset"; replacing Inception-V3 with DINOv2 ViT-L/14 (self-supervised, trained on 142M images without labels) "recovers the correlation". Diffusion models fooled their human raters more often yet were ranked worse than GANs by FID. EDM2 ({{Karras et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02696}}) reports both: FID 1.81 and FDDINOv2 33.09 for EDM2-XXL with guidance on ImageNet-512 (Tables 2 and 5), and found that the two metrics want different settings (best guidance 1.4 for FID, 1.9 for FDDINOv2), so they tuned each separately. FD values live on DINOv2's scale, not FID's: never compare an FDDINOv2 with an FID.

Here: DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (the model pinned by the sibling page {{Vision encoders: ViT, CLIP and SigLIP|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c99f60ceb2f02cf5ea}}; the paper's L/14 was not run), its own processor, the final [CLS] vector. CMMD: a kernel distance on CLIP features

{{Jayasumana et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09603}} ("Rethinking FID") replace both parts: CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 px embeddings, L2-normalised, and MMD with a Gaussian kernel exp(−‖x − y‖² / 2σ²), σ = 10, times 1,000. They report that FID needed "more than 20,000 images" to settle on COCO-30K subsets while "CMMD provides consistent estimates even with small image sets", and that on PartiPrompts raters preferred a fully trained Muse model 92.5% to 6.9% over an early-stopped one while FID preferred the early-stopped one; CMMD agreed with the raters.

Here: the official code's kernel, scale and estimator, on CLIP ViT-B/32 embeddings (the topic's pinned CLIP) for the full sweep, because the paper's ViT-L/14 at 336 px ran at about 10 images per second on this laptop (the 10,000 test images took 1,022 s, measured), too slow for 100,000 images. The paper and its code disagree
The paper presents the unbiased estimator (its Eq. 4 leaves out the diagonal k(x, x) terms). The official code computes k_xx = jnp.mean(jnp.exp(-gamma * (...))) over the full matrix, diagonal included; its docstring says it "implements the minimum-variance/biased version of the estimator" (google-research cmmd/distance.py read on 2026-10-17, last changed in commit 5b09c22: docstring line 35, the means on lines 59 to 90, _SIGMA = 10 and _SCALE = 1000 on lines 25 and 28; the embeddings are L2-normalised by the scenic CLIP model it calls). Biased estimates carry a floor that falls with N. Measured here with the code's estimator: the real set scores at 250 and at 10,000 (CLIP B/32 features). A second detail: on unit vectors ‖x − y‖² is at most 4, so with σ = 10 the kernel never leaves the range exp(−0.02) to 1, where it is almost a straight line: exp(−d²/200) ≈ 1 − d²/200. Put that into the MMD and every term cancels except the means, so CMMD ≈ 1,000 · ‖μg − μr‖² / σ² = 10 · ‖μg − μr‖², the squared distance between the two mean embeddings (checked at 10,000: ). With the code's σ, it mostly compares averages.
