8. Vision-language models: benchmarks with answer keys

A VLM answers questions about images, so it is graded like a language model: questions with known answers. The catalogue (MMMU, MathVista, ChartQA, DocVQA, video and audio suites) with current scores lives on {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}; the statistics of grading (error bars, paired tests) on {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}. 9. Video and audio, in brief

The same recipe with other feature extractors. FVD ({{Unterthiner et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01717}}) is the Fréchet distance on features of I3D, an action-recognition network trained on Kinetics. It inherits FID's sample-count and preprocessing traps and adds one of its own: {{Ge et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12391}} found that FVD "increases only slightly with large temporal corruption" and that picking motionless samples can "drastically decrease FVD without improving the temporal quality": it mostly scores frames, not motion. Movie Gen's authors state that FVD and IS "do not correlate with human evaluation scores" and use human comparisons instead; VBench ({{Huang et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17982}}) splits video quality into 16 dimensions, each with its own prompts and evaluator. FAD ({{Kilgour et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08466}}) is the Fréchet distance on VGGish audio embeddings; on artificial distortions it correlated 0.52 with human ratings, against 0.39 for the signal-to-distortion ratio. The sibling page {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}} covers the models these score.

Common mistakes

Comparing FIDs computed at different sample counts
The real set alone goes from at 250 samples to at 10,000 (section 3). Use 50,000 against full training-set statistics when you can, the same N always, and an extrapolated FID∞ or KID when you cannot.
Fewer samples than feature dimensions
With N below 2,048 the Inception covariance is singular (below 128 for the classifier's features). Scoring 20 samples in the classifier space during this page's CPU smoke test, the topic's own fid.py printed LinAlgWarning: Matrix is singular. The result might be inaccurate or the array might not have a square root. The number still comes out, and means little.
Taking the first N samples of a class-sorted set
Saving samples as JPEG, or resizing one set differently
Real images saved at JPEG 75 score against the PNG reference, where nothing is wrong scores . Save PNG; resize both sets with the same function; say which (section 5).
Comparing FID with FD-DINOv2, KID or CMMD values
Each lives on its own scale and features: an FDDINOv2 of 33 (EDM2) is not "worse" than an FID of 1.81 from the same model. Compare like with like; compare rankings across metrics.
Calling KID or CMMD "unbiased" without checking the code
KID in torch-fidelity drops the diagonal (unbiased); the official CMMD code keeps it (biased), unlike the CMMD paper. The real set's CMMD here falls from to as N grows.
Reading one low FID as "better images"
Strong guidance can lower a distance while losing variety, and a perfect but narrow model can score worse than a blurry broad one (section 2). Report precision and recall, or look at samples per class.
Using CLIPScore as an image-quality score
It measures agreement with the prompt as CLIP sees it; it misses missing objects and wrong counts when the rest of the scene matches (section 6). Pair it with question-based checks and a fidelity metric.
Mixing CLIPScore conventions
2.5 · cos with ViT-B/32 (Hessel et al.), 100 · cos with ViT-L/14 (many text-to-image papers), with or without "A photo depicts": three scales for one idea. The same SD image scores .
Trusting a VLM benchmark score without a blind baseline
GeminiPro scores 42.9% on MMMU with no image. Report the text-only score, and the yes-rate for yes/no questions (section 8).
Optimising a generator against a reward model and reporting the same reward model
The score goes up by construction. Hold out a different reward model, a question-based check and a human comparison.

Interview questions

What does FID compute, and what are its two terms?
Fit a Gaussian (mean, covariance) to Inception-v3 pool features of real and of generated images; FID = ‖μr−μg‖² + Tr(Σr+Σg−2(ΣrΣg)1/2). The mean term measures average content; the trace term measures spread (diversity). Lower is better.
Why is FID biased, which way, and what do you do about it?
The covariance is estimated from N samples and enters through a matrix square root; estimation noise adds distance, so FIDN is too large, by roughly K/N with a model-dependent K. Use the same and large N (50k), extrapolate in 1/N (Chong and Forsyth), or use KID, which is unbiased.
FID went from 9.1 to 8.4 after your change. Is it better?
Not yet: same reference set, N, resize, file format and code? Several seeds? A 0.7 difference between single runs can be noise. Check precision/recall and samples, and a second metric with different features (FD-DINOv2 or CMMD).
How do precision and recall differ from FID?
They separate fidelity (share of generated samples inside the real manifold) from coverage (share of real samples inside the generated manifold), using k-NN balls. Guidance typically raises precision and lowers recall; mode dropping lowers recall.
Why might FD-DINOv2 or CMMD be preferred to FID today?
Inception is an ImageNet classifier: its features are tied to ImageNet classes (gameable, Kynkäänniemi et al. 2022) and correlate poorly with human judgments beyond simple data (Stein et al.). DINOv2 (self-supervised) and CLIP features track human judgments better; CMMD also drops the Gaussian assumption. Check the estimator: CMMD's code is biased.
How would you evaluate prompt following for a text-to-image model?
Several layers: question-based checks (GenEval with a detector for counts, colours, positions; TIFA/DSG questions or VQAScore with a strong VLM), CLIPScore only as a coarse signal, a fixed prompt set covering composition and text rendering, human side-by-side comparisons on a sample, with error bars across prompts and seeds.
Design: you ship a new image model weekly. Design its evaluation.
Fixed, versioned harness. Fidelity and diversity: FD-DINOv2 and FID (torch-fidelity, PNG, clean resize) on 30k to 50k samples against a fixed reference, plus precision/recall, with seed spread. Prompt following: GenEval-style detector checks and VQA-based scores on a fixed prompt suite, broken down by skill (counting, text, spatial). Preference: a held-out reward model that is never the training reward, plus a weekly blind human side-by-side against the current model (a few hundred pairs, Bradley-Terry with intervals). Safety and regressions: a red-team prompt set. Gate releases on human preference, not on any single automatic metric; track automatic metrics for regressions between human rounds.
Design: how would you measure and reduce VLM hallucination?
Measure with POPE-style balanced yes/no questions (random, popular, adversarial absent objects; report yes-rate and F1), a caption hallucination rate, and a blind baseline. Reduce with higher resolution or more image tokens, negative examples ("no") in instruction data, preference tuning against hallucinated answers; re-measure on held-out images.
What does FVD miss?
Motion: it is dominated by per-frame appearance; temporally corrupted or motionless videos barely change it or even improve it (Ge et al. 2024). Use video-specific dimensions (VBench) and human comparisons.
