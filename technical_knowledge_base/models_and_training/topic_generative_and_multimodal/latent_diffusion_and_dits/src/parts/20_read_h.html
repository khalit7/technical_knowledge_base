Common mistakes

Reading attention_head_dim in an SD 1.x or 2.x config as the head width
In UNet2DConditionModel configs it is the number of heads (SD 1.5: 8 heads of width 40, 80 and 160; SDXL's [5, 10, 20]: heads of width 64). diffusers keeps the old name for "40,000+ configurations" and maps it at {{unet_2d_condition.py line 254|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_2d_condition.py#L254}}. Transformer configs (SD3Transformer2DModel, FluxTransformer2DModel) mean what they say: 64 and 128.
Writing prompts longer than 77 tokens for a CLIP-conditioned model
Everything past position 77 is dropped, with only a logged warning (the real one is in section 3). SD 1.5 and SDXL never see the end of a long prompt; SD3 sees it through T5 only. Fixes: shorten, put what matters first, or use a long-prompt helper that encodes chunks of 75 and concatenates them (the model then sees several start and end tokens, which it was not trained on). A T5- or LLM-conditioned model accepts 256 to 512 tokens.
Sampling far from the training resolution
SD 1.5 at 1024 pixels (section 8) is a real run on this page: asked for one puppy on a canvas four times its training area, it drew four puppies of the usual size. It is not a bug in the sampler; it is the denoiser's learned statistics. Use a model trained near the target size and aspect ratio (bucketing), or generate at the native size and upscale (a second img2img pass at the high resolution is the common recipe).
Comparing models by parameter count when the question is cost
DiT-XL/8 and DiT-XL/2 have the same 675M parameters and differ 16 times in compute and 5 times in FID. FLUX.1 [dev] has 1.5 times SD3.5 Large's parameters but costs about 1.2 times as much per image because it needs one pass per step instead of two. Count FLOPs per pass, multiply by steps and passes (section 9).
Trusting a parameter count without knowing what was counted
Three cases found while counting for this page: SD3's checkpoints include a 192 × 192 position table that diffusers holds as a buffer (56.6M numbers for SD3 Medium, 89.7M for SD3.5 Large, section 5); diffusers' port of DiT-XL/2 has 749,826,464 parameters against the authors' 675,129,632, because its conversion script copies the timestep and class embedders into every one of the 28 blocks ({{convert_dit_to_diffusers.py line 34|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/scripts/convert_dit_to_diffusers.py#L34}}), 27 extra copies of 2,777,472 parameters, and holds the position table as a buffer where the authors' code has a frozen parameter (256 × 1,152 = 294,912 at 256 pixels): 27 × 2,777,472 − 294,912 = 74,696,832, the whole gap; and model cards round ("12 billion" for 11.9B). State what a number includes.
Running classifier-free guidance on a guidance-distilled model
FLUX.1 [dev]'s guidance_scale is an input to the network (an embedding, default 3.5), not the CFG weight; the diffusers pipeline runs one pass per step unless true_cfg_scale is set above its default 1.0 with a negative prompt, which doubles the cost and was not what the model was distilled for. [schnell] has no guidance embedder at all (10,229,760 fewer parameters, section 6).
Feeding a model text features from the wrong layer
SD 1.5 was trained on CLIP's last layer, SDXL on the penultimate layers of two encoders ({{line 414|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion_xl/pipeline_stable_diffusion_xl.py#L414}}), Z-Image on Qwen3's second-to-last layer. The two layers differ: measured here on SD 1.5's encoder, the cosine between the last and penultimate layer's output is across the prompt's positions. "Clip skip" settings in user interfaces swap these; on a model trained with one, the other shifts every cross-attention input.
Running a DiT at a size its position table was not built for
The authors' DiT code builds its sine-cosine table for one latent size; giving it another fails in the patch embedding before attention is reached. The real error, from counting DiT-XL/2 at 1024 pixels on this page before rebuilding the model per size: 
AssertionError: Input height (128) doesn't match model (32).
Rebuild (or interpolate) the table for the new grid; RoPE models (section 7) have no table to rebuild, but still need training at the new size.
Expecting a small transformer to beat a U-Net on a small budget
The tiny experiment in section 4: at equal training compute the topic's 1M-parameter U-Net beat a 4M-parameter DiT by a wide margin. DiT's advantage is in how it scales with compute and how well it fits modern hardware and systems, not in data efficiency at toy scale.

Interview questions

Why do most image generators run diffusion in a VAE latent instead of on pixels?
A denoiser runs 20 to 50 times per image, so its cost per pass dominates. An f = 8 VAE cuts positions 64 times (48 times fewer numbers for SD's 4 channels), and attention cost falls with the square: counted here, SD 1.5's U-Net would cost about 680 times more per pass on a 512 × 512 pixel grid. The price is a quality ceiling set by the decoder and the need to normalise the latent (scaling factor).
Where are a U-Net's parameters, and where are its FLOPs?
Parameters at the low-resolution, high-channel levels (weights grow with channels squared); FLOPs at the high-resolution levels (cost grows with positions). For SD 1.5 at 512: the two 64 × 64 stages do 38% of the FLOPs with 3.4% of the weights, while up_blocks.1 (16 × 16) holds 258M parameters.
What is adaLN-Zero and why initialise it at zero?
The conditioning vector (timestep plus class or pooled text) is mapped per block to a shift and scale for each LayerNorm and a gate on each residual branch. Zero-initialising that mapping makes every block the identity at the start, so a deep stack trains stably from a well-behaved function; in DiT's ablation it beat plain adaLN (FID 19.5 vs 25.2), cross-attention (26.1) and in-context tokens (35.2). Its cost: the modulation layer is about a third of each block's parameters, which PixArt cut with adaLN-single and FLUX.2 with shared modulation.
Cross-attention or joint attention (MMDiT) for text: what are the trade-offs?
Cross-attention: text is read-only, cost N × T per layer, a cheap way to add text to any backbone. Joint attention: one softmax over image and text, so text tokens are updated by the image and both modalities interact symmetrically; cost (N + T)2 and a second set of weights per block (or shared weights in single-stream blocks). SD3 found MMDiT better than cross-attention DiT and UViT at matched settings; nearly every 2025 to 2026 open model uses joint attention.
Why did the DiT paper report Gflops rather than parameters?
Because quality tracked compute per forward pass, not parameters: shrinking the patch size quadruples tokens and Gflops without changing the weights, and FID improved accordingly. Their "Gflops" are multiply-accumulates (counted here at exactly half the FLOPs).
FLUX.1 [dev] against [schnell]: what differs in the weights and in the cost?
[dev] has a guidance embedder (10.2M parameters) because it was distilled to take the guidance scale as an input: one pass per step, default 28 steps. [schnell] was distilled further (latent adversarial diffusion distillation, per its card) to 1 to 4 steps without guidance. Same transformer otherwise: 19 double and 38 single-stream blocks.
How would you support many aspect ratios and resolutions?
Bucketed training at roughly constant area (similar token counts per batch), size and crop conditioning or their equivalent, position encodings that extend (RoPE or a large cropped table), a resolution-dependent timestep shift, and progressive training from low to high resolution. Check by sampling at every bucket and at an unseen size.
Design question: a text-to-image service must return 1024 × 1024 images in about a second on one datacenter GPU. Sketch the model and the arithmetic.
Work backwards from compute. If the GPU sustains S TFLOP/s on the denoiser, one second buys S TFLOP. A FLUX.1-sized model costs 74 TFLOP per pass at 1024 (counted), so even 4 passes need 300 TFLOP: plausible only with a few-step distilled model and a fast kernel. Levers in order of size: (1) steps and passes: a distilled few-step model (adversarial or consistency distillation) with guidance distilled in, 1 pass per step; (2) tokens: a deeper-compression VAE (f = 16 or 32 with more channels, as in Sana's DC-AE) cuts tokens 4 to 16 times; (3) attention cost: linear or windowed attention at high resolution; (4) model size: a 2 to 5B denoiser rather than 12B; (5) text encoder: a 2 to 4B LLM or T5, run once and cached for repeated prompts. Then measure: the calculator gives FLOPs, but kernel efficiency decides the seconds. Keep a quality check (a preference or VQA-based metric) in the loop, since every lever trades quality.
Why does SD 1.5's cross-attention put so much weight on the start token?
CLIP's text encoder is causal, so the start token's output is the same constant for every prompt. Softmax attention has to put its weight somewhere; a position whose content no word concerns puts it on that constant slot, which acts as a no-op (an attention sink). Measured here: about of the 16 × 16 layers' cross-attention for the bicycle prompt.

What transfers from this laptop

Measured on Apple M1 Pro: SD 1.5 in float16 with attention slicing on the laptop's GPU (MPS), one process, peak memory ; the tiny models in float32 for 4 to 32 minutes each; all parameter and FLOP counts on CPU without weights. What transfers exactly: parameter counts, FLOPs per pass and their breakdowns (shape arithmetic, identical on any device), the cross-attention patterns up to float16 rounding, and the token counts behind every cost. What transfers in direction only: the tiny results (U-Net ahead of DiT at small compute, smaller patches better at equal steps; one seed each, a 28 × 28 dataset, a classifier-space FD). What does not transfer: seconds. MPS with attention slicing runs attention far less efficiently than a datacenter GPU with a fused attention kernel (FlashAttention), so the share of time spent in attention, the achieved TFLOP/s and the relative cost of the large-resolution passes all change; the FLOP shares on this page are the device-independent part.
