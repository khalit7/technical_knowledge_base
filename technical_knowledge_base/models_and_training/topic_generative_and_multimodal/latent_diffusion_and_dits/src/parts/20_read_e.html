4. The diffusion transformer (DiT): patches, adaLN-Zero, and scaling with compute

The problem. The U-Net is a hand-built stack of different blocks; a transformer is one block repeated, with well-understood scaling and years of systems work behind it. Peebles and Xie asked whether a plain transformer can replace the U-Net inside latent diffusion, and what to scale ({{Scalable Diffusion Models with Transformers, Dec 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748}}).

How it works. Four pieces, all visible in the animation of section 2 (switch it to DiT):

  1. Patchify. Cut the I × I × C latent into p × p patches and embed each linearly (a convolution with kernel and stride p): T = (I / p)2 tokens of width d. At 256 × 256 the latent is 32 × 32 × 4, so p = 2 gives 256 tokens; at 512, 1,024 tokens.
  2. Positions. Add a fixed 2-D sine-cosine embedding (as in ViT and MAE): the tokens carry where they came from (section 7).
  3. N identical DiT blocks with adaLN-Zero conditioning (below).
  4. Unpatchify. A final adaptive LayerNorm and a linear layer map each token back to p × p × 2C numbers (the noise and, for DiT, a learned variance), reshaped into the latent grid ({{models.py line 218|https://github.com/facebookresearch/DiT/blob/ed81ce2229091fd4ecc9a223645f95cf379d582b/models.py#L218}}).

adaLN-Zero. The timestep and class embeddings are summed into one vector c. Each block feeds c through SiLU and one linear layer to 6d numbers: a shift and scale for the LayerNorm before attention and before the MLP (adaptive LayerNorm, the same trick as FiLM and StyleGAN), and a gate that multiplies each branch before its residual add. That linear layer is initialised to zero, so every gate starts at 0 and every block starts as the identity. The authors' code, as it ran here for counting ({{models.py lines 119 to 121|https://github.com/facebookresearch/DiT/blob/ed81ce2229091fd4ecc9a223645f95cf379d582b/models.py#L119}} and {{207|https://github.com/facebookresearch/DiT/blob/ed81ce2229091fd4ecc9a223645f95cf379d582b/models.py#L207}}):

def forward(self, x, c):
    shift_msa, scale_msa, gate_msa, shift_mlp, scale_mlp, gate_mlp = self.adaLN_modulation(c).chunk(6, dim=1)
    x = x + gate_msa.unsqueeze(1) * self.attn(modulate(self.norm1(x), shift_msa, scale_msa))
    x = x + gate_mlp.unsqueeze(1) * self.mlp(modulate(self.norm2(x), shift_mlp, scale_mlp))
    return x

adaLN-Zero beat the other three ways of bringing in the condition

Table 4 of the paper: each a DiT-XL/2 trained 400K steps, FID-50K without guidance, lower is better (published, the authors' runs). In-context: c appended as two extra tokens. Cross-attention: an extra cross-attention layer reading c, "roughly a 15% overhead" in Gflops. adaLN: regress shift and scale only (4d outputs), no gates. adaLN-Zero: shift, scale and zero-initialised gates (6d). Scaling: what to grow. DiT's central result: across 12 models (four widths and depths times three patch sizes), FID after 400K steps follows the forward-pass compute, not the parameter count. Shrinking the patch from 8 to 2 leaves the parameters almost unchanged and multiplies the tokens, and so the compute, by 16.

Sampling compute does not buy back model compute: DiT-L/2 with 1,000 sampling steps (80.7 Tflops per image) scored FID-10K 25.9, DiT-XL/2 with 128 steps (15.2 Tflops) 23.7. The headline FID 2.27 used guidance on three of the four latent channels only, a detail in the appendix; four-channel guidance at scale 1.375 gave 2.20, so the choice did not flatter the result, but a reimplementation that guides all channels at 1.5 is not the published configuration.

At toy scale the U-Net wins. Trained here with the topic's flow-matching recipe on Fashion-MNIST, a 4.1M-parameter DiT given the same training compute as the 1.0M-parameter U-Net scored a Fréchet distance of 38.1 against the U-Net's 23.75 (lower is better, guidance 1.5): convolutions build in locality a small transformer must learn. DiT wins by how it scales with compute, not by data efficiency. Cheaper modulation: adaLN-single, and attention without N2

adaLN-Zero has a price the paper's Gflops hide: each block's modulation layer is d × 6d, a third of the block (attention 4d2, MLP 8d2, modulation 6d2). Counted here, DiT-XL/2's modulation layers hold 225.8M of its 675.1M parameters (33.4%). PixArt-α found the same, "a substantial proportion (27%) of the parameters" in its text-conditioned DiT, and replaced them with adaLN-single: one MLP computes the six vectors from the timestep once, shared by all blocks, plus a small learned per-block offset ({{Chen et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00426}}). Sana (section 1's 32 × DC-AE) went further and replaced softmax attention by ReLU linear attention ({{attention_processor.py line 5371|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/attention_processor.py#L5371}}), so its cost grows linearly with tokens: counted here, attention scores are 2% of Sana's FLOPs at 1024 pixels, against 15 to 37% for the softmax transformers on this page.
