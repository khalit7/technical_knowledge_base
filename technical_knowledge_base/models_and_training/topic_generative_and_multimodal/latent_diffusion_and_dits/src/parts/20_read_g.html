8. Resolution and aspect ratio

The problem. A denoiser learns the statistics of the latent grids it was trained on: their size, their shape, and how much of a subject one position covers. SD 1.5 was fine-tuned at 512 × 512 (its model card: 595k steps at that size). Ask it for another size and nothing in the code stops you: the U-Net is convolutional and its attention takes any number of positions.

One prompt, one seed, four sizes: at four times the training area SD 1.5 draws four puppies

What the four sizes show. At 512, the training size, one puppy fills the frame. At 768 (2.25 times the area) one puppy still holds together, with more background. At 1024 (four times the area) the model draws four puppies: each region of the 128 × 128 latent is denoised with the statistics of a 64 × 64 training image, so the canvas fills with several subjects of the usual size; the U-Net's attention reaches the whole grid, but nothing in its training taught it what one large subject looks like at this scale. At 256 the puppy is cropped at the edge and its proportions are off: too few positions for the layout it learned. The same seed and settings throughout; no sampler change fixes this.

How production models handle it.

  • Train at the sizes you will sample. SDXL pretrained at 256 × 256, then 512, then fine-tuned on "different aspect ratios ... of ~1024 × 1024 pixel area" ({{Podell et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952}}, section 2.5).
  • Aspect-ratio bucketing (from NovelAI's 2022 work, which SDXL and SD3 cite): sort the training images into buckets of similar shape, keep the pixel count as close to 10242 as the shape allows, with height and width in multiples of 64, and build each batch from one bucket (SDXL section 2.3, which also feeds the bucket's size to the model as a condition). For a transformer, equal area means a similar number of tokens, so the cost per batch stays level.
  • Tell the model what it is looking at. SDXL's micro-conditioning adds the original size, crop offsets and target size to the time embedding as Fourier features ({{pipeline line 734|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/stable_diffusion_xl/pipeline_stable_diffusion_xl.py#L734}}). The size condition kept the 39% of pretraining data smaller than 256 pixels that would otherwise be discarded; the crop condition targets SD 1.5's cut-off heads, which the paper blames on random crops: sampling at (0, 0) asks for an uncropped image, other values "simulate the amount of cropping" (section 2.2, Figures 4 and 5; vendor).
  • Shift the noise schedule with resolution. More pixels carry more redundant signal, so the same noise level destroys less of the image; SD3 shifts its timesteps by a factor of √(m / n) (m pixels at the new resolution, n at the base) and FLUX computes the shift from the image's token count ({{pipeline_flux.py line 73|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/pipelines/flux/pipeline_flux.py#L73}}). {{Flow matching|n:3fc5c17b0d0d8136a1efc73aaa446ceb}}, section 11, owns the details.

9. What one image costs

The formula. One image = text encoder once (twice with guidance and a negative or empty prompt) + steps × passes per step × FLOPs per pass + one VAE decode. Passes per step is 2 with classifier-free guidance (the conditional and the unconditional prediction, batched together) and 1 for a guidance-distilled model (FLUX.1 [dev]) or a model sampled without guidance (FLUX.1 [schnell], Z-Image-Turbo). FLOPs per pass grow with the image tokens N (linear layers, convolutions) and with N2 (attention), so at high resolution attention takes over: counted here, attention scores are of SD 1.5's FLOPs at 512 pixels and at 2,048. A video clip multiplies the image tokens by its frames, so the N2 term grows with the square of the duration: the clip-length slider of {{Video and audio generation|n:3c65c17b0d0d8198bd7be111a3dc4c3a}}, section 4 ("Duration is the expensive axis"), shows it overtaking everything else.

10. Few steps: distillation

The problem. The cost formula's biggest lever is passes per image: 50 for SD 1.5 at the topic's settings, 100 for SDXL at its pipeline default, against one decode. Better solvers bring 1,000 steps down to 10 to 25 ({{Diffusion from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9bf0d94e57ce9164}}, section 5), but no solver follows a curved path in one or two straight steps. Below about 8 steps you need a second model trained to jump: distillation, where a student learns to do in a few passes what the teacher (the original model plus its sampler) does in many. A student removes one of two things.

Distilled students cut passes per image 7 to 100 times; FLOPs per pass stay the same

One square is one denoiser pass; blue conditional, orange the unconditional passes guidance adds. Teachers at the topic's settings (SD 1.5) or the diffusers pipeline defaults. Students: LCM-LoRA 4 steps, guidance off (measured on {{Diffusion from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9bf0d94e57ce9164}}, section 7); SDXL-Turbo 1 step, guidance_scale=0.0 ({{card|https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/sdxl-turbo}}: "1 to 4 steps"); SD3-Turbo "four unguided sampling steps" ({{LADD|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12015}}); FLUX.1 [schnell] 4 steps ({{card|https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell}}: "1 to 4 steps"). [dev] and [schnell] share one 11.9B transformer, 74.38 TFLOP per pass at 1024: 28 passes are 2,083 TFLOP, 4 are 298. derived

  • Remove the second pass per step: guidance distillation. Train a student that takes the guidance scale w as an input and outputs the guided prediction in one call ({{Meng et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03142}}). FLUX.1 [dev] is such a model ("Trained using guidance distillation", its {{model card|https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-dev}}), and it shows in the weights: the 10,229,760-parameter guidance embedder of section 6. Halves the cost at the same steps; negative prompts lose their slot, since there is no unconditional pass to replace.
  • Remove steps: four families of students.
    • Progressive distillation ({{Salimans and Ho 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00512}}): a student does in one step what the teacher does in two; repeat, halving each round.
    • Consistency distillation ({{Song et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469}}): train a map from any point on the sampling path straight to its end. Latent consistency models do it in SD's latent with guidance baked in ({{Luo et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04378}}); LCM-LoRA packs the result into a 67.5M-parameter LoRA that plugs into an unchanged SD 1.5 ({{Luo et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05556}}).
    • Adversarial distillation: add a GAN loss so one or two steps give sharp images. ADD ({{Sauer et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17042}}, SDXL-Turbo) judges decoded pixels with a frozen DINOv2 discriminator; LADD ({{Sauer et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12015}}) judges in the latent, with generative features of a pretrained latent diffusion model instead of a pixel-space discriminator, which enables "high-resolution multi-aspect ratio image synthesis": SD3-Turbo, and FLUX.1 [schnell] ("Trained using latent adversarial diffusion distillation", its card).
    • Distribution matching ({{DMD, Yin et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18828}}; {{DMD2, 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14867}}): match the student's output distribution to the teacher's, using two score models.

What it costs. A student is a new model: training it takes compute and data (LCM about 32 A100 GPU hours), it is tied to its teacher's weights, and the few-step regime has its own failures. Measured on SD 1.5 by {{Diffusion from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9bf0d94e57ce9164}} (section 7): "LCM-LoRA is sharp at 1 to 4 steps but drops the lighthouse; DPM-Solver++ needs 4 steps to keep it". Flow-based shortcuts (rectified flow, reflow, one-step distillation of a flow) are on {{Flow matching|n:3fc5c17b0d0d8136a1efc73aaa446ceb}}, sections 8 and 9.
