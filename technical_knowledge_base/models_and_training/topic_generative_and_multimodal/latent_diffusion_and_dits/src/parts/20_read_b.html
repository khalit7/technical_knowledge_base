A text-to-image model is three networks in a row: a text encoder that turns the prompt into vectors, a denoiser that runs once per sampling step on a small latent (a compressed grid standing in for the image), and the VAE decoder that turns the final latent into pixels once. This page is about the middle network and how the text reaches it: why it works on latents, how its two families (the convolutional U-Net and the diffusion transformer, DiT) are built, and what one image costs. Reading time about 35 minutes (words outside widgets at 200 a minute; the folded answers add about 4).

1. Why latent diffusion: the compute arithmetic

The problem. A diffusion or flow model runs its denoiser tens of times per image (the parent page's Diffusion lab: 25 steps, each two passes with guidance). Everything the denoiser costs is multiplied by that. And a 512 × 512 colour image is 786,432 numbers, while the details that matter to a viewer (shapes, layout, texture statistics) are far fewer.

How it works. Train an autoencoder once, then train and run the denoiser on its latent. Stable Diffusion's VAE maps 512 × 512 × 3 to 64 × 64 × 4 = 16,384 numbers, 48 times fewer (the downsampling factor f = 8 per side, 4 channels). The decoder runs once at the end; the denoiser never sees a pixel. Rombach et al. called this splitting "perceptual compression" (the autoencoder) from "semantic compression" (the generator); the paper's own f sweep is rebuilt on the {{LDM paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}.

The same U-Net on latents and on pixels

The cost has two parts, and they scale differently. Convolutions and the linear layers of attention cost in proportion to the number of positions N; the attention scores cost in proportion to N2 (every position compares itself with every other). SD 1.5 puts self-attention on its 64 × 64 level, so at 512 × 512 that level already compares 4,096 positions with 4,096. On pixels the same level would hold 262,144 positions: an attention matrix of 6.9 × 1010 entries per head per layer, which is why no one runs this network on pixels and why pixel-space models (DDPM, ADM, Imagen's base model) worked mostly at 64 to 256 pixels and reached higher resolutions with separate upsampling models.

Predict: double the image side, 512 to 1024 pixels (four times the positions). How much more does one SD 1.5 U-Net pass cost?

When it matters, and the price. Latents make high resolution affordable, but they cap quality at what the decoder can draw (small text, faces at a distance: the sibling page Autoencoders and VAEs measures nine decoders), and the denoiser must learn the latent's statistics, which is why the scaling factor exists (the parent's Latent lab). The trend since 2023 has been to spend the saved compute on a bigger denoiser and to push the compression further with more channels (FLUX.1 and SD3: 16 channels at f = 8; Sana's DC-AE: 32 channels at f = 32), not to go back to pixels.
