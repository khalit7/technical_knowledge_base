A text-to-image model runs one large network, the denoiser, tens of times on a small VAE latent while a text encoder steers it, so the price of an image is FLOPs per denoiser pass × passes per image. Every design on this page changes one of those two factors or how the text reaches the image: the latent shrinks the pass, the U-Net and the diffusion transformer (DiT) are two ways to build it, cross-attention and joint attention are two ways to read the prompt, and distillation cuts the passes.

Fig. 1, step by step: one image from Stable Diffusion 1.5: the denoiser runs 50 times, everything else once

  1. The prompt, once. CLIP's tokenizer cuts the prompt into 77 positions (section 3); CLIP ViT-L's text encoder (123.1M parameters) turns them into 77 vectors of 768, once per prompt (twice with guidance, for the empty prompt).
  2. A latent, not an image. The denoiser works on a 64 × 64 × 4 latent, 16,384 numbers standing for the 512 × 512 × 3 image, and starts from pure noise. Here the two are not to scale; Fig. 2 draws them so.
  3. The denoiser, 50 times. The U-Net (859.5M parameters, 0.80 TFLOP per pass at 512 pixels, counted) runs at every one of 25 sampler steps, twice per step with classifier-free guidance (the prompt and the empty prompt, batched), and every pass reads the 77 text vectors.
  4. The VAE decoder, once. 83.7M parameters draw the final latent as the 512 × 512 × 3 image.
  5. Where the time goes. The two columns are to scale: 25 guided steps of 0.610 s against one decode of 0.488 s. The price of an image is FLOPs per pass × passes per image.

Latent
the VAE's compressed grid standing in for the image: 64 × 64 × 4 for a 512 × 512 SD 1.5 image.
Denoiser, timestep
the network that takes a noisy latent and its noise level (the timestep) and predicts the noise or velocity at every position.
U-Net
a convolutional denoiser that shrinks the grid level by level and grows it back, with skip connections across.
DiT, patchify
a transformer denoiser: the latent is cut into p × p patches, each patch one token.
Cross-attention
image positions as queries, the prompt's text vectors as keys and values.
adaLN-Zero
conditioning by predicting each block's LayerNorm shift and scale and a residual gate, initialised to zero.
MMDiT, single stream
one attention over image and text tokens, with separate weights per modality (MMDiT) or shared weights (single stream).
Passes per step
2 with classifier-free guidance (conditional and unconditional prediction), 1 without or after guidance distillation.
Distillation
training a student to do in a few passes what the teacher sampler does in many.

1. Why latent diffusion: the compute arithmetic

The problem. The denoiser runs tens of times per image (at the topic's settings: 25 steps, each two passes with guidance), so everything it costs is multiplied. A 512 × 512 colour image is 786,432 numbers, while the details that matter to a viewer (shapes, layout, texture statistics) are far fewer.

How it works. Train an autoencoder once, then train and run the denoiser on its latent. Stable Diffusion's VAE maps 512 × 512 × 3 to 64 × 64 × 4 = 16,384 numbers, 48 times fewer (the downsampling factor f = 8 per side, 4 channels). The decoder runs once at the end; the denoiser never sees a pixel. Rombach et al. called this splitting "perceptual compression" (the autoencoder) from "semantic compression" (the generator); the paper's own f sweep is rebuilt on the {{LDM paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81bb98adc0daf8462cb6}}, with a live toy of latent against pixel diffusion at fixed compute.

Fig. 2, step by step: the same image as pixels and as a latent, drawn to scale

  1. The image: 786,432 numbers. Three 512 × 512 colour planes (grid lines every 32 pixels).
  2. Its latent, to scale: 16,384 numbers. The VAE's encoder maps it to 64 × 64 × 4: an eighth of the side, 4 channels, 48 times fewer numbers. This small grid is all the denoiser ever sees.
  3. The same U-Net costs hundreds of times more on pixels than on the latent. One SD 1.5 U-Net pass (batch 1, 77 text tokens) is 0.80 TFLOP on the latent and 545 TFLOP on a 512 × 512 pixel grid, 679 times as much; the columns are to scale, so the latent's is a sliver. The latent pass is counted on meta tensors; the pixel pass is the same network with 512 × 512 positions at its top level (the input convolution would take 3 channels instead of 4, a negligible change), from the counted formula, which is exact for this network.
  4. The N2 part grows fastest. The darker part of each column is the formula's N2 terms (attention scores): 15% of the latent pass, 92% of the pixel pass. Positions grow 64 times, attention entries 4,096 times.

Two parts that scale differently. Convolutions and linear layers cost in proportion to the positions N, attention scores to N2. SD 1.5's top level already compares 4,096 positions with 4,096; on pixels it would hold 262,144, an attention matrix of 6.9 × 1010 entries per head per layer. That is why pixel-space models (DDPM, ADM, Imagen's base model) worked mostly at 64 to 256 pixels and reached higher resolutions with separate upsampling models.

Predict: double the image side, 512 to 1024 pixels (four times the positions). How much more does one SD 1.5 U-Net pass cost?

The price. Latents cap quality at what the decoder can draw (small text, faces at a distance: {{Autoencoders and VAEs|n:3c65c17b0d0d814da93bdb159b7c1e11}} measures nine decoders), and the denoiser must be fed a latent with the statistics it was trained on, which is what the scaling factor is for (same page, section 11). Since 2023 the saved compute has gone into a bigger denoiser and deeper compression with more channels (FLUX.1 and SD3: 16 channels at f = 8; Sana's DC-AE: 32 channels at f = 32), not back to pixels.
