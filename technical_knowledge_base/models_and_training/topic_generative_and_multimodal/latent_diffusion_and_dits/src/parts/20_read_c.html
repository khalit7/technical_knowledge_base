2. The U-Net, read from Stable Diffusion 1.5's config

The problem. The denoiser takes a noisy latent and the step's noise level (the timestep) and predicts the noise (or the velocity, for a flow model) at every position. It must see fine detail (to remove noise position by position) and the whole layout (to know that the blob in the corner is a wheel). A U-Net (Ronneberger et al. 2015, {{arXiv:1505.04597|https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597}}) gets both by shrinking the grid level by level, so that a 3 × 3 convolution at the bottom covers a large part of the image, then growing it back, with skip connections that hand each level's features straight across to the matching level on the way up.

How it works, read from the config. SD 1.5's unet/config.json at the pinned revision says everything: block_out_channels [320, 640, 1280, 1280] (four levels, at 64, 32, 16 and 8 positions per side for a 512 image), down_block_types three CrossAttnDownBlock2D then one plain DownBlock2D (no attention at 8 × 8, except in the middle block), layers_per_block 2 (two ResNet blocks per level on the way down, three on the way up), cross_attention_dim 768 (the width of CLIP ViT-L's text vectors) and attention_head_dim 8, which diffusers reads as the number of heads, so heads are 40, 80 and 160 wide ({{unet_2d_condition.py line 249|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_2d_condition.py#L249}}; a classic mistake, closed block at the end).

Fig. 3, step by step: one latent through SD 1.5's U-Net, every stage from its config

  1. The noisy latent in. 64 × 64 × 4 (with guidance, two copies, prompt and empty prompt, as a batch). conv_in, a 3 × 3 convolution, widens every position to 320 channels. Each glass plate stands for 160 channels; a plate's width grows with the grid side (square-root scale).
  2. Down: the grid shrinks, the channels grow. 64 × 64 × 320, 32 × 32 × 640, 16 × 16 × 1,280, 8 × 8 × 1,280. Each of the first three levels runs two ResNet blocks, each followed by a transformer block, then a stride-2 convolution halves the grid; the 8 × 8 level has two ResNet blocks, no attention and no downsampling.
  3. The middle, at 8 × 8. ResNet, a transformer block (self- and cross-attention over 64 positions), ResNet. Each 3 × 3 convolution now sees most of the image.
  4. Up, with twelve skips. Each up level runs three ResNet blocks, and each takes one of the twelve saved down-path maps concatenated as extra channels (the arcs, consumed last-saved first), then a 2 × upsample. The doubled input channels are why the up path holds most of the parameters: up_blocks.1 at 16 × 16, with three transformer blocks, is the single largest stage at 258.3M.
  5. Text enters at 16 transformer blocks. Two per level on the way down at 64, 32 and 16, one in the middle, three per level on the way up at 16, 32 and 64 (up_blocks.0 at 8 × 8 has none): each one's cross-attention reads the 77 text vectors (section 3).
  6. Parameters sit low, FLOPs sit high. Columns per stage, to scale: share of the 859.5M parameters (orange) and of the pass's 803.3 GFLOP at 512 pixels (blue). down_blocks.0 holds 1.2% of the parameters and does 13.6% of the FLOPs, up_blocks.3 2.2% and 24.6%, up_blocks.1 30.1% and 18.9%. Parameters and FLOPs per stage counted on meta tensors (src/arch/extras.py), one pass, batch 1. derived
  7. The prediction out. GroupNorm, SiLU and a 3 × 3 convolution (conv_out) back to 4 channels: the predicted noise for every one of the 16,384 latent numbers.

ResNet block (the convolutional part)

GroupNorm, SiLU, 3 × 3 convolution; then the time embedding is projected to the block's channels and added to every position (time_emb_proj); GroupNorm, SiLU, 3 × 3 convolution; plus the input (a residual). The time embedding is a 320-wide sinusoid of the timestep ({{line 569|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_2d_condition.py#L569}}) through a two-layer MLP to 1,280.

Transformer block (the attention part)

Flatten the grid into a sequence; self-attention (attn1: every position looks at every other), cross-attention (attn2: every position looks at the 77 text vectors, {{attention.py line 1043|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/attention.py#L1043}}), a GEGLU feed-forward, each with LayerNorm and a residual; reshape back to a grid. One per ResNet block at the 64, 32 and 16 levels, one in the middle.

Skip connections are concatenations, not sums: each up-block ResNet receives its input with the matching down-level features stacked on as extra channels ({{unet_2d_blocks.py line 2444|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_2d_blocks.py#L2444}}). Twelve feature maps go across (the input convolution, two ResNets and a downsampler at each of the first three levels, two ResNets at the last), and the up path consumes three per level. That is why the up blocks hold most of the parameters: their first convolutions see twice the channels.

The lesson of the three views: parameters sit where the grid is small and the channels many (the 16 × 16 and 8 × 8 levels hold most of them), while the FLOPs sit where the grid is large (the two 64 × 64 levels do 38% of the work with 3.4% of the weights). A parameter count says what the model can store; FLOPs per pass say what an image costs. DiT's authors measured complexity in Gflops for this reason (section 4).

Parameters sit where the grid is small; FLOPs and time sit where it is large

The cost of the design. The U-Net's convolutions build in locality and multi-scale structure, which makes it data-efficient at small scale (this page's tiny experiment in section 4 shows it) but heterogeneous: every level has its own width, block mix and attention cost, so scaling it means hand-redistributing blocks. SDXL did exactly that: "we omit the transformer block at the highest feature level, use 2 and 10 blocks at the lower levels, and remove the lowest level" ({{Podell et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952}}, section 2.1), for a 2.6B U-Net (counted here: 2,567,463,684).
