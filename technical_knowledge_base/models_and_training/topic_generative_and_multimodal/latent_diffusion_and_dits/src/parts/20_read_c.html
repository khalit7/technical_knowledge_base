2. The U-Net, read from Stable Diffusion 1.5's config

The problem. The denoiser takes a noisy latent and the step's noise level (the timestep) and predicts the noise (or the velocity, for a flow model) at every position. It must see both fine detail (to remove noise pixel by pixel) and the whole layout (to know that the blob in the corner is a wheel). A U-Net (Ronneberger et al. 2015, {{arXiv:1505.04597|https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597}}) gets both by shrinking the grid level by level, so that a 3 × 3 convolution at the bottom covers a large part of the image, then growing it back, with skip connections that hand each level's features straight across to the matching level on the way up.

One latent through the U-Net, then through a DiT

Both networks take the same 64 × 64 × 4 latent of a 512 × 512 image (DiT-XL/2 as the authors' 512 model: patch 2, 1,024 tokens). Boxes are drawn to scale: height is the grid side (or, for the DiT, the number of tokens), width the channels per position. Parameters and FLOPs per stage counted on meta tensors (src/arch/extras.py), one pass, batch 1. derived

How it works, read from the config. SD 1.5's unet/config.json at the pinned revision says everything: block_out_channels [320, 640, 1280, 1280] (four levels, at 64, 32, 16 and 8 positions per side for a 512 image), down_block_types three CrossAttnDownBlock2D then one plain DownBlock2D (no attention at 8 × 8, except in the middle block), layers_per_block 2 (two ResNet blocks per level on the way down, three on the way up), cross_attention_dim 768 (the width of CLIP ViT-L's text vectors) and attention_head_dim 8, which is not a head dimension at all: diffusers reads it as the number of heads, so heads are 40, 80 and 160 wide. Its own source says so ({{unet_2d_condition.py line 249|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_2d_condition.py#L249}}): "This looks weird upon first reading it and it is."

ResNet block (the convolutional part)

GroupNorm, SiLU, 3 × 3 convolution; then the time embedding is projected to the block's channels and added to every position (time_emb_proj); GroupNorm, SiLU, 3 × 3 convolution; plus the input (a residual). The time embedding is a 320-wide sinusoid of the timestep ({{line 569|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_2d_condition.py#L569}}) through a two-layer MLP to 1,280.

Transformer block (the attention part)

Flatten the grid into a sequence; self-attention (attn1: every position looks at every other), cross-attention (attn2: every position looks at the 77 text vectors, {{attention.py line 1043|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/attention.py#L1043}}), a GEGLU feed-forward, each with LayerNorm and a residual; reshape back to a grid. One per ResNet block at the 64, 32 and 16 levels, one in the middle.

Skip connections are concatenations, not sums: each up-block ResNet receives its input with the matching down-level features stacked on as extra channels ({{unet_2d_blocks.py line 2444|https://github.com/huggingface/diffusers/blob/v0.41.0/src/diffusers/models/unets/unet_2d_blocks.py#L2444}}). Twelve feature maps go across (the input convolution, two ResNets and a downsampler at each of the first three levels, two ResNets at the last), and the up path consumes three per level. That is why the up blocks hold most of the parameters: their first convolutions see twice the channels.

Where the parameters are, where the FLOPs are, where the time goes

The lesson of the three views: parameters sit where the grid is small and the channels many (the 16 × 16 and 8 × 8 levels hold most of them), while the FLOPs sit where the grid is large (the two 64 × 64 levels do 38% of the work with 3.4% of the weights). A parameter count says what the model can store; FLOPs per pass say what an image costs. DiT's authors made the same point and measured complexity in Gflops for this reason (section 4).

The cost, and when it matters. The U-Net's convolutions build in locality and multi-scale structure, which makes it data-efficient at small scale (this page's tiny experiment in section 4 shows it) but heterogeneous: every level has its own width, block mix and attention cost, so scaling it means hand-redistributing blocks. SDXL did exactly that: "we omit the transformer block at the highest feature level, use 2 and 10 blocks at the lower levels, and remove the lowest level" ({{Podell et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01952}}, section 2.1), for a 2.6B U-Net (counted here: 2,567,463,684).
