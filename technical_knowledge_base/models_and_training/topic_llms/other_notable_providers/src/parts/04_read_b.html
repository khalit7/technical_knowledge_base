Auditability and on-prem: Cohere and IBM

Cohere sells to enterprises that will not send their data to a public API, and every part of its lineup follows from that ({{Cohere research|https://cohere.com/research}}). Command R and R+ were the first models explicitly post-trained for grounded generation with citations: given retrieved documents in context, the model answers from those documents and emits inline references to the passages it used, so a reviewer can check each claim against its source and an unsupported sentence becomes visible. That is a materially different objective from ordinary instruction tuning, and it made them the default RAG models for a period (Command R+ was 104B under CC-BY-NC 4.0, {{Cohere|https://cohere.com/blog/command-r-plus-microsoft-azure}}).

Command A (March 2025) is 111B and dense, which sounds backwards until you notice the deployment target. Cohere's card says it is "deployable on just two GPUs" ({{Command A model card|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/c4ai-command-a-03-2025}}); a dense model has predictable memory and no router, whereas an MoE of equal quality needs every expert resident and a router that complicates multi-GPU serving. Sizing a model to a customer's two-card server is the product decision. The arithmetic adds a caveat the card leaves out: 111B × 2 bytes = 222 GB in 16-bit, more than two 80 GB cards hold, so "two GPUs" holds at 8-bit (111 GB). The flagship has since moved on: Command A+ (May 2026) is a 218B total, 25B active MoE under Apache 2.0 ({{Command A+ model card|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/command-a-plus-05-2026-bf16}}), so Cohere now accepts the MoE serving cost too.

Aya, from Cohere Labs, is the open multilingual research line (Aya 23, Aya Expanse, Aya Vision), notable for building instruction data across languages through a large annotator community rather than by machine-translating English datasets, which is what usually leaves "multilingual" models fluent but culturally hollow. Tiny Aya (February 2026, 3.35B, 70+ languages, CC-BY-NC 4.0) pushes that to phone-capable sizes ({{Tiny Aya|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global}}).

North is the secure agent platform: agents that run inside the customer's environment against internal systems. North Mini Code (June 2026, 30B total with 3B active, Apache 2.0, 256K context) is Cohere's first open agentic coding model ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/North-Mini-Code-1.0}}). North Small Translate (September 2026) applies the same instinct to a single task: a 218B total, 25B active MoE built only for translation, with 128 experts and 8 per token, 4,096-token sliding-window attention and a 16K context. It reports 83.6 on WMT26 across 50+ languages against DeepL NextGen's 81.37 and Google Translate's 68.20, on Cohere's own evaluation with GPT-5.6-Sol as judge. It is free on the Cohere API up to rate limits and non-commercial (CC-BY-NC 4.0) for self-hosting, and its 4-bit (W4A4) checkpoint runs on one B200 or two H100s ({{Cohere docs|https://docs.cohere.com/docs/north-small-translate-1.0}}, {{Cohere blog|https://cohere.com/blog/north-small-translate}}, 8 min). A dedicated 218B translation model is a reminder that single-task specialists at mixture-of-experts scale are still economically viable against generalists.

IBM Granite runs on the same architectural logic as Nemotron Nano (a constant-size recurrent state for most layers lets long documents be processed on modest hardware, above). What IBM adds is not capability but the compliance surface: an ISO-certified governance process for how the models were built, published training-data provenance, and indemnification, which is what actually unblocks a bank or a hospital ({{IBM Granite|https://www.ibm.com/granite/docs/}}). Its function-calling quality per parameter is strong, which matters because the enterprise use is structured tool invocation rather than open-ended chat. It is chosen for the paperwork as much as for the model.

Whether a model fits the customer's hardware is a question of two independent levers, and the next chart separates them. Pick a model and a precision:

Weights in memory = parameters × bits ÷ 8 (decimal GB); the KV cache and activations come on top. Compute per token ≈ 2 × active parameters FLOPs, the standard estimate for a forward pass. Red lines are single-card or single-node memory: RTX 5090 32 GB, H100 80 GB, B200 180 GB (a DGX B200's 1,440 GB ÷ 8), 8 × B200. Picking a model with a published hardware claim switches to the precision that claim implies and checks it. Defaults reproduce Prism's "5.9 GB" and "nine times smaller" for Bonsai 2 by construction (27B × 1.76 ÷ 8 = 5.94 GB; 16 ÷ 1.76 = 9.09), and Tencent's 1.56 TB for Hy4 at about 2.03 bytes per parameter, which shows the release is essentially all 16-bit.

What it shows. Bits per weight move a model left and right (memory); active parameters move it up and down (compute per token). The two are independent: North Small Translate stores 218B parameters but computes like a 25B model, and Bonsai 2 keeps all 27B active but stores them in 6 GB.

Distribution and billing: Amazon Nova and Bedrock

Nova is Amazon's closed in-house family, available only through Bedrock. The first generation (December 2024) was a straight capability ladder, Micro, Lite, Pro and Premier, differing in size, latency and price, so an application can route cheap turns to Micro and hard ones to Pro without changing vendor. Two siblings are more interesting. Nova Sonic is speech-to-speech: audio in, audio out, with no transcribe-then-generate-then-synthesise pipeline, which removes the latency of three sequential models and preserves the prosody, interruption and tone that a text bottleneck discards. Nova Act is trained to drive a browser, emitting UI actions (click, type, scroll) against a page rather than prose about it. The Nova 2 generation (from December 2025) is current, with Premier retired in favour of Nova 2 Lite ({{AWS: Nova 2 Lite|https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/introducing-amazon-nova-2-lite-a-fast-cost-effective-reasoning-model/}}, {{Bedrock model catalog 2026|https://hidekazu-konishi.com/entry/amazon_bedrock_model_catalog_2026.html}}).

Nova itself is competent mid-tier and priced to undercut. Amazon's real position is elsewhere. Bedrock is the aggregator: one API, one bill, one IAM policy and one VPC boundary across models from Anthropic, Meta, Mistral, Cohere and others, which is what large enterprises actually buy. And Trainium, Amazon's in-house training accelerator, is the compute behind Project Rainier for Anthropic, which makes Amazon a supplier of an alternative to Nvidia rather than merely a customer of one. Microsoft's Azure plays the same distribution game (above), and NVIDIA's Hugging Face purchase is the open-weights version of it (above).

Open scale at a low price: Tencent, Xiaomi, StepFun and the 300B to 1T band

Five Chinese labs from the long tail now ship mixture-of-experts models at the size of the frontier band, most of them open. The common pattern is large total size, a modest active count, and prices far below closed models. Their histories matter: Xiaomi and Tencent released open models at this scale long before their current lines, which the Scale and sparsity tab plots over time.

Tencent: Hunyuan and Hy4

Tencent published competitive open weights across text and multimodal for two years without being in the frontier conversation, including large open MoE models well before Hy4: Hunyuan-Large (389B total / 52B active, November 2024, under Tencent's own licence; {{arXiv 2411.02265|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02265}}) and Hy3 (295B / 21B, Apache 2.0, open weights July 2026 after an April preview; {{Hy3|https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy3}}). The Hy4 preview (August 2026) is what put it in the frontier band: 770B total / 49B active, a context window above 1M tokens, two reasoning levels with high on by default and a no_think switch to turn it off, Apache 2.0, and about 1.56 TB of weights on Hugging Face, with vLLM support landing in v0.29.0 ({{Tencent|https://www.tencent.com/tencent-releases-and-open-sources-tencent-hy4-preview/}}, 8 min; {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy4-preview}}; {{vLLM releases|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases}}).

Two things are worth carrying. Its sparsity, 770 ÷ 49 = 15.7×, is conservative next to Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's 125 ÷ 6 = 20.8×, so Tencent bought its place in the band with size rather than with an aggressive active-parameter bet, which also makes it expensive to serve for its tier. And it is positioned on real-world productivity rather than benchmark maxima: an internal blind evaluation with 163 experts across 203 engineering tasks scored Hy4 at 2.99 / 4.00 against GLM-5.3 at 2.92 and Kimi K3 at 2.94, inside the noise, but the right shape of claim for a model sold on coding, office work and scientific research. Hy4 is not yet on the Artificial Analysis index; Hy3 scores 25.3 on v4.3 ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/hy3}}).

The detail that connects it to a larger argument: Tencent says the model took part in the automated optimisation of its own training methods, with autonomous infrastructure work producing a 31.8% throughput gain. That is the same claim shape as Anthropic's recursive-self-improvement report and Zhipu's inference-stack post, three labs within a month, and all three are self-reported by the party that benefits ({{Simon Willison's hands-on|https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/29/hy4/}}, 10 min).

Xiaomi: MiMo

Xiaomi had been releasing open weights under MIT for most of a year before its current line: MiMo-V2-Flash (309B total / 15B active, December 2025; {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2-Flash}}), then MiMo-V2.5 (310B / 15B; {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.5}}) and MiMo-V2.5-Pro (1.02T / 42B; {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.5-Pro}}) in April 2026. So MiMo-V2.6 (22 September 2026) is a consumer-electronics company's third open generation at this scale, not its arrival. MiMo-V2.6-Pro is 1.02T total / 42B active and MiMo-V2.6-Flash 309B / 15B, both MIT with weights on Hugging Face, both with a 1M-token context, and both trained with reinforcement learning as the final stage ({{Xiaomi release post|https://mimo.mi.com/docs/en-US/news/latest/v2-6}}, {{Pro weights|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL}}). During the post-training run Xiaomi published a live dashboard streaming reward and evaluation metrics while it was still going, the first time an active frontier-adjacent post-training run had been observable from outside as it happened.

Xiaomi's own table gives Pro 72.6 on DeepSWE v1.1 (58.4 for V2.5) and 89.9 on Terminal Bench 2.1, while conceding that it loses Terminal-Bench 4.0 to Claude Opus 5 by 14 points (34.9 against 49.0) and trails every closed model on ExploitBench: an unusually honest vendor table. The 2.1 figure is on the older Terminal-Bench scale and does not compare with the 4.0 figures other labs now report ({{ComputingForGeeks|https://computingforgeeks.com/xiaomi-mimo-v2-6-pro-flash/}}, 10 min). On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (methodology v4.3.2, read 1 October 2026), Pro scores 46.3, the highest open-weight score on that version, ahead of GLM-5.3 at max effort on 44.8 and below GPT-6 Sol at max effort on 47.6; Flash scores 37.9 ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/mimo-v2-6-pro}}). Launch coverage reported Pro at 46 and first among open weights without naming the index version ({{Unite.AI|https://www.unite.ai/xiaomis-new-flagship-model-leads-open-weight-rankings-with-a-score-of-46/}}). Prices per million tokens: Pro $0.435 input on a cache miss, $0.0036 on a hit and $0.87 output; Flash $0.14, $0.0028 and $0.28. A cache hit on Pro costs 0.83% of a miss (0.0036 ÷ 0.435).

The disclosure worth more than any score is the training cost: the final RL stage ran for under six days across more than 7,000 RL task environments, about 750,000 trajectories in total, at $2.62 million for Pro (about $0.85 million for Flash). That is a published price for the last stage of a trillion-parameter open model, which almost no lab publishes. Two techniques come with it and are the reusable part: the mixture-of-experts router is frozen to keep large-scale RL stable, and layered defences against reward hacking are described rather than alluded to. Recipe detail is on {{Topic: llm-training-and-post-training|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}}.

StepFun: Step 5 Preview

StepFun is a multimodal-first Chinese lab that shipped steadily at second-tier visibility, with open weights before this release too (Step-3, 321B / 38B, Apache 2.0, July 2025; Step 3.5 Flash, 196B / 11B, February 2026; {{StepFun on Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai}}). Step 5 Preview (launched 20 September 2026; Artificial Analysis lists 18 September) moved it into the frontier band at 600B total and 27B active, with a 92-layer narrow-deep layout, a 1M-token context, text, image and video input and up to 64K output tokens ({{Pandaily|https://pandaily.com/stepfun-step-5-preview-600b-moe-1m-context}}, {{StepFun docs|https://platform.stepfun.ai/docs/en/guides/models/step-5-preview}}). At release that was the smallest active-parameter count among decoder-only open-class models of 600B or more (DeepSeek V3 37B, GLM-5 40B, MiMo-V2.6-Pro 42B, Hy4 49B, Kimi K3 104B). Its sparsity of 600 ÷ 27 = 22.2× is ordinary next to DeepSeek V4 Pro's 1,600 ÷ 49 = 32.7×; the unusual number is the absolute one, since 27B active is what a mid-size dense model costs to decode.

Pricing is the argument: $2.70 per million output tokens, $1.00 input on a cache miss and $0.05 on a hit ({{StepFun pricing|https://platform.stepfun.ai/docs/en/pricing/details}}), 2.70 ÷ 15 = 18% of Kimi K3's $15 output price ({{Kimi pricing|https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/pricing/chat}}). On StepFun's own table it sits with GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 on most metrics and behind GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 on the hardest coding work, notably DeepSWE v1.1 at 67.7% against 74.0 to 74.1%, with claimed strength on finance and long-horizon autonomous tasks. Artificial Analysis measures it at 43.7 on v4.3 ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/step-5-preview}}). StepFun promised open weights for 15 October 2026 with no licence named; at release there were no weights on Hugging Face and no technical report, so it is an API product until that changes.

NaiveAI, Shanghai AI Laboratory and the rest of China's mix

NaiveAI: Naive-N0.5-Flash (27 September 2026, MIT) is 309B total / 15.5B active with a 1M-token context, from a Beijing startup valued at about $1.4B and founded by Tsinghua's Jifeng Dai, and built on Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.5 base ({{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/NaiveAI/Naive-N0.5-Flash}}, {{NaiveAI research|https://naive.ai/en/research/}}). MiMo-V2.5 is 310B in Xiaomi's release, so the sizes match only approximately; the exact 309B match is with MiMo-V2-Flash. Its attention is the unusual part: no full-attention layer at all. Each module is five 128-token sliding-window layers and one DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA) layer, which selects the top 2,048 tokens, with the first layer also replaced by DSA: 39 sliding-window and 9 DSA layers across 48, so no layer attends densely to the whole context. Arriving five days after MiMo-V2.6-Flash (309B / 15B), it made two 309B-class open models in one week, which measures how crowded the flash-tier open band has become. The valuation and founder are from launch coverage and are unconfirmed here.

Shanghai AI Laboratory: Atria Dawn Preview (September 2026, MIT) is a 744B agentic MoE built as a post-training layer over an existing open base, Zhipu's GLM-5.2, rather than trained from scratch ({{GitHub|https://github.com/atria-asi/Atria-Dawn-Preview}}), a release shape worth recognising when it recurs.

The rest of China's open and closed mix: Baidu ERNIE is Baidu's long-running in-house line tied to its search and cloud products; ByteDance's Seed-OSS ships genuinely competitive open weights across text and multimodal, and Doubao is the consumer assistant that dominates Chinese usage by volume, which makes ByteDance's data position the one to watch; Ant's Ling and Ring lines and the Yi bilingual models are second-tier in visibility but ship regularly. inclusionAI Ling 3.0 Flash VL (September 2026) is a flash-tier vision-language model, free on OpenRouter at launch.
