Bits per weight: Prism ML Bonsai 2

Prism ML is a compression house rather than a model lab. Its Bonsai 2 27B (17 September 2026) is a ternary rebuild of Qwen3.8 27B: weights drawn from {−1, 0, +1} with FP16 group-wise scaling, applied end to end across the language model, at 1.76 effective bits per weight ({{Prism ML|https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-27b}}, 10 min). The footprint follows from the bits alone:

27×109×1.76÷8=5.94 GB

against, in 16-bit

27×109×2=54 GB

The ratio is the "nine times smaller" of its announcement (16 ÷ 1.76 = 9.09). Prism reports it keeps 98.2% of aggregate benchmark performance (83.9 overall; maths 96.57, instruction following 82.66, coding 81.58), all its own figures. It has a 262K context, text and image input and agentic tool use, under Apache 2.0, on Hugging Face and GitHub, and runs on CUDA and MLX at up to 143 tokens per second on an RTX 5090 and 46.8 on an M5 Max. It is the current reference point for how far weight compression goes before quality breaks, and it composes with the open frontier's other lever: fewer active parameters cut compute per token, fewer bits per parameter cut memory, and the two are independent, as the two-lever chart above shows.

Openness as the product: IFM, Prime Intellect, Hugging Face and others

Institute of Foundation Models (Abu Dhabi): K2 Horizon. IFM is the second lab, alongside Ai2, publishing fully open models rather than merely open weights, which puts two such labs on two continents with different funders where there was one ({{Ai2: OLMo|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e08697df661f83c3ac}}). K2 Horizon (September 2026) is six Apache 2.0 models at 0.9B, 3.7B, 7B, 32B, a 36B sparse variant (4B active) and 375B (23B active, 512K context), released with weights, training code, training data and methodology: the combination that makes contamination auditing, data ablations and training-dynamics research possible at all ({{IFM press release|https://ifm.ai/k2/press-release/}}, 8 min; {{K2 Horizon 375B|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-375B-A23B}}). The family shares two ideas: diffusion distillation, adapters that generate a block of tokens in parallel rather than one at a time, for a claimed 3× speedup ({{K2-Horizon-7B-Uno|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-7B-Uno}}; the 3× is from IFM's press release, which blocked re-fetching, so it is unconfirmed here); and Mixture-of-Values attention (MoVA), aimed at reasoning ({{MoVA 36B|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B}}). IFM claims state of the art at 0.9B, 3.7B and 7B and open-weight competitiveness at 375B, a vendor claim until rerun independently; Artificial Analysis measures the 375B at 30.5 on v4.3, against 46.3 for MiMo-V2.6-Pro ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/k2-horizon-375b-a23b}}). It is on Hugging Face with vLLM and SGLang support, with API access through Compass, Cerebras and Nebius. Abu Dhabi now funds two distinct open-model lines, this one and TII's Falcon (above).

Prime Intellect: INTELLECT. These are proofs of concept for decentralised training: one model trained across clusters in different places connected by ordinary internet links rather than a datacentre fabric. Synchronising gradients over such links every step is hopelessly slow, so it needs low-communication algorithms that let workers take many local steps and synchronise rarely. INTELLECT-1 (10B, November 2024, Apache 2.0, with data and checkpoints released) used DiLoCo; the animation runs its measured timings against the method it replaced.

Inputs from the {{INTELLECT-1 technical report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01152}}, Table 2: 100 inner steps take 38 minutes on an 8 × H100 node; the median all-reduce of the int8 pseudo-gradient takes 103 s (USA), 382 s (USA and Europe) or 469 s (global). Compute utilisation = 38 min ÷ (38 min + all-reduce). Defaults reproduce the report's 95.7%, 85.6% and 83.0% independently (the global case computes to 82.9%, a rounding difference). The sync-every-step run is this page's extrapolation, labelled as such: the fp32 gradient is 4 times the int8 payload (10B × 4 bytes = 40 GB), and its all-reduce is assumed to take 4 times as long. Communication falls 100 × 4 = 400 times. The animation stops when off screen and starts paused if your system asks for reduced motion.

What it shows. Synchronising every step over internet links leaves GPUs idle about 95% of the time (5.2% utilisation in the USA case); DiLoCo keeps them busy 83 to 96% of the time by synchronising once per 100 steps and sending a quarter of the bytes. The models are not competitive on quality; the point is that frontier-scale training need not be geographically centralised, which matters strategically far more than the checkpoints do.

Hugging Face SmolLM3 (3B, July 2025, Apache 2.0) is a small model whose value is documentation rather than capability. Every stage of the recipe, the data mixture and the ablations are published, making it the reference for how a competent 3B-class model is actually built ({{SmolLM3 blog|https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3}}, {{model|https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM3-3B}}). It is also a usable reference for NoPE (no positional encoding) in a fraction of layers: those layers get no explicit position signal at all and rely on the causal mask alone to convey order, a choice associated with better generalisation beyond the trained context length.

Arcee AI Trinity Large (January 2026; about 398B total, often rounded to 400B, with about 13B active, 4 of 256 experts per token, OpenMDW-1.1 licence, {{model card|https://huggingface.co/arcee-ai/Trinity-Large-Preview}}, {{technical report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17004}}) is a rare US startup attempt at open-weight frontier scale, from a company otherwise known for model merging and small-model distillation. It is interesting as evidence about whether a small team can still reach that scale, more than for the model itself.

Nous Research Hermes: post-training specialists who take open bases and produce instruct and reasoning variants with their own data and recipes. They publish datasets and methodology, so the Hermes line is a usable control for "how much of this model's behaviour came from post-training".

Upstage Solar popularised depth up-scaling: duplicate a slice of an existing base model's layers to build a deeper network, then continue pretraining so the seams heal, producing a larger model without training one from scratch. SOLAR 10.7B took two copies of a 32-layer base, dropped 8 layers from each, and joined them: 2 × (32 − 8) = 48 layers ({{SOLAR paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15166}}). Reka builds natively multimodal models (text, image, audio and video in one encoder path) from a small team. Reka, AI21 and Upstage are niche but recur in evaluations.

Not a single model: orchestrators and structured-decision models

Two 2026 products from the long tail are sold as models and are not models in the sense the rest of this page uses the word, and each moves the question a deployment answers.

Sakana AI: Fugu

Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2 (10 to 11 September 2026; a first Fugu Ultra appeared in June) are a learned orchestrator. Fugu reads an incoming query, builds an agentic scaffold for it on the fly, and routes the work across a pool of other models behind one OpenAI-compatible API. It was trained by combining fine-tuning, evolutionary search and reinforcement learning, drawing on two ICLR 2026 papers: TRINITY, which assigns participating models Thinker, Worker or Verifier roles, and The Conductor, which learns coordination strategies by RL rather than having them specified.

Fugu Max is the cost-optimised configuration at $2 input and $6 output per million tokens, an output price Sakana puts 40 to 60% below Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Terra and Kimi K3, and on the cost-performance Pareto frontier for 7 of 10 benchmarks. Fugu Ultra v2 targets hard reasoning, at $5 and $30 on OpenRouter ({{OpenRouter|https://openrouter.ai/sakana/fugu-ultra-v2}}): Chartography 48.3 against Claude Opus 5's 27.3, DeepSWE (version not stated) 74.3, best or joint-best on 5 of 8 benchmarks. All are Sakana's own figures; closed weights; not available in the EU or EEA ({{Sakana|https://sakana.ai/fugu-max-release/}}, 10 min).

Why track it: if a routed ensemble sold as one endpoint holds the Pareto frontier, "which model" stops being the question a deployment answers and "which orchestrator" replaces it, the same boundary shift ARC Prize's Provider Adapter produced from the benchmark side (on {{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}). The scaffold rather than the checkpoint is the subject of {{Topic: agentic-harnesses|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d881a8fe98b4cff7bb}}.

TypeSafe AI: Jev

Jev (early access 15 September 2026) is what TypeSafe calls a "System One model". It does not generate prose: it takes unstructured state and returns type-safe structured values with calibrated confidence scores, sampling outputs in parallel rather than one token at a time, so a type error is impossible by construction rather than caught by validation.

TypeSafe claims it is 40× to 200× faster than frontier models on comparable tasks, 70 to 500 ms against 3 to 329 seconds, with input at $0.042 per million tokens and output free. The training method is the part worth tracking: Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions, optimising for honest probability estimates on structured decisions rather than for human preference, a different objective from both RLHF and RLVR. Founded by Diogo Almeida, formerly of OpenAI. A Jev Router appeared on OpenRouter on 25 September ({{OpenRouter|https://openrouter.ai/typesafe/jev-router}}). Everything is vendor-reported with no independent evaluation, and a 1,282-point Hacker News thread the same week claimed prior art on non-autoregressive decision models trained with RL, so the novelty of the category is contested ({{TypeSafe|https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev}}, 15 min).

Why track it: if a large share of production model calls are really structured-decision calls wearing a text interface, this is a different product shape rather than a smaller model.
