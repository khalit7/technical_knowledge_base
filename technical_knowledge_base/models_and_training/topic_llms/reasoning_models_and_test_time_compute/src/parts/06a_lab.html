Sampling lab: a whole benchmark, not one problem

The Reading tab's formulas are for one problem with one success probability p. A benchmark is many problems with different p, and that changes the shape of every curve. Here p varies across problems as a Beta(α, β) distribution, an illustrative choice you can move; wrong answers on each problem split evenly over m values. Everything is computed exactly except the optional simulated benchmark.

Why aggregate coverage follows a power law

On one problem the failure rate (1 − p)k falls exponentially, a curve that bends down on log-log axes. Averaged over a benchmark it falls as a straight line, a power law, because the few problems with tiny p dominate the average at large k. {{Schaeffer et al. (2025)|@schaeffer}} showed this explains the power laws of {{Brown et al.|@brown}}: per-problem exponential scaling "can be made consistent with aggregate polynomial scaling if the distribution of single-attempt success probabilities is heavy tailed".
