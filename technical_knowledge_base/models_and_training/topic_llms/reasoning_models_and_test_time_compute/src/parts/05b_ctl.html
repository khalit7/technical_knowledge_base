Controlling how much it thinks

Once every flagship reasons, the control problem is how much. Five mechanisms are in production, at different layers. The lab pages show each vendor's own dials (see Further reading); this table compares the mechanisms.

Interleaved thinkingModel, per step, between tool callsDepth where an agent's uncertainty arrives: think hard before a consequential action, skim after a trivial tool resultThinking spread over many blocks, so cost is harder to predict per turn

Budgets. Anthropic's budget_tokens (introduced with Claude 3.7 Sonnet, "think for no more than N tokens, for any value of N up to its output limit of 128K tokens", {{Anthropic, February 2025|@c37}}, and the only mode on the Claude 4 and 4.5 models) and Gemini 2.5's original thinking budget are the token-count form. Anthropic's must be at least 1,024 tokens and below max_tokens, and it "sets a target" rather than a strict cap; with interleaved thinking it can exceed max_tokens because "the budget spans all thinking blocks within one assistant turn" ({{Anthropic docs|@anthx}}). s1's budget forcing is the simplest working control: end the thinking forcibly when the budget is spent, or append "Wait" when the model tries to stop early, which makes it re-examine its answer; this took s1-32B "from 50% to 57% on AIME24" ({{Muennighoff et al., 2025|@s1}}). The better version conditions the model on the budget during training, so it learns to compress and land a conclusion inside the allowance; that is where the research is.

Effort levels. Anthropic replaced the budget with adaptive thinking and an effort setting: manual budgets are deprecated on the 4.6 models, and Claude 4.7 and later "reject requests that use it, returning a 400 error" ({{Anthropic docs|@anthx}}). Effort is "soft guidance for how willing Claude should be to think and how deeply", and at low effort the model may skip thinking on simple queries ({{Anthropic: steering thinking|@anths}}). Gemini models "engage in dynamic thinking by default", controlled by thinking_level; the accepted levels (minimal, low, medium, high) and the default vary by model, and the Gemini API now uses it for the 2.5 models too ({{Gemini docs|@gem}}).

Routers. GPT-5 shipped as "a smart, efficient model", "a deeper reasoning model" and "a real-time router that quickly decides which to use based on conversation type, complexity, tool needs, and your explicit intent" ({{OpenAI, August 2025|@gpt5}}). Research systems now train the decision per step: CogRouter reports an 82.3% success rate with "62% fewer tokens" than its baselines ({{Yang et al., February 2026|@cog}}), and Ares cuts reasoning tokens "by up to 52.7% compared to fixed high-effort reasoning, while introducing minimal degradation in task success rates" ({{Yang et al., March 2026|@ares}}).

How the category evolved

2022 to 2023 Prompting era. Chain-of-thought prompting (asking for step-by-step working), self-consistency ({{Wang et al.|@wang}}) and STaR (bootstrapping by fine-tuning on the model's own correct chains) showed latent reasoning could be elicited by prompt alone and, more consequentially, trained on.
Sep 2024 o1: the first production model trained with large-scale RL to reason in a hidden chain of thought, with large jumps on AIME, GPQA and Codeforces. It made the test-time scaling curve a public artefact: accuracy against inference compute, on a log axis, rising where everyone had assumed the model was fixed at deployment ({{OpenAI|@o1}}).
Jan 2025 DeepSeek-R1 replicated the result and published the recipe above. R1-Zero showed that pure RL on a strong base model (V3) produces emergent reflection with no SFT; R1 added the cold-start stage for readability; the distilled 1.5B to 70B variants seeded an open ecosystem. MIT licence, o1-class scores, a fraction of the price ({{DeepSeek-R1|@r1}}).
2025 Hybridisation. Claude 3.7 Sonnet introduced extended thinking with a caller-set budget_tokens; Gemini 2.5 shipped thinking budgets; Qwen3 shipped think and no-think modes in one checkpoint; GPT-5 put a fast model and a reasoning model behind a real-time router. o3, Grok 4, Kimi K2 Thinking, GLM-4.x and MiniMax M1 and M2 all shipped reasoning-first; Grok 4 Heavy brought "parallel test-time compute, which allows Grok to consider multiple hypotheses at once" ({{xAI|@grok4}}). In July an advanced Gemini Deep Think, using "parallel thinking", solved five of six IMO problems for 35 points ({{Google DeepMind|@imo}}), and an experimental OpenAI reasoning model also reached gold-medal level ({{Alexander Wei|@wei}}).
2026 Absorption and the move to latent depth. Essentially no flagship ships without a reasoning mode, so the standalone category dissolved into the mainline and the open questions became when and how much to think, not whether. Explicit token budgets gave way to effort levels with adaptive thinking, and in September GPT-6 Astra was reported to move part of the budget out of tokens into recurrent depth.

Current state

  • Depth control is universal. GPT-5.x routes per request; current Claude and Gemini models take an effort or thinking level (their earlier generations a token budget); most open models a reasoning-effort knob or a thinking on/off switch; interleaved thinking is the dominant agent-harness pattern.
  • Token efficiency is the competition. Frontier marketing leads with "same score, fewer thinking tokens", because at equal accuracy the thinking budget is the price: GPT-5.6 Sol is pitched as outperforming "with fewer tokens and at lower estimated cost" ({{OpenAI|@gpt56}}), and a customer quoted at the Opus 5 launch reported "similar performance while generating 26% fewer tokens on average compared to Opus 4.8 at max reasoning" ({{Anthropic|@op5}}). Research systems train per-step adaptive depth (CogRouter, Ares, above).
  • The harness now carries part of the score. Once part of the deliberation lives outside the transcript, the serving adapter that preserves it between requests is worth points. ARC Prize measured GPT-6 Astra on the ARC-AGI-3 Semi-Private set at 62.7% for $26K through its Standard harness and 99.9% for $19K through a Provider Adapter that "preserves opaque reasoning state between requests and uses compaction for longer conversations": 37 points on the same weights (99.9 − 62.7 = 37.2), with the adapter run also about 3.66 times faster by elapsed time and using 49% fewer total tokens on the 167 game-reasoning pairs both harnesses solved ({{ARC Prize, 3 September 2026|@arc}}). The {{OpenAI page|n:3c65c17b0d0d814abf90d5b9e567d72f}} animates the two harnesses. No test-time-compute number means anything without its harness named.
  • Verification-heavy pipelines. Deep-research products chain reasoning with search and tools, and parallel-sampling plus verifier setups (Deep Think, Grok Heavy tiers) hold the top of the hardest benchmarks (HLE, olympiad sets, ARC-AGI).
  • Open replication is complete. R1's lineage (Qwen3, GLM-5.x, Kimi K3, OLMo 3 Think, MiniMax M3) means RLVR recipes, reasoning datasets and, in OLMo's case, full training logs are public. Remaining frontier gaps come from scale, data and infrastructure rather than secret algorithms.
  • What is measured. AIME and HMMT (competition mathematics), GPQA (graduate-level science), SWE-bench, FrontierSWE and Terminal-Bench (agentic coding), HLE (Humanity's Last Exam, deliberately at the edge of expert knowledge), ARC-AGI-2 and ARC-AGI-3 (abstraction from few examples), and long-horizon agent evaluations of the GDPval kind that score economically valuable multi-step work.
