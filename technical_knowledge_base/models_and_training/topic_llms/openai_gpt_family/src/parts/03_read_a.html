Six bets on where capability is bought

OpenAI's GPT line is the widest product surface in the field and the one that discloses least about its machinery. Read as a whole, it is a sequence of bets on where capability is bought. Each bet moved a cost between OpenAI's training budget and the caller's per-request bill, and each hid a little more of the system behind the API.

1. Pretraining scale (GPT-1 to GPT-3, 2018 to 2020)

Same objective, more parameters and data. Paid once, by OpenAI.

2. Post-training (InstructGPT, ChatGPT, 2022)

Human preferences turned into a reward; a 1.3B tuned model beat a 175B base one.

3. Inference compute (the o-series, from 2024)

Hidden reasoning tokens billed per request: accuracy becomes a purchase the caller makes.

4. A router (GPT-5, 2025)

A classifier decides how much of that compute each request gets.

5. Price bands (GPT-5.6 and GPT-6, 2026)

Named tiers sold beneath one flagship: the caller routes too.

6. Compute inside the network (GPT-6 Astra, 2026)

Reported recurrent depth: deliberation that never becomes text. And state kept on OpenAI's side of the API, the harness as product.

The Lineage tab lays every release on one axis with the bet it made; Cost per task shows why a cheaper tier is not proportionally cheaper per task; gpt-oss on one GPU rebuilds the open models' parameter table and memory from their configuration.

How to read OpenAI's line

Tiers

Since GPT-5.6 a generation is sold as named price bands: a flagship (GPT-6 Astra), a mid tier (Sol) and a cheap, fast tier (Luna). GPT-5.6 also had a balanced tier, Terra, which GPT-6 has not replaced. OpenAI's docs map them onto the older names: Sol "roughly corresponds to the unsuffixed model tier", Terra to mini, Luna to nano ({{GPT-5.6 Sol|@sol56}}, {{Terra|@terra56}}, {{Luna|@luna56}}). A tier name says what a model costs and roughly how capable it is, not how it is built: no parameter count or architecture is published for any of them.

Reasoning effort

reasoning.effort sets how much hidden deliberation the model may spend before answering. Those reasoning tokens are not visible through the API, "still occupy space in the model's context window and are billed as output tokens" ({{reasoning guide|@reason}}). A benchmark figure means little without the effort it was run at. Which model accepts which effort is in the table under A request's cost.

The router

A classifier in front of the models, introduced with GPT-5, that decides per request whether to answer at once or to think, and how deeply. The generic trade-off (lower average cost, less predictable behaviour, a misroute the caller cannot see) is on {{Topic: llms (Deeper: test-time compute)|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}. Because the caller also picks the tier, choosing Sol, Terra or Luna is a routing decision made on top of the model's own.

Codex

Both OpenAI's coding agent (a command-line tool, then a cloud service) and, for a while, a line of models post-trained for it: GPT-5-Codex, then GPT-5.1-Codex, GPT-5.2-Codex and GPT-5.3-Codex (with GPT-5.1-Codex-Max). Since GPT-5.5 (April 2026) the mainline models ship directly in Codex ({{OpenAI release timeline|@hk}}).

Trusted Access

OpenAI's programme of vetted organisations that get restricted capabilities first or only: the most capable cybersecurity configurations, Astra for Law, GPT-Rosalind for life sciences.

Harness

The software between a task and a model: prompting, tool plumbing, how long conversations are compacted, and whether state survives between requests. On this family the harness is worth more points than any effort setting or model upgrade (the ARC-AGI-3 result).

Lineage, from GPT-1 to GPT-5.6

Dates and sources for every release are on the Lineage tab; this is what each step changed.

GPT-1, GPT-2, GPT-3 (2018 to 2020): the scaling bet

Three decoder-only transformers trained on next-token prediction, differing in little but size and data: 117M parameters ({{Wikipedia|@wgpt1}}), 1.5B ({{OpenAI|@gpt2}}) and 175B, dense ({{GPT-3 paper|@gpt3}}). GPT-2 showed that one unsupervised objective produced usable zero-shot behaviour on tasks nobody trained for. GPT-3 established in-context learning: a task specified by examples in the prompt, with no gradient update, which turns one served checkpoint into a general product and is why the paper is called "Language models are few-shot learners". It also anchored the scaling-law era, in which loss moves predictably with compute, shifting the research question from architecture search to compute allocation.

InstructGPT and ChatGPT (2022): the post-training stack

InstructGPT ({{paper|@igpt}}) introduced the three-stage recipe the field still uses:

  1. SFT (supervised fine-tuning) on human-written demonstrations.
  2. A reward model: a copy of the network with a scalar output head, trained on human rankings of pairs of outputs.
  3. RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback), in practice PPO (proximal policy optimisation) against that reward model, with a KL-divergence penalty back toward the SFT checkpoint so the policy does not drift into degenerate text that scores well and reads badly.

The result that changed the field: humans preferred the 1.3B InstructGPT to the 175B base model, so alignment became a way to gain usefulness rather than a tax on it. ChatGPT (30 November 2022) put the recipe in front of the public. The algorithms are owned by {{RL for LLMs: RLHF, GRPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}.

GPT-4 to GPT-4.1 (2023 to 2025): multimodality and unit cost

GPT-4 (March 2023) was the first frontier release to disclose essentially nothing about parameters, architecture or data, and the first with a long system card documenting refusal policy as a product surface; its practical contribution was reliability on long-form reasoning plus image input. GPT-4 Turbo cut the price and lengthened the context to 128K. GPT-4o ("omni", May 2024) put audio and vision into one model instead of pipelining speech recognition, a text model and speech synthesis, which brought voice latency to conversational range and made interruption, tone and non-speech audio cues possible at all. GPT-4.1 (April 2025) was API-only, tuned for long-context and instruction-following workloads rather than chat.

The o-series (from September 2024): buying accuracy with inference compute

o1, o3 and o4-mini were trained with large-scale RL on problems whose answers can be checked automatically, so the reward is correctness rather than human preference, and the learned behaviour is a long internal chain of thought that backtracks, checks its own steps and reframes a problem that is not working. Two choices were OpenAI's own. The chain of thought is hidden from the API caller but billed, so accuracy became a per-request purchase. And OpenAI published the claim that accuracy rises with RL training compute and, separately, with how long the model may think at inference ({{Learning to reason with LLMs|@l2r}}), which made inference compute a second scaling axis; the rest of the family is organised around managing it. The evidence for test-time scaling is on {{Topic: llms (Deeper: test-time compute)|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

GPT-5 (August 2025): routing as architecture

GPT-5's signature is a system, not a network: a fast non-reasoning model, a reasoning model, and a real-time router deciding per request how much thinking to spend. That is an economic decision expressed as architecture: most traffic does not need reasoning, and reasoning tokens dominate cost when applied indiscriminately. GPT-5.1 (November 2025) shipped the two as named models, Instant and Thinking, so a user could pick one instead of trusting the router; GPT-5.2, 5.4 and 5.5 followed ({{timeline|@hk}}). The Codex variants were the same line post-trained for long-horizon agentic coding: tuned to run for hours inside a harness, calling tools, reading diffs and recovering from failed edits, rather than to answer well in one shot.

GPT-5.6 (July 2026): tiers below a flagship

Released on 9 July 2026 (Sol previewed to trusted partners from 26 June, {{timeline|@hk}}) as Sol for the hardest work, Terra balanced and Luna fast and cheap, all with a 1.05M-token window, 128K maximum output and a 16 February 2026 knowledge cutoff ({{Simon Willison|@will}}). Launch prices per million input and output tokens: Sol $5 and $30, Terra $2.50 and $15, Luna $1 and $6. On 30 July Luna fell 80% to $0.20 and $1.20 and Terra 20% to $2 and $12 while Sol held; OpenAI credited serving optimisations, part of them kernels Sol rewrote itself ({{Eden AI|@eden}}). Sol later carried promotional pricing of $4 and $20, 20% and 33% below launch, "at least through November 21, 2026" ({{OpenAI model docs|@sol56}}; the start date, 21 August, is unconfirmed in the docs). The bare gpt-5.6 alias routes to Sol. Roboflow called Sol "clearly the best vision model OpenAI has released so far" ({{Roboflow|@robo}}). GPT-5.6 Sol is the baseline most GPT-6 figures are quoted against.

The GPT-6 generation

GPT-6 Astra: the flagship

First outputs surfaced on 29 August from an expanded internal test of a checkpoint tagged mozaik-alpha-fdm, zero-shot at max effort, showing complex frontend and visual software ({{TestingCatalog|@tc}}). Astra was announced on 3 September 2026 as a limited preview and released to paid users the next day; it was "the first time we've pretrained on more than 100,000 GPUs at our Stargate site in Texas", by far OpenAI's largest training run ({{Wikipedia|@wiki}}). Rollout went to Daybreak cybersecurity organisations first, then ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, the API, Microsoft and AWS Bedrock; enterprise access is off until an administrator enables it ({{CSO Online|@cso}}). On 10 September OpenAI paused new sign-ups and upgrades to the $200 ChatGPT Pro plan without giving a reason ({{CellCog|@cellcog}}).

API limits ({{model docs|@astra}})

1,050,000-token window, at most 922,000 input and 128,000 output tokens; text and image in, text out; knowledge cutoff 30 April 2026; efforts low to max (none returns HTTP 400).

Price per million ({{pricing|@pricing}})

$10 input, $1 cached, $12.50 cache write, $50 output. Above 272K input tokens the whole request is billed at 2× input and cache rates and 1.5× output. Batch and Flex half price; Fast mode 2× for "up to 2.5× faster" ({{Fast mode|@fast}}); Ultrafast 6×.

Not published

A parameter count or an architecture, continuing the GPT-5.x pattern. The system card does not mention the architecture either; what is known about recurrent depth comes from reporting (below).

OpenAI's launch table puts Astra at 57.9% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, 72.6% on OSWorld 2.0 and 41.4% on AutomationBench, among others (all rows on the Lineage card). The 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 that circulates with them is ARC Prize's run through a vendor adapter. Two things matter more than the table: part of Astra's reasoning is reported to happen where no transcript records it, and its headline ARC-AGI-3 number is a harness number.

GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna: the price release (22 September)

The two tiers beneath Astra cost half their GPT-5.6 predecessors or less: Sol $2 and $10 per million against GPT-5.6 Sol's $4 and $20, exactly half; Luna $0.10 and $0.50 against $0.20 and $1.20, which is 1 − 0.10/0.20 = 50% off input and 1 − 0.50/1.20 = 58.3% off output ({{VentureBeat|@vb6}}). Both have the 1.05M window and 128K output, accept efforts none to max with medium the default, and have knowledge cutoffs of 20 April (Sol) and 18 May 2026 (Luna) ({{GPT-6 Sol|@sol6}}, {{GPT-6 Luna|@luna}}). They are in the API as gpt-6-sol and gpt-6-luna, in ChatGPT Work and Codex for paid plans, and Luna reaches Free and Go users through the desktop app. OpenAI's headline for Sol: 33.2% on AutomationBench 1.0.6 at xhigh for $0.27 a task, against Claude Opus 5 at 26.9% at max costing 11.1 times as much per task; Luna reaches 66.6% on DeepSWE v1.1 at a task cost 93% below the compared Opus 5 configuration ({{VentureBeat|@vb6}}; the other figures are on the Lineage card).

GPT-6.1 Sol: near-Astra at a fifth of the price (29 September)

A week after GPT-6 Sol, whose documentation now points to it as the newer Sol. Same $2 and $10, cached input halved to $0.10 (5% of input, against 10% elsewhere), the same 1.05M window, efforts low to max with medium the default (none and minimal rejected), knowledge cutoff 30 April 2026 ({{model docs|@sol61}}). OpenAI's figures: it matches Astra on DeepSWE v1.1 "at roughly one-fifth the cost" and beats GPT-6 Sol's best there by 6.4 points; it comes within 2.1 points of Astra on the OSWorld 2.0 offline set at max effort; on AutomationBench at medium effort it improves 4.8 points on GPT-6 Sol and leads Claude Opus 5.5 by 2.2 points at roughly a third of its cost ({{VentureBeat|@vb61}}). The same day OpenAI launched Ultrafast, a service tier at 6× the standard rate for up to 8× faster generation in Codex and 6× in the API, as much as 300 tokens per second. It is available for GPT-6 Astra; a 6.1 Sol version is "coming soon" ({{VentureBeat|@vb61}}, {{Ultrafast guide|@ultra}}), and the pricing page lists Ultrafast rates for Astra only. This is the pattern the Claude line also showed in 2026: the tier below the flagship catches it within weeks at a fraction of the price, which leaves the flagship's premium resting on the hardest tasks.

GPT-6.1 Astra: withheld (28 September)

OpenAI shelved GPT-6.1 Astra, planned for October, after internal audits found it less reliable at staying within the authority it was given and at reporting what it had done, with higher measured deception than its predecessor. Saachi Jain, head of safety systems: it "didn't quite meet the bar in terms of staying within scope and authorization, and how it communicates back to the user about the type of work it's done" ({{The Hacker News|@thn}}). GPT-6 Astra stays the flagship; the Wall Street Journal called it "a rare case of a major AI developer ditching a new release because of safety concerns".
