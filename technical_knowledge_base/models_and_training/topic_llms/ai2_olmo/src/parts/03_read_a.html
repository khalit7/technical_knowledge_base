Open weights against fully open

"Open weights" and "open source" get used interchangeably and are not remotely the same thing. A typical open-weights release (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek) gives you one artefact: the final parameter tensors, plus a model card and a table of scores. Everything that produced them stays inside the lab.

OLMo, from the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2), established the practice of releasing the production process rather than the product ({{OLMo 1 paper|@o1}}: "unlike most prior efforts that have only released model weights and inference code, we release OLMo alongside open training data and training and evaluation code"). Each item it adds unlocks a kind of research that is otherwise impossible. It was the only line doing so until September 2026, when the Institute of Foundation Models in Abu Dhabi published K2 Horizon, six Apache 2.0 models from 0.9B to 375B with weights, training code, training data and methodology (detail on {{Other notable providers|n:3c65c17b0d0d8154ba44e521a4ed8749}}; the largest is listed on {{Artificial Analysis|@aak2}} as released 3 September 2026). The category now has two members on two continents under different funders, which matters because a single example of a practice is a curiosity and two is a norm forming.

Ai2 calls the whole path the model flow: "every stage, checkpoint, dataset, and dependency required to create and modify it" ({{Olmo 3 blog|@o3b}}). What it ships that others withhold, with the Olmo 3 32B release as the example:

1. The corpus and its tooling

The pretraining corpus itself (Dolma) as downloadable documents rather than a description, plus the deduplication, quality-filtering, decontamination and tokenisation pipeline that turned raw crawl into it, so the corpus can be rebuilt or varied rather than merely inspected. Olmo 3's pool is 9.31T tokens; its heuristic web filtering took 252.6B documents to 38.7B, removing 84.9%, in about 1,030 hours on EC2 instances ({{Olmo 3 report|@o3r}}, Tables 4 and 36).

2. The mixture and its order

The proportions of each source and the curriculum across stages. Two models with identical architectures and different mixtures are different models, and the mixture is the part labs guard hardest. Olmo 3's three mixes are drawn in the Data mix tab. Ai2 notes the released mixes are "reconstructions of the data sampled during our actual training runs" ({{report|@o3r}}, note to Table 4).

3. Training code and full configs

So a run can be reproduced rather than approximated from a paper's hyperparameter table: the {{OLMo-core|@ocore}} trainer, {{open-instruct|@oi}} for post-training, {{OLMES|@olmes}} for evaluation, and every stage's batch size, learning rate and parallelism ({{report|@o3r}}, Tables 34 and 35).

4. Intermediate checkpoints

Not just the final one: the single most valuable item on the list, and the rarest. The Olmo 3 32B base repository has 718 checkpoint branches: 655 from pretraining, 49 from midtraining, 14 from long-context extension ({{Hugging Face|@hf32ref}}, counted 1 October 2026).

5. Training logs

Loss curves, spikes, restarts, and what was changed when. The failures are as informative as the final number and are almost never published. Ai2's Weights & Biases projects are public: 69 runs for Olmo 3 32B alone ({{W&B ai2-llm|@wb32}}), drawn in the Training log tab.

6. The post-training stack

Tulu 3 for OLMo 2, Dolci for Olmo 3: the actual SFT datasets, preference data, RL prompt sets and recipe code (OlmoRL), not a paragraph describing them. Dolci "provides separate mixes for each stage of post-training: SFT, DPO, and RLVR" ({{Olmo 3 blog|@o3b}}).

What the extra artefacts let you do

None of this matters if you only run inference. It matters if you do research. Pick a question to see which artefacts it needs, and whether an open-weights release could answer it.

The two that matter most in practice: contamination becomes checkable, because if you cannot search the training corpus every benchmark number is unfalsifiable in principle, and here you can grep for the eval set; and interventions get a real control, because a fork of a released OLMo checkpoint with the released mixture is a controlled experiment, while fine-tuning someone's closed-corpus weights is not.

The price: capability

Publishing your corpus means you cannot use data you have no clear right to publish, and that constraint alone puts a ceiling on OLMo relative to labs that train on whatever they can reach. Ai2 also says it does not "collect from sites that explicitly disallow it" ({{Olmo 3 blog|@o3b}}). So treat the family as the reference stack for understanding how models are trained, and as the base for reproducible research (Ettin, the ladder-scaling studies), rather than as the model you would serve for raw scores.
