Open weights against fully open

"Open weights" and "open source" get used interchangeably and are not remotely the same thing. A typical open-weights release (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek) gives you one artefact: the final parameter tensors, plus a model card and a table of scores. Everything that produced them stays inside the lab.

OLMo, from the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2), established the practice of releasing the production process rather than the product (OLMo 1 paper: "unlike most prior efforts that have only released model weights and inference code, we release OLMo alongside open training data and training and evaluation code"). Each item it adds unlocks a kind of research that is otherwise impossible. It was the only line doing so until September 2026, when the Institute of Foundation Models in Abu Dhabi published K2 Horizon, six Apache 2.0 models from 0.9B to 375B with weights, training code, training data and methodology (detail on Other notable providers; the largest is listed on Artificial Analysis as released 3 September 2026). The category now has two members on two continents under different funders, which matters because a single example of a practice is a curiosity and two is a norm forming.

Ai2 calls the whole path the model flow: "every stage, checkpoint, dataset, and dependency required to create and modify it" (Olmo 3 blog). What it ships that others withhold, with the Olmo 3 32B release as the example:

1. The corpus and its tooling

The pretraining corpus itself (Dolma) as downloadable documents rather than a description, plus the deduplication, quality-filtering, decontamination and tokenisation pipeline that turned raw crawl into it, so the corpus can be rebuilt or varied rather than merely inspected. Olmo 3's pool is 9.31T tokens; its heuristic web filtering took 252.6B documents to 38.7B, removing 84.9%, in about 1,030 hours on EC2 instances (Olmo 3 report, Tables 4 and 36).

2. The mixture and its order

The proportions of each source and the curriculum across stages. Two models with identical architectures and different mixtures are different models, and the mixture is the part labs guard hardest. Olmo 3's three mixes are drawn in the Data mix tab. Ai2 notes the released mixes are "reconstructions of the data sampled during our actual training runs" (report, note to Table 4).

3. Training code and full configs

So a run can be reproduced rather than approximated from a paper's hyperparameter table: the OLMo-core trainer, open-instruct for post-training, OLMES for evaluation, and every stage's batch size, learning rate and parallelism (report, Tables 34 and 35).

4. Intermediate checkpoints

Not just the final one: the single most valuable item on the list, and the rarest. The Olmo 3 32B base repository has 718 checkpoint branches: 655 from pretraining, 49 from midtraining, 14 from long-context extension (Hugging Face, counted 1 October 2026).

5. Training logs

Loss curves, spikes, restarts, and what was changed when. The failures are as informative as the final number and are almost never published. Ai2's Weights & Biases projects are public: 69 runs for Olmo 3 32B alone (W&B ai2-llm), drawn in the Training log tab.

6. The post-training stack

Tulu 3 for OLMo 2, Dolci for Olmo 3: the actual SFT datasets, preference data, RL prompt sets and recipe code (OlmoRL), not a paragraph describing them. Dolci "provides separate mixes for each stage of post-training: SFT, DPO, and RLVR" (Olmo 3 blog).

What the extra artefacts let you do

None of this matters if you only run inference. It matters if you do research. Pick a question to see which artefacts it needs, and whether an open-weights release could answer it.

The two that matter most in practice: contamination becomes checkable, because if you cannot search the training corpus every benchmark number is unfalsifiable in principle, and here you can grep for the eval set; and interventions get a real control, because a fork of a released OLMo checkpoint with the released mixture is a controlled experiment, while fine-tuning someone's closed-corpus weights is not.

The price: capability

Publishing your corpus means you cannot use data you have no clear right to publish, and that constraint alone puts a ceiling on OLMo relative to labs that train on whatever they can reach. Ai2 also says it does not "collect from sites that explicitly disallow it" (Olmo 3 blog). On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index the gap is wide, though the bars below mix that constraint with size and age, so they do not measure the constraint on its own:

Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3, read 1 October 2026. Olmo 3.1 32B Think's score is marked by AA as estimated; it is a 32B dense model from December 2025, the others are larger and newer. The median is over the 21 open-weights models with a score in this knowledge base's snapshot.

So treat the family as the reference stack for understanding how models are trained, and as the base for reproducible research (Ettin, the ladder-scaling studies), rather than as the model you would serve for raw scores.

Dolma is arguably the more important artefact: it made "here is exactly what the model read" possible at all.

OLMo 2: stability fixes and a two-stage curriculum

7B, 13B and 32B trained on 4T, 5.6T and 6.6T tokens in total (OLMo 2 report), and the release that documented training-stability fixes other labs treat as tribal knowledge. Two are worth knowing.

Reordered (post) normalisation. The norm moves from the input of the attention and feed-forward blocks to their output, so the residual stream itself stays unnormalised. OLMo-0424 used the first line, OLMo 2 the second (report, Table 1 and section 3.3.2):
h=x+Attn(LN(x)),hout=h+MLP(LN(h))h=x+RMSNorm(Attn(x)),hout=h+RMSNorm(MLP(h))
QK-norm. Queries and keys are normalised before their dot product, which bounds the attention logits. Unbounded logit growth is a standard cause of mid-run divergence at scale, and this removes it cheaply. OLMo-0424 instead clipped QKV values to 8:
logits=RMSNorm(q)·RMSNorm(k)dhead

The report credits the two together with fewer loss spikes in its runs, alongside a z-loss term, a plain normal initialisation, AdamW epsilon 10⁻⁸, no weight decay on embeddings and filtering of repeated n-grams (all on the OLMo 2 paper page). Because both runs' logs are public, the effect can be checked rather than taken on trust:

Spike score as the OLMo 2 report defines it (section 3.2): the share of values at least 7 standard deviations from the mean of the previous 1,000. Computed here on the points Weights & Biases samples from each run's log, not every step, so it is comparable between the two runs but not with the report's own figures. Both curves are in the Training log tab.

The two-stage curriculum

Bulk pretraining on the broad web mixture (OLMo 2 Mix 1124, 3.9T tokens, over 95% web), then a final annealing phase on Dolmino, a much smaller high-quality mix (filtered web, FLAN, peS2o papers, Wikipedia, Stack Exchange and a 10.7B-token maths set) fed while the learning rate decays to zero (report, Tables 4, 5 and 13). Each annealing run is done several times with different data orders and the weights averaged ("souping"). The chart shows the base model's scores after each stage; Olmo 3 kept the recipe and added a long-context stage.

Concentrating the good data at the end, when updates are small and precise, lifts the scores sharply: on the OLMo 2 7B, Math goes from 12.7 to 41.7 with about 4% more tokens. Whether this beats spreading the same tokens through the whole run is not directly measured in either report (unconfirmed); the reports measure the gain over the pretraining checkpoint. The two 32B souping figures disagree inside the Olmo 3 report: its text says the soup improved Math by 2.9 and 1.6 over the two runs, its Table 13 gives 69.7 against 66.8 and 65.4, which is 2.9 and 4.3.

The 32B was claimed as the "first fully-open model (all data, code, weights, and details are freely available) to outperform GPT3.5-Turbo and GPT-4o mini" (Ai2, March 2025). Tulu 3 shipped alongside as the post-training recipe: supervised fine-tuning, then DPO (Direct Preference Optimization, which optimises the policy directly on pairs of preferred and rejected responses using a closed-form rewriting of the RLHF objective, so no separate reward model and no rollouts are needed), then RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards, where the reward is a mechanical check of the final answer, matching a maths result or running unit tests, rather than a learned preference model that can be gamed by style) (Tulu 3). The OLMo 2 1B and 32B used GRPO for the RL step (OLMo 2 paper page).

Olmo 3: the model flow

Instead of a model, Ai2 published the path: "checkpoints from each major training milestone: the base pretrained model, the mid-trained model after targeted skill enhancement, the long-context-extended version, plus post-training checkpoints for the Olmo 3-Think, Olmo 3-Instruct, and Olmo 3-RL Zero flows", with the data used at each stage, so a researcher can "fork the model at whatever point best fits" (Olmo 3 blog). At launch (20 November 2025) that meant Base and Think at 7B and 32B, Instruct at 7B and RL-Zero; Instruct 32B followed with 3.1.

  • Base. Dense, 32 and 64 layers; the 32B uses grouped-query attention (40 query, 8 key-value heads); sliding-window attention on 3 of 4 layers with a 4,096-token window; QK-norm and output norms carried over from OLMo 2 (report, Table 33). Pretrained on Dolma 3 Mix, midtrained on Dolmino, then extended from 8,192 to 65,536 tokens of context on Longmino.
  • Think. SFT on Dolci-Think, then DPO with "delta learning": chosen answers from Qwen3-32B thinking, rejected ones from the much weaker Qwen3-0.6B, because fine-tuning on the strong answers alone hurt (report, Table 21), then RLVR with OlmoRL, whose inflight weight updates and continuous batching made RL "4x more efficient" (blog). It was the first fully open 32B reasoning model with the entire RL pipeline public.
  • Cost. About 56 days from the start of training to the evaluated Think 32B on a dedicated cluster of 1,024 H100s (report, section 2.4):
1,024 GPUs × 56 days × 24 h = 1,376,256 GPU-hours; × $2 per hour = $2.75M
The $2 per H100-hour and $2.75M are the report's. Olmo 3.1's RL extension, 224 GPUs × 21 days × 24 h = 112,896 GPU-hours, about $0.23M at the same price, is derived here, not stated by Ai2.

"Six times fewer tokens"

The efficiency claim the family rests on, in Ai2's words: Think 32B is "narrowing the gap to the best open-weight models of similar scale – such as Qwen 3 32B – while training on roughly 6x fewer tokens" (blog). It is often summarised as matching Qwen3-32B; Ai2's own table does not support that across the board:

36T ÷ (5.5T + 0.1T + 0.1T + 0.1T) = 36 ÷ 5.8 ≈ 6.2
Qwen3's approximately 36T pretraining tokens (Qwen) against Olmo 3 32B's 5.5T pretraining, two 100B midtraining runs and 100B long-context (report, Table 35). Table 13 gives a cumulative 6.2T, but that does not match Table 35's own stages, which sum to 5.8T; with 6.2T the ratio would be 5.8. Post-training tokens are excluded on both sides.

Olmo 3.1 (12 December 2025) resumed the best RL run "for an additional 21 days on 224 GPUs with extra epochs over our Dolci-Think-RL dataset", gaining "5+ points on AIME, 4+ points on ZebraLogic, 4+ points on IFEval" (blog); in the table, IFBench gains 20.5. The same update added Olmo 3.1 Instruct 32B, Ai2's "most capable fully open chat model to date".

Ettin: one variable at a time

Paired encoders and decoders from 17M to 1B parameters, trained on up to 2T tokens by Johns Hopkins with LightOn (Ettin paper). Each pair shares one ModernBERT-style recipe built from open OLMo-family data (DCLM with Dolma 1.7 sources, then Dolmino-style sources for the later phases), with only the attention pattern and objective differing (Ettin paper page).

The value is methodological. Encoders (bidirectional attention, masked language modelling, contextual embeddings for retrieval and classification) and decoders (causal attention, next-token prediction) had never been compared with data, scale, recipe and tokeniser all held fixed, so every prior comparison confounded the objective with everything else. With only the objective varying, encoders win on classification and retrieval and decoders win on generation, and continued training across objectives does not close either gap: "a 400M encoder outperforms a 1B decoder on MNLI, and vice versa for generative tasks" (abstract). Directly useful when choosing an embedding backbone versus a generative one: the pretraining objective, not size, decides it. Ettin also releases 200+ checkpoints and the training order segmented by checkpoint, the OLMo practice carried over.

How the stack gets used

  • The standard testbed for interventions. Per-stage checkpoints make the family the default base for data ablations, RL variants and interpretability work that needs a training trajectory. OlmoTrace, in Ai2's playground, traces parts of a response back to training documents (blog).
  • Efficiency over scale. Competitive quality at roughly 6x fewer tokens, through data curation (Dolma 3, the Dolmino and Dolci mixes) and staged curricula rather than more compute.
  • Ladder scaling. Fitting scaling curves on many small controlled runs to predict a large model's downstream task accuracy before paying for it: model and data size predict a loss, the loss predicts task accuracy, and the ladder costs 1% of the compute of the 7B (4T tokens) and 13B (5T tokens) targets it predicts (paper). That needs dozens of runs with a fixed recipe and open data, which only this stack provides.
  • Provenance. US-origin fully open matters politically as well as scientifically: an answer to Chinese open weights for auditable government and regulated deployments, where provenance is a procurement requirement rather than a preference, and a buyer who must answer "what is in this model" has an answer. It is no longer the only such answer: K2 Horizon gives Gulf-funded buyers a second fully open provenance chain.

Training science in general (curricula, annealing, post-training) belongs to Topic: llm-training-and-post-training; OLMo 3 Think as an open replication of reasoning training is on Topic: llms (Deeper: test-time compute).

Data mix

The mixture is the part labs guard hardest, and the part Ai2 publishes in full. Each stage draws a mix from a larger pool: a source sampled at more than 1x is repeated or upweighted, one below 1x is thinned. Compare the stages to see the curriculum: broad web first, then a small high-quality and synthetic mix while the learning rate decays, then long documents.
