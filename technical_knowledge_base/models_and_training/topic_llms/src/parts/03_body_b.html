Release timeline

Every significant release of every lab on this page, February 2023 to September 2026, one row per lab: filled dots are open weights, rings are closed. The bars underneath count releases per quarter (per month or week when zoomed in). Read it for three things a leaderboard cannot show: the rhythm of each lab, the order in which ideas spread (mixture-of-experts, then reasoning, then hybrid attention), and the share of releases that ship open weights. The last quarter is the densest; zoom in to 2026 or to August and September to separate its releases.

Try: pick Reasoning and tick label each lab's first: OpenAI's o1-preview in September 2024, then Qwen, Google, DeepSeek and Moonshot within four months. Do the same for MoE, where xAI, Mistral and DeepSeek were first and OpenAI's first open MoE arrived in August 2025. Click any dot for the release and its source.

How to read it

  • The steps, not every version. The table behind it keeps each release that defined a step and drops minor point releases, so "first" means first in this table; the counts per quarter show the pace of steps, not of every update.
  • Dates are launch dates from each lab's announcement or a dated report; seven are known only to the month and sit mid-month.
  • Sizes are only what labs publish, which is why closed models carry none.
  • The data is the child page LLM release history, one sourced row per release.

Benchmarks: the jagged frontier

Every reported score for the benchmarks that at least three models report, model by model. Read across a row to see where a model is strong; read down a column to see who leads a task. Two things stand out before any number: no model leads every column, and most cells are empty, because each lab publishes the benchmarks it chooses.

Try: switch between the two shadings. Within each benchmark darkens the best score in each column, so you compare models on the same test. Raw score shades by the percentage itself, so you see which tests are still hard. Click any score to see who reported it and the sentence it comes from.
Filled: · = not reported◆ = run by an independent evaluator (Artificial Analysis), the rest are reported by the labs themselvesTwo values in a cell: the lab's figure, then the independent one

How to read it

  • A number travels with its version. Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 2.1 are different tests, as are DeepSWE and DeepSWE v1.1; they are kept in separate columns or left out, never merged.
  • Most of these are vendor tables. Anthropic's own table supplies most of the Claude, Opus 5 and Astra cells; where one sentence gives two rivals' scores, they are read in the order the sentence lists them.
  • Same model, two numbers. Grok 4.7 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 is 38.0% in xAI's figure and 33% when Artificial Analysis ran it inside Grok Build.
  • GDPval-AA is an Elo rating, not a percentage, so it is shaded only within its own column.

Capability against price

Each point is a model on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (a composite of many evaluations), against what it costs. The dashed staircase is the efficient frontier: the models no other model beats on both axes at once. Anything below and to the right of it buys less capability for more money than something on it.

Try: switch the horizontal axis from cost per task to price per token and watch the frontier change. Price per token rewards cheap tokens; cost per task also counts how many tokens a model spends thinking, the point of the Reading tab's section on cost per task. Then tick show effort settings: the same model at higher effort climbs and moves right, buying capability with tokens.

On the frontier now:

How to read it

  • One index version only. Every value is v4.3, read on 1 October 2026. Figures published before the September rescaling, such as launch-day scores of 57 to 62, are on an earlier scale and cannot be placed on this chart.
  • Cheap tokens can mean an expensive task. Claude Sonnet 5.5 at max effort costs half as much per token as Opus 5.5 but more per index task ($7.62 against $5.98), because it spends more tokens.
  • Open weights sit on the cheap end of the frontier. MiMo-V2.6-Pro reaches 46.3 for about $0.13 a task; the closed leaders reach the mid-fifties for several dollars.
  • A composite hides the spread. The Benchmarks tab shows how differently the same models rank task by task.
