Release history

Every significant release of every lab on this page, February 2023 to September 2026: one sourced row per release, with its date, weights and licence, published size and why it matters. Every view below draws from the same 140 rows and the same filters, and each answers something a leaderboard cannot: the rhythm of each lab, the order in which ideas spread (mixture-of-experts, then reasoning, then hybrid attention), how total size and compute per token came apart, and the share of releases that ship open weights.

Sizes: published size over time

Sizes are in billions of parameters (1T = 1,000B). A dense model uses every parameter for every token, so it has one number. A mixture-of-experts (MoE) model routes each token through a few of many experts, so it has two, total / active: total must sit in memory, active sets the compute per token. Total ÷ active is its sparsity; a dense model's is 1 ({{inside an MoE|#t-moe}} has the mechanism).

Defaults reproduce the rows' own ratios, by construction (same two numbers): DeepSeek-V3 671 ÷ 37 = 18.1, the note's "18x sparsity"; DeepSeek V4 Pro 1,600 ÷ 49 = 32.7, the note's "33x".

Below, every filtered row with a published size against its date, on a log scale. In Total and active an MoE release is a line from its total (top) to its active parameters (bottom); a dense release is one dot. The dashed step is the largest open total so far; the band is the median active size of open releases over the trailing four quarters.

What it shows. Total size and compute per token came apart. The largest open model grew about 43-fold in three and a half years (65.2B LLaMA to 2.8T Kimi K3), but the typical open release still computes each token with a few tens of billions of parameters, because almost every large release since DeepSeek-V3 is an MoE. Memory (total) became the cost that grew; compute per token (active) is the one labs held down.

What it shows. About the data

What it is. The significant releases of each lab this page covers, 24 labs from February 2023 to 30 September 2026 (data as of 1 October 2026), one row each. Dates and sizes come from the lab's announcement, model card or a dated report, linked on every row; where this page gives a date or a parameter count elsewhere, the table agrees with it. A row's date is the first public announcement or release: where a preview and general availability differ, the row uses the first and the row's date note gives the other. Minor point releases this page never names are left out, so the table shows the steps, not every version. Rows per year: 2023, 12; 2024, 22; 2025, 46; 2026 (nine months), 60.

Kind tags (flagship, reasoning, MoE, dense, multimodal, small, hybrid attention, encoder-decoder) were assigned by whoever compiled the table, from each row's note and source: they are this table's labels, not the labs' own categories, and they drive the filters and the firsts.

Size conventions. Closed models almost never publish a size, and "not disclosed" says so rather than estimating; the two closed rows with a size are Qwen3.8-Max-0902, the API snapshot of the open 2.4T-A95B weights, and Step 5 Preview, whose size was announced before its weights. Family rows (Gemma, Gemma 3, Gemma 4, Llama 2, Llama 3, OLMo 2, Qwen2.5, Aya Expanse) give the largest published size; Llama 4 uses Maverick (400 / 17; Scout is 109B total); K2 Horizon gives its 375B-A23B member of six; DeepSeek V4.1-Flash gives the 16B active in decode (8B in prefill). Where the old table had no figure for an open model, it is now filled from the model card and the row marked (corrections below).

Weights and licences. Open means the weights can be downloaded, not one set of rights. Grouping the licences into four kinds (this page's grouping) shows the shift: in 2023 and 2024 fewer than half of open releases came under Apache 2.0 or MIT (4 of 9, then 4 of 16); from 2025 three in four did (25 of 32, then 24 of 33).

Counted from the Weights column. Permissive: Apache 2.0 or MIT. With conditions: Modified MIT, and GLM-5.3's MIT-style licence with a security review for model-as-a-service firms above $10B revenue. Own licence: Llama, Gemma, DeepSeek, Tongyi Qianwen, Qwen, Tencent, MiniMax, Kimi K3 and ChatGLM2 licences. Non-commercial: research-only licences and CC-BY-NC 4.0. Magistral counts as permissive for its open Small model. Click a segment to filter the table to it.

Caveats

Seven rows are dated to the month only.
Hunyuan-T1 (2025-03), Step-3 (2025-07), Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B (2025-09), Kimi K2 Thinking (2025-11), OLMo 3.1 32B (2025-12), Step 3.5 Flash (2026-02) and Hy3 (2026-04) could not be pinned to a day from a primary source. They sort to the start of their month and are drawn at the 15th.
Point releases are left out.
GPT-4 Turbo, o1, o3-mini, GPT-4.5, GPT-5.1, 5.2, 5.4 and 5.5, Claude 2 and 2.1, Opus 4.1 and 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Grok 4.1, Gemma 2, Gemini 2.0 Flash, PaLM 2, and minor Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok, MiniMax and Cohere point releases. Counts per lab therefore measure steps, not every version, and "first" means first in this table (OpenAI's first open weights here is gpt-oss in August 2025, "since GPT-2" by its own row: {{InfoQ|https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/08/openai-gpt-oss/}}).
Two rows are not language models in the usual sense.
Fugu (Sakana AI) is an orchestrator over other models and Jev (TypeSafe AI) a structured-decision model; both are listed because this page discusses them. Use the search box or the lab filter to leave them out.
Some dates and weights need a footnote.
Grok-1 is dated to its 3 November 2023 chatbot launch; its 314B MoE weights came under Apache 2.0 on 17 March 2024 ({{xAI|https://x.ai/news/grok-os}}). Step 5 Preview is listed as closed: weights are promised for 15 October 2026 with no licence named, and the Hugging Face repository was empty on 30 September. Llama 3 covers the April 2024 8B and 70B release; the 405B has its own Llama 3.1 row (July 2024), while "Llama 3: 8B, 70B and 405B" elsewhere follows the Llama 3 Herd paper, which covers both.
Where sources disagree.
MiMo-V2.6-Pro and Flash: Wikipedia and DataNorth say 21 September, Unite.ai and others 22 September (likely the time zone); the table keeps the 22nd ({{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaomi_MiMo}}). MiMo-V2.5: Xiaomi's release gives 310B total, while Naive-N0.5-Flash's "matching size" implies 309B; the table keeps 310, and 309B is MiMo-V2-Flash ({{Xiaomi|https://x.com/XiaomiMiMo/status/2048821516079661561}}).
Sources and licences not all primary.
Several rows cite Wikipedia or secondary reports (DataCamp, Unite.ai, llm-stats, MarkTechPost, Neowin) where the lab's page could not be reached (openai.com refused automated requests) or was undated; primary sources were used wherever they could be fetched. Licence names for some older models (the DeepSeek License for LLM 67B to V3, the Tongyi Qianwen License for Qwen1.5-MoE, the Qwen License for Qwen2.5-72B, MIT for Phi-2) were not all confirmed against the original release pages. Unconfirmed details: the KDA and IndexPool names for GLM-5.3-Flash were not found in its sources; qk-clip is confirmed for the Kimi K2 line, not for K3.
