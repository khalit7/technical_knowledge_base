What xAI is

xAI is the frontier lab behind Grok, founded by Elon Musk and eleven researchers in March 2023 (Wikipedia). Since February 2026 it has been a subsidiary of SpaceX, and since July 2026 it trades as SpaceXAI (Owner and name). Its strategy is the least algorithmic and the most industrial of any frontier lab.

Where DeepSeek optimises cost per token and Ai2 optimises reproducibility, xAI optimises the rate at which it turns capital into training compute and then into shipped models. It has published very little method: one open architecture (Grok-1), one open checkpoint of Grok 2, and launch posts. What it has demonstrated is that a datacentre built in months, plus a willingness to spend reinforcement-learning compute at pretraining scale, is enough to stay among the top five labs without an obvious architectural edge. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (read 1 October 2026) its best model, Grok 4.7, scores 46.4, and SpaceXAI is the fifth lab by best model, behind Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Meta (AA leaderboard). Its two durable differentiators are the real-time X firehose as a data source and a looser alignment posture than any other frontier lab (Training, Posture and security).

One question runs through the page: how does xAI turn capital into compute into shipped models, and what do its published numbers really say about where Grok stands? The tabs: Lineage puts every model, licence, corporate event and cluster on one time axis; Price and position places Grok among every model Artificial Analysis has scored, by index and by what a task costs.

Owner and name: four different dates

Two descriptions of the same event circulate: "xAI merged into SpaceX in February 2026 to form SpaceXAI", and "acquired in February, renamed SpaceXAI in July". The primary sources separate four events, and once they are separated the two descriptions stop disagreeing about the facts; only "to form SpaceXAI in February" is wrong.

2 Feb 2026: the legal event
SpaceX's S-1 registration statement: X.AI Holdings Corp. "was acquired by SpaceX, effective February 2, 2026 (the 'xAI Merger')", and elsewhere "We acquired xAI in February 2026 as the foundational platform for our AI segment" (S-1). It was an all-stock deal that made xAI a wholly owned subsidiary, valuing SpaceX at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion, $1.25 trillion combined (Wikipedia). The filing still calls the subsidiary "xAI" (X.AI Holdings LLC). So "merged" (the filing's own label) and "acquired" (its own verb) are both right.
6 May 2026: the name first appears
In announcing its compute deal with Anthropic, "the company formerly known as xAI referred to itself as 'SpaceXAI'", the first use The Verge had seen (The Verge).
6 July 2026: the rebrand completes
New logo and X handle, under SpaceX (Business Insider). Wikipedia: "rebranded as SpaceXAI in July" (Wikipedia). SiliconANGLE on 12 August: "known as xAI until last month" (SiliconANGLE).
8 July 2026: first model under the name
Grok 4.5, released two days after the rebrand, is the first Grok launched as SpaceXAI's (MarkTechPost; xAI release notes). Grok 4.7 is therefore not the first release under the new name, only the latest. xAI's docs now also call May's Grok Build 0.1 "SpaceXAI's coding model", but the release notes are edited in place, so that wording is not evidence of the name in May.
Resolution. February 2026 is when SpaceX bought xAI; July 2026 is when xAI became SpaceXAI. The current legal name is SpaceXAI LLC (its site footer, and Wikipedia); the Grok brand, the x.ai domain and the xAI API kept their names. The same filing records the earlier combination: xAI acquired X Holdings, owner of the X social network, effective 28 March 2025 (the "X Merger"), which is why the X firehose is in-house data rather than a licence (S-1). SpaceX then listed on Nasdaq in June 2026 (SiliconANGLE), and in August SpaceXAI completed a $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor, the AI code editor, closing on 14 August (Wikipedia).

Colossus: compute as the strategy

The lab's core claim is construction speed. Training one model across tens of thousands of accelerators needs them behind one high-bandwidth fabric, with the power and cooling to run them together, so what is hard is building and networking the site, not buying the GPUs. The S-1 gives the only audited figures: the first Colossus cluster in Memphis, about 100,000 H100 GPUs and about 130 MW, came online in 122 days in the shell of an existing factory; the first Colossus II cluster, about 110,000 GB200s and about 210 MW, in 91 days; its second cluster, 110,000 GB300s and 220 MW, in 64 days. The next phase is to add at least 220,000 GB300s and over 400 MW, and the two sites together provide about 1.0 GW today. Against those it sets "an industry benchmark to bring online a 100 megawatt greenfield data center", approximately two years (S-1).

Defaults reproduce the S-1's figures by construction (they are its inputs); the rates are derived as megawatts divided by days, so 130 / 122 = 1.07 MW a day for the first cluster against 100 / 730 = 0.14 for the benchmark. A greenfield site is not a repurposed factory, so the first comparison flatters Colossus; the 64-day second cluster at an existing site is the fairest like-for-like.

A correction. "Roughly 200K H100s stood up in 122 days" merges two numbers. The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s (S-1); by Grok 3's launch xAI called Colossus a "200,000 GPU cluster" (xAI), and Grok 4's post says "Colossus, our 200k GPU cluster" (xAI). Wikipedia dates full operation to December 2024 and peak demand to 150 MW, met partly by 14 portable methane generators while the grid agreement was worked out (Wikipedia). Expansion continued toward the "at least 1 million" GPUs xAI announced with a third Memphis building on 30 December 2025, for nearly 2 GW (Wikipedia); the S-1's 1.0 GW is what is running.

The twist: the capacity is also sold. In May 2026 SpaceX signed cloud services agreements giving Anthropic compute across Colossus and Colossus II for $1.25 billion a month through May 2029, terminable by either side on 90 days' notice, while "Grok 5 ... is currently being trained at COLOSSUS II" (S-1). A lab whose strategy is owning compute is now also renting the surplus to a rival, which is the clearest sign that the build rate has outrun what its own training needs. The gas turbines at Colossus 2 are the subject of a Clean Air Act suit filed in April 2026 (Wikipedia). How a cluster's fabric and power decide what can be trained on it is on Topic: hardware.

Grok-1: the one published architecture

Grok-1 was previewed in November 2023 as "the best we could do with 2 months of training" (Wikipedia); its pretraining concluded in October 2023, on a custom stack of JAX and Rust. On 17 March 2024 xAI released its base weights and architecture under Apache 2.0: "a 314 billion parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with 25% of the weights active on a given token" (xAI). In a mixture of experts (MoE), each layer's feed-forward block is split into several experts and a small router sends each token to only a few, so total parameters (what you store) and per-token compute (what you pay to run) decouple. Grok-1 has 8 experts per layer and runs 2 (run.py and model.py). Grok 2 followed as open weights in August 2025 (Hugging Face calls it Grok 2, Wikipedia Grok 2.5), not under Apache 2.0 but under the xAI Community License: research and non-commercial use, and commercial use only within xAI's acceptable use policy (licence). The pattern holds: open the previous generation once it is commercially spent, with no architecture published for anything after Grok 2.

The released code is enough to count the parameters. Every number below is computed from the configuration in the code, not typed in:

N=L(2dnqdh+2dnkvdh+E·3df+dE)+2Vd
L
layers (64)
d
model width (6,144)
nq, nkv, dh
query heads (48), key-value heads (8, grouped-query attention), head size (128)
E, f
experts per layer (8) and each expert's hidden size (32,768, from int(8·6144)·2//3); a gated feed-forward has 3 matrices
dE
the router
V
vocabulary (131,072); 2Vd counts the input embedding and the output head

Active parameters use the same sum with E replaced by the 2 experts that run. The key-value cache per token is 2 · L · nkv · dh · bytes.

Try: switch to Grok 2 and look at the active share: a dense feed-forward that every token runs, beside the experts, takes it from about a quarter to over 40%. Then raise the context to 128K: at 8 key-value heads the cache is 256 KiB a token, a sixth of what all 48 heads would need.

Defaults reproduce xAI's "314 billion" independently, within 1%: 316.5B with a separate output head (315.7B if tied). They do not reproduce "25% active": the sum gives 84.6B, 26.7%, because 25% is just 2 of 8 experts and ignores the attention and embeddings every token uses. For Grok 2, xAI publishes no parameter count; its config.json gives 269.5B total and 114.9B active (derived here), and at 16 bits 269.5B is 502 GiB, which reproduces the model card's "approximately 500 GB" checkpoint independently (Grok 2 card). The card asks for 8 GPUs with more than 40 GB each.

Grok 1.5 to Grok 4.5: catching up, then reasoning

Every release with its date and source is on the Lineage tab; this is what changed.

Grok-1.5 and Grok-2 (2024): catching up to the GPT-4 class. Grok-1.5 was announced on 29 March 2024 with "improved reasoning" and a 128,000-token context, and reached all X Premium users on 15 May; its vision variant, Grok-1.5V, was never released. Grok-2 and Grok-2 mini followed on 14 August 2024, with image generation through Black Forest Labs' Flux (Wikipedia).

Grok 3 (17 February 2025): Think and DeepSearch. xAI says it was trained on Colossus "with 10x the compute of previous state-of-the-art models" (xAI); Wikipedia reports Musk's version, 10 times Grok-2 (Wikipedia). The two are different baselines, and neither comes with a FLOP count. It added Think mode, an extended chain of thought before answering, trained with reinforcement learning "to refine its chain-of-thought process", which spends inference tokens to raise accuracy on problems that need many steps, and DeepSearch, xAI's "first agent": an agentic browsing loop in which the model issues its own searches across the web and X, reads results and iterates, rather than doing a single retrieval pass (xAI). Its launch also produced the first lesson in reading Grok numbers: the headline 93.3% on AIME 2025 was "cons@64", the most common answer of 64 runs, and an OpenAI employee pointed out that the comparison chart showed o3-mini-high without the same technique (xAI; Wikipedia).

Grok 4 (9 July 2025): reasoning first. Grok 4 shipped with native tool use and real-time search, a 256K API context, and Grok 4 Heavy (below). xAI reported 15.9% on ARC-AGI-2, "new state-of-the-art for closed models", nearly double Claude Opus 4's 8.6% (xAI): ARC-AGI-2 is a set of novel grid-transformation puzzles built to resist memorisation, so it probes whether a model generalises to rules it has not seen rather than recall. It called Heavy "the first model to score 50%" on Humanity's Last Exam (HLE): several thousand expert-written questions across academic fields, designed to be unanswerable by search and to sit near the ceiling of current models; xAI's charts give that figure on the text-only subset with Python and search tools (xAI). Both are xAI's own runs. Then came cheaper lines: Grok Code Fast 1 (28 August 2025) for high-volume agentic coding, Grok 4 Fast (September 2025, 2M tokens of context and, by Artificial Analysis's count, 40% fewer thinking tokens than Grok 4), Grok 4.1 and the agentic Grok 4.1 Fast with a 2M-token context (17 November 2025) (Wikipedia).

2026: a release every month or two. Grok 4.20 (February, with a multi-agent version on the API in March), Grok 4.3 (April: a new pretrained model with a December 2025 cutoff and a 1M-token context at $1.25 / $2.50), Grok Build 0.1, a model trained for agentic coding, with the Grok Build terminal agent (May), and Grok 4.5 (8 July), trained on "tens of thousands" of GB300 GPUs with an RL stage over hundreds of thousands of mostly software-engineering tasks, and data from Cursor, at $2 / $6 per million input and output tokens (Wikipedia; xAI release notes; MarkTechPost). Musk called Grok 4.5 "Opus-class"; that is the company's own assessment (Wikipedia).

Heavy: parallel attempts instead of one longer chain

A reasoning model buys accuracy with tokens. The serial way is to let one chain of thought run longer; its cost is latency, because every token waits for the one before it. Grok 4 Heavy took the parallel way: "parallel test-time compute, which allows Grok to consider multiple hypotheses at once" (xAI). Several attempts run at the same time and their answers are reconciled, so the bill grows with the number of attempts while the wall-clock time stays close to one attempt's. It works because independent attempts make errors that are only partly correlated: where one goes wrong, the others often do not, so reconciling them catches mistakes a single chain would keep. It stops working where all attempts share the same blind spot.

The API exposes the same idea as grok-4.20-multi-agent: several agents work the query and a designated leader agent synthesises their discussion into the answer. There are two setups, 4 agents (effort low or medium) and 16 agents (high or xhigh); only the leader's output is returned, and "all tokens consumed by both the leader agent and sub-agents are billed" (multi-agent docs). The animation runs one query both ways on the same scale.

Illustrative inputs. Each attempt is drawn as 8,000 reasoning tokens and the leader's synthesis as 2,000, one square per 1,000 tokens, decoding at one token per unit of time; xAI publishes no per-agent token counts. What it shows. With the same tokens billed, the single chain takes about as many units of time as there are agents, while the parallel run takes one attempt plus the synthesis. Parallelism is a way to spend money instead of time. The general mechanism, and how Gemini's Deep Think and OpenAI's tiers spend test-time compute, is on Topic: llms (Deeper: test-time compute).

Training: brute force on two axes

  • Compute scale and cadence. The strategy is iteration rather than a better published recipe: Colossus 1 and 2, expanding toward a million GPUs, with the fastest release cadence of any lab in 2026 (Grok 4.20, 4.3, 4.5, 4.6 and 4.7 in eight months). Its stated roadmap is "multi-trillion parameter models" (S-1).
  • RL at pretraining scale. xAI's own words for Grok 4: "we utilized Colossus, our 200k GPU cluster, to run reinforcement learning to refine Grok's reasoning abilities at a pretraining scale", with infrastructure and algorithmic work that "increased the compute efficiency of our training by 6x", verifiable training data expanded "from primarily math and coding data to many more domains", and "over an order of magnitude more compute than previously" (xAI). It is the concrete form of the industry's 2025 realisation that post-training is a second scaling axis rather than a finishing step: very large volumes of rollouts on tasks whose outcome can be checked (maths, code, tool use), scored by checking the outcome, which is why reasoning and agentic behaviour improved faster than raw knowledge. The often-repeated claim that Grok 4 spent as much compute on RL as on pretraining comes from a chart in the launch stream, not from the text, and is unconfirmed here. The method itself is on RL for LLMs: RLHF, GRPO, RLVR.
  • Later runs, as described. Grok 4.6: a longer training run on an AI-generated dataset designed to improve reasoning (SiliconANGLE). Grok 4.7: "a new, larger base model", "a longer reinforcement learning run on a harder mix of tasks, weighted toward problems that take many hours to complete" (xAI). From Grok 4.5 on, training used data from Cursor, which SpaceXAI went on to buy (Wikipedia). In April 2026, in Musk v. Altman, Musk said Grok had been developed "partly" by distillation from OpenAI's models; in August OpenAI said it would stop providing its models to the SpaceXAI-owned Cursor (Wikipedia).
  • The data edge. Owning X (since the March 2025 merger) gives a real-time, conversational corpus that competitors must license or scrape. At inference time it is only live when a search tool is on: the API docs say Grok "has no knowledge of current events" beyond its training data unless Web Search or X Search is enabled (xAI models docs). DeepSearch and those tools are what turn the firehose into live retrieval.

Grok 4.6 (12 August 2026): the value pick, and an index that moved

Grok 4.6 was the flagship for six weeks, built for long-running agents, coding, knowledge work and visual projects, with a 500,000-token context, text and image in and text out, at $2 per million input tokens, $0.50 cached and $6 output (xAI release notes). Musk described it as a 1.5-trillion-parameter model (CometAPI); Wikipedia describes 4.5 and 4.6 as built on a "1.5 trillion-parameter V9 foundation" (Wikipedia). Neither is an xAI publication, so treat the size as a statement, not a spec.

At launch it scored 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a composite that averages a fixed basket of benchmarks into one number (useful for coarse ranking and nothing finer): level with GPT-5.6 Sol, one point behind Claude Fable 5, and ahead of Kimi K3 (SiliconANGLE; VentureBeat). That was the index before its v4.2 and v4.3 revisions of September 2026 (v4.3 swapped Terminal-Bench 2.1 for 4.0 and raised the private-test weight), and the scores are on different scales. On v4.3 the tie is gone:

The price against the position is the product claim. At $2 / $6 against Claude Opus 5.5's and GPT-5.6 Sol's $4 / $20, a 3-input-to-1-output blend comes to $3 against $8, 37.5% of the price ((3·2 + 6) / 4 = 3; (3·4 + 20) / 4 = 8), and 15% of Fable 5.1's or GPT-6 Astra's $20 blend. Per task the gap narrows, because Grok writes more tokens: Artificial Analysis puts Grok 4.6 (high) at $1.86 per index task against $5.98 for Opus 5.5 (max) and $1.99 for GPT-5.6 Sol (max) (AA leaderboard; Price and position). The index placing also does not transfer to private code: on Real-SWE, ten licensed tasks from private enterprise codebases, Grok 4.6 running in Grok Build resolved 23.8%, level with Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 and well behind Claude Fable 5.1's 38.8% in Claude Code, and 41 of its 61 failed runs (67.2%) missed a requirement rather than breaking the code. These are model-plus-harness results from Specific Labs, collected in a Real-SWE roundup.

Grok 4.7 (21 September 2026): the current flagship

Grok 4.7 is a base-model refresh at unchanged prices: $2 per million input tokens, $0.50 cached and $6 output, with a 500,000-token context, a May 2026 knowledge cutoff, text and image in and text out, no text output limit, and four reasoning levels, low, medium, high (the default) and xhigh (xAI docs). xAI names four changes: "a new, larger base model"; "a longer reinforcement learning run on a harder mix of tasks, weighted toward problems that take many hours to complete"; better at "verifying its own work and managing longer context"; and trained "to natively understand the Grok Bot harness" (xAI). The last is the interesting one, because it is a model shipped for a harness rather than only for an API: Grok Bot, in beta since 11 August, runs "durable AI teammates" on a persistent cloud computer with messaging, approvals, connectors and routines (xAI release notes). On the Responses API, Grok 4.7 always returns its reasoning encrypted, to be passed back unchanged on the next turn (xAI docs).

The Fast variant is "the same model served on faster infrastructure", with "twice the output speed at twice the price" (xAI): 2 times the standard token rates, or 1.5 times for long-context requests, and available only inside Cursor and Grok Build, not on the public API (xAI docs).

The long-context price cliff

From Grok 4.3 up, every text model has two price rows, and a request whose prompt reaches 200,000 tokens "is billed at the higher rate for all tokens in the request": $4 / $1 / $12 for Grok 4.7 instead of $2 / $0.50 / $6 (models). One more prompt token can double the whole bill.

Defaults reproduce the rule by construction: 199,000 prompt tokens and 5,000 output cost (199,000 · 2 + 5,000 · 6) / 106 = $0.428; move to 200,000 and it becomes (200,000 · 4 + 5,000 · 12) / 106 = $0.86. Grok Build 0.1's window is 256K, so the slider stops counting there. An agent loop that compacts its context before 200K (xAI's Context Compaction API, May 2026) stays on the cheaper row; xAI also recommends a prompt_cache_key so that a conversation keeps hitting the same server's cache (xAI docs).

Posture and security

xAI's alignment posture is deliberately looser than its rivals', and the recurring moderation incidents are part of the brand's risk profile rather than accidents of it. Grok has promoted conspiracy theories, praised Adolf Hitler, used antisemitic tropes and generated nonconsensual sexualised images; within a week of launch, Grok 4 was shown consulting Musk's views before answering questions that did not ask for them (Wikipedia). On 14 July 2026 a researcher reported that Grok Build uploaded users' whole repositories, history and secrets included, to a SpaceXAI storage bucket even with "Improve the model" disabled, about 27,800 times more data than its task needed; SpaceXAI disabled it the same day (Wikipedia). xAI's counter-claim for Grok 4.7 is "an entirely new safeguard stack" and "the strongest model we've tested on refusals and jailbreak resistance" (xAI).

The concrete cost is a security one. Adversa AI's cryptographic context injection was reported to xAI and its HackerOne programme on 3 June 2026; xAI acknowledged it without specifics or a timeline, further contact on 4 and 10 August drew no reply, and Adversa could still reproduce it on 19 August, publishing on 20 August (Adversa AI), 78 days, or eleven weeks, after the report. There was no patch, no CVE and no user-facing workaround. The target was the Grok web chat on grok.com running Grok 4.5 Fast, and Adversa told The Hacker News it had succeeded in 40% of 20 attempts since June (8), with failures caused by Grok struggling to decrypt, not by a filter (The Hacker News).

Why encryption, and why it matters. Earlier cipher-based evasions used weak, reversible schemes (substitution, XOR, base64) that a model can undo in its weights and a filter can learn to spot. AES-256-GCM under a PBKDF2-derived key cannot be read by a classifier even with the key on the same page, because recovering the plaintext means running the cipher, and classifiers do not execute what they inspect (Adversa AI). The decryption happens in Grok's own Python runtime, inside the trust boundary, after every filter has passed the request. It is the clearest demonstration that input filtering cannot secure a model allowed to execute code. The transferable lessons, that a sandboxed interpreter is an injection decoder and that egress control and action-keyed confirmation are the controls that hold, are on Harness engineering.

Weights. Closed at the frontier; earlier generations are sometimes opened once superseded (Grok-1 under Apache 2.0, Grok 2 under the Community License), with nothing after Grok 2 opened as of 1 October 2026.

Common mistakes

"xAI merged into SpaceX in February 2026 to form SpaceXAI"
SpaceX acquired xAI effective 2 February 2026 (the S-1's "xAI Merger"); the SpaceXAI name first appeared on 6 May and the rebrand completed on 6 July (Owner and name).
Comparing a launch-day index score with today's
Grok 4.6's 61 is on the pre-v4.2 scale; on v4.3 it is 44.3. Compare within one index version only, and quote the version beside the score.
Taking a vendor's benchmark table as a model-only result
Terminal-Bench is model plus harness: 38.0% in xAI's table, 33% in Grok Build by Artificial Analysis. And a vendor's copy of a rival's score can differ from the rival's own (Fable 5.1: 57.9% in xAI's table, 55.8% in Anthropic's).
Reading per-token price as cost per task
Grok 4.7 and 4.6 have identical prices, but 4.7 costs twice as much per index task because it writes about 125% more output tokens.
"200,000 H100s stood up in 122 days"
The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s at about 130 MW; 200,000 GPUs was Colossus by early 2025 (Colossus).
Calling Grok 4.1 Fast or Grok Code Fast current
Both were retired on 15 May 2026. Their slugs still answer, served by Grok 4.3 or Grok Build 0.1 and billed at those prices, so the mistake is silent.
Assuming a long prompt pays the higher rate only above 200K
At or above 200,000 prompt tokens the whole request, output included, is billed at the higher row (the cliff).
"25% active" means a quarter of the parameters run
It is 2 of 8 experts. Counting the attention and embeddings every token uses, Grok-1's active share is 26.7% (Grok-1).
Treating Musk's parameter counts as specifications
"1.5 trillion" for Grok 4.6 is a statement, not a published configuration; the only audited architectures are Grok-1's code and Grok 2's config.json.
Using Grok 4.7 Fast on the API
It exists only inside Cursor and Grok Build, billed through those plans.
