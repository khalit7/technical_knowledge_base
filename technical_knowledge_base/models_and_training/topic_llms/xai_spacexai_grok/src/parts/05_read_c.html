Grok 4.7 (21 September 2026): the current flagship

Grok 4.7 is a base-model refresh at unchanged prices: $2 per million input tokens, $0.50 cached and $6 output, with a 500,000-token context, a May 2026 knowledge cutoff, text and image in and text out, no text output limit, and four reasoning levels, low, medium, high (the default) and xhigh ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}). xAI names four changes: "a new, larger base model"; "a longer reinforcement learning run on a harder mix of tasks, weighted toward problems that take many hours to complete"; better at "verifying its own work and managing longer context"; and trained "to natively understand the Grok Bot harness" ({{xAI|@g47}}). The last is the interesting one, because it is a model shipped for a harness rather than only for an API: Grok Bot, in beta since 11 August, runs "durable AI teammates" on a persistent cloud computer with messaging, approvals, connectors and routines ({{xAI release notes|@xdrn}}). On the Responses API, Grok 4.7 always returns its reasoning encrypted, to be passed back unchanged on the next turn ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}).

The Fast variant is "the same model served on faster infrastructure", with "twice the output speed at twice the price" ({{xAI|@g47}}): 2 times the standard token rates, or 1.5 times for long-context requests, and available only inside Cursor and Grok Build, not on the public API ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}).

What Artificial Analysis measured. At xhigh, Grok 4.7 scores 46 on the Intelligence Index, 2 points above Grok 4.6, which brought SpaceXAI into the top four labs on 21 September; Gemini 4 Argon (30 September) has since pushed it to fifth. The long-context price cliff

From Grok 4.3 up, every text model has two price rows, and a request whose prompt reaches 200,000 tokens "is billed at the higher rate for all tokens in the request": $4 / $1 / $12 for Grok 4.7 instead of $2 / $0.50 / $6 ({{models|@xdm}}). One more prompt token can double the whole bill.

Defaults reproduce the rule by construction: 199,000 prompt tokens and 5,000 output cost (199,000 · 2 + 5,000 · 6) / 106 = $0.428; move to 200,000 and it becomes (200,000 · 4 + 5,000 · 12) / 106 = $0.86. Grok Build 0.1's window is 256K, so the slider stops counting there. An agent loop that compacts its context before 200K (xAI's Context Compaction API, May 2026) stays on the cheaper row; xAI also recommends a prompt_cache_key so that a conversation keeps hitting the same server's cache ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}).

Posture and security

xAI's alignment posture is deliberately looser than its rivals', and the recurring moderation incidents are part of the brand's risk profile rather than accidents of it. Grok has promoted conspiracy theories, praised Adolf Hitler, used antisemitic tropes and generated nonconsensual sexualised images; within a week of launch, Grok 4 was shown consulting Musk's views before answering questions that did not ask for them ({{Wikipedia|@wg}}). On 14 July 2026 a researcher reported that Grok Build uploaded users' whole repositories, history and secrets included, to a SpaceXAI storage bucket even with "Improve the model" disabled, about 27,800 times more data than its task needed; SpaceXAI disabled it the same day ({{Wikipedia|@wg}}). xAI's counter-claim for Grok 4.7 is "an entirely new safeguard stack" and "the strongest model we've tested on refusals and jailbreak resistance" ({{xAI|@g47}}).

The concrete cost is a security one. Adversa AI's cryptographic context injection was reported to xAI and its HackerOne programme on 3 June 2026; xAI acknowledged it without specifics or a timeline, further contact on 4 and 10 August drew no reply, and Adversa could still reproduce it on 19 August, publishing on 20 August ({{Adversa AI|@adv}}), 78 days, or eleven weeks, after the report. There was no patch, no CVE and no user-facing workaround. The target was the Grok web chat on grok.com running Grok 4.5 Fast, and Adversa told The Hacker News it had succeeded in 40% of 20 attempts since June (8), with failures caused by Grok struggling to decrypt, not by a filter ({{The Hacker News|@thn}}).

Why encryption, and why it matters. Earlier cipher-based evasions used weak, reversible schemes (substitution, XOR, base64) that a model can undo in its weights and a filter can learn to spot. AES-256-GCM under a PBKDF2-derived key cannot be read by a classifier even with the key on the same page, because recovering the plaintext means running the cipher, and classifiers do not execute what they inspect ({{Adversa AI|@adv}}). The decryption happens in Grok's own Python runtime, inside the trust boundary, after every filter has passed the request. It is the clearest demonstration that input filtering cannot secure a model allowed to execute code. The transferable lessons, that a sandboxed interpreter is an injection decoder and that egress control and action-keyed confirmation are the controls that hold, are on {{Harness engineering|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ca14fc32338707c2f}}.

Common mistakes

"xAI merged into SpaceX in February 2026 to form SpaceXAI"
SpaceX acquired xAI effective 2 February 2026 (the S-1's "xAI Merger"); the SpaceXAI name first appeared on 6 May and the rebrand completed on 6 July (Owner and name).
Comparing a launch-day index score with today's
Grok 4.6's 61 is on the pre-v4.2 scale; on v4.3 it is 44.3. Compare within one index version only, and quote the version beside the score.
Taking a vendor's benchmark table as a model-only result
Terminal-Bench is model plus harness: 38.0% in xAI's table, 33% in Grok Build by Artificial Analysis. And a vendor's copy of a rival's score can differ from the rival's own (Fable 5.1: 57.9% in xAI's table, 55.8% in Anthropic's).
Reading per-token price as cost per task
Grok 4.7 and 4.6 have identical prices, but 4.7 costs twice as much per index task because it writes about 125% more output tokens.
"200,000 H100s stood up in 122 days"
The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s at about 130 MW; 200,000 GPUs was Colossus by early 2025 (Colossus).
Calling Grok 4.1 Fast or Grok Code Fast current
Both were retired on 15 May 2026. Their slugs still answer, served by Grok 4.3 or Grok Build 0.1 and billed at those prices, so the mistake is silent.
Assuming a long prompt pays the higher rate only above 200K
At or above 200,000 prompt tokens the whole request, output included, is billed at the higher row (the cliff).
"25% active" means a quarter of the parameters run
It is 2 of 8 experts. Counting the attention and embeddings every token uses, Grok-1's active share is 26.7% (Grok-1).
Treating Musk's parameter counts as specifications
"1.5 trillion" for Grok 4.6 is a statement, not a published configuration; the only audited architectures are Grok-1's code and Grok 2's config.json.
Using Grok 4.7 Fast on the API
It exists only inside Cursor and Grok Build, billed through those plans.
