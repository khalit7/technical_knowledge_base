3. One task, taken apart

Writing in the cached share h of input tokens, billed at the cache price pc:

Ctask=Tin(1−h)pin+Tinhpc+Toutpout106

AA publishes each model's total tokens over the whole index run and the run's total cost, but neither the cached share nor how tasks are weighted. So h is back-solved from the run's total, and tokens per task are the run totals scaled by AA's cost per task over the run's total cost. The defaults therefore reproduce AA's cost per task by construction; what the view adds is where the money goes, and what each lever moves.

Try: the input side is most of 3.8 Flash's bill. Drag the cached share to 0 and the task costs three times as much; halve the output tokens (roughly what a lower thinking level does, by an amount Google does not publish) and it falls by a fifth.

Batching: when the MoE stops being cheaper to decode

The decode ceiling above is for one sequence. A server decodes a batch of B sequences per step: the weights are read once for the whole batch, the arithmetic grows with B, and each sequence still reads its own cache. The Scaling Book's general step time ({{Scaling Book, inference|@sbinf}}):

tstep=BSKVW+max(2BNactiveF,P(B)W),Bcrit=FW P(B)=Pshared+E(B)Pexpert,E(B)=n[1−(1−k/n)B]
SKV
one sequence's KV cache in bytes (context and cache settings from the controls above)
F, W
the chip's peak FLOP/s (bf16) and memory bandwidth in bytes/s
P(B)
weight bytes read per step: all of them for the dense 31B; for the MoE the shared part plus every expert at least one token in the batch was routed to
E(B)
expected distinct experts touched per layer when each token picks k = 8 of n = 128 at random (uniform routing, illustrative: real routers are skewed)
Bcrit
the batch at which a bf16 weight matmul turns from memory-bound to compute-bound: arithmetic intensity, FLOPs per byte of weights read, reaches the chip's F / W

Pshared and Pexpert are back-solved from the report's two MoE figures, 52.0 GB resident and 7.6 GB active in bf16 (16.2 and 2.8 GB at Q4_0) ({{Gemma 4 report|@g4r}}): 120 Pexpert = 52.0 − 7.6, so Pexpert = 0.37 GB and Pshared = 7.6 − 8 × 0.37 = 4.64 GB. P(1) = 7.6 GB and P(∞) = 52.0 GB therefore hold by construction.

Try: the defaults are TPU v5e, bf16 and a batch of 32. Under uniform routing those 32 tokens already touch 87% of the experts, so the MoE reads 46 GB of weights per step, not 7.6, and its lead over the 31B shrinks from about 8 times at batch 1 to under 2. Slide the batch up: the 31B turns compute-bound near 250, the MoE only near 1,640, and at that point its 8 times smaller arithmetic is what counts again. Switch to Ironwood to see the crossover move.

One chip, peak rates, no overheads, and memory capacity ignored: on n chips F and W both scale by n, so Bcrit is unchanged. The report's 64.0 GB for the 31B is 2.08 bytes per active parameter, which is why its crossover is 250 rather than exactly 240.

Speculative decoding with the MTP drafter

Every Gemma 4 model ships a small drafter, a 4-layer Transformer that cross-attends to the main model's KV cache (76M parameters on E2B, 77M on E4B, 400M on the 12B, 430M on 26B A4B, 500M on the 31B; {{Gemma 4 report|@g4r}}). The drafter proposes γ tokens; the target model checks all of them in one forward pass, keeps the longest prefix it agrees with, and adds one token of its own. The output distribution is exactly the target's ({{Leviathan et al.|@lev}}, {{Hugging Face|@hfg4}}). With an acceptance rate α per drafted token and a drafter that costs c of a target pass (Leviathan et al., Equation 1 and Theorem 3.8):

tokens per target pass=1−αγ+11−α,speed-up=1−αγ+1(1−α)(γc+1)
α
probability that the target accepts a drafted token (assumed independent token to token)
γ
tokens drafted per step
c
time of one drafter run over time of one target run

The speed-up assumes the target can check γ + 1 positions for the price of one, which holds while decoding is memory-bound: at small batches, the left of the roofline above. The paper's Corollary 3.9: some γ gives a speed-up whenever α > c.

Try: the defaults are the first row of the paper's Table 4 (T5-XXL drafted by T5-small, translation, greedy), and the formula gives 3.16 against the paper's 3.2. Pick other rows: all twelve land within 0.1. Then pick the Gemma 4 preset: Google gives no acceptance rate, and "up to ~3x" needs α of about 0.77 at γ = 4 (fitted, and only if the assumed γ and c are right). Raise γ and watch the speed-up peak and then fall as the drafts that get thrown away cost more than they save.
