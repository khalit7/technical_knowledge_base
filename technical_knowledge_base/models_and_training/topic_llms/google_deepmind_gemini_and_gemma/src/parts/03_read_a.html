One research pipeline, two families

Google DeepMind ships Gemini, closed and served through the Gemini API, Vertex AI, Google's own products and, since September 2026, Apple's Siri; and Gemma, open weights you download and run yourself, under Apache 2.0 since Gemma 4.

Three things make Google worth understanding as a lab rather than as a list of releases, and one question runs through all of them: how many tokens does a request cost, and what does each token cost?

  • Its own silicon and software. It trains on TPUs with JAX and Pathways, so its costs and constraints are not NVIDIA's (Training).
  • Sparse and natively multimodal since Gemini 1.5. Mixture-of-experts keeps the compute per token bounded, and video is just more tokens in the same window, which is why a million-token window holding an hour of video is a product rather than a demo (Media and the window).
  • A thinking level per request. The caller chooses how hard the model reasons, and that choice, more than the price list, decides what a task costs (Thinking, Works harder).

The Lineage tab puts both families on one time axis; Cost per task takes a task's bill apart; Gemma on one machine works out what fits in a given amount of memory.

The families at a glance

Pro, Flash, Flash-Lite

The three Gemini price tiers, largest to smallest. Pro trains at the frontier; Flash and Flash-Lite are smaller models distilled from a larger teacher (Training). Since 2026 the cadence has been on Flash: four Flash generations between May and September 2026, while Pro stayed on 3.1 Pro, still a preview.

Gemini 4 Argon

The frontier model above Pro, announced 30 September 2026, in limited preview for vetted cyber defenders first.

Live

The real-time voice line, served through the Live API: audio in, audio out, with tool calls during the conversation.

Deep Think

A reasoning mode that runs several lines of thought in parallel and combines them; a mode in the Gemini app, not a model you call in the API.

Cyber variants, Fairwind Program

3.5 Flash Cyber and 3.8 Flash Cyber, tuned to find and fix software vulnerabilities, are not sold openly. Fairwind is Google's vetted-access channel for them, open to governments, critical infrastructure operators and software maintainers who apply; Argon rolls out through it first.

Model codes and status

Every API model has a code such as gemini-3.8-flash. Stable codes are pinned; preview codes can change and are deprecated with at least two weeks' notice ({{models page|@models}}): gemini-3-pro-preview was shut down on 9 March 2026 and now points to gemini-3.1-pro-preview ({{release notes|@log}}). The latest aliases (gemini-flash-latest, gemini-pro-latest) follow the newest release and may break with two weeks' notice.

Gemma naming

E2B, E4B: effective 2B and 4B, the parameters that must sit in accelerator memory, not the checkpoint size (mechanism under Gemma 3n). 26B A4B: 26B total, about 4B active per token, a mixture of experts.

Lineage: Gemini (closed)

Dates and one-line summaries for every release are on the Lineage tab; this is what changed and why it matters.

The PaLM era (2022 to 2023)

PaLM and PaLM 2 were dense models trained on TPU pods. PaLM's 540B run showed Google could train at frontier scale on its own silicon and orchestration layer, and it was one of the models on which chain-of-thought prompting was first shown to work at scale. After Google Brain and DeepMind merged in 2023, Gemini was built as the successor with two departures designed in: sparsity instead of dense scaling, and multimodality in pretraining instead of bolted on afterwards.

Gemini 1.0 (December 2023) and 1.5 (February 2024): long context

1.0 came in Ultra, Pro and Nano; Nano (1.8B and 3.25B parameters) was the on-device model ({{Wikipedia|@wgem}}). 1.5 Pro was the breakthrough: launched with a 1M-token window, opened to every developer at 2M tokens in mid-2024, and reported with "near-perfect retrieval (>99%) up to at least 10M tokens", against 128K (GPT-4 Turbo) and 200K (Claude 3.0) at its rivals ({{Gemini 1.5 report|@g15}}). Two design choices carry it:

  • Sparse MoE (mixture of experts). A router sends each token to a few expert sub-networks, so compute per token scales with the active parameters, not the total, which keeps the cost of pushing a million tokens through the model bounded.
  • Native multimodality. Text, images, audio and video become tokens in one sequence, trained jointly from the start, rather than a separately trained vision encoder projected into a finished text model. So you can hand Gemini an hour of video and ask about one moment: the video is more tokens in the same window, not a separate pipeline with its own failure modes.

The report's other memorable result: given a grammar manual for Kalamang, a language with fewer than 200 speakers, the model learned to translate English to Kalamang at about the level of a person who learned from the same material ({{Gemini 1.5 report|@g15}}).

2.0 (December 2024) and 2.5 (March 2025): thinking, and distilled tiers

2.5 Pro made thinking the default, and the 2.5 models gave the caller a thinking budget, a cap on reasoning tokens, which "allows users to trade off performance with cost" ({{Gemini 2.5 report|@g25}}). The budget has since been replaced by thinking levels, even on the 2.5 models (Thinking). The report states the architecture plainly: sparse MoE transformers with native multimodal input, a 1M-token window on Pro and Flash, and Flash and below trained by distillation. The agentic push dates from here: Project Mariner for browser control, and Jules, an asynchronous coding agent ({{OpenAI and Google harnesses|n:3c65c17b0d0d810380c0d932d132c7b6}}).

Deep Think (July 2025): parallel test-time compute

Instead of one longer chain of thought, Deep Think explores several reasoning threads at once and then selects or combines among them: the same budget spent in width rather than depth. A Deep Think version of Gemini reached the official gold-medal standard at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad: 35 of 42 points, 5 of 6 problems, end to end in natural language within the 4.5-hour limit. The year before, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reached silver (28 points) only after experts translated the problems into the formal language Lean, and took two to three days ({{Google DeepMind|@imo}}). Parallel exploration buys independence between attempts, which one longer trace does not; the selection step is where it succeeds or fails. Gemini 3 Deep Think followed in December 2025 as a mode in the Gemini app.

Gemini 3 (November 2025) and 3.1 Pro (February 2026)

Gemini 3 Pro (18 November 2025) is a sparse MoE transformer with native text, vision and audio input, a 1M-token window and 64K output, trained on TPUs with JAX and ML Pathways ({{model card|@mc3}}). It introduced thinking levels and media-resolution controls in the API; 3 Flash followed on 17 December ({{release notes|@log}}). 3.1 Pro (19 February 2026) replaced 3 Pro and is the current Pro tier, 1,048,576 tokens in and 65,536 out, still a preview more than seven months on.

The Flash cadence (May to September 2026)

Four Flash generations reached general availability in four months: 3.5 Flash (19 May, at Google I/O), 3.6 Flash with 3.5 Flash-Lite (21 July), 3.7 Flash (13 August) and 3.8 Flash (2 September) ({{release notes|@log}}). 3.6 Flash used 17% fewer output tokens than 3.5 Flash, and 3.5 Flash-Lite generated 350 output tokens per second in Artificial Analysis' measurement ({{Google|@b36}}). The 21 July release also deprecated the temperature, top_p and top_k sampling parameters across the API ({{release notes|@log}}).

Gemini 3.8 Flash and Flash Cyber (September 2026)

The current volume model. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (methodology v4.3.2, read 1 October 2026), 3.8 Flash at high thinking scores 40.9 against 39.1 for 3.7 Flash, below GPT-5.6 Sol (47.0) and Grok 4.6 (44.3) ({{Artificial Analysis|@aalb}}). Launch-day index figures, such as The Register's 59 against 56 and "about 40 percent more per task" ({{The Register|@reg}}), were on a version of the index before v4.2 and do not compare with these.

Google reports 54.9% on HLE-Verified and gains over 3.7 Flash on DeepSWE v1.1 long-horizon software engineering, Vals Finance Agent V2 and Harvey's legal agent benchmark, and claims it beats most larger frontier models on long-horizon software engineering ({{Google|@b38}}). How the gain was bought is the part worth carrying: Google says the model works harder, "executing extra reasoning steps, and calling tools iteratively", so cost per index task rose by about a third over 3.7 Flash ($1.24 against $0.93) although the per-token price did not move (Works harder). A Flash-tier model spending more test-time compute by default blurs the tier distinction the price list implies.

Introductory pricing is $0.75 input and $3.75 output per million tokens through 31 December 2026, doubling to $1.50 and $7.50 from 1 January 2027 ({{pricing|@pricing}}). On Real-SWE, a benchmark of private enterprise code, it resolves 31.2% of tasks, third behind Claude Fable 5.1 (38.8%) and GPT-6 Astra (33.8%), with overlapping 95% confidence intervals ([25%, 38%], [32%, 45%], [27%, 40%]) ({{DEV Community|@dev}}).

Flash Cyber is the same model tuned for vulnerability detection and remediation and distributed only through Fairwind: 47.2% pass@1 on CWE-Bench against 47.8% for the leading frontier model, and Chrome's security team got 2.6 times more correct vulnerability patches ({{Google|@b38}}).

Gemini 3.8 Live and Live Extended Thinking (15 September 2026): a tier, not a flagship

Production voice-agent models doing near real-time speech with visual grounding, detecting and switching between 97 languages mid-conversation. Extended Thinking is the one with an idea in it: it reasons and speaks at the same time, filling with verbal cues ("Let me check that") while tool and API calls run in the background instead of going silent. Every other way of spending a test-time budget makes the caller wait; this one covers the latency with speech, a product answer to a systems constraint and the first shipped one. Extended Thinking scored 82.6 on Artificial Analysis' speech-to-speech quality index at launch, first on it, with 68.6% on τ-Voice, 35.1% on Sierra's τ-Voice-banking and 97.7% on Big Bench Audio; all audio output carries a SynthID watermark. Both are in the Gemini API and AI Studio, in private preview in Gemini Enterprise, and behind Search Live, Gmail and Keep. Google publishes no millisecond latency figure ({{Google|@blive}}). The audio side of voice models is on {{Topic: generative-and-multimodal|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ab6ade318917bff55}}.

Gemini 4 Argon (30 September 2026): the next frontier, gated

For long-horizon software engineering, legal and finance work and cyber defence. Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, first place on AutomationBench at 51.3%, 91.7% on LVBench video understanding, a tie for first on CWE-bench v1 at 68%, and the lead on the Vals Index. The output limit rises to 1M tokens from the previous 64K. It rolls out first to trusted cyber defenders through Fairwind, then to paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers, at an introductory $2 input and $10 output per million ($4 and $20 afterwards), with cached input 95% off. Google names monitoring of the model's internal activations among its safeguards ({{Google|@bargon}}). On the Intelligence Index v4.3 it scores 52.6 at $1.99 per task at the introductory price ({{Artificial Analysis|@aaargon}}). Gating a frontier model to defenders first is the same release shape as Anthropic's Mythos class ({{Anthropic: Claude family|n:3c65c17b0d0d814cb979d0eabca59a45}}).

Lineage: Gemma (open)

Gemma 1 and 2 (2024)

Small open models built from Gemini research, first released 21 February 2024 (2B and 7B); Gemma 2 followed on 27 June 2024 ({{Wikipedia|@wgemma}}). Gemma 2 (2B, 9B, 27B) trained its 2B and 9B by knowledge distillation from a larger teacher rather than plain next-token prediction, and interleaved local and global attention layers one to one, which is why its small models competed with models two to three times their size ({{Gemma 2 report|@g2}}).

Gemma 3 (12 March 2025)

1B, 4B, 12B and 27B; 32K context on the 1B and 128K on the rest; image input through a 400M-parameter SigLIP encoder that turns an 896 by 896 image into 256 tokens. The architectural point is the 5:1 local-to-global ratio: five of every six layers attend only within a sliding window of the last 1,024 tokens, one in six across the whole sequence, up from Gemma 2's one to one with a 4,096-token window. The global layers use RoPE base 1,000,000 against 10,000 on the local ones, since only they see long distances. Pretraining ran from 2T tokens for the 1B to 14T for the 27B, distilled from a teacher by sampling 256 of its logits per token ({{Gemma 3 report|@g3}}). How the layout sets memory is worked through under Gemma attention.

Gemma 3n (full release 26 June 2025): built for phones

Two ideas make an 8B checkpoint behave like a 4B one on a device ({{Gemma 3n docs|@g3n}}, {{developer guide|@g3nb}}):

  • Per-Layer Embeddings (PLE). Each decoder layer gets its own small embedding table per token; those tables can be computed outside accelerator memory, cached to fast storage and added as each layer runs. So E2B, which loads over 5B parameters in standard execution, can run with just under 2B (1.91B) resident.
  • MatFormer (a Matryoshka transformer) nests a smaller model inside the larger: E2B is a sub-model of E4B trained with it, so layers can be mixed to fit a memory budget between the two sizes.

Gemma 4 (2 April 2026): Apache 2.0 and a small MoE

Four sizes, E2B, E4B, 26B A4B (a mixture of experts) and a dense 31B, joined on 3 June 2026 by a dense 12B Unified ({{Wikipedia|@wgemma}}). It is the first Gemma under Apache 2.0; earlier generations used Google's own Gemma terms, so this is the release that made Gemma unrestricted for commercial use. Google reports over 400 million Gemma downloads since the first generation; at launch the 31B ranked third among open models on the Arena text leaderboard and the 26B sixth ({{Google|@g4b}}).   • Vocabulary 262K tokens on every size.
  • The MoE. 26B A4B routes each token to 8 of 128 experts plus 1 shared expert, so it holds 26B of weights but does about 4B of matmuls per token: a bet that the memory-versus-compute trade favours sparsity even on one workstation (Sparse vs dense).
  • Attention. Local and global interleaved, 5:1 on every size except E2B (4:1), the final layer always global, plus two savings on the global layers: keys reused as values (on all but E2B and E4B) and p-RoPE (rotary position applied to a quarter of each head's dimensions). The edge models also share KV caches across layers (Gemma attention).
  • 12B Unified is encoder-free. Instead of a separate vision encoder (550M parameters on the 26B and 31B, 150M on E2B and E4B), it projects raw 48 by 48 pixel patches straight into the embedding space with one 35M-parameter matrix, and audio the same way.
  • A built-in draft model. Each model ships a separate 4-layer multi-token prediction (MTP) drafter for speculative decoding (76M parameters on E2B to 500M on the 31B). What it buys depends on an acceptance rate Google does not publish: Gemma on one machine works it through.
  • Thinking. A <|think|> token switches on a reasoning channel in the output.

T5Gemma (April 2025) and T5Gemma 2 (December 2025): the encoder-decoder branch

A research line rather than a product tier, and the only open encoder-decoder LLMs a frontier lab currently ships. The method is adaptation: copy a pretrained decoder-only Gemma checkpoint into an encoder-decoder shell (the encoder's self-attention switched from causal to bidirectional, the decoder gaining cross-attention over the encoder output) and continue pretraining, which is cheaper than pretraining from scratch and, above roughly 100M parameters, better. Two results carry beyond Gemma: asymmetric sizing (a 9B encoder with a 2B decoder runs at Gemma 2 2B's latency and scores clearly above it, the right shape for long-input, short-output work and impossible in a decoder-only model) and long context (T5Gemma 2 4B-4B scores 81.7 on RULER 32K against Gemma 3 4B's 66.8, despite pretraining at only 16K). EmbeddingGemma is built on T5Gemma 2 checkpoints. The method, ablations and every number are on {{Encoder-Decoder Gemma and T5Gemma 2|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}.
