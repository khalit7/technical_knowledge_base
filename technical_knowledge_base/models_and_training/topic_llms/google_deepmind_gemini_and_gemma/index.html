One research pipeline, two families

Google DeepMind ships Gemini, closed and served through the Gemini API, Vertex AI, Google's own products and, since September 2026, Apple's Siri; and Gemma, open weights you download and run yourself, under Apache 2.0 since Gemma 4.

Three things make Google worth understanding as a lab rather than as a list of releases, and one question runs through all of them: how many tokens does a request cost, and what does each token cost?

  • Its own silicon and software. It trains on TPUs with JAX and Pathways, so its costs and constraints are not NVIDIA's (Training).
  • Sparse and natively multimodal since Gemini 1.5. Mixture-of-experts keeps the compute per token bounded, and video is just more tokens in the same window, which is why a million-token window holding an hour of video is a product rather than a demo (Media and the window).
  • A thinking level per request. The caller chooses how hard the model reasons, and that choice, more than the price list, decides what a task costs (Thinking, Works harder).

The Lineage tab puts both families on one time axis; Cost per task takes a task's bill apart; Gemma on one machine works out what fits in a given amount of memory.

The families at a glance

Pro, Flash, Flash-Lite

The three Gemini price tiers, largest to smallest. Pro trains at the frontier; Flash and Flash-Lite are smaller models distilled from a larger teacher (Training). Since 2026 the cadence has been on Flash: four Flash generations between May and September 2026, while Pro stayed on 3.1 Pro, still a preview.

Gemini 4 Argon

The frontier model above Pro, announced 30 September 2026, in limited preview for vetted cyber defenders first.

Live

The real-time voice line, served through the Live API: audio in, audio out, with tool calls during the conversation.

Deep Think

A reasoning mode that runs several lines of thought in parallel and combines them; a mode in the Gemini app, not a model you call in the API.

Cyber variants, Fairwind Program

3.5 Flash Cyber and 3.8 Flash Cyber, tuned to find and fix software vulnerabilities, are not sold openly. Fairwind is Google's vetted-access channel for them, open to governments, critical infrastructure operators and software maintainers who apply; Argon rolls out through it first.

Model codes and status

Every API model has a code such as gemini-3.8-flash. Stable codes are pinned; preview codes can change and are deprecated with at least two weeks' notice (models page): gemini-3-pro-preview was shut down on 9 March 2026 and now points to gemini-3.1-pro-preview (release notes). The latest aliases (gemini-flash-latest, gemini-pro-latest) follow the newest release and may break with two weeks' notice.

Gemma naming

E2B, E4B: effective 2B and 4B, the parameters that must sit in accelerator memory, not the checkpoint size (mechanism under Gemma 3n). 26B A4B: 26B total, about 4B active per token, a mixture of experts.

Lineage: Gemini (closed)

Dates and one-line summaries for every release are on the Lineage tab; this is what changed and why it matters.

The PaLM era (2022 to 2023)

PaLM and PaLM 2 were dense models trained on TPU pods. PaLM's 540B run showed Google could train at frontier scale on its own silicon and orchestration layer, and it was one of the models on which chain-of-thought prompting was first shown to work at scale. After Google Brain and DeepMind merged in 2023, Gemini was built as the successor with two departures designed in: sparsity instead of dense scaling, and multimodality in pretraining instead of bolted on afterwards.

Gemini 1.0 (December 2023) and 1.5 (February 2024): long context

1.0 came in Ultra, Pro and Nano; Nano (1.8B and 3.25B parameters) was the on-device model (Wikipedia). 1.5 Pro was the breakthrough: launched with a 1M-token window, opened to every developer at 2M tokens in mid-2024, and reported with "near-perfect retrieval (>99%) up to at least 10M tokens", against 128K (GPT-4 Turbo) and 200K (Claude 3.0) at its rivals (Gemini 1.5 report). Two design choices carry it:

  • Sparse MoE (mixture of experts). A router sends each token to a few expert sub-networks, so compute per token scales with the active parameters, not the total, which keeps the cost of pushing a million tokens through the model bounded.
  • Native multimodality. Text, images, audio and video become tokens in one sequence, trained jointly from the start, rather than a separately trained vision encoder projected into a finished text model. So you can hand Gemini an hour of video and ask about one moment: the video is more tokens in the same window, not a separate pipeline with its own failure modes.

The report's other memorable result: given a grammar manual for Kalamang, a language with fewer than 200 speakers, the model learned to translate English to Kalamang at about the level of a person who learned from the same material (Gemini 1.5 report).

2.0 (December 2024) and 2.5 (March 2025): thinking, and distilled tiers

2.5 Pro made thinking the default, and the 2.5 models gave the caller a thinking budget, a cap on reasoning tokens, which "allows users to trade off performance with cost" (Gemini 2.5 report). The budget has since been replaced by thinking levels, even on the 2.5 models (Thinking). The report states the architecture plainly: sparse MoE transformers with native multimodal input, a 1M-token window on Pro and Flash, and Flash and below trained by distillation. The agentic push dates from here: Project Mariner for browser control, and Jules, an asynchronous coding agent (OpenAI and Google harnesses).

Deep Think (July 2025): parallel test-time compute

Instead of one longer chain of thought, Deep Think explores several reasoning threads at once and then selects or combines among them: the same budget spent in width rather than depth. A Deep Think version of Gemini reached the official gold-medal standard at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad: 35 of 42 points, 5 of 6 problems, end to end in natural language within the 4.5-hour limit. The year before, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reached silver (28 points) only after experts translated the problems into the formal language Lean, and took two to three days (Google DeepMind). Parallel exploration buys independence between attempts, which one longer trace does not; the selection step is where it succeeds or fails. Gemini 3 Deep Think followed in December 2025 as a mode in the Gemini app.

Gemini 3 (November 2025) and 3.1 Pro (February 2026)

Gemini 3 Pro (18 November 2025) is a sparse MoE transformer with native text, vision and audio input, a 1M-token window and 64K output, trained on TPUs with JAX and ML Pathways (model card). It introduced thinking levels and media-resolution controls in the API; 3 Flash followed on 17 December (release notes). 3.1 Pro (19 February 2026) replaced 3 Pro and is the current Pro tier, 1,048,576 tokens in and 65,536 out, still a preview more than seven months on.

The Flash cadence (May to September 2026)

Four Flash generations reached general availability in four months: 3.5 Flash (19 May, at Google I/O), 3.6 Flash with 3.5 Flash-Lite (21 July), 3.7 Flash (13 August) and 3.8 Flash (2 September) (release notes). 3.6 Flash used 17% fewer output tokens than 3.5 Flash, and 3.5 Flash-Lite generated 350 output tokens per second in Artificial Analysis' measurement (Google). The 21 July release also deprecated the temperature, top_p and top_k sampling parameters across the API (release notes).

Gemini 3.8 Flash and Flash Cyber (September 2026)

The current volume model. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (methodology v4.3.2, read 1 October 2026), 3.8 Flash at high thinking scores 40.9 against 39.1 for 3.7 Flash, below GPT-5.6 Sol (47.0) and Grok 4.6 (44.3) (Artificial Analysis). Launch-day index figures, such as The Register's 59 against 56 and "about 40 percent more per task" (The Register), were on a version of the index before v4.2 and do not compare with these.

Google reports 54.9% on HLE-Verified and gains over 3.7 Flash on DeepSWE v1.1 long-horizon software engineering, Vals Finance Agent V2 and Harvey's legal agent benchmark, and claims it beats most larger frontier models on long-horizon software engineering (Google). How the gain was bought is the part worth carrying: Google says the model works harder, "executing extra reasoning steps, and calling tools iteratively", so cost per index task rose by about a third over 3.7 Flash ($1.24 against $0.93) although the per-token price did not move (Works harder). A Flash-tier model spending more test-time compute by default blurs the tier distinction the price list implies.

Introductory pricing is $0.75 input and $3.75 output per million tokens through 31 December 2026, doubling to $1.50 and $7.50 from 1 January 2027 (pricing). On Real-SWE, a benchmark of private enterprise code, it resolves 31.2% of tasks, third behind Claude Fable 5.1 (38.8%) and GPT-6 Astra (33.8%), with overlapping 95% confidence intervals ([25%, 38%], [32%, 45%], [27%, 40%]) (DEV Community).

Flash Cyber is the same model tuned for vulnerability detection and remediation and distributed only through Fairwind: 47.2% pass@1 on CWE-Bench against 47.8% for the leading frontier model, and Chrome's security team got 2.6 times more correct vulnerability patches (Google).

Gemini 3.8 Live and Live Extended Thinking (15 September 2026): a tier, not a flagship

Production voice-agent models doing near real-time speech with visual grounding, detecting and switching between 97 languages mid-conversation. Extended Thinking is the one with an idea in it: it reasons and speaks at the same time, filling with verbal cues ("Let me check that") while tool and API calls run in the background instead of going silent. Every other way of spending a test-time budget makes the caller wait; this one covers the latency with speech, a product answer to a systems constraint and the first shipped one. Extended Thinking scored 82.6 on Artificial Analysis' speech-to-speech quality index at launch, first on it, with 68.6% on τ-Voice, 35.1% on Sierra's τ-Voice-banking and 97.7% on Big Bench Audio; all audio output carries a SynthID watermark. Both are in the Gemini API and AI Studio, in private preview in Gemini Enterprise, and behind Search Live, Gmail and Keep. Google publishes no millisecond latency figure (Google). The audio side of voice models is on Topic: generative-and-multimodal.

Gemini 4 Argon (30 September 2026): the next frontier, gated

For long-horizon software engineering, legal and finance work and cyber defence. Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, first place on AutomationBench at 51.3%, 91.7% on LVBench video understanding, a tie for first on CWE-bench v1 at 68%, and the lead on the Vals Index. The output limit rises to 1M tokens from the previous 64K. It rolls out first to trusted cyber defenders through Fairwind, then to paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers, at an introductory $2 input and $10 output per million ($4 and $20 afterwards), with cached input 95% off. Google names monitoring of the model's internal activations among its safeguards (Google). On the Intelligence Index v4.3 it scores 52.6 at $1.99 per task at the introductory price (Artificial Analysis). Gating a frontier model to defenders first is the same release shape as Anthropic's Mythos class (Anthropic: Claude family).

Lineage: Gemma (open)

Gemma 1 and 2 (2024)

Small open models built from Gemini research, first released 21 February 2024 (2B and 7B); Gemma 2 followed on 27 June 2024 (Wikipedia). Gemma 2 (2B, 9B, 27B) trained its 2B and 9B by knowledge distillation from a larger teacher rather than plain next-token prediction, and interleaved local and global attention layers one to one, which is why its small models competed with models two to three times their size (Gemma 2 report).

Gemma 3 (12 March 2025)

1B, 4B, 12B and 27B; 32K context on the 1B and 128K on the rest; image input through a 400M-parameter SigLIP encoder that turns an 896 by 896 image into 256 tokens. The architectural point is the 5:1 local-to-global ratio: five of every six layers attend only within a sliding window of the last 1,024 tokens, one in six across the whole sequence, up from Gemma 2's one to one with a 4,096-token window. The global layers use RoPE base 1,000,000 against 10,000 on the local ones, since only they see long distances. Pretraining ran from 2T tokens for the 1B to 14T for the 27B, distilled from a teacher by sampling 256 of its logits per token (Gemma 3 report). How the layout sets memory is worked through under Gemma attention.

Gemma 3n (full release 26 June 2025): built for phones

Two ideas make an 8B checkpoint behave like a 4B one on a device (Gemma 3n docs, developer guide):

  • Per-Layer Embeddings (PLE). Each decoder layer gets its own small embedding table per token; those tables can be computed outside accelerator memory, cached to fast storage and added as each layer runs. So E2B, which loads over 5B parameters in standard execution, can run with just under 2B (1.91B) resident.
  • MatFormer (a Matryoshka transformer) nests a smaller model inside the larger: E2B is a sub-model of E4B trained with it, so layers can be mixed to fit a memory budget between the two sizes.

Gemma 4 (2 April 2026): Apache 2.0 and a small MoE

Four sizes, E2B, E4B, 26B A4B (a mixture of experts) and a dense 31B, joined on 3 June 2026 by a dense 12B Unified (Wikipedia). It is the first Gemma under Apache 2.0; earlier generations used Google's own Gemma terms, so this is the release that made Gemma unrestricted for commercial use. Google reports over 400 million Gemma downloads since the first generation; at launch the 31B ranked third among open models on the Arena text leaderboard and the 26B sixth (Google). From the model card and report (model card, Gemma 4 report):

  • Vocabulary 262K tokens on every size.
  • The MoE. 26B A4B routes each token to 8 of 128 experts plus 1 shared expert, so it holds 26B of weights but does about 4B of matmuls per token: a bet that the memory-versus-compute trade favours sparsity even on one workstation (Sparse vs dense).
  • Attention. Local and global interleaved, 5:1 on every size except E2B (4:1), the final layer always global, plus two savings on the global layers: keys reused as values (on all but E2B and E4B) and p-RoPE (rotary position applied to a quarter of each head's dimensions). The edge models also share KV caches across layers (Gemma attention).
  • 12B Unified is encoder-free. Instead of a separate vision encoder (550M parameters on the 26B and 31B, 150M on E2B and E4B), it projects raw 48 by 48 pixel patches straight into the embedding space with one 35M-parameter matrix, and audio the same way.
  • A built-in draft model. Each model ships a separate 4-layer multi-token prediction (MTP) drafter for speculative decoding (76M parameters on E2B to 500M on the 31B). What it buys depends on an acceptance rate Google does not publish: Gemma on one machine works it through.
  • Thinking. A <|think|> token switches on a reasoning channel in the output.

T5Gemma (April 2025) and T5Gemma 2 (December 2025): the encoder-decoder branch

A research line rather than a product tier, and the only open encoder-decoder LLMs a frontier lab currently ships. The method is adaptation: copy a pretrained decoder-only Gemma checkpoint into an encoder-decoder shell (the encoder's self-attention switched from causal to bidirectional, the decoder gaining cross-attention over the encoder output) and continue pretraining, which is cheaper than pretraining from scratch and, above roughly 100M parameters, better. Two results carry beyond Gemma: asymmetric sizing (a 9B encoder with a 2B decoder runs at Gemma 2 2B's latency and scores clearly above it, the right shape for long-input, short-output work and impossible in a decoder-only model) and long context (T5Gemma 2 4B-4B scores 81.7 on RULER 32K against Gemma 3 4B's 66.8, despite pretraining at only 16K). EmbeddingGemma is built on T5Gemma 2 checkpoints. The method, ablations and every number are on Encoder-Decoder Gemma and T5Gemma 2.

How thinking is controlled

Gemini models think dynamically by default: the model decides per request how much reasoning a prompt needs. The caller steers it with one parameter, thinking_level, in the generation config: minimal, low, medium or high. Google's guidance is minimal or low for fact retrieval and classification, the default for comparisons and creative reasoning, and high for advanced coding, mathematics and multi-step planning (thinking guide). Four details are Google-specific and are where code breaks:

  • Levels and defaults vary by model, so a request written for one model is not portable to the next. Try it below.
  • The token budget is gone. The 2.5 models were steered by thinking_budget, a token count. The current guide describes only thinking_level, including for 2.5 Pro, 2.5 Flash and 2.5 Flash-Lite (the last ships with thinking off by default). Code that still sends a budget was written against an API that has moved.
  • You pay for thinking you do not see. Thinking tokens are billed as output, and the bill counts every thought token generated even when only a summary returns: "Pricing is based on the full thought tokens the model needs to generate". Summaries are controlled by thinking_summaries; by default only the final answer returns.
  • Thought signatures must go back. Each response carries encrypted thought signatures, a representation of the model's reasoning state, present even after minimal reasoning. If you manage history yourself, every thought block must be resent exactly as received, or multi-turn tool use loses the reasoning that led to the call. With server-side state (previous_interaction_id) Google handles them.

Try: pick the level your code sends. Models that reject it are struck out; the ringed cell is each model's default. minimal fails on 3.8 Flash, 3.7 Flash and 3.1 Pro.

From the per-model table in the thinking guide. The guide lists the 2.5 Pro and 2.5 Flash defaults only as "on", with no level, and still lists gemini-3-pro-preview, which was shut down on 9 March 2026.

The two outer designs this sits between (a caller-set token budget, and a hidden router that decides for you) and the research behind them are on Reasoning models and test-time compute; Anthropic's equivalent, adaptive thinking with an effort setting, is on Anthropic: Claude family.

How media is counted against the window

Native multimodality makes media a token cost like any other, so "will this fit, and what will it cost" is arithmetic. On the Gemini 3 models a video frame costs 70 tokens at the default, low and medium media resolution and 280 at high; frames are sampled at 1 per second; audio costs 25 tokens per second at every resolution; an image costs 1,120 tokens at the default (280 low, 560 medium, 2,240 ultra high, set per item); a PDF page 560 at the default (media resolution). Window 1,048,576 tokens for every 3.x model (models). Prices from pricing; 3.1 Pro's rate for the whole prompt doubles above 200K tokens. The 2.5 rates: 258 tokens per frame by default, 66 at low (Gemini 2.5 report), audio 32 tokens per second (the generic tokens page, which also still quotes 263 video tokens per second for older models).

Worked example: one hour on 3.8 Flash. Default resolution: 3,600 × (1 × 70 + 25) = 3,600 × 95 = 342,000 tokens, 32.6% of the window, costing 342,000 / 106 × $0.75 = $0.2565 (about 51 cents from 2027). At high: 3,600 × 305 = 1,098,000 tokens, over the window by 49,424. The longest video that fits is 1,048,576 / 95 ≈ 11,038 seconds (3 h 4 min) at default and 1,048,576 / 305 ≈ 3,438 seconds (57 min) at high. So "an hour of video" is a third of the window at the default and does not fit at high. The 2.5 report made the same point with its own figures: dropping from 258 to 66 tokens per frame took the window from about 1 hour of video to about 3 (Gemini 2.5 report).

On 1 September 2026 Google added agentic video understanding for 3.7 Flash, 3.6 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite (release notes): instead of every frame at 1 per second, the model loads only the transcript, frames or audio it needs, so "a 1-hour lecture that would use ~1.08M tokens in static mode might use ~108K tokens", depending on prompt and content (tokens page). Cost per task is tokens per task times price per token, and a release can move either factor:

Worked example: 3.7 Flash to 3.8 Flash. The per-token prices are identical ($0.75 and $3.75 introductory), so the whole change is in T.

  1. On the Intelligence Index v4.3, both at high thinking, 3.8 Flash costs $1.24 per task and 3.7 Flash $0.93 (3.8 Flash, 3.7 Flash): 1.24 / 0.93 ≈ 1.34, about a third more, for 40.9 on the index against 39.1.
  2. With identical prices the cost ratio is a token ratio, but a price-weighted one. Over the whole index run 3.8 Flash used 45% more input tokens (5.97B against 4.12B), 65% more output tokens (172M against 104M) and 2.3 times the reasoning tokens (136M against 60M). "Works harder" means more turns, and every turn re-reads the context.
  3. On 1 January 2027 both prices double, so the same task costs about 1.24 × 2 = $2.48 on 3.8 Flash ($2.49 from AA's unrounded $1.2428), with no change to the model.
  4. Even then it is well under Claude Fable 5.1's $7.63 per task at maximum effort, which is about 6.2 times the introductory figure.

The lesson travels: compare models on cost per task, not on the price list, and expect a newer "cheap" tier to spend more tokens than its predecessor. The caller holds two levers: a lower thinking_level cuts Tout, and context caching, a tenth of the input price, cuts what the repeated input costs. The Cost per task tab moves both.

Gemma attention: why memory stops growing

During generation a model keeps every earlier token's keys and values, the KV cache, so they are not recomputed. A sliding-window ("local") layer only ever looks back W tokens, so its cache stops growing at the window; a global layer keeps every token. For one sequence of T tokens:

KV=(LgTeg+Llmin(T,W)el)×bel=2Hkvdh
Lg, Ll
number of global and local layers
eg, el
elements stored per token in one global or local layer: keys plus values, 2 × KV heads × head dimension (one copy instead of two where keys are reused as values)
W
the sliding window, 1,024 tokens on Gemma 3 and the larger Gemma 4 models
b
bytes per element: 2 for bf16, 1 for int8

Past T = W only the global term grows, so long-context memory is set almost entirely by the global layers. Gemma 3's report puts the cache's share of inference memory at about 60% for a global-only model against under 15% with sliding windows of 1,024, measured with a 32K-token prefill (Gemma 3 report). The cost is that most layers cannot see distant tokens directly and depend on the global layers to carry them.

Try: each cell is one layer of the chosen model; global layers are dark. The default, Gemma 3 27B at 128K tokens, reproduces about 10.4 GiB of cache against 62 GiB if every layer were global. Move the context below 1,024 tokens: the two layouts cost the same. Switch to Gemma 4 31B to see wider global heads paid for by reusing keys as values.

Gemma 3 27B: 62 layers (52 local, 10 global), 16 KV heads of dimension 128, so 8 KiB per layer per token in bf16, the gallery's 496 KiB per token if no window were credited (gallery; LLM Architecture Gallery page). The middle option is illustrative: Gemma 3 27B's dimensions under Gemma 2's one-to-one layout with a 4,096 window. Gemma 4 31B from its config: 50 local layers with 16 KV heads of 256, 10 global layers with 4 KV heads of 512 and keys reused as values. GiB is 230 bytes.

Gemma 4's two global-layer savings. Reusing keys as values stores one vector per global head instead of two. p-RoPE applies rotary position to only a fraction p = 0.25 of each global head's dimensions (config: partial_rotary_factor 0.25, base 1,000,000 against 10,000 on local layers). The report says these, "combined with KV cache sharing and the reuse of keys as values in global layers ... reduce the global KV cache footprint by up to 37.5%" (Gemma 4 report), and does not show the arithmetic.

One accounting that gives exactly 37.5% (our reconstruction): if the rotated part of each key must be stored separately from the unrotated value, a global head stores the value (d) plus the rotated fraction of the key (p d) instead of key and value (2d), a saving of (1 − p) / 2. Move p: at 0.25 it lands on the report's figure; at 1 (ordinary RoPE) sharing saves nothing; at 0 it saves half.

The report's own memory table, and the gallery's 840 KiB per token for the 31B, count one stored vector per global head, a 50% cut; recomputed from the config that reproduces the table's 1.10 GB cache at 32K (see Gemma on one machine). So the two figures in the report imply slightly different accounting.

The edge models go further and share KV caches across layers: in E2B the last 20 of 35 layers, and in E4B the last 18 of 42, reuse earlier layers' caches (Gemma 4 report; num_kv_shared_layers in the configs). The generic mechanism of sliding windows, and the other attention variants, are on LLM Architecture Gallery and the architectural deltas that matter.

Gemma 4 on one machine: sparse against dense

Gemma 4 offers one quality band two ways, a 26B mixture of experts and a 31B dense model, and the choice is memory against compute. A transformer's forward pass costs about two floating-point operations per parameter that touches the token:

F≈2Nactive
F
floating-point operations per generated token
Nactive
parameters used for that token: all of them in a dense model, the routed experts plus the shared parts in an MoE

Memory, by contrast, has to hold every parameter, active or not. Worked example, figures from the Gemma 4 report's memory table (text only, KV cache in int8 at a 32K context, Gemma 4 report):

  1. Compute: 26B A4B has 3.8B active, so F ≈ 2 × 3.8 × 109 = 7.6 GFLOP per token; 31B has F ≈ 2 × 30.7 × 109 = 61.4 GFLOP. The MoE does about 8 times less arithmetic per token.
  2. Weights in bf16: 52.0 GB against 64.0 GB, only about 20% less (52 / 64 = 0.81), because every expert has to be resident. Both fit one 80 GB H100 unquantized (Google).
  3. Weights at 4 bits (Q4_0): 16.2 GB against 19.2 GB, so both fit a 24 GB card, the MoE with more room to spare.
  4. KV cache at 32K: 0.28 GB against 1.10 GB; the MoE has 30 layers against the dense model's 60, and fewer, narrower KV heads.
  5. Decode reads the weights it uses for every token. The table gives the MoE's active weights as 7.6 GB in bf16 and 2.8 GB at 4 bits, against all 19.2 GB for the dense 31B at 4 bits, about 7 times less to read per token.

So with memory to spare, the MoE generates far faster at a similar footprint. When memory is the constraint (a 16 GB card), neither fits at 4 bits, and the 12B Unified (24.0 GB in bf16, 7.65 GB at 4 bits) is the model that does. Gemma on one machine lets you set the memory, context and precision. The routing mechanism and the general trade are on Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models.

How Google trains

Everything on TPUs

Google's model cards name only its own hardware and software. Gemini 3 Pro was trained on TPUs with JAX (a functional array library that traces Python into XLA-compiled programs, with sharding expressed as annotations on arrays rather than as a wrapper around a module tree) and ML Pathways (the orchestration layer that lets one program drive many TPU pods asynchronously) (model card). Gemini 2.5 trained with synchronous data parallelism across multiple 8,960-chip TPU v5p pods in several datacenters, and its slice-granularity elasticity kept about 97% of throughput through local failures by continuing on fewer slices (Gemini 2.5 report). Gemma 4 trained on TPU v6e (4,096 chips for E2B up to 10,240 for the 31B), the 12B on 12,288 TPU v4 chips (Gemma 4 report).

It buys freedom from GPU supply, pricing and allocation politics, and a pod interconnect built for exactly this collective pattern. It costs transferability: essentially none of the stack carries to an outside practitioner the way PyTorch, FSDP and NCCL do, and the work is correspondingly hard to reproduce. Since 2026 the dependency also runs outward: Ironwood, the seventh TPU generation, is the first Google sells for other companies' workloads, and Anthropic's deal for up to a million TPUs runs on it (TPUs: systolic arrays and pod-scale machines); Alphabet's Q2 2026 results say Google Cloud "generates product revenues primarily from the sale of TPU systems" (Alphabet Q2 2026). The software is on Topic: jax-and-tpu.

Native multimodality from pretraining

Trained in from the start rather than added as an adapter, since 1.0: audio and video in, images out through integrated generation (the Nano Banana image models), all through one context window. Gemma 4's 12B Unified carries the idea to the open family by dropping the separate encoder altogether. Where this sits among shallow, modular and unified designs is on Multimodal LLM Architectures.

One distillation pipeline, many products

In knowledge distillation a small student is trained to match a large teacher's whole next-token probability distribution, not just the single correct token, which carries more information per example. Storing a full distribution over a vocabulary of about 262,000 tokens for every training token is too expensive, so Google keeps part of it: Gemini 2.5 approximates the teacher "using a k-sparse distribution over the vocabulary" (only the most likely tokens) (Gemini 2.5 report), and Gemma 3 samples 256 logits per token weighted by the teacher's probabilities (Gemma 3 report). Flash and below have been distilled this way since Gemini 1.5, and Gemma is the open end of the same pipeline, which is why the open models track the closed ones so closely in behaviour.

Try: how much teacher output one student run would have to store. The default is Gemma 3 27B's 14T tokens with 256 entries kept per token.

Vocabulary 262,144 entries. Bytes per entry are illustrative: 4 bytes per probability for the full distribution, 4 for the probability plus 4 for the token id when only k entries are kept. Google does not publish how it stores them.

Distribution is the strategic asset

Search (AI Overviews and AI Mode), Workspace, Android and Vertex all ship Gemini by default. The Gemini app had 950 million monthly active users, and Gemini models process 22 billion API tokens a minute, in Alphabet's Q2 2026 results (Alphabet Q2 2026) Earlier reporting gave 750 million at the end of 2025; that figure is not in the Q2 release and its source is not confirmed here. It now reaches a rival's flagship surface: Apple's rebuilt Siri entered public beta on 14 September 2026 running on models "custom-built in collaboration with Google and its Gemini models", split between the device and Apple's Private Cloud Compute, with the EU excluded at launch and China awaiting regulatory approval (AI Weekly, citing Apple). Google supplies the assistant model on the phones it competes with: distribution bought rather than owned, and evidence that the training stack rather than the app is the durable asset.

Common mistakes

Comparing Flash generations on the per-token price.
3.6, 3.7 and 3.8 Flash have the same price list; 3.8 Flash costs about a third more per task ($1.24 against $0.93 on the Intelligence Index v4.3) because it spends more tokens.
Sending one thinking configuration to every model.
Minimal is not supported on 3.8 Flash, 3.7 Flash or 3.1 Pro, defaults differ (medium, high or minimal), and thinking_budget belongs to an API that has moved.
Assuming you pay only for the summary.
Thinking tokens are billed in full as output even when only a summary returns.
Dropping thought signatures.
Rebuilding history without the thought blocks breaks multi-turn tool use.
Taking "1M tokens" as "an hour of anything".
An hour of video is 342,000 tokens at the default resolution and over the limit at high.
Planning around a preview model as if it were stable.
3 Pro Preview (18 November 2025) was shut down and redirected on 9 March 2026, within four months of launch.
Taking the Pro tier for the strongest open-API model.
On the current index 3.1 Pro (29.7) sits below 3.8 Flash at high (40.9); tier names track size and price, not this year's scores.
Reading E4B as a 4B checkpoint.
It is an 8B checkpoint with 4.5B effective parameters; the saving depends on running PLE outside accelerator memory.
Choosing the MoE when memory is the constraint.
26B A4B is about 8 times cheaper in compute per token than 31B but only about 20% smaller in memory.
Assuming Gemma carries its old licence.
Gemma 4 is Apache 2.0; Gemma 3 and earlier are under Google's Gemma terms.

Batching: when the MoE stops being cheaper to decode

The decode ceiling above is for one sequence. A server decodes a batch of B sequences per step: the weights are read once for the whole batch, the arithmetic grows with B, and each sequence still reads its own cache. The Scaling Book's general step time (Scaling Book, inference):

tstep=BSKVW+max(2BNactiveF,P(B)W),Bcrit=FW P(B)=Pshared+E(B)Pexpert,E(B)=n[1−(1−k/n)B]
SKV
one sequence's KV cache in bytes (context and cache settings from the controls above)
F, W
the chip's peak FLOP/s (bf16) and memory bandwidth in bytes/s
P(B)
weight bytes read per step: all of them for the dense 31B; for the MoE the shared part plus every expert at least one token in the batch was routed to
E(B)
expected distinct experts touched per layer when each token picks k = 8 of n = 128 at random (uniform routing, illustrative: real routers are skewed)
Bcrit
the batch at which a bf16 weight matmul turns from memory-bound to compute-bound: arithmetic intensity, FLOPs per byte of weights read, reaches the chip's F / W

Pshared and Pexpert are back-solved from the report's two MoE figures, 52.0 GB resident and 7.6 GB active in bf16 (16.2 and 2.8 GB at Q4_0) (Gemma 4 report): 120 Pexpert = 52.0 − 7.6, so Pexpert = 0.37 GB and Pshared = 7.6 − 8 × 0.37 = 4.64 GB. P(1) = 7.6 GB and P(∞) = 52.0 GB therefore hold by construction.

Try: the defaults are TPU v5e, bf16 and a batch of 32. Under uniform routing those 32 tokens already touch 87% of the experts, so the MoE reads 46 GB of weights per step, not 7.6, and its lead over the 31B shrinks from about 8 times at batch 1 to under 2. Slide the batch up: the 31B turns compute-bound near 250, the MoE only near 1,640, and at that point its 8 times smaller arithmetic is what counts again. Switch to Ironwood to see the crossover move.

Speculative decoding with the MTP drafter

Every Gemma 4 model ships a small drafter, a 4-layer Transformer that cross-attends to the main model's KV cache (76M parameters on E2B, 77M on E4B, 400M on the 12B, 430M on 26B A4B, 500M on the 31B; Gemma 4 report). The drafter proposes γ tokens; the target model checks all of them in one forward pass, keeps the longest prefix it agrees with, and adds one token of its own. The output distribution is exactly the target's (Leviathan et al., Hugging Face). With an acceptance rate α per drafted token and a drafter that costs c of a target pass (Leviathan et al., Equation 1 and Theorem 3.8):

tokens per target pass=1−αγ+11−α,speed-up=1−αγ+1(1−α)(γc+1)
α
probability that the target accepts a drafted token (assumed independent token to token)
γ
tokens drafted per step
c
time of one drafter run over time of one target run

The speed-up assumes the target can check γ + 1 positions for the price of one, which holds while decoding is memory-bound: at small batches, the left of the roofline above. The paper's Corollary 3.9: some γ gives a speed-up whenever α > c.

Try: the defaults are the first row of the paper's Table 4 (T5-XXL drafted by T5-small, translation, greedy), and the formula gives 3.16 against the paper's 3.2. Pick other rows: all twelve land within 0.1. Then pick the Gemma 4 preset: Google gives no acceptance rate, and "up to ~3x" needs α of about 0.77 at γ = 4 (fitted, and only if the assumed γ and c are right). Raise γ and watch the speed-up peak and then fall as the drafts that get thrown away cost more than they save.
