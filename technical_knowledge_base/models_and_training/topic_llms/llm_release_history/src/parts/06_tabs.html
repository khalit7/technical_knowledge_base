Published size over time

Every row with a published size, against its release date, on a log scale. In Total and active mode an MoE release is a line from its total (top) down to its active parameters (bottom); a dense release is one dot. The step line is the largest open total so far; the band is the median active size of open releases over the trailing four quarters.

Try: press Play to watch it fill quarter by quarter: the record line climbs from 65B to 2.8T while the trailing median active size never leaves 7B to 45B. Then switch to Sparsity: total ÷ active climbs from Mixtral's 3.6 to DeepSeek V4.1-Flash's 34.5. Click a mark for the row.

Sizes are as published, in billions of parameters, from each row's source (open circles: closed weights; hollow marker: Grok-1's active size, derived as 25% of 314B from {{xAI's statement|https://x.ai/news/grok-os}}). Family rows use their largest member. Month-only rows are drawn at the 15th. 48 of the 50 closed rows publish no size and cannot be drawn, so this is a picture of open models. Medians and the record line are computed from the rows on this page (trailing median: all open sized releases in the four quarters ending with the one shown). The chart stops when it is off screen, and starts paused if your system asks for reduced motion.

What it shows. Total size and compute per token came apart. The largest open model grew about 43-fold in three and a half years (65.2B LLaMA to 2.8T Kimi K3), but the typical open release still computes each token with a few tens of billions of parameters, because almost every large release since DeepSeek-V3 is an MoE. Memory (total) became the cost that grew; compute per token (active) is the one labs held down. The parent page explains why ({{Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models|n:3c65c17b0d0d810080cce6648ac8f2f2}} owns the mechanism).

What it shows. Reasoning spread fastest: from one lab (o1-preview, September 2024) to eleven within eight months, and 18 by April 2026. MoE took longer: two labs in 2023 (xAI with Grok-1, Mistral with Mixtral), and Meta's first MoE only with Llama 4 in April 2025. Open weights came first for most of the Chinese labs and last for OpenAI. Hybrid attention is the newest idea and still a minority, ten labs by September 2026.

Cadence: releases per lab per quarter

Each cell counts a lab's rows in one quarter, green for open weights and purple for closed, filled to the share of each. Totals sit at the end of each row and at the foot of each column. The median gap is the median number of days between a lab's consecutive rows (labs with at least three rows; month-only rows at the 15th).

Counted from the table, so point releases left out of it are not counted either: a lab with few rows may still ship often. 2026 Q3 is the busiest quarter in the table.

What it shows. The field's acceleration is not one lab speeding up: the number of labs shipping in a quarter grew too, and the Chinese open labs (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax, MiMo) release as often as the US closed labs. Sort by median gap to see who ships most steadily.
