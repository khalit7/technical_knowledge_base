What this table is

The significant releases of each lab covered by {{Topic: llms|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}, from February 2023 to the end of September 2026, one row per release: the date it shipped, whether its weights are open and under which licence, its size where the lab has published one, and why it matters. The parent page's release timeline is drawn from this table.

Counted from the rows below. Rows per year: 2023, 12; 2024, 22; 2025, 46; 2026 (nine months), 60. The tabs: Sizes plots every published size against its date, total and active, and plays it quarter by quarter; Firsts shows when each lab first shipped open weights, MoE, reasoning, multimodal input and hybrid attention; Cadence counts each lab's releases per quarter. The lane-by-lane timeline itself lives on the parent page.

How it was compiled

Each date and size is taken from the lab's announcement, model card or a dated report, linked in the last column; where the parent page gives a date or a parameter count, the table agrees with it. Row dates are the lab's first public announcement or release: where a preview and general availability differ, the row uses the first public date. Closed models almost never publish a size, and "not disclosed" says so rather than estimating. Minor point releases the parent page never names are left out, so the table shows the steps rather than every version.

The kind tags in the table (flagship, reasoning, MoE, dense, multimodal, small, hybrid attention, encoder-decoder) were assigned by whoever compiled the table, from each row's note and source; they are this table's labels, not the labs' own categories, and they drive the filters and the Firsts tab.

How to read the size column

Sizes are in billions of parameters (1T = 1,000B). A dense model uses every parameter for every token, so it has one number. A mixture-of-experts (MoE) model routes each token through a few of many expert blocks, so it has two, written total / active: total is what has to sit in memory, active is what each token is computed with, which is roughly what sets the compute per token. Their ratio is the model's sparsity:

sparsity=total parametersactive parameters

Dense models have sparsity 1. Pick any MoE row to see its two numbers to scale.

Defaults reproduce the rows' own ratios, by construction (same two numbers): DeepSeek-V3 671 ÷ 37 = 18.1, the note's "18x sparsity"; DeepSeek V4 Pro 1,600 ÷ 49 = 32.7, the note's "33x".

Three conventions to know. Family rows (Gemma, Gemma 3, Gemma 4, Llama 2, Llama 3, OLMo 2, Qwen2.5, Aya Expanse) give the largest published size; Llama 4 uses Maverick (400 / 17; Scout is 109B total); K2 Horizon gives its 375B-A23B member of six; DeepSeek V4.1-Flash gives the 16B active in decode (8B in prefill). Where the table had no figure for an open model, this version fills it from the model card and marks the row (see Caveats). And closed rows are "not disclosed" except two: Qwen3.8-Max-0902, the API snapshot of the open 2.4T-A95B weights, and Step 5 Preview, whose size was announced before its weights.

Weights and licences

Open means the weights can be downloaded; it does not mean one set of rights. Grouping the licences named in the table into four kinds (this page's grouping) shows the shift: in 2023 and 2024 fewer than half of open releases came under Apache 2.0 or MIT (4 of 9, then 4 of 16), while from 2025 three in four did (25 of 32, then 24 of 33).

Counted from the table's Weights column. Permissive: Apache 2.0 or MIT. With conditions: Modified MIT and GLM-5.3's MIT-style licence with a security review for model-as-a-service firms above $10B revenue. Own licence: Llama, Gemma, DeepSeek, Tongyi Qianwen, Qwen, Tencent, MiniMax, Kimi K3 and ChatGLM2 licences. Non-commercial: research-only licences and CC-BY-NC 4.0. Magistral counts as permissive for its open Small model. Click a bar segment to filter the table.

Caveats

"First" means first in this table.
Every "first" on this page (the Firsts tab, the notes) is the first row in this table, which starts in February 2023 and leaves out minor releases. A lab's earlier history does not count: OpenAI's first open weights here is gpt-oss in August 2025, though the row itself says it is OpenAI's first open weights "since GPT-2" ({{InfoQ|https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/08/openai-gpt-oss/}}).
Seven rows are dated to the month only.
Hunyuan-T1 (2025-03), Step-3 (2025-07), Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B (2025-09), Kimi K2 Thinking (2025-11), OLMo 3.1 32B (2025-12), Step 3.5 Flash (2026-02) and Hy3 (2026-04) could not be pinned to a day from a primary source. They sort to the start of their month here and are drawn at the 15th in the charts.
Point releases are left out.
Minor releases the parent page never names (GPT-4 Turbo, o1, Claude 2.1, Grok 4.1 and similar) are left out. Also trimmed: o3-mini, GPT-4.5, GPT-5.1, 5.2, 5.4 and 5.5, Claude 2, Opus 4.1 and 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Gemma 2, Gemini 2.0 Flash, PaLM 2, and minor Qwen, DeepSeek, GLM, Grok, MiniMax and Cohere point releases. Counts per lab therefore measure steps, not every version.
Two rows are not language models in the usual sense.
Fugu (Sakana AI) is an orchestrator over other models and Jev (TypeSafe AI) a structured-decision model; both are listed because the parent page discusses them. Use the search box or the lab filter to leave them out.
Some dates and weights need a footnote.
Grok-1 is dated to its 3 November 2023 chatbot launch; its 314B MoE weights were released under Apache 2.0 on 17 March 2024 ({{xAI|https://x.ai/news/grok-os}}). Step 5 Preview is listed as closed: weights are promised for 15 October 2026 with no licence named, and the Hugging Face repository was empty on 30 September. Llama 3 covers the April 2024 8B and 70B release; the 405B has its own Llama 3.1 row (July 2024), while the parent page's "Llama 3: 8B, 70B and 405B" follows the Llama 3 Herd paper, which covers both.
Where sources disagree.
MiMo-V2.6-Pro and Flash: Wikipedia and DataNorth say 21 September, Unite.ai and others 22 September (likely the time zone); the table keeps the 22nd ({{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiaomi_MiMo}}). MiMo-V2.5: Xiaomi's release gives 310B total, while the parent page implies 309B through Naive-N0.5-Flash's "matching size"; the table keeps 310, and 309B is MiMo-V2-Flash ({{Xiaomi|https://x.com/XiaomiMiMo/status/2048821516079661561}}).
Sources and licences not all primary.
Several rows cite Wikipedia or secondary reports (DataCamp, Unite.ai, llm-stats, MarkTechPost, Neowin) where the lab's page could not be reached (openai.com refused automated requests) or was undated; primary sources were used wherever they could be fetched. Licence names for some older models (the DeepSeek License for LLM 67B to V3, the Tongyi Qianwen License for Qwen1.5-MoE, the Qwen License for Qwen2.5-72B, MIT for Phi-2) were not all confirmed against the original release pages. Unconfirmed details carried from the parent page: the KDA and IndexPool names for GLM-5.3-Flash were not found in its sources; qk-clip is confirmed for the Kimi K2 line, not for K3.
