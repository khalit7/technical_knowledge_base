How Mistral trains

Mistral publishes weights first and papers rarely, so most of its recipes have to be inferred from the artefacts. Two things are documented, both in the {{Magistral paper|@magp}}.

Distillation from its own larger models. Magistral Small (24B, Apache 2.0) was cold-started with supervised fine-tuning of Mistral Small 3 on reasoning traces from Magistral Medium (correct traces from Medium's RL run, plus Medium's answers to OpenThoughts and OpenR1 code prompts, plus 10% general instruction data), then trained with reinforcement learning on top. That is distillation in the ordinary sense: the small model learns from the large one's outputs rather than from raw data alone.

Magistral's RL recipe, the one place the lab was genuinely open about method. Magistral Medium was trained from Mistral Medium 3 by pure reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR): no learned reward model, and no reasoning traces distilled from someone else's reasoner. A rollout is scored by mechanically checking its final answer (a maths result by a rule-based checker with SymPy, or code against 20 unit tests), so style cannot game the reward the way it can game a preference model. The algorithm is GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimisation: sample a group of answers to one prompt and score each against the group's mean instead of a learned critic; the full objective is on {{RL for LLMs: RLHF, GRPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}), with Mistral's own changes:

  • No KL penalty to the starting model, so the policy may move as far as the reward pulls it; Mistral found the policy diverges regardless and the reference copy was not worth its compute.
  • Clip-higher: the upper clipping threshold εhigh raised so rare but useful tokens can gain probability faster: tuned between 0.26 and 0.28 for Medium, 0.3 for Small. For scale, PPO's original setting is 0.2 on both sides ({{PPO paper|@ppo}}); the Magistral paper does not state its lower bound. A token at probability 0.01 may rise to 0.0128 in one update at 0.28, against 0.012 at 0.2.
  • Length-normalised loss over all tokens of all answers in a group, so long answers do not dominate; the advantage is simply ri − μ (no division by the group's spread), normalised again across each minibatch.
  • Zero-advantage filtering: groups where every answer got the same reward carry no learning signal and are dropped from the batch.
  • Reward: 0 if the format is wrong (one <think> block, the answer in \boxed{} or a fenced code block); otherwise 0.1 for the format, a further 0.9 for a correct answer, a soft length penalty of up to −0.1 that grows linearly once an answer passes lmax − lcache (raised from 16k to 32k tokens during Medium's run), and 0.1 more when the problem, the reasoning and the answer are all in the same language (10% of problems were translated into French, Spanish, Italian, German, Chinese and Russian).

Try: one group of eight answers to the same problem. Toggle what each answer got right and watch the rewards, the advantages and the filter. Make every answer correct and the group is dropped.

Reward rules from the {{Magistral paper|@magp}}, section 2.2; the group of eight and the answers are illustrative (the paper does not state the group size). "Half into the penalty zone" is an answer halfway between lmax − lcache and lmax. Standard GRPO divides by the group's standard deviation; Magistral does not, and normalises across the minibatch instead.

The result, on the paper's own evaluation: AIME 2024 pass@1 went from 26.8% for Mistral Medium 3 to 73.6% for Magistral Medium (90.0% by majority vote over 64 samples), and Magistral Small reached 70.7%, against 65.4% with the distilled traces alone. The broader story of reasoning models and how their budgets are spent is on {{Reasoning models and test-time compute|n:3c65c17b0d0d8190b9b4cd0acbcb66df}}.

Apache 2.0 is the rung that made Mistral's name. It matters as an engineering fact, not only a legal one: no acceptable-use annex and no user threshold of the kind in Meta's Llama community licence (which incorporates an acceptable use policy and requires a separate licence above 700 million monthly active users, {{Llama 3.1 licence|@llama}}), so a company can fine-tune the model and run it inside a product without asking anyone. It is also the wedge into government, defence and on-premises deals, where open weights under EU jurisdiction beat a few points of benchmark. But since 2025 the ladder has widened at both ends: the dense agentic flagships carry a revenue carve-out that bites at $20M a month, far below Llama's threshold; the voice model is non-commercial; and code completion, OCR and embeddings are API only. "Mistral is Apache 2.0" is true of its MoE flagship and its small models, not of the catalogue.

The business around the models

  • Products. The consumer assistant Le Chat became Vibe on 28 May 2026 ("Le Chat is now Vibe", {{Mistral|@vibe}}); Vibe is also the name of Mistral's open-source (Apache 2.0) command-line coding agent, launched with Devstral 2. Developers use the API platform and AI Studio; enterprises building custom models use Forge (March 2026, {{Mistral|@forge}}). Since 11 August 2026 the platform offers regional endpoints that let customers choose Europe or the US for inference, a priority tier for critical workloads (public preview), and third-party open models beside Mistral's own ({{Mistral|@region}}; the {{catalogue|@models}} lists Z.ai GLM 5.3).
  • Compute. Mistral Compute (announced June 2025) is a private stack of GPUs, orchestration, APIs and services on Nvidia reference architectures, with "tens of thousands of GPUs" ({{Mistral|@compute}}). The aim of 1 GW of compute capacity in Europe by 2030 was stated on 11 August 2026 ({{Mistral|@region}}) and repeated after the Series D.
  • Money. A €1.7B Series C in September 2025, led by ASML, at an €11.7B post-money valuation (often quoted as about $14B) ({{Mistral|@raise}}). Then a €3B Series D on 8 September 2026, led by Samsung Electronics with EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund and PSG Equity as co-leads, at more than €21B post-money, which Mistral calls the largest equity round ever completed by a European technology company; the money goes to compute, infrastructure, commercial growth and international expansion ({{TechCrunch|@tc}}). It acquired Koyeb (a Paris start-up, February 2026, {{Wikipedia|@wiki}}) and Emmi AI (industrial engineering simulation, May 2026, {{Mistral|@emmi}}), and opened a Munich hub for industrial AI on 28 September 2026 ({{Mistral|@munich}}).
  • Distribution. Since 16 September 2026 Mistral Small 4 is one of the selectable models in Mozilla's Firefox Smart Window beta, starting in France and North America, with zero data retention agreed by Mistral ({{Mistral|@moz}}; {{Gigazine|@gig}}). It is the sovereignty argument reaching a consumer surface rather than a procurement one.

Which Mistral model when

  • The most capable open weights you can run yourself under Apache 2.0: Large 3, if you have a full eight-GPU node. It buys the best Mistral weights with no licence conditions, and costs 675 GB of FP8 weights.
  • Cheap, fast and still multimodal with a reasoning switch: Small 4. It buys 6B-active decode cost and a small cache, and costs memory for all 119B parameters.
  • Agentic coding and tool use, self-hosted: Medium 3.5, unless your company is over the $20M monthly revenue line, in which case Devstral Small 2 (Apache 2.0, 24B, weights only now) is the unencumbered option, or Medium 3.5 through the API.
  • On a phone or laptop: Ministral 3 at 3B or 8B.
  • A guard model in front of any generator, Mistral's or not: Shieldstral, with your policy written as text.
  • Code completion inside an editor: Codestral, through the API.

Common mistakes and misconceptions

"Mistral models use sliding-window attention."
Mistral 7B did; Mixtral, a few months later, already used full attention over 32k, and the current models use full grouped-query or latent attention. Treat SWA as a 2023 Mistral choice, not a family trait.
"8x7B means 56B parameters."
Only the feed-forward networks are replicated; attention and embeddings are shared, so it is 46.7B total and 12.9B active.
"Mixtral's experts are topic experts."
Mistral's own routing analysis found syntactic rather than topical specialisation, and strong repetition between consecutive tokens.
"Everything Mistral ships is Apache 2.0."
Medium 3.5 and Devstral 2 are under a modified MIT licence with a $20M monthly revenue exception, Voxtral TTS is non-commercial, and Codestral, OCR and the embedding models have no public weights.
"Magistral is Mistral's reasoning model."
It was; reasoning is now a setting on Small 4 and Medium 3.5, and every Magistral version is deprecated in the catalogue.
"Magistral was all pure RL."
Magistral Medium was; Magistral Small started from Magistral Medium's traces.
"The 131K reach means Mistral 7B handles 131K-token inputs."
It is a theoretical bound on how far information can propagate through 32 windowed layers; the model was trained on 8,192-token contexts.
"Large 3 is a scaled-up Mixtral."
Its configuration is DeepSeek-V3's skeleton, latent attention and a shared expert included, with 128 experts instead of 256. Mixtral's design survives in the name "MoE", not in the shape.
