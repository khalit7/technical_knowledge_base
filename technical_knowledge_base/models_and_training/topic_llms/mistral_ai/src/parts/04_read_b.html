Mixtral: Mistral 7B with eight feed-forward networks

Mixtral 8x7B (December 2023) is Mistral 7B's block with one change that mattered and one that is easy to miss ({{Mixtral paper|@mixp}}; {{config.json|@cfgx7}}). The change that mattered: each layer's feed-forward network becomes 8 expert networks of the same shape plus a router that sends every token to the top 2, mixing their outputs by the router's weights (a softmax over the two kept scores). The one easy to miss: it dropped the sliding window for full attention over 32k tokens. Everything else (width 4,096, 32 layers, 32 query and 8 key-value heads) is Mistral 7B's.

That is why "8x7B" is about 47B parameters, not 56B: only the feed-forward networks are copied; attention and embeddings are shared. The sum is 46.7B total and 12.9B active per token, a sparsity ratio (total over active parameters) of 3.6; the generic mechanism is on {{Topic: llms (Deeper: inside an MoE)|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

Try: play Mixtral, then switch to Mistral 7B (dense) to see the block it replaced, and to Small 4 to see where Mistral went next: 128 experts a seventh of the size. All three are drawn at one scale, block area proportional to parameters.

Two Mistral-specific results from the paper still frame how people think about experts. First, Mixtral "outperforms or matches Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 across all evaluated benchmarks" at the per-token compute of a 13B model, under Apache 2.0 ({{paper|@mixp}}), which is what made open sparse models mainstream. Second, its routing analysis found experts do not specialise by topic: expert distributions over ArXiv, PubMed, PhilPapers and the rest of The Pile are nearly identical, and the router shows "structured syntactic behavior", so tokens such as self in Python or "Question" in English "often get routed through the same expert". Consecutive tokens are assigned the same expert far more often than chance in the middle and late layers, which matters for load balancing (a hot pair of experts can over-subscribe one GPU) and can be exploited for caching:

Share of consecutive tokens given the same expert, by layer and dataset, from the {{Mixtral paper|@mixp}}, Table 5. Dashed line: random assignment (1/8 = 12.5% for the first choice; 1 − (6/8)(5/7) ≈ 46% for either of two). Layer 0 is near random; layers 15 and 31 are not.

Mixtral 8x22B (April 2024) scaled the same design to 141B total and 39B active with a 64k context ({{Mistral|@x22}}). Eight large experts are now the classic baseline rather than the frontier: the Chinese open-weight labs moved to hundreds of much smaller experts with always-on shared ones, and Mistral followed.

The current line: granular MoE and big dense models side by side

Mistral now ships both architectures and picks per product, which makes it a useful natural experiment.

Mistral Large 3 · December 2025 · Apache 2.0

A "granular" (fine-grained) mixture-of-experts model: 675B total, 41B active, of which 673B and 39B are the language model plus a 2.5B vision encoder; 256k context; image input; trained from scratch on 3,000 Nvidia H200 GPUs ({{Mistral|@m3}}; {{model card|@l3card}}). The FP8 checkpoint runs on one node of eight B200s or H200s, the NVFP4 one on a node of H100s or A100s. At launch it debuted second among open non-reasoning models on the LMArena leaderboard, by Mistral's report. Its {{params.json|@cfgl3}} is DeepSeek-V3's skeleton: width 7,168, 61 layers, 128 heads with multi-head latent attention (a 512-number latent plus a 64-number position key), first 3 layers dense, one shared expert. The difference is in the experts: 128 of width 4,096 with 4 active, where {{DeepSeek-V3|@dsv3}} has 256 of width 2,048 with 8 active, the same routed parameters per layer cut into half as many, twice as wide.

Mistral Small 4 · 16 March 2026 · Apache 2.0

119B total, 128 experts with 4 active per token plus one shared, latent attention as well (a 256-number latent), 256k context, image input ({{Mistral|@s4}}; {{params.json|@cfgs4}}). It merges three former lines into one checkpoint: instruct (Small), reasoning (Magistral) and agentic coding (Devstral), with a reasoning_effort setting of none or high instead of separate models.

Mistral Medium 3.5 · April 2026 · modified MIT

The other way: a dense 128B model with grouped-query attention (96 query heads, 8 key-value heads), a 256k context, a vision encoder trained from scratch, and the same none or high reasoning setting, published as open weights ({{model card|@m35card}}). Weights and API arrived on 28 to 29 April ({{docs|@m35doc}}); Mistral's announcement followed on 22 May, when it replaced Medium 3.1 and Magistral in Mistral's assistant and Devstral 2 in its coding agent ({{Mistral|@m35post}}). Mistral reports 77.6% on SWE-bench Verified. Its text {{config|@cfgm35}} is identical to Devstral 2's (width 12,288, 88 layers, feed-forward 28,672): same shape, plus the vision encoder.

Devstral 2 · 9 December 2025

Also dense: 123B under the modified MIT licence, with Devstral Small 2 at 24B under Apache 2.0, both 256k context, reported at 72.2% and 68.0% on SWE-bench Verified ({{Mistral|@dev2}}). Both are now retired from Mistral's API (Small 2 on 31 March 2026, Devstral 2 on 31 July), with Medium 3.5 as the replacement ({{catalogue|@models}}); the weights remain on Hugging Face.

Why both? Memory is priced on total parameters and compute on active ones. Small 4 and Medium 3.5 occupy about the same memory (119B against 128B), but a token through Small 4 touches 6B to 8B parameters and a token through Medium 3.5 touches all 128B, 16 to 21 times more work per token. Sparse wins when throughput per GPU is the bill; dense keeps every parameter engaged on every token, avoids the routing and all-to-all machinery, and is simpler to fine-tune and serve, which is the trade Mistral made for its agentic coding flagship. The cache tells the same story: the MoE models use latent attention and the dense one does not, so at 256k tokens one sequence's cache is 5.6 GiB on Small 4, 17.2 GiB on Large 3 and 88 GiB on Medium 3.5 (Weights and cache). The general argument is on {{Topic: llms (Deeper: inside an MoE)|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

Which "active" count?
Mistral states Small 4's active parameters three ways: 6B per token "(8B including embedding and output layers)" in its {{announcement|@s4}}, and 6.5B on the {{model card|@s4card}}. Summing the config: the layers alone are 5.56B, 6.10B with the output layer and 6.53B with the vision encoder too, so 6B and 6.5B both reproduce; no sum reaches 8B (everything included is 7.06B). The same convention (output layer counted, input embedding not, since looking up a row is not a multiplication) reproduces Large 3's 39B for the language model exactly (39.0B); adding the vision encoder gives 41.6B against the stated 41B. The Weights and cache tab sums every model every way.

Lineage: specialists, then consolidation

Every release with its date, licence and source is on the Lineage tab; this is the arc.

  • Mistral 7B (September 2023): sliding window plus GQA, Apache 2.0; beat Llama 2 13B.
  • Mixtral 8x7B (December 2023) and 8x22B (April 2024): open sparse MoE at 13B and 39B active, Apache 2.0.
  • The specialist years, 2024 to 2025. A flagship that started API-only (Mistral Large 1, February 2024; Large 2 in July 2024 released its weights under the research-only Mistral Research License, with a paid licence for commercial self-deployment, {{Mistral|@large2}}) and price tiers (Small 3 and 3.1 back on Apache 2.0, Medium 3 on the API), plus a model per job: Codestral for code, trained for fill-in-the-middle so it completes inside a file given both the prefix and the suffix, the shape editor autocomplete needs; Ministral edge models; Pixtral for vision (from September 2024), an image encoder feeding the language decoder so images and text interleave in one context; Voxtral for speech understanding (July 2025); Magistral (June 2025), Mistral's first reasoning models; and Devstral (May 2025) for agentic coding, tuned for the loop of reading a repository, editing files and running tests inside a scaffold rather than emitting one code block.
  • Mistral 3 (2 December 2025): Large 3 plus Ministral 3 at 3B, 8B and 14B, each in base, instruct and reasoning variants, all multimodal and Apache 2.0; Mistral reports 85% on AIME 2025 for the 14B reasoning variant ({{Mistral|@m3}}). Devstral 2 and Devstral Small 2 followed a week later with the Vibe command-line coding agent.
  • Consolidation, 2026. The specialists folded back into generalists: Small 4 (March) absorbed Magistral and Devstral behind a reasoning setting, and Medium 3.5 (April, announced in May) replaced Magistral and Devstral 2 in Mistral's own products. Vision is now built into Large 3, Small 4 and Medium 3.5, and every Pixtral, Magistral and Devstral version is in the deprecated table of {{Mistral's catalogue|@models}}. Specialists remain where the job is different in kind: speech (Voxtral TTS, March, open weights under a non-commercial licence; the Voxtral transcription models), document OCR (OCR 4 in June, replaced by OCR 4.1 in July), formal proofs (Leanstral), safety (Shieldstral, August) and robotics (Robostral Navigate, July).
  • The next flagship. In July 2026 Mistral opened partner early access to a new open-weight model that Tech Times reports is expected to be considerably larger than Large 3, and which Mensch called a "fat but sparse" family, which points to a mixture of experts ({{Tech Times|@tt}}). By the end of September 2026 no name, size or licence had been published (nothing in Mistral's {{news index|@news}} through 28 September names one).
Shieldstral 1.0 · 4 August 2026 · Apache 2.0

A guard model (text and image input, 32k context, public preview) that takes a safety policy written in natural language as a question and answers yes or no, covering prompt moderation, response moderation, prompt-response pairs and refusal detection ({{model card|@shield}}). Because the policy is an input rather than a fixed list of harm categories trained into the weights, a policy change needs no retraining, and because it is a separate model, the policy lives outside the generator. Mistral says it "matches models up to 7x its size on text safety" and sets a new state of the art on multimodal moderation ({{Mistral|@shieldn}}); it does not win every text benchmark (85.0 on BeaverTails against 85.9 for Qwen3Guard-8B, {{Hugging Face|@shieldhf}}). Size: 3.8B on the docs card, "3B" in the post and the repository name (built on Ministral 3 3B).

Robostral Navigate · 8 July 2026 · licence not named

An 8B model that takes images from a single RGB camera plus a plain-language instruction ("leave the lobby, walk through the corridor, enter the supply room and stop to face the second shelf") and outputs navigation actions: a target point in the image with an orientation, or a displacement in the robot's frame. Trained entirely in simulation on about 2.4 million trajectories across 350,000 scenes, then refined with online reinforcement learning; Mistral reports 76.6% success on unseen environments of the R2R-CE vision-and-language navigation benchmark ({{Mistral|@robo}}). The announcement names no licence and links no weights.

Leanstral · March 2026; 1.5 · July 2026 · Apache 2.0

Open-weight Lean 4 proof generation, built on the Small 4 family: 119B total and 6B active by the blog (6.5B on the card) ({{Leanstral|@lean}}; {{Leanstral 1.5|@lean15}}; {{Hugging Face|@leanhf}}). Leanstral 1.5 solves 587 of 672 PutnamBench problems by Mistral's report. Both versions are now retired from the API (30 June and 30 September 2026); the weights remain.
