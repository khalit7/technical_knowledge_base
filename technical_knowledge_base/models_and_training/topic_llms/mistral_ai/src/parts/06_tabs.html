Weights and cache: every model rebuilt from its config

Every number here is summed from the model's published configuration (layer count, widths, heads, experts) rather than copied from a headline, so you can see which headline counts what, and what each model takes to hold and to run.

1. Where the headline parameter count comes from

Try: Small 4 is selected. Untick the input embedding: the active sum lands on the model card's 6.5B; untick the vision encoder as well and it lands on the blog's 6B. Nothing reaches the blog's 8B. Then pick Devstral 2 and Medium 3.5: the same text model, counted two ways.

Sums: attention projections, norms, routers and feed-forward or expert weights per layer from the config, plus the embedding tables; the vision projector's shape (a 2 × 2 patch merger and a two-layer adapter) is assumed, so vision totals carry a few hundredths of a billion of uncertainty. Verdict: ✓ inside the stated figure's rounding, ≈ within 1%, ✗ beyond. These reproduce Mistral's figures independently, not by construction; where none does (Small 4's 8B), the table says so.

2. What it takes to hold and run

Try: set FP8, 256K and 8 sequences: Large 3 still fits one 8 × H200 node, while Medium 3.5's cache alone is larger than its weights.

Grey: weights at the chosen precision (Mistral's stated totals; Ministral 3 at the computed totals). Colour: the KV cache in 16-bit for all sequences, from each config (Mistral 7B capped at its 4,096 window; contexts capped at each model's maximum). Activations and framework overhead are left out, so real deployments need headroom. GPU sizes: {{H100 80 GB|@h100}}, {{H200 141 GB|@h200}}. Defaults reproduce, independently, the model card's statement that FP8 Large 3 runs on one node of eight H200s.

3. Cache per sequence against context

Bytes per token from each config: GQA models store 2 × layers × key-value heads × 128 numbers; latent-attention models store (latent + 64) × layers. Large 3 and Small 4 cache 576 and 320 numbers per layer per token (68.6 KiB and 22.5 KiB per token over all layers), against 2,048 per layer for Medium 3.5 (2 × 8 heads × 128) over 88 layers, 352 KiB per token. Mistral 7B flattens at its window; the tick marks its 8,192-token training context. Defaults reproduce the worked example (128 KiB per token, 512 MiB at 32k with the window, 4 GiB without) independently. Medium 3.5 and Devstral 2 share one line, as they share one shape.
