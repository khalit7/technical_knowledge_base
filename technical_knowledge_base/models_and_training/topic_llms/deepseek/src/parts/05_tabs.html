Cache across generations

The KV cache one sequence needs, against context length, for each of DeepSeek's attention designs and the two standard baselines, all computed from the per-token figures on the Reading tab. This is the single number every DeepSeek attention generation attacks.

Try: read the pinned line first: DeepSeek's first generation reproduces the model card's "437-fold" reduction. Then move the context to 1M: at V3's dimensions MHA would need about 3.6 TiB for one sequence and GQA-8 233 GiB; MLA brings it to 65 GiB, V4-Pro's compressed attention to 4.6 GiB, and V4.1-Flash under 1 GiB. Change the budget to see how many sequences fit. Hover or tap a line's label for its arithmetic.

Per-token sizes: MHA, GQA-8 and MLA at V3's dimensions (128 heads of 128, dc = 512, dRh = 64, 61 layers, BF16), from the V3 report; V3.2's DSA stores the same MLA cache. V4-Pro is the estimate reproduced on the Reading tab (30 CSA and 31 HCA layers, FP4 indexer keys) plus its fixed sliding windows (128 × 576 bytes × 61 layers), from the V4 report's configuration. V4-Flash is an estimate on the same method with an extra assumption, that its 41 alternating layers split 21 CSA and 20 HCA (3,450 bytes per token plus 43 layers of windows); against it, V4.1-Flash's reported 890 bytes per token (model card) is 26%, consistent with "roughly a quarter". DeepSeek LLM 67B, the first generation, is plain grouped-query attention with 8 key/value heads of dimension 128 over 95 layers in BF16 (config.json). DeepSeek's own chart of these generations (V4.1-Flash announcement) gives V4-Flash 3,514 bytes per token, 1.9% above the 3,450 estimated here, and V3.2 48,068 bytes, smaller than the BF16 MLA line here because V3.2 decodes from an FP8 cache (FlashMLA's FP8 sparse decoding kernel). 1M means 1,000,000 tokens; GiB is 230 bytes.

Lineage

Dates and what changed at each release; the mechanisms are explained on the Reading tab. Dot size follows total parameters where published.

Try: click a dot to jump to its release.

DeepSeek LLM and Coder

2023 (DeepSeek LLM 67B: 29 November 2023, GitHub)
  • A High-Flyer (quant fund) spinoff's first models: early Llama-style dense models, notable for the scaling-law and data work that set up what followed.

V2

May 2024 (6 May, GitHub) · V2 paper
  • Introduced MLA and DeepSeekMoE (fine-grained plus shared experts).
  • Against DeepSeek 67B: 42.5% lower training cost, a 93.3% smaller KV cache and 5.76 times the generation throughput.
  • MLA's serving cost is what let DeepSeek start the Chinese API price war.

V3

December 2024 (26 December, Hugging Face) · V3 report
  • MLA plus a 256-expert MoE (8 routed active, 1 shared), aux-loss-free bias-based balancing, multi-token prediction, FP8 mixed-precision training, DualPipe.
  • The official training run took 2.788M H800 GPU hours, which DeepSeek priced at $5.576M assuming $2 per GPU hour on a 2,048-GPU cluster, excluding prior research and ablations. The figure shook the industry, and the report is unusually explicit about the infrastructure behind it.

R1

January 2025 (20 January, DeepSeek) · R1 paper
  • GRPO-based RL on the V3 base produced o1-class reasoning; R1-Zero showed the behaviour emerging from pure RL.
  • MIT licence, plus distillations from 1.5B to 70B.
  • Triggered the "DeepSeek moment" and Nvidia's record one-day selloff.

V3.1

August 2025 (21 August, DeepSeek)
  • Chat and reasoning merged into one hybrid-thinking checkpoint.

V3.2-Exp

September 2025 (29 September, DeepSeek) · V3.2 paper
  • DSA, the lightning-indexer sparse attention, continued from V3.1-Terminus.
  • API prices cut by more than half at launch.

V4: V4 Pro and V4 Flash

April 2026 (24 April, The Next Web) · V4 report
  • The current family: two MoE models, V4 Pro (1.6T total / 49B active) and V4 Flash (284B / 13B).
  • Hybrid compressed attention (CSA over groups of 4 with top-k selection, HCA over groups of 128) with low-rank queries and grouped output projections holds the KV cache near 2% of BF16 8-group GQA's at 1M context; mHC residuals; Muon; FP4 expert weights.
  • V4-Pro at maximum reasoning effort reports 80.6% resolved on SWE-bench Verified with DeepSeek's in-house harness (a bash tool and a file-edit tool, up to 500 steps and 512K tokens of context), against 80.2 for Kimi K2.6 Thinking in the same table of the V4 report.
  • V4-Flash at maximum effort reports 73.2 on BrowseComp against V4-Pro's 83.4, with a quarter of the active parameters.

V4 Flash vision

August 2026 (21 August) · API docs
  • deepseek-v4-flash-vision-exp, an experimental vision variant of V4 Flash and, until V4.1-Flash, the only DeepSeek model accepting image input.
  • Images are normalised to at most 384 tokens each, up to 600 images per request.
  • Since 14 September its name routes to V4.1-Flash (API changelog).

V4.1-Flash

September 2026 (10 September, API changelog) · model card
  • 552B total, 8B active during prefill and 16B during decode; MIT licence; weights on Hugging Face in 48 safetensors shards.
  • A causal encoder-decoder, 40 layers split 20 and 20: the first frontier-scale open model to abandon the decoder-only consensus. 1M-token context, native vision, and a reasoning-effort dial from 1 to 100.
  • Reported Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 90.6, Codeforces 3471 and GPQA Diamond 90.9, with DeepSeek conceding it holds up better on shorter agent loops than on the longest-horizon evaluations: the generation bought serving cost rather than capability headroom.
  • Pricing $0.30 input and $1.20 output per million tokens at peak and half that off-peak, cache hits from $0.006 (pricing).
  • The API id is deepseek-flash, and since 14 September at noon Beijing time the deepseek-v4-flash names route here, so a call naming V4 Flash is answered by V4.1-Flash. V4 Pro is not rerouted: it keeps its own service and pricing ($1.32 input and $3.96 output per million at peak, cache hits $0.044).

The DeepSeek template across the field

DeepSeek's layout (MLA attention, many narrow routed experts plus a shared one, a few dense layers first) became a template other labs reuse. Pick two models: the card highlights every configuration value that differs, and the rows beneath compute what those values buy. MLA's cache depends only on the latent, the RoPE key and the layer count, never on the number of heads, so models that change the heads change their attention compute and not their cache.

Try: the default pair is V3 against Kimi K2: half the heads and 384 experts, yet exactly the same 70,272 bytes of cache per token, and half the attention FLOPs. Then pick Mistral Large 3 (same cache again) and GLM-4.5, which uses grouped-query attention instead.
