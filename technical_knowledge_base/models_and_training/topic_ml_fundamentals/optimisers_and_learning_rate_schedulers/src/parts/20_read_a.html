The idea in one screen

An optimiser turns each gradient into a step in two moves: it smooths the gradient over time (momentum) and it reshapes it (per coordinate in Adam, per matrix in Shampoo and Muon, to a sign in Lion); the schedule then multiplies the step by a base rate ηt that warms up, holds and decays. The two are always chosen as a pair, because adaptive methods only rescale relative to η.

Every optimiser on this page is one path through the same three boxes

Read left to right (top to bottom on a phone): the gradient gt is smoothed, reshaped, scaled by the schedule and applied with decoupled weight decay. The number under each box is the state it keeps per parameter, which is what an optimiser costs in memory (section 3). The schedule's shape is illustrative.

Momentum
a running sum or average of past gradients; steps along directions where gradients agree grow.
Preconditioner
whatever divides or transforms the gradient before the step: Adam's √v per coordinate, Shampoo's matrix roots.
Bias correction
Adam's division by 1 − βt, which undoes averages that started at zero.
Decoupled decay
AdamW's θ ← θ − ηλθ, applied outside the adaptive division.
Orthogonalise
replace a matrix by the nearest one whose singular values are all 1 (Muon, by Newton-Schulz iterations).
Warmup, cooldown
a ramp of η from near 0 at the start; a decay towards 0 at the end.
WSD
warmup, stable, decay: a constant rate with a short cooldown, so the run's length can be chosen late.
Critical batch
the batch size beyond which more examples per step stop saving steps (section 6).
The takeaway, as of October 2026
AdamW with warmup and a decaying schedule (cosine, or WSD when the run's length may change) is still the safe default. Muon on hidden matrices, with AdamW for everything else, is the challenger with frontier-scale evidence: it trained the 1T-parameter Kimi K2 and the GLM-4.5 family (section 4). But every independent re-tuning finds the challengers' speed-ups smaller than their papers report, and shrinking with model size: about 1.1× at 1.2B parameters against a well-tuned AdamW ({{Wen et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02046}}; section 5).

1 · Momentum: smooth the gradient over steps

The problem every method below answers is conditioning. When the loss curves 100 times more sharply across a valley than along it (condition number 100), the largest stable step for the steep direction is tiny for the shallow one, and gradient descent crawls along the floor while zig-zagging across it. Momentum adds up the gradients of successive steps: the components along the floor agree and grow, the ones across it flip sign and cancel.

vt = β vt−1 + gt,   θt = θt−1 − η vt
gt
the gradient of the loss at θt−1 on the current mini-batch
vt
the velocity: past gradients, each older one weighted by a further factor β (usually 0.9)
η
the learning rate
  • Heavy ball ({{Polyak 1964|https://doi.org/10.1016/0041-5553(64)90137-5}}). Along a consistent direction the step grows up to 1/(1 − β) = 10 times at β = 0.9. On a quadratic with condition number R, tuned momentum needs about √R times fewer iterations than steepest descent ({{Sutskever et al. 2013, section 2|http://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/sutskever13.pdf}}). Momentum 0.9 is not the optimum for this valley; the √R speed-up needs momentum tuned to the curvature, which nobody knows for a network, so 0.9 is the habit.
  • Nesterov (NAG) ({{Nesterov 1983|http://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/sutskever13.pdf}}, in Sutskever et al.'s form): take the gradient at the look-ahead point θ − ηβvt−1, where momentum is about to carry you, so the step is corrected before it is taken. PyTorch implements the equivalent rewrite θ ← θ − η(gt + βvt), which needs no second gradient evaluation.
  • Two conventions, one trap. The formula above is PyTorch's ({{torch.optim.SGD|https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.optim.SGD.html}}, dampening 0): a sum. Many papers write vt = βvt−1 + (1 − β)gt, an average; it traces the same path only with a learning rate 1/(1 − β) = 10 times larger, so a rate never transfers between the two without that factor.
The landscape vocabulary: optima, saddles, simultaneous updates, batch, stochastic and mini-batch (basics)
  • Local optimum: the gradient is zero and moving in any direction increases the loss.
  • Saddle point: the gradient is zero, but the loss decreases along some directions and increases along others (a horse saddle); the second derivative says it is neither a maximum nor a minimum. Saddles are frequent in high dimensions but are not a big problem in practice: the descending direction is there to be found, and momentum, mini-batch noise and even floating-point rounding push the optimiser off. In the race's {{Saddle|#t-race}}, starting 0.001 off the ridge, plain SGD needs 15 steps to leave, while Adam, AdaGrad and RMSProp leave on the first step, because they divide the tiny gradient by its own size.
  • All parameters update simultaneously. J(θ0, θ1) is always evaluated at the previous parameters: compute every gradient first, then update every weight. Updating θ0 and then using the new θ0 to compute θ1's gradient is a different (coordinate-descent) algorithm.
