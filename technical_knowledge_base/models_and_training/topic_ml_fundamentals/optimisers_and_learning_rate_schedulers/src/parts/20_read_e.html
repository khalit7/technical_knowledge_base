"Annealing" and "learning-rate decay": three meanings (vocabulary)
SenseWhat it meansWhere you meet it
1. Classical, optimisationLowering the learning rate, full stop. Borrowed from simulated annealing, where a temperature is reduced so the search settles. Here annealing and decay are exact synonyms."cosine annealing", CosineAnnealingLR, eta_min; anything before 2023
2. Modern LLM pretrainingA training phase (the final 10 to 30% of tokens or so) defined by two simultaneous changes: the rate decays towards zero and the data mixture switches to curated high-quality tokens (mathematics, code, instruction-like, reasoning).Llama 3's annealing, OLMo 2's Dolmino mid-training mix, MiniCPM's WSD decay phase; "the annealed model"
3. Annealing as an experimentA cheap evaluation protocol: take a fixed mid-run checkpoint, anneal it on a candidate dataset, compare scores. Used to price a dataset, not to train a final model.Llama 3 and OLMo both evaluate candidate corpora this way
  • The two levers are separable, and papers usually pull both at once. WSD lets you decay the rate with no data change; you can also switch to a high-quality mixture while the rate stays flat (WSM switches the data at its Tswitch with the rate held constant). Any claim that "annealing gave +X" is a compound of two effects unless the ablation separated them; {{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}} separates them on OLMo 2's numbers and on a toy.
  • They work for different reasons that reinforce each other. A low rate means the model descends into a basin and stops moving much; low gradient noise near the end is also when scarce high-quality tokens stick best rather than being diluted across a 10T-token stream. That is why the data switch is scheduled to coincide with the decay, not because one requires the other.
  • Reading papers: "decay phase" or "cooldown" means the schedule alone; "anneal" or "annealed checkpoint" almost always implies the data switch too. WSM removes sense 1 entirely (constant rate forever) while keeping sense 2 (the curated-data switch), and recovers the decay's benefit by merging.
  • Unrelated homonyms: simulated annealing proper (a combinatorial optimisation algorithm) and sampling temperature at inference ({{Sampling and Decoding|n:3c65c17b0d0d8115bafcda29a2c7086e}}) share the metaphor and nothing else.
Common mistakes, in one list (each is taught above; here as a checklist)
  • Copying a learning rate between momentum conventions, or between optimisers. The averaged momentum form needs a rate 10 times larger at β = 0.9; Adam's step is about η per parameter whatever the gradient's size, SGD's is η times the gradient, so their rates are unrelated; Lion wants a rate 3 to 10 times smaller than AdamW's, schedule-free SGD 10 to 50 times larger than SGD's.
  • Using L2 regularisation with Adam and calling it weight decay (torch.optim.Adam(weight_decay=...)); they are the same only for plain SGD.
  • Removing warmup because Adam already corrects its bias: correction fixes the bias, not the variance.
  • Reading an intermediate cosine checkpoint as "the model at that size of run" (Kaplan's mistake, corrected by Chinchilla).
  • Evaluating a schedule-free model at the training weights y instead of the average x: call optimizer.eval() first.
  • Applying Muon to every parameter: embeddings, the output head, norms and biases go to AdamW.
  • Believing a new optimiser's speed-up before the baseline is tuned and fully decayed.
  • Crediting "annealing" for a gain that came from the data switch (or the reverse).
  • Assuming a diagonal adaptive method handles bad conditioning: the rotated ravine.
  • Scaling the batch without the rate and betas: linear for SGD, square root and β′ = 1 − κ(1 − β) for Adam.
