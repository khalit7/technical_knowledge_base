4 · Preconditioning beyond the diagonal

  • Newton's method uses the Hessian H, the matrix of second derivatives: θ ← θ − H−1∇J. It undoes the curvature in every direction at once and converges in far fewer steps, but H has n² entries (O(n²) memory) and inverting it costs O(n³): for a network with 109 weights, impossible. Quasi-Newton methods (L-BFGS) build a low-rank approximation from recent gradient differences and Hessian-free methods use Hessian-vector products; both work for small, full-batch problems and break down with mini-batch noise. The practical descendants are the structured preconditioners below.
  • Full-matrix AdaGrad ({{Duchi et al. 2011|https://jmlr.org/papers/v12/duchi11a.html}}) divides by the square root of the whole matrix Σ g g⊤ instead of its diagonal: rotation-invariant, but n² again.
  • Shampoo ({{Gupta, Koren and Singer 2018|https://arxiv.org/html/1802.09568}}) exploits the fact that a weight is a matrix W (m × n). It keeps one m × m factor for the rows and one n × n factor for the columns, L ← L + G G⊤ and R ← R + G⊤G, and steps W ← W − η L−1/4 G R−1/4: a Kronecker-factored approximation of full-matrix AdaGrad at m² + n² memory instead of (mn)². For a tensor of order k each factor gets the exponent −1/(2k); for a vector (k = 1) that is −1/2, which is full-matrix AdaGrad (derived from the paper's section 4.2). The matrix roots are expensive, so practical versions recompute them only every few steps.
  • SOAP ({{Vyas et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11321}}) starts from the observation that Shampoo (with the 1/2 power) is Adafactor run in the eigenbasis of Shampoo's preconditioner, and replaces Adafactor with Adam: rotate the gradient into the eigenbases of L and R, run Adam there, rotate back. Its one hyperparameter beyond Adam's is how often the eigenbasis is refreshed. Reported: on 360M and 660M language models with 2M-token batches, at least 40% fewer iterations and at least 35% less wall-clock time than AdamW, and about 20% better than Shampoo on both.
  • Adafactor ({{Shazeer and Stern 2018|https://arxiv.org/html/1804.04235}}) goes the other way, towards less memory: it keeps only the row and column sums of the squared gradients of each matrix and rebuilds a rank-1 estimate of v from them, "from O(nm) to O(n+m)". It was the optimiser of {{T5|https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683}}.

The rotated ravine in the {{Optimiser race|#t-race}} shows the point in two dimensions: Shampoo reaches 10−3 in 24 steps on both the axis-aligned and the rotated valley, where AdaGrad and RMSProp only manage it on the first.

What the optimiser state costs

Optimiser state is often the largest item in training memory. In mixed precision with Adam, each parameter needs 2 bytes for the bf16 weight, 2 for its gradient, 4 for an fp32 master copy and 4 each for m and v: 16 bytes ({{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}}; 2 + 2 + 4 + 4 × 2 = 16, reproduced by construction). Optimiser states "use 33–75% of the total memory footprint during training" ({{Dettmers et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/html/2110.02861#S1}}). The table recounts each optimiser's state for four real models from their config.json files.

Try: switch models. Muon's state is close to half of AdamW's, a little more on small models whose embedding is a bigger share (89% of OLMo 2 7B sits in hidden matrices, 99.8% of Kimi K2). SOAP's state on Kimi K2 is about nine times its parameter count: each of its 69,300 expert matrices (7,168 × 2,048) carries a 7,168 × 7,168 factor and a 2,048 × 2,048 one, each with its eigenbasis.

State values per parameter are counted per matrix: Adam 2mn, Muon mn on hidden matrices and 2mn on the embedding, output head and every 1-D parameter (norms, router biases), Adafactor m + n, SOAP 2mn plus, for each side d ≤ 10,000, a d × d factor and its d × d eigenbasis (max_precond_dim = 10000 and precondition_1d = False are soap.py's defaults; larger sides, such as a vocabulary, get no factor). Bytes: 4 per state value, 1 for 8-bit Adam. Training bytes per parameter = 2 (bf16 weight) + 2 (bf16 gradient) + 4 (fp32 master) + state bytes, before activations and before any ZeRO sharding. Parameter recounts: SmolLM3 3.08B, OLMo 2 7.30B, Llama 3.1 405.85B, Kimi K2 1,026.41B (within 3,416 parameters of Hugging Face's count; the K2 report's 1.04T is discussed on {{Moonshot AI: Kimi|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f5abf5dbdbac821a12}}). Shampoo is not listed because its memory depends on the blocking an implementation chooses.

5 · Orthogonal and sign updates: Muon and Lion

Muon

{{Muon|https://kellerjordan.github.io/posts/muon/}} (Keller Jordan, 2024; "MomentUm Orthogonalized by Newton-Schulz") treats each hidden weight matrix as a matrix. For one m × n weight with gradient G:

  1. Update a momentum buffer B ← μB + (1 − μ)G with μ = 0.95, and form the Nesterov-style update U = (1 − μ)G + μB (the defaults of the {{reference code|https://github.com/KellerJordan/Muon}} and of torch.optim.Muon).
  2. Normalise U to unit Frobenius norm, then apply five iterations of X ← aX + (bXX⊤ + c(XX⊤)²)X with (a, b, c) = (3.4445, −4.7750, 2.0315). This pushes every singular value of U towards 1 without computing an SVD: the result is approximately UV⊤ from U's singular value decomposition, the nearest semi-orthogonal matrix. The coefficients were tuned to push small singular values up as fast as possible, so the output's singular values land roughly between 0.5 and 1.5 rather than exactly at 1, which the authors found does not hurt.
  3. Step W ← W − η·s·X, where s rescales for the matrix's shape: √max(1, m/n) in Jordan's version; Moonshot's 0.2·√max(m, n), which matches the update's size to AdamW's typical RMS of 0.2 to 0.4 so AdamW's learning rate and decay can be reused ({{Liu et al. 2025, section 2.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16982#S2.SS2}}). PyTorch offers both as adjust_lr_fn options.
  • Why: a gradient matrix is usually dominated by a few singular directions; orthogonalising gives every direction the gradient spans a comparable step, so rare directions are not drowned out. {{Bernstein|https://jeremybernste.in/writing/deriving-muon}} derives it as steepest descent under the spectral norm. The parent page draws the singular values flattening over the five iterations on one real gradient.
  • Which parameters: only 2-D hidden matrices. Embeddings, the output head, norms and biases stay on AdamW; Jordan found using AdamW for the embedding and head important even though they are 2-D. GLM-4.5 runs Muon on "all parameters except word embedding, bias, and weights for RMSNorm" with 5 Newton-Schulz steps, momentum 0.95 and update RMS 0.2 ({{GLM-4.5, section 2.4|https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06471#S2.SS4}}).
  • Cost: one state buffer instead of AdamW's two (the memory table above), and a FLOP overhead of at most T·m/B for T iterations, model width m and batch B tokens: 5 × 768 / 524,288 = 0.7% for the speedrun and 5 × 16,384 / 16M = 0.5% for a Llama 405B-sized run (Jordan's arithmetic, reproduced). The real catch is that orthogonalisation needs the whole matrix while large runs shard it, which is why Moonshot published a distributed version built on ZeRO-1 ({{Liu et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16982#S1}}).
  • History: it set a NanoGPT speedrun record on 15 October 2024 (training 35% faster) and the CIFAR-10 record (94% in 2.6 A100-seconds, from 3.3); the speedrun record by record is on {{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}}. Moonshot added weight decay and the shape scaling, reporting "~2x computational efficiency compared to AdamW with compute optimal training" (52% of the FLOPs) with the 16B Moonlight model; then MuonClip (Muon plus QK-Clip, which rescales a head's query and key projections whenever its largest attention logit exceeds τ = 100) trained the 1T-parameter Kimi K2 on 15.5T tokens "with zero loss spike" ({{Kimi K2 report|https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20534}}; mechanism on {{Moonshot AI: Kimi|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f5abf5dbdbac821a12}}).
  • Availability: torch.optim.Muon ({{docs|https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.optim.Muon.html}}; first in the PyTorch 2.9 documentation, inferred from the versioned docs) and optax.contrib.muon in JAX ({{docs|https://optax.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/contrib.html}}). Note that PyTorch runs the Newton-Schulz iterations in bfloat16 and defaults to weight decay 0.1.

Lion

{{Lion|https://arxiv.org/html/2302.06675}} ("EvoLved Sign Momentum", Chen et al. 2023) was found by a program search over update rules. Its step is the sign of an interpolation between the momentum and the current gradient, and the momentum is updated afterwards with a different rate:

θ ← θ − η·(sign(β1m + (1−β1)g) + λθ),   m ← β2m + (1−β2)g
β1, β2
0.9 and 0.99 by default
  • One buffer of state, and every parameter moves by exactly ±η per step, so the update's size is larger than Adam's: the paper recommends a learning rate 3 to 10 times smaller than AdamW's and a weight decay λ 3 to 10 times larger, to keep ηλ similar.
  • Reported gains: up to 2% ImageNet accuracy for ViT, up to 5× less pretraining compute on JFT, 2.3× less for diffusion; "similar or better" than Adam on language modelling. Its own limitations section: similar to AdamW on the large-scale language model and on C4 masked language modelling, gains shrink with strong augmentation, and "likely" no better than AdamW below batch size 64.
  • In the race, Lion's fixed-size steps zig-zag across the ravine and stall at a distance set by η unless the schedule decays: a constant-step method needs its schedule more than Adam does.

What to conclude
  • Most of a published speed-up can be the baseline. Wen et al. find that tuning a single hyperparameter (the learning rate) in the GPT-3 recipe gives up to 2× on a 100M model: the size of many published gains. Kaddour et al. find that a baseline whose learning rate is fully decayed erases the gains of Lion and Sophia.
  • Matrix-aware methods (Muon, SOAP, Shampoo) are the ones that survive tuning, at about 1.3× for small models and shrinking with scale in Wen et al.'s runs, and with a measured end-to-end win for Shampoo in AlgoPerf.
  • Muon's scale evidence is real but not controlled: Kimi K2 and GLM-4.5 trained with it, and Moonlight's scaling study is Moonshot's own, and neither the K2 nor the GLM-4.5 report includes a matched AdamW run at full scale. The two studies disagree mainly on batch size: Semenov et al. tune most at 0.1M and 0.02M-token batches, Wen et al. at 0.4M or more (as Wen et al. note).
