4 · Backward: where gradients vanish or explode

Backpropagation applies the chain rule from the loss back to every weight, so a weight in layer 1 of 16 receives a product of 15 layer factors: below 1 the product vanishes, above 1 it explodes, the same arithmetic in a deep stack and in a recurrent network unrolled through time (thread 3). The bottom panel of the step 2 animation is this step: in the sigmoid network the first layer's gradient is 9.5 × 10−11 against 0.18 for the last, about 2 billion times smaller derived (each sigmoid layer multiplies by its slope, at most 0.25); in the pre-norm SwiGLU network the ratio is 1.45 to 1.17, because the residual path carries the gradient down untouched.

5 · Update: optimisers and schedules

The update turns a gradient into a weight change through three choices made together: smoothing over steps (momentum), a step size per parameter (adaptive methods), and the base rate's path over the run (the schedule). Adaptive methods only rescale relative to the base rate, so an optimiser and a schedule are always chosen as a pair. The families, by what they change:

FamilyWhat it changesWhere it is the defaultOwner's section
SGD + momentum (Nesterov)direction smoothed over steps; one step size for allvision, ResNet-style training1
Adam, AdamWper-parameter steps from a running RMS of the gradient, bias-corrected; AdamW decouples the weight decayessentially every LLM2
Shampoo, SOAPfull-matrix (Kronecker-factored) preconditioningAlgoPerf winners; little LLM use3
Muon, Lionthe update's shape: Muon orthogonalises each hidden matrix's momentum (Newton-Schulz), Lion takes its signMuon pretrains Kimi K2 (1T parameters, as MuonClip)4
Schedule-free, WSMremove the decay curve: iterate averaging, or merging checkpointsruns whose length is not known8

Schedules differ on one question: does the schedule need the run's length in advance? Warmup then cosine to about 10% of the peak needs it, so a cosine run stopped early sits at an untuned rate; WSD (warmup, stable, decay) holds the rate flat and decays over the last 10 to 20%, so the end can be chosen late. At a fixed, known budget the shapes nearly tie. Warmup exists because Adam's second-moment estimate rests on a handful of gradients in the first steps.

6 · Regularise: fit the pattern, not the batch

Regularisation trades a little training fit for better performance on unseen data. Each kind acts at a different moment of the step:

KindActs atHow
Penaltythe loss (step 3) or the update (step 5)L1 (lasso) zeroes some weights, L2 (ridge) shrinks all, elastic net adds both
Dropoutthe forward pass (step 2)drop each unit with probability p in training, so no unit relies on one partner; survivors are rescaled in training, so inference drops nothing
Augmentationthe data (step 1)random but realistic transforms enlarge the training set
Early stoppingthe measurement (step 7)stop when validation loss turns up; keep the best checkpoint

LLM pretraining uses almost none of this: it sees most data about once, so there is little to overfit. What remains is weight decay, a regulariser by intent and an optimiser property by implementation (thread 4): in the step strip the classic recipe folds 10−4·w into the gradient, while the 2026 recipe shrinks every weight by ηλ = 3 × 10−4 of itself, separately.

7 · Measure: on data the step did not see

The toy's single step shows why measuring needs held-out data: under the 2026 recipe, the loss on the batch it trained on fell from 2.60 to 0.22 and accuracy on that batch went from 9.4% to 100%, while on the 256 held-out examples the loss moved from 2.77 to 2.71 and accuracy from 11.3% to 11.7%. One step memorised its batch and learned almost nothing general.

What to measure falls into three families. Classification numbers come from the confusion matrix at a threshold (precision, recall, F1) or across all thresholds (ROC-AUC, precision-recall); when positives are rare, ROC barely moves while precision-recall collapses, because the false positive rate divides by the whole negative class. Text scores compare an output with a reference (BLEU on clipped n-gram precision, ROUGE on recall, METEOR with stems and synonyms, BERTScore on embeddings), and perplexity, exp(mean negative log-likelihood per token), is the loss itself read as a measurement, comparable only under the same tokenizer. Information quantities (entropy, cross entropy, KL) are thread 1.
