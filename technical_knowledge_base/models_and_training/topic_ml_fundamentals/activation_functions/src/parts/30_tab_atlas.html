Activation atlas

Every activation on this page and its derivative, on the same axes. The derivative is what backpropagation multiplies by: where it is near 0 the gradient dies (saturation), where it is exactly 0 the unit can die (dead region).

Try: tick the functions to compare (GELU and SiLU together, to see that they are not the same curve; GELU's three forms, to see that they nearly are). Pick one to shade: its saturated region (|f′| below the threshold) and its dead region (f′ exactly 0) are marked on both panels. Hover or tap the plot to read every ticked function at that x.

f(x) the activation
f′(x) its slope: what the gradient is multiplied by

Exact formulas as PyTorch defines them (F.gelu with an exact error function, leaky slope 0.01, PReLU at its initial 0.25, ELU and SELU with their default constants, softplus with β = 1). The page's own JavaScript was compared with torch.nn.functional and autograd in float64 on 2,401 points from −12 to 12: the largest relative difference is below 10−12 for every function and derivative (src/recompute.py, 120 checks, 0 failures).

On a standard normal input

What one layer of each function does to an input drawn from N(0, 1), the situation initialisation tries to create. The variance-preserving gain is 1/√E[f(z)2]: the factor by which to scale weights of variance 1/n so that the next layer's pre-activations keep variance 1. E[f′(z)2] is the matching factor for the gradient on the way back. PyTorch's calculate_gain is shown beside it.

Computed in your browser by Simpson's rule on [−12, 0] and [0, 12], and checked against SciPy's adaptive quadrature to 10−9. Reproduces independently: ReLU's gain √2 (He et al.'s factor 2) and leaky ReLU's √(2/(1 + α2)), exactly PyTorch's formulas; SELU's mean 0 and second moment 1 (Klambauer et al.'s fixed point). Does not reproduce, by design: PyTorch's tanh gain is 5/3, a heuristic, against 1.59 here; its sigmoid gain is 1 against 1.85; its SELU gain is 3/4, which its documentation says "sacrifices the normalization effect for more stable gradient flow in rectangular layers" and tells SELU users to pass nonlinearity='linear' (gain 1) instead (torch.nn.init). A gain only fixes the second moment at initialisation; the parent's thread 2 shows what happens over 16 layers, and {{Normalisation and initialisation|n:3c65c17b0d0d81369e1cc38ed4e11d48}} owns the schemes.
