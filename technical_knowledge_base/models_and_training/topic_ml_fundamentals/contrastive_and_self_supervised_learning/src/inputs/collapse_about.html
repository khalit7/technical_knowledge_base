Real data, toy scale. The 1,797 scikit-learn digits (8 × 8 pixels, from the UCI ML hand-written digits set); 200 held out (20 per class) for every measurement, 1,597 for training. Every run uses the same encoder, an MLP 64-256-256-16 with BatchNorm after each layer (SimSiam's projector layout, scaled down), the same two augmentations per image (shift up to 2 pixels, 25% of pixels dropped, contrast ×0.6 to ×1.4, Gaussian noise of std 0.1) and SGD with momentum 0.9, learning rate 0.05 and weight decay 5 × 10−4, for 3,000 steps of 256 images. Predictors are 16-8-16 MLPs; BYOL's target and DINO's teacher are moving averages at 0.99 and 0.996; DINO uses 32 prototypes, student temperature 0.1, teacher 0.04 (0.1 when sharpening is removed) and a centre momentum of 0.9; VICReg uses λ = μ = 25, ν = 1 on the 16-d output. These settings were chosen once, before the ablations, with one exception: DINO's teacher momentum was raised from 0.99 to 0.996 (the paper's starting value) after a first try at 0.99 learned poorly. Three seeds per run; the animation shows seed 0. Two honest caveats. A collapsed run still keeps a speck of residual spread (std about 0.001) that carries some class information, which a 5-NN classifier at full float precision can read: that is why some collapsed runs show 50 to 65% accuracy, and why the std, not the accuracy, is the collapse measure. And the digits are easy: a 5-NN on raw pixels already scores 90% on the same 200 digits, and even the untrained encoder scores 82%, so the toy shows collapse and its prevention, not that self-supervision beats pixels. Full DINO is the least stable run here: one seed of three stays healthy, the other two drift towards the uniform collapse (teacher entropy near ln 32), which a toy this small and a 16-d output do not let us diagnose further.
