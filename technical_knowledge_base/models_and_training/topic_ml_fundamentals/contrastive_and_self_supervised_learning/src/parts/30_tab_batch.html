One real batch: InfoNCE on real sentence embeddings

Forty-eight pairs of sentences that human raters scored as meaning the same thing (gold 4.5 to 5 out of 5), drawn from the test split of the STS benchmark. Each first sentence is an anchor; its partner is the positive; the other partners in the batch are the negatives. The cosines are real: the same BERT-base before and after contrastive training (unsupervised SimCSE), computed offline (src/real_embeddings.py). Everything else, the softmax, the loss, the gradient, is computed in your browser from the formula on the Reading tab.

Try: at the defaults (τ = 0.05, all 48 pairs) switch between the two models: the matrix looks alike, but the loss falls from about 0.29 to 0.04 and the hardest negative's share of the push doubles. Then push the temperature down to 0.01, where the gradient piles onto the one or two hardest negatives, and up to 1, where every negative gets the same small push and the loss sits near ln N. Click a row to inspect that anchor; row 31 is the interesting one.

What the numbers say

Spearman correlation (× 100) between cosine and the human score over all 1,379 test pairs; alignment over pairs scored 4 or more; uniformity and mean cosine over random pairs of test sentences (both defined on the Reading tab). "First-last avg" averages the token vectors of the first and last transformer layers (Su et al.'s pooling, 59.04 in SimCSE Table B.2); "embedding + last avg" averages the embedding layer's output with the last layer and reproduces SimCSE's own Table 5 baseline, 53.87; SimCSE's unsupervised model is read at the last layer's [CLS] vector without its training-time MLP, as the authors' repository does.

Read the matrix like this
  • This batch is easy. The pairs are near-paraphrases that share most of their words, so even raw BERT ranks 47 of the 48 positives first. What contrastive training changes is the margin: at τ = 0.05 the mean loss falls from 0.29 to 0.04 nats, and the loss-against-temperature curve sits lower at every τ.
  • Before contrastive training random sentences already have a cosine near 0.6 (the anisotropy of the Reading tab) and positives about 0.92. After, the negatives fall to about 0.3 while the positives stay near 0.9: the gap, divided by τ, is what the softmax sees.
  • The push concentrates. Averaged over the 48 anchors at τ = 0.05, the hardest negative takes 20% of the push under BERT and 44% after SimCSE: once most negatives are far away, the few hard ones get the gradient.
  • A false negative. The only anchor either model gets wrong is row 31, "A woman is dancing in the rain.": both rank another pair's sentence, "A man and woman are dancing in rain.", above its own positive, "A woman dances in the rain out side." (cosines 0.92 and 0.87 after SimCSE). InfoNCE pushes those two apart, though a reader would call them near-paraphrases.
