InfoNCE, the workhorse loss

On the hero's batch at τ = 0.05, BERT's mean loss is 0.29 nats and SimCSE's 0.04, and after SimCSE the single most similar negative takes almost half of each anchor's push. Both come from one formula:

Li = −log exp(sim(zi, zi+) / τ)Σk=1..N exp(sim(zi, zk) / τ)
zi
the anchor's embedding; zi+ its positive; the sum runs over the positive and the N − 1 negatives
sim
cosine similarity, the dot product of l2-normalised vectors, so every logit lies in [−1/τ, 1/τ]
τ
the temperature, a fixed hyperparameter or (in CLIP) learned

It is the softmax cross entropy of an N-way classification whose classes are the candidates in the batch and whose right answer is the positive (van den Oord et al. 2018, CPC, §2.3); temperature as a softmax knob in general is on {{Activation functions|n:3c65c17b0d0d819f8049c255e6f206e2}}. Three facts make it special.

1 · The gradient goes where the softmax puts its mass

Write sij for the cosine between anchor i and candidate j and pij for the softmax over sij/τ. Differentiating derived:

∂Li/∂sij = (pij − 1[j is the positive]) / τ

The positive is pulled up by (1 − ppositive)/τ and each negative is pushed down by pij/τ; the pushes add up to the pull. Unlike the triplet hinge, every negative gets some push, in proportion to how similar it already is. The temperature sets how sharply: as τ falls the push concentrates on the hardest negatives; as τ rises every negative gets the same small push. Wang and Liu (2021) call this the hardness-aware property ("the temperature τ controls the strength of penalties on hard negative samples"), and name the cost of going too low the "uniformity-tolerance dilemma": things that genuinely belong together get pushed apart.

2 · The batch is the task

A model that knows nothing puts probability 1/N on the positive and scores ln N, so the batch size changes the task itself, not only the gradient noise: drag N above and watch the dotted chance line move. A lower loss at a bigger batch is not a better model; compare ln N − L, or better, a downstream probe. CPC proves the loss bounds the mutual information between the two views from below, I ≥ ln N − L, "which becomes tighter as N becomes larger" (§2.3), and the bound can never exceed ln N (Poole et al. 2019: "upper bounded by log K", K the batch size): ln 4,096 = 8.3 nats is the most a SimCLR batch can certify derived.

Correction to the old page
"Maximises a lower bound on mutual information" is true but often over-read. The bound is capped at ln N, it is loose when the true information is larger, and later work credits InfoNCE's success to the geometry it produces (alignment and uniformity) rather than to information it measures (Wang and Isola 2020). Also, in SimCLR each anchor has 2(N − 1) negatives, not N − 1: both augmented views of every other image count ("we treat the other 2(N−1) augmented examples within a minibatch as negative examples", §2.1).

Hence the engineering that buys negatives: big batches (SimCLR trains at 4,096 by default and sweeps 256 to 8,192, §2.2; CLIP uses 32,768); a memory bank of every image's last-seen feature (Wu et al. 2018: many negatives, but stale ones from older versions of the encoder); a queue fed by a momentum encoder (MoCo, next section), which decouples the number of negatives from the batch; or a sigmoid instead of the softmax: SigLIP scores every image-text pair as an independent binary decision, so the loss "does not require a global view of the pairwise similarities for normalization" (Zhai et al. 2023; the {{CLIP paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d8194a85dfc5f3bf3f799}} animates softmax against sigmoid on one batch).

3 · Negatives are sampled, so some are wrong

Random negatives include items that share the anchor's meaning or class, and the loss pushes them away anyway. The batch has one: the only anchor either model gets wrong is row 31, "A woman is dancing in the rain.", whose nearest candidate is another pair's "A man and woman are dancing in rain." (cosine 0.92 after SimCSE) rather than its own positive, "A woman dances in the rain out side." (0.87). It is also the one triplet SimCSE leaves active above. Chuang et al. (2020) correct the objective for these false negatives; Robinson et al. (2021) go the other way and sample hard negatives on purpose, with a knob for how hard. In practice: deduplicate, use larger and more diverse batches, and when mining hard negatives for retrieval, filter the ones that are actually positives; how often a mined "negative" is really relevant on SciFact, and a positive-aware filter, are measured on {{Embedding models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81508f99e1fa78c2bb41}}, sections 4 and 5.

SimCLR: the recipe most image methods inherit

SimCLR (Chen et al. 2020) calls the loss NT-Xent ("normalized temperature-scaled cross entropy") and finds three things matter. Augmentation: "the combination of random crop and color distortion is crucial" (§3). A projection head: a small MLP g maps the representation h to the z the loss sees; a nonlinear head beats a linear one by about 3% and no head by more than 10% (§4.2), and after training "we throw away the projection head" and use h (using z downstream is a common mistake). Scale: large batches and long training. Linear evaluation on ImageNet reaches 69.3% top-1 with a ResNet-50 and 76.5% with a ResNet-50 four times wider, "matching the performance of a supervised ResNet-50"; fine-tuned on 1% of the labels it reaches 85.8% top-5 (Table 6 and abstract).

Correction
SimCLR's often-quoted 76.5% is for ResNet-50 (4×), a network with four times the width; the plain ResNet-50 gets 69.3%. And the 1%-label result, 85.8%, is top-5 accuracy, not top-1.

MoCo: negatives from a queue

MoCo (He et al. 2019) encodes the query view with the trained encoder and the key view with a second encoder whose weights are a moving average of the first, θk ← mθk + (1 − m)θq. Each batch's keys join a queue of K = 65,536 and the oldest leave, so the negatives are many and, because the key encoder changes slowly, consistent with each other: a large momentum (m = 0.999) "works much better than a smaller value". τ = 0.07. The number of negatives no longer depends on the batch.
