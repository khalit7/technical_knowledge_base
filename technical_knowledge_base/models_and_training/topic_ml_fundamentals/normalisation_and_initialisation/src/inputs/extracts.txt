Xiong et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.04745 (PDF, pdftotext):
- Lemma 2. At initialization, for the Post-LN Transformer, E(||x^{post,5}_{l,i}||^2) = 3/2 d for all l > 0 and i. For the Pre-LN Transformer, (1 + l/2)d <= E(||x^{pre}_{l,i}||^2) <= (1 + 3l/2)d for all l > 0 and i.
- Theorem 1: Post-LN ||dL/dW^{2,L}||_F <= O(d sqrt(ln d)); Pre-LN <= O(d sqrt(ln d / L)).
- Abstract: "the expected gradients of the parameters near the output layer are large"; "The warm-up stage is practically helpful for avoiding this problem"; Pre-LN "the gradients are well-behaved at initialization".

Ioffe and Szegedy 2015, arXiv:1502.03167: "Batch Normalization achieves the same accuracy with 14 times fewer training steps"; "reaching 4.9% top-5 validation error (and 4.8% test error)".
Wu and He 2018, arXiv:1803.08494: "GN has 10.6% lower error than its BN counterpart when using a batch size of 2".
Zhang and Sennrich 2019, arXiv:1910.07467: "RMSNorm achieves comparable performance against LayerNorm but reduces the running time by 7%~64% on different models"; LayerNorm "significantly slows the underlying network, e.g. RNN in particular".
Santurkar et al. 2018, arXiv:1805.11604: "such distributional stability of layer inputs has little to do with the success of BatchNorm"; "it makes the optimization landscape significantly smoother".
PyTorch 2.14.1 BatchNorm1d docstring: "the variance is calculated via the biased estimator ... the value stored in the moving average of the variance is calculated via the unbiased estimator"; "This momentum argument is different from one used in optimizer classes ... x_new = (1 - momentum) * x_hat + momentum * x_t"; default momentum 0.1, eps 1e-5.
PyTorch 2.14.1 nn.Linear.reset_parameters: "Setting a=sqrt(5) in kaiming_uniform is the same as initializing with uniform(-1/sqrt(in_features), 1/sqrt(in_features))". nn.Embedding: init.normal_(self.weight).
Keras BatchNormalization docs: momentum 0.99, epsilon 0.001; moving_mean = moving_mean * momentum + mean(batch) * (1 - momentum).
Glorot and Bengio 2010: Eq. 1 "commonly used heuristic" W ~ U[-1/sqrt(n), 1/sqrt(n)]; Eq. 16 normalized initialization W ~ U[-sqrt6/sqrt(n_j + n_j+1), sqrt6/sqrt(n_j + n_j+1)].
He et al. 2015, arXiv:1502.01852 section 2.2: "We note that it is sufficient to use either Eqn.(14) or Eqn.(10) alone"; "our initialization method allows for extremely deep models (e.g., 30 conv/fc layers) to converge, while the 'Xavier' method ..." (stalls).
GPT-2 report section 2.3: "Layer normalization (Ba et al., 2016) was moved to the input of each sub-block ... and an additional layer normalization was added after the final self-attention block. A modified initialization which accounts for the accumulation on the residual path with model depth is used. We scale the weights of residual layers at initialization by a factor of 1/sqrt(N) where N is the number of residual layers."
Gemma 3 report, arXiv:2503.19786 section 2: "We use a Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) with post-norm and pre-norm with RMSNorm"; "we replace the soft-capping of Gemma 2 with QK-norm".
ViT-22B, arXiv:2302.05442 section 2: "we observed divergent training loss after a few thousand steps. In particular, this instability was observed for models with around 8B parameters ... caused by extremely large values in attention logits ... we adopt the approach of Gilmer et al. (2023), which applies LayerNorm to the queries and keys before the dot-product attention computation."
Henry et al. 2020, arXiv:2010.04245: QKNorm, l2 normalisation of queries and keys with a learnable scale.
Wortsman et al. 2023, arXiv:2309.14322: "these instabilities also appear in small models when training at high learning rates, and that mitigations previously employed at large scales are equally effective in this regime".
Kimi K2 report, arXiv:2507.20534: "MuonClip with tau=100"; mid-scale: "attention logits rapidly exceed 1000".
DeepNet, arXiv:2203.00555: "We successfully scale Transformers up to 1,000 layers"; decoder-only alpha = (2M)^(1/4), beta = (8M)^(-1/4); beta scales FFN, value and output projections.
Fixup, arXiv:1901.09321: "training residual networks with Fixup to be as stable as training with normalization -- even for networks with 10,000 layers".
DyT, arXiv:2503.10622: "layer normalization in Transformers often produces tanh-like, S-shaped input-output mappings".
Peri-LN, arXiv:2502.02732: "This strategy places normalization layer peripherally around sublayers, a design we term Peri-LN."
muP, arXiv:2203.03466, Table 3 (Adam): input weights and biases Var 1/fan_in, LR 1; output weights Var 1/fan_in^2, LR 1/fan_in; hidden weights Var 1/fan_in, LR 1/fan_in; "Transformer muP requires one more modification (1/d attention instead of 1/sqrt d)"; abstract: "by transferring from 40M parameters, we outperform published numbers of the 6.7B GPT-3 model, with tuning cost only 7% of total pretraining cost".
