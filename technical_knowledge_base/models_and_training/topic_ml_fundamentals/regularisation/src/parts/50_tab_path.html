Coefficient paths on the diabetes data

Under the lasso, coefficients leave the model one at a time as λ grows; under ridge, all ten shrink and none reaches 0.

Ten baseline measurements of 442 diabetes patients (age, sex, body mass index, blood pressure and six blood serum measurements) and a measure of disease progression one year later: the dataset Efron, Hastie, Johnstone and Tibshirani used to introduce least angle regression ({{Efron et al. 2004, Table 1|https://hastie.su.domains/Papers/LARS/LeastAngle_2002.pdf}}), here as shipped in scikit-learn (load_diabetes). Every coefficient below is an exact fit computed in your browser: the lasso path by least angle regression with the lasso modification (piecewise linear, so exact between its knots), ridge in closed form, elastic net by coordinate descent.

Try: slide λ from the left (no penalty) to the right. Under the lasso, variables leave the model one at a time; under ridge none ever reaches 0. Held-out error against λ for any training size is in the Reading tab, section 1.

    Defaults reproduce Efron et al. (2004), section 1, independently: the lasso reaches least squares at t = Σ|b| = 3,459.98 (printed: 3,460.00; the published data carry rounded values) and at t = 1,000 only bmi, bp, s3 and s5 are in the model (printed: "only variables 3, 9, 4, and 7", the same four). The path has 12 steps for 10 variables because one variable (s3) leaves and re-enters, the lasso modification of least angle regression (section 3.1).

    Dropout on the inputs is ridge here
    Because every column has unit length, Srivastava et al.'s result (Reading, section 4) says input dropout with keep probability p equals ridge with scikit-learn alpha = (1 − p)/p: keep 0.8 is alpha 0.25, λ = 0.25/442 = 5.7 × 10−4 in this page's convention derived. In ridge mode the counters show the keep probability matching the current λ. On the full data this is a very light penalty, and the fit barely moves.
