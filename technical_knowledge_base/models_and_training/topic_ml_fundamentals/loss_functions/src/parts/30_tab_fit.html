Fit a line

Every regression loss on this page fitting the same straight line, exactly, on real data or on points you place. It extends "What an outlier does to a fit" and "Robust to bad targets is not robust to unusual inputs" in the {{Reading|#t-read}} tab.

Try: on Anscombe III, slide δ from 10 down to 0.1 and watch Huber move from MSE's line to MAE's. Switch to the stars and see every loss lose the main sequence. Choose "your points", tap to add points and tap a point to remove it; build a bad target, then a leverage point, and compare.

Each point's pull at the selected fit ψ(u), and its lever on the slope ψ(u)·(x − x̄); points in x order

Defaults reproduce Anscombe's y = 3.00 + 0.500x for set III (Anscombe 1973), independently. Fits are exact minimisers of the mean loss: closed form (MSE), iteratively reweighted least squares to convergence (Huber, log-cosh), and the best line through two data points (MAE, pinball, MAPE), which is where a linear programme's optimum lies. Checked against PyTorch's MSELoss, L1Loss and HuberLoss with autograd, and SciPy's linprog and BFGS (src/recompute.py: objectives agree to 10−15, parameters to 10−6). Data: Anscombe's set III (R datasets::anscombe); robustbase starsCYG, giants are observations 11, 20, 30, 34.
