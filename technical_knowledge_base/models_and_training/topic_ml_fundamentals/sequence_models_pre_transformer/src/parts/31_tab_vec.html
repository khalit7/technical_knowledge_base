Word vectors: real GloVe, honestly tested

2,000 words of the real GloVe 6B vectors (50 dimensions, trained on Wikipedia 2014 plus Gigaword 5, 400,000-word vocabulary; Pennington et al. 2014), stored in this page as 8-bit numbers. Ask for neighbours, solve analogies with and without the rule every published score uses, and run the analogy test yourself.

What is in the page, and what is not
The 2,000 words are every word of the analogy test (905 words) plus the most frequent other words. Searching 2,000 words is easier than searching 400,000, so answers here can be better than the full vocabulary's; for the preset examples the full-vocabulary answers, computed offline, are shown beside the page's. Rounding to 8 bits moves no cosine similarity by more than 0.0001. Source of the file: the gensim-data mirror glove-wiki-gigaword-50, the Stanford file with a header line. Script: src/glove_subset.py.

Nearest neighbours

Try: "frog". The GloVe site shows its neighbours as frogs, toad, litoria, leptodactylidae, rana, lizard and eleutherodactylus (it does not say which of its vector sets); these 50-dimension vectors give snake, ape, toad, monkey, spider, lizard: the same neighbourhood, coarser. Then "bank": all ten full-vocabulary neighbours are financial, because one vector per word mixes every sense into one point, and here the financial sense dominates it.

      Analogies, with and without excluding the question words

      The analogy test asks "a is to b as c is to ?" and answers with the word whose vector is closest to b − a + c (cosine; "3CosAdd"). Every published score, word2vec's and GloVe's, removes a, b and c from the candidates first. Without that rule the closest word is very often one of the question words, because b − a + c stays near b and c (Linzen 2016; Nissim, van Noord and van der Goot 2020).

      Try: man : king :: woman : ? with the rule switched off: the answer is "king". Then man : doctor :: woman : ?: "nurse" appears only because "doctor" is forbidden as an answer, which is Nissim et al.'s point about how such bias examples are produced.

          Run the analogy test

          The test set is questions-words.txt from the word2vec repository (Mikolov et al. 2013): 19,544 questions in 14 sections, 5 semantic (capitals, currencies, cities in states, family) and 9 syntactic (adjective to adverb, opposites, comparatives, superlatives, participles, nationalities, past tense, plurals, verb forms). The page runs a fixed sample of 40 questions per section (560) on its 2,000 words; the table below it has the full test on the full vocabulary, computed offline.

          Full-vocabulary rows: all 19,544 questions (every word is in GloVe's lowercased vocabulary), 3CosAdd on unit vectors; "top 30,000" restricts both questions and candidates to the 30,000 most frequent words, as word2vec's own evaluation tool does. Measured here, not published: the GloVe paper reports 100 and 300 dimensions only (Table 2: 6B tokens, 300 dimensions, 71.7% total; 1.6B tokens, 100 dimensions, 60.3%), so there is no published 50-dimension figure to reproduce.
