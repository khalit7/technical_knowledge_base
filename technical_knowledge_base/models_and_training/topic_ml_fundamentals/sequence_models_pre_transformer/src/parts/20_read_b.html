The LSTM, gate by gate

The long short-term memory network gives each unit a memory cell C whose update is an addition, guarded by gates (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997). The input xt and the previous hidden state ht−1 are concatenated and fed to four parallel layers; three of them are sigmoid gates, outputs between 0 and 1 that multiply a stream of information element by element.

PartFormulaRole
Forget gateft = σ(Wf[ht−1, xt] + bf)how much of the old cell Ct−1 to keep
Input gateit = σ(Wi[ht−1, xt] + bi)which units to write
CandidateC̃t = tanh(WC[ht−1, xt] + bC)what would be written
Output gateot = σ(Wo[ht−1, xt] + bo)what part of the cell to expose
UpdatesCt = ft ⊙ Ct−1 + it ⊙ C̃t;   ht = ot ⊙ tanh(Ct)the cell is added to, not rewritten

Why it works. Along the cell, ∂Ct/∂Ct−1 = diag(ft) plus terms through the gates' dependence on h: no weight matrix and no squashing slope on the direct path, only the forget gate, which the network can hold near 1. It is a skip connection through time, the same idea residual networks later used through depth. It does not abolish vanishing gradients: the path through h still multiplies Jacobians, and a forget gate of 0.9 still shrinks the cell path by 0.950 ≈ 0.005 over 50 steps. Setting the forget-gate bias to 1 or more at initialisation, so that the cell starts by remembering, is the standard recommendation (Jozefowicz, Zaremba and Sutskever 2015) and is used in the model below.

Correction: the 1997 LSTM had no forget gate
The original LSTM had input and output gates around a "constant error carrousel", a cell whose self-connection was fixed at 1.0 (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber 1997, §3.2 and §4). The forget gate, which lets the cell decay or reset, was added by Gers, Schmidhuber and Cummins 2000, and "peephole" connections from the cell to the gates by Gers and Schmidhuber the same year. The LSTM in every library today is the 2000 version, usually without peepholes (Greff et al. 2015, §III, has the history).

The GRU: two gates, one state

The gated recurrent unit merges the cell and the hidden state and uses two gates (Cho et al. 2014, who called it a new hidden unit; the name is from Chung et al. 2014):

ht = (1 − zt) ⊙ ht−1 + zt ⊙ h̃t,   h̃t = tanh(W xt + U(rt ⊙ ht−1))
zt
update gate: one gate doing the forget and input jobs together (keep 1 − z, write z)
rt
reset gate: how much of the old state the candidate may look at

The same interpolating, additive trick, with three weight blocks instead of four, so at equal width a GRU has exactly 3/4 of an LSTM's parameters: 4n(n + m + 1) against 3n(n + m + 1) for n units and m inputs derived (PyTorch keeps two bias vectors, so +2 instead of +1: the toy's recurrent layers, n = 8, m = 9, have 456 against 608). Quality is usually comparable: Chung et al. "could not make concrete conclusion on which of the two gating units was better", and Greff et al.'s search over eight LSTM variants found none significantly better than the standard LSTM, with the forget gate and the output activation its most critical parts.

Measured: who learns to remember

The same task, run as an experiment: each cell trained from scratch on sequences up to 10, 20, 50 or 100 symbols long, at three learning rates (0.003, 0.01, 0.03) and three seeds, 3,000 Adam steps of 64 sequences, gradient norm clipped at 1; then tested on 2,000 fresh sequences at each length from 5 to 200. Three things the runs show. The plain RNN can hold a memory but cannot learn one from long sequences: trained on lengths up to 10 it solved the task in of 9 runs, and two of those hold the key correctly at every test length up to 200; trained on lengths up to 50 or 100 it solved it in none of 18, ending at 25% (chance) or 50% (one bit of the key kept). The difference is visible before any training (second view): on the untrained networks the gradient that reaches the key's step from 50 steps later is about ; the LSTM starts five or six orders of magnitude better off, because its forget gates begin near σ(1) ≈ 0.73 rather than at whatever slope the tanh gives. That is Bengio et al.'s 1994 diagnosis on a laptop: the trouble is the gradient that must find the key, not the capacity to store it.
