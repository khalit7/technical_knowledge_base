Four mistakes the maths above guards against

The comparison-level mistakes (comparing scores without budget and harness, reading the chain of thought as an explanation, expecting thinking to add knowledge, treating a budget as a hard cap) are on the root; these are the ones this page's formulas catch.

Reading a pass@1 gain from RL as new capability. RLVR reliably converts pass@k into pass@1. Measure pass@k at large k against the base model before claiming the model can now solve problems it could not (section 8).

Assuming majority voting always helps. It amplifies the most likely answer. Where a systematic wrong answer is more likely than the right one, more votes lower accuracy (set p below the wrong answer's share in section 3's selector chart).

Scaling best-of-n against a reward model without limit. Past the peak the proxy score keeps rising while true quality falls, and nothing in the proxy signals it (section 3).

Forgetting that sequential tokens are serial. Doubling the thinking budget doubles the time to the first answer token; no amount of hardware parallelism removes it (section 2).
