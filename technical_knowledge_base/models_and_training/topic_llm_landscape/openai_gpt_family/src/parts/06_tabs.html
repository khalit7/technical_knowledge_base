gpt-oss on one GPU

Everything on this tab is computed from the two models' published configuration files ({{120b config|@cfg120}}, {{20b config|@cfg20}}) and checked against the model card's Table 1 ({{model card|@oss}}): what the parameters are, what they weigh in memory, and how the KV cache grows with context.

1. The parameter table, rebuilt

Per layer: each expert is a gated SwiGLU, 2,880 × 5,760 + 5,760 + 2,880 × 2,880 + 2,880 = 24,891,840 parameters (fused gate and up projection, down projection, biases); the router is 2,880 × E + E; attention is the query, key, value and output projections with biases (64 query heads and 8 KV heads of 64) plus one sink per head. Embedding and unembedding are separate, 201,088 × 2,880 each. Active counts 4 experts per layer, all attention and the unembedding, but not the embedding, as the card does.

Reproduces the card independently: every row matches Table 1 to its stated precision, and the checkpoint, with the MoE weights at 4.25 bits and everything else at 2 bytes, comes out at 60.70 against 60.8 GiB and 12.80 against 12.8 GiB, which confirms that the non-expert weights ship in 16 bits.

2. Memory against a budget

Try: the defaults are gpt-oss-120b as released on an 80 GB card at the full 131,072-token context. Switch the weights to BF16 and it needs about three cards. Then turn off GQA or the sliding window to see what each saves in cache.

KV cache against context

The general KV-cache calculator, for any model, is {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}}'s; this one applies it to gpt-oss's two layer types. KV cache per token per layer is 2 (keys and values) × KV heads × 64 × bytes; 18 of 120b's 36 layers (12 of 20b's 24) keep every token, the other half only the last 128. "FP8 experts" and the GQA and window switches are illustrative alternatives, not shipped variants. GB is 109 bytes and the 80 GB card is taken as 80 × 109 bytes; activations and runtime overheads are not counted, so a real fit needs some headroom.

3. One MXFP4 block

The format, its clipping and its comparison with NVFP4 are taught on {{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}; here is one block as gpt-oss stores its expert weights. MXFP4 stores each block of 32 values as 4-bit floating-point numbers (E2M1: one sign bit, two exponent bits, one mantissa bit, so magnitudes 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 6) sharing one 8-bit power-of-two scale, chosen from the block's largest magnitude ({{MX formats|@mx}}). The scale is why a block can follow the weights' range, and the 8 extra bits per 32 values are the 0.25 in 4.25.

Try: draw new blocks. Most values land near their originals; the largest set the scale, and the smallest round to zero.

Block values are illustrative (random, weight-like). Rounding to the nearest representable value; the shared exponent is the block's largest exponent minus 2, the largest exponent E2M1 can represent.
