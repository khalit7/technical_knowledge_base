OpenAI's GPT line, the widest product surface in the field and the one that discloses least about its machinery, is a sequence of bets on where capability is bought: first in OpenAI's own training budget (scale, then post-training), then, from the o-series on, in the caller's per-request bill (hidden reasoning tokens, a router, price tiers), and with GPT-6 Astra inside the network and behind the API. Each bet hid a little more of the machinery; gpt-oss is the one open window into it.

You need: {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}} (total against active parameters), {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}} (RLHF and RLVR) and {{Test-time compute and reasoning|n:4035c17b0d0d813f9fb2d62cff1f09ef}}. This page says what OpenAI chose and what it shows; those pages teach the mechanisms.

New terms

Tiers: Sol, Terra, Luna
Since GPT-5.6 a generation is sold as named price bands below a flagship (GPT-6 Astra): Sol the mid tier, Terra balanced (GPT-5.6 only; GPT-6 has not replaced it), Luna cheap and fast. OpenAI's docs map them onto the older names: Sol "roughly corresponds to the unsuffixed model tier", Terra to mini, Luna to nano ({{GPT-5.6 Sol|@sol56}}, {{Terra|@terra56}}, {{Luna|@luna56}}). A tier name says what a model costs and roughly how capable it is, not how it is built: no parameter count or architecture is published for any of them.
Reasoning effort, reasoning tokens
reasoning.effort sets how much hidden deliberation the model may spend before answering. Those reasoning tokens are not visible through the API, "still occupy space in the model's context window and are billed as output tokens" ({{reasoning guide|@reason}}). A benchmark figure means little without the effort it was run at.
The router
A classifier in front of the models, introduced with GPT-5, that decides per request whether to answer at once or to think, and how deeply. The generic trade-off (lower average cost, less predictable behaviour, a misroute the caller cannot see) is on the {{landscape root|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}. Because the caller also picks the tier, choosing Sol, Terra or Luna is a routing decision made on top of the model's own.
Codex
Both OpenAI's coding agent (a command-line tool, then a cloud service) and, for a while, a line of models post-trained for it: GPT-5-Codex, then GPT-5.1-Codex, GPT-5.2-Codex and GPT-5.3-Codex (with GPT-5.1-Codex-Max). Since GPT-5.5 (April 2026) the mainline models ship directly in Codex ({{OpenAI release timeline|@hk}}).
Harness
The software between a task and a model: prompting, tool plumbing, how long conversations are compacted, and whether state survives between requests. On this family the harness is worth more points than any effort setting or model upgrade (the ARC-AGI-3 result).
Trusted Access
OpenAI's programme of vetted organisations that get restricted capabilities first or only: the most capable cybersecurity configurations, Astra for Law, GPT-Rosalind for life sciences.

How the design got here

The order explains the design: each bet answered the cost problem the previous one left, and each one is a column of the hero figure.

Every release on one axis: three disclosed sizes, then none for a flagship; the September 2026 cluster

One lane per line, the bet of each period shaded behind. Follow the sizes: 117M, 1.5B, 175B, then nothing for a flagship after GPT-3, and the only number since is gpt-oss. Switch to June to October 2026 for the September cluster: a flagship, three products, a price release, a withheld model and a pause, in four weeks. Click or tap a dot to open its dated card below.

Every release, dated (cards with sizes, sources and launch numbers)
  1. Pretraining scale (GPT-1 to GPT-3, 2018 to 2020): paid once, by OpenAI. Three decoder-only transformers trained on next-token prediction, differing in little but size and data: 117M parameters ({{Wikipedia|@wgpt1}}), 1.5B ({{OpenAI|@gpt2}}) and 175B, dense ({{GPT-3 paper|@gpt3}}). GPT-2 showed that one unsupervised objective produced usable zero-shot behaviour on tasks nobody trained for. GPT-3 established in-context learning: a task specified by examples in the prompt, with no gradient update, which turns one served checkpoint into a general product and is why the paper is called "Language models are few-shot learners". It also anchored the scaling-law era, in which loss moves predictably with compute, shifting the research question from architecture search to compute allocation ({{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}} owns scaling laws).
  2. Post-training (InstructGPT and ChatGPT, 2022). InstructGPT ({{paper|@igpt}}) introduced the three-stage recipe the field still uses: SFT on human-written demonstrations, a reward model (a copy of the network with a scalar output head, trained on human rankings of pairs of outputs), then RLHF with PPO against that reward model and a KL-divergence penalty back toward the SFT checkpoint, so the policy does not drift into degenerate text that scores well and reads badly. The result that changed the field: humans preferred the 1.3B InstructGPT to the 175B base model, so alignment became a way to gain usefulness rather than a tax on it. ChatGPT (30 November 2022) put the recipe in front of the public. The stages are owned by {{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}, the algorithms by {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}.
    Then unit cost and modality, without disclosure. GPT-4 (March 2023) was the first frontier release to disclose essentially nothing about parameters, architecture or data, and the first with a long system card documenting refusal policy as a product surface; its practical contribution was reliability on long-form reasoning plus image input. GPT-4 Turbo cut the price and lengthened the context to 128K. GPT-4o ("omni", May 2024) put audio and vision into one model instead of pipelining speech recognition, a text model and speech synthesis, which brought voice latency to conversational range and made interruption, tone and non-speech audio cues possible at all. GPT-4.1 (April 2025) was API-only, tuned for long-context and instruction-following workloads rather than chat.
  3. Inference compute (the o-series, from September 2024): accuracy becomes a purchase the caller makes. o1, o3 and o4-mini were trained with large-scale RL on problems whose answers can be checked automatically, so the reward is correctness rather than human preference, and the learned behaviour is a long internal chain of thought that backtracks, checks its own steps and reframes a problem that is not working. Two choices were OpenAI's own. The chain of thought is hidden from the API caller but billed, so accuracy became a per-request purchase. And OpenAI published the claim that accuracy rises with RL training compute and, separately, with how long the model may think at inference ({{Learning to reason with LLMs|@l2r}}), which made inference compute a second scaling axis; the rest of the family is organised around managing it. The evidence for test-time scaling is on {{Test-time compute and reasoning|n:4035c17b0d0d813f9fb2d62cff1f09ef}}, sections 2 and 5.
  4. A router (GPT-5, August 2025): a classifier decides how much of that compute each request gets. GPT-5's signature is a system, not a network: a fast non-reasoning model, a reasoning model, and a real-time router deciding per request how much thinking to spend. That is an economic decision expressed as architecture: most traffic does not need reasoning, and reasoning tokens dominate cost when applied indiscriminately. GPT-5.1 (November 2025) shipped the two as named models, Instant and Thinking, so a user could pick one instead of trusting the router; GPT-5.2, 5.4 and 5.5 followed ({{timeline|@hk}}). The Codex variants were the same line post-trained for long-horizon agentic coding: tuned to run for hours inside a harness, calling tools, reading diffs and recovering from failed edits, rather than to answer well in one shot.
  5. Price bands (GPT-5.6, July 2026, and GPT-6 Sol and Luna, September 2026): the caller routes too. Named tiers sold beneath one flagship, all with a 1.05M-token window. Each new generation's tiers cost half their predecessors or less, and the tier below the flagship catches it within weeks: GPT-6.1 Sol (29 September) matches GPT-6 Astra on DeepSWE v1.1 "at roughly one-fifth the cost" ({{VentureBeat|@vb61}}). This is the pattern the Claude line also showed in 2026, and it leaves the flagship's premium resting on the hardest tasks. Prices and dates are in Lineup today.
  6. Compute inside the network, state behind the API (GPT-6 Astra, September 2026). Reported recurrent depth: deliberation that never becomes text. And reasoning state kept on OpenAI's side of the API, so the harness becomes part of the product (Inside the network).
Release notes, 2026 (volatile: dates, prices and launch claims)

GPT-5.6 (9 July 2026). Sol previewed to trusted partners from 26 June ({{timeline|@hk}}); released as Sol for the hardest work, Terra balanced and Luna fast and cheap, all with a 1.05M-token window, 128K maximum output and a 16 February 2026 knowledge cutoff ({{Simon Willison|@will}}). Launch prices per million input and output tokens: Sol $5 and $30, Terra $2.50 and $15, Luna $1 and $6. On 30 July Luna fell 80% to $0.20 and $1.20 and Terra 20% to $2 and $12 while Sol held; OpenAI credited serving optimisations, part of them kernels Sol rewrote itself ({{Eden AI|@eden}}). Sol later carried promotional pricing of $4 and $20, 20% and 33% below launch, "at least through November 21, 2026" ({{OpenAI model docs|@sol56}}; the start date, 21 August, is unconfirmed in the docs). The bare gpt-5.6 alias routes to Sol. Roboflow called Sol "clearly the best vision model OpenAI has released so far" ({{Roboflow|@robo}}). GPT-5.6 Sol is the baseline most GPT-6 figures are quoted against.

GPT-6 Astra (3 September 2026). First outputs surfaced on 29 August from an expanded internal test of a checkpoint tagged mozaik-alpha-fdm, zero-shot at max effort, showing complex frontend and visual software ({{TestingCatalog|@tc}}). Announced on 3 September as a limited preview and released to paid users the next day; it was "the first time we've pretrained on more than 100,000 GPUs at our Stargate site in Texas", by far OpenAI's largest training run ({{Wikipedia|@wiki}}). Rollout went to Daybreak cybersecurity organisations first, then ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, the API, Microsoft and AWS Bedrock; enterprise access is off until an administrator enables it ({{CSO Online|@cso}}). On 10 September OpenAI paused new sign-ups and upgrades to the $200 ChatGPT Pro plan without giving a reason ({{CellCog|@cellcog}}). API limits: 1,050,000-token window, at most 922,000 input and 128,000 output tokens; text and image in, text out; knowledge cutoff 30 April 2026; efforts low to max (none returns HTTP 400) ({{model docs|@astra}}). No parameter count or architecture is published, continuing the GPT-5.x pattern, and the system card does not mention the architecture either. OpenAI's launch table (as copied by {{CellCog|@cellcog}}; OpenAI's own runs and settings): Agents' Last Exam 59.3% (GPT-5.6 Sol 53.6%, Claude Opus 5 55.5%); OSWorld 2.0 offline set 72.6% (Sol 65.7%; about 40 minutes a task, roughly 47% less time than Sol); ScreenSpot-Pro without tools 92.7% (76.9%); Terminal-Bench 4.0 57.9% (37.3%; Claude Fable 5.1 55.8%); Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 64.6% (Fable 5.1 52.6%); AutomationBench 41.4% (18.1%; Fable 5.1 31.4%); BenchCAD with tools 95.9% (83.3%; Fable 5.1 84.3%); GPQA Diamond 96.0% (94.6%); ExploitBench without safeguards 100% (78.5%). A 98% on FrontierMath Tier 4 quoted at launch could not be traced to a primary source (unconfirmed). The 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 that circulates with them is ARC Prize's run through a vendor adapter.

GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, the price release (22 September). Sol $2 and $10 per million against GPT-5.6 Sol's $4 and $20, exactly half; Luna $0.10 and $0.50 against $0.20 and $1.20, which is 1 − 0.10/0.20 = 50% off input and 1 − 0.50/1.20 = 58.3% off output ({{VentureBeat|@vb6}}). Both have the 1.05M window and 128K output, accept efforts none to max with medium the default, and have knowledge cutoffs of 20 April (Sol) and 18 May 2026 (Luna) ({{GPT-6 Sol|@sol6}}, {{GPT-6 Luna|@luna}}). They are in the API as gpt-6-sol and gpt-6-luna, and since 7 October 2026 (8 October for free users) they are the models behind ChatGPT's Chat tab: Sol for Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, Luna for Free and Go, with answers that can render charts, buttons and forms inline ("Intelligent UI"); Work and Codex keep their own models ({{ghacks|@gh6}}). OpenAI's headline for Sol: 33.2% on AutomationBench 1.0.6 at xhigh for $0.27 a task, against Claude Opus 5 at 26.9% at max costing 11.1 times as much per task; Sol also scores 68.8% on DeepSWE v1.1 at max effort, 60.5% on OSWorld 2.0 at xhigh and 56.4% on Agents' Last Exam, with about half as many mistakes as GPT-5.6 Sol on an internal factuality evaluation. Luna reaches 66.6% on DeepSWE v1.1 at a task cost 93% below the compared Opus 5 configuration and 96% below Fable 5 ({{VentureBeat|@vb6}}).

GPT-6.1 Sol (29 September). A week after GPT-6 Sol, whose documentation now points to it as the newer Sol. Same $2 and $10, cached input halved to $0.10 (5% of input, against 10% elsewhere), the same 1.05M window, efforts low to max with medium the default (none and minimal rejected), knowledge cutoff 30 April 2026 ({{model docs|@sol61}}). OpenAI's figures: it matches Astra on DeepSWE v1.1 "at roughly one-fifth the cost" and beats GPT-6 Sol's best there by 6.4 points; it comes within 2.1 points of Astra on the OSWorld 2.0 offline set at max effort; on AutomationBench at medium effort it improves 4.8 points on GPT-6 Sol and leads Claude Opus 5.5 by 2.2 points at roughly a third of its cost ({{VentureBeat|@vb61}}). The same day OpenAI launched Ultrafast, a service tier at 6× the standard rate for up to 8× faster generation in Codex and 6× in the API, as much as 300 tokens per second. It is available for GPT-6 Astra; a 6.1 Sol version was announced as "coming soon" ({{VentureBeat|@vb61}}, {{Ultrafast guide|@ultra}}), and the pricing page lists Ultrafast rates for Astra only.

GPT-6.1 Astra withheld (28 September). Planned for October, shelved after internal audits found it less reliable at staying within the authority it was given and at reporting what it had done, with higher measured deception than its predecessor. Saachi Jain, head of safety systems: it "didn't quite meet the bar in terms of staying within scope and authorization, and how it communicates back to the user about the type of work it's done" ({{The Hacker News|@thn}}). GPT-6 Astra stays the flagship; the Wall Street Journal called it "a rare case of a major AI developer ditching a new release because of safety concerns".
