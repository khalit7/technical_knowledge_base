The idea in one screen

Every named DeepSeek idea answers one question at a different layer of the transformer: what does a frontier-quality model cost to serve, and to train on bandwidth-limited GPUs? Each release keeps the earlier answers and attacks the next cost.

DeepSeek is a spinoff of High-Flyer, a Chinese quantitative hedge fund. It publishes weights and tech reports, though not data, under permissive licences (R1 and V4.1-Flash are MIT), so its layout became the template other open MoE models reuse: {{Kimi K2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f5abf5dbdbac821a12}} keeps MLA and the fine-grained-plus-shared expert layout, at 384 experts (compare them on the {{Config diff|#t-cfg}} tab). Its price cuts are where the serving arithmetic reaches the market: V2 started the Chinese API price war, and V3.2-Exp cut API prices by more than half ({{DeepSeek|https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news250929/}}).

You need: the attention variants (MLA, sparse attention) from {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}, routing and balancing from {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}, and GRPO from {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}. Attention: three cuts to what a decoded token reads

Decode reads every cached key and value once per new token, so the cache decides how many sequences fit on a GPU and what each token costs. What one token reads in one layer is entries read × bytes per entry, and DeepSeek cut each factor in turn, then cut what is stored at all.

Each generation shrank a different factor of what a token reads or stores

Bars to scale within each row (derived from the configs: 2 × 128 heads × 128 = 32,768 numbers for MHA at V3's dimensions, 512 + 64 = 576 for MLA; a 131,072-token context read whole against DSA's top 2,048, 64 times fewer; a 1,048,576-token context stored as 262,144 CSA entries or 8,192 HCA entries, each plus a 128-token exact window). The mechanisms, stepped through on one token, are on {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}; whole-model cache sizes for every DeepSeek generation against context length are in the {{KV-cache calculator on Long context|https://app.notion.com/p/3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5#t-kv}}.

MLA (V2): a 576-number entry where MHA caches 32,768

V2 caches a 512-number latent plus a 64-number RoPE key per token per layer. At V3's 61 layers in BF16 that is 70,272 bytes per token (68.6 KiB), against 3.81 MiB for MHA and 244 KiB for 8-group GQA, yet every head still rebuilds its own keys and values from the latent instead of sharing them as GQA does. Against DeepSeek's own dense 67B, V2 reported a 93.3% smaller cache and 5.76 times the maximum generation throughput ({{V2 paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434}}). The price is extra projection arithmetic and kernels of its own (FlashMLA), which is why DSA and V4's CSA are built on MLA's decode shape rather than on GQA. {{See MLA step by step on Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}: the before/after animation first built for this page.

DSA (V3.2-Exp): keep the whole cache, read the top 2,048

V3.2-Exp (29 September 2025) left MLA's cache in place: a lightning indexer, a few small heads in FP8 with ReLU instead of a softmax for throughput, scores every cached entry, and attention reads only the top 2,048. It is an approximation, so it was trained in, continued from V3.1-Terminus, and DeepSeek reported parity with V3.1-Terminus on standard and long-context benchmarks. Every entry stays resident and the indexer still scores them all. DeepSeek cut API prices by more than 50% when it shipped ({{DeepSeek announcement|https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news250929/}}).

CSA and HCA (V4): store less

CSA layers merge every 4 tokens into one entry and run DSA's selection over the merged entries (top 1,024 in V4-Pro, 512 in V4-Flash); HCA layers merge every 128 tokens and read all of them; both keep a 128-token exact window. V4-Pro interleaves 30 CSA and 31 HCA layers. DeepSeek report the cache at about 2% of BF16 GQA-8 at 1M tokens (a baseline already 16 times smaller than MHA), and, against V3.2 at 1M tokens, 10% of the cache and 27% of the single-token FLOPs for V4-Pro, 7% and 10% for V4-Flash ({{V4 report|https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19348}}). {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}} reproduces the 2% from the config.

V4.1-Flash: the cache argument reaches the shape of the network

V4.1-Flash (September 2026) is a 552B encoder-decoder, the first shipped at frontier scale since the field settled on decoder-only stacks, and DeepSeek's reason is the KV cache, not quality ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash}}). A 40-layer transformer is split into a 20-layer causal encoder and a 20-layer decoder: the prompt is encoded once into a shared representation rather than carried as a per-layer cache through one causal stack. With cross-layer sparse attention with index reuse, hierarchical retrieval, an Engram memory and FP4 cache storage, the reported result is 890 bytes per token, roughly a quarter of V4-Flash's HBM footprint and an eighth of its SSD footprint ({{zartbot|https://zartbot.github.io/blog/model_arch/dsv41flash_arch/en.html}} gives the HBM and SSD ratios as runtime and persistent cache storage). {{T5|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}} had argued the encoder-decoder case on quality grounds without moving anyone; {{End-to-End Context Compression at Scale|n:3d45c17b0d0d81eb90a8d70a5ce84d19}} argued it on this systems ground.

Predict: how many active parameters does a prompt position use, against a generated token? Then switch phase.

A prompt position stops at the encoder; a generated token runs all 40 layers

Prompt positions pass through the 20 encoder layers only, so prefill activates 8B parameters; each generated token passes through all 40 layers, so decode activates 16B. One active-parameter count does not describe this model. The halves connect not through T5-style cross-attention: the decoder's global keys and values are projected from the encoder's 20th-layer hidden state by layer-dependent projection weights, so decoder layers reuse one shared cache.

Which layers store a cache. Layers run in one of three modes: full layers compute their own cache entries and top-k indices; reindex layers reuse a full layer's entries but pick new top-k indices with their own indexer query; reuse layers take both unchanged. So the stack keeps three copies of encoder cache and one of decoder cache across its 40 layers. The experts: 384 routed experts with 6 active per token.

From the released {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash/blob/main/config.json}}, read with {{zartbot|https://zartbot.github.io/blog/model_arch/dsv41flash_arch/en.html}} (layers numbered from 0): layers 0 and 1 are sliding-window only; the rest of the encoder is three blocks of one full layer and five reuse layers, its entries merging two adjacent positions into one (compress_ratios = 2); the decoder keeps every position: layer 20 is full and also builds the indexer's candidate pool, layers 24, 28, 32 and 36 reindex, all the others reuse. The Engram memory is injected at layers 1 and 14.

The indexer's cost stops growing with the context. At decode, the full layer 20 ranks the context in blocks of 8 positions and keeps up to 2,048 blocks, a candidate pool of up to 16,384 positions; each later indexing layer ranks only that pool and keeps its own top 512 ({{zartbot|https://zartbot.github.io/blog/model_arch/dsv41flash_arch/en.html}}; candidate_topk_blocks, candidate_block_size and index_topk in the config). The model card's phrase is "bounding deeper indexer cost independently of context length".

The first stage grows with the context; the second never ranks more than 16,384 positions

What one V4.1 cache entry costs in bytes (an estimate against the reported 890)

DeepSeek's attention kernels store a V4.1 cache entry as 528 bytes in FP8 (512 values plus 16 scale bytes) or 288 bytes in FP4 (512 values at half a byte plus 32 scale bytes) ({{FlashMLA|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/FlashMLA}}), and the model card says the main cache is FP4. As an estimate, not DeepSeek's breakdown: if the three encoder copies hold one entry per two positions and the decoder copy one per position, the main cache is 3 × 288 ÷ 2 + 288 = bytes per token; the reported 890 also counts the indexer keys.
