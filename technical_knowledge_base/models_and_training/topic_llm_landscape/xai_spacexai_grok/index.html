The idea in one screen

xAI's edge is industrial, not algorithmic: it turns capital into training compute faster than any other lab (a Colossus cluster online in 64 to 122 days), spends that compute on reinforcement learning at pretraining scale and on parallel attempts at test time rather than on a published architecture, and ships a new Grok every month or two at a low price per token, so its models are best read by how much compute and how many tokens went in, and its numbers by who ran them.

Five stages from capital to a shipped Grok; the X firehose feeds them, the looser posture is what they cost

Each box links to its section. Numbers: the S-1 for build times and power (S-1), Grok-1's released code (GitHub), xAI's Grok 4 post for RL at pretraining scale (xAI), xAI's multi-agent docs (docs), Artificial Analysis for tokens per task (Artificial Analysis), xAI's docs for prices (xAI docs).

xAI was founded by Elon Musk and eleven researchers in March 2023 (Wikipedia); since February 2026 it has been a subsidiary of SpaceX, and since July 2026 it trades as SpaceXAI. Where DeepSeek optimises cost per token and Ai2 reproducibility, xAI optimises the rate at which capital becomes compute and compute becomes shipped models. It has published little method (one open architecture, Grok-1; one open checkpoint, Grok 2; launch posts), yet a datacentre built in months plus RL compute at pretraining scale keep it among the top five labs without an obvious architectural edge: on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (read 1 October 2026) its best model, Grok 4.7, scores 46.4, and SpaceXAI is the fifth lab by best model, behind Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Meta (AA leaderboard). Its two durable differentiators are the real-time X firehose as data and a looser alignment posture than any other frontier lab. How it compares with every other lab is on the landscape root.

Colossus: compute as the strategy

The lab's core claim is construction speed. Training one model across tens of thousands of accelerators needs them behind one fabric with the power and cooling to run together, so the hard part is building and networking the site, not buying GPUs. The S-1 gives the only audited figures, against its own yardstick: "an industry benchmark to bring online a 100 megawatt greenfield data center" of approximately two years (S-1).

Each Colossus build went faster than the last, and every one beat the greenfield benchmark several times over

S-1 figures: the first Colossus cluster in Memphis, about 100,000 H100 GPUs and about 130 MW, online in 122 days in the shell of an existing factory; the first Colossus II cluster, about 110,000 GB200s and about 210 MW, in 91 days; its second, 110,000 GB300s and 220 MW, in 64 days. Next: at least 220,000 more GB300s and over 400 MW. The two sites provide about 1.0 GW today.

Defaults reproduce the S-1's figures by construction (they are its inputs); rates are megawatts divided by days, so 130 / 122 = 1.07 MW a day for the first cluster against 100 / 730 = 0.14 for the benchmark. A greenfield site is not a repurposed factory, so the first comparison flatters Colossus; the 64-day second cluster at an existing site is the fairest like-for-like. How a cluster's fabric and power decide what can be trained on it is on Topic: hardware.

Correction: "roughly 200K H100s stood up in 122 days" merges two numbers
The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s (S-1); by Grok 3's launch xAI called Colossus a "200,000 GPU cluster" (xAI), and Grok 4's post says "Colossus, our 200k GPU cluster" (xAI). Wikipedia dates full operation to December 2024 and peak demand to 150 MW, met partly by 14 portable methane generators while the grid agreement was worked out (Wikipedia). Expansion continued toward the "at least 1 million" GPUs announced with a third Memphis building on 30 December 2025, for nearly 2 GW (Wikipedia); the S-1's 1.0 GW is what is running.

The twist: the capacity is also sold. In May 2026 SpaceX signed cloud services agreements giving Anthropic compute across Colossus and Colossus II for $1.25 billion a month through May 2029, terminable by either side on 90 days' notice, while "Grok 5 ... is currently being trained at COLOSSUS II" (S-1). A lab whose strategy is owning compute now rents the surplus to a rival: the build rate has outrun its own training needs. The gas turbines at Colossus 2 are the subject of a Clean Air Act suit filed in April 2026 (Wikipedia).

Grok-1: the one published architecture

Grok-1 is a 314-billion-parameter mixture of experts with 8 experts per layer, of which 2 run for each token; xAI calls that "25% of the weights active on a given token" (xAI; run.py and model.py). It was previewed in November 2023 as "the best we could do with 2 months of training" (Wikipedia), pretrained to October 2023 on a custom JAX and Rust stack, and released on 17 March 2024 as base weights plus architecture under Apache 2.0. Grok 2 followed as open weights in August 2025 (Hugging Face calls it Grok 2, Wikipedia Grok 2.5) under the xAI Community License: research and non-commercial use, and commercial use only within xAI's acceptable use policy (licence). The pattern: open the previous generation once it is commercially spent; nothing after Grok 2 has a published architecture. The released code is enough to count every parameter; switch models and move the context to see where they sit:

Almost all of Grok-1 is experts, so "2 of 8 experts" nearly is the active share; Grok 2's dense block changes that

Predict, then try: Grok 2 has a dense feed-forward block that every token runs beside its 8 experts. Will its active share stay near a quarter? Switch and see: over 40%. Then raise the context to 128K: at 8 key-value heads (grouped-query attention) the cache is 256 KiB a token, a sixth of what all 48 heads would need.

Active parameters use the same sum with E replaced by the 2 experts that run. The key-value cache per token is 2 · L · nkv · dh · bytes; whole-model cache sizing at any context is the KV-cache calculator's job.

Defaults reproduce xAI's "314 billion" independently, within 1%: 316.5B with a separate output head (315.7B if tied). They do not reproduce "25% active": the sum gives 84.6B, 26.7%, because 25% is just 2 of 8 experts and ignores the attention and embeddings every token uses. RL at pretraining scale is the method. xAI on Grok 4: "we utilized Colossus, our 200k GPU cluster, to run reinforcement learning to refine Grok's reasoning abilities at a pretraining scale", with infrastructure and algorithmic work that "increased the compute efficiency of our training by 6x" (xAI). It is the concrete form of the industry's 2025 realisation that post-training is a second scaling axis rather than a finishing step: very large volumes of rollouts on tasks whose outcome can be checked (maths, code, tool use), scored by checking the outcome, which is why reasoning and agentic behaviour improved faster than raw knowledge. The algorithms are on RL for LLMs. The often-repeated claim that Grok 4 spent as much compute on RL as on pretraining comes from a chart in the launch stream, not from the text, and is unconfirmed here.

Scale and cadence are the rest. Colossus 1 and 2, expanding toward a million GPUs, with the fastest release cadence of any lab in 2026, and a stated roadmap of "multi-trillion parameter models" (S-1).

The data edge. Owning X since the March 2025 merger gives a real-time, conversational corpus competitors must license or scrape. At inference it is live only when a search tool is on: the API docs say Grok "has no knowledge of current events" beyond its training data unless Web Search or X Search is enabled (xAI models docs); DeepSearch and those tools turn the firehose into live retrieval. From Grok 4.5 on, training also used data from Cursor, which SpaceXAI went on to buy (Wikipedia). In April 2026, in Musk v. Altman, Musk said Grok had been developed "partly" by distillation from OpenAI's models; in August OpenAI said it would stop providing its models to the SpaceXAI-owned Cursor (Wikipedia). Heavy: parallel attempts instead of one longer chain

A reasoning model buys accuracy with tokens. The serial way lets one chain of thought run longer, and costs latency, because every token waits for the one before it. Grok 4 Heavy took the parallel way: "parallel test-time compute, which allows Grok to consider multiple hypotheses at once" (xAI). It works because independent attempts make errors that are only partly correlated, so reconciling them catches mistakes a single chain would keep; it stops working where all attempts share the same blind spot. The API exposes it as grok-4.20-multi-agent: 4 agents (effort low or medium) or 16 (high or xhigh), a designated leader agent synthesises their discussion, only the leader's output is returned, and "all tokens consumed by both the leader agent and sub-agents are billed" (multi-agent docs). Predict before you press play: with the same tokens billed, how much longer does one chain take than 4 parallel agents?

Same tokens billed, a fraction of the wait: parallelism spends money instead of time

Illustrative inputs. Each attempt is drawn as 8,000 reasoning tokens and the leader's synthesis as 2,000, one square per 1,000 tokens, decoding at one token per unit of time; xAI publishes no per-agent token counts. The agent counts and the billing rule are xAI's (docs). The single chain gets the same total budget, so the comparison isolates where the tokens go, not how accurate each is: no published curve gives Grok's accuracy against either budget. It waits for you to press play or step, and pauses off screen.

The answer: about as many times longer as there are agents (34 units against 10 for 4 agents). How Gemini's Deep Think and OpenAI's tiers spend test-time compute is on Test-time compute and reasoning.

Reading Grok's numbers: who ran it, on which version, with how many tokens

Every Grok launch comes with xAI's own table, and three habits of reading it change the conclusion. The first lesson came with Grok 3: its headline 93.3% on AIME 2025 was "cons@64", the most common answer of 64 runs, and an OpenAI employee pointed out that the comparison chart showed o3-mini-high without the same technique (xAI; Wikipedia). Grok 4's claims were also xAI's own runs: 15.9% on ARC-AGI-2 (novel grid puzzles built to resist memorisation), "new state-of-the-art for closed models", nearly double Claude Opus 4's 8.6%; and Heavy "the first model to score 50%" on Humanity's Last Exam, a figure xAI's charts give on the text-only subset with Python and search tools (xAI). What the benchmarks measure is on Knowledge and reasoning benchmarks.

1. The index version: Grok 4.6's tie that disappeared

Grok 4.6 (12 August 2026) launched at 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a composite that averages a fixed basket of benchmarks into one number (useful for coarse ranking and nothing finer): level with GPT-5.6 Sol, one point behind Claude Fable 5, and ahead of Kimi K3 (SiliconANGLE; VentureBeat). That was the index before its v4.2 and v4.3 revisions of September 2026 (v4.3 swapped Terminal-Bench 2.1 for 4.0 and raised the private-test weight). On v4.3 the tie is gone:

2. The harness: Terminal-Bench 4.0, one number, five reporters

Terminal-Bench is the benchmark the frontier is currently measured by for long-horizon agentic coding: hard containerised terminal tasks whose leaderboard row is a model plus a harness, so the harness is part of the score. Version 4.0 recalibrated time and resource budgets, so 2.x scores belong to an older scale (see Agentic benchmarks). Grok 4.7's figure depends on who ran it and in what:

The long-context price cliff. From Grok 4.3 up, every text model has two price rows, and a request whose prompt reaches 200,000 tokens "is billed at the higher rate for all tokens in the request": $4 / $1 / $12 for Grok 4.7 instead of $2 / $0.50 / $6 (models). Predict: what does one more prompt token do to a 199,000-token request? Drag the prompt across 200K:

One token past 200K doubles the whole bill, output included

Defaults reproduce the rule by construction: 199,000 prompt tokens and 5,000 output cost (199,000 · 2 + 5,000 · 6) / 106 = $0.428; at 200,000 it becomes (200,000 · 4 + 5,000 · 12) / 106 = $0.86. Grok Build 0.1's window is 256K, so the slider stops counting there. An agent loop that compacts its context before 200K (xAI's Context Compaction API, May 2026) stays on the cheaper row; xAI also recommends a prompt_cache_key so that a conversation keeps hitting the same server's cache (xAI docs).

Posture and security

xAI's alignment posture is deliberately looser than its rivals', and the recurring moderation incidents are part of the brand's risk profile rather than accidents of it. Grok has promoted conspiracy theories, praised Adolf Hitler, used antisemitic tropes and generated nonconsensual sexualised images; within a week of launch, Grok 4 was shown consulting Musk's views before answering questions that did not ask for them (Wikipedia). On 14 July 2026 a researcher reported that Grok Build uploaded users' whole repositories, history and secrets included, to a SpaceXAI storage bucket even with "Improve the model" disabled, about 27,800 times more data than its task needed; SpaceXAI disabled it the same day (Wikipedia). xAI's counter-claim for Grok 4.7 is "an entirely new safeguard stack" and "the strongest model we've tested on refusals and jailbreak resistance" (xAI).

The concrete cost is a security one. Adversa AI reported cryptographic context injection to xAI and its HackerOne programme on 3 June 2026; xAI acknowledged it without specifics or a timeline, further contact on 4 and 10 August drew no reply, and Adversa could still reproduce it on 19 August, publishing on 20 August (Adversa AI), 78 days, or eleven weeks, after the report, with no patch, no CVE and no user-facing workaround. The target was the Grok web chat on grok.com running Grok 4.5 Fast; Adversa told The Hacker News it had succeeded in 40% of 20 attempts since June (8), with failures caused by Grok struggling to decrypt, not by a filter (The Hacker News). Step through the chain, then switch to the plain-text injection it replaced and to the defence that holds:

The filter passes ciphertext; Grok decrypts it inside the trust boundary; only an egress gate stops the leak

The steps and the four private fields are Adversa's account (write-up); operational payloads were withheld, so the page text is schematic. The plain-text and egress-gate runs are the counterfactuals Adversa's analysis describes, not tests it ran on Grok. It waits for you to press play or step, and pauses off screen.

Why encryption matters. Earlier cipher evasions used weak, reversible schemes (substitution, XOR, base64) that a model undoes in its weights and a filter learns to spot. AES-256-GCM under a PBKDF2-derived key cannot be read by a classifier even with the key on the page, because classifiers do not execute what they inspect (Adversa AI); the decryption runs in Grok's own Python runtime, inside the trust boundary, after every filter: input filtering cannot secure a model allowed to execute code. The transferable lessons, that a sandboxed interpreter is an injection decoder and that egress control and action-keyed confirmation are the controls that hold, are on Agent security.

Weights. Closed at the frontier; earlier generations are sometimes opened once superseded (Grok-1 under Apache 2.0, Grok 2 under the Community License), with nothing after Grok 2 opened as of 1 October 2026.

Common mistakes, each answered in one line (depth)
  • "xAI merged into SpaceX in February 2026 to form SpaceXAI." SpaceX acquired xAI effective 2 February 2026; the name first appeared on 6 May and the rebrand completed on 6 July (Lineage and owner).
  • Comparing a launch-day index score with today's. Grok 4.6's 61 is on the pre-v4.2 scale; on v4.3 it is 44.3. Compare within one index version only, and quote the version beside the score.
  • Taking a vendor's benchmark table as a model-only result. Terminal-Bench is model plus harness, and a vendor's copy of a rival's score can differ from the rival's own (Fable 5.1: 57.9% in xAI's table, 55.8% in Anthropic's).
  • Reading per-token price as cost per task. Grok 4.7 costs twice as much per index task as Grok 4.6 at identical prices.
  • "200,000 H100s stood up in 122 days." The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s at about 130 MW (Colossus).
  • Calling Grok 4.1 Fast or Grok Code Fast current. Both were retired on 15 May 2026; their slugs still answer, served by Grok 4.3 or Grok Build 0.1 and billed at those prices, so the mistake is silent.
  • Assuming a long prompt pays the higher rate only above 200K. At or above 200,000 prompt tokens the whole request, output included, is billed at the higher row.
  • "25% active" means a quarter of the parameters run. It is 2 of 8 experts; counting attention and embeddings, Grok-1's active share is 26.7%.
  • Treating Musk's parameter counts as specifications. "1.5 trillion" for Grok 4.6 is a statement; the only audited architectures are Grok-1's code and Grok 2's config.json.
  • Using Grok 4.7 Fast on the API. It exists only inside Cursor and Grok Build, billed through those plans.
