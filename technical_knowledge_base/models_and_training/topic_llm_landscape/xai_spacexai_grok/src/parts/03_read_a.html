What xAI is

xAI is the frontier lab behind Grok, founded by Elon Musk and eleven researchers in March 2023 ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}). Since February 2026 it has been a subsidiary of SpaceX, and since July 2026 it trades as SpaceXAI (Owner and name). Its strategy is the least algorithmic and the most industrial of any frontier lab.

Where DeepSeek optimises cost per token and Ai2 optimises reproducibility, xAI optimises the rate at which it turns capital into training compute and then into shipped models. It has published very little method: one open architecture (Grok-1), one open checkpoint of Grok 2, and launch posts. What it has demonstrated is that a datacentre built in months, plus a willingness to spend reinforcement-learning compute at pretraining scale, is enough to stay among the top five labs without an obvious architectural edge. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (read 1 October 2026) its best model, Grok 4.7, scores 46.4, and SpaceXAI is the fifth lab by best model, behind Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Meta ({{AA leaderboard|@aalb}}). Its two durable differentiators are the real-time X firehose as a data source and a looser alignment posture than any other frontier lab (Training, Posture and security).

One question runs through the page: how does xAI turn capital into compute into shipped models, and what do its published numbers really say about where Grok stands? Owner and name: four different dates

Two descriptions of the same event circulate: "xAI merged into SpaceX in February 2026 to form SpaceXAI", and "acquired in February, renamed SpaceXAI in July". The primary sources separate four events, and once they are separated the two descriptions stop disagreeing about the facts; only "to form SpaceXAI in February" is wrong.

2 Feb 2026: the legal event
SpaceX's S-1 registration statement: X.AI Holdings Corp. "was acquired by SpaceX, effective February 2, 2026 (the 'xAI Merger')", and elsewhere "We acquired xAI in February 2026 as the foundational platform for our AI segment" ({{S-1|@s1}}). It was an all-stock deal that made xAI a wholly owned subsidiary, valuing SpaceX at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion, $1.25 trillion combined ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}). The filing still calls the subsidiary "xAI" (X.AI Holdings LLC). So "merged" (the filing's own label) and "acquired" (its own verb) are both right.
6 May 2026: the name first appears
In announcing its compute deal with Anthropic, "the company formerly known as xAI referred to itself as 'SpaceXAI'", the first use The Verge had seen ({{The Verge|@verge}}).
6 July 2026: the rebrand completes
New logo and X handle, under SpaceX ({{Business Insider|@bi}}). Wikipedia: "rebranded as SpaceXAI in July" ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}). SiliconANGLE on 12 August: "known as xAI until last month" ({{SiliconANGLE|@sa46}}).
8 July 2026: first model under the name
Grok 4.5, released two days after the rebrand, is the first Grok launched as SpaceXAI's ({{MarkTechPost|@mtp45}}; {{xAI release notes|@xdrn}}). Grok 4.7 is therefore not the first release under the new name, only the latest. xAI's docs now also call May's Grok Build 0.1 "SpaceXAI's coding model", but the release notes are edited in place, so that wording is not evidence of the name in May.
Resolution. February 2026 is when SpaceX bought xAI; July 2026 is when xAI became SpaceXAI. The current legal name is SpaceXAI LLC (its site footer, and {{Wikipedia|@wsx}}); the Grok brand, the x.ai domain and the xAI API kept their names. The same filing records the earlier combination: xAI acquired X Holdings, owner of the X social network, effective 28 March 2025 (the "X Merger"), which is why the X firehose is in-house data rather than a licence ({{S-1|@s1}}). SpaceX then listed on Nasdaq in June 2026 ({{SiliconANGLE|@sa46}}), and in August SpaceXAI completed a $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Cursor, the AI code editor, closing on 14 August ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}).

Colossus: compute as the strategy

The lab's core claim is construction speed. Training one model across tens of thousands of accelerators needs them behind one high-bandwidth fabric, with the power and cooling to run them together, so what is hard is building and networking the site, not buying the GPUs. The S-1 gives the only audited figures: the first Colossus cluster in Memphis, about 100,000 H100 GPUs and about 130 MW, came online in 122 days in the shell of an existing factory; the first Colossus II cluster, about 110,000 GB200s and about 210 MW, in 91 days; its second cluster, 110,000 GB300s and 220 MW, in 64 days. The next phase is to add at least 220,000 GB300s and over 400 MW, and the two sites together provide about 1.0 GW today. Against those it sets "an industry benchmark to bring online a 100 megawatt greenfield data center", approximately two years ({{S-1|@s1}}).

Defaults reproduce the S-1's figures by construction (they are its inputs); the rates are derived as megawatts divided by days, so 130 / 122 = 1.07 MW a day for the first cluster against 100 / 730 = 0.14 for the benchmark. A greenfield site is not a repurposed factory, so the first comparison flatters Colossus; the 64-day second cluster at an existing site is the fairest like-for-like.

A correction. "Roughly 200K H100s stood up in 122 days" merges two numbers. The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s ({{S-1|@s1}}); by Grok 3's launch xAI called Colossus a "200,000 GPU cluster" ({{xAI|@g3}}), and Grok 4's post says "Colossus, our 200k GPU cluster" ({{xAI|@g4}}). Wikipedia dates full operation to December 2024 and peak demand to 150 MW, met partly by 14 portable methane generators while the grid agreement was worked out ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}). Expansion continued toward the "at least 1 million" GPUs xAI announced with a third Memphis building on 30 December 2025, for nearly 2 GW ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}); the S-1's 1.0 GW is what is running.

The twist: the capacity is also sold. In May 2026 SpaceX signed cloud services agreements giving Anthropic compute across Colossus and Colossus II for $1.25 billion a month through May 2029, terminable by either side on 90 days' notice, while "Grok 5 ... is currently being trained at COLOSSUS II" ({{S-1|@s1}}). A lab whose strategy is owning compute is now also renting the surplus to a rival, which is the clearest sign that the build rate has outrun what its own training needs. The gas turbines at Colossus 2 are the subject of a Clean Air Act suit filed in April 2026 ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}). How a cluster's fabric and power decide what can be trained on it is on {{Topic: hardware|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}}.

Grok-1: the one published architecture

Grok-1 was previewed in November 2023 as "the best we could do with 2 months of training" ({{Wikipedia|@wg}}); its pretraining concluded in October 2023, on a custom stack of JAX and Rust. On 17 March 2024 xAI released its base weights and architecture under Apache 2.0: "a 314 billion parameter Mixture-of-Experts model with 25% of the weights active on a given token" ({{xAI|@g1os}}). In a mixture of experts (MoE), each layer's feed-forward block is split into several experts and a small router sends each token to only a few, so total parameters (what you store) and per-token compute (what you pay to run) decouple. Grok-1 has 8 experts per layer and runs 2 ({{run.py and model.py|@g1gh}}). Grok 2 followed as open weights in August 2025 (Hugging Face calls it Grok 2, Wikipedia Grok 2.5), not under Apache 2.0 but under the xAI Community License: research and non-commercial use, and commercial use only within xAI's acceptable use policy ({{licence|@g2lic}}). The pattern holds: open the previous generation once it is commercially spent, with no architecture published for anything after Grok 2.

The released code is enough to count the parameters. Every number below is computed from the configuration in the code, not typed in:

N=L(2dnqdh+2dnkvdh+E·3df+dE)+2Vd
L
layers (64)
d
model width (6,144)
nq, nkv, dh
query heads (48), key-value heads (8, grouped-query attention), head size (128)
E, f
experts per layer (8) and each expert's hidden size (32,768, from int(8·6144)·2//3); a gated feed-forward has 3 matrices
dE
the router
V
vocabulary (131,072); 2Vd counts the input embedding and the output head

Active parameters use the same sum with E replaced by the 2 experts that run. The key-value cache per token is 2 · L · nkv · dh · bytes.

Try: switch to Grok 2 and look at the active share: a dense feed-forward that every token runs, beside the experts, takes it from about a quarter to over 40%. Then raise the context to 128K: at 8 key-value heads the cache is 256 KiB a token, a sixth of what all 48 heads would need.

Defaults reproduce xAI's "314 billion" independently, within 1%: 316.5B with a separate output head (315.7B if tied). They do not reproduce "25% active": the sum gives 84.6B, 26.7%, because 25% is just 2 of 8 experts and ignores the attention and embeddings every token uses. For Grok 2, xAI publishes no parameter count; its {{config.json|@g2cfg}} gives 269.5B total and 114.9B active (derived here), and at 16 bits 269.5B is 502 GiB, which reproduces the model card's "approximately 500 GB" checkpoint independently ({{Grok 2 card|@g2}}). The card asks for 8 GPUs with more than 40 GB each.
