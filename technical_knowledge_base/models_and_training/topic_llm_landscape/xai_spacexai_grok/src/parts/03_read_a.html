The idea in one screen

xAI's edge is industrial, not algorithmic: it turns capital into training compute faster than any other lab (a Colossus cluster online in 64 to 122 days), spends that compute on reinforcement learning at pretraining scale and on parallel attempts at test time rather than on a published architecture, and ships a new Grok every month or two at a low price per token, so its models are best read by how much compute and how many tokens went in, and its numbers by who ran them.

xAI was founded by Elon Musk and eleven researchers in March 2023 ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}); since February 2026 it has been a subsidiary of SpaceX, and since July 2026 it trades as SpaceXAI. Where DeepSeek optimises cost per token and Ai2 reproducibility, xAI optimises the rate at which capital becomes compute and compute becomes shipped models. It has published little method (one open architecture, Grok-1; one open checkpoint, Grok 2; launch posts), yet a datacentre built in months plus RL compute at pretraining scale keep it among the top five labs without an obvious architectural edge: on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (read 1 October 2026) its best model, Grok 4.7, scores 46.4, and SpaceXAI is the fifth lab by best model, behind Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Meta ({{AA leaderboard|@aalb}}). Its two durable differentiators are the real-time X firehose as data and a looser alignment posture than any other frontier lab. Colossus: compute as the strategy

The lab's core claim is construction speed. Training one model across tens of thousands of accelerators needs them behind one fabric with the power and cooling to run together, so the hard part is building and networking the site, not buying GPUs. The S-1 gives the only audited figures, against its own yardstick: "an industry benchmark to bring online a 100 megawatt greenfield data center" of approximately two years ({{S-1|@s1}}).

Each Colossus build went faster than the last, and every one beat the greenfield benchmark several times over

S-1 figures: the first Colossus cluster in Memphis, about 100,000 H100 GPUs and about 130 MW, online in 122 days in the shell of an existing factory; the first Colossus II cluster, about 110,000 GB200s and about 210 MW, in 91 days; its second, 110,000 GB300s and 220 MW, in 64 days. Next: at least 220,000 more GB300s and over 400 MW. The two sites provide about 1.0 GW today.

Defaults reproduce the S-1's figures by construction (they are its inputs); rates are megawatts divided by days, so 130 / 122 = 1.07 MW a day for the first cluster against 100 / 730 = 0.14 for the benchmark. A greenfield site is not a repurposed factory, so the first comparison flatters Colossus; the 64-day second cluster at an existing site is the fairest like-for-like. How a cluster's fabric and power decide what can be trained on it is on {{Topic: hardware|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}}.

Correction: "roughly 200K H100s stood up in 122 days" merges two numbers
The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s ({{S-1|@s1}}); by Grok 3's launch xAI called Colossus a "200,000 GPU cluster" ({{xAI|@g3}}), and Grok 4's post says "Colossus, our 200k GPU cluster" ({{xAI|@g4}}). Wikipedia dates full operation to December 2024 and peak demand to 150 MW, met partly by 14 portable methane generators while the grid agreement was worked out ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}). Expansion continued toward the "at least 1 million" GPUs announced with a third Memphis building on 30 December 2025, for nearly 2 GW ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}); the S-1's 1.0 GW is what is running.

The twist: the capacity is also sold. In May 2026 SpaceX signed cloud services agreements giving Anthropic compute across Colossus and Colossus II for $1.25 billion a month through May 2029, terminable by either side on 90 days' notice, while "Grok 5 ... is currently being trained at COLOSSUS II" ({{S-1|@s1}}). A lab whose strategy is owning compute now rents the surplus to a rival: the build rate has outrun its own training needs. The gas turbines at Colossus 2 are the subject of a Clean Air Act suit filed in April 2026 ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}).
