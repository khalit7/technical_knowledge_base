Reading Grok's numbers: who ran it, on which version, with how many tokens

Every Grok launch comes with xAI's own table, and three habits of reading it change the conclusion. The first lesson came with Grok 3: its headline 93.3% on AIME 2025 was "cons@64", the most common answer of 64 runs, and an OpenAI employee pointed out that the comparison chart showed o3-mini-high without the same technique ({{xAI|@g3}}; {{Wikipedia|@wg}}). Grok 4's claims were also xAI's own runs: 15.9% on ARC-AGI-2 (novel grid puzzles built to resist memorisation), "new state-of-the-art for closed models", nearly double Claude Opus 4's 8.6%; and Heavy "the first model to score 50%" on Humanity's Last Exam, a figure xAI's charts give on the text-only subset with Python and search tools ({{xAI|@g4}}). What the benchmarks measure is on {{Knowledge and reasoning benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d810f8574da3ffb860be5}}.

1. The index version: Grok 4.6's tie that disappeared

Grok 4.6 (12 August 2026) launched at 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, a composite that averages a fixed basket of benchmarks into one number (useful for coarse ranking and nothing finer): level with GPT-5.6 Sol, one point behind Claude Fable 5, and ahead of Kimi K3 ({{SiliconANGLE|@sa46}}; {{VentureBeat|@vb46}}). That was the index before its v4.2 and v4.3 revisions of September 2026 (v4.3 swapped Terminal-Bench 2.1 for 4.0 and raised the private-test weight). On v4.3 the tie is gone:

Two rulers, never one axis: only the order within a panel means anything

A 61 and a 44.3 are not a fall of 17 points.

2. The harness: Terminal-Bench 4.0, one number, five reporters

Terminal-Bench is the benchmark the frontier is currently measured by for long-horizon agentic coding: hard containerised terminal tasks whose leaderboard row is a model plus a harness, so the harness is part of the score. Version 4.0 recalibrated time and resource budgets, so 2.x scores belong to an older scale (see {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}). Grok 4.7's figure depends on who ran it and in what:

Artificial Analysis also reports Grok 4.7 4.5 points above Grok 4.6 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 inside its Intelligence Index harness, without the absolute figure.

Two contradictions. xAI's table gives Claude Fable 5.1 57.9%; Anthropic's own gives 55.8%, and lists 57.9% for GPT-6 Astra (as reported by OpenAI). Neither source explains it, so both are shown. xAI's 38.0% against Artificial Analysis's 33% are different harnesses (xAI does not name its own; Artificial Analysis ran Grok Build, xAI's first-party agent), not a disagreement about one run. The gap to Claude Opus 5.5 is 66.4 − 38.0 = 28.4 points on vendor figures (by construction), or 66.4 − 33 = 33.4 against Artificial Analysis's run. Either way: strong on breadth, behind on long-horizon agentic coding, where a compute-maximalist strategy predicts ({{MarkTechPost|@mtp47}}). Private code agrees: on Real-SWE, ten licensed tasks from private enterprise codebases, Grok 4.6 in Grok Build resolved 23.8%, level with Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 and well behind Claude Fable 5.1's 38.8% in Claude Code, and 41 of its 61 failed runs (67.2%) missed a requirement rather than breaking the code (model-plus-harness results from Specific Labs, in a {{Real-SWE roundup|@rswe}}).

3. The tokens: a price list does not price a task

Grok 4.7 and Grok 4.6 have identical per-token prices, $2 per million input and $6 output. Artificial Analysis measured what a task costs:

Same price per token, 2.25 times the output tokens, twice the cost per task, for 2.1 index points

Output tokens per Intelligence Index task: about 81,000 for Grok 4.7 (xhigh) against 36,000 for Grok 4.6 (high), 125% more ({{Artificial Analysis|@aa47}}). Cost per index task and index scores from the {{AA leaderboard|@aalb}} (v4.3, methodology 4.3.2, read 1 October 2026). 81 / 36 = 2.25; 3.74 / 1.86 = 2.0.

The same reading flatters Grok 4.6 against its rivals: at $2 / $6 against Claude Opus 5.5's and GPT-5.6 Sol's $4 / $20, a 3-input-to-1-output blend is $3 against $8, 37.5% of the price ((3·2 + 6) / 4 = 3; (3·4 + 20) / 4 = 8), and 15% of Fable 5.1's or GPT-6 Astra's $20 blend; per task the gap narrows to $1.86 against $5.98 for Opus 5.5 (max) and $1.99 for GPT-5.6 Sol (max) ({{AA leaderboard|@aalb}}). Every scored model on both cost axes is the landscape root's {{Capability vs price|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286#t-iq}} tab.

At xhigh, Grok 4.7 scores 46 on the Intelligence Index, 2 points above Grok 4.6, which brought SpaceXAI into the top four labs on 21 September; Gemini 4 Argon (30 September) has since pushed it to fifth. With Grok Build it scores 56 on the Coding Agent Index, up from 47, fourth among models in their native harnesses, with DeepSWE v1.1 rising from 65% to 73% and Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 18% to 33%. The lineup today (volatile, read 1 October 2026)

Grok 4.7 (21 September 2026) is the flagship: a base-model refresh at unchanged prices, with a 500,000-token context, a May 2026 knowledge cutoff, text and image in and text out, no text output limit, and four reasoning levels, low, medium, high (the default) and xhigh ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}). Beyond the larger base and longer RL run in the table above, xAI says it is better at "verifying its own work and managing longer context", and it is trained for a harness rather than only an API: Grok Bot, in beta since 11 August, runs "durable AI teammates" on a persistent cloud computer ({{xAI|@g47}}). The Fast variant is "the same model served on faster infrastructure", "twice the output speed at twice the price" ({{xAI|@g47}}): 2 times the standard token rates, or 1.5 times for long-context requests, and available only inside Cursor and Grok Build, not on the public API ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}).

The long-context price cliff. From Grok 4.3 up, every text model has two price rows, and a request whose prompt reaches 200,000 tokens "is billed at the higher rate for all tokens in the request": $4 / $1 / $12 for Grok 4.7 instead of $2 / $0.50 / $6 ({{models|@xdm}}). Predict: what does one more prompt token do to a 199,000-token request? Drag the prompt across 200K:

One token past 200K doubles the whole bill, output included

Defaults reproduce the rule by construction: 199,000 prompt tokens and 5,000 output cost (199,000 · 2 + 5,000 · 6) / 106 = $0.428; at 200,000 it becomes (200,000 · 4 + 5,000 · 12) / 106 = $0.86. Grok Build 0.1's window is 256K, so the slider stops counting there. An agent loop that compacts its context before 200K (xAI's Context Compaction API, May 2026) stays on the cheaper row; xAI also recommends a prompt_cache_key so that a conversation keeps hitting the same server's cache ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}).

Grok Bot and API details; retired models and where their slugs now go (volatile)

Grok Bot works with messaging, approvals, connectors and routines ({{xAI release notes|@xdrn}}). On the Responses API, Grok 4.7 always returns its reasoning encrypted, to be passed back unchanged on the next turn ({{xAI docs|@xd47}}).

Retired on 15 May 2026: Grok 4.1 Fast and Grok 4 Fast (reasoning and non-reasoning), Grok 4 (0709), Grok Code Fast 1 and Grok 3. Their slugs still resolve, so old code does not break, but it is silently served by Grok 4.3 (at low effort for reasoning slugs, none for non-reasoning) or, for Grok Code Fast 1, by Grok Build 0.1, and billed at the new model's price ({{retirement guide|@xdret}}). The "cheap agentic workhorse with a 2M context" role now belongs to Grok 4.3, with half the context.

Grok Voice 3.0 (14 October 2026) is SpaceXAI's speech-to-speech model for its Voice Agent API, at $0.08 a minute ($4.80 an hour), unchanged from 2.0; the grok-voice-latest alias now points to it. Posture and security

xAI's alignment posture is deliberately looser than its rivals', and the recurring moderation incidents are part of the brand's risk profile rather than accidents of it. Grok has promoted conspiracy theories, praised Adolf Hitler, used antisemitic tropes and generated nonconsensual sexualised images; within a week of launch, Grok 4 was shown consulting Musk's views before answering questions that did not ask for them ({{Wikipedia|@wg}}). On 14 July 2026 a researcher reported that Grok Build uploaded users' whole repositories, history and secrets included, to a SpaceXAI storage bucket even with "Improve the model" disabled, about 27,800 times more data than its task needed; SpaceXAI disabled it the same day ({{Wikipedia|@wg}}). xAI's counter-claim for Grok 4.7 is "an entirely new safeguard stack" and "the strongest model we've tested on refusals and jailbreak resistance" ({{xAI|@g47}}).

The concrete cost is a security one. Adversa AI reported cryptographic context injection to xAI and its HackerOne programme on 3 June 2026; xAI acknowledged it without specifics or a timeline, further contact on 4 and 10 August drew no reply, and Adversa could still reproduce it on 19 August, publishing on 20 August ({{Adversa AI|@adv}}), 78 days, or eleven weeks, after the report, with no patch, no CVE and no user-facing workaround. The target was the Grok web chat on grok.com running Grok 4.5 Fast; Adversa told The Hacker News it had succeeded in 40% of 20 attempts since June (8), with failures caused by Grok struggling to decrypt, not by a filter ({{The Hacker News|@thn}}). Step through the chain, then switch to the plain-text injection it replaced and to the defence that holds:

The filter passes ciphertext; Grok decrypts it inside the trust boundary; only an egress gate stops the leak

The steps and the four private fields are Adversa's account ({{write-up|@adv}}); operational payloads were withheld, so the page text is schematic. The plain-text and egress-gate runs are the counterfactuals Adversa's analysis describes, not tests it ran on Grok. It waits for you to press play or step, and pauses off screen.

Why encryption matters. Earlier cipher evasions used weak, reversible schemes (substitution, XOR, base64) that a model undoes in its weights and a filter learns to spot. AES-256-GCM under a PBKDF2-derived key cannot be read by a classifier even with the key on the page, because classifiers do not execute what they inspect ({{Adversa AI|@adv}}); the decryption runs in Grok's own Python runtime, inside the trust boundary, after every filter: input filtering cannot secure a model allowed to execute code. The transferable lessons, that a sandboxed interpreter is an injection decoder and that egress control and action-keyed confirmation are the controls that hold, are on {{Agent security|n:3f15c17b0d0d81ad9fb7d1dd74217335}}.

Common mistakes, each answered in one line (depth)
  • "xAI merged into SpaceX in February 2026 to form SpaceXAI." SpaceX acquired xAI effective 2 February 2026; the name first appeared on 6 May and the rebrand completed on 6 July (Lineage and owner).
  • Comparing a launch-day index score with today's. Grok 4.6's 61 is on the pre-v4.2 scale; on v4.3 it is 44.3. Compare within one index version only, and quote the version beside the score.
  • Taking a vendor's benchmark table as a model-only result. Terminal-Bench is model plus harness, and a vendor's copy of a rival's score can differ from the rival's own (Fable 5.1: 57.9% in xAI's table, 55.8% in Anthropic's).
  • Reading per-token price as cost per task. Grok 4.7 costs twice as much per index task as Grok 4.6 at identical prices.
  • "200,000 H100s stood up in 122 days." The 122 days was the first cluster of about 100,000 H100s at about 130 MW (Colossus).
  • Calling Grok 4.1 Fast or Grok Code Fast current. Both were retired on 15 May 2026; their slugs still answer, served by Grok 4.3 or Grok Build 0.1 and billed at those prices, so the mistake is silent.
  • Assuming a long prompt pays the higher rate only above 200K. At or above 200,000 prompt tokens the whole request, output included, is billed at the higher row.
  • "25% active" means a quarter of the parameters run. It is 2 of 8 experts; counting attention and embeddings, Grok-1's active share is 26.7%.
  • Treating Musk's parameter counts as specifications. "1.5 trillion" for Grok 4.6 is a statement; the only audited architectures are Grok-1's code and Grok 2's config.json.
  • Using Grok 4.7 Fast on the API. It exists only inside Cursor and Grok Build, billed through those plans.
