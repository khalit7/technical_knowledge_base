Grok-1: the one published architecture

Grok-1 is a 314-billion-parameter mixture of experts with 8 experts per layer, of which 2 run for each token; xAI calls that "25% of the weights active on a given token" ({{xAI|@g1os}}; {{run.py and model.py|@g1gh}}). It was previewed in November 2023 as "the best we could do with 2 months of training" ({{Wikipedia|@wg}}), pretrained to October 2023 on a custom JAX and Rust stack, and released on 17 March 2024 as base weights plus architecture under Apache 2.0. Grok 2 followed as open weights in August 2025 (Hugging Face calls it Grok 2, Wikipedia Grok 2.5) under the xAI Community License: research and non-commercial use, and commercial use only within xAI's acceptable use policy ({{licence|@g2lic}}). The pattern: open the previous generation once it is commercially spent; nothing after Grok 2 has a published architecture. Checks against xAI's figures (depth)

Defaults reproduce xAI's "314 billion" independently, within 1%: 316.5B with a separate output head (315.7B if tied). They do not reproduce "25% active": the sum gives 84.6B, 26.7%, because 25% is just 2 of 8 experts and ignores the attention and embeddings every token uses. For Grok 2, xAI publishes no parameter count; its {{config.json|@g2cfg}} gives 269.5B total and 114.9B active (derived here), and at 16 bits 269.5B is 502 GiB, which reproduces the model card's "approximately 500 GB" checkpoint independently ({{Grok 2 card|@g2}}). The card asks for 8 GPUs with more than 40 GB each.

RL at pretraining scale is the method. xAI on Grok 4: "we utilized Colossus, our 200k GPU cluster, to run reinforcement learning to refine Grok's reasoning abilities at a pretraining scale", with infrastructure and algorithmic work that "increased the compute efficiency of our training by 6x" ({{xAI|@g4}}). It is the concrete form of the industry's 2025 realisation that post-training is a second scaling axis rather than a finishing step: very large volumes of rollouts on tasks whose outcome can be checked (maths, code, tool use), scored by checking the outcome, which is why reasoning and agentic behaviour improved faster than raw knowledge. The algorithms are on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}. The often-repeated claim that Grok 4 spent as much compute on RL as on pretraining comes from a chart in the launch stream, not from the text, and is unconfirmed here.

Scale and cadence are the rest. Colossus 1 and 2, expanding toward a million GPUs, with the fastest release cadence of any lab in 2026, and a stated roadmap of "multi-trillion parameter models" ({{S-1|@s1}}).

The data edge. Owning X since the March 2025 merger gives a real-time, conversational corpus competitors must license or scrape. At inference it is live only when a search tool is on: the API docs say Grok "has no knowledge of current events" beyond its training data unless Web Search or X Search is enabled ({{xAI models docs|@xdm}}); DeepSearch and those tools turn the firehose into live retrieval. From Grok 4.5 on, training also used data from Cursor, which SpaceXAI went on to buy ({{Wikipedia|@wg}}). In April 2026, in Musk v. Altman, Musk said Grok had been developed "partly" by distillation from OpenAI's models; in August OpenAI said it would stop providing its models to the SpaceXAI-owned Cursor ({{Wikipedia|@wsx}}). Distillation itself is on {{Distillation and Small Language Models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81efa2a8c3b3b623eb09}}.

Heavy: parallel attempts instead of one longer chain

A reasoning model buys accuracy with tokens. The serial way lets one chain of thought run longer, and costs latency, because every token waits for the one before it. Grok 4 Heavy took the parallel way: "parallel test-time compute, which allows Grok to consider multiple hypotheses at once" ({{xAI|@g4}}). It works because independent attempts make errors that are only partly correlated, so reconciling them catches mistakes a single chain would keep; it stops working where all attempts share the same blind spot. The API exposes it as grok-4.20-multi-agent: 4 agents (effort low or medium) or 16 (high or xhigh), a designated leader agent synthesises their discussion, only the leader's output is returned, and "all tokens consumed by both the leader agent and sub-agents are billed" ({{multi-agent docs|@xdma}}). Predict before you press play: with the same tokens billed, how much longer does one chain take than 4 parallel agents?

Same tokens billed, a fraction of the wait: parallelism spends money instead of time

Illustrative inputs. Each attempt is drawn as 8,000 reasoning tokens and the leader's synthesis as 2,000, one square per 1,000 tokens, decoding at one token per unit of time; xAI publishes no per-agent token counts. The agent counts and the billing rule are xAI's ({{docs|@xdma}}). The single chain gets the same total budget, so the comparison isolates where the tokens go, not how accurate each is: no published curve gives Grok's accuracy against either budget. The answer: about as many times longer as there are agents (34 units against 10 for 4 agents). How Gemini's Deep Think and OpenAI's tiers spend test-time compute is on the landscape root's {{Test-time compute and reasoning|n:4035c17b0d0d813f9fb2d62cff1f09ef}} tab.
