Post-training: one checkpoint that thinks or not, and a ladder that ships at once

One checkpoint, two modes. From Qwen3, a single checkpoint serves both a fast direct answer and a long chain of thought ({{Qwen3 report|@q3r}}, section 4.3). The caller picks with a chat-template switch, or with /think and /no_think flags in the system or user message; the model follows the last flag it sees and thinks by default. In no-think mode the assistant turn still carries an empty thinking block, so the format is identical in both modes. The behaviour comes from stage 3, thinking mode fusion: continued fine-tuning on a mix of thinking and non-thinking data, each marked with its flag, the thinking data generated by the stage-2 model itself through rejection sampling.

The thinking budget. The caller caps the thinking tokens. When the chain reaches the cap, the serving code halts it and inserts a fixed instruction, "Considering the limited time by the user, I have to give the solution based on the thinking directly now.", then the closing think tag, and the model answers from the partial reasoning. The report says this ability "is not explicitly trained but emerges naturally" from thinking mode fusion; accuracy on its mathematics, coding and STEM evaluations rose smoothly with the budget, up to the 32K-token budgets plotted. Reasoning depth becomes a runtime dial, which matters because reasoning tokens are the dominant cost and latency term in an agentic loop and most requests do not need them. How other labs expose the same dial is on {{Topic: llm-landscape (test-time compute)|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

A budget below what the model wants cuts the chain and forces an answer; above it, nothing happens

With a 1,024-token budget, a chain that wanted 3,000 tokens stops at token 1,024 and answers from about a third of its reasoning; at 4,096 the chain ends on its own and the budget never binds. Chain lengths are illustrative; the inserted text is the report's.

The split. One set of weights serving both modes appears to leave quality on the table at the top end, presumably because the two behaviours want different post-training distributions; the trade is operational simplicity (one deployment, one KV cache, one set of weights in memory) against peak quality in each mode. Alibaba acknowledged it: the Qwen3-2507 refresh shipped separate Instruct (non-thinking) and Thinking checkpoints at 235B-A22B, 30B-A3B and 4B, between 21 July and 5 August 2025 ({{Instruct-2507|@i2507}}, {{Thinking-2507|@t2507}}). The split persists at the top: the open Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B is thinking-only and text-only ("thinking cannot be disabled"), while the hosted Qwen3.8-Max adds vision input, non-thinking modes and built-in tools ({{2.4T card|@c24}}). Flash-Next and 27B think by default but can switch it off per request, with a reasoning_effort of low, medium or xhigh ({{Flash-Next card|@fn}}).

Strong-to-weak distillation: why the whole ladder ships the same day

The Qwen3 report builds the five small dense models (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B) and Qwen3-30B-A3B by distilling from Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B in two phases instead of four stages: off-policy, fine-tuning on outputs the teachers generated in both /think and /no_think modes; then on-policy, where the student generates its own responses and its next-token distribution on them is pulled toward the teacher's by minimising the KL divergence, so it learns from the teacher's whole distribution at every position rather than one correct token. On Qwen3-8B, from the same off-policy checkpoint (55.0 and 42.8 on AIME'24 and '25), on-policy distillation reached 74.4 and 65.5 on AIME'24 and '25 for 1,800 GPU hours, where RL reached 67.6 and 55.5 for 17,920: 1,800 ÷ 17,920 = 10.0%, the report's "approximately only 1/10 of the GPU hours" (Table 21). Distillation also raised pass@64 (90.0 to 93.3 on AIME'24); RL did not move it. That ratio makes a family from 0.6B to 235B releasable at once (the same mechanism made R1's distilled models work), at the cost of a ceiling: a student inherits its teacher's limits as well as its skills. {{Distillation and small language models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81efa2a8c3b3b623eb09}} owns on-policy distillation and puts this result beside the others; the {{Qwen3 Technical Report|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a19006e6b096a6e14b}} paper page reruns Table 21 as a toy.

The data behind it. Qwen3 was pretrained on about 36T tokens in 119 languages and dialects (Qwen2.5: 29), in three stages: over 30T general tokens at 4,096-token sequences; about 5T higher-quality tokens weighted to STEM, code, reasoning and synthetic data; and hundreds of billions of tokens at 32,768-token sequences for long context ({{Qwen3 report|@q3r}}, section 3). Much of the specialised data is synthetic: Qwen2.5-VL transcribed PDF-like documents, and Qwen2.5, Qwen2.5-Math and Qwen2.5-Coder synthesised trillions of tokens of textbooks, question-answer pairs, instructions and code. Each release is a data generator for the next, particularly where correctness can be checked automatically and bad samples filtered out. Qwen3.5 extended coverage to 201 languages and dialects ({{Qwen3.5 card|@c35}}).
