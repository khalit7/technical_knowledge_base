Alibaba: Qwen

Alibaba's Qwen model line: why its models are built the way they are, and the evidence Qwen published for each choice. Every figure links to its source; data as of 1 October 2026. Reading: 23 min, plus 11 in closed details.

Qwen competes on coverage, a full ladder of sizes and modalities in every generation, and pays for it with one loop: an experimental model tests an idea, the flagship adopts it, the mainline inherits it, and the small models are distilled from the result. Since Qwen3-Next (September 2025) every idea in that loop has bought capacity without paying for it per token.

  1. Gated DeltaNet (green), three layers in four: a fixed-size state, not a growing cache. From Qwen3-Next, September 2025.
  2. Gated attention (blue): softmax over every key, a sigmoid gate per head, head dimension 256, rotary position on a quarter of the dimensions. Flash-Next (August 2026) swaps it for QSA, which reads 2,048 selected tokens.
  3. MoE after every mixer: 512 experts, 10 routed plus 1 shared (Qwen3: 128, top-8, no shared expert).
  4. The block repeats 12 times in Qwen3-Next and Flash-Next, 15 in Qwen3.5-397B, 16 in Qwen3.8-27B (dense feed-forward), 23 in Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B (config.json).

You need: Mixture of experts (routing, shared experts), Beyond attention (linear attention and the delta rule), Attention variants (GQA, DSA and other trained sparse attention) and Long context (KV-cache sizing). This page says what Qwen chose and why; those pages teach the mechanisms.

New terms

A-notation
"235B-A22B": 235 billion parameters stored, 22 billion used per token. Memory is priced on the first, decode speed on the second; their ratio is the sparsity ratio (about 11 times here).
Shared expert
An expert every token passes through regardless of the router, beside the top k routed experts.
Gated DeltaNet (GDN)
Qwen's linear-attention layer: before writing a value it erases what the state stores under that key (the delta rule), and it decays the whole state by a learned per-token gate, so memory per request does not grow with context.
Hybrid layout
A stack that interleaves linear-attention and full-attention layers (Qwen: three to one).
Gated attention, QSA
Qwen's softmax attention with a sigmoid gate on each head's output; Qwen Sparse Attention, where a small indexer scores micro-blocks of tokens and attention reads only the selected ones.
Thinking budget, snapshot id
A caller-set cap on chain-of-thought tokens; a date suffix (Qwen3.8-Max-0902) naming one post-trained state of a model whose name stays fixed.

The strategy: breadth, and a licence per checkpoint

Where DeepSeek optimises cost per served token and Moonshot optimises frontier capability per training run, Qwen optimises coverage. Every generation ships a ladder from sub-billion models that run on a phone, through mid-size dense models, to a sparse flagship, in several modalities: Coder, VL (vision-language), Audio, Math and Embedding. A Qwen base exists at whatever size and licence a project needs, so Qwen bases became the default starting point for open research and for distillation, including DeepSeek's own R1 distillations. The same models are the paid, hosted option on Alibaba Cloud Model Studio.

The licence is not one licence: check the checkpoint, not the family

Every open checkpoint from Qwen3 to Qwen3.5 was Apache 2.0 (commercial use and redistribution with essentially no conditions), and the mid-size mainline still is: Qwen3.8-27B (model card). Before Qwen3 and at the top after Qwen3.5, it is not:

How the design got here

The order matters because it is the loop in action. A dense ladder at every release from 2023 to 2024 made Qwen bases the default open starting point, under licences that tightened at the largest sizes. Qwen3 (April 2025) put every open checkpoint under Apache 2.0, with hybrid thinking. Qwen3-Next (September 2025) tested the 3:1 hybrid and 512 experts; Qwen3.5 (February 2026) took that layout to flagship scale; Qwen3.6 and 3.7 (April to June 2026) kept the top tier closed. Qwen3.8 (August 2026) open-weighted a Max-class flagship for the first time under a custom licence, shipped the Apache 2.0 Qwen3.8-27B, and previewed Qwen4 with Flash-Next, which now tests QSA the same way. The hosted Max has since moved to dated snapshots.

The MoE layer: from no shared expert to 512 tiny experts

Qwen3: 128 experts, top-8, no shared expert. Qwen3-235B-A22B has 94 layers, each with 128 experts, of which the router picks 8 per token (Qwen3 report, Table 2; config). The report states both departures from its predecessor: "Unlike Qwen2.5-MoE, the Qwen3-MoE design excludes shared experts", and it adopts the global-batch load-balancing loss "to encourage expert specialization". DeepSeek, by contrast, routes 8 of 256 finer experts plus a shared expert that absorbs the common computation. Leaving it out is simpler and adds no fixed cost per token, at the risk that every routed expert relearns general behaviour.

Global-batch balancing: balance over the mix, not over each document

A load-balancing loss (Mixture of experts owns it) is usually computed per micro-batch: a few sequences, often packed from one domain, so a micro-batch of pure code is pushed to spread over experts that should not see code. Qwen computes the same statistics over the whole global batch, synchronised across data-parallel ranks, so a code sequence may route to code experts as long as the diverse mix is balanced.

The same routing costs 4 times the minimum per micro-batch and nothing over the global batch

Predict, then slide: at 100% each domain sends its tokens to its own two experts; towards 0% routing is uniform and the two measures agree. Illustrative toy: 8 experts, four single-domain micro-batches, Pi = fi; the formula is the paper's (eq. 2 and 3), in the depth block.

The evidence (Qiu et al., 2025, up to 42.8B total parameters on 400B tokens): about 0.1 lower pre-training perplexity, about 2 points on benchmarks, much stronger domain specialisation of experts, and under 3% extra latency, because the synchronised statistic is one number per expert. The catch: a router balanced over the corpus can be unbalanced within one sequence, and so on individual devices at serving time.

From Qwen3-Next on: 512 experts, 10 routed plus 1 shared

Qwen3-Next reversed the no-shared-expert choice and pushed granularity much further: 512 experts, 10 routed per token plus 1 shared, each expert's hidden width only 512, and the router's parameters normalised at initialisation so no expert is favoured before training (model card, blog, config). Every later large Qwen keeps the layout: Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next all route 10 of 512 plus 1 shared (Qwen3.5 card, 2.4T card, Flash-Next card). "Qwen has no shared expert" is true of the Qwen3 generation only.

A token now lights 2.1% of a layer's experts instead of 6.25%, and each expert is bigger

One square is one expert in one layer, area to scale between the two layouts; blue squares are the routed experts this token uses, green the shared expert. 10 of 512 routed is 1.95% (2.1% counting the shared expert) against 8 of 128, 6.25%: that is what lets total parameters grow far faster than per-token compute. The routing pattern is random, as an illustration; counts and parameters come from each config.json.

Where the parameters live. Each expert is a SwiGLU block with three matrices (gate, up, down), so Pexpert = 3dmodeldff, stored L × E × Pexpert and active L × k × Pexpert (E and k count the shared expert).

For Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B: 3 × 8,192 × 2,048 = 50,331,648 per expert, × 513 experts (512 routed plus 1 shared of the same width) × 92 layers = 2.375T, reproducing the 2.4T headline (config). Experts are 97 to 99% of every Qwen MoE; the worked examples are in the depth block.

Flash-Next's 21x is the most aggressive ratio among the flash-tier models on Mixture of experts, though not in the open frontier as a whole: DeepSeek V4 Pro sits near 33x and Kimi K3 near 27x at flagship scale. What 6B active buys is the absolute figure, since 6B active costs roughly what a 6B dense model costs to decode. High sparsity still costs memory for every expert, all-to-all traffic, harder balancing and harder fine-tuning, because each expert sees fewer tokens; the shared expert's return answers the last: at 512 experts each routed expert sees about one fiftieth of the token stream (10/512), and an always-on expert keeps the common computation in one well-trained place.

Attention: the 3:1 hybrid of Gated DeltaNet and gated attention

A linear-attention layer keeps a fixed-size state per head instead of a growing cache, so its memory per request is constant and its cost linear in sequence length; the price is lossy recall of early tokens, which a few full-attention layers buy back. Beyond attention owns the mechanism (the gated delta rule St = αtSt−1(I − βtktkt⊤) + βtvtkt⊤ of Yang et al., 2024, with a worked demo of why a write that replaces beats one that piles up). What Qwen chose:

  • The ratio. Qwen3-Next has 48 layers as 12 × (3 × (Gated DeltaNet → MoE) → 1 × (Gated Attention → MoE)): 36 linear and 12 full. Qwen's blog says that at 3:1 "the model consistently outperforms any monolithic architecture", all-attention or all-linear (Qwen3-Next blog).
  • The output gate. In Flash-Next the DeltaNet output gate is a sigmoid where the original Gated DeltaNet uses SiLU; the paper reports "consistent improvements" (Flash-Next architecture paper, eq. 6 to 11). The Qwen3.8-27B and 2.4T configs still list output_gate_type: swish (27B config, 2.4T config); Flash-Next's lists sigmoid (config).
  • The interval, everywhere since. Qwen3.5-397B has 60 layers, 15 full; Qwen3.8-27B 64, 16 full (its feed-forward layers are dense, so it is "dense" in the MoE sense only); Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B 92, 23 full; Flash-Next 48, 12 of them sparse attention.
With one full-attention layer in four, the Gated DeltaNet hybrid beats all-full attention by 3.9 points

Average of 9 base-model benchmarks (MMLU, MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, MATH, GSM8K, BBH, MMMLU, EvalPlus, MultiPL-E), unweighted. Each model is a 28-layer 25B-A3B MoE on the Qwen3.5 architecture, trained on 400B tokens at 4K and then 80B at 32K; the sliding-window (SWA) layers use a 128-token window. The GDN hybrid beats full attention on 8 of the 9 benchmarks and the SWA hybrid on 7 (Flash-Next paper, Table 1: 49.87, 51.15, 53.81).

Why not more linear layers. Recall through the state is lossy; the retrieval and copying behaviours agents depend on travel through the full-attention layers, which the paper calls "particularly important for long-context performance". Those layers keep their rotary embedding: a variant without positional encoding (NoPE) looked the same in pre-training, but after post-training showed "a substantially higher rate of endless generation" (Flash-Next paper).

Qwen's full-attention layer: gated attention

  • An output gate: each head's output is multiplied by σ(Wgxt) before the output projection. Qwen's own NeurIPS 2025 paper found it removes the attention sink (attention on the first token fell from 46.7% to 4.8%) and the massive activations that go with it, and improves stability and long-context extrapolation (Qiu et al., 2025); Attention variants owns sinks and gates, with the full numbers.
  • Head dimension 256 instead of 128, with few key-value heads: 2 in Qwen3-Next, Qwen3.5 and Flash-Next, 4 in Qwen3.8-27B and the 2.4T model.
  • Rotary embedding on the first 25% of dimensions only (64 of 256, partial_rotary_factor: 0.25 in every hybrid's config); the rest carry no position signal, which the blog credits with better length extrapolation. Partial RoPE is taught on Positional encodings.

What the cache costs. Only the full-attention layers grow a cache. Qwen3-235B (94 full layers, 4 KV heads of 128) stores 188 KiB per token, 47 GiB for one request at 262,144 tokens; Qwen3-Next stores 24 KiB per token, 6 GiB, plus 36 MiB of fixed DeltaNet states, which are cheaper than the cache they replace beyond 512 tokens. Across the 3.8 generation: Qwen3.5-397B 30 KiB, Qwen3.8-27B 64 KiB, Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B 92 KiB, half of what Qwen3-235B needs for ten times the parameters. The arithmetic is in the depth block; every Qwen checkpoint is a preset in the KV-cache calculator.

What it bought, as Qwen reported it (Qwen3-Next blog, vendor figures on Qwen's own serving stack):

  • Training. Qwen3-Next was pretrained on 15T tokens, a uniformly sampled subset of Qwen3's 36T, with less than 80% of the GPU hours of Qwen3-30B-A3B and 9.3% of the compute cost of the dense Qwen3-32B, and outperformed Qwen3-32B-Base on most benchmarks.
  • Throughput against Qwen3-32B. Prefill nearly 7 times at 4K context and over 10 times beyond 32K; decode nearly 4 times at 4K and over 10 times beyond 32K.

QSA and the Qwen4 preview: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next was released on 26 August 2026 as an explicit preview of the Qwen4 architecture, to give developers "an opportunity to prepare for the full Qwen4 model family" (TechNode). It keeps the 3:1 layout and changes what the quarter of global layers do (Flash-Next card, Flash-Next architecture paper). The model is pretrained with ordinary full attention there; at continued pretraining (256K-token sequences) those layers, and the one in the MTP module, are replaced by Qwen Sparse Attention, two passes per query:

  1. A compressed indexer scores micro-blocks. Keys are average-pooled over blocks of r = 4 tokens, and a small multi-query attention (4 query heads, 1 shared key head of 128) scores every block.
  2. Attention reads only what was selected. Attention (24 query heads, 2 KV heads of 256) reads the top ⌈K / r⌉ = 512 blocks, a token budget of K = 2,048, plus the tokens of the last, unfinished block, which are always kept.

Why blocks. The indexer's own cost. DeepSeek's DSA scores individual tokens, and "as sequence length increases, the overhead of its O(n²) indexer remains non-negligible"; compressing keys fourfold cuts indexer work and top-k selection by about the compression ratio, from O(n2) to O(n2/r). Switch the animation above to "Hybrid + QSA" to watch both passes. With QSA, Qwen joins DeepSeek (DSA, then CSA), Moonshot (KDA) and MiniMax (MSA) in shipping a trainable sparse or linear attention in an open-weight line; Zhipu's GLM-5.3-Flash, released the same week, pools every four indexer keys into one in the same way (Zhipu: GLM; MarkTechPost comparison).

Trained in two phases (indexer alone on about 2B tokens, then indexer and backbone together on about 200B), the language-modelling loss stays within about 10−4 of the full-attention run.

Results against full attention (same model, Flash-Next paper, Tables 2 to 4): QSA matches or beats full attention on 7 of 8 short-context benchmarks (average 75.9 to 76.8), and does better as context grows: RULER beyond 512K from 90.08 to 93.00; 8-needle MRCR from 30.66 to 40.53 at 512K and from 20.71 to 26.44 at 1M. The MTP draft's mean accepted length is unchanged (4.06 against 4.07).

Worked example at T = 1,000,000 (Flash-Next's extended context): the indexer scores 1,000,000 ÷ 4 = 250,000 blocks; attention reads 2,048 tokens instead of 1,000,000, about 488 times fewer. The measured speedup of the attention module at 1M is 7.6 times for prefill and 4.9 times for decode, not 488, because the indexer still scans every block and selection and gathering cost time (kernel-level measurements; speedups start at 64K).

The cache does not shrink

Every key must stay available for the indexer to choose from, so Flash-Next still stores 12 × 2 × 2 × 256 × 2 bytes, 24 KiB per token, plus the indexer's keys. QSA cuts reads and arithmetic, not storage. And the selection is hard: a block the indexer scores low is never read, however much it mattered.

The other Qwen4-preview changes: capacity off the accelerator

  • The n-gram embedding is looked up at layer 2 with a contextual gate. Its address is known from the input tokens alone, so the table can live in host memory and be prefetched while layer 1 runs: capacity at negligible per-token compute and no accelerator memory. A larger n-gram vocabulary lowered loss monotonically while downstream accuracy saturated, one of several places where the paper found loss and benchmarks disagreeing.
  • A gated residual stream widened to 4 branches, read through a sigmoid gate and written back with one scalar per branch, instead of hyper-connections' 4 × 4 mixing matrix.
  • Muon for the two-dimensional weight matrices, AdamW for the rest; the full run had no loss spike, without qk-clip or other activation clipping.
  • Also: a vision encoder, and 262,144 tokens of native context, extensible to 1,000,000.

The headline. The base model was compared with the previous generation's 397B-A17B flagship, which the paper's Table 11 names as Qwen3.7-Plus, a closed, hosted model (not the open Qwen3.5-397B-A17B of the same size). Flash-Next leads on 8 of 14 benchmarks and trails on the other 6 by at most 2.6 points, with a third of the activated parameters, a third of the training tokens and about a ninth of the training FLOPs. It also beats Qwen3.8-27B-Base on all 14.

Base models, pre-training evaluation, the paper's own pipeline. The ninth of the FLOPs is the paper's figure; Qwen3.7-Plus's token count is unpublished, so it cannot be recomputed.

It shipped the same week as Zhipu's GLM-5.3-Flash (320B total, 18B active; TechNode) and Tencent's Hy4 preview (770B total, 49B active; Hugging Face): the open frontier competes on active-parameter efficiency and attention sparsity rather than total parameter count.

Post-training: one checkpoint that thinks or not, and a ladder that ships at once

One checkpoint, two modes. From Qwen3, a single checkpoint serves both a fast direct answer and a long chain of thought (Qwen3 report, section 4.3). The caller picks with a chat-template switch, or with /think and /no_think flags in the system or user message; the model follows the last flag it sees and thinks by default. In no-think mode the assistant turn still carries an empty thinking block, so the format is identical in both modes. The behaviour comes from stage 3, thinking mode fusion: continued fine-tuning on a mix of thinking and non-thinking data, each marked with its flag, the thinking data generated by the stage-2 model itself through rejection sampling.

The thinking budget. The caller caps the thinking tokens. When the chain reaches the cap, the serving code halts it and inserts a fixed instruction, "Considering the limited time by the user, I have to give the solution based on the thinking directly now.", then the closing think tag, and the model answers from the partial reasoning. The report says this ability "is not explicitly trained but emerges naturally" from thinking mode fusion; accuracy on its mathematics, coding and STEM evaluations rose smoothly with the budget, up to the 32K-token budgets plotted. Reasoning depth becomes a runtime dial, which matters because reasoning tokens are the dominant cost and latency term in an agentic loop and most requests do not need them. How other labs expose the same dial is on Topic: llm-landscape (test-time compute).

A budget below what the model wants cuts the chain and forces an answer; above it, nothing happens

With a 1,024-token budget, a chain that wanted 3,000 tokens stops at token 1,024 and answers from about a third of its reasoning; at 4,096 the chain ends on its own and the budget never binds. Chain lengths are illustrative; the inserted text is the report's.

The split. One set of weights serving both modes appears to leave quality on the table at the top end, presumably because the two behaviours want different post-training distributions; the trade is operational simplicity (one deployment, one KV cache, one set of weights in memory) against peak quality in each mode. Alibaba acknowledged it: the Qwen3-2507 refresh shipped separate Instruct (non-thinking) and Thinking checkpoints at 235B-A22B, 30B-A3B and 4B, between 21 July and 5 August 2025 (Instruct-2507, Thinking-2507). The split persists at the top: the open Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B is thinking-only and text-only ("thinking cannot be disabled"), while the hosted Qwen3.8-Max adds vision input, non-thinking modes and built-in tools (2.4T card). Flash-Next and 27B think by default but can switch it off per request, with a reasoning_effort of low, medium or xhigh (Flash-Next card).

Strong-to-weak distillation: why the whole ladder ships the same day

The Qwen3 report builds the five small dense models (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B) and Qwen3-30B-A3B by distilling from Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B in two phases instead of four stages: off-policy, fine-tuning on outputs the teachers generated in both /think and /no_think modes; then on-policy, where the student generates its own responses and its next-token distribution on them is pulled toward the teacher's by minimising the KL divergence, so it learns from the teacher's whole distribution at every position rather than one correct token. On Qwen3-8B, from the same off-policy checkpoint (55.0 and 42.8 on AIME'24 and '25), on-policy distillation reached 74.4 and 65.5 on AIME'24 and '25 for 1,800 GPU hours, where RL reached 67.6 and 55.5 for 17,920: 1,800 ÷ 17,920 = 10.0%, the report's "approximately only 1/10 of the GPU hours" (Table 21). Distillation also raised pass@64 (90.0 to 93.3 on AIME'24); RL did not move it. That ratio makes a family from 0.6B to 235B releasable at once (the same mechanism made R1's distilled models work), at the cost of a ceiling: a student inherits its teacher's limits as well as its skills. Distillation and small language models owns on-policy distillation and puts this result beside the others; the Qwen3 Technical Report paper page reruns Table 21 as a toy.

The data behind it. Qwen3 was pretrained on about 36T tokens in 119 languages and dialects (Qwen2.5: 29), in three stages: over 30T general tokens at 4,096-token sequences; about 5T higher-quality tokens weighted to STEM, code, reasoning and synthetic data; and hundreds of billions of tokens at 32,768-token sequences for long context (Qwen3 report, section 3). Much of the specialised data is synthetic: Qwen2.5-VL transcribed PDF-like documents, and Qwen2.5, Qwen2.5-Math and Qwen2.5-Coder synthesised trillions of tokens of textbooks, question-answer pairs, instructions and code. Each release is a data generator for the next, particularly where correctness can be checked automatically and bad samples filtered out. Qwen3.5 extended coverage to 201 languages and dialects (Qwen3.5 card).

Global-batch balancing: the loss, and why micro-batches are the problem
LBL=NE∑i=1NEfiPi
NE
number of experts
fi
fraction of the batch's tokens routed to expert i
Pi
the router's average probability for expert i over the same tokens

The loss is smallest (1) when traffic is even. Computed per micro-batch and then averaged, it forces even traffic within a handful of documents. Computing fi and Pi over the global batch, synchronised across all data-parallel ranks, demands balance only over the diverse mix. The extra communication is one number per expert per step (Qiu et al., 2025).

Expert parameters, worked: Qwen3-235B-A22B and Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B
  • Qwen3-235B-A22B: dmodel = 4,096, dff = 1,536 (config), so Pexpert = 3 × 4,096 × 1,536 = 18,874,368. Stored: 94 × 128 × 18.87M = 227.1B, which is 97% of the 235B total. Active: 94 × 8 × 18.87M = 14.2B of the 22B; attention, norms and embeddings make up the rest.
  • Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B: dmodel = 8,192, dff = 2,048, 92 layers, 513 experts stored per layer. Stored: 92 × 513 × 50.33M = 2.375T. Active: 92 × 11 × 50.33M = 50.9B of the 95B: at this scale the attention and DeltaNet layers are a much larger share of per-token compute than in Qwen3.
Qwen's Gated DeltaNet layer, in detail
  • Short causal convolution. Queries, keys and values pass through a short causal convolution and a SiLU before the recurrence (kernel 4 in every config). Queries and keys are L2-normalised.
  • Gate parameterisation. The write strength is βt = σ(Wβxt), and the decay αt is an exponential of a softplus, so it stays in (0, 1). The output is RMS-normalised and multiplied by the output gate.
  • Custom kernels. The recurrence has to run in a chunked parallel form to use tensor cores at all. Qwen's FlashQLA kernels run 2 to 3 times faster forward, and about 2 times backward, than the reference Triton kernels (FlashQLA, via the Flash-Next paper).
  • Two stability changes that arrived with Next. Zero-centred, weight-decayed layer normalisation: the norm's scale is parameterised as an offset from 1 and weight decay is applied to it, so norm weights cannot grow without bound (Flash-Next applies the same norm everywhere). And multi-token prediction (MTP): extra heads predict tokens further ahead and serve as a built-in draft for speculative decoding (the mechanism is on Families and objectives).
Gated attention: what Qwen's paper measured

Qwen's NeurIPS 2025 paper tested the output gate across 30 variants of 15B MoE (2.54B active) and 1.7B dense models trained on up to 3.5T tokens (Qiu et al., 2025). The gate adds non-linearity to the otherwise low-rank value-to-output path; makes the output query-dependently sparse; removes the attention sink, where heads dump weight on the first token because softmax must put its probability somewhere (46.7% of attention across layers went to the first token in the baseline, 4.8% with the gate), and the associated massive activations; and improves training stability, tolerance of higher learning rates and long-context extrapolation.

The cache arithmetic, Qwen3-235B against Qwen3-Next
KV bytes per token=Lfull×2×Hkv×dh×b

Lfull full-attention layers, one key and one value, Hkv key-value heads of dimension dh, b bytes per number (2 for 16-bit). A DeltaNet layer instead holds a fixed Hv × dk × dv numbers per request.

  • Qwen3-235B: 94 × 2 × 4 × 128 × 2 = 192,512 bytes, 188 KiB per token, 47 GiB at 262,144 tokens.
  • Qwen3-Next: 12 × 2 × 2 × 256 × 2 = 24,576 bytes, 24 KiB per token, 6 GiB at 262,144 tokens. Its 36 DeltaNet layers each hold 32 value heads of 128 × 128: 36 × 32 × 128 × 128 = 18,874,368 numbers, 36 MiB at 2 bytes, whatever the context.
  • Crossover. Had those 36 layers been full attention they would add 36 × 2 × 2 × 256 × 2 = 73,728 bytes (72 KiB) per token, so the fixed state is cheaper after 36 MiB ÷ 72 KiB = 512 tokens.
  • Qwen3.5-397B: 15 full layers with 2 KV heads of 256, 30 KiB per token; Qwen3.8-27B: 16 layers with 4 heads, 64 KiB; Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B: 23 layers with 4 heads, 92 KiB.
  • These configurations also set mamba_ssm_dtype: float32. If serving keeps the DeltaNet state in 32-bit (unconfirmed), every state figure doubles; the KV-cache calculator has the toggle.
QSA in detail: the indexer, training, measurement, and the alternatives rejected

Indexer. Keys are average-pooled over blocks of 4 tokens, then normalised. Pooling happens before positional encoding, so tokens with different rotary phases are not averaged together; each block gets one position, its first token's. The indexer's rotary embedding covers 64 of its 128 dimensions. A block's score is the ReLU of the query-key similarity, summed over the 4 heads; only fully observed blocks can be scored.

Training. First only the indexer, for 1,000 steps of 8 sequences of 256K tokens (about 2B tokens), to match full attention's own distribution through a KL loss (summed over heads and max-pooled to blocks, so one salient token is not diluted). Then backbone and indexer jointly with QSA on, for 8,000 steps of 96 such sequences (about 200B tokens); the language-modelling loss stays within about 10−4 of the full-attention run.

Measurement. The 7.6 and 4.9 times speedups at 1M are kernel-level measurements against FlashInfer's paged grouped-query attention, with chunked prefill in 16K chunks at batch 1, and decode at batch 4 with three MTP steps.

IndexShare. The paper compared intra-layer compression with IndexShare, which shares one top-k across adjacent full-attention layers. QSA matched full attention at a quarter of the indexer latency; IndexShare stayed below full attention even at half. In a hybrid the full-attention layers sit three DeltaNet layers apart and resemble each other too little to share indices.

Unconfirmed. A second benefit of blocks is often cited: gathering runs of 4 adjacent tokens suits GPU memory access better than scattered single-token gathers. That is plausible, but the paper does not make the argument, so treat it as unconfirmed.

Flash-Next's gated residual and Muon, in detail
  • Gated residual. Each layer reads the 4 branches through an elementwise sigmoid gate predicted from all branches, with bottleneck rank d/8 = 2,560/8 = 320, and writes its output back to every branch through one data-dependent scalar per branch. The 4 × 4 branch-mixing matrix of hyper-connections was dropped as adding little. The gate decides how the extra capacity is used and supplies the rescaling that keeps training stable.
  • Muon. It runs 8 Newton-Schulz orthogonalisation steps, and fused matrices are split before orthogonalising. The router, the embeddings, the output head and the gated-residual projections stay on AdamW. A refitted scaling law raised the learning rate and batch size and dropped batch-size warm-up, which ended no better and cost 18.8% more optimiser steps (Flash-Next paper).
The 38,912-token figure

The 38,912-token figure sometimes quoted beside the thinking budget is the maximum output length the Qwen3 report allowed on AIME, not a budget setting.

Common misreadings
  • "Qwen has no shared expert." True of Qwen3 only; every large Qwen from Qwen3-Next on routes 10 of 512 experts plus 1 shared.
  • "Qwen is Apache 2.0." True of every open checkpoint from Qwen3 to Qwen3.5, and of the 3.6 and 3.8 mid-size models; not of the pre-Qwen3 flagships, Qwen2.5-3B, or the open 2.4T flagship, Flash-Next and Qwen-Image-2.1, which ship under three different custom licences.
  • "Qwen3.8-27B is a standard dense transformer." Its feed-forward layers are dense, but 48 of its 64 layers are Gated DeltaNet.
  • "The model was trained to respect the thinking budget." The serving code enforces it by inserting a stop-thinking instruction; answering well from truncated thinking emerged from thinking mode fusion.
  • "QSA shrinks the KV cache." It cuts what each query reads, not what is stored.
  • "Sparse attention cuts attention time by the selection ratio." 488 times fewer tokens read at 1M gives 7.6 times (prefill) and 4.9 times (decode), because the indexer pass remains.
  • "Flash-Next was compared with Qwen3.5-397B." The paper's "397B-A17B predecessor" is the closed Qwen3.7-Plus.
  • "Qwen3.8-Max" as one model. It is a series of dated snapshots, and the hosted model and the open 2.4T-A95B checkpoint differ in modalities and modes.
  • Reading an Arena placing as a capability score. It is a preference ranking at one moment.
