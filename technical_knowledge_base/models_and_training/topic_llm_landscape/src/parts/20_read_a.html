The core idea. Every serious lab now ships the same skeleton, a sparse mixture-of-experts model that reasons before it answers and reads about a million tokens of context, so the labs differ on three axes only: where the thinking goes (and who controls it), what a token costs to serve, and how open the release is. On one independent scale the closed labs lead on capability and the open labs on price.

Fig. 1 is the map: each lab's flagship from section 1 on the three axes, each marker named after the child page that owns the lab.

  6. Fully open: weights, data and recipe.   7. Open does the volume, closed takes the money. On Vercel's AI Gateway in August 2026 open weights carried 56% of tokens and 14% of spend (section 4); the dots in each stream are in those proportions, from a schematic source.

You need nothing beyond what an LLM is. How the models are built (attention, mixture of experts, the KV cache) is on {{Topic: llm-architecture|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8172b490dc9f9ac281df}}; this page compares the labs and links there for each mechanism.

New terms on this page (each defined again where it is used):
Intelligence Index: Artificial Analysis's independent composite of ten evaluations; versions do not compare.
Test-time compute: computation spent at inference before answering; a reasoning model is trained to use it.
Total / active parameters: what must sit in memory / what one token multiplies through (they differ in MoE).
Sparsity ratio: total ÷ active; 1 for a dense model.
Open weights / fully open: downloadable weights / weights plus data, code and recipe.
Harness: the code around a model when it is run (prompt, tools, retries, kept state).
Cost per task: the dollars a task really costs, thinking tokens and caching included.

1. Who leads, and on what

The closed frontier is a near tie between three labs, and how far behind open weights sit depends on which version of the ruler is read. On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index v5, rebuilt on 27 October 2026 on a new scale with most of its weight on private test sets, Gemini 4 Carbon leads at 59 (gated to vetted cyber defenders), ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 58 and GPT-6 Astra at 57 (read 2 November 2026). The best open-weight score on v5 is Kimi K3.1 Pro's 46, for a model whose weights were still unreleased on 2 November: 12 points behind the top at v5's launch and 13 once Carbon was added, where on v4.3.2 it had been 3. The best released weights, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 and GLM-5.3, score 32 each ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v5}}). No single model leads every test, though. On independent runs read between 1 October and 2 November 2026 the lead splits by kind of work, as the table below shows (dated; it changes monthly).
