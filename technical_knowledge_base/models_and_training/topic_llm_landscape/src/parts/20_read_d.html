5. How the labs converged on one skeleton

The skeleton in the core idea assembled in under three years, and the order is visible in the {{Release history|#t-time}}: 146 sourced releases from 24 labs, February 2023 to mid-October 2026. Each lab's own line is on its page; this is the order across labs.

  • Mixture-of-experts came first. xAI (Grok-1, November 2023) and Mistral (Mixtral, December 2023) shipped the first MoE rows; DeepSeek and Google followed within two months, and 17 labs had one by September 2026.
  • Reasoning spread fastest. From OpenAI's o1-preview (September 2024) to eleven labs within eight months and 18 by April 2026.
  • Hybrid attention is the newest. From MiniMax-Text-01 (January 2025) to ten labs. Nine labs now have all three in the table, all of them labs that publish weights; the closed US labs disclose too little about architecture to count, which is "not disclosed", not "not used".
Size, pace and licences across the table
  • Total size grew, compute per token did not. The largest open model went from 65.2B (LLaMA) to 2.8T (Kimi K3), about 43-fold, while the median active size of open releases stayed between 13B and 32B a year, because MoE went from 2 of 9 open sized releases in 2023 to 29 of 36 in 2026.
  • The pace and the field grew together. Rows per year went 12, 22, 46 and 66 (2026 to mid-October only); the number of labs releasing in a quarter went from 3 in early 2023 to 20 in 2026 Q3, the busiest quarter (38 rows), and the Chinese open labs (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax, MiMo) release as often as the US closed labs.
  • Open releases got more permissive, and a smaller share. Open weights were 75% of rows in 2023 and 70% in 2025 but 55% in 2026, as the closed labs shipped more often; within open releases, Apache 2.0 or MIT went from 4 of 9 (2023) to 25 of 36 (2026).
How reasoning models formed, 2022 to 2026
  • 2022 to 2023: chain-of-thought prompting, self-consistency (Wang et al.) and STaR (fine-tuning on the model's own correct chains) showed reasoning could be elicited, and trained on.
  • September 2024: o1, the first production model trained with large-scale RL to reason in a hidden chain of thought, with large jumps on AIME, GPQA and Codeforces, made accuracy against inference compute a public curve (OpenAI).
  • January 2025: DeepSeek-R1 replicated it openly, MIT-licensed, with the recipe and distilled 1.5B to 70B variants that seeded an open ecosystem (DeepSeek-R1).
  • 2025: hybridisation: budgets (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5), two modes in one checkpoint (Qwen3), a router (GPT-5); o3, Grok 4, Kimi K2 Thinking, GLM-4.x and MiniMax M1 and M2 shipped reasoning-first. In July Gemini Deep Think's "parallel thinking" solved five of six IMO problems for 35 points (Google DeepMind), and an experimental OpenAI model also reached gold-medal level (Alexander Wei).
  • 2026: absorption: no flagship ships without reasoning, budgets gave way to effort levels, and in September GPT-6 Astra was reported to move part of the budget into recurrent depth.

6. Price per token is not cost per task

List prices are per million tokens, but a task's cost also depends on how many tokens the model spends, thinking included, and how much of the input is a cached prefix re-read at a discount.

Try: an illustrative agent turn re-reads a 200,000-token cached prefix, adds 20,000 new tokens and writes 5,000: $0.22 on Claude Opus 5.5. Untick the cache and the same turn costs $0.98. Click a bar for its arithmetic. Prices: Artificial Analysis, 1 October 2026, from each lab's own API.

A price can also move with no change to the model: Gemini 3.8 Flash's $1.24 per index task becomes about $2.49 on 1 January 2027, when its token prices double ({{Google DeepMind: Gemini and Gemma|n:3c65c17b0d0d8173a0aafcb27cf7a5f3}}). Each lab's own bill mechanics (OpenAI's 272K surcharge, Anthropic's cache economics) are on its page.

7. Reading the numbers

Most published figures are the labs' own. A number means something only with its harness (the code around the model when it is run: prompt, tools, retries, state kept between requests), its benchmark version, and who ran it. Labs' own figures tend to run higher than independent reruns of the same test: Terminal-Bench 4.0 gives Claude Opus 5.5 66.4% in Anthropic's table and 59.6% from Artificial Analysis; Grok 4.7 38.0% in xAI's table, 37.58% on the official Terminal-Bench board (read 4 October 2026, {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}), 33% inside xAI's own Grok Build harness and 25.8% inside Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index harness (Artificial Analysis). GPT-6 Astra is the exception, 57.9% from OpenAI against 59.1%. The {{Benchmarks|#t-bench}} tab shows the independent figure and keeps the lab's in each cell's detail. The extreme case is the same weights in two harnesses: GPT-6 Astra on ARC-AGI-3 scored 62.7% through ARC Prize's standard harness and 99.9% through its Provider Adapter, which keeps Astra's hidden reasoning state ({{OpenAI: GPT family|n:3c65c17b0d0d814abf90d5b9e567d72f}}).

Common misreadings across labs

Reading total parameters as cost or quality. Memory follows total parameters, compute per token follows active ones, and neither is a score.

"Sparsest" without saying of what. Three quantities get the label. The ratio sets memory per unit of decode speed: DeepSeek V4 Pro leads at about 33x. The active count sets what a token costs: Step 5 Preview's 27B is the smallest in the frontier band although its 22x ratio is unremarkable, and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's 6B of 125B the smallest among flash models. A phase-dependent model breaks both: DeepSeek V4.1-Flash activates 8B of 552B in prefill (69x) and 16B in decode (35x) (DeepSeek changelog). And Moonshot's "sparsity 48" counts experts (384 / 8), not parameters (about 32x).

Taking a benchmark speed as a property of an MoE model. Routing depends on the input, so throughput does too: a speed describes the model under that prompt mix, batch size and expert placement ({{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}).

Treating open weights as open source. Downloadable weights under a custom licence are not a fully open release; only OLMo and K2 Horizon publish the data and recipe.

Believing a vendor table because it is detailed. Weight the independent reruns, such as ARC Prize's harness and Artificial Analysis' runs.

Comparing test-time-compute results without the budget and the harness. A score is a point on a cost curve; a comparison that omits thinking tokens, sample count or harness compares nothing.

Expecting thinking to add knowledge. More tokens reorganise what the weights and tools already hold. A factual gap needs retrieval or a better base model.

Lab-specific misreadings live with the lab: "Mixtral 8x7B is eight 7B models" on {{Mistral AI|n:3c65c17b0d0d814a9882e6ebc326fa56}}, a tier name taken for an architecture on {{OpenAI: GPT family|n:3c65c17b0d0d814abf90d5b9e567d72f}}, an unchanged token price taken for an unchanged cost on {{Google DeepMind: Gemini and Gemma|n:3c65c17b0d0d8173a0aafcb27cf7a5f3}}.
