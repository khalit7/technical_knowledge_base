3. Axis 2: what a token costs to serve

Weights: total against active parameters

In a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model each layer's feed-forward block is split into many experts and a router sends each token to a few. Total parameters must all sit in memory; active parameters, the ones a token passes through, set the compute per token. "600B/27B" means 600 billion total, 27 billion active; total divided by active is the sparsity ratio, 1 for a dense model. How the router, load balancing and expert parallelism work: {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}.

Why the labs went sparse, and where the ratio settled

At the same active budget, more total parameters buy quality: Mixtral 8x7B (47B total, 13B active) "outperforms or matches Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 across all evaluated benchmarks" (Mixtral paper). The ratio then climbed and settled into a band: Mixtral at 3.6x in 2023, DeepSeek-V3 at 18x at the end of 2024, Kimi K2 at about 32x in 2025 (1.04T over 32B, K2 report), and 2026 frontier-size models between about 16x and 33x, the shaded band above: 2026 moved the band's centre rather than its ceiling. Moonshot's sparsity scaling law shows the pull and K2 stopped at 48 experts per active one for infrastructure reasons ({{Moonshot AI: Kimi|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f5abf5dbdbac821a12}}).

Three things set the ceiling. Memory: every total parameter is stored and paid for at serving time, which is why native low-precision checkpoints matter (Kimi K2 Thinking ships INT4, Kimi K3 MXFP4 weights, gpt-oss MXFP4 experts). Communication: each token is sent to k experts, so traffic between GPUs grows with k. Under-trained experts: each expert sees about k/N of the tokens, so at very large N experts may see too few to specialise; Kimi K3's Stable LatentMoE (896 experts, 16 active, 2 shared) targets this.

How the labs route

A shared expert bypasses the router and runs for every token; fine-grained experts are many narrow experts with a larger k in place of a few wide ones, at a similar active budget.

Two conventions won. Fine-grained experts with a shared one are now the default, though shared experts are a choice rather than settled practice: Qwen3 dropped them and did fine, then Qwen reinstated one from Qwen3-Next on. And DeepSeek-V3's sigmoid router with bias balancing (scoring_func: sigmoid, topk_method: noaux_tc in the configs) has largely replaced softmax routers with heavy auxiliary losses: V3, Kimi K2 and K3, GLM-5.2 and GLM-5.3-Flash all ship it. MoE everywhere above about 130B; dense survives below it, where Mistral Medium 3.5 (128B, card) and Command A (111B, card) are the largest current dense models on the scatter. MoE also reached small models: Gemma 4 26B-A4B (Google), Cohere North Mini Code 30B-A3B (Cohere) and gpt-oss-20b need memory for the whole model but compute for a fraction, which suits a laptop with plenty of unified memory and little compute.

Context: the KV cache

Attention stores a key and a value for every earlier token, the KV cache, and it grows with the context, so a million-token window is first a memory problem: at Mixtral 8x7B's layout it is 128 KiB per token, 128 GiB for one sequence stretched to a million tokens, past one 80GB accelerator (sizing for any model: {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}}). Linear attention replaces the growing cache with a fixed-size state; sparse attention attends to a learned selection of earlier positions. A fixed-size state cannot hold every token exactly, so real designs mix them with some full-attention layers. Every open-frontier lab that publishes its architecture now ships one of them:

4. Axis 3: how open it is

Closed
reachable only through an API
Open weights
the trained weights can be downloaded, under a licence that may be permissive (MIT, Apache 2.0) or restrictive (non-commercial, revenue thresholds)
Fully open
weights plus training data, code and recipe, which independent study of training needs. "Open source" in the strict sense means this, not open weights

The US frontier labs are closed; OpenAI and Google also ship small open models (gpt-oss, Gemma), and Meta, once the open-weights standard with Llama, took its frontier line closed with Muse Spark and Muse Wonder, then returned to large open weights in October 2026 with the 816B Muse Spark Open, under a licence with a revenue clause and seven points below the open leaders. Open weights are led from China (DeepSeek, Qwen, Moonshot, Zhipu, MiniMax, Xiaomi, Tencent, StepFun), with Mistral the main Western counterweight (its 1T Large 4 was API-only at launch, weights promised). Only Ai2's OLMo and the Institute of Foundation Models' K2 Horizon are fully open; what a fully open release ships, artefact by artefact, is on {{Other notable providers (Ai2 OLMo)|n:3c65c17b0d0d8154ba44e521a4ed8749#s-olmo}}. Licences now differ checkpoint by checkpoint, even within one family.

Open models do the volume, closed models take the money

On Vercel's AI Gateway in August 2026 open-weight models carried 56% of tokens and 14% of spend (Vercel).
