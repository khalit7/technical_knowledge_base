Google trains one sparse, natively multimodal model line on its own TPUs and distils it downwards: into the Flash and Flash-Lite tiers of the closed Gemini API, and into the open Gemma models built to fit one machine. Since Gemini 2.5 the caller also sets how hard each request thinks, so what a task costs depends more on how many tokens it spends than on the price list.

You need: {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}} (total against active parameters), {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}} (sliding-window layers), {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}} (KV-cache sizing) and {{Distillation and small language models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81efa2a8c3b3b623eb09}}. This page says what Google chose and what it shows; those pages teach the mechanisms.

New terms

Pro, Flash, Flash-Lite
The three Gemini price tiers, largest to smallest. Pro trains at the frontier; Flash and Flash-Lite are smaller models distilled from a larger teacher. Gemini 4 Argon (September 2026) sits above Pro.
Thinking level
thinking_level: minimal, low, medium or high, set per request; it replaced the 2.5 models' token budget.
Thought signature
An encrypted representation of the model's reasoning state that every response carries and multi-turn tool use must send back.
Media resolution
How many tokens an image, video frame or PDF page costs (70 or 280 tokens per frame on Gemini 3).
Effective parameters (E2B, E4B)
Effective 2B and 4B: the parameters that must sit in accelerator memory, not the checkpoint size.
A-notation (26B A4B)
26B total, about 4B active per token: a mixture of experts.
Fairwind Program
Google's vetted-access channel, open to governments, critical infrastructure operators and software maintainers who apply, for models not sold openly.

How the design got here

The order explains the design: each step below added one of the properties in the hero figure.

Two families on one axis: the 2026 Flash cadence against a Pro tier that stayed on one preview
Gemini releaseshutdown or restrictionGemma release

Gemini (closed) above the line, Gemma (open) below. Click or tap a dot to open its dated card under "Every release, dated" below.

  1. Dense, on its own silicon (2022 to 2023). PaLM and PaLM 2 were dense models trained on TPU pods. PaLM's 540B run showed Google could train at frontier scale on its own silicon and orchestration layer, and it was one of the models on which chain-of-thought prompting was first shown to work at scale. After Google Brain and DeepMind merged in 2023, Gemini was built as the successor with two departures designed in: sparsity instead of dense scaling, and multimodality in pretraining instead of bolted on afterwards.
  2. Gemini 1.0 (December 2023) came in Ultra, Pro and Nano; Nano (1.8B and 3.25B parameters) was the on-device model ({{Wikipedia|@wgem}}).
  3. Long context from sparsity (Gemini 1.5, February 2024). A sparse MoE keeps compute per token at the active parameters, so pushing a million tokens through the model stays affordable; native multimodality makes video more tokens in the same window rather than a separate pipeline with its own failure modes. 1.5 Pro launched with a 1M-token window, opened to every developer at 2M tokens in mid-2024, and was reported with "near-perfect retrieval (>99%) up to at least 10M tokens", against 128K (GPT-4 Turbo) and 200K (Claude 3.0) at its rivals ({{Gemini 1.5 report|@g15}}).
  4. Thinking, and distilled tiers (2.0 in December 2024, 2.5 in March 2025). 2.5 Pro made thinking the default, and the 2.5 models gave the caller a thinking budget, a cap on reasoning tokens, which "allows users to trade off performance with cost" ({{Gemini 2.5 report|@g25}}); thinking levels have since replaced it, even on the 2.5 models. The report states the architecture plainly: sparse MoE transformers with native multimodal input, a 1M-token window on Pro and Flash, and Flash and below trained by distillation.
  5. Width instead of depth (Deep Think, July 2025). Several reasoning threads at once, then a selection or combination step: the same budget spent in width. It reached the official gold-medal standard at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad, 35 of 42 points, 5 of 6 problems, end to end in natural language within the 4.5-hour limit ({{Google DeepMind|@imo}}). Parallel exploration buys independence between attempts, which one longer trace does not; the selection step is where it succeeds or fails. The general mechanism is on {{Topic: llm-landscape (Deeper: test-time compute)|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.
  6. Per-request controls (Gemini 3, November 2025). Gemini 3 Pro (18 November 2025) is a sparse MoE transformer with native text, vision and audio input, a 1M-token window and 64K output, trained on TPUs with JAX and ML Pathways ({{model card|@mc3}}). It introduced thinking levels and media-resolution controls in the API; 3 Flash followed on 17 December ({{release notes|@log}}). 3.1 Pro (19 February 2026) replaced 3 Pro and is the current Pro tier, 1,048,576 tokens in and 65,536 out, still a preview more than seven months on.
  7. Flash carries the cadence (2026). Four Flash generations reached general availability in four months: 3.5 Flash (19 May, at Google I/O), 3.6 Flash with 3.5 Flash-Lite (21 July), 3.7 Flash (13 August) and 3.8 Flash (2 September) ({{release notes|@log}}). The gains came partly from spending more test-time compute per task (Works harder), which blurs the tier distinction the price list implies. The frontier model, Gemini 4 Argon, went first to vetted cyber defenders through Fairwind, the same release shape as Anthropic's Mythos class ({{Anthropic: Claude family|n:3c65c17b0d0d814cb979d0eabca59a45}}).
Every release, dated (the cards behind the timeline)
Release notes: what Google and independent evaluators reported for the 2025 and 2026 releases

Gemini 1.5's other memorable result. Given a grammar manual for Kalamang, a language with fewer than 200 speakers, the model learned to translate English to Kalamang at about the level of a person who learned from the same material ({{Gemini 1.5 report|@g15}}).

2.5 and agents. The agentic push dates from here: Project Mariner for browser control, and Jules, an asynchronous coding agent ({{Other coding harnesses|n:3c65c17b0d0d819fa3cefdcd2ccff2f7}}).

Deep Think against formal provers. The year before the IMO gold, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reached silver (28 points) only after experts translated the problems into the formal language Lean, and took two to three days ({{Google DeepMind|@imo}}). Gemini 3 Deep Think followed in December 2025 as a mode in the Gemini app.

3.6 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite (21 July 2026). 3.6 Flash used 17% fewer output tokens than 3.5 Flash, and 3.5 Flash-Lite generated 350 output tokens per second in Artificial Analysis' measurement ({{Google|@b36}}). The same release deprecated the temperature, top_p and top_k sampling parameters across the API ({{release notes|@log}}).

3.8 Flash (2 September 2026). On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (methodology v4.3.2, read 1 October 2026), 3.8 Flash at high thinking scores 40.9 against 39.1 for 3.7 Flash, below GPT-5.6 Sol (47.0) and Grok 4.6 (44.3) ({{Artificial Analysis|@aalb}}). Launch-day index figures, such as The Register's 59 against 56 and "about 40 percent more per task" ({{The Register|@reg}}), were on a version of the index before v4.2 and do not compare with these. Google reports 54.9% on HLE-Verified and gains over 3.7 Flash on DeepSWE v1.1 long-horizon software engineering, Vals Finance Agent V2 and Harvey's legal agent benchmark, and claims it beats most larger frontier models on long-horizon software engineering ({{Google|@b38}}). On Real-SWE, a benchmark of private enterprise code, it resolves 31.2% of tasks, third behind Claude Fable 5.1 (38.8%) and GPT-6 Astra (33.8%), with overlapping 95% confidence intervals ([25%, 38%], [32%, 45%], [27%, 40%]) ({{DEV Community|@dev}}).

3.8 Flash Cyber. The same model tuned for vulnerability detection and remediation, distributed only through Fairwind: 47.2% pass@1 on CWE-Bench against 47.8% for the leading frontier model, and Chrome's security team got 2.6 times more correct vulnerability patches ({{Google|@b38}}).

3.8 Live and Live Extended Thinking (15 September 2026): a tier, not a flagship. Production voice-agent models doing near real-time speech with visual grounding, detecting and switching between 97 languages mid-conversation. Extended Thinking is the one with an idea in it: it reasons and speaks at the same time, filling with verbal cues ("Let me check that") while tool and API calls run in the background instead of going silent. Every other way of spending a test-time budget makes the caller wait; this one covers the latency with speech, a product answer to a systems constraint and the first shipped one. Extended Thinking scored 82.6 on Artificial Analysis' speech-to-speech quality index at launch, first on it, with 68.6% on τ-Voice, 35.1% on Sierra's τ-Voice-banking and 97.7% on Big Bench Audio; all audio output carries a SynthID watermark. Both are in the Gemini API and AI Studio, in private preview in Gemini Enterprise, and behind Search Live, Gmail and Keep. Google publishes no millisecond latency figure ({{Google|@blive}}). The audio side of voice models is on {{Topic: generative-and-multimodal|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ab6ade318917bff55}}.

Gemini 4 Argon (30 September and 13 October 2026). Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, first place on AutomationBench at 51.3%, 91.7% on LVBench video understanding, a tie for first on CWE-bench v1 at 68%, and the lead on the Vals Index. Google names monitoring of the model's internal activations among its safeguards ({{Google|@bargon}}). Argon reached its defenders first without cyber guardrails for them, and became generally available on 13 October 2026 on the paid Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity and Gemini Enterprise, and in the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers; it is not on the free tier ({{VentureBeat|@vbargon}}). Independent readings put it in the frontier band but not alone at the top. Vals AI ranks it first on the Vals Index at 68.90%, ahead of Claude Sonnet 5.5 (67.04%) and Opus 5.5 (66.97%) ({{VentureBeat|@vbargon}}). On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (methodology v4.3.2) it scores 53 at high (52.6 to one decimal, at $1.99 per task at the introductory price), tied with GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 and behind Opus 5.5 (58), Sonnet 5.5 and Meta's Muse Wonder Full (56) ({{Artificial Analysis|@aaargon}}, read 19 October 2026); Epoch AI's early estimate on its Capabilities Index is 165, against 167 for Opus 5.5 and 166 for Astra ({{Epoch AI|@eci}}). It trails on terminal work: Terminal-Bench 4.0 57.4% against Opus 5.5's 66.4% in Google's own table, and 57.58% on Vals AI's fixed mini-swe-agent harness ({{Vals AI|@valstb}}). On ARC-AGI-3 (semi-private set, verified by ARC Prize) it shows how far the harness moves a score: 97.5% through the Provider Adapter, 49.8% through the standard harness at low effort, the best standard-harness score outside OpenAI ({{ARC Prize|@arcargon}}).

Argon in the API (gemini-4-argon). Input, thinking and output share one 1,048,576-token context. A response defaults to 65,536 output tokens; longer ones, up to the 1M limit minus the input, run as background execution in 64K hops. Thinking levels are low, medium (the default) and high; explicit caching is not supported, and temperature, top_p and top_k are ignored ({{API docs|@argondoc}}).
