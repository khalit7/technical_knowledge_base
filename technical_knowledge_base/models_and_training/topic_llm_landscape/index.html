The core idea. Every serious lab now ships the same skeleton, a sparse mixture-of-experts model that reasons before it answers and reads about a million tokens of context, so the labs differ on three axes only: where the thinking goes (and who controls it), what a token costs to serve, and how open the release is. On one independent scale the closed labs lead on capability and the open labs on price.

2. Axis 1: where the thinking goes

Test-time compute is computation spent at inference, deliberating before answering. A reasoning (or "thinking") model is trained to use it well, and every flagship is now one. The labs differ in where the computation goes, who decides how much, and what the caller can see. Thinking is paid for as output tokens, so each design is also a price decision (section 6).

Try: step through the four designs and watch what reaches the caller. Recurrent depth is the only one that leaves no text at all.

The same 10,000-token budget spent both ways, to scale, recurrent depth layer by layer, and the pass@k, voting and best-of-n maths: Test-time compute and reasoning.

The chain of thought is not a faithful window. Models can reach an answer for reasons their stated reasoning does not reflect; recurrent depth is the sharper version, no window at all.

Where it stands in October 2026, and where the scaling stops paying
  • Token efficiency is the competition. At equal accuracy the thinking is the price, so launches lead with "same score, fewer tokens": GPT-5.6 Sol outperforms "with fewer tokens and at lower estimated cost" (OpenAI); a customer at the Opus 5 launch reported "similar performance while generating 26% fewer tokens on average compared to Opus 4.8 at max reasoning" (Anthropic).
  • The harness carries part of the score once deliberation lives outside the transcript (section 7). On the 167 game-reasoning pairs both of ARC Prize's harnesses solved, Astra's Provider Adapter was also about 3.66 times faster and used 49% fewer tokens (ARC Prize).
  • Verification-heavy pipelines hold the top of the hardest benchmarks (HLE, olympiad sets, ARC-AGI): parallel sampling plus a verifier (Deep Think, Grok Heavy), and deep-research products chaining reasoning with search and tools.
  • Open replication is complete. R1's lineage (Qwen3, GLM-5.x, Kimi K3, OLMo 3 Think, MiniMax M3) made RLVR recipes and reasoning datasets public, with full training logs for OLMo; the remaining frontier gaps are scale, data and infrastructure.
  • Overthinking: on some tasks longer reasoning lowers accuracy, across model families (Gema et al.), so "always think harder" is not a safe default.
  • RL sharpens, it does not extend: RLVR models beat their base models at pass@1, but the base models reach more problems at large k (Yue et al.); pretraining still sets the ceiling.
  • The selector caps parallel scaling: best-of-n against a learned reward model saturates and then reverses; a vote stops improving once settled, and gets worse where a wrong answer is the most likely one.
  • The economics are unfavourable by construction: cost and latency grow linearly with thinking tokens, accuracy roughly with their logarithm over a useful range (o1's curves use a log axis, OpenAI) and then flattens, so each point costs more than the last.
  • Monitoring: whether chains of thought stay monitorable enough for oversight is open, and harder as training rewards short traces; an unfaithful trace is a window that distorts, latent deliberation is the endpoint Ryan Greenblatt calls "full-on opaque reasoning architectures with extreme depth" (Greenblatt).

3. Axis 2: what a token costs to serve

Weights: total against active parameters

In a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model each layer's feed-forward block is split into many experts and a router sends each token to a few. Total parameters must all sit in memory; active parameters, the ones a token passes through, set the compute per token. "600B/27B" means 600 billion total, 27 billion active; total divided by active is the sparsity ratio, 1 for a dense model. How the router, load balancing and expert parallelism work: Mixture of experts.

Why the labs went sparse, and where the ratio settled

At the same active budget, more total parameters buy quality: Mixtral 8x7B (47B total, 13B active) "outperforms or matches Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 across all evaluated benchmarks" (Mixtral paper). The ratio then climbed and settled into a band: Mixtral at 3.6x in 2023, DeepSeek-V3 at 18x at the end of 2024, Kimi K2 at about 32x in 2025 (1.04T over 32B, K2 report), and 2026 frontier-size models between about 16x and 33x, the shaded band above: 2026 moved the band's centre rather than its ceiling. Moonshot's sparsity scaling law shows the pull and K2 stopped at 48 experts per active one for infrastructure reasons (Moonshot AI: Kimi).

Three things set the ceiling. Memory: every total parameter is stored and paid for at serving time, which is why native low-precision checkpoints matter (Kimi K2 Thinking ships INT4, Kimi K3 MXFP4 weights, gpt-oss MXFP4 experts). Communication: each token is sent to k experts, so traffic between GPUs grows with k. Under-trained experts: each expert sees about k/N of the tokens, so at very large N experts may see too few to specialise; Kimi K3's Stable LatentMoE (896 experts, 16 active, 2 shared) targets this.

How the labs route

A shared expert bypasses the router and runs for every token; fine-grained experts are many narrow experts with a larger k in place of a few wide ones, at a similar active budget.

Two conventions won. Fine-grained experts with a shared one are now the default, though shared experts are a choice rather than settled practice: Qwen3 dropped them and did fine, then Qwen reinstated one from Qwen3-Next on. And DeepSeek-V3's sigmoid router with bias balancing (scoring_func: sigmoid, topk_method: noaux_tc in the configs) has largely replaced softmax routers with heavy auxiliary losses: V3, Kimi K2 and K3, GLM-5.2 and GLM-5.3-Flash all ship it. MoE everywhere above about 130B; dense survives below it, where Mistral Medium 3.5 (128B, card) and Command A (111B, card) are the largest current dense models on the scatter. MoE also reached small models: Gemma 4 26B-A4B (Google), Cohere North Mini Code 30B-A3B (Cohere) and gpt-oss-20b need memory for the whole model but compute for a fraction, which suits a laptop with plenty of unified memory and little compute.

Context: the KV cache

Attention stores a key and a value for every earlier token, the KV cache, and it grows with the context, so a million-token window is first a memory problem: at Mixtral 8x7B's layout it is 128 KiB per token, 128 GiB for one sequence stretched to a million tokens, past one 80GB accelerator (sizing for any model: Long context). Linear attention replaces the growing cache with a fixed-size state; sparse attention attends to a learned selection of earlier positions. A fixed-size state cannot hold every token exactly, so real designs mix them with some full-attention layers. Every open-frontier lab that publishes its architecture now ships one of them:

4. Axis 3: how open it is

Closed
reachable only through an API
Open weights
the trained weights can be downloaded, under a licence that may be permissive (MIT, Apache 2.0) or restrictive (non-commercial, revenue thresholds)
Fully open

The US frontier labs are closed; OpenAI and Google also ship small open models (gpt-oss, Gemma), and Meta, once the open-weights standard with Llama, took its frontier line closed with Muse Spark and Muse Wonder, then returned to large open weights in October 2026 with the 816B Muse Spark Open, under a licence with a revenue clause and seven points below the open leaders. Open weights are led from China (DeepSeek, Qwen, Moonshot, Zhipu, MiniMax, Xiaomi, Tencent, StepFun), with Mistral the main Western counterweight (its 1T Large 4 was API-only at launch, weights promised). Only Ai2's OLMo and the Institute of Foundation Models' K2 Horizon are fully open; what a fully open release ships, artefact by artefact, is on Other notable providers (Ai2 OLMo). Size, pace and licences across the table
  • Total size grew, compute per token did not. The largest open model went from 65.2B (LLaMA) to 2.8T (Kimi K3), about 43-fold, while the median active size of open releases stayed between 13B and 32B a year, because MoE went from 2 of 9 open sized releases in 2023 to 29 of 36 in 2026.
  • The pace and the field grew together. Rows per year went 12, 22, 46 and 66 (2026 to mid-October only); the number of labs releasing in a quarter went from 3 in early 2023 to 20 in 2026 Q3, the busiest quarter (38 rows), and the Chinese open labs (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM, MiniMax, MiMo) release as often as the US closed labs.
  • Open releases got more permissive, and a smaller share. Open weights were 75% of rows in 2023 and 70% in 2025 but 55% in 2026, as the closed labs shipped more often; within open releases, Apache 2.0 or MIT went from 4 of 9 (2023) to 25 of 36 (2026).
How reasoning models formed, 2022 to 2026
  • 2022 to 2023: chain-of-thought prompting, self-consistency (Wang et al.) and STaR (fine-tuning on the model's own correct chains) showed reasoning could be elicited, and trained on.
  • September 2024: o1, the first production model trained with large-scale RL to reason in a hidden chain of thought, with large jumps on AIME, GPQA and Codeforces, made accuracy against inference compute a public curve (OpenAI).
  • January 2025: DeepSeek-R1 replicated it openly, MIT-licensed, with the recipe and distilled 1.5B to 70B variants that seeded an open ecosystem (DeepSeek-R1).
  • 2025: hybridisation: budgets (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5), two modes in one checkpoint (Qwen3), a router (GPT-5); o3, Grok 4, Kimi K2 Thinking, GLM-4.x and MiniMax M1 and M2 shipped reasoning-first. In July Gemini Deep Think's "parallel thinking" solved five of six IMO problems for 35 points (Google DeepMind), and an experimental OpenAI model also reached gold-medal level (Alexander Wei).
  • 2026: absorption: no flagship ships without reasoning, budgets gave way to effort levels, and in September GPT-6 Astra was reported to move part of the budget into recurrent depth.

7. Reading the numbers

Most published figures are the labs' own. A number means something only with its harness (the code around the model when it is run: prompt, tools, retries, state kept between requests), its benchmark version, and who ran it. Labs' own figures tend to run higher than independent reruns of the same test: Terminal-Bench 4.0 gives Claude Opus 5.5 66.4% in Anthropic's table and 59.6% from Artificial Analysis; Grok 4.7 38.0% in xAI's table, 37.58% on the official Terminal-Bench board (read 4 October 2026, Agentic benchmarks), 33% inside xAI's own Grok Build harness and 25.8% inside Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index harness (Artificial Analysis). GPT-6 Astra is the exception, 57.9% from OpenAI against 59.1%. The Benchmarks tab shows the independent figure and keeps the lab's in each cell's detail. Common misreadings across labs

Reading total parameters as cost or quality. Memory follows total parameters, compute per token follows active ones, and neither is a score.

"Sparsest" without saying of what. Three quantities get the label. The ratio sets memory per unit of decode speed: DeepSeek V4 Pro leads at about 33x. The active count sets what a token costs: Step 5 Preview's 27B is the smallest in the frontier band although its 22x ratio is unremarkable, and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's 6B of 125B the smallest among flash models. A phase-dependent model breaks both: DeepSeek V4.1-Flash activates 8B of 552B in prefill (69x) and 16B in decode (35x) (DeepSeek changelog). And Moonshot's "sparsity 48" counts experts (384 / 8), not parameters (about 32x).

Taking a benchmark speed as a property of an MoE model. Routing depends on the input, so throughput does too: a speed describes the model under that prompt mix, batch size and expert placement (Mixture of experts).

Treating open weights as open source. Downloadable weights under a custom licence are not a fully open release; only OLMo and K2 Horizon publish the data and recipe.

Believing a vendor table because it is detailed. Weight the independent reruns, such as ARC Prize's harness and Artificial Analysis' runs.

Comparing test-time-compute results without the budget and the harness. A score is a point on a cost curve; a comparison that omits thinking tokens, sample count or harness compares nothing.

Expecting thinking to add knowledge. More tokens reorganise what the weights and tools already hold. A factual gap needs retrieval or a better base model.

Lab-specific misreadings live with the lab: "Mixtral 8x7B is eight 7B models" on Mistral AI, a tier name taken for an architecture on OpenAI: GPT family, an unchanged token price taken for an unchanged cost on Google DeepMind: Gemini and Gemma.

Sizes: published size over time

Sizes are in billions of parameters (1T = 1,000B). A dense model uses every parameter for every token, so it has one number. A mixture-of-experts (MoE) model routes each token through a few of many experts, so it has two, total / active: total must sit in memory, active sets the compute per token. Total ÷ active is its sparsity; a dense model's is 1 (Mixture of experts has the mechanism).

Defaults reproduce the rows' own ratios, by construction (same two numbers): DeepSeek-V3 671 ÷ 37 = 18.1, the note's "18x sparsity"; DeepSeek V4 Pro 1,600 ÷ 49 = 32.7, the note's "33x".
