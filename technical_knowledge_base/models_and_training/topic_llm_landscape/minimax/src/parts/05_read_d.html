Post-training: CISPO and what M1's RL run cost

CISPO (Clipped Importance-Sampling Policy Optimization) came with M1 ({{M1 report, arXiv 2506.13585|@pm1}}). {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}} owns it, with its formula and a toy of one rare token through sixteen updates; what MiniMax saw is the part that belongs here. Reflective tokens such as "However", "Recheck", "Wait" and "Aha" fork a reasoning path but get low probability from the base model, so one update can double their probability; with PPO or GRPO's clip their ratio then sits past 1 + ε and their gradient is exactly zero for the rest of the batch (M1 ran 16 off-policy updates per generation batch). DAPO's answer, a higher upper clip bound, was "less effective" in this setup. CISPO clips the importance weight instead, under a stop-gradient: each token's update is sg(min(r, 1 + εIShigh)) · Â · ∇ log π, so no token is silenced. A "Wait" token at r = 2.5, Â = +1, ε = 0.2 (illustrative): PPO weight 0, CISPO weight 1.2. There is no KL penalty.

  • What it bought. On a controlled zero-RL comparison from Qwen2.5-32B-base on AIME 2024, CISPO matched DAPO in 50% of the training steps and beat both GRPO and DAPO at equal steps.
  • The bill. M1's full RL run took three weeks on 512 H800 GPUs, a rental cost of $534,700, a figure few labs publish. The hybrid made the long rollouts cheap: M1 uses less than 50% of DeepSeek-R1's FLOPs at a 64K-token generation length and about 25% at 100K.
  • A precision fix that travels. Training and inference kernels disagreed on token probabilities; computing the language-model head in FP32 raised the correlation between the two from about 0.9 to 0.99 ({{M1 report|@pm1}}).
  • Kept after the reversal. M2's agentic RL kept CISPO with the weight's lower bound set to 0, clip(r, 0, 1 + εIShigh), so tokens that became improbable can be down-weighted aggressively ({{M2 report|@pm2}}, section 6.1.4); the M2.5 line still used it ({{M2.5 card|@km25}}).

The MoE side: from few large experts to many small ones

Every MiniMax model is a mixture of experts ({{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}} owns the mechanism). The layouts moved from 32 large experts with a softmax gate to hundreds of small ones with sigmoid gating (each expert scored independently, so several can be strongly active at once) and a learnable routing bias.

Licences and who pays

The weights are open but the licences are not uniform: Apache 2.0 for M1, a modified MIT licence for the M2 line, and the custom MiniMax Community License for M3 and M3.1 Flash ({{M1 card|@km1}}, {{M2 card|@km2}}, {{M3 card|@km3}}). M3.1 Flash's copy is non-commercial by default: non-commercial use is free; any commercial use, which includes serving a fine-tuned derivative, must display "Built with MiniMax M3.1-Flash" and send MiniMax a one-time notice, needs prior written authorisation above 20 million US dollars of yearly revenue, and military use is prohibited outright ({{M3.1 Flash licence|https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3.1-Flash}}). Revenue does not fund the model line: in 2025 it was $79.0 million (up 158.9%), 67.2% from "AI-native products" (the Talkie and Xingye character apps, Hailuo AI video and MiniMax Agent) and 32.8% from the Open Platform API and enterprise services, more than 70% from outside China, while research and development cost $252.8 million and the adjusted net loss was $250.9 million ({{2025 annual report|@ar}}). The models run on investor capital, now including public markets: MiniMax listed in Hong Kong on 9 January 2026, the day after Zhipu ({{Wikipedia|@wiki}}; {{Zhipu on Wikipedia|@wz}}). Open weights at low prices are how a lab without frontier brand recognition buys attention (the page's reading, not a MiniMax statement).

Depth

MSA's training and FLOP model, in full (the KL loss, the warmup, eq. 12, ablations)

The index branch learns with a KL-divergence loss that pulls its distribution towards the main branch's group-averaged attention on the selected tokens; its inputs are detached, so this loss updates only the two index projections and cannot simplify the main model's attention to lower itself. The indexer warms up under full attention (40B tokens in the paper's runs) before sparsity is switched on. MSA works both from scratch and by converting a full-attention checkpoint.

ℒKL=1NHkv∑i=1N∑r=1HkvDKL(sg(Pi(r))∥Piidx,(r))
Pi(r)
the main branch's attention over the selected tokens, averaged over the G query heads of group r
Piidx,(r)
the softmax of the index scores over the same tokens
sg
stop-gradient; N is the sequence length, Hkv the number of groups

The paper's own count averages over a whole prefill of N tokens, so the causal triangle halves the full-attention and index terms (FLOPs, two per multiply-add; eq. 12 of the {{MSA paper|@pmsa}}):

FGQA=2HqdhN2,FMSA=HkvdidxN2+4HqdhNkBk

In ablations, block size (32, 64 or 128 at the same token budget) made little difference to quality, and a FLOP-matched sliding window had higher perplexity than MSA on agent tasks throughout training.

M3.1 Flash: a model shipped inside a harness first (volatile, dated)

M3.1 Flash reached users in two stages. As M3.1-Flash-Preview (27 September 2026) it ran only inside MiniMax's own coding agent and subscription plan, with no published price, speed, benchmark or parameter count ({{MiniMax docs|@tg}}; {{DataNorth|@dn}}); an earlier version had already served traffic anonymously on OpenRouter as "Space Bunny". On 19 October it reached the API at $0.15 / $0.60 per million ($0.015 cached), with MiniMax claiming about three times the inference speed and 70% lower agentic-task cost than its predecessor, and its own scores of 73.7% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 61.7 on OSWorld V2 ({{MiniMax|@m31f}}). MiniMax published no technical report; the weights followed on 23 October with a model card that gives about 428B total and 23B active (MarkTechPost had read the same from the product page, {{MarkTechPost|@mtp31}}). The Hugging Face widget shows 220.5B because 206.6B of the stored values are bytes that each pack two 4-bit weights: a storage count, not the model's size. The point worth carrying: a model can now ship inside a harness weeks before it is sold per token, and it can be measured independently only from the day it is.

On the same index reading of 1 October 2026, at M3's $0.30 / $1.20 list price DeepSeek V4.1 Flash scored 39.5 (45 for its 1017 checkpoint by 19 October), and Zhipu's GLM 5.3 Flash scored 41.8 at $0.15 / $0.50 ({{leaderboard|@aalb}}; {{Zhipu: GLM|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a88a84c2ca84b4daf4}}); M3 was among the cheapest capable open frontier options at release and is now one of several. StepFun's Step 5 Preview does not undercut M3: it lists at $1.00 / $2.70, and its weights arrived only in mid-October 2026 ({{Other notable providers|n:3c65c17b0d0d8154ba44e521a4ed8749}}).

Misreadings worth catching (each seen in coverage or in this page's sources)
  • "MiniMax-01 has no KV cache." Its 10 softmax layers do: 40 KiB per token.
  • "M2 went back to full attention because linear attention is slow." The stated reasons are quality on multi-hop reasoning and retrieval-style circuits, an evaluation gap that hid it, and immature infrastructure; speed was the attraction, not the problem.
  • "MSA shrinks the KV cache." It shrinks what each query reads; M3's smaller cache comes from 4 KV heads instead of 8.
  • "The 4M-token context was trained." Training reached 1M; 4M is inference extrapolation.
  • "Full attention scales exponentially with context." Quadratically: prefill grows with n2 and each decoded token's reads with n. The phrase appears in launch coverage ({{Fireworks|@fw}}).
  • "MiniMax's apps pay for its models." The apps provide two-thirds of revenue, but 2025 revenue of $79.0 million was under a third of R&D spending.
