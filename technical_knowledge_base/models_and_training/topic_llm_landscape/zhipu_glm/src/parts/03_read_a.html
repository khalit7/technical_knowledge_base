The idea in one screen

GLM is one agentic model assembled from the open frontier's published parts (DeepSeek's routing, latent and sparse attention; Moonshot's optimizer and linear attention), with Zhipu's own engineering added wherever the serving or training bill is, and the weights given away cheaply.

Zhipu AI is a 2019 spin-out of Tsinghua University, founded in Beijing by Tang Jie and branded internationally as Z.ai since July 2025; GLM stands for General Language Model ({{Wikipedia|@wiki}}). Two corporate facts explain much of what follows. The US Commerce Department put it on the Entity List in January 2025, which is why GLM is now trained and served on Chinese-made accelerators. And it listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on 8 January 2026, the first major Chinese LLM company to go public, so market share is the story being sold, which is the context for the pricing ({{Wikipedia|@wiki}}).

The question this page keeps returning to: what does it cost to train and serve an agentic, million-token model, and which of Zhipu's choices moves that cost? One caution for every GLM number: many headline benchmark figures are Zhipu's own runs (Z.ai Code Bench is in-house), and arena placings are human preference votes, not task success.

You need: MLA and DeepSeek Sparse Attention from {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}; linear attention and the delta rule (KDA) from {{Beyond attention|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8142bc9ecac1fc366966}}; total against active parameters and routing from {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}; GRPO, PPO and asynchronous rollouts from {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}. ARC: one checkpoint for three jobs that pull apart

ARC asks one model to be good at agentic behaviour (long-horizon tool use, browsing, staying coherent over hundreds of steps), reasoning and coding ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}). It is a design brief rather than marketing because the three conflict in post-training: reasoning training teaches a model to deliberate, which costs latency and can degrade instruction following; agentic training rewards short, decisive tool calls; coding wants exact, formatted, non-chatty output. The usual answer is a specialised checkpoint per mode, which is expensive for whoever serves them. GLM's answer is to train specialists, then unify:

Two published recipes: specialists merged at the end, or RL stages in sequence pulled back by distillation

GLM-5 ends with distillation because, in the report's words, "sequentially optimizing for distinct objectives can lead to the cumulative degradation of previously acquired capabilities"; the last stage pulls the model back towards what each earlier stage taught. For GLM-5.2, slime ran this on-policy distillation in parallel and merged more than ten expert models into the final model in about two days ({{GLM-5.2 blog|@b52}}). On-policy distillation itself is on {{Distillation and small language models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81efa2a8c3b3b623eb09}}.

Behaviour is selected per request, not per checkpoint. GLM-4.5 and GLM-4.5-Air shipped hybrid thinking and non-thinking modes chosen through the prompt template ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}). The control has since become an effort dial: GLM-5.2 offers High and Max effort ({{GLM-5.2 blog|@b52}}), and GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-Flash take reasoning_effort set to low, high or max, defaulting to max ({{GLM-5.3 card|@c53}}, {{Flash card|@cfl}}); on the hosted Flash API thinking cannot be switched off ({{Z.ai docs|@dfl}}). Effort levels across labs are on the {{landscape root|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

It buys one deployment covering an entire agentic workload. It costs what Alibaba conceded when it split Qwen3-2507 back into separate Instruct and Thinking checkpoints, the Instruct model supporting only non-thinking mode ({{Qwen3-2507 card|@qwen2507}}; {{Alibaba: Qwen|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fd8c36ce06672bb235}}): a unified model gives up a little at the top end of each mode.

The shape: deeper, more heads, then fewer layers for the network

Every GLM flagship since GLM-4.5 is a mixture of experts (MoE), and the ratio of total to active parameters has grown from about 11 to about 18 ({{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}} owns routing and the total-against-active trade). GLM routes with sigmoid gates and loss-free balance routing, a per-expert bias nudged between steps instead of an auxiliary loss, and keeps the first layers dense (three in GLM-4.5, GLM-5 and Flash, one in Air), a pattern shared with DeepSeek ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}, {{Flash config|@cfgfl}}). The usual reasoning, which Zhipu does not spell out: early layers compute generic features every token needs, and routers are least stable early in the network and in training, so routing those layers buys little specialisation and adds risk.

The 744B and 753B parameter counts (provenance)

The report and most coverage give 744B for the GLM-5 generation; the Hugging Face checkpoints of GLM-5 to GLM-5.3 hold 753.3 to 753.9B parameters ({{HF org|@hf}}), and some coverage quotes 753B ({{Labellerr|@lab}}). The sources do not reconcile the two counts. The GLM-4.5 report states its own rule: counts "include the parameters of MTP layers but not word embeddings and the output layer" ({{Table 1|@r45}}); whether GLM-5's 744B follows the same rule is not stated.

Deeper and narrower, with 2.4 times the heads

Against DeepSeek-V3 and Kimi K2, the GLM-4.5 report reduces "the width (hidden dimension and number of routed experts)" and increases the number of layers, "as we found that deeper models exhibited better reasoning capacity" ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}):

GLM-4.5 is half again as deep and about 30% narrower than the two models it was set against

Each block is drawn to scale: one stripe per layer (lighter stripes are the dense layers, MTP excluded), width is hidden size. Numbers from the GLM-4.5 report's Table 1: GLM-4.5 92 layers × 5,120 with 160 experts and 96 heads; DeepSeek-V3 61 × 7,168, 256 experts, 128 heads; Kimi K2 61 × 7,168, 384 experts, 64 heads. 92 / 61 = 1.51; 5,120 / 7,168 = 0.71.

It also uses far more attention heads than the width would conventionally imply: 96 heads of 128 dimensions on a 5,120-wide model, where 5,120 / 128 = 40 is the conventional count, so 96 / 40 = 2.4 times as many (the report rounds this to "2.5 times"). The finding is the interesting part: the extra heads do "not improve training loss compared to models with fewer heads", but "consistently" improve reasoning benchmarks such as MMLU and BBH ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}). Loss is a proxy for capability, and an architecture choice can buy capability the loss curve does not show. The heads did not cost cache, because only the key-value heads are cached:

96 query heads, 8 cached key-value heads: more heads did not grow the cache

The query projection outputs 96 × 128 = 12,288 numbers per token, 2.4 times the hidden size. Grouped-query attention lets each group of 12 query heads share one key-value head, so each layer caches 2 × 8 × 128 = 2,048 numbers per token, and over 92 layers at 16 bits 92 × 4,096 = 376,832 bytes, 368 KiB per token: 46 GiB for one 128K request, the number GLM-5's attention redesign attacks.

Fewer layers again in GLM-5, for the network's sake. GLM-5 doubled the total to 744B, grew to 256 experts and reduced the layer count "to minimize expert parallelism communication overhead" ({{GLM-5 report|@r5}}): every MoE layer sends tokens to the devices holding their experts and back, so each layer removed saves two all-to-all exchanges per step. Flash goes further: against GLM-4.5's similar total (320B against 355B) it nearly halves both the active parameters (18B against 32B) and the layer count (45 against 92), which Zhipu credits to its architecture changes including mHC, four parallel residual streams mixed by learned constrained matrices ({{Z.ai docs|@dfl}}, {{Flash config|@cfgfl}}; mHC is on {{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}}).

MTP: the draft model that ships inside the model

Every GLM since 4.5 carries multi-token prediction (MTP) layers, which double as a built-in draft model for speculative decoding: for a lab whose strategy is price, a free multiple on decode throughput is margin. The acceptance length is the average number of tokens accepted per verification step. The mechanism is on {{Speculative decoding|n:3f35c17b0d0d81e086fde85940ebac1c}}; Zhipu's choices were:

  • GLM-4.5: one MoE layer as its MTP layer "to support speculative decoding during inference" ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}).
  • GLM-5: three MTP layers sharing parameters, so training covers several draft steps without the memory of several layers; acceptance length 2.76 tokens against 2.55 for DeepSeek-V3.2 ({{GLM-5 report|@r5}}).
  • GLM-5.2: IndexShare on the MTP layer as well, the first draft step's KV cache reused in later steps, and rejection sampling. On GLM-5.1's backbone with 7 draft steps the acceptance length rises from 4.56 to 5.47 tokens, 5.47 / 4.56 = 1.20 times ({{GLM-5.2 blog|@b52}}); the changes were measured together, so IndexShare's share alone is not known ({{Raschka|@rasch}}).

Muon, and Muon Split for MLA

GLM has trained with Moonshot's Muon optimizer since GLM-4.5, observing faster convergence and tolerance of larger batches ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}). It buys better loss per token and a smaller optimizer state than AdamW, and costs a few extra matrix products per step and a much shorter safety record. The update itself is on {{Optimisers and learning-rate schedulers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817080bbc90954681d09}}; Moonshot's QK-Clip for the attention-logit growth it causes is on {{Moonshot AI: Kimi|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f5abf5dbdbac821a12}}.

GLM-4.5's Muon settings (depth)

Muon for every parameter except the word embeddings, biases and RMSNorm weights, with 5 Newton-Schulz steps, momentum 0.95 and the update rescaled to an RMS of 0.2 ({{GLM-4.5 report|@r45}}).

GLM-5 added Muon Split because of MLA: MLA's up-projections stack every head's weights into one matrix, and orthogonalising that matrix as a whole forces all heads to move at one shared scale. Muon Split orthogonalises each head's slice separately, so "projection weights for different attention heads" can "update at different scales", which brought MLA's quality up to that of 8-group GQA in Zhipu's ablation ({{GLM-5 report|@r5}}).
