The overtraining bet, in numbers

Llama 3 8B was trained on 15T tokens: 15×1012 / 8×109 = 1,875 tokens per parameter, about 94 times Chinchilla's 20 (Llama 1 7B was at 1012 / 7×109 ≈ 143, about 7 times). Meta's own loss curves show why it kept going: the smallest model was still improving when the run stopped.

The argument is a cost one, with the names of {{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}} (which derives the Chinchilla rule):

C≈6ND,N*=C120,Cserve≈2NT
C
training compute in FLOPs; 6 is two FLOPs per parameter per token forward and four backward
N, D
parameters (for an MoE model, the active parameters per token) and training tokens
N*
the compute-optimal size: 120 is 6 times 20, from substituting D = 20N, so D* = 20N*
Cserve, T
lifetime inference compute over T served tokens; 2 is one multiply and one add per parameter per token

Training is paid once and scales with ND; serving is paid on every token and scales with N alone. Shrinking N and growing D moves cost from the bill that recurs to the bill that does not.

One budget, two shapes

The animation spends Llama 3 8B's training budget twice: as Meta did, and on the Chinchilla-optimal model for the same budget. The loss figures in the last step use Chinchilla's fitted loss law as refitted by Sardana et al. (the explorer below); they are illustrative, since the law was fitted to Chinchilla's data, not Meta's.

The arithmetic behind it: C = 6 × 8×109 × 15×1012 = 7.2×1023 FLOPs; the compute-optimal model for that budget is N* = √(7.2×1023/120) = √(6×1021) ≈ 7.75×1010, a 77.5B model on 20 × 77.5B = 1.55T tokens. Per served token the 8B costs 2 × 8×109 = 1.6×1010 FLOPs and the 77.5B 1.55×1011, a ratio of about 9.7. So the same training budget buys either the lower-loss 77.5B or an 8B nearly ten times cheaper on every token it ever serves; Meta chose the second, because the 8B is good enough for most deployments and runs on one GPU. Serving 15T tokens costs the 8B 2 × 8×109 × 15×1012 = 2.4×1023 FLOPs, a third of its training compute; the bet pays more the more the model is used.

The flagship is the counter-case. The 405B on 15.6T tokens is at 15.6×1012 / 405×109 ≈ 38.5 tokens per parameter, close to compute-optimal, and 6 × 405×109 × 15.6×1012 = 3.79×1025 FLOPs reproduces the report's 3.8×1025 ({{Llama 3 paper|@l3h}}). Meta's own fitted scaling law, run on that budget, predicted "a 402B parameter model on 16.55T tokens" (about 41 tokens per parameter), and Meta trained 405B. The overtraining was in the small models, the ones served at volume; the largest was sized to the budget.

How long should a model be trained once serving counts?

Chinchilla's answer counts training only. Fix the loss you need, then pick the N and D on the curve of equal loss that minimise 6ND + 2NT over a lifetime of T served tokens ({{Sardana et al.|@sard}}). Llama 4's training choices

A pretraining mixture of "more than 30 trillion tokens" of text, image and video across 200 languages (the model card gives about 40T tokens for Scout and 22T for Maverick); what Meta calls early fusion, image tokens from a MetaCLIP-based vision encoder entering the same backbone as text from the first layer, though the encoder itself was trained separately ({{Native multimodal and any-to-any models|n:3c65c17b0d0d8139bd6cd4137dd2a4d8}} sorts out what "early fusion" means in different documents); FP8 pretraining, which for Behemoth on 32K GPUs reached 390 TFLOPs per GPU ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}); MetaP, Meta's method for reliably setting "critical model hyper-parameters such as per-layer learning rates" and initialisation scales so that they transfer across batch size, width, depth and token count; codistillation of Maverick from Behemoth; and iRoPE for the extreme context claim ({{Llama 4 announcement|@l4}}).

MSL-era training details are mostly undisclosed. Muse Spark and its successors have no technical report, the concrete cost of the closed pivot for anyone who used Meta's reports as the field's reference documentation. The one MSL report is for the small open model: Muse Glimmer's model card gave the outline (logit distillation from Muse Spark, agent-focused mid-training, SFT and RL) and the full architecture ({{model card|@glim}}, {{VentureBeat|@vbglim}}), and a 329-author technical report followed on arXiv in October 2026 ({{arXiv 2610.24628|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24628}}).

Inside Llama 4: experts and iRoPE, layer by layer

Both models have 48 layers of width 5,120 with 40 query heads sharing 8 key-value heads of 128 dimensions. Scout puts an MoE block in every layer (16 experts plus the shared one); Maverick alternates: odd layers are MoE with 128 experts plus the shared one, even layers are dense with a feed-forward block twice an expert's width ({{Scout config|@sc4}}, {{Maverick config|@mv4}}, read from Unsloth's mirrors because Meta's repositories are gated). So "alternates dense and MoE layers" is true of Maverick only. Each token computes with the attention of all 48 layers plus two expert-sized blocks per layer (the shared expert and one routed one, or the dense block of the same total width), which is why both are 17B active while storing 109B and 400B (rebuilt from the config below, Maverick comes to 400.7B and 17.2B, independently of Meta's rounded figures).

The context claim rests on iRoPE (interleaved RoPE): three layers in four use rotary embeddings and attend only within chunks of 8,192 tokens; every fourth layer has no positional encoding and attends to the whole context, so it is length-agnostic by construction. Those global layers also scale their queries by a temperature that grows with position, the "inference time temperature scaling of attention" of the announcement ({{Llama 4 announcement|@l4}}); in Hugging Face's implementation the factor is 1 + 0.1 ln(1 + ⌊(p+1)/8192⌋) at position p, after {{Nakanishi's scalable softmax|@ssmax}} ({{code|@hfl4}}), so the softmax stays sharp over millions of keys. RoPE, NoPE layers and Llama 4's config fields are taught on {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}. Move the position and watch which layers' caches keep growing.
