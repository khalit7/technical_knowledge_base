The idea in one screen

Each Meta model line is a deliberate trade of where a cost or a risk sits: Llama moved compute from serving into training (small models trained far past compute-optimal), Muse moved the frontier weights behind an API to get back to the frontier, and the Muse agent moves trust out of the model into machinery the model cannot talk its way past.

The Llama era: one idea per generation

Llama 1 February 2023: the overtraining bet, and the leak

A research-only release of 7B to 65B models trained only on publicly available data. The technical contribution was to take the Chinchilla result (for a fixed training budget the loss-optimal allocation is roughly 20 training tokens per parameter; {{Hoffmann et al.|@chin}}) and deliberately overshoot it: Chinchilla's objective "disregards the inference budget", and "a smaller one trained longer will ultimately be cheaper at inference"; where Chinchilla recommends a 10B model on 200B tokens, Meta found a 7B model still improving after 1T ({{LLaMA paper|@l1}}, and its own loss curves in the next section). The 7B and 13B saw 1.0T tokens and the 33B and 65B 1.4T, and the 13B beat GPT-3 (175B) on most benchmarks. That reframing, inference paid on every request forever while training is paid once, is why the 7B and 13B sizes exist at all and why nearly every open model since has been overtrained by Chinchilla's standard.

The weights leaked, and the leak created the local-LLM ecosystem, above all {{llama.cpp|@lcpp}}, a dependency-free C and C++ engine with aggressive weight quantisation that put 7B-class models on laptop CPUs ({{Ollama, llama.cpp, and local serving|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f7a144fe25d799e811}}).

Llama 2 July 2023: a legal base

7B, 13B and 70B with a 4,096-token context (doubled from Llama 1's 2,048), the first openly licensed weights usable commercially, with chat variants tuned by rejection sampling and RLHF using PPO, and an unusually candid safety appendix ({{Llama 2 paper|@l2}}). The 70B used grouped-query attention, which Llama 3 then adopted at every size. The licence, more than the model, is what let a downstream fine-tuning industry form.

Two Meta figures for one training set (2T or 1.8T tokens)
The Llama 2 paper says "We trained on 2 trillion tokens of data" ({{Llama 2 paper|@l2}}); the Llama 3 paper says Llama 3's corpus is "about 15T multilingual tokens, compared to 1.8T tokens for Llama 2" ({{Llama 3 paper|@l3h}}). Both are Meta's own; the page uses 2T, the figure in the paper that trained the models, and the 7B is then at 2T / 7B ≈ 286 tokens per parameter (≈ 257 on 1.8T).

Llama 3 to 3.3 April 2024 to December 2024: open frontier, kept dense

Dense 8B and 70B (April 2024), then the 405B with Llama 3.1 (July 2024), all on about 15T multilingual tokens with a 128K-token vocabulary and, from 3.1, a 128K-token context. The 405B saw 15.6T tokens and "delivers comparable quality to leading language models such as GPT-4 on a plethora of tasks"; it was deliberately kept dense rather than mixture-of-experts "to maximize training stability" ({{Llama 3 paper|@l3h}}). The report itself became the field's most complete public account of how a frontier model is trained (what it disclosed).

Llama 3.3 70B (6 December 2024), the same shape as 3.1 70B, came close to 405B through post-training alone, including "over 25M synthetically generated examples", and so became the practical default in serving stacks that could not justify 405B's memory. Llama 3.2 (25 September 2024) added 11B and 90B vision models and 1B and 3B text models for devices, with 128K context ({{Meta|@l32}}).

Llama 3.3 against 405B, benchmark by benchmark
86.0 against the 405B's 88.6 on MMLU with chain of thought (the same 86.0 as Llama 3.1 70B), and ahead on MATH at 77.0 against 73.8 (Llama 3.1 70B: 68.0) ({{model card|@l33}}; 405B figures from the {{Llama 3 paper|@l3h}}).

Llama 4 5 April 2025: the MoE pivot that misfired

The line moved to mixture of experts, so the parameters stored decouple from those each token computes with ({{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}): in Maverick "MoE layers use 128 routed experts and a shared expert. Each token is sent to the shared expert and also to one of the 128 routed experts" ({{Llama 4 announcement|@l4}}). Scout is 109B total with 17B active across 16 experts, trained on about 40T tokens, with a claimed 10M-token context and, with Int4 weights, a fit on one H100; Maverick is 400B total with 17B active across 128 experts, about 22T tokens and a 1M-token context, sized for one 8-GPU H100 host ({{model card|@mc4}}). The layer layout and the iRoPE design behind the context claim are in Inside Llama 4.

Reception was poor: the headline LMArena score came from a different checkpoint than the one shipped, and real-world coding quality lagged the published numbers badly; the episode cost Meta credibility it never recovered for the Llama line. Behemoth, the teacher with "288 billion active parameter[s] ... 16 experts" and "nearly two trillion total parameters" from which Maverick was codistilled while Behemoth was still training ({{Llama 4 announcement|@l4}}), never shipped.

The LMArena episode (what happened, sourced)
An experimental chat-tuned variant, Llama-4-Maverick-03-26-Experimental, produced the headline LMArena score of 1417 (second on the leaderboard) while a different checkpoint shipped; the experimental one gave long answers full of emojis, the released one short plain ones. LMArena said "Meta's interpretation of our policy did not match what we expect from model providers" and changed its policies ({{The Register, 8 April 2025|@reg}}).

The reorganisation mid 2025: Meta Superintelligence Labs

MSL was formed on 30 June 2025 under Alexandr Wang, founder of Scale AI (the data-labelling company that supplied much of the industry's RLHF and evaluation data), with an aggressive hiring drive from rival labs. TBD Lab took over frontier model work and the open Llama roadmap stalled behind it.

The deal, the four groups and the departures (dated)
After an August 2025 restructuring MSL holds four groups: TBD Lab (frontier models, under Wang), FAIR, Products and Applied Research (under Nat Friedman, former GitHub CEO) and MSL Infra (under Aparna Ramani) ({{Wikipedia|@wmsl}}). Meta spent "more than $14 billion to acquire a 49% non-voting stake in Scale AI" ({{Wikipedia|@wmsl}}; the widely reported $14.3 billion is unconfirmed in the sources read here), which brought Wang in. Meta cut 600 jobs at MSL in October 2025, and Yann LeCun, FAIR's founding director, left on 20 November 2025 to found Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs ({{Wikipedia|@wmsl}}).
