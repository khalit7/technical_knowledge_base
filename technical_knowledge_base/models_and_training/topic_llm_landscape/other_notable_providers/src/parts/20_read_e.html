Openness itself: Ai2 OLMo, the fully open recipe

"Open weights" and "open source" are used interchangeably and are not the same thing. A typical open-weights release (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, DeepSeek) gives you one artefact: the final parameter tensors, a model card and a table of scores; everything that produced them stays inside the lab. OLMo, from the Allen Institute for AI (Ai2), established the practice of releasing the production process rather than the product ({{OLMo 1 paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00838}}: "unlike most prior efforts that have only released model weights and inference code, we release OLMo alongside open training data and training and evaluation code"). Ai2 calls the whole path the model flow: "every stage, checkpoint, dataset, and dependency required to create and modify it" ({{Olmo 3 blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}). It was the only line doing so until September 2026, when IFM's K2 Horizon joined it (below): two members on two continents under different funders, a norm forming rather than a curiosity. What OLMo ships that others withhold, with the Olmo 3 32B release as the example:

1. The corpus and its tooling

The pretraining corpus (Dolma) as downloadable documents, plus the deduplication, filtering, decontamination and tokenisation pipeline, so the corpus can be rebuilt or varied, not merely inspected. Olmo 3's pool is 9.31T tokens; heuristic web filtering took 252.6B documents to 38.7B, removing 84.9%, in about 1,030 hours on EC2 instances ({{Olmo 3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, Tables 4 and 36).

2. The mixture and its order

The proportions of each source and the curriculum across stages, the part labs guard hardest: two models with identical architectures and different mixtures are different models. Drawn in the Inspect an open run tab. The released mixes are "reconstructions of the data sampled during our actual training runs" ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, note to Table 4).

3. Training code and full configs

So a run can be reproduced rather than approximated: the {{OLMo-core|https://github.com/allenai/OLMo-core}} trainer, {{open-instruct|https://github.com/allenai/open-instruct}} for post-training, {{OLMES|https://github.com/allenai/olmes}} for evaluation, and every stage's batch size, learning rate and parallelism ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, Tables 34 and 35).

4. Intermediate checkpoints

The most valuable item and the rarest. The Olmo 3 32B base repository has 718 checkpoint branches: 655 from pretraining, 49 from midtraining, 14 from long-context extension ({{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/api/models/allenai/Olmo-3-1125-32B/refs}}, counted 1 October 2026). An open-weights release has one.

5. Training logs

Loss curves, spikes, restarts and what was changed when; the failures are as informative as the final number and almost never published. Ai2's Weights & Biases projects are public, 69 runs for Olmo 3 32B alone ({{W&B ai2-llm|https://wandb.ai/ai2-llm/Olmo-3-1125-32B}}).

6. The post-training stack

Tulu 3 for OLMo 2, Dolci for Olmo 3: the SFT datasets, preference data, RL prompt sets and recipe code (OlmoRL). Dolci "provides separate mixes for each stage of post-training: SFT, DPO, and RLVR" ({{Olmo 3 blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}).

Durations from the {{Olmo 3 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, section 2.4: pretraining about 9.5 days on 512 GPUs then 35 days on 1,024; midtraining about 1.5 days; long-context about 1 day; post-training about 9 days, of which SFT about 2 and the final RL about 5 (DPO's 2 is the remainder, derived); Olmo 3.1's RL extension 21 days on 224 GPUs ({{blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}). Checkpoint counts are Hugging Face branches on 1 October 2026; post-training checkpoints are released as separate models and counted once each. GPU-days are cluster size times days, so they include crashes and evaluation. It waits until you press play or step.

What the extra artefacts let you do

None of this matters if you only run inference; it matters for research. Pick a question to see which artefacts it needs and whether an open-weights release could answer it.

The two that matter most: contamination becomes checkable, because without the corpus every benchmark number is unfalsifiable in principle, and here you can grep for the eval set (decontamination in general: {{Decontamination, PII and governance|n:3f95c17b0d0d8100b7e4e0f96074c309}}); and interventions get a real control, because a fork of a released OLMo checkpoint with the released mixture is a controlled experiment, while fine-tuning someone's closed-corpus weights is not.

The price: capability

Publishing your corpus means you cannot use data you have no clear right to publish, and that alone caps OLMo against labs that train on whatever they can reach; Ai2 also does not "collect from sites that explicitly disallow it" ({{Olmo 3 blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}). On Artificial Analysis's index the gap is wide, though the bars mix that constraint with size and age:

{{Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index|https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models}} v4.3, read 1 October 2026: Olmo 3.1 32B Think 7.1 (marked by AA as estimated; a 32B dense model from December 2025, the others larger and newer), K2 Horizon 375B 30.5, median open-weights model 33.7 (21 models in this knowledge base's snapshot), MiMo-V2.6-Pro 46.3, Claude Opus 5.5 57.6. So treat the family as the reference stack for how models are trained and the base for reproducible research, not as the model you serve for raw scores.

What the open runs teach (1): OLMo 2's stability fixes, checked from the logs

OLMo 2 (7B, 13B and 32B, trained on 4T, 5.6T and 6.6T tokens in total; {{OLMo 2 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656}}) documented training-stability fixes other labs treat as tribal knowledge: reordered normalisation (the norm moves from the input of the attention and feed-forward blocks to their output, so the residual stream stays unnormalised) and QK-norm (queries and keys normalised before their dot product, which bounds the attention logits whose unbounded growth is a standard cause of mid-run divergence), alongside a z-loss term, a plain normal initialisation, AdamW epsilon 10⁻⁸, no weight decay on embeddings and filtering of repeated n-grams. How each fix works and why they only help together: {{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}}. The fully open point is that both runs' logs are public, so the effect can be checked rather than taken on trust:

Spike score as the {{OLMo 2 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656}} defines it (section 3.2): the share of values at least 7 standard deviations from the mean of the previous 1,000, applied here to W&B's sampled points (about one per 19 steps for OLMo 1.7 and one per 62 for OLMo 2), not every step, so it is comparable between the two runs but not with the report's own figures. The two fixes as formulas, and what OLMo-0424 did instead

Reordered norm: OLMo-0424 used the first line, OLMo 2 the second ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656}}, Table 1 and section 3.3.2).

h=x+Attn(LN(x)),hout=h+MLP(LN(h))h=x+RMSNorm(Attn(x)),hout=h+RMSNorm(MLP(h))

QK-norm (OLMo-0424 instead clipped QKV values to 8):

logits=RMSNorm(q)·RMSNorm(k)dhead

What the open runs teach (2): save the best data for the end

OLMo 2 and Olmo 3 pretrain in two stages: bulk pretraining on the broad web mixture (OLMo 2 Mix 1124, 3.9T tokens, over 95% web), then a short anneal on Dolmino, a much smaller high-quality mix (filtered web, FLAN, peS2o papers, Wikipedia, Stack Exchange and a 10.7B-token maths set) fed while the learning rate decays to zero ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656}}, Tables 4, 5 and 13). Each anneal is run several times with different data orders and the weights averaged (souping). Olmo 3 kept the recipe and added a long-context stage (curricula and anneal mixes in general: {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}}). Predict: how much of the final Math score does the 4% of tokens in the anneal buy?

A few percent more tokens, at the end, lift Math by 20 to 36 points

On the OLMo 2 7B, Math goes from 12.7 to 41.7 with about 4% more tokens. Whether this beats spreading the same tokens through the whole run is not directly measured in either report (unconfirmed); they measure the gain over the pretraining checkpoint. The two 32B souping figures disagree inside the Olmo 3 report: its text says the soup improved Math by 2.9 and 1.6 over the two runs, its Table 13 gives 69.7 against 66.8 and 65.4, which is 2.9 and 4.3.

Tulu 3, the open post-training recipe that shipped with OLMo 2, and the 32B's claim

Tulu 3 shipped alongside as the open post-training recipe, first applied to Llama 3.1 bases: supervised fine-tuning, then DPO (the policy is optimised directly on pairs of preferred and rejected responses, so no separate reward model and no rollouts), then RLVR (the reward is a mechanical check of the final answer, a maths result or unit tests, rather than a learned preference model that style can game), with all data and code ({{Tulu 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}; the stages in depth: {{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}). The OLMo 2 1B and 32B used GRPO for the RL step ({{OLMo 2 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fb9857fb956165ae1c}}). The 32B was claimed as the "first fully-open model (all data, code, weights, and details are freely available) to outperform GPT3.5-Turbo and GPT-4o mini" ({{Ai2, March 2025|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo2-32B}}).

What the open runs teach (3): Olmo 3, a reasoning model from 6x fewer tokens

Instead of a model, Ai2 published the path: "checkpoints from each major training milestone: the base pretrained model, the mid-trained model after targeted skill enhancement, the long-context-extended version, plus post-training checkpoints for the Olmo 3-Think, Olmo 3-Instruct, and Olmo 3-RL Zero flows", so a researcher can "fork the model at whatever point best fits" ({{Olmo 3 blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}). At launch (20 November 2025) that meant Base and Think at 7B and 32B, Instruct at 7B and RL-Zero; Instruct 32B followed with 3.1.

  • Base. Dense, 32 and 64 layers; the 32B uses grouped-query attention (40 query, 8 key-value heads); sliding-window attention on 3 of 4 layers with a 4,096-token window; QK-norm and output norms carried over from OLMo 2 ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, Table 33). Pretrained on Dolma 3 Mix, midtrained on Dolmino, then extended from 8,192 to 65,536 tokens of context on Longmino.
  • Think. SFT on Dolci-Think; then DPO with "delta learning": chosen answers from Qwen3-32B thinking, rejected ones from the much weaker Qwen3-0.6B, because fine-tuning on the strong answers alone hurt ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, Table 21); then RLVR with OlmoRL, whose inflight weight updates and continuous batching made RL "4x more efficient" ({{blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}). The first fully open 32B reasoning model with the entire RL pipeline public.
  • Cost. About 56 days from the start of training to the evaluated Think 32B on a dedicated cluster of 1,024 H100s ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, section 2.4): 1,024 GPUs × 56 days × 24 h = 1,376,256 GPU-hours; × $2 per hour = $2.75M (both the price and the total are the report's).

The efficiency claim the family rests on, in Ai2's words: Think 32B is "narrowing the gap to the best open-weight models of similar scale – such as Qwen 3 32B – while training on roughly 6x fewer tokens" ({{blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}). It is often summarised as matching Qwen3-32B; Ai2's own table does not support that across the board.

36T ÷ (5.5T + 0.1T + 0.1T + 0.1T) = 36 ÷ 5.8 ≈ 6.2
Qwen3's approximately 36T pretraining tokens ({{Qwen|https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/}}) against Olmo 3 32B's 5.5T pretraining, two 100B midtraining runs and 100B long-context ({{report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}, Table 35). Post-training tokens are excluded on both sides.

On Ai2's own suite, Qwen 3 32B still scores higher than Olmo 3 Think 32B on most benchmarks: Olmo is close on maths and HumanEvalPlus, behind on ZebraLogic, LiveCodeBench and GPQA, and ahead on instruction following. Olmo 3.1 (12 December 2025) resumed the best RL run "for an additional 21 days on 224 GPUs with extra epochs over our Dolci-Think-RL dataset", gaining "5+ points on AIME, 4+ points on ZebraLogic, 4+ points on IFEval" ({{blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}); IFBench gains 20.5.

The open stack is also what other people build controlled experiments on: Ettin held everything but the objective fixed to compare encoders with decoders, and the ladder-scaling studies predicted a 7B and a 13B from small runs at 1% of the compute.

Ettin, ladder scaling, the testbed and provenance: how the open stack gets used

Ettin: one variable at a time. Ettin (Johns Hopkins with LightOn, July 2025) trains paired encoders and decoders from 17M to 1B parameters on up to 2T tokens with one ModernBERT-style recipe built from open OLMo-family data (DCLM with Dolma 1.7 sources, then Dolmino-style sources for the later phases), with only the attention pattern and objective differing ({{Ettin paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11412}}). Encoders and decoders had never been compared with data, scale, recipe and tokeniser all held fixed, so every earlier comparison confounded the objective with everything else. With only the objective varying, encoders win on classification and retrieval, decoders on generation, and continued training across objectives does not close either gap: "a 400M encoder outperforms a 1B decoder on MNLI, and vice versa for generative tasks". For choosing an embedding backbone against a generative one, the pretraining objective, not size, decides ({{Ettin paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81139cffea35080afe2b}}; objectives in general: {{Families and objectives|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8105b616c17c1439f766}}). Ettin also releases 200+ checkpoints and the training order segmented by checkpoint, the OLMo practice carried over.

How the stack gets used.

  • The standard testbed for interventions. Per-stage checkpoints make the family the default base for data ablations, RL variants and interpretability work that needs a training trajectory; OlmoTrace, in Ai2's playground, traces parts of a response back to training documents ({{blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}).
  • Ladder scaling. Fit scaling curves on many small controlled runs to predict a large model's downstream accuracy before paying for it: model and data size predict a loss, the loss predicts task accuracy, and the ladder costs 1% of the compute of the 7B (4T tokens) and 13B (5T tokens) targets it predicts ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04403}}). That needs dozens of runs with a fixed recipe and open data, which only this stack provides.
  • Provenance. US-origin fully open matters politically as well as scientifically: an answer to Chinese open weights for auditable government and regulated deployments, where a buyer who must answer "what is in this model" has an answer. It is no longer the only such answer: K2 Horizon gives Gulf-funded buyers a second fully open provenance chain.

Training science in general (curricula, annealing, post-training) belongs to {{Topic: llm-training-and-post-training|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}}; OLMo 3 Think as an open replication of reasoning training is on {{Topic: llm-landscape (Deeper: test-time compute)|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

Lineage, February 2024 to October 2026, public checkpoints per model and the current models (volatile)

Ai2 wrote "OLMo" until Olmo 3, which it writes "Olmo".

Feb 2024
OLMo 1 and Dolma. The first genuinely fully open 7B ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00838}}), and the release that established Dolma, a three-trillion-token English corpus of web content, papers, code, public-domain books, social media and encyclopedic text, shipped with its curation toolkit open-sourced ({{Dolma paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00159}}). Dolma is arguably the more important artefact: it made "here is exactly what the model read" possible at all.
Apr 2024
OLMo 1.7 (OLMo-7B-0424). The run whose loss spikes the OLMo 2 fixes were designed against ({{OLMo 2 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656}}, Table 1); its log is the "before" in the stability chart.
Sep 2024
OLMoE. A fully open sparse mixture-of-experts model, OLMoE-1B-7B: 7B parameters, 1B used per token, pretrained on 5T tokens, with weights, data, code and logs all released ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02060}}). It makes router behaviour, expert specialisation and load balancing studiable with the data and checkpoints in hand rather than inferred from released weights; the paper reports high expert specialisation in routing.
Sep 2024
Molmo. The open vision-language line, not built by distilling captions out of a proprietary VLM, which is how most "open" multimodal models are made ("the strongest open-weight models rely heavily on synthetic data from proprietary VLMs"). Its PixMo datasets were "collected without the use of external VLMs" ({{Molmo paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17146}}): open all the way down rather than a laundered closed one.
Nov 2024
Tulu 3. The open post-training recipe ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}), above.
Nov 2024 to Apr 2025
OLMo 2. 7B and 13B (November 2024), 32B (March 2025), 1B (April 2025).
Jul 2025
Ettin (in the closed block above). FlexOlmo: each data owner branches from a shared public model, trains an expert on its own data locally and contributes the expert, not the data, and "can deactivate them at any time" ({{Ai2|https://allenai.org/blog/flexolmo}}).
20 Nov 2025
Olmo 3. The model-flow release.
Dec 2025
Molmo 2 (11 December): video understanding, pointing and tracking. The 8B and 4B are built on Qwen 3; only Molmo 2-O (7B, on Olmo) is "a fully open end-to-end model flow including the underlying LLM" ({{Ai2|https://allenai.org/blog/molmo2}}). Olmo 3.1 (12 December): extended-RL Think 32B and a new Instruct 32B, Ai2's most performant models to date ({{blog|https://allenai.org/blog/olmo3}}).
5 Mar 2026
Olmo Hybrid 7B. Transformer attention mixed with linear recurrent layers; on MMLU it "reaches the same accuracy as Olmo 3 using 49% fewer tokens", roughly 2x data efficiency ({{Ai2|https://allenai.org/blog/olmohybrid}}).
1 Oct 2026
Olmo-core 3. A training stack rather than a model: an open mixture-of-experts implementation in the {{OLMo-core|https://github.com/allenai/OLMo-core}} trainer. In Ai2's preliminary numbers a 47B MoE (128 experts, 4 active, about 3.2B active parameters) trains at 52,000 tokens per second per GPU on 8 B300s against 19,400 with the earlier implementation, about 2.7 times, and a 1.2T-parameter model with 58.36B active ran on 512 GPUs. Ai2 says the next Olmo will be a mixture of experts and "our most capable Olmo yet", the line's first MoE since OLMoE in 2024 ({{Ai2|https://allenai.org/blog/olmocore3}}). The same week it released AstaBrief 8B with weights and training data ({{Ai2 newsletter|https://allenai.org/newsletters/2026-10-newsletter}}).
