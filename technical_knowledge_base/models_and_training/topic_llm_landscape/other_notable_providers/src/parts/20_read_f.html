Openness as the product, beyond Ai2: IFM, Prime Intellect, Hugging Face

Institute of Foundation Models (Abu Dhabi): K2 Horizon. IFM is the second lab, alongside Ai2, publishing fully open models. K2 Horizon (September 2026) is six Apache 2.0 models at 0.9B, 3.7B, 7B, 32B, a 36B sparse variant (4B active) and 375B (23B active, 512K context), released with weights, training code, training data and methodology: the combination that makes contamination auditing, data ablations and training-dynamics research possible at all ({{IFM press release|https://ifm.ai/k2/press-release/}}, 8 min; {{K2 Horizon 375B|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-375B-A23B}}). Two design ideas run through the family: diffusion distillation, adapters that generate a block of tokens in parallel rather than one at a time, for a claimed 3× speedup ({{K2-Horizon-7B-Uno|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-7B-Uno}}; the 3× is from IFM's press release, which blocked re-fetching, so it is unconfirmed here); and Mixture-of-Values attention (MoVA), aimed at reasoning ({{MoVA 36B|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B}}). IFM claims state of the art at 0.9B, 3.7B and 7B and open-weight competitiveness at 375B, a vendor claim until rerun; Artificial Analysis measures the 375B at 30.5 on v4.3, against 46.3 for MiMo-V2.6-Pro ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/k2-horizon-375b-a23b}}, which lists it as released 3 September 2026). It is on Hugging Face with vLLM and SGLang support, with API access through Compass, Cerebras and Nebius. Abu Dhabi now funds two distinct open-model lines, this one and TII's Falcon (above).

Prime Intellect: training across the internet

INTELLECT models are proofs of concept for decentralised training: one model trained across clusters in different places joined by ordinary internet links rather than a datacentre fabric. Synchronising gradients over such links every step is hopelessly slow, so it needs low-communication algorithms that let workers take many local steps and synchronise rarely. INTELLECT-1 (10B, November 2024, Apache 2.0, with data and checkpoints released) used DiLoCo: each node takes 100 inner steps alone, then all nodes average the change in their weights (the pseudo-gradient, sent as int8) once, and an outer optimiser applies it. Predict: synchronising every step over these links, what share of the time are the GPUs busy?

Before and after: syncing once per 100 steps keeps GPUs busy 83 to 96% of the time

Inputs from the {{INTELLECT-1 technical report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01152}}, Table 2: 100 inner steps take 38 minutes on an 8 × H100 node; the median all-reduce of the int8 pseudo-gradient takes 103 s (USA), 382 s (USA and Europe) or 469 s (global). Compute utilisation = 38 min ÷ (38 min + all-reduce). Defaults reproduce the report's 95.7%, 85.6% and 83.0% independently (the global case computes to 82.9%, a rounding difference). The sync-every-step run is this page's extrapolation, labelled as such: the fp32 gradient is 4 times the int8 payload (10B × 4 bytes = 40 GB), and its all-reduce is assumed to take 4 times as long. Communication falls 100 × 4 = 400 times. It waits until you press play or step.

What it shows. Synchronising every step over internet links leaves the GPUs idle about 95% of the time (5.2% utilisation in the USA case); DiLoCo keeps them busy 83 to 96% of the time by synchronising once per 100 steps and sending a quarter of the bytes. The models are not competitive on quality; the point is that frontier-scale training need not be geographically centralised, which matters strategically far more than the checkpoints do. (Data parallelism inside one datacentre: {{Distributed training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}.)

Hugging Face SmolLM3 and the smaller specialists

Hugging Face SmolLM3 (3B, July 2025, Apache 2.0) is valued for its documentation rather than its capability: every stage of the recipe, the data mixture and the ablations are published, making it the reference for how a competent 3B-class model is built ({{SmolLM3 blog|https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3}}, {{model|https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM3-3B}}). Its one architectural choice to know: NoPE (no positional encoding) in a fraction of layers, which get no explicit position signal and rely on the causal mask alone, a choice associated with better generalisation beyond the trained context ({{Positional encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}).

  • Nous Research Hermes: post-training specialists who take open bases and produce instruct and reasoning variants with their own data and recipes. They publish datasets and methodology, so the Hermes line is a usable control for "how much of this model's behaviour came from post-training".
  • Upstage Solar popularised depth up-scaling: duplicate a slice of an existing base model's layers to build a deeper network, then continue pretraining so the seams heal, producing a larger model without training one from scratch. SOLAR 10.7B took two copies of a 32-layer base, dropped 8 layers from each, and joined them: 2 × (32 − 8) = 48 layers ({{SOLAR paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15166}}).
  • Reka builds natively multimodal models (text, image, audio and video in one encoder path) from a small team. Reka, AI21 and Upstage are niche but recur in evaluations.
