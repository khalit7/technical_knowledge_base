Auditability and on-prem: Cohere and IBM

Cohere sells to enterprises that will not send their data to a public API, and its design choices follow from that ({{Cohere research|https://cohere.com/research}}).

  • Grounded generation with citations. Command R and R+ were the first models explicitly post-trained so that, given retrieved documents in context, the model answers from them and emits inline references to the passages it used: a reviewer can check each claim against its source and an unsupported sentence becomes visible. That objective differs from ordinary instruction tuning, and it made them the default RAG models for a period (Command R+ was 104B under CC-BY-NC 4.0, {{Cohere|https://cohere.com/blog/command-r-plus-microsoft-azure}}).
  • Sized to the customer's server. Command A (March 2025) is 111B and dense: Cohere's card says it is "deployable on just two GPUs" ({{Command A model card|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/c4ai-command-a-03-2025}}). A dense model has predictable memory and no router, where an MoE of equal quality needs every expert resident and a router that complicates multi-GPU serving. The arithmetic adds a caveat the card leaves out: 111B × 2 bytes = 222 GB in 16-bit, more than two 80 GB cards hold, so "two GPUs" holds at 8-bit (111 GB). The flagship has since moved to Command A+ (May 2026), a 218B total, 25B active MoE under Apache 2.0 ({{Command A+ model card|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/command-a-plus-05-2026-bf16}}), so Cohere now accepts the MoE serving cost too.
  • Multilingual data from people, not translation. Aya (Cohere Labs: Aya 23, Aya Expanse, Aya Vision) builds instruction data across languages through a large annotator community rather than by machine-translating English datasets, which is what usually leaves "multilingual" models fluent but culturally hollow.
  • Agents inside the customer's network. North is the secure agent platform, running against internal systems in the customer's environment. Its single-task specialist, North Small Translate (September 2026), is a 218B total, 25B active MoE built only for translation, a reminder that specialists at mixture-of-experts scale are still economically viable against generalists.

IBM Granite runs on the same architectural logic as Nemotron Nano (above: a constant-size state for most layers lets long documents be processed on modest hardware). What IBM adds is the compliance surface: an ISO-certified governance process for how the models were built, published training-data provenance, and indemnification, which is what actually unblocks a bank or a hospital ({{IBM Granite|https://www.ibm.com/granite/docs/}}). Its function-calling quality per parameter is strong, which matters because the enterprise use is structured tool calls rather than open-ended chat. It is chosen for the paperwork as much as for the model.

Whether a model fits the customer's hardware is a question of two independent levers. Predict: does North Small Translate, 218B parameters, fit on one GPU?

Bits per weight move a model left and right; active parameters move it up and down

Weights in memory = parameters × bits ÷ 8 (decimal GB); the KV cache and activations come on top. Compute per token ≈ 2 × active parameters FLOPs, the standard estimate for a forward pass. Red lines are single-card or single-node memory: RTX 5090 32 GB, H100 80 GB, B200 180 GB (a DGX B200's 1,440 GB ÷ 8), 8 × B200. Picking a model with a published hardware claim switches to the precision that claim implies and checks it. Defaults reproduce Prism's "5.9 GB" and "nine times smaller" for Bonsai 2 by construction (27B × 1.76 ÷ 8 = 5.94 GB; 16 ÷ 1.76 = 9.09), Cohere's W4A4 North Small Translate at 218 × 4 ÷ 8 = 109 GB (one B200 or two H100s), and Tencent's 1.56 TB for Hy4 at about 2.03 bytes per parameter, which shows that release is essentially all 16-bit.

What it shows. The levers are independent: North Small Translate stores 218B parameters but computes like a 25B model, and at 4 bits fits one B200; Bonsai 2 keeps all 27B active but stores them in 6 GB.

Cohere's other releases and their numbers (volatile, read October 2026)

Tiny Aya (February 2026, 3.35B, 70+ languages, CC-BY-NC 4.0) pushes the Aya data to phone-capable sizes ({{Tiny Aya|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/tiny-aya-global}}). North Mini Code (June 2026, 30B total with 3B active, Apache 2.0, 256K context) is Cohere's first open agentic coding model ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/North-Mini-Code-1.0}}).

North Small Translate: 128 experts with 8 per token, 4,096-token sliding-window attention and a 16K context. It reports 83.6 on WMT26 across 50+ languages against DeepL NextGen's 81.37 and Google Translate's 68.20, on Cohere's own evaluation with GPT-5.6-Sol as judge. It is free on the Cohere API up to rate limits and non-commercial (CC-BY-NC 4.0) for self-hosting, and its 4-bit (W4A4) checkpoint runs on one B200 or two H100s ({{Cohere docs|https://docs.cohere.com/docs/north-small-translate-1.0}}, {{Cohere blog|https://cohere.com/blog/north-small-translate}}, 8 min).

Bits per weight: Prism ML Bonsai 2

Prism ML is a compression house rather than a model lab. Bonsai 2 27B (17 September 2026) is a ternary rebuild of Qwen3.8 27B: weights drawn from {−1, 0, +1} with FP16 group-wise scaling, applied end to end across the language model, at 1.76 effective bits per weight ({{Prism ML|https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-27b}}, 10 min; formats and ternary training in general: {{Quantization and precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}). The footprint follows from the bits alone:

27×109×1.76÷8=5.94 GB

against, in 16-bit

27×109×2=54 GB

The ratio is the "nine times smaller" of the announcement (16 ÷ 1.76 = 9.09). Prism reports it keeps 98.2% of aggregate benchmark performance, its own figure. It is the current reference point for how far weight compression goes before quality breaks, and it composes with the open frontier's other lever, as the two-lever chart shows: fewer active parameters cut compute per token, fewer bits per parameter cut memory.

Bonsai 2's reported scores and speeds (vendor, September 2026)

83.9 overall; maths 96.57, instruction following 82.66, coding 81.58, all Prism's figures. 262K context, text and image input and agentic tool use, Apache 2.0, on Hugging Face and GitHub; runs on CUDA and MLX at up to 143 tokens per second on an RTX 5090 and 46.8 on an M5 Max.

Distribution and billing: Amazon Nova and Bedrock

Nova is Amazon's closed in-house family, available only through Bedrock. The first generation (December 2024) was a capability ladder, Micro, Lite, Pro and Premier, differing in size, latency and price, so an application can route cheap turns to Micro and hard ones to Pro without changing vendor. Two siblings make distinct design choices. Nova Sonic is speech-to-speech: audio in, audio out, with no transcribe, generate, synthesise pipeline, which removes the latency of three sequential models and keeps the prosody, interruption and tone that a text bottleneck discards. Nova Act is trained to drive a browser, emitting UI actions (click, type, scroll) against a page rather than prose about it. The Nova 2 generation (from December 2025) is current, with Premier retired in favour of Nova 2 Lite ({{AWS: Nova 2 Lite|https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/introducing-amazon-nova-2-lite-a-fast-cost-effective-reasoning-model/}}, {{Bedrock model catalog 2026|https://hidekazu-konishi.com/entry/amazon_bedrock_model_catalog_2026.html}}).

Nova is competent mid-tier and priced to undercut; Amazon's real position is elsewhere. Bedrock is the aggregator: one API, one bill, one IAM policy and one VPC boundary across models from Anthropic, Meta, Mistral, Cohere and others, which is what large enterprises buy. Trainium, Amazon's training accelerator, is the compute behind Project Rainier for Anthropic, which makes Amazon a supplier of an alternative to Nvidia rather than only a customer. Microsoft's Azure plays the same game, serving everyone's models regardless of its own: the strategy is to own the distribution layer and treat the model layer as replaceable. NVIDIA's Hugging Face purchase (above) is the open-weights version of it.
