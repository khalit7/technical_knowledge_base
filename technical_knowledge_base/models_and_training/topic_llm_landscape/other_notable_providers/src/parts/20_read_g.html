Not a single model: orchestrators and structured-decision models

Two 2026 products from the long tail are sold as models and are not models in the sense the rest of this page uses the word; each moves the question a deployment answers, and the second became a category within a month.

Three things sold behind one endpoint: a model, an orchestrator, a decision model

Structure as each vendor describes it (Sakana, TypeSafe); no routing data or internals are published, so the diagram shows the shape of the call, not measured behaviour.

Sakana AI: Fugu. Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2 (10 to 11 September 2026; a first Fugu Ultra appeared in June) are a learned orchestrator: Fugu reads an incoming query, builds an agentic scaffold for it on the fly, and routes the work across a pool of other models behind one OpenAI-compatible API. It was trained by combining fine-tuning, evolutionary search and reinforcement learning, drawing on two ICLR 2026 papers: TRINITY, which assigns participating models Thinker, Worker or Verifier roles, and The Conductor, which learns coordination strategies by RL rather than having them specified ({{Sakana|https://sakana.ai/fugu-max-release/}}, 10 min). If a routed ensemble sold as one endpoint holds the Pareto frontier, "which model" stops being the question a deployment answers and "which orchestrator" replaces it, the same boundary shift ARC Prize's Provider Adapter produced from the benchmark side (on {{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}). The scaffold rather than the checkpoint is the subject of {{Topic: agentic-harnesses|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d881a8fe98b4cff7bb}}.

TypeSafe AI: Jev. Jev (early access 15 September 2026) is what TypeSafe calls a "System One model". It does not generate prose: it takes unstructured state and returns type-safe structured values with calibrated confidence scores, sampling outputs in parallel rather than one token at a time, so a type error is impossible by construction rather than caught by validation. Its training objective is the part worth tracking: Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions, optimising for honest probability estimates on structured decisions rather than for human preference, a different objective from both RLHF and RLVR ({{TypeSafe|https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev}}, 15 min). If a large share of production calls are structured-decision calls wearing a text interface, this is a different product shape, not a smaller model. Everything is vendor-reported with no independent evaluation, and a 1,282-point Hacker News thread the same week claimed prior art on non-autoregressive decision models trained with RL, so the novelty of the category is contested.

The decision-model tier. Within three weeks of Jev, three larger companies shipped the same shape. Cloudflare Clef and Clef-flash (1 October 2026, Apache 2.0) put a rank-256 LoRA and a routing head on frozen Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3.5-9B backbones with 64K context ({{Cloudflare|https://blog.cloudflare.com/clef-decision-models/}}). Amazon Strands Decider 2B is an open (Apache 2.0) decision model for Amazon's Strands agent framework. OpenAI's Decisions API (public beta 6 October) puts the shape behind an endpoint, POST /v1/decisions on GPT-6 Luna, billed $0.10 per million input tokens with no output charge ({{OpenAI: GPT family|n:3c65c17b0d0d814abf90d5b9e567d72f}}). None has an independent evaluation yet; the common bet is that routing and classification calls should be priced on input alone.

Their prices, speeds and benchmark claims (vendor, September and October 2026)

Fugu Max is the cost-optimised configuration at $2 input and $6 output per million tokens, an output price Sakana puts 40 to 60% below Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Terra and Kimi K3, and on the cost-performance Pareto frontier for 7 of 10 benchmarks. Fugu Ultra v2 targets hard reasoning, at $5 and $30 on OpenRouter ({{OpenRouter|https://openrouter.ai/sakana/fugu-ultra-v2}}): Chartography 48.3 against Claude Opus 5's 27.3, DeepSWE (version not stated) 74.3, best or joint-best on 5 of 8 benchmarks. All are Sakana's own figures; closed weights; not available in the EU or EEA.

Jev: TypeSafe claims it is 40× to 200× faster than frontier models on comparable tasks, 70 to 500 ms against 3 to 329 seconds, with input at $0.042 per million tokens and output free. Founded by Diogo Almeida, formerly of OpenAI. A Jev Router appeared on OpenRouter on 25 September ({{OpenRouter|https://openrouter.ai/typesafe/jev-router}}).

Clef: Cloudflare reports median latencies of 209 ms and 39 ms against Jev's 524 ms, and first place on TypeSafe's own Jev Decision Index, all its own measurements.
