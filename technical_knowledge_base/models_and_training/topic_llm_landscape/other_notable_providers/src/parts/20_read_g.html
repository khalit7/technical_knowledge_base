Not a single model: orchestrators and structured-decision models

Two 2026 products from the long tail are sold as models and are not models in the sense the rest of this page uses the word; each moves the question a deployment answers, and the second became a category within a month.

Sakana AI: Fugu. Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2 (10 to 11 September 2026; a first Fugu Ultra appeared in June) are a learned orchestrator: Fugu reads an incoming query, builds an agentic scaffold for it on the fly, and routes the work across a pool of other models behind one OpenAI-compatible API. It was trained by combining fine-tuning, evolutionary search and reinforcement learning, drawing on two ICLR 2026 papers: TRINITY, which assigns participating models Thinker, Worker or Verifier roles, and The Conductor, which learns coordination strategies by RL rather than having them specified ({{Sakana|https://sakana.ai/fugu-max-release/}}, 10 min). If a routed ensemble sold as one endpoint holds the Pareto frontier, "which model" stops being the question a deployment answers and "which orchestrator" replaces it, the same boundary shift ARC Prize's Provider Adapter produced from the benchmark side (on {{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}); a router over the models an organisation enables, GitHub Copilot Fusion, took first place on the Terminal-Bench 4.1 board in November 2026, above every single model ({{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}). The scaffold rather than the checkpoint is the subject of {{Topic: agentic-harnesses|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d881a8fe98b4cff7bb}}.

TypeSafe AI: Jev. Jev (early access 15 September 2026) is what TypeSafe calls a "System One model". It does not generate prose: it takes unstructured state and returns type-safe structured values with calibrated confidence scores, sampling outputs in parallel rather than one token at a time, so a type error is impossible by construction rather than caught by validation. Its training objective is the part worth tracking: Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions, optimising for honest probability estimates on structured decisions rather than for human preference, a different objective from both RLHF and RLVR ({{TypeSafe|https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev}}, 15 min). If a large share of production calls are structured-decision calls wearing a text interface, this is a different product shape, not a smaller model. Outside tests since launch find it accurate and cheap on some classification tasks and badly calibrated on others, and show it reads a question's options together rather than scoring each alone (closed block below); "calibrated" is the vendor's word until a reliability diagram on your own task agrees. A 1,282-point Hacker News thread the same week claimed prior art on non-autoregressive decision models trained with RL, so the novelty of the category is contested.

The decision-model tier. Within three weeks of Jev, three larger companies shipped the same shape. Cloudflare Clef and Clef-flash (1 October 2026, Apache 2.0) put a rank-256 LoRA and a routing head on frozen Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3.5-9B backbones with 64K context ({{Cloudflare|https://blog.cloudflare.com/clef-decision-models/}}). Amazon Strands Decider 2B is an open (Apache 2.0) decision model for Amazon's Strands agent framework. OpenAI's Decisions API (public beta 6 October) puts the shape behind an endpoint, POST /v1/decisions on GPT-6 Luna, billed $0.10 per million input tokens with no output charge ({{OpenAI: GPT family|n:3c65c17b0d0d814abf90d5b9e567d72f}}). Microsoft's Decision 1.1, generally available from 17 November 2026, is the same tier from a fourth large company, sold as a router that sends work to larger, pricier models only when needed ({{Microsoft AI|https://microsoft.ai/news/microsoft-ignite-2026/}}). None has an independent evaluation yet; the common bet is that routing and classification calls should be priced on input alone.

Their prices, speeds and benchmark claims (vendor, September and October 2026)

Fugu Max is the cost-optimised configuration at $2 input and $6 output per million tokens, an output price Sakana puts 40 to 60% below Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Terra and Kimi K3, and on the cost-performance Pareto frontier for 7 of 10 benchmarks. Fugu Ultra v2 targets hard reasoning, at $5 and $30 on OpenRouter ({{OpenRouter|https://openrouter.ai/sakana/fugu-ultra-v2}}): Chartography 48.3 against Claude Opus 5's 27.3, DeepSWE (version not stated) 74.3, best or joint-best on 5 of 8 benchmarks. All are Sakana's own figures; closed weights; not available in the EU or EEA.

Jev: TypeSafe claims it is 40× to 200× faster than frontier models on comparable tasks, 70 to 500 ms against 3 to 329 seconds, with input at $0.042 per million tokens and output free. Founded by Diogo Almeida, formerly of OpenAI. A Jev Router appeared on OpenRouter on 25 September ({{OpenRouter|https://openrouter.ai/typesafe/jev-router}}).

Clef: Cloudflare reports median latencies of 209 ms and 39 ms against Jev's 524 ms, and first place on TypeSafe's own Jev Decision Index, all its own measurements.

Jev measured from outside (tests of September and October 2026, read 27 November 2026)
  • IMDb, one run (Sebastian Raschka, 29 September): 96.47% on the 25,000 test reviews with no IMDb training, in 22 minutes for $0.65, above every trained model in his comparison; IMDb has been public since 2011, so contamination cannot be excluded.
  • About 10,000 API calls on Jev 1.13.0 (Archer Hume, 17 September): 84.6% on MMLU-Pro, frontier-scale knowledge (TypeSafe's FAQ: "neither small nor an LLM"); an irrelevant fifth option lowered the log-odds between two others from +0.38 to +0.11 in all ten blocks, so it reads its options together; expected calibration error (ECE) 0.031 on 1,200 MMLU items.
  • BTZSC pilot (preregistered, 100 examples per dataset, 17 September): against GLiNER2.5, far more accurate on AG News (0.910 against 0.700) and Banking77 (0.870 against 0.610), tied on emotion with much worse probabilities (ECE 0.351 against 0.117).
  • Enterprise jobs, human labels (Eugene Orlov and Olga Matveeva, 14 October): behind TF-IDF on complaint issues (46.6% against 61.2%); confidence 1.00 on 581 of 1,000 complaints but right on 90.2% of those, so no threshold reached 95%; one ordinary sentence inside a complaint moved 55% of its routing decisions. Jev won on checking AI answers and on unseen languages.
  • Jev 1.14 (14 October): better multilingual support, images in requests, a Box question type and a 262K-token context.
