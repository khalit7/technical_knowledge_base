Open scale at a low price: Tencent, Xiaomi, StepFun and the 300B to 1T band

Five Chinese labs from the long tail now ship mixture-of-experts models at the size of the frontier band, most of them open, and three labs outside China have joined them. The shared pattern is a large total, a modest active count and prices far below closed models. Two of them, Xiaomi and Tencent, were releasing open models at this scale long before their current lines. Predict: which of these labs makes the most aggressive sparsity bet?

Tencent: size, not sparsity

Tencent published competitive open weights for two years without being in the frontier conversation, including large open MoE models well before Hy4: Hunyuan-Large (389B total / 52B active, November 2024, Tencent's own licence; {{arXiv 2411.02265|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02265}}) and Hy3 (295B / 21B, Apache 2.0, open weights July 2026 after an April preview; {{Hy3|https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy3}}). The Hy4 preview (August 2026, Apache 2.0) put it in the band at 770B total / 49B active ({{Tencent|https://www.tencent.com/tencent-releases-and-open-sources-tencent-hy4-preview/}}, 8 min). Its sparsity, 770 ÷ 49 = 15.7×, is conservative next to Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's 125 ÷ 6 = 20.8×: Tencent bought its place with size rather than an aggressive active-parameter bet, which makes it expensive to serve for its tier. It is positioned on real-world productivity (coding, office work, scientific research) rather than benchmark maxima.

Hy4's release facts, evaluation and self-optimisation claim (volatile, read October 2026)

Tencent says the model took part in the automated optimisation of its own training methods, with autonomous infrastructure work producing a 31.8% throughput gain: the same claim shape as Anthropic's recursive-self-improvement report and Zhipu's inference-stack post, three labs within a month, all self-reported by the party that benefits ({{Simon Willison's hands-on|https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/29/hy4/}}, 10 min).

Context above 1M tokens; two reasoning levels, high on by default, with a no_think switch to turn it off; about 1.56 TB of weights on Hugging Face ({{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hy4-preview}}); vLLM support landed in v0.29.0 ({{vLLM releases|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases}}). An internal blind evaluation with 163 experts across 203 engineering tasks scored Hy4 at 2.99 / 4.00 against GLM-5.3 at 2.92 and Kimi K3 at 2.94: inside the noise, but the right shape of claim for a model sold on productivity. Hy4 is not yet on the Artificial Analysis index; Hy3 scores 25.3 on v4.3 ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/hy3}}).

Xiaomi: MiMo, and a published price for the last RL stage

Xiaomi had released open weights under MIT for most of a year before its current line: MiMo-V2-Flash (309B total / 15B active, December 2025; {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2-Flash}}), then MiMo-V2.5 (310B / 15B; {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.5}}) and MiMo-V2.5-Pro (1.02T / 42B; {{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.5-Pro}}) in April 2026. MiMo-V2.6 (22 September 2026) is a consumer-electronics company's third open generation at this scale, not its arrival: Pro is 1.02T total / 42B active and Flash 309B / 15B, both MIT with weights on Hugging Face, both with a 1M-token context, both with reinforcement learning as the final stage ({{Xiaomi release post|https://mimo.mi.com/docs/en-US/news/latest/v2-6}}, {{Pro weights|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL}}). The line's attention since V2-Flash is a hybrid of 128-token sliding-window layers with a few global ones (MiMo-V2.5: 39 and 9 across 48 layers; V2.5-Pro: 60 and 10 across 70), which Xiaomi says cuts KV-cache storage by nearly 6× ({{MiMo-V2.5 card|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.5}}; drawn in the NaiveAI figure below).

The disclosure worth more than any score is the cost: the final RL stage ran for under six days across more than 7,000 RL task environments, about 750,000 trajectories in total, at $2.62 million for Pro (about $0.85 million for Flash), a published price for the last stage of a trillion-parameter open model, which almost no lab publishes. Two techniques come with it and are the reusable part: the mixture-of-experts router is frozen to keep large-scale RL stable, and layered defences against reward hacking are described rather than alluded to (the failure itself: {{Reward hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}}; other published budgets: {{Topic: llm-training-and-post-training|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}}). During the run Xiaomi published a live dashboard streaming reward and evaluation metrics, the first time an active frontier-adjacent post-training run was observable from outside as it happened. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3, Pro scores 46.3, the highest open-weight score on that version (read 1 October 2026).

MiMo-V2.6's benchmarks and prices (volatile, read October 2026)

Xiaomi's own table gives Pro 72.6 on DeepSWE v1.1 (58.4 for V2.5) and 89.9 on Terminal Bench 2.1, while conceding that it loses Terminal-Bench 4.0 to Claude Opus 5 by 14 points (34.9 against 49.0) and trails every closed model on ExploitBench: an unusually honest vendor table. The 2.1 figure is on the older Terminal-Bench scale and does not compare with the 4.0 figures other labs now report ({{ComputingForGeeks|https://computingforgeeks.com/xiaomi-mimo-v2-6-pro-flash/}}, 10 min). On AA v4.3 (methodology v4.3.2), Pro's 46.3 is ahead of GLM-5.3 at max effort on 44.8 and below GPT-6 Sol at max effort on 47.6; Flash scores 37.9 ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/mimo-v2-6-pro}}). Launch coverage reported Pro at 46 and first among open weights without naming the index version ({{Unite.AI|https://www.unite.ai/xiaomis-new-flagship-model-leads-open-weight-rankings-with-a-score-of-46/}}). Prices per million tokens: Pro $0.435 input on a cache miss, $0.0036 on a hit and $0.87 output; Flash $0.14, $0.0028 and $0.28. A cache hit on Pro costs 0.83% of a miss (0.0036 ÷ 0.435).

NaiveAI: the same base with no full attention at all

Naive-N0.5-Flash (27 September 2026, MIT) is 309B total / 15.5B active with a 1M-token context, built on Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.5 base ({{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/NaiveAI/Naive-N0.5-Flash}}, {{NaiveAI research|https://naive.ai/en/research/}}). Its design choice is the attention: at a million tokens the base's few global-attention layers dominate decoding, so NaiveAI replaced each of them with DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), in which a lightweight indexer scores the whole history and the layer attends only to the selected tokens (how DSA works: {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}). Adapting the model to the new layers was one objective of continued pretraining.

Before and after: the nine global layers become sparse, so no layer reads the whole context

Eight six-layer modules, each five sliding-window layers then one global layer, with the first layer of the first module also global: 39 sliding-window and 9 DSA layers across 48 in Naive-N0.5-Flash, against 39 sliding-window and 9 full-attention layers in MiMo-V2.5 ({{MiMo-V2.5 card|https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.5}}; positions from NaiveAI's card, which states the global layers were replaced). SWA uses a 128-token window; DSA selects the top 2,048 tokens. NaiveAI also swaps DSA's original MLA for grouped-query attention with four KV groups. The indexer still scans the full history and the full KV cache is kept: sparse attention cuts attention compute and memory reads, not the cache.

Naive's lineage caveats and the crowded flash band

MiMo-V2.5 is 310B in Xiaomi's release, so the sizes match only approximately; the exact 309B match is with MiMo-V2-Flash. Arriving five days after MiMo-V2.6-Flash (309B / 15B), it made two 309B-class open models in one week, which measures how crowded the flash-tier open band has become. NaiveAI is a Beijing startup valued at about $1.4B and founded by Tsinghua's Jifeng Dai; the valuation and founder are from launch coverage and are unconfirmed here.

StepFun: 27B active at 600B total

StepFun is a multimodal-first Chinese lab with open weights before this release too. Step 5 Preview (launched 20 September 2026) is 600B total and 27B active, with a 92-layer narrow-deep layout and a 1M-token context ({{Pandaily|https://pandaily.com/stepfun-step-5-preview-600b-moe-1m-context}}, {{StepFun docs|https://platform.stepfun.ai/docs/en/guides/models/step-5-preview}}). At release that was the smallest active count among decoder-only open-class models of 600B or more (DeepSeek V3 37B, GLM-5 40B, MiMo-V2.6-Pro 42B, Hy4 49B, Kimi K3 104B). Its sparsity of 600 ÷ 27 = 22.2× is ordinary; the unusual number is the absolute one, since 27B active is what a mid-size dense model costs to decode. Pricing is the argument: $2.70 per million output tokens ({{StepFun pricing|https://platform.stepfun.ai/docs/en/pricing/details}}), 2.70 ÷ 15 = 18% of Kimi K3's $15 ({{Kimi pricing|https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/pricing/chat}}). Artificial Analysis measures it at 43.7 on v4.3 ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/step-5-preview}}).

StepFun's earlier open models, Step 5 Preview's release facts and StepFun's table (volatile, read October 2026)

Earlier open weights: Step-3, 321B / 38B, Apache 2.0, July 2025; Step 3.5 Flash, 196B / 11B, February 2026 ({{StepFun on Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai}}). Artificial Analysis lists the launch as 18 September. Text, image and video input, up to 64K output tokens; input $1.00 per million on a cache miss and $0.05 on a hit. On StepFun's own table it sits with GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 on most metrics and behind GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5 on the hardest coding work, notably DeepSWE v1.1 at 67.7% against 74.0 to 74.1%, with claimed strength on finance and long-horizon autonomous tasks. It launched as an API product with no weights or technical report; the weights followed on Hugging Face in mid-October 2026 in BF16 and NVFP4, under the StepFun Model License 1.0, which StepFun describes as Apache-2.0-style ({{Hugging Face|https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/Step-5-Preview}}).

Post-training on someone else's base, and the rest of China

Shanghai AI Laboratory's Atria Dawn Preview (September 2026, MIT) is a 744B agentic MoE built as a post-training layer over an existing open base, Zhipu's GLM-5.2, rather than trained from scratch ({{GitHub|https://github.com/atria-asi/Atria-Dawn-Preview}}): with NaiveAI on MiMo, a release shape worth recognising when it recurs.

Baidu, ByteDance, Ant, Yi and inclusionAI

Baidu ERNIE is Baidu's long-running in-house line tied to its search and cloud products. ByteDance's Seed-OSS ships genuinely competitive open weights across text and multimodal, and Doubao is the consumer assistant that dominates Chinese usage by volume, which makes ByteDance's data position the one to watch. Ant's Ling and Ring lines and the Yi bilingual models are second-tier in visibility but ship regularly. inclusionAI Ling 3.0 Flash VL (September 2026) is a flash-tier vision-language model, free on OpenRouter at launch.

Cost per task, not price, decides who sits on the frontier

Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 (methodology v4.3.2) against cost to run one index task, read 1 October 2026 ({{leaderboard|https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models}}); each model at its top effort; hover a dot for its values. Labs here that AA has not measured (Hy4, Naive, Cohere, NVIDIA and the rest) cannot be placed, and K2 Horizon 375B has an index score (30.5) but no published cost per task. Among models scoring 40 or more, the cheapest per task is MiMo-V2.6-Pro at $0.133, then GLM-5.3-Flash at $0.253; Step 5 Preview costs $0.718.

What it shows. MiMo-V2.6-Pro sits on the cost frontier: no model scores higher for less per task. Step 5 Preview scores close to it at more than five times the cost per task despite its low price, because cost per task counts the tokens a model spends, not only its price.

The same band from outside China

  • Arcee AI Trinity Large (January 2026; about 398B total, often rounded to 400B, about 13B active, 4 of 256 experts per token, OpenMDW-1.1 licence; {{model card|https://huggingface.co/arcee-ai/Trinity-Large-Preview}}, {{technical report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17004}}) is a rare US startup attempt at open-weight frontier scale, from a company otherwise known for model merging and small-model distillation: evidence about whether a small team can still reach that scale, more than a model to deploy.
  • Reflection AI Beam (5 October 2026) is the other US entry: a text-only sparse MoE of 501B total and 23B active, 52 layers with interleaved local and global attention, trained on 23.8T tokens in under four weeks on 6,144 GB300s, with a 1M-token context after midtraining. Access is a waitlist and the weights are promised under Apache 2.0 ({{Reflection AI|https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam}}).
  • Aleph Alpha Kolibri (3 October 2026, Apache 2.0) is Europe's sovereign answer at the small-active end: 78.1B total and 3.46B active, English and German, 384 experts with 6 active plus 1 shared, a 512-token sliding window with full attention every fifth layer, 20T pretraining tokens, trained to 256K context and served to 1M ({{Aleph Alpha|https://aleph-alpha.com/en/blog/kolibri-has-landed-a-sovereign-open-weight-model/}}).
Their own benchmark claims (vendor, October 2026)

Reflection: 80.9 on SWE-Bench Verified and 80.1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, below Kimi K3's 88.3 on the latter, and a claim of GLM-5.2-level reasoning at three to four times less inference compute. Aleph Alpha: 96.9 on AIME 2025 and 84.3 on GPQA on its own harnesses.
