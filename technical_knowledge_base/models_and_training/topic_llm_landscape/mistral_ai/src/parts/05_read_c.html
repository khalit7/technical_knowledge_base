How Mistral trains: Magistral's recipe

Mistral publishes weights first and papers rarely, so most of its recipes have to be inferred from the artefacts. Two things are documented, both in the {{Magistral paper|@magp}}.

Pure RL for the big model. Magistral Medium was trained from Mistral Medium 3 by reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) alone: no learned reward model, and no reasoning traces distilled from someone else's reasoner. A rollout is scored by mechanically checking its final answer (a maths result by a rule-based checker with SymPy, or code against 20 unit tests), so style cannot game the reward the way it can game a preference model. The algorithm is GRPO (score each of a group of answers to one prompt against the group's mean instead of a learned critic; the full objective is on {{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}), with Mistral's own changes:

  • No KL penalty to the starting model: Mistral found the policy diverges regardless, and the reference copy was not worth its compute.
  • Clip-higher: the upper clipping threshold εhigh raised to between 0.26 and 0.28 for Medium, 0.3 for Small, so rare but useful tokens gain probability faster. PPO's original setting is 0.2 on both sides ({{PPO paper|@ppo}}); the paper does not state its lower bound. A token at probability 0.01 may rise to 0.0128 in one update at 0.28, against 0.012 at 0.2.
  • Length-normalised loss over all tokens of all answers in a group, so long answers do not dominate; the advantage is simply ri − μ (no division by the group's spread), normalised again across each minibatch.
  • Zero-advantage filtering: groups where every answer got the same reward carry no signal and are dropped.
  • Reward: 0 if the format is wrong (one <think> block, the answer in \boxed{} or a fenced code block); otherwise 0.1 for the format, a further 0.9 for a correct answer, a soft length penalty of up to −0.1 that grows linearly once an answer passes lmax − lcache (raised from 16k to 32k tokens during Medium's run), and 0.1 more when problem, reasoning and answer share a language (10% of problems were translated into French, Spanish, Italian, German, Chinese and Russian).

Predict: what happens to the group if every answer is correct? One group of eight answers to the same problem: toggle what each got right and watch the rewards, the advantages and the filter.

Reward rules from the {{Magistral paper|@magp}}, section 2.2; the group of eight and the answers are illustrative (the paper does not state the group size). "Half into the penalty zone" is an answer halfway between lmax − lcache and lmax. Standard GRPO divides by the group's standard deviation; Magistral does not, and normalises across the minibatch instead.

Distillation for the small one. Magistral Small (24B, Apache 2.0) was not pure RL: it was cold-started with supervised fine-tuning of Mistral Small 3 on reasoning traces from Magistral Medium (correct traces from Medium's RL run, plus Medium's answers to OpenThoughts and OpenR1 code prompts, plus 10% general instruction data), then trained with RL on top. Distillation as a method is on {{Distillation and small language models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81efa2a8c3b3b623eb09}}.

What it bought, on the paper's own evaluation: AIME 2024 pass@1 went from 26.8% for Mistral Medium 3 to 73.6% for Magistral Medium (90.0% by majority vote over 64 samples), and Magistral Small reached 70.7%, against 65.4% with the distilled traces alone. Magistral no longer ships: reasoning is now a setting on Small 4 and Medium 3.5, and every Magistral version is deprecated. Reasoning modes and adjustable effort in general are on the {{landscape root|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

Apache 2.0 is the rung that made Mistral's name, and it is an engineering fact, not only a legal one: no acceptable-use annex and no user threshold of the kind in Meta's Llama community licence (which incorporates an acceptable use policy and requires a separate licence above 700 million monthly active users, {{Llama 3.1 licence|@llama}}), so a company can fine-tune the model and run it inside a product without asking anyone. It is also the wedge into government, defence and on-premises deals. Since 2025 the ladder has widened at both ends: the dense agentic flagships carry a revenue carve-out that bites at $20M a month, far below Llama's threshold; the voice model is non-commercial; code completion, OCR and embeddings are API only.

Which Mistral model when

Not a frontier lab on capability, but one of the leading Western open-weight providers and the EU's strategic champion: you choose it for the licence, the jurisdiction or the price per token, not for a leaderboard.

  • Mistral's most capable model: Large 4 through the API preview. It buys the best Mistral scores by a wide margin, and costs a preview's stability and, until the weights ship, the right to self-host; Weights cannot size it yet.
  • The most capable weights under Apache 2.0: Large 3, if you have a full eight-GPU node: no licence conditions, 675 GB of FP8 weights.
  • Cheap, fast, multimodal, with a reasoning switch: Small 4: 6B-active decode cost and a small cache, but memory for all 119B parameters.
  • Agentic coding and tool use, self-hosted: Medium 3.5, unless your company is over the $20M monthly revenue line; then Devstral Small 2 (Apache 2.0, 24B, weights only now) is the unencumbered option, or Medium 3.5 through the API.
  • On a phone or laptop: Ministral 3 at 3B or 8B. A guard model in front of any generator: Shieldstral, with your policy written as text. Code completion in an editor: Codestral, through the API.
The business around the models: products, compute, money, distribution (volatile, dated)
  • Products. The consumer assistant Le Chat became Vibe on 28 May 2026 ("Le Chat is now Vibe", {{Mistral|@vibe}}); Vibe is also the name of Mistral's open-source (Apache 2.0) command-line coding agent, launched with Devstral 2. Developers use the API platform and AI Studio; enterprises building custom models use Forge (March 2026, {{Mistral|@forge}}). Since 11 August 2026 the platform offers regional endpoints that let customers choose Europe or the US for inference, a priority tier for critical workloads (public preview), and third-party open models beside Mistral's own ({{Mistral|@region}}; the {{catalogue|@models}} lists Z.ai GLM 5.3).
  • Compute. Mistral Compute (announced June 2025) is a private stack of GPUs, orchestration, APIs and services on Nvidia reference architectures, with "tens of thousands of GPUs" ({{Mistral|@compute}}). The aim of 1 GW of compute capacity in Europe by 2030 was stated on 11 August 2026 ({{Mistral|@region}}) and repeated after the Series D.
  • Money. A €1.7B Series C in September 2025, led by ASML, at an €11.7B post-money valuation (often quoted as about $14B) ({{Mistral|@raise}}). Then a €3B Series D on 8 September 2026, led by Samsung Electronics with EQT's Scaleup Europe Fund and PSG Equity as co-leads, at more than €21B post-money, which Mistral calls the largest equity round ever completed by a European technology company; the money goes to compute, infrastructure, commercial growth and international expansion ({{TechCrunch|@tc}}). It acquired Koyeb (a Paris start-up, February 2026, {{Wikipedia|@wiki}}) and Emmi AI (industrial engineering simulation, May 2026, {{Mistral|@emmi}}), and opened a Munich hub for industrial AI on 28 September 2026 ({{Mistral|@munich}}).
  • Distribution. Since 16 September 2026 Mistral Small 4 is one of the selectable models in Mozilla's Firefox Smart Window beta, starting in France and North America, with zero data retention agreed by Mistral ({{Mistral|@moz}}; {{Gigazine|@gig}}): the sovereignty argument reaching a consumer surface rather than a procurement one.
Common misconceptions, each answered in one line (depth)
  • "8x7B means 56B parameters": only the feed-forward networks are replicated; attention and embeddings are shared, so it is 46.7B total and 12.9B active.
  • "Mixtral's experts are topic experts": Mistral's own routing analysis found syntactic rather than topical specialisation, and strong repetition between consecutive tokens.
  • "Everything Mistral ships is Apache 2.0": Medium 3.5 and Devstral 2 are under a modified MIT licence with a $20M monthly revenue exception, Voxtral TTS is non-commercial, and Codestral, OCR and the embedding models have no public weights.
  • "Magistral was all pure RL": Magistral Medium was; Magistral Small started from Magistral Medium's traces.
