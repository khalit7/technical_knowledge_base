The idea in one screen

Mistral is a fast follower: it takes an efficiency idea that already works elsewhere (a sliding window with shared key-value heads, sparse experts, then DeepSeek's latent attention and fine-grained experts), ships it early at a size people can run on their own hardware, and decides what you may do with each model by putting it on one rung of a four-rung licence ladder.

Mistral AI is a Paris lab founded in April 2023 by Arthur Mensch (from Google DeepMind) and Guillaume Lample and Timothée Lacroix (both from Meta) ({{Wikipedia|@wiki}}). Its bet is that a cheaper model with open weights and EU jurisdiction wins the deals raw benchmark position does not: sovereign and defence work, regulated on-premises installs, anyone who cannot send tokens to a US API. Mensch, July 2026: "we do not yet own the best language models, but we've constantly reduced that gap" ({{Tech Times|@tt}}). It is the European counterweight to the Chinese open-weight labs, alongside Ai2 and the Institute of Foundation Models (the page's own positioning, not a sourced ranking); how it compares with every other lab is on the {{landscape root|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

Mistral 7B: a sliding window and shared key-value heads

Mistral 7B (September 2023) attacked the KV cache twice. Grouped-query attention: 32 query heads share 8 key-value heads, so the cache stores a quarter of what multi-head attention (MHA) would. Sliding-window attention: each layer attends only to the last W = 4,096 tokens, implemented as a rolling buffer that overwrites the token that just left the window. Windows stack, so after k layers a token can be influenced by about k · W earlier tokens, the way stacked convolutions grow a receptive field; anything beyond the window arrives only through what the intermediate states carried, so exact long-range recall is weaker than with full attention. Both mechanisms, and the reach stepper, are on {{Attention variants|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8120859ecc2d0236e846}}; here is what they bought on Mistral's own shape.

Concretely ({{Mistral 7B paper|@m7p}}, Table 1; {{config.json|@cfg7}}): width 4,096, 32 layers, head dimension 128, feed-forward width 14,336, 32 query heads, 8 key-value heads, window 4,096, context length 8,192, vocabulary 32,000, Apache 2.0. One token leaves 2 × 32 × 8 × 128 × 2 = 131,072 bytes, 128 KiB, of 16-bit keys and values. At 32,768 tokens a full cache would need 4 GiB; the rolling buffer holds only min(32,768, 4,096) positions, 512 MiB, eight times less: "On a sequence length of 32k tokens, this reduces the cache memory usage by 8x, without impacting the model quality" ({{paper|@m7p}}). Full MHA with no window would need 16 GiB, more than the model's own 14.5 GB of weights. The two savings multiply: 4 from sharing heads, 8 from the window at 32k, 32 in all.

In symbols: btok = 2 · L · nkv · dh · s bytes per token (key and value, layers, key-value heads, head dimension, bytes per number), and the buffer holds M(n) = btok · min(n, W); the reach bound is R(k) ≈ k · W, an upper bound on influence, not a guarantee the information survived. What it bought. Mistral 7B outperformed "the best open 13B model (Llama 2) across all evaluated benchmarks, and the best released 34B model (Llama 1) in reasoning, mathematics, and code generation" ({{paper|@m7p}}), which established the lab, and it became a common small base for fine-tuning (unconfirmed as "the default"). What did not last: Mistral itself dropped the window in Mixtral, which supports "a fully dense context length of 32k tokens" ({{Mixtral paper|@mixp}}); every current Mistral model uses full grouped-query or latent attention. Treat the window as a 2023 Mistral choice, not a family trait.

Parameter count and the 131K reach, checked (depth)

Mistral's announcement calls it a 7.3B-parameter model ({{Mistral|@m7}}); summing the table gives 7.24B (32 layers of 218.1M plus 0.26B of input and output embeddings), about 14.5 GB at 16 bits.

The 131K reach does not mean Mistral 7B handles 131K-token inputs: it is a theoretical bound on how far information can propagate through 32 windowed layers, and the model was trained on 8,192-token contexts.
