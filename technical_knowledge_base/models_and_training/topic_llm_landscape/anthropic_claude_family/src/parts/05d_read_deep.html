Release notes, September and October 2026, with every number volatile

Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 (1 September 2026): one model, two safeguard configurations. Fable 5.1 is generally available with production safeguards; Mythos 5.1 is the same model under more permissive ones, for organisations in Anthropic's Cyber Verification and Life Sciences Verification programmes, US-only at launch ({{Anthropic|@fab51}}). The capability jump over Fable 5 is concentrated in long-horizon agentic work: Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 from 24.7% to 52.6%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 42.0% to 55.8% (Mythos 5.1: 60.9%), AutomationBench from 17.1% to 31.4%, CursorBench 3.2.0 from 70.5% to 73.4%. The commercially significant change is a 75% cut to cached input, from $1.00 to $0.25 per million against $10 standard input; Anthropic puts the saving at "around 25%" for typical workloads and "up to around 45%" for highly agentic ones (the Session cost tab shows which token mixes give which). Enterprise Frontier Safeguards let customers keep their data on their own cloud infrastructure while Anthropic can still detect misuse, rolling out from autumn 2026 ({{Anthropic|@efs}}). The release's other case for Fable 5.1 is its writing: Glean's judges preferred its answers "roughly 2-to-1" over Fable 5 on everyday knowledge questions, and Canva called the writing "the standout". Coverage: {{VentureBeat|@vb}}.

Earlier 2026 prices and dates. Mythos Preview was offered to a coalition including AWS, Apple, Google, Microsoft and NVIDIA at $25 and $125 per million input and output tokens, with $100 million in usage credits ({{Anthropic|@glass}}). Fable 5 and Mythos 5 cost $10 and $50; access to Fable 5 was suspended from 12 June to 1 July ({{Anthropic|@nf5}}). Sonnet 5 landed on 30 June ({{Anthropic|@ns5}}) and Opus 5 on 24 July at $5 and $25 ({{Anthropic|@no5}}). Opus 4.6 launched its 1M window as a beta priced at $10 and $37.50 above 200K input tokens ({{Anthropic|@no46}}). Opus 4.6 (5 February), Sonnet 4.6 (17 February), Opus 4.7 (16 April) and Opus 4.8 (28 May) were incremental in capability and consequential for anyone writing against the API. In 2025: Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 (22 May; Opus 4 scored 72.5% on SWE-bench Verified, {{Anthropic|@n4}}), Opus 4.1 (August), Sonnet 4.5 (29 September), Haiku 4.5 (15 October); Claude 2 (11 July 2023) kept 100K ({{Anthropic|@n2}}). Claude Code, Anthropic's terminal coding agent, appeared alongside 3.7 Sonnet as a research preview.

Opus 5.5 (22 September 2026) costs $4 and $20 per million, 20% below Opus 5, with cache reads at $0.20, 60% below Opus 5's $0.50; Anthropic puts the overall saving at 40% on typical workloads and output generation at more than 30% faster, with output "40% less verbose" than Opus 5 ({{Anthropic|@op55}}). On the same tokens the price list alone makes it 35% cheaper (the worked session: $4.50 against $6.875), so the rest of the claimed 40% is fewer tokens. Against GPT-6 Astra the sweep is not clean: Astra leads on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 and narrowly on AutomationBench. Anthropic also claims that Opus 5.5 at its default medium effort beats Astra at max effort on GDPval-AA v2.1, and beats Astra's top FrontierCode v1.1 score, each for about a fifth of the cost per task.

Sonnet 5.5 followed six days later at unchanged Sonnet list prices ($2 and $10; its cache reads, launched at $0.20, were halved to $0.10 on 7 October), "30% faster" than Sonnet 5 and costing "up to 30% less per task" ({{Anthropic|@so55}}). It lands next to Opus 5.5: ahead on Terminal-Bench 4.0, eight points behind on FrontierCode 1.1, two Elo points behind on GDPval-AA v2.1 (measured in pre-release testing that a structured-output bug could degrade, an effect Anthropic expects to be minimal). Both made thinking harder to switch off: Opus 5.5's adaptive thinking cannot be disabled, and Sonnet 5.5's lowest setting, between_tools, removes only the up-front thinking.

Haiku 5.5 (7 October) completed the generation, a year after Haiku 4.5, as "the cheapest, fastest, and most capable small model we've ever released": 1M tokens of context against Haiku 4.5's 200K, adaptive thinking with the full effort range, and two price tiers split at 100K input tokens. A request whose input stays within 100K tokens pays $0.10 and $0.50, 90% below Haiku 4.5's $1 and $5; above 100K the whole request pays $0.50 and $2.50, 50% below. Anthropic puts the average saving at about 75% and notes that Haiku 5.5 spends slightly more tokens per task. On Anthropic's table it moves the small tier from absent to usable on agentic work: Terminal-Bench 4.0 39.2% against Haiku 4.5's 0.0% and GPT-6 Luna's 16.4%, OSWorld 2.1 (offline subset) 72.4% against 15.7% and 48.9%, GDPval-AA v2.1 1620 Elo against 735 and 1437 ({{Anthropic|@nh55}}). Six days after Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 came close to it at half Opus's price, and nine days after that Haiku 5.5 brought the small tier onto agentic benchmarks at a tenth of Haiku 4.5's price for requests under 100K tokens.

Cyber Verification Program (6 October 2026). The programme split into three tiers, each giving access to Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Mythos 5.1: Defense (security operations, incident response, malware reverse engineering), Red Team (adds authorised penetration testing, organisations only) and Specialized (the fewest cyber blocks, for verified organisations testing safety-critical systems, reviewed with the US government). Project Glasswing was folded in, its members moving to Specialized. Anthropic's own CyScenarioBench run of Opus 5.5 (10 challenges, 5 attempts each) shows what a tier changes: with no programme access every attempt was blocked at the first prompt, Defense access completed 4 of 50, Red Team access hit no blocks and completed 34 of 50 ({{Anthropic|@cvp}}). Mythos 5.1 is not a choice for most teams: it is reached through these tiers for security work and the Life Sciences Verification programme for biology.

Independent measurements of Claude volatile

The cross-lab composite rankings live on the root. What is measured directly on Claude is narrower and firmer. Real-SWE, published in September 2026 by Specific Labs on tasks licensed from private production codebases, put Fable 5.1 in Claude Code first at 38.8% (GPT-6 Astra in Codex 33.8%, Gemini 3.8 Flash 31.2%, with overlapping confidence intervals); it did not include Opus 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 ({{DEV Community|@rswe}}). On Terminal-Bench 4.0 run through one fixed minimal harness (Vals AI: mini-swe-agent, one bash tool, average of three runs, board updated 18 October 2026), Haiku 5.5 scores 36.36%, against the 39.2% Anthropic reports for it ({{Vals AI|@valstb}}). Artificial Analysis scores Haiku 5.5 at max effort 43 on Intelligence Index v4.3, at $0.21 per index task ({{Artificial Analysis|@aahk}}).

Two Claude-specific readings of the cross-lab indexes, kept here because the root shows only the leaders: Opus 5.5 at its default medium effort scores 51.2 at $1.34 per index task against GPT-6 Astra at max effort on 52.7 at $3.26, a point and a half lower for 41% of the cost (1.336 / 3.2575; {{Artificial Analysis|@aaop}}); and Epoch AI's Capabilities Index, which combines more than 50 benchmarks, puts Fable 5.1 at 165, two points behind Opus 5.5's 167 ({{Epoch AI|@eci}}, October 2026).

Index versions matter more than ranks. The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index is a composite of ten evaluations (agents 30%, coding 20%, general knowledge 30%, scientific reasoning 20%) on one 0 to 100 scale ({{methodology|@aameth}}). Version 4.2 (4 September 2026) dropped GPQA Diamond as saturated; version 4.3 (7 September) moved Terminal-Bench to 4.0, replaced tau3-Banking with AutomationBench-AA and raised the weight of private held-out tests from 40% to 45%; methodology 4.3.2 (19 September) re-anchored the GDPval-AA Elo scale. Before v4.2 the composite was on a different scale, on which Opus 5 led through August 2026 at roughly 63 and Fable 5 at roughly 62; on v4.3 Opus 5 (max) is 50.8 and Fable 5.1 (max) 53.4, and no v4.3 model inherits the old figures. Artificial Analysis has announced an Intelligence Index v5 that it says will not be directly comparable with v4.3 scores.

Thinking and effort: per-model details

Changing effort mid-conversation. On Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5, Opus 5 and Sonnet 5.5, a per-message setting (beta: an empty system message carrying the new output_config.effort) changes it from the next user turn and keeps the prompt cache. Changing the top-level value between requests restarts the cache, because effort is rendered into the prompt itself ({{effort docs|@effort}}).

Progress updates. The newest models (Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5) write short progress updates between tool calls, notes for the person watching the agent rather than reasoning, and fewer of them at higher effort; a beta display: "updates" shows only those. On Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Fable 5 a prompt that tries to pull the reasoning into the visible answer can be refused as reasoning_extraction ({{thinking docs|@think}}).

Sonnet 5.5's effort. Its docs suggest starting agentic coding at medium effort; its thinking can be reduced to between_tools (accepted at high effort or below) when up-front deliberation is not worth paying for, and its levels are recalibrated against Sonnet 5.

Preserved thinking and other labs. Preserved thinking narrows what a neutral harness can replay or reconstruct from a transcript: the same direction as GPT-6 Astra's recurrent depth, for a different reason (detail on {{OpenAI: GPT family|n:3c65c17b0d0d814abf90d5b9e567d72f}}). Preserved thinking extends Anthropic's publishing asymmetry to the reasoning itself, which is summarised or hidden and now also protected against extraction.

Model IDs, lifecycle, tokenizer and older models

Snapshots. Every ID (claude-opus-5-5) is a pinned snapshot whose behaviour does not change under you. From the 4.6 generation IDs carry no date; earlier ones do (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001) ({{models overview|@models}}). Lifecycle: active, legacy (served, no longer updated), deprecated (a retirement date is set) or retired (requests fail). Anthropic gives at least 60 days' notice before retiring a public model, and every current model's retirement is set for not sooner than one year after its release ({{model deprecations|@depr}}). Sonnet 4.5 was deprecated on 30 September 2026 and retires on 30 November: 61 days.

Tokenizer. Claude Opus 4.7 and later use the newer tokenizer, about 30% more tokens for the same text; Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5 and earlier use the old one ({{pricing|@pricing}}). 1M tokens is about 555K English words on the new tokenizer and about 750K on the old ({{models overview|@models}}). Haiku 4.5, now legacy, keeps its 200K window, 64K output limit, February 2025 cutoff and the older tokenizer.

Business shape volatile

Revenue skews to API, enterprise and coding against OpenAI's consumer skew, which explains the product priorities: agentic tooling, long context and reliability over voice, image generation and consumer surface area. The restricted Mythos releases add a second shape: capability sold first to vetted security and life-sciences organisations, with a safeguarded twin for everyone else.

The scale, as reported rather than filed: the IPO prospectus shows 2025 revenue of nearly $4.6 billion against operating losses above $8 billion, with about a quarter of revenue from two customers, and an investor presentation reported by the Financial Times put third-quarter 2026 revenue at $16.8 billion, up from about $11.6 billion in the second quarter ({{TechStartups on the FT report|@q3}}). The Wall Street Journal, reading a confidential draft S-1 (reported 20 October 2026; no public filing on EDGAR as of 26 October), gives first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion against $1.1 billion a year earlier, and a net loss of $80.7 billion of which about $72 billion is a non-cash fair-value charge on convertible notes and other financing instruments ({{Investing.com on the WSJ report|https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-revenue-hits-163-billion-as-preliminary-s1-outlines-ipo-risks--wsj-4960928}}); draft figures can change before filing. Anthropic's tally counts sales made through its cloud partners and OpenAI's does not, so the two labs' revenue figures are not directly comparable ({{Axios|@axrev}}). On 18 October 2026 Anthropic committed 5% of its shares, subject to stockholder approval, to a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, with 2% more for each 50% rise in value above a baseline ({{Anthropic|@pbf}}).

Common mistakes
  • Sending budget_tokens to a current model: a 400 error on Opus 4.7 and everything after; steer with effort and cap with max_tokens.
  • Assuming hidden thinking is free: with the default display: "omitted" the thinking text is empty but every thinking token is billed as output.
  • Comparing per-token prices across tokenizers: Opus 4.7 and later count the same text as about 30% more tokens than Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5 and earlier, so per-token prices understate how much cheaper Haiku 4.5 is per word. Compare cost per task.
  • Carrying effort settings across models: Opus 5.5 defaults to medium where Opus 5 defaulted to high; rerun an effort sweep on your own evaluations.
  • Quoting a benchmark number without its harness: Anthropic's Opus 5.5 figures are at max effort except Terminal-Bench 4.0 (xhigh), OSWorld comes in 2.0 and 2.1 versions and in partial and strict scoring, and GDPval-AA v2 and v2.1 sit on different Elo anchors.
  • Carrying an old index score forward: the roughly 63 and 62 Artificial Analysis figures belong to Opus 5 and Fable 5 on the pre-v4.2 scale.
