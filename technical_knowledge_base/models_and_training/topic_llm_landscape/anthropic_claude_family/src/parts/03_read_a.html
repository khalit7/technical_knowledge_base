The idea in one screen

Claude is one closed-weight recipe, aligned with Constitutional AI and trained hard with agentic RL, sold at four price rungs; its API is built around a model that decides for itself how long to think and must see an append-only history, so an agent's bill is set by the cache-read price and by how many tokens the caller's effort level lets it write.

What Anthropic chose, what it publishes, and what the caller still controls

Solid boxes are published (papers, docs, price list); dashed boxes are undisclosed; RLVR and agentic RL are named in release posts but never specified. Prices are dollars per million input and output tokens, read 19 October 2026 ({{pricing|@pricing}}). Anthropic publishes no parameter count and no architecture for any Claude model.

Tier, or rung
Haiku (fast, cheap), Sonnet (the balanced default), Opus (complex, long-running agentic work), Fable (the most capable generally available model). Named sizes and prices, not architectures.
Mythos class
The tier above Opus that appeared in 2026. Fable is its generally available model; Mythos is the same model under looser safeguards, restricted to vetted organisations.
Adaptive thinking
The model decides, per request and per step, whether to think and for how long.
Effort
The caller's coarse level, low to max, that steers how many tokens the model writes. Guidance, not a budget.
Thinking block, signature
Reasoning tokens returned in thinking blocks and billed as output; each carries a signature, an encrypted copy of the full reasoning that must be passed back unmodified.
Cache read, cache write
Storing a prompt prefix (write, 1.25 times the input price) so later requests re-read it (read, a few per cent of the input price).

Read across a lane for each tier's cadence; read down to see the tier below catching the top (Opus 5 six weeks after Fable 5; Opus 5.5 three weeks after Fable 5.1). The year 2026 is drawn wider in the full view.

2023: Constitutional AI and long context. Anthropic was founded in 2021 by former OpenAI safety leadership ({{Wikipedia|@wanth}}) on the bet that the expensive human-preference step in RLHF could largely be replaced by the model judging itself against written principles (Training). Claude 1 was announced on 14 March 2023 ({{Anthropic|@n1}}); in May its context grew from 9K to 100K tokens ({{Anthropic|@k100}}), well ahead of rivals, and Claude 2.1 (21 November 2023) doubled it to 200K ({{Anthropic|@n21}}). Document-heavy work was the early niche.

2024: the three-tier ladder, and post-training over size. Claude 3 (March 2024) named Haiku, Sonnet and Opus as a size, price and latency ladder from one training programme, now the industry default ({{Anthropic|@n3}}). Claude 3.5 Sonnet then beat the larger 3 Opus on Anthropic's internal agentic coding evaluation, 64% against 38%, at twice the speed and $3 and $15 per million tokens ({{Anthropic|@n35}}): the first clear public sign that post-training and data had overtaken parameter count for coding. The upgraded 3.5 Sonnet (22 October 2024) shipped computer use, screenshots in and mouse and keyboard actions out, the first production API for GUI agents ({{Anthropic|@ncu}}).

2025: hybrid reasoning with a caller's budget, then agentic coding. Claude 3.7 Sonnet (February) put both modes in one checkpoint: the same weights answer at once or think first, and the caller set a budget_tokens cap per request, up to the 128K output limit ({{Anthropic|@n37}}). It was the deliberate counter-design to OpenAI's router: the caller owned the cost decision instead of a classifier it could not inspect, and with one model there was no capability jump between the fast and slow paths, at the price of having to know which tasks needed thinking. Claude 4 to 4.5 then aimed at long-horizon tool use: interleaved thinking between tool calls (beta with Claude 4, {{Anthropic|@n4}}), file-backed memory that survives beyond the context (the memory tool and context editing with Sonnet 4.5, {{Anthropic|@nctx}}), and a 1M-token context beta (Sonnet 4, August 2025, {{Anthropic|@n1m}}). Opus 4.5 (24 November) cut the Opus price from $15 and $75 to $5 and $25 and introduced effort: at medium effort it matched Sonnet 4.5's best SWE-bench Verified score with 76% fewer output tokens, and closed 2025 on top of the coding benchmarks ({{Anthropic|@no45}}).

Early 2026: the model takes over the thinking decision. Opus 4.6 (5 February) brought adaptive thinking steered by effort and deprecated the manual budget ({{Anthropic|@no46}}); from Opus 4.7 (16 April) a request carrying budget_tokens is rejected ({{thinking docs|@think}}), as are non-default temperature, top_p and top_k ({{model deprecations|@depr}}). Opus 4.7 also added xhigh and a new tokenizer that counts the same text as more tokens (Anthropic: 1.0 to 1.35 times, {{Anthropic|@no47}}; the pricing page: "approximately 30%"). Every 4.6-and-later model now bills its full 1M window at the standard rate ({{pricing|@pricing}}).

2026: the Mythos class and safeguards as configuration. Claude Mythos Preview (7 April) was withheld from general release for its ability to find and exploit software vulnerabilities and offered only to Project Glasswing's infrastructure and security partners ({{Anthropic|@glass}}). Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (9 June) made it a tier above Opus, the first break in the three-name ladder since Claude 3: one underlying model, Fable with classifiers that route sensitive cybersecurity, biology and distillation requests to Opus 4.8, Mythos without them for approved partners ({{Anthropic|@nf5}}). Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 (1 September) repeated the pattern and brought preserved thinking and a 75% cut to cached input ({{Anthropic|@fab51}}).

The pattern. Twice in 2026 the tier below caught the top within weeks: Opus 5 came "close to the frontier intelligence of Claude Fable 5 at half the price" ({{Anthropic|@no5}}), and Opus 5.5 beat Fable 5.1 on every row of Anthropic's table three weeks after it, at 40% of its list price ({{Anthropic|@op55}}). Evaluation moved with the models, from single answers to SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench, which reward recovering from failure (what they measure: {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}).
