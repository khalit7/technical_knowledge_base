Why understand Claude as a family

Claude is Anthropic's family of closed-weight large language models, sold through the Claude API, the major clouds and Anthropic's own apps. The weights are never released and the architecture is undisclosed.

Three things make it worth understanding as a family rather than as a list of releases, and one question runs through all three: what does a Claude task cost, and which dials does the caller still hold?

  • A price and capability ladder. Haiku, Sonnet, Opus, and above them the Mythos class: choosing a model is choosing a rung (Current lineup).
  • Constitutional AI. Anthropic's method of aligning a model against a written set of principles, still the base of its training (Training).
  • Thinking the caller steers. How much the model thinks decides what a task costs and how an agent harness has to be written (Thinking, Session cost).

The tabs: Lineage puts every release and every change to the API on one time axis; Session cost prices an agent session on every model at once; Effort and cost per task takes Artificial Analysis's measured runs apart; Task length shows how long a task each model can finish, from METR's data.

The terms you need

Tier

A rung of the ladder. Haiku is the fast, cheap tier; Sonnet the balanced default; Opus the tier for complex, long-running agentic work; Fable the most capable generally available model. Tiers are named sizes and prices, not architectures: Anthropic publishes no parameter counts and no architecture for any of them.

Mythos class

The tier above Opus that appeared in 2026. Fable is its generally available model; Mythos is the same model under looser safeguards, restricted to vetted organisations.

Snapshot and model ID

Every ID (claude-opus-5-5) is a pinned snapshot whose behaviour does not change under you. From the 4.6 generation IDs carry no date; earlier ones do (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001) ({{models overview|@models}}).

Lifecycle

Active, legacy (served, no longer updated), deprecated (a retirement date is set) or retired (requests fail). Anthropic gives at least 60 days' notice before retiring a public model, and every current model's retirement is set for not sooner than one year after its release ({{model deprecations|@depr}}). Sonnet 4.5 was deprecated on 30 September 2026 and retires on 30 November: 61 days.

Thinking

Reasoning tokens the model writes before or between its answers, returned in thinking blocks and billed as output (Thinking).

Prompt caching

Storing a prompt prefix so later requests re-read it at a fraction of the input price. For an agent that re-reads a long context every turn, the cache-read price is usually the line item that decides the bill (Session cost).

Tokenizer

Claude Opus 4.7 and later models use a newer tokenizer that produces about 30% more tokens for the same text; Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5 and earlier use the old one ({{pricing|@pricing}}). 1M tokens is about 555K English words on the new tokenizer and about 750K on the old ({{models overview|@models}}).

Lineage

Every release with its date, price and source is on the Lineage tab; this is what changed and why it matters.

2023: Claude 1 and 2, and Constitutional AI

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by former OpenAI safety leadership ({{Wikipedia|@wanth}}) on the bet that the expensive human-preference step in RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback) could largely be replaced by the model judging itself against written principles: Constitutional AI, described under Training. Claude 1 was announced on 14 March 2023 ({{Anthropic|@n1}}). In May 2023 its context window grew from 9K to 100K tokens ({{Anthropic|@k100}}), well ahead of rivals; Claude 2 (11 July 2023) kept 100K ({{Anthropic|@n2}}) and Claude 2.1 (21 November 2023) doubled it to 200K ({{Anthropic|@n21}}). That made document-heavy work the family's early niche.

Claude 3 (March 2024): the three-tier shape

Haiku, Sonnet and Opus named a size, price and latency ladder produced from one training programme, now the industry default ({{Anthropic|@n3}}). Claude 3.5 Sonnet (June 2024) became the developer favourite by beating the larger Opus on code: 64% against Claude 3 Opus's 38% on Anthropic's internal agentic coding evaluation, at twice the speed and the Sonnet price of $3 and $15 per million tokens ({{Anthropic|@n35}}), the first clear public sign that post-training and data had overtaken parameter count for coding. The upgraded 3.5 Sonnet (22 October 2024) shipped computer use: a tool loop in which the model receives screenshots and returns mouse and keyboard actions, the first production API for GUI agents ({{Anthropic|@ncu}}).

Claude 3.7 Sonnet (February 2025): hybrid reasoning

Rather than a separate reasoning model, both modes live in one checkpoint: the same weights either answer at once or think first ({{Anthropic|@n37}}). The caller set a token budget for thinking on each request (budget_tokens, up to the 128K output limit), the deliberate counter-design to OpenAI's router: the caller owned the cost and latency decision instead of delegating it to a classifier it could not inspect, and with one model there was no capability jump between the fast and slow paths. The cost was that the caller had to know when a task needed thinking, which is what the 2026 redesign handed back to the model. Claude Code, Anthropic's terminal coding agent, appeared alongside as a research preview.

Claude 4 to 4.5 (2025): agentic coding

Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 (22 May 2025), Opus 4.1 (August), Sonnet 4.5 (29 September), Haiku 4.5 (15 October) and Opus 4.5 (24 November) all pointed at long-horizon tool use rather than single-turn answers. Three concrete pieces:

  • Interleaved thinking: the model reasons between tool calls, not only before the first, so it can respond to what a command returned instead of following a plan made blind ("extended thinking with tool use", beta with Claude 4: {{Anthropic|@n4}}).
  • Memory: file-backed notes the model writes and re-reads across a long session, so state survives beyond what fits in context (Opus 4's memory files; the memory tool and context editing with Sonnet 4.5, {{Anthropic|@nctx}}).
  • A 1M-token context beta for the cases where it does fit (Sonnet 4, August 2025, {{Anthropic|@n1m}}).

Evaluation moved with the models, from single-answer benchmarks to SWE-bench (real GitHub issues, scored by whether the repository's own tests pass after the model's patch; Opus 4 scored 72.5% on the Verified set, {{Anthropic|@n4}}) and Terminal-Bench (multi-step tasks judged by the end state of a real shell). Both reward recovering from failure rather than first-try correctness. Opus 4.5 cut the Opus price from $15 and $75 to $5 and $25 and introduced the effort parameter: at medium effort it matched Sonnet 4.5's best SWE-bench Verified score with 76% fewer output tokens ({{Anthropic|@no45}}). It closed 2025 on top of the coding benchmarks.

Early 2026, Opus 4.6 to 4.8: the control surface changes

Opus 4.6 (5 February), Sonnet 4.6 (17 February), Opus 4.7 (16 April) and Opus 4.8 (28 May) were incremental in capability and consequential for anyone writing against the API.

  • Adaptive thinking, in which the model decides when and how deeply to think, arrived with Opus 4.6, with effort levels low to max steering it; the manual budget was deprecated ({{Anthropic|@no46}}). From Opus 4.7 a request carrying budget_tokens is rejected ({{thinking docs|@think}}).
  • 1M tokens of context at the standard rate. Opus 4.6 launched its 1M window as a beta priced at $10 and $37.50 above 200K input tokens ({{Anthropic|@no46}}); today every 4.6-and-later model bills the full window at the standard per-token rate ({{pricing|@pricing}}).
  • Opus 4.7 added the xhigh effort level and the new tokenizer (Anthropic's launch post gives 1.0 to 1.35 times as many tokens, {{Anthropic|@no47}}; the pricing page "approximately 30%"), and began rejecting non-default temperature, top_p and top_k, so sampling is steered by prompting rather than by parameters ({{model deprecations|@depr}}).

2026: the Mythos class

The class began as Claude Mythos Preview, announced on 7 April 2026 through Project Glasswing: a model whose ability to find and exploit software vulnerabilities Anthropic judged too dangerous for general release, offered only to a coalition of infrastructure and security organisations (among them AWS, Apple, Google, Microsoft and NVIDIA) at $25 and $125 per million input and output tokens, with $100 million in usage credits ({{Anthropic|@glass}}). Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (9 June 2026) turned it into a tier above Opus, breaking the three-name ladder for the first time since Claude 3: one underlying model, with Fable 5 carrying classifiers that route sensitive cybersecurity, biology and distillation requests to Opus 4.8, and Mythos 5 without them for approved partners, both at $10 and $50 ({{Anthropic|@nf5}}). Access to Fable 5 was suspended from 12 June to 1 July. Sonnet 5 landed on 30 June ({{Anthropic|@ns5}}), and Opus 5 on 24 July, "close to the frontier intelligence of Claude Fable 5 at half the price" ($5 and $25 against $10 and $50, {{Anthropic|@no5}}).

Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 (1 September 2026): one model, two safeguard configurations

One underlying model shipped twice: Fable 5.1 generally available with production safeguards, Mythos 5.1 the same model under more permissive ones, for organisations in Anthropic's Cyber Verification and Life Sciences Verification programmes, US-only for now ({{Anthropic|@fab51}}). The capability jump over Fable 5 is concentrated in long-horizon agentic work: Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 from 24.7% to 52.6%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 42.0% to 55.8% (Mythos 5.1: 60.9%), AutomationBench from 17.1% to 31.4%, CursorBench 3.2.0 from 70.5% to 73.4%. The commercially significant change is a 75% cut to cached input, from $1.00 to $0.25 per million against $10 standard input; Anthropic puts the saving at "around 25%" for typical workloads and "up to around 45%" for highly agentic ones (the Session cost tab shows which token mixes give which). Enterprise Frontier Safeguards let customers keep their data on their own cloud infrastructure while Anthropic can still detect misuse, rolling out from autumn 2026 ({{Anthropic|@efs}}). It also introduced preserved thinking (Passing thinking back). Coverage: {{VentureBeat|@vb}}.

Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 (22 and 28 September 2026): the Claude 5.5 family

Opus 5.5 costs $4 and $20 per million, 20% below Opus 5, with cache reads at $0.20, 60% below Opus 5's $0.50; Anthropic puts the overall saving at 40% on typical workloads and output generation at more than 30% faster, with output "40% less verbose" than Opus 5 ({{Anthropic|@op55}}). On the same tokens the price list alone makes it 35% cheaper (the worked session below: $4.50 against $6.875), so the rest of the claimed 40% is fewer tokens. On Anthropic's own table it beats Fable 5.1 on every row. Against GPT-6 Astra the sweep is not clean: Astra leads on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 and narrowly on AutomationBench. Anthropic also claims that Opus 5.5 at its default medium effort beats Astra at max effort on GDPval-AA v2.1, and beats Astra's top FrontierCode v1.1 score, each for about a fifth of the cost per task; the independent aggregate is less one-sided (Rankings).

Sonnet 5.5 followed six days later at unchanged Sonnet pricing ($2 and $10, cache reads $0.20), "30% faster" than Sonnet 5 and costing "up to 30% less per task" ({{Anthropic|@so55}}). It lands next to Opus 5.5: ahead on Terminal-Bench 4.0, eight points behind on FrontierCode 1.1, two Elo points behind on GDPval-AA v2.1 (measured in pre-release testing that a structured-output bug could degrade, an effect Anthropic expects to be minimal). Both made thinking harder to switch off: Opus 5.5's adaptive thinking cannot be disabled, and Sonnet 5.5's lowest setting, between_tools, removes only the up-front thinking.

The pattern

Twice in 2026 the tier below caught the top within weeks: Opus 5 came close to Fable 5 at half the price six weeks after it, and Opus 5.5 beat Fable 5.1 on every row of Anthropic's table three weeks after Fable 5.1, at 40% of its list price. Six days later Sonnet 5.5 came close to Opus 5.5 at half Opus's price. The Lineage tab shows the rhythm.
