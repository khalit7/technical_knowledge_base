The idea in one screen

A small model, the student, gets the most quality per parameter when a big one, the teacher, trains it. Distillation comes down to two choices: whose text the student trains on (the teacher's or its own) and how much of the teacher's opinion it sees at each token (the one token the teacher wrote, its whole distribution, or its grade on the student's token). Pruning adds a third: start the student from the teacher's own weights, cut down.

One position, three training signals: the same toy teacher, the same 35-token vocabulary

You need: softmax and KL divergence (assumed), and for the on-policy part, how an RL loss turns a per-token advantage into a gradient ({{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}). Where distillation sits among the training stages, and how each open recipe used a teacher, is on the parent ({{Topic: llm-training-and-post-training|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}}, Open recipes compared).

Soft target
the teacher's full next-token distribution, as opposed to a hard (one-hot) label.
Temperature T
divides the logits before the softmax; above 1 it flattens the distribution and lifts the small probabilities.
Forward, reverse KL
KL(teacher ‖ student) covers every mode the teacher has; KL(student ‖ teacher) commits to one.
Off-policy, on-policy
trained on text the teacher (or a corpus) wrote, or on text the student itself samples.
Exposure bias
errors that compound at inference because the student never trained on states its own mistakes lead to.
Pruning, healing
cutting heads, channels, embedding width or layers from a trained model, then training to recover the loss.

Distillation is one of four ways to make a model smaller (pruning, quantisation, low-rank factorisation, distillation) and the one that buys the most quality per parameter: almost every capable model under 10B parameters in 2026 was taught by a bigger one. It combines with the others: prune then distil, distil then quantise.

The four levers compared (where each is taught)
LeverWhat it doesWhere it stands
PruningRemoves single weights (unstructured sparsity) or whole structures: heads, MLP channels, embedding width (width pruning) or layers (depth pruning). Quality drops, so the pruned model is healed by more training, now usually distillation from the unpruned original.NVIDIA's Minitron recipe made Llama-3.1-Minitron and Nemotron Nano for a fraction of the tokens ({{below|#s-prune}}).
QuantisationStores each weight in fewer bits; no new model is trained unless it is quantisation-aware.Ternary Bonsai 2 27B keeps 98.2% of Qwen3.8 27B's aggregate score at 1.76 effective bits, 5.9 GB: nine times smaller for about 1.5 points, a better trade than most distillation, so try it first ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}).
Low-rank factorisationReplaces a weight matrix W by a product of two thin ones.Rarely used alone to shrink an LLM; it lives on in LoRA's updates ({{PEFT|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba08eef56b3e3b4eb}}) and in DeepSeek's multi-head latent attention, which caches a low-rank latent ({{DeepSeek|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a2ae7ec6796947a235}}).
DistillationTrains the student on the teacher's outputs.The dominant lever for quality per parameter; the rest of this page.
