Practical notes

A shared vocabulary, or no logits

Logit distillation compares two distributions over the same vocabulary, position by position. Across model families the vocabularies differ, and so do the positions: the same text splits into different tokens. Real tokenizations from this page's data script:

Across families you either distil at the sequence level (generated text works with any tokenizer) or use a cross-tokenizer loss such as ULD, which "uses optimal transport theory to enable distillation across different architectures and tokenizers" ({{Boizard et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12030}}).

On-policy distillation across tokenizers: more alignment is not better (depth)

Across three teacher-student pairs with different tokenizers (maths and code), strict 1:1 aligned positions already covered most student tokens, reverse KL on a student-selected top-16 of the shared vocabulary at those positions matched full shared-vocabulary distillation, and adding span-level supervision to reach complete coverage reduced accuracy, because those weakly aligned gradients disagree with the strict ones ({{Rethinking Cross-Tokenizer OPD (arXiv 2610.08448)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08448}}).

The capacity gap

  • A much stronger teacher is not always better. "The student network performance degrades when the gap between student and teacher is large"; an intermediate teacher assistant bridges it ({{Mirzadeh et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03393}}). On-policy distillation is the other mitigation, and even there a bigger teacher stops paying at about the student's size: fitted power laws for same-family on-policy distillation show the student's peak improving with teacher scale "only up to roughly the student's scale", and in every weak-to-strong pair tested the larger student's peak exceeded its smaller teacher's own ({{Scaling Properties of Same-Family OPD (arXiv 2609.32722)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32722}}).
  • It depends on how long you train. Gemma 3 trained the same student with a small and a large teacher: "for short training horizons, the smaller teacher is better, but the trend is reversed for longer training", which may explain the common finding, since most such studies are short (§5.4). Gemma 2's 7B teacher helped students from 200M to 1B alike (Table 7).
  • Correct the teacher first when you cannot use its original data: Minitron fine-tuned the teacher on about 100B tokens of the distillation set, which "yields over a 6% reduction in LM validation loss" ({{Minitron in practice|https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11796}}).

Reasoning traces: fewer, better, verified

Quality and diversity of the teacher's traces matter more than their number, and keeping only verified-correct traces (rejection sampling) is standard. s1 fine-tuned Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on 1,000 traces distilled from Gemini Thinking Experimental, chosen for quality, difficulty and diversity: 50.0 on AIME 2024, against 53.3 for all 59K candidates (inside its 95% interval) and 36.7 for 1,000 chosen at random ({{s1, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393}}). At the smallest scale, a Qwen 3.8 4B query planner was taught with 400 GPT-6 Astra trajectories (440 generated, 40 kept for validation) through a LoRA ({{PEFT|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba08eef56b3e3b4eb}}).

Evaluate beyond the teacher's benchmarks

A student inherits its teacher's style and quirks, including what it refuses and how it fails. Check robustness and refusals independently, and remember that a student distilled on benchmark-like prompts looks better on benchmarks than in use ({{Reward Hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}} covers optimising a proxy).

When distillation does not pay (four cases)
  • T5Gemma 2 dropped the distillation its predecessor used: UL2 with and without it scored within 0.4 points, not worth the data-loading overhead ({{Encoder-Decoder Gemma and T5Gemma 2|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}).
  • Ettin's small encoders, trained without distillation, beat distilled baselines: 87.2 GLUE for the 68M against DistilRoBERTa's 83.8 ({{Seq vs Seq (Ettin)|n:3c65c17b0d0d81139cffea35080afe2b}}).
  • At the frontier there is no stronger teacher: distillation copies, it does not discover ({{Distil or RL?|#s-rl}}).
  • Self-distillation in agentic RL has not yet beaten plain GRPO reliably: ten such methods against GRPO on ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, Search-QA and WebShop at three model sizes (132 runs, 12,124.4 H800 GPU-hours) gave "no method consistently outperforming it across settings", strong final scores that hid unstable training, and extra cost that did not consistently buy accuracy ({{A Reality Check on Self-Distillation in Agentic RL (arXiv 2610.19789)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19789}}).

Whose terms

Distilling a proprietary model through its API is sequence-level distillation: collect the answers, fine-tune on them. Frontier terms of service forbid it; the restriction is contractual, not technical, and enforcement is now public. Open-weight teachers often allow it: DeepSeek-R1's licence supports "any modifications and derivative works, including, but not limited to, distillation for training other LLMs", and its students also come under their own base models' terms (Apache 2.0 for the Qwen2.5 ones, the Llama 3.1 and 3.3 licences for the Llama ones) ({{DeepSeek-R1 model card, §7|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1}}). Whose terms a pipeline is built on is a design decision.

The September 2026 accusations, the counterweight, and a correction (dated; news)

The old page added that labs watermark or perturb the log-probabilities they expose unconfirmed.

  • The accusations. On 10 September 2026 Anthropic said it had identified and disrupted distillation attacks from seven labs based in China, through proxy services using false identities, fake or stolen cards and stolen API keys. It attributes over 151 million exchanges to Alibaba between May and July 2026, peaking at "nearly 3 million exchanges per day launched from more than 3,500 fraudulent accounts", in two pools of accounts, the first of "nearly 5,000"; over 23 million to Moonshot ({{Anthropic threat report, September 2026|https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026}}). The Batch adds that several labs routed their own customers' queries to Claude and presented the answers as their own ({{issue 371|https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-371/}}). These are accusations, unadjudicated.
  • The counterweight. The Batch's editorial note: "You can't distill your way to a frontier model", and the accused labs "have achieved significant technical breakthroughs, many of them published openly", which matches the technical point: a student copies what its teacher was asked to do.
  • The other direction. Y Combinator's Garry Tan argued for "an American distillation regime": US open-weight labs should be free to distil US frontier models through the front door ({{TechCrunch, 11 September 2026|https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/}}).
