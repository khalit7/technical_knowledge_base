Prune, then distil: the token budgets

Pruning starts the student from the teacher's own weights instead of from random ones, so it has much less to learn. NVIDIA's recipe ranks every attention head, MLP channel, embedding dimension and layer by its effect on activations over a small calibration set (1,024 samples), cuts the least important to the target size in one shot, then heals the result with logit distillation from the unpruned model. Its best practices: prefer width pruning to depth pruning, and "train the largest one and prune+distill iteratively to smaller LLMs" ({{Minitron, §4.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14679}}). The headline is the token budget, drawn here against the tokens the teacher (or the paper's comparison model) was trained on.

Minitron prices training the 15B, 8B and 4B Nemotron-4 sizes from scratch at (4.4 + 2.5 + 1.2) × 1017 FLOPs per step, against (4.4 + 2.5/40 + 1.2/40) × 1017 when the small two are pruned from the big one: derived 1.80 times cheaper, the paper's 1.8. The saving is bounded by the big model, which still has to be trained in full.
  • Not every pruned model is cheap. Llama 3.2 1B and 3B were pruned from Llama 3.1 8B in one shot and then pretrained on "up to 9T tokens" with the 8B's and 70B's logits as token-level targets. Their card prices it: 370K and 460K H100-hours of training, plus 86K hours just to generate the teachers' logits, derived 10.4% on top of the students' own training. (The card's "916k" cumulative total does not match its own table's 833K + 86K = 919K.) ({{Llama 3.2 model card|https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama3_2/MODEL_CARD.md}}; the parent recipe: {{Llama 3 Herd|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}.)
  • Distillation without pruning, for the whole of pretraining. Gemma 2's 2B and 9B were trained on 2T and 8T tokens of teacher distributions, "more than 50×" the compute-optimal amount (by 20 tokens per non-embedding parameter it is 49 and 48 times, derived, so the report's rule is not stated). The ablation that justified it: a 2B trained on 500B tokens scored 60.3 from scratch and 67.7 distilled from a 7B teacher (Table 6), and the gain held from 200M to 1B parameters (Table 7) ({{Gemma 2|https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00118}}). Gemma 3 distilled every size, 1B to 27B, on 2T to 14T tokens ({{Gemma 3|https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19786}}).
  • Pruning without distillation works too, less well: Sheared-LLaMA pruned LLaMA 2 7B to 2.7B and healed it with 50B tokens of plain continued pretraining ({{Sheared LLaMA|https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06694}}); the Minitron papers' ablations found distillation recovers more than conventional fine-tuning (Minitron in practice, Figure 7).

Defaults reproduce Minitron's 40x and 1.8x, the Llama-3.1-Minitron 150x (as 159.6, which the paper rounds down) and MN-Minitron's 40x independently from the papers' token counts; Gemma 2's "more than 50x" does not reproduce exactly at 20 tokens per parameter (49 and 48), shown as such.

The small tier in 2026

The 0.5B to 9B tier is now genuinely capable: tool use, a thinking mode, 128K context and images in some. Three things drove it: better data, token budgets far past Chinchilla's 20 per parameter, and distillation from frontier teachers. The {{Small models compared|#t-small}} tab places 110 small open-weight models on Artificial Analysis's own measurements over time; the lab names below are the ones worth knowing, and how each was made.

Qwen3 0.6B to 14B (Alibaba)

Strong-to-weak distillation: off-policy, then on-policy logit distillation from Qwen3-32B or 235B-A22B, with both thinking modes in one model ({{Qwen3 §4.5|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a19006e6b096a6e14b}}). Lab page: {{Alibaba: Qwen|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fd8c36ce06672bb235}}.

SmolLM3 3B (Hugging Face)

The fully open blueprint: 11.2T pretraining tokens in three stages, then 1.8B tokens of SFT (1B without reasoning, 0.8B with), much of it generated by "prompting Qwen3-32B in reasoning mode", and preference pairs with Qwen3-32B's answer as chosen and Qwen3-0.6B's as rejected. Claims to beat Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B and to stay competitive with 4B models reported ({{SmolLM3 blog|https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3}}, 8 July 2025).

Gemma 4 (Google)

E2B and E4B for devices, a dense 12B Unified and 31B, and 26B A4B, a mixture of experts with 25.2B weights and 3.8B active per token (8 of 128 experts plus a shared one). You pay for 26B of weights in memory to run about 4B of matmuls per token: the right trade when latency-bound and memory-rich, the wrong one on a phone. Apache 2.0 ({{Google DeepMind page|n:3c65c17b0d0d8173a0aafcb27cf7a5f3}}).

Phi-4 family (Microsoft)

14B and mini (3.8B): the synthetic-data lineage. Phi-4's pretraining and DPO data were generated with GPT-4o, sequence-level distillation at scale, and the report notes it surpasses its teacher on STEM questions ({{Phi-4 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08905}}). Strong benchmarks; its "mixed real-world reputation" is the old page's judgement unconfirmed, and a generator that has seen the evaluations makes contamination hard to rule out ({{Other notable providers|n:3c65c17b0d0d8154ba44e521a4ed8749}}).

Llama 3.2 1B and 3B (Meta)

Pruned from Llama 3.1 8B, then pretrained on up to 9T tokens against the 8B's and 70B's logits (above). Lab page: {{Meta: Llama|n:3c65c17b0d0d81949ad8e17bcb3567f1}}.

Nemotron Nano (NVIDIA) and OLMo 2 1B (Ai2)

The pruning-and-distillation lane and the fully open lane. Nemotron Nano 9B v2 was pruned from a 12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer trained on 20T tokens and healed with about 136B tokens of forward-KL distillation ({{Nemotron Nano 2 §4|https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14444}}). OLMo publishes weights, data and code at every size ({{Ai2: OLMo|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e08697df661f83c3ac}}).

MiniCPM5-2B (OpenBMB)

A dense on-device model under Apache 2.0 for local assistants and coding, averaging 53.9 across 34 benchmarks, the lab's own figure reported ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B}}). The card calls it 2.5B; Artificial Analysis counts 2.6B and measured 12.5 on its Intelligence Index v4.3, the third-highest measured score under 16B on 3 October 2026.

K2 Horizon 0.9B, 3.7B, 7B (IFM)

The small tier of a fully open fleet, with the lab claiming state of the art at all three sizes reported ({{Pretraining|n:3c65c17b0d0d814f82cffd0d5c0dd5ba}}). Because the training data shipped with the weights, the claim can be audited for contamination rather than argued about. Independent check: Artificial Analysis measured 20.6 and 15.6 for the 7B and 3.7B, the two highest measured scores of any open model under 16B; the 0.9B scored 3.0 ({{Small models compared|#t-small}}).

The recurring recipe: overtrain a small dense model on the best data available, distil from a frontier teacher (increasingly on-policy), then run the same post-training pipeline as the big models (SFT, preference optimisation, RL with verifiable rewards; see {{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO Family, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}).

Whose terms: the contractual layer

Distilling a proprietary model through its API is the same mechanism as sequence-level distillation: collect the teacher's answers, fine-tune on them. That is why frontier terms of service restrict it, and, the old page added, why labs watermark or perturb the log-probabilities they expose unconfirmed. The restriction is contractual rather than technical, and enforcement is now public.

  • The accusations. On 10 September 2026 Anthropic said it had identified and disrupted distillation attacks from seven labs based in China, through proxy services using false identities, fake or stolen cards and stolen API keys. It attributes over 151 million exchanges to Alibaba between May and July 2026, peaking at "nearly 3 million exchanges per day launched from more than 3,500 fraudulent accounts", in two pools of accounts, the first of "nearly 5,000"; over 23 million to Moonshot ({{Anthropic threat report, September 2026|https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026}}). The Batch adds that several labs routed their own customers' queries to Claude and presented the answers as their own ({{issue 371|https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-371/}}). These are accusations, unadjudicated.
  • The counterweight. The Batch's editorial note: "You can't distill your way to a frontier model", and the accused labs "have achieved significant technical breakthroughs, many of them published openly". That matches the technical point above: a student copies what its teacher was asked to do.
  • The other direction. Y Combinator's Garry Tan argued for "an American distillation regime": US open-weight labs should be free to distil US frontier models through the front door ({{TechCrunch, 11 September 2026|https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/}}).
Corrected from the old page
The old page said Alibaba ran 151 million exchanges "through about 5,000 fraudulent accounts between May and June 2026", and attributed the counterweight to Andrew Ng. Anthropic's report gives May to July, a peak from more than 3,500 accounts, and nearly 5,000 as the first of two account pools; the counterweight is The Batch's unsigned editorial note in issue 371, not Ng's letter (checked in the {{21 September tech news issue|n:3e25c17b0d0d813dba03fa81f5d8cc39}}).

Whose terms you build a distillation pipeline on is a design decision, not a formality. Open-weight teachers often allow it explicitly: DeepSeek-R1's licence section says the series supports "any modifications and derivative works, including, but not limited to, distillation for training other LLMs", and notes that its students also come under the terms of their own base models (Apache 2.0 for the Qwen2.5 ones, the Llama 3.1 and 3.3 licences for the Llama ones) ({{DeepSeek-R1 model card, §7|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1}}).

Practical notes

A shared vocabulary, or no logits

Logit distillation compares two distributions over the same vocabulary, position by position. Across model families the vocabularies differ, and so do the positions: the same text splits into different tokens. Real tokenizations from this page's data script:

With different splits there is no position at which to compare, so across families you either distil at the sequence level (generated text works with any tokenizer) or use a cross-tokenizer loss such as ULD, which "uses optimal transport theory to enable distillation across different architectures and tokenizers" ({{Boizard et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12030}}).

The capacity gap

  • A much stronger teacher is not always better. "The student network performance degrades when the gap between student and teacher is large"; an intermediate teacher assistant bridges it ({{Mirzadeh et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03393}}). On-policy distillation is the other mitigation: the student is graded on what it can actually produce.
  • It depends on how long you train. Gemma 3 trained the same student with a small and a large teacher: "for short training horizons, the smaller teacher is better, but the trend is reversed for longer training", which they suspect explains the common finding, since most such studies are short (§5.4). Gemma 2's 7B teacher helped students from 200M to 1B alike (Table 7).
  • Correct the teacher first when you cannot use its original data: Minitron fine-tuned the teacher on about 100B tokens of the distillation set, which "yields over a 6% reduction in LM validation loss" ({{Minitron in practice|https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11796}}).

Reasoning traces: fewer, better, verified

Quality and diversity of the teacher's traces matter more than their number, and filtering for verified-correct traces (rejection sampling) is standard: DeepSeek-R1 kept only correct samples from its RL checkpoint. s1 fine-tuned Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on 1,000 traces distilled from Gemini Thinking Experimental and chosen for quality, difficulty and diversity: 50.0 on AIME 2024, against 53.3 for all 59K candidates (a difference inside its 95% interval) and 36.7 for 1,000 chosen at random ({{s1, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19393}}). The same lever at the smallest scale: a Qwen 3.8 4B query planner was taught with 400 GPT-6 Astra trajectories (440 generated, 40 kept for validation) through a LoRA ({{PEFT|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba08eef56b3e3b4eb}}).

Evaluate beyond the benchmarks

Distilled models inherit their teacher's style and can overfit its quirks, including what it refuses and how it fails. Check robustness and refusal behaviour independently of the teacher's benchmark suite, and remember that a student distilled on benchmark-like prompts looks better on benchmarks than in use ({{Reward Hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}} covers the general failure of optimising a proxy).

When distillation does not pay

  • T5Gemma 2 dropped the distillation its predecessor used: UL2 with and without it scored within 0.4 points, not worth the data-loading overhead ({{Encoder-Decoder Gemma and T5Gemma 2|n:3d45c17b0d0d81a9a22ffaada508db5c}}).
  • Ettin's small encoders, trained without distillation, beat distilled baselines: 87.2 GLUE for the 68M against DistilRoBERTa's 83.8 ({{Seq vs Seq (Ettin)|n:3c65c17b0d0d81139cffea35080afe2b}}).
  • At the frontier there is no stronger teacher: distillation copies, it does not discover ({{Distil or RL?|#s-rl}}).
