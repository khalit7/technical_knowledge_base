Over-optimisation curves

Gao, Schulman and Hilton's fitted gold-reward curves for every proxy reward-model size they trained, 3M to 3B parameters, for best-of-n and for RL (PPO, no KL penalty), with a 1.2B policy and 90,000 training comparisons ({{arXiv 2210.10760|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760}}; the Reading tab's {{"The curve"|#t-read}} explains the forms). The question it answers: across reward-model sizes, how far can you optimise before the true reward turns over, and how high does it get?

Try: move the reward-model size and watch the peak move later and higher (β, the bend, shrinks with size). Switch to RL: the same reward model tolerates several times more KL, because RL "consumes" KL faster than best-of-n for the same optimisation. Choose "Both" to see the two methods for one size on one axis. Drag the KL marker to read the gold score at any distance.

Where the numbers come from, and what they do not reproduce

The paper prints its functional forms but no coefficient values: αbon, βbon and βRL appear only as dots in Figure 3, and αRL, held "constant across all RM sizes", is never given as a number ({{§3.2, Figure 3|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760#S3.F3}}). The values here are read from figure, to about ±0.003 for α and ±0.002 for β; no data or code was released.

  • BoN reproduces Figure 1a, independently: the Figure 3 coefficients give the 3M model a peak gold of 0.50 at KL 4.2, as Figure 1a shows (about 0.50 near KL 4 to 5). For 3B at KL 10 they give 1.19 against about 1.24 plotted.
  • RL does not reproduce Figure 1b with Figure 3c's β: with αRL = 0.35 (estimated from Figure 1b's fit lines, 0.34 to 0.35 unconfirmed), the 3M curve would return to zero at KL 55, where Figure 1b's 3M fit returns to zero near KL 95. Scaling every βRL by 0.88 matches Figure 1b (3M peak 0.55 near KL 13, zero near KL 94), so that is the default, matched by construction. The paper does not explain the 12% gap; switch the source above to see both.
  • Only the gold curve is drawn: the authors "were unable to obtain a satisfactory fit" for the proxy score, which grows "roughly linearly in sqrt(KL)" and keeps rising (§3.1, §3.2). Scores are in units of the gold reward model's standard deviation, 0 = the initial policy.
  • Solid lines span the KL range the paper measured (BoN to 10 nats, RL to about 100); dashed lines extrapolate the fitted forms beyond it.

Every size

Peaks derived from the forms: BoN d* = α/2β, gold α²/4β; RL d* = eα/β − 1, gold βd*, back to zero at d = eα/β; KL = d². Recomputed in src/recompute.py. * RL columns use the βRL source and αRL chosen above. The non-monotone step at 1.2B is in the plotted dots (1.2B's βRL dot sits above 680M's).

Three things the curves imply

  • Bigger reward models buy distance, not immunity. Every size turns over; under BoN a 3B proxy moves the peak from KL 4 (3M) to about 13, past the 10 nats the paper measured.
  • KL is not a common currency. At 3M the RL peak sits at about 3 times the BoN peak's KL with a similar gold score derived; the paper concludes "KL distance is an inadequate metric for quantity of (over)optimization" across methods.
  • A KL penalty does not appear here. In the paper's runs the penalty moved where training stopped along this same curve and did not raise it (§3.6); the parent page's KL-leash animation shows a run stopping near the peak ({{Topic: llm-training-and-post-training|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}}).
