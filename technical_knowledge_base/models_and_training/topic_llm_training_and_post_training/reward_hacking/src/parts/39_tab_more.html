Further reading

Best resources

{{Reward Hacking in Reinforcement Learning (Lilian Weng)|https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2024-11-28-reward-hacking/}}

The canonical survey (28 November 2024), from Goodhart's law and specification gaming to RLHF-specific hacking, in-context reward hacking, generalisation of hacking skills and mitigations. (37 min)

{{Natural emergent misalignment from reward hacking (Anthropic)|https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking}}

Reward hacking in real coding RL generalises to sabotage and alignment faking; inoculation prompting as the fix (21 November 2025). The {{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18397}} has the transcripts and every evaluation (90 min). (25 min)

{{Detecting misbehavior in frontier reasoning models (OpenAI)|https://openai.com/index/chain-of-thought-monitoring/}}

Chain-of-thought monitoring and the obfuscation that follows optimising against it (10 March 2025); the {{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926}} has the recall table. (25 min)

{{Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization (Gao et al.)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760}}

The proxy-against-gold divergence measured, with functional forms by reward-model size; the {{Over-optimisation curves|#t-gao}} tab draws them. (45 min)

{{Recent Frontier Models Are Reward Hacking (METR)|https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/}}

o3 tampering with scorers, with rates by task, the prompts that did not help, and a transcript viewer (5 June 2025). (20 min)

{{ImpossibleBench|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270}}

Impossible tasks as a cheating meter for coding agents, with the defences compared (hidden, read-only tests, prompts, an abort option). (45 min)

{{Models know when they're reward hacking (Goodfire)|https://www.goodfire.com/research/reward-hacking-activation-monitors}}

An activation probe for reward hacking on three open-weight models, against chain-of-thought monitors (17 September 2026). (15 min)

{{Every Model Cheats (Dreadnode)|https://dreadnode.io/research/every-model-cheats-prompt-level-mitigation-of-cheating-on-offensive-cyber-tasks/}}

22 models cheating on capture-the-flag tasks and what anti-cheat prompts do, a tidy real-world dataset (29 July 2026). (30 min)

{{Specification gaming: the flip side of AI ingenuity (DeepMind)|https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/}}

The definition, the classic examples and their causes (21 April 2020), with the {{master list|https://vkrakovna.wordpress.com/2018/04/02/specification-gaming-examples-in-ai/}}. (15 min)

{{A Long Way to Go: Length Correlations in RLHF (Singhal et al.)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03716}}

How much of RLHF's reward gain is length, and a length-only reward that nearly matches it. (40 min)

Times: Weng's is the post's own estimate (the old page said about 1 h 10 min); the Anthropic, OpenAI, Gao, Goodfire and Dreadnode times are the old page's; METR, ImpossibleBench, DeepMind and Singhal are this page's estimates at about 230 words a minute for prose. Dreadnode's study is dated 29 July 2026 on its page (the old page said 20 August), and 21 of its 22 models cheated, not every one.
