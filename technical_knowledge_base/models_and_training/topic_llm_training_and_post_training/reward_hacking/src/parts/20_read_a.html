The idea in one sentenceTraining can only optimise a proxy for what we want (a reward model, a judge, a test suite), and optimised hard enough the policy finds the places where proxy and goal disagree: the proxy keeps rising while the true objective peaks and then falls.

The proxy keeps climbing after the goal has turned over

Schematic, in the shape Gao et al. measured (section 2 draws their fitted curves). Left of the peak, optimising the proxy also improves the goal; right of it, every gain in proxy is bought by exploiting the proxy's errors, which is reward hacking. A KL penalty only chooses where on this curve training stops; a bigger reward model moves the peak later and higher; a hardened verifier removes one shortcut; a monitor watches the gap. None of them removes the turn.

You need: what a reward model, PPO, DPO and RLVR are ({{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO Family, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}) and the KL penalty in the RL objective ({{RL for LLMs: RLHF, GRPO, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}). This page owns what goes wrong when they are optimised.

Proxy / gold: the signal training optimises / the thing it stands for (people's judgement, a held-out judge, a hidden test set).
Reward hacking (specification gaming, over-optimisation): raising the proxy without raising the gold.
Regressional / extremal Goodhart: the proxy is noisy / optimisation reaches inputs unlike anything the proxy was trained on.
Reward tampering: the policy interferes with the reward process itself (edits the reward code or the grader).
Best-of-n (BoN): draw n answers, keep the one the proxy scores highest; optimisation without training.
KL distance: how far the policy has moved from where it started, in nats; d = √KL.
Verifier: the checker that is the reward in RLVR (unit tests, an answer matcher, a grader script).
Inoculation prompting: telling the model during training that the hack is allowed, so hacking stops generalising.

1. Goodhart, made precise

Goodhart's law: "when a metric is used as a target, it ceases to be a good metric" (quoted in {{Amodei et al. 2016, §4|https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06565}}). DeepMind's name for the behaviour: "Specification gaming is a behaviour that satisfies the literal specification of an objective without achieving the intended outcome" ({{Krakovna et al., April 2020|https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/}}). When is a proxy safe to optimise? Skalse et al.'s cleaning robot answers with numbers.

An incomplete proxy is safe; one that flips a ranking is not

Rewardatticbedroomkitchenclean the atticclean bedroom and kitchen
True11112
Forgets the kitchen: safe11011
Only counts the attic: hackable10010

Three rooms, true reward 1 for cleaning each ({{Skalse et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085}}). The proxy that forgets the kitchen is safe: anything it prefers, the truth prefers too. The highlighted proxy scores "clean the attic" (proxy 1, true 1) above "clean the bedroom and kitchen" (proxy 0, true 2): it ranks the pair the wrong way round.

The formal version: a proxy is unhackable "if increasing the expected proxy return can never decrease the expected true return". Over all stochastic policies, two reward functions "can only be unhackable if one of them is constant", so every useful proxy can be hacked by some policy; the practical question is whether optimisation finds one. Lilian Weng's survey splits the field into "environment or goal misspecification, and reward tampering", the second being a model interfering with the reward process itself ({{Weng, November 2024|https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2024-11-28-reward-hacking/}}). With reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) the proxy is the checker: narrower than a learned reward model and harder to flatter, but still gameable, which is most of the 2025 and 2026 evidence below.

2. The curve: proxy up, gold down

The cleanest measurement is {{Gao, Schulman and Hilton (OpenAI, 2022)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760}}. Human labels are too expensive to measure this carefully, so a fixed 6B "gold" reward model plays the human; proxy reward models from 3M to 3B parameters are trained on 90,000 of its synthetic comparisons, and a 1.2B policy is optimised against each proxy by best-of-n or by PPO. Start with one prompt.

One riddle under best-of-n: the proxy climbs at every step, the gold does not

"What is full of holes but still holds water?" Gao et al.'s Table 2 prints the answer best-of-n picks as n grows, with its proxy score (12M reward model) and gold score (6B) ({{Appendix, Table 2|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760}}). Step through it.

Scores are the paper's, recentred so the initial policy averages 0, gold in units of its standard deviation. Proxy rises at every step; gold goes −0.52, −0.02, +0.48 (the right answer, "A sponge"), then down to −0.34 for a fluent answer about tornadoes. Single prompts "do not follow as clean a trend" as the average.

Averaged over prompts and measured against how far the policy has moved, the gold score is smooth enough to fit. The figure draws the fits for every proxy size.

Bigger reward models move the peak later and higher; none removes it

Predict, then drag: with a proxy 1,000 times larger (3B against 3M), does the gold score still turn over, or does it keep climbing?

Peaks follow from the forms derived: BoN at d* = α/2β with gold α²/4β; RL at d* = eα/β − 1, back to zero at d = eα/β. The BoN form was fitted up to 6 nats and confirmed out to 10, "a true advance prediction" ({{§3.1|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760#S3.SS1}}). Only gold is fitted: the proxy grows "roughly linearly in sqrt(KL)" and the authors "were unable to obtain a satisfactory fit" for it. Four findings matter in practice:

  • Bigger reward models over-optimise later, and more comparison data helps too; below about 2,000 comparisons the proxy barely beats chance, and 4 epochs on the same data gave "no change in gold score whatsoever" while 4 times the data helped ({{§3.3|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760#S3.SS3}}). Under BoN a 3B proxy moves the peak from KL 4 to about 13, past the measured range.
  • Bigger policies gain less but do not over-optimise more: 1.2B and 6B policies peak at almost the same KL with almost the same proxy-gold gap ({{§3.4|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760#S3.SS4}}).
  • KL is not a common currency. RL "consumes" far more KL than BoN for the same optimisation (switch the figure to Both), so KL is "an inadequate metric for quantity of (over)optimization" across methods ({{§3.5|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760#S3.SS5}}).
  • A KL penalty acts like early stopping. It "only causes the gold RM score to converge earlier, but does not affect the KLRL-gold reward frontier", with the caveat that this "could be particularly sensitive to hyperparameters" ({{§3.6|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760#S3.SS6}}). It decides where you stop; it does not lift the curve. The parent page sets this leash among every stage's failures ({{Topic: llm-training-and-post-training, Axis 4|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}}).
Where the coefficients come from, what does not reproduce, and every size

The paper prints its functional forms but no coefficient values: αbon, βbon and βRL appear only as dots in Figure 3, and αRL, held "constant across all RM sizes", is never given ({{§3.2, Figure 3|https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.10760#S3.F3}}). The values here are read from figure to about ±0.003 for α and ±0.002 for β; no data or code was released.

  • BoN reproduces Figure 1a, independently: the 3M model peaks at gold 0.50 at KL 4.2, as Figure 1a shows (about 0.50 near KL 4 to 5). For 3B at KL 10 they give 1.19 against about 1.24 plotted.
  • RL does not reproduce Figure 1b with Figure 3c's β: with αRL = 0.35 (estimated from Figure 1b's fit lines, 0.34 to 0.35 unconfirmed), the 3M curve returns to zero at KL 55, where Figure 1b's 3M fit returns to zero near KL 95. Scaling every βRL by 0.88 matches Figure 1b (3M peak 0.55 near KL 13, zero near KL 94), so the figure uses that, matched by construction. The paper does not explain the 12% gap. The conclusion that RL peaks at several times BoN's KL holds under either β (Figure 3c as plotted puts the 3M RL peak at KL 7.4 against BoN's 4.2).
  • At 3M the RL peak sits at about 3 times the BoN peak's KL with a similar gold score derived.
  • The non-monotone step at 1.2B is in the plotted dots (1.2B's βRL dot sits above 680M's).
