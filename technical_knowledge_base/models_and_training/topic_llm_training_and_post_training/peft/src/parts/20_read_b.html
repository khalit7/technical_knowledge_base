LoRA, on one real matrix

LoRA ({{Hu et al. 2021|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}) leaves a pretrained matrix W0 frozen and learns its change as the product of two thin matrices:

h = W0x + (α / r) · B A x
W0
the frozen weight, dout × din
A
r × din, starts random (the paper: Gaussian; PEFT: Kaiming-uniform)
B
dout × r, starts at zero, so ΔW = BA = 0 and training begins exactly at the base model
r
the rank: ΔW can point in at most r directions; trainable numbers r(din + dout) instead of dindout
α / r
a fixed scale, so the effective step size does not change when you change r

The animation runs both on a real matrix: the query projection of layer 14 of {{SmolLM2-135M|https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M}} (576 × 576). For the "after" it uses a real fine-tuning update: the same matrix in {{SmolLM2-135M-Instruct|https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M-Instruct}}, which its model card says was made from the base by SFT and then DPO. Full fine-tuning produced that update; LoRA cannot, because it is limited to rank r, so the LoRA mode shows the closest rank-r matrix to it (its truncated SVD, the best any rank-r update can do in squared error). Heatmaps show the top-left 32 × 32 corner; counters are for the whole matrix.

Four practical details sit in that formula:

  • Zero-initialised B. Because ΔW starts at exactly zero, step 0 of training is the base model, and the first gradient step moves only B (the gradient of A is multiplied by B, which is zero). Microsoft's own {{loralib|https://github.com/microsoft/LoRA/blob/main/loralib/layers.py}} initialises A Kaiming-uniform, "different than what is described in the paper but should not affect performance".
  • α / r, and rsLoRA. Dividing by r keeps the optimal learning rate roughly independent of rank; Thinking Machines found the learning curves of different ranks "exactly the same at the beginning of training" ({{LoRA Without Regret|https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/}}). {{rsLoRA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03732}} argues the right divisor is √r, because 1/r "results in slowed learning and stunted performance" at higher ranks; PEFT switches with use_rslora=True. Changing r under one convention while keeping α from a recipe written for the other changes the effective learning rate.
  • LoRA+. {{Hayou et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12354}} show that one learning rate for A and B is suboptimal at large width and give B a larger one: 1 to 2% better and up to about 2 times faster, at the same cost.
  • Merging. After training, W = W0 + (α/r)BA is one ordinary matrix: the merged model has exactly the base's shape and speed. Subtracting the product un-merges it, which is how one base can switch tasks.

The LoRA paper's own animation of a token through LoRA against an adapter, its GPT-3 parameter calculator, and a LoRA you can train in the browser on a task whose true update is known are on the {{LoRA paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}; this page does not repeat them.

How low is the rank of a real update?

LoRA's bet is that the change fine-tuning needs is low-rank. The LoRA paper supported it with GPT-3, where rank 1 to 4 on the query and value matrices already matched larger ranks on its tasks. Full fine-tuning has no such limit, and its updates are not low-rank. Biderman et al. found full fine-tuning on code and math learns "perturbations with a rank that is 10-100× greater than typical LoRA configurations", rising with more data and higher in the MLP than in attention ({{LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673}}).

What this shows, and what it does not:

  • The update is spread over many directions: a median of about 200 of 576 directions to hold 90% for the attention query and output, about 350 for the MLP matrices. A rank-16 LoRA could hold between a quarter and a third of an attention query or output matrix's update and about a sixth of an MLP matrix's.
  • The MLP updates are the most spread out, in line with Biderman et al. and with the advice to put LoRA on the MLP first (next section).
  • It does not show that LoRA would be worse here. Full fine-tuning has no reason to keep its update low-rank, so much of it may be directions that do not matter for the task. LoRA finds a different solution, not a compressed copy of this one; whether it scores as well is measured by training both, as the studies in {{Against full fine-tuning|#s-regret}} do. The share held is an upper bound on how closely any rank-r update could copy this one, nothing more.
  • Some single matrices did change in few directions: layer 0's value projection moved by 33% of its size with 90% of that in 22 directions.

Which matrices to adapt

The LoRA paper adapted only the attention query and value matrices of GPT-3. Practice has moved to every linear layer, especially the MLP:

  • QLoRA could not match full fine-tuning with LoRA on q and v; "LoRA on all linear transformer block layers are required", and the total number of adapters mattered more than the rank ({{QLoRA §4|n:3c65c17b0d0d815a8f4edc33f9583671}}).
  • LoRA Without Regret: attention-only LoRA underperforms MLP-only LoRA "even when we match the number of trainable parameters by using higher rank for attention-only LoRA"; MLP-only performs about as well as MLP plus attention; and "LoRA performs better when applied to all weight matrices, especially MLP and MoE layers" ({{Thinking Machines|https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/lora/}}). QLoRA found MLP plus attention above MLP alone; the two studies differ on that second step only.
  • Biderman et al. recommend targeting "All" modules, at rank 256 when memory allows, because ranks 16 to 64 "tend not to suffice for code tasks".

On a dense model most of an all-linear adapter's parameters sit in the MLP, because the MLP matrices are the widest: on Llama 3 8B at any rank, the three MLP matrices hold 67.5% of the adapter (55,296 of the 81,920 numbers per unit of rank per layer: each matrix costs din + dout; derived from the {{config|https://huggingface.co/NousResearch/Meta-Llama-3-8B/blob/main/config.json}}). The {{Trainable parameters|#t-count}} tab breaks any configuration down matrix by matrix, including a mixture-of-experts model where the expert matrices dominate.
