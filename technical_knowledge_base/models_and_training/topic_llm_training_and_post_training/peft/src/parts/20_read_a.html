Why PEFT

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning freezes the pretrained model and trains a small set of numbers, usually new ones added beside it. The frozen base is shared; what a task learns lives in the small set.

Three things follow, and they are the reasons to use it:

  • Memory. Training with Adam in mixed precision holds about 16 bytes per trained parameter: the bf16 weight and gradient (2 + 2) and fp32 master weight and two moments (12) ({{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}}). A frozen weight needs no gradient and no optimizer state, only its own 2 bytes. Biderman et al. put the model and optimizer state of a 7B model at 112 GB with Adam and 15.12 GB with Adam plus LoRA, before activations ({{Table S14|https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09673v2#A8.T14}}); derived 112 = 16 × 7 and 15.12 = 2 × 7 + 16 × 0.07, so their row assumes adapters of about 1% of the model. The parent page's {{bytes-per-parameter calculator|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}} (Machinery section) compares full fine-tuning, LoRA and QLoRA for any model size; this page does not repeat it.
  • Cheap per-task copies. A full fine-tune of GPT-3 175B is a 350 GB checkpoint; its LoRA adapter (rank 4 on the query and value matrices) is about 35 MB ({{LoRA §4.2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}). Adapters can be swapped in seconds, or many can be served at once against one base (see {{Serving many adapters|#s-serve}} below; vLLM calls this multi-LoRA).
  • Less forgetting. The base weights never move, and a low-rank change can only move the model so far. Biderman et al. measured it: LoRA "learns less and forgets less" than full fine-tuning (chart in {{Against full fine-tuning|#s-regret}}).

The price is capacity: a small set of numbers can store only so much. For the instruction and preference data of ordinary post-training that is rarely binding; for billions of tokens of new domain text it is (the same section).

The families in brief, oldest first:

  1. Head-only tuning: freeze the backbone, train a task head. The classic transfer-learning baseline; too weak to change how a generative model behaves, because nothing inside the network moves.
  2. Adapters ({{Houlsby et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751}}): small bottleneck MLPs inserted into each block. On GLUE they came within 0.4% of full fine-tuning while adding 3.6% parameters per task. Their cost is latency: the adapter sits in series, so every token runs extra sequential layers, which the LoRA paper measured as a single forward pass of GPT-2 medium 20.7% to 30.3% slower at batch size 1 and 128 tokens, shrinking to 2.2% to 3.0% at batch 32 and 512 tokens ({{LoRA Table 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}). LoRA, which merges away, superseded them.
  3. Soft prompts: learn embeddings instead of words. Prompt tuning ({{Lester et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08691}}) prepends them to the input only, and "closes the gap" with full tuning only as T5 models exceed billions of parameters. Prefix tuning ({{Li and Liang 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190}}) prepends keys and values at every layer, about 0.1% of the parameters; P-tuning v2 ({{Liu et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07602}}) made deep prompts match fine-tuning on understanding tasks with 0.1% to 3% of the parameters. No architecture change and tiny counts, but they spend context positions, train less stably and are weaker than LoRA; niche today. On LLaMA-7B commonsense reasoning, prefix tuning averaged 64.6 against LoRA's 74.7 ({{DoRA Table 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09353v6}}, baselines from LLM-Adapters).
  4. (IA)³ ({{Liu et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05638}}): rescale activations with learned vectors; among the fewest parameters of any method here (fewer than LoRA by about 80 times on Llama 3 8B), built for few-shot fine-tuning of T0 (the T-Few recipe).
  5. LoRA ({{Hu et al. 2021|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}): learn the change to a frozen matrix as a product of two thin matrices. The default today; the next three sections are about it.
  6. LoRA's descendants each change one thing. QLoRA stores the base in 4 bits; DoRA separates magnitude from direction; LoRA-FA ({{Zhang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03303}}) freezes A, arguing the update collapses to a regression on B, which saves activation memory; VeRA ({{Kopiczko et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11454}}) shares one frozen random pair across all layers and trains only scaling vectors, for storage-bound settings with many adapters; rsLoRA and LoRA+ change only the scale and the learning rates (next section).
