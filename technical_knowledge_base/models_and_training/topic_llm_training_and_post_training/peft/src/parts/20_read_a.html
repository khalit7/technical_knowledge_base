The idea in one screen

PEFT freezes the pretrained weights and trains a small set of new numbers beside them. LoRA, the default, learns each weight matrix's change as the product of two thin matrices, ΔW = (α/r)BA: training holds about 2 bytes per frozen parameter instead of 16, each task ships as megabytes instead of a copy of the model, and the change folds back into the weights for serving.

One query matrix of Llama 3 8B, to scale: full fine-tuning trains all 16,777,216 numbers; LoRA at rank 16 trains 131,072 (0.78%) beside the frozen matrix

Shapes from the {{config|https://huggingface.co/NousResearch/Meta-Llama-3-8B/blob/main/config.json}} (q_proj is 4,096 × 4,096). derived training state: 16 bytes per trained number (bf16 weight and gradient, fp32 master copy and two Adam moments, the {{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}} accounting) and 2 bytes per frozen one, so 268 MB against 35.7 MB for this matrix at rank 16. B and A are drawn at their true thickness, at least 1.5 px.

What follows from freezing the base, with the numbers:

  • Memory. A frozen weight needs no gradient and no optimiser state. Llama 3 8B needs about 128 GB of training state for full fine-tuning, 16.7 GB for LoRA at rank 16 and 6.4 GB for QLoRA, before activations ({{Memory and QLoRA|#s-mem}} has the calculator).
  • Cheap per-task copies. A full fine-tune of GPT-3 175B is a 350 GB checkpoint; its LoRA adapter (rank 4 on the query and value matrices) is about 35 MB ({{LoRA §4.2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}; derived 18.9M numbers × 2 bytes = 37.7 MB, 36 MiB). Adapters swap in seconds, and many can be served at once against one base ({{Serving many adapters|#s-serve}}).
  • Less forgetting, and less learning. The base never moves and a low-rank change can move the model only so far: LoRA "learns less and forgets less" than full fine-tuning (Biderman et al., in {{Against full fine-tuning|#s-regret}}). For the instruction and preference data of ordinary post-training the capacity is rarely binding; for billions of tokens of new domain text it is.

You need: what a matrix's rank and its singular value decomposition (SVD) are; where Adam's 16 bytes per parameter come from ({{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}}); the post-training stages a LoRA usually runs ({{Alignment|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}).

Why LoRA won. Adapters sit in series, so every token runs extra sequential layers: the LoRA paper measured a forward pass of GPT-2 medium 20.7% to 30.3% slower at batch size 1 and 128 tokens, shrinking to 2.2% to 3.0% at batch 32 and 512 tokens ({{LoRA Table 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}). Soft prompts spend context positions, train less stably and score lower: on LLaMA-7B commonsense reasoning, prefix tuning averaged 64.6 against LoRA's 74.7 ({{DoRA Table 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09353v6}}, baselines from LLM-Adapters). LoRA sits in parallel and merges away, so it costs nothing at serving time; its descendants each change one thing (QLoRA the base's storage, DoRA magnitude against direction, LoRA-FA and VeRA what is trained, rsLoRA and LoRA+ the scale and learning rates).

The families one by one, with their papers (optional)
  1. Head-only tuning: freeze the backbone, train a task head. The classic transfer-learning baseline; too weak to change how a generative model behaves, because nothing inside the network moves.
  2. Adapters ({{Houlsby et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751}}): small bottleneck MLPs inserted into each block. On GLUE they came within 0.4% of full fine-tuning while adding 3.6% parameters per task.
  3. Soft prompts: learn embeddings instead of words. Prompt tuning ({{Lester et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08691}}) prepends them to the input only, and "closes the gap" with full tuning only as T5 models exceed billions of parameters. Prefix tuning ({{Li and Liang 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190}}) prepends keys and values at every layer, about 0.1% of the parameters; P-tuning v2 ({{Liu et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07602}}) made deep prompts match fine-tuning on understanding tasks with 0.1% to 3% of the parameters. Niche today.
  4. (IA)³ ({{Liu et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05638}}): rescale activations with learned vectors; among the fewest parameters of any method here (fewer than LoRA by about 80 times on Llama 3 8B), built for few-shot fine-tuning of T0 (the T-Few recipe).
  5. LoRA ({{Hu et al. 2021|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}): learn the change to a frozen matrix as a product of two thin matrices.
  6. LoRA-FA ({{Zhang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03303}}) freezes A, arguing the update collapses to a regression on B, which saves activation memory; VeRA ({{Kopiczko et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11454}}) shares one frozen random pair across all layers and trains only scaling vectors, for storage-bound settings with many adapters.
