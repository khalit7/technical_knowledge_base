Stochastic sampling: the standard stack

Sampling draws the next token at random, but rarely from the raw distribution: pure sampling at T = 1 lets the long tail of unlikely tokens in, and a few bad draws derail the text (Holtzman et al. measured perplexity 22.73 for pure sampling against 12.38 for human text). The standard stack reshapes the distribution first, in this order: temperature, then truncation, then renormalise, then sample.

  • Temperature T: divide the logits by T before the softmax. T < 1 sharpens (more deterministic; T → 0 approaches greedy); T = 1 is the raw distribution; T > 1 flattens towards uniform (more random, more "creative"). Reasoning models often want T around 0.6 to 1.0 to keep exploring (settings below).
  • Top-k keeps only the k most probable tokens, renormalises and samples ({{Fan et al., 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04833}}, who used k = 10 for stories). Its flaw: a fixed k ignores the shape of the distribution. When the distribution is sharp, k lets junk in; when it is flat, k cuts off good options.
  • Top-p (nucleus) keeps the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability reaches p (for example 0.9), renormalises and samples ({{Holtzman et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09751}}, Eq. 2: the smallest V(p) with Σx∈V(p) P(x) ≥ p). The set adapts to the distribution; top-p was the long-time default for open-ended generation.
  • Min-p keeps tokens whose probability is at least pmin × P(top token), for example 0.05 to 0.1 of the maximum ({{Nguyen et al., ICLR 2025 oral|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01082}}). The cut scales with the model's confidence: confident steps become near-greedy, uncertain steps stay diverse. Its authors report better quality and diversity than top-p especially at high temperature; it is implemented in Hugging Face transformers, vLLM, SGLang and llama.cpp, and llama.cpp enables it by default (min-p 0.05, {{common.h|https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/common/common.h}}). Whether it is better is disputed: see the box below.

Try: the confident step at T 1.5. Top-p 0.9 now keeps 5,166 tokens (exact full-vocabulary count; the animation's binned estimate says 5,209), because at T 1.5 "Canberra" falls from 96% to 59% and about 30% of the mass spreads thinly beyond the top 12 tokens, which top-p must collect back token by token measured here; min-p keeps only "Canberra". At the uncertain step and T 2, top-p keeps about 35,700 tokens and min-p 46. That is the whole argument for min-p, on a real distribution.

Disputed: "min-p beats top-p"
The min-p paper reports better quality and diversity on GPQA, GSM8K and AlpacaEval creative writing, and a human preference for min-p. A re-analysis by {{Schaeffer, Kazdan and Denisov-Blanch (June 2025)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13681}} found omitted data and incorrect statistical tests in the human evaluation, found that "min-p did not outperform baselines in quality, diversity, or a trade-off between quality and diversity" once hyperparameter counts were controlled, and noted that the community-adoption figures were removed from the paper as unsubstantiated. What is not in dispute is the mechanism shown above: at high temperature, min-p's cut follows the top token and top-p's does not. Our own GPT-2 run in {{Twelve decoders|#t-deg}} agrees with the mechanism (at T 1.5, min-p 0.1 stays near human perplexity while top-p 0.95 explodes) but measures likelihood under the same model, not quality as judged by people.

The order is not universal

"Temperature, then truncation" is how {{Hugging Face transformers|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/generation/utils.py}} applies its warpers (temperature, top-k, top-p, min-p, typical) and how {{vLLM|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/vllm/v1/sample/sampler.py}} does it (penalties, temperature, min-p, then top-k and top-p). {{llama.cpp|https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/common/common.h}} applies temperature last by default (penalties, DRY, top-n-sigma, top-k, typical, top-p, min-p, XTC, temperature), so its truncation sees the T = 1 distribution and temperature only reshapes the survivors. With llama.cpp's own defaults (top-k 40, top-p 0.95, min-p 0.05) at T 1.5, the uncertain step keeps 14 tokens with temperature last and 34 with temperature first measured here. The {{Sampler lab|#t-lab}} has the switch.

Repetition controls

Blunt instruments, needed less as models improved and still useful for small models:

  • Repetition penalty ({{CTRL, Keskar et al., 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05858}}, who found θ ≈ 1.2 a good balance with greedy sampling): every token already in the context has its logit divided by θ if positive and multiplied by θ if negative ({{transformers' implementation|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/generation/logits_process.py}}, which counts the prompt too).
  • Presence and frequency penalties (the OpenAI-style pair): zj → zj − cj·αfrequency − [cj > 0]·αpresence, where cj counts how often token j was already sampled; "around 0.1 to 1" to reduce repetition somewhat, up to 2 to suppress it strongly at a cost in quality ({{OpenAI docs|https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/advanced-usage}}). Qwen3's model card suggests presence_penalty 0 to 2 against endless repetition, warning of language mixing at high values ({{card|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B}}).
  • No-repeat n-gram bans any token that would complete an n-gram already generated (transformers' no_repeat_ngram_size).

They are blunt because they cannot tell a loop from a correct repeat. In the lab's "pattern" prompt (continue "red, blue, red, blue, red,") the right answer is a word already in the context; a repetition penalty of 1.3 pushes it down. Try it on the {{Sampler lab|#t-lab}}.

Diversity against quality

Every truncation trades diversity for staying in the model's high-probability region. The {{Twelve decoders|#t-deg}} tab measures the trade on GPT-2 small: four passages, 48 new tokens, 16 samples per decoder measured here. The pattern reproduces Holtzman et al.'s Table 1 independently, on a smaller model and different text:

  • Greedy and beam search sit far below human perplexity (2.3 and 2.4 against 31.0 for the real continuation) and repeat: 53% of greedy's 4-grams are repeats.
  • Pure sampling overshoots (47.2); top-p 0.95 at T 1 lands closest to human text (31.6 against 31.0), as nucleus sampling did in the paper (13.13 against 12.38).
  • Raise the temperature and top-p falls apart (perplexity about 3,900 at T 1.5) while min-p 0.1 stays near human text up to T 2 (16.1 at T 1.5, 40.0 at T 2).
