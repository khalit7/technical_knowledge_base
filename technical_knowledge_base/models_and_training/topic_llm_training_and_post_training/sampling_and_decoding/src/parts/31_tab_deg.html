Twelve decoders, real text

The question behind every sampler: across all decoders and temperatures, how does staying probable trade against sounding like a person? This tab reruns the comparison of {{Holtzman et al. (2019)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09751}} on a small scale. GPT-2 small (124M) is given the first 48 tokens of four paragraphs from Jerome K. Jerome's {{Three Men in a Boat|https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/308}} (public domain) and writes 48 more, 16 times per decoder; the real next 48 tokens are the human reference measured here.

Measures, all on GPT-2 small:Perplexity: exp of minus the mean log-probability per token at T = 1 (how surprised the model is by the text; human text is the target, not the minimum)Repetition: share of a text's 4-grams that repeat an earlier 4-gramDiversity: distinct-2 over the 16 samples of a passage (greedy and beam give one text 16 times, so their figure is divided by 16)

Hover a dot for its decoder; on a narrow screen only a few dots are labelled and the table below has every value.

Read the samples

Every sampled decoder starts from the same random seed, so pure sampling and top-p 0.95 often write the same words until the first draw that top-p's cut removes. Texts are GPT-2's, unedited except that newlines are shown as ↵ and any em-dash the model wrote is shown as "--". Three samples per passage are shown, skipping any that match a short list of slurs, sexual and violent words (GPT-2 at high temperature produces them); the measures use all 16. Em-dashes in Jerome's text were replaced by commas before tokenising. Script: src/make_data.py degen; numbers: src/recompute.py.
