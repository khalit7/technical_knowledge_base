Goodput calculator: failures and checkpoints at any scale

How much of a synchronous run's wall-clock time becomes training, for any cluster size, failure rate, checkpoint pause and restart time. It uses the two formulas from Reading ({{Failures are routine|#t-read}}): job MTBF = 1 / (servers × failure rate per server-day), and Meta's long-job ETTR with the Young and Daly interval. Try: start at Llama 3 (90.6%), then press 131,072: at the same failure rate a 10-minute restart costs more than the checkpoints do, and even a 10-second pause leaves about half the time. Then lower the restart to 1 minute, which is what in-memory recovery and replica groups buy.

Too short an interval and the pauses dominate; too long and each failure throws away half an interval of work. The optimum sits where the two costs are equal.

What a checkpoint weighs

A floor for the synchronous pause: bytes to write over the storage fabric's bandwidth. Real pauses are longer (serialisation, metadata, the slowest rank): IBM's 7B checkpoint took 148.8 s, an effective 0.56 GB/s if it saved 12 bytes per parameter derived.

Defaults: Llama 3 405B (405,853,388,800 parameters, config) and Tectonic's sustained 2 TB/s (§3.3.1). The per-GPU share uses the GPU count above; Llama 3 reports 1 MB to 4 GB per GPU, because ranks hold different parts of the model.
