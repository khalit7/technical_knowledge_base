Price list

Every training run whose cost has been published or reported, on one log axis, with what it bought and, above all, what the figure includes. A headline price is usually one stage of one run, priced at an assumed GPU rental rate; another is a whole programme with staff. They only compare when they cover the same things, so each figure says what it covers.

    How to read it

    • Three units, never mixed. Dollars, GPU-hours and FLOPs each have their own axis. A dollar figure becomes GPU-hours only where the source gives the rate (DeepSeek's $2 per H800-hour); a GPU-hour is not the same work on an A100, an H100 or an H800, so the GPU is named on every figure.
    • Four kinds of number. A filled circle was published by the team; a ring was reported in the press or by an executive; a diamond is a third party's estimate (Epoch AI, Karpathy); a square is this page's arithmetic on published numbers (GPUs times hours), with the formula in the card.
    • Epoch AI's two methods disagree by 2 to 7 times on the same run. Amortised hardware and energy (what owning the chips costs over the run) and cloud list prices (what renting them would cost) are both estimates of the final run alone, in 2023 dollars.
    • Compare like with like. Xiaomi's $2.62M is one final RL stage; Thomson Reuters' $40M is two years of talent and compute, and its own final run cost $450,000. MiMo's stage is 5.8 times Thomson's final run, not a fifteenth of its programme.
    • Cheap runs stand on expensive ones. Every fine-tuning price here leaves out the base model (LLaMA, Qwen) and most leave out the teacher model whose outputs became the data.
