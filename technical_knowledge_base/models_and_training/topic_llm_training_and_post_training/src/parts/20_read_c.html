The machinery under every stage

Every stage runs on the same machinery: a memory budget per parameter, a way of splitting the model across GPUs, a number format, and a cluster that fails. The stages differ mainly in how much of it they need.

Bytes per parameter

Training with Adam in mixed precision holds about 16 bytes per parameter before activations: 2 for bf16 weights, 2 for gradients, 12 for fp32 master weights and two moments ({{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}}). Freezing the base (PEFT) drops that to the 2 bytes of the frozen weights plus a small adapter; storing the frozen base in 4 bits (QLoRA) to about half a byte; serving needs only the weights, in whatever format they ship.

Try: 65B is selected, the QLoRA paper's case: full fine-tuning needs over a terabyte, QLoRA's frozen base 33.5 GB, under one 48 GB card. Pick 405B for Llama 3's size.

Memory can also be bounded by scheduling when each tensor must exist rather than by approximating anything. A September 2026 paper does this for the four memory peaks of long-context MoE training (expert dispatch, vocabulary projection, activation checkpoints, optimiser state): the vocabulary projection's peak falls 86.6%, from 79.5 to 10.6 GiB, for 4.4% more time with an exact loss, and the whole stack trains 120B to 667B MoE models at a 1M-token context on 16 to 64 H200s, 8 to 32 times the context of a tuned FSDP2 baseline, a weak baseline and single runs ({{paper page|n:3e95c17b0d0d8148a1f3d412918234fc}}, arXiv).

Correction
The old page, written from a newsletter abstract, called this paper "the first thing to reach for when sizing a sparse run against a fixed pair of consumer cards". Everything in it is measured on H200 nodes with 2 TB of host memory, 16 to 64 GPUs end to end; two of its four operators are scheduled offload; and it builds on ZeRO-3 and Megatron's vocabulary sharding rather than replacing them ({{paper page|n:3e95c17b0d0d8148a1f3d412918234fc}}, Table 5, §7).

Precision, kernels, clusters

  • Precision: bf16 is the training default; DeepSeek-V3 showed fine-grained FP8 training at 671B, under 0.25% relative loss gap against BF16 in its ablations ({{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}), and Blackwell's NVFP4 and MXFP4 are now arriving for training, not just storage (NVFP4 pretraining).
  • Kernels: FlashAttention computes exact attention in tiles that stay in on-chip memory, moving 9 times fewer bytes through GPU memory on GPT-2 medium and making attention memory linear in sequence length ({{FlashAttention|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e4a3e8e7a2c4771381}}).
  • Clusters: Llama 3 trained at 38 to 43% model FLOPs utilisation on 8,192 to 16,384 H100s ({{Llama 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}); the schedulers, frameworks (Megatron-Core, torchtitan, NeMo) and checkpoint cadence are on {{Training Infrastructure|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c9b2cac997583bf572}}. The Hugging Face Ultra-Scale Playbook distils 4,000+ benchmarked runs into how to combine the splits above.
  • Components fixed before training: the tokenizer (vocabularies grew from 32K to 128K to 256K entries: fewer tokens per text, larger embedding matrices; {{Tokenizers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817187bcd94a1b0f15ea}}) and the positional encoding (rotary embeddings, RoPE, are the default, rotating queries and keys so attention depends only on relative distance, {{RoFormer|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cfa5e9f54459720098}}; extending context means rescaling them during a long-document CPT phase, {{Positional Encodings|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a5b7c9e578fcdfac00}}).
  • The inference end: how a trained model is sampled changes what it appears to have learned. Temperature, then truncation (top-p, or min-p, which keeps tokens above a fraction of the top token's probability), then sampling; greedy decoding and beam search repeat themselves on open-ended text; constrained decoding forces valid JSON or a grammar. Sampling also feeds training: RL and best-of-n draw from the same stack ({{Sampling and Decoding|n:3c65c17b0d0d8115bafcda29a2c7086e}}).

How we got here

The lifecycle was assembled in about seven years, mostly by papers that changed one stage each. The recipes that put them together in the open, stage by stage, are compared in {{Open recipes compared|#t-recipes}}.

2019

Pretraining becomes an experiment you can control: T5 fixes one pipeline and moves one choice at a time; denoising beats plain language modelling, cleaned web text (C4, 745 GB) beats eight times more unfiltered text, and multi-task pretraining then fine-tuning only matches plain pretraining ({{T5|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}). ZeRO and Megatron-LM make models bigger than one GPU trainable.

2020

Scaling laws: loss falls as a smooth power law in parameters, data and compute; the paper's advice to spend compute mostly on parameters was later overturned ({{Kaplan et al.|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b08a1debb0c15cd252}}).

2021

LoRA makes fine-tuning a 35 MB adapter instead of a 350 GB copy ({{LoRA|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}); RoPE ({{RoFormer|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cfa5e9f54459720098}}) and top-1 MoE routing ({{Switch Transformers|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a8b541c9babbf40c94}}) arrive.

2022

Chinchilla: grow parameters and tokens together, about 20 tokens per parameter; a 70B model on 1.4T tokens beat the 280B Gopher at the same compute, 67.6% against 60.0% on MMLU ({{Chinchilla|n:3c65c17b0d0d8116b7ddffba5c599f3f}}). InstructGPT fixes the three-step recipe (SFT, reward model, PPO with a KL penalty) ({{InstructGPT|n:3c65c17b0d0d8180b958d8299996a063}}); Constitutional AI replaces human harmlessness labels with a model reading 16 written principles ({{Constitutional AI|n:3c65c17b0d0d815a9b31e9443961c700}}). FlashAttention ({{paper|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e4a3e8e7a2c4771381}}).

2023

DPO removes the reward model and the RL loop ({{DPO|n:3c65c17b0d0d818bb1d8cafd30e20f9e}}); QLoRA fine-tunes 65B on one GPU ({{QLoRA|n:3c65c17b0d0d815a8f4edc33f9583671}}); LIMA argues 1,000 curated examples suffice for SFT (LIMA).

2024

GRPO drops PPO's critic ({{DeepSeekMath|n:3c65c17b0d0d817f9fc5cb9a9fbcbee5}}); Llama 3 publishes the most detailed frontier-scale run, with six rounds of reward model, rejection sampling, SFT and DPO ({{Llama 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}); DeepSeek-V3 trains in FP8; OLMo 2 releases everything, logs included ({{OLMo 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fb9857fb956165ae1c}}).

2025

DeepSeek-R1: outcome-only RL on a base model produces long self-checking reasoning, and SFT on 800K of its samples beats RL for smaller models ({{DeepSeek-R1|n:3c65c17b0d0d813faca4f7a51eaa0c65}}). Open replications follow; DAPO releases algorithm, data and code, reaching 50 points on AIME 2024 from the Qwen2.5-32B base (GitHub, paper), the runnable baseline for RLVR.

2026

RL moves into environments (MiMo-V2.6, SWE-2, Mercor's 397B run) and its infrastructure gets published (DSec); training data comes from production traffic (NeoHorse-1); and models do part of the research. Anthropic's internal measurements: over 80% of code merged into its production codebase written by Claude by May 2026; the model's research suggestions beating the human choice 64% of the time in April 2026, against 51% in November 2025; Claude leading 26% of its AI research work, with over 30,000 agents active on its most-used internal platform; continuous-integration load up 25 times in six months self-reported (When AI builds itself, measuring the pace). Z.ai reports GLM-5.3 building the inference stack that serves it in 13 days, while saying it has not reached recursive self-improvement because humans kept the objectives (Z.ai). Both labs draw the line in the same place: the loop closes on execution, not on choosing goals. The full series with every check is in the {{21 September tech news issue|n:3e25c17b0d0d813dba03fa81f5d8cc39}}.

Corrections

The old page listed DAPO among September 2026 results; it was released in March 2025 (the 50-point checkpoint in May 2025). "When AI builds itself" first appeared in early June 2026, and 18 September updated one chart. The model's research suggestions beat the human choice 64% of the time, not "matched"; the "roughly 90% of their work" is the share of work at or above "AI collaborates", not staff time; the 30,000 agents are those on Anthropic's most-used internal platform; and the CI post gives the 10-fold growth of the test suite as a cause of the 25-fold CI growth, not a response to it (checks of 2 October 2026 in the {{21 September|n:3e25c17b0d0d813dba03fa81f5d8cc39}} and {{28 September|n:3e95c17b0d0d81e69f97de1d3b6960f4}} issues).

Common mistakes

Treating "20 tokens per parameter" as how long to train
Chinchilla's ratio minimises training compute for a given loss. A model that will serve many tokens is trained far longer, because a smaller model is cheaper to run: Llama 3 405B saw 15.6T tokens, 38.5 per parameter derived, and its small models many times more. Compare both regimes in the {{Scaling calculator|#t-scale}}.
Reading a published training cost as the cost of the model
DeepSeek-V3's $5.576M is 2.788M H800 hours at an assumed $2 for the final official run only, with research and ablations excluded; R1's $294K sits on top of it. Each figure on the {{Price list|#t-price}} says what it includes.
"DPO is RLHF without the downsides"
It removes the reward model and the sampling loop, and with them on-policy learning: DPO never sees its own new answers. Its wins over PPO in the original paper are single runs about one standard error apart ({{DPO|n:3c65c17b0d0d818bb1d8cafd30e20f9e}}); reasoning models use online RL.
"SFT taught it to reason" or "RL gave it new abilities"
SFT teaches the format of reasoning and RL sharpens what the base model already reaches with enough samples (see Axis 1). A capability absent from pretraining rarely appears later.
Reading a rising reward as a better model
Against a learned reward, the proxy keeps rising after the true quality has peaked (Axis 4). Track a held-out gold measure and the KL from the reference.
"LoRA always matches full fine-tuning"
It does for typical post-training datasets when applied to every weight matrix (Thinking Machines); it does not absorb billions of tokens of new knowledge, and LoRA on attention only underperforms. The LoRA paper's own margins over full fine-tuning sit inside the noise ({{LoRA|n:3c65c17b0d0d81018f15edd19fa5c28a}}).
"Quantization is free"
At 8 bits it nearly is. At about 4.9 bits (llama.cpp's Q4_K_M) perplexity rose by 0.05 on LLaMA-7B, but newer, smaller, more heavily trained models lose more, and dense models fall off a cliff below about 3 bits ({{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}).
Reading a self-improvement paper as measured self-improvement
NeoHorse-1's loop ran once; Agora and Dream-RSI are single runs without a comparison; Anthropic's figures are internal measurements on internal tasks. The mechanisms are real; the compounding is not yet measured.
Comparing token counts across models
Token counts depend on the tokenizer: the same text costs several times more tokens in a low-resource language, and a 256K vocabulary packs more text per token than a 32K one ({{Tokenizers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817187bcd94a1b0f15ea}}).
