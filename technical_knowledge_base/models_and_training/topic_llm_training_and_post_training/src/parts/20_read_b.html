Axis 3: what each stage costs

Pretraining's cost is arithmetic: about 6 floating-point operations per parameter per token, so C ≈ 6ND, divided by what the cluster sustains. Post-training used to be a rounding error on top of it, and reasoning and agentic RL changed that, by moving the cost from the backward pass to generating rollouts and running the environments they act in:

  • 2022: InstructGPT's RLHF on the 175B model took 60 petaflop/s-days against 3,640 to pretrain GPT-3: 60 / 3,640 = 1.6% derived, and SFT 0.13% more ({{InstructGPT|n:3c65c17b0d0d8180b958d8299996a063}}, §5.1).
  • 2025: DeepSeek-R1's RL and SFT data took 147K H800 hours ($294K at an assumed $2 an hour), on top of 2,788K for DeepSeek-V3's official run: 147 / 2,788 = 5.3% derived ({{DeepSeek-R1|n:3c65c17b0d0d813faca4f7a51eaa0c65}}, {{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}).
  • 2026: the final RL stage of Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro (1.02T total, 42B active) ran live for under six days across 7,000+ task environments for $2.62M; Flash's cost about $850,000 (model card, alphaXiv). A stage short enough to rerun weekly is limited by how many environments and evaluations a lab can supply, not by the run.

Those are the ratios; every disclosed run with its dollar figure and what that figure includes (one stage, one run, or a whole programme) is on the {{Price list|#t-price}}, and FLOPs and GPU-days for any model and token count, Chinchilla-optimal or over-trained, are in the {{Scaling calculator|#t-scale}}.

Same batch, three algorithms

The memory side of post-training is set by how many models must sit on the GPUs at once. PPO, the algorithm of InstructGPT-style RLHF, holds four: the policy being trained, a frozen reference copy for the KL penalty, a reward model, and a critic that estimates how good each partial answer is. GRPO (from {{DeepSeekMath|n:3c65c17b0d0d817f9fc5cb9a9fbcbee5}}) drops the critic: it samples a group of answers per prompt and scores each against the group's mean. DPO drops sampling altogether and trains on fixed pairs.

Try: step one batch of four prompts through each. PPO's memory bar is twice GRPO's at the same model size; GRPO pays instead in rollouts, 8 per prompt here where PPO samples one.

Memory derived: a trained model holds 16 bytes per parameter (bf16 weights and gradients, fp32 master weights and two Adam moments, {{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}} §3.1), a frozen one 2; activations and the rollout engine's cache are left out. PPO 36 bytes per parameter, 252 GB at 7B; GRPO with a verifier and DPO 18, 126 GB: "roughly half the memory", as the RL page puts it. Group size 8 and the right or wrong answers are illustrative; DeepSeekMath used 64 per prompt and R1-Zero 16. DPO's reference log-probabilities can be computed once and cached, which removes the reference from memory during training (4 forward passes per pair, 2 cacheable: {{DPO|n:3c65c17b0d0d818bb1d8cafd30e20f9e}}).

The trade is visible in what each wins. DPO is cheap and stable and was adopted everywhere; on harder tasks, later controlled studies found well-tuned on-policy RL ahead, which is why reasoning models use GRPO and its successors. GRPO's memory saving was argued, not measured, in the paper that introduced it ({{DeepSeekMath|n:3c65c17b0d0d817f9fc5cb9a9fbcbee5}}); its standing rests on cost and on R1.

The environment is now the expensive part

  • Sandboxes at rollout rate. Each agentic rollout needs its own isolated machine. DeepSeek's DSec offers four isolation levels behind one SDK (function call, container, microVM, full VM), so a rollout pays only for the isolation it needs, and keeps stateful rollouts alive when the GPU trainer is preempted. One unit of about 160 nodes runs about 3 million sandboxes a day and creates over 5,000 a second, peaking near 380,000 at once self-reported: about 31 creations per node per second derived ({{DSec|n:3e95c17b0d0d81bd927be047bb0d7b0b}}, arXiv). A lab without such a layer cannot run the same experiments, whatever recipe it copies.
  • The harness decides as much as the algorithm. Mercor and SkyRL's account of RL on Qwen3.5-397B-A17B over 1,928 expert-made tasks is mostly token accounting, asynchronous RL, environment robustness and harness design; APEX-Agents Pass@1 rose from 16.11% to 27.29%, +69.4% relative (Mercor). {{Terminal-Universe|n:3d45c17b0d0d814abc07fde424e09a31}} industrialises the environment supply.
  • The cheap end: one task, one checker. A Qwen 3.8 4B model learned to write Postgres query plans from 400 GPT-6 Astra trajectories (SFT through a 21.2M-parameter LoRA) and then anchored-GRPO RL on 13,646 queries: 1.81 times the built-in planner's speed (geometric mean, 113 held-out queries) for $1,200 in total (write-up). With a cheap verifier, specialisation beats scale.
  • Fewer GPU hours on the wrong experiment. Meta FAIR's AI Research Preference Models have frozen language models rank 15 candidate experiments in a pairwise knockout before running any: average normalised score 0.684 for random choice, 0.711 and 0.729 for the two variants, reaching the baseline's 24-hour result about 1.6 times sooner (arXiv); the margins are narrow and pairwise judges carry the biases of {{Topic: evaluation-and-llm-judges|n:3c65c17b0d0d8181b351e1b8fc6a1546}}. Dream-RSI re-scores new search policies against a finished search tree instead of paying for new evaluations (a Lasso-solver search fell from 550 to 317 agent calls), on single runs whose kernel speedups shrink at matched performance ({{Dream-RSI|n:3e25c17b0d0d813ba021d805ba80ef54}}).

Reward hacking, and the leash against it

A learned reward model is a proxy for what people want. Optimised hard enough, the policy finds what the proxy over-rewards: the proxy score keeps climbing while the true ("gold") score peaks and falls. Gao, Schulman and Hilton measured the shape: as a function of how far the policy has moved from where it started, d = √KL, the gold reward under RL follows d(α − β ln d), rising and then falling, and the gap grows smoothly with optimisation and shrinks with reward-model size (Gao et al.). The KL penalty is the leash: it charges the policy for distance, so it stops near where the proxy still tracks the gold.

Try: run the same training twice. Without the leash the proxy rises all the way while the gold reward turns over around step 35 and ends far below where it started; with it, the policy settles where both are still high.

Gao et al.'s functional form with illustrative coefficients (gold α = 1, β = 0.6; proxy α = 1, β = 0.08; penalty 0.22 × KL), simulated as gradient ascent on proxy minus penalty; recomputed in src/read/recompute.py. The shape is the published one; the numbers are not any real run's.

RLVR replaces the learned proxy with a checker, which removes this curve but not the problem: the checker is the new proxy, narrower and still gameable. Two 2026 responses move the defence out of the reward. DSec treats misbehaviour, reward hacking included, as a property of the sandbox: an environment that cannot be escaped removes a class of shortcut before the reward has to resist it ({{DSec|n:3e95c17b0d0d81bd927be047bb0d7b0b}}). And reward hacking learned in training can generalise into broad misalignment, which telling the model during training that the hack is acceptable ("inoculation prompting") largely prevents (Anthropic; detail on {{Reward Hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}}).

Loops that train on their own history narrow. Dream-RSI's replay is valid only inside the region its history covers, so it sharpens a mapped search rather than extending it ({{Dream-RSI|n:3e25c17b0d0d813ba021d805ba80ef54}}). NVIDIA's Agora had 13 agents publish 1,703 contributions over 11 days and 19 hours as Git commits, taking a language-model evaluator from 3.39 to 1.899 bits per byte with 165 independent reproductions and no reported failure, but effort converged on one lineage ("shared visibility supported reuse, but did not by itself sustain broad exploration"), and the authors stepped in several times during the run; with one task, one run and no comparison, their changes cannot be separated from the effect of the system's diversity views ({{Agora|n:3e25c17b0d0d81399738c3e65f4bf900}}).
