Quantization-aware training (QAT)

Insert fake-quantization operations during training: quantize then dequantize in the forward pass, so the loss sees the rounding, and pass the gradient straight through the rounding in the backward pass (the straight-through estimator, Bengio et al. 2013), so the model learns weights that survive the eventual precision. It costs extra training compute and recovers most of PTQ's loss at 4 bits and below. Current practice: short QAT fine-tunes after PTQ (torchao), official QAT checkpoints (Google's Gemma 3; Meta's Llama 3.2 1B and 3B), and "QAT from scratch" for models native to 4 bits or fewer, such as the ternary models above. Qwen ships official AWQ, GPTQ-Int4 and FP8 checkpoints but no QAT ones, as of this check (Qwen docs). QLoRA-style training (frozen NF4 base plus bf16 adapters) is the budget alternative: it trains adapters on a quantized base rather than teaching the base to be quantized ({{PEFT|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba08eef56b3e3b4eb}}, {{QLoRA|n:3c65c17b0d0d815a8f4edc33f9583671}}).

The 4-bit frontier (2025 to 2026)

Blackwell tensor cores natively support MXFP8, MXFP4 and NVFP4, moving 4-bit from a storage trick to a compute format: "Tensor Cores deliver FP4 computations at 2× (on GB200 chips) and 3× (on GB300 chips) higher math throughput rates compared to FP8" (NVIDIA 2025, §2).

  • NVFP4 pretraining (NVIDIA 2025): a 12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer trained on 10T tokens in NVFP4 matched its FP8 baseline closely (MMLU-Pro 62.58 against 62.62; MMLU 76.57 against 77.36; HumanEval+ 57.43 against 59.93). It needed four things: random Hadamard transforms (16 × 16, on the weight-gradient inputs) to smooth outliers; 2D 16 × 16 block scaling for weights so the forward and backward passes see the same quantised weights; stochastic rounding on gradients; and keeping some layers in BF16 (the first two and the last eight blocks, 16% of the linear layers).
  • NVFP4 against MXFP4: NVFP4's 16-element blocks with an E4M3 scale beat MXFP4's 32-element power-of-two blocks on quality. In the same paper's 8B experiment, MXFP4 matched NVFP4's loss only with 36% more tokens (1.36T against 1T), and its loss gap to BF16 was about 2.5% against NVFP4's 1.5%. The animation above shows why on one block: an E8M0 scale is a power of two, so a block's largest value lands anywhere between 4 and 8 times the scale and above 6 it clips, while E4M3 puts it on 6 almost exactly. MXFP4's advantage is portability: it is an open standard that also runs on AMD (the MI355X, per Spheron's guide, a vendor blog that calls its own accuracy table directional).
  • Low-precision RL is live (LMSYS, 29 July 2026): in the Miles framework (SGLang rollouts, Megatron and Transformer Engine training), MXFP8 runs end to end, "rollout, forward propagation, weight-gradient GEMMs, and data-gradient GEMMs"; NVFP4 covers only the MoE experts in rollout and forward, with the backward pass in high precision and the rest of the model (shared experts, the last 15% of layers) in BF16. On Qwen3-30B-A3B with GRPO, "all five low-precision reward curves closely track the BF16 reward curve", although "both low-precision formats show higher train-inference mismatch than BF16". Both cut rollout time; MXFP8 training is faster than BF16, the NVFP4 backward variants slower in that implementation.
  • Inference default stack, 2026: bf16, then FP8 W8A8 (near-lossless), then NVFP4 or INT4 weight-only for memory-bound serving; MoE models quantize their experts aggressively. NVFP4 is about 3.5 times smaller than FP16 and 1.8 times smaller than FP8 (NVIDIA; 16 / 4.5 = 3.56, 8 / 4.5 = 1.78 derived). llama.cpp's ggml has begun adding an NVFP4 block type too (ggml-common.h).
Corrections
The old page described NVFP4 pretraining as "matching-quality training with 2-3x matmul speedups": the 2 to 3 times is the hardware's peak FP4 rate against FP8 on GB200 and GB300, not a speedup measured in the paper. It put the MXFP4 "36% more tokens" result without its setting: it is the 8B experiment, 1.36T against 1T tokens. And it said low-precision RL reduces "the rollout/trainer precision mismatch that destabilises RL": the LMSYS post reports that both low-precision formats show higher train-inference mismatch than BF16, with reward curves that still track BF16, and that NVFP4 there is used for MoE experts in rollout and forward only, not for "rollouts with bf16 training" across the model.

Common mistakes

"bf16 is more precise than fp16"
It is less precise (7 mantissa bits against 10, a relative step of 0.0078 against 0.00098). It wins for training because of range: its exponent is fp32's, so gradients do not underflow and no loss scaling is needed.
"4-bit means 4 bits per weight"
Scales cost bits: 4.25 for MXFP4 and INT4 g128, 4.5 for NVFP4 and Q4_K, about 4.9 for a whole Q4_K_M file. Budget memory on effective bits per weight, or use the calculator on {{Topic: llm-training-and-post-training|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}}.
"Lower weight error means a better quantization"
GPTQ and AWQ raise the plain weight error on the layer used here (10.8% to 18.1% and 12.8%) while lowering the output error (7.8% to 6.1% and 6.2%). What matters is error weighted by the activations each weight meets ({{One real layer, every scheme|#t-layer}}).
"Integers are always better than floats at the same bits"
With one scale per tensor, FP8 E4M3 left 1.9% output error on this layer and INT8 10.5%: floating point spaces its levels by magnitude, so one outlier does not starve the small weights. With a scale per channel, INT8 wins (0.76%). Granularity and format interact.
"An uploader's perplexity proves the quant is fine"
Perplexity on the calibration distribution hides losses off it, and schemes with similar perplexity diverge on instruction-following and reasoning (Kurt 2026). Evaluate on your own tasks.
"Convert to MXFP4 for free speed"
gpt-oss was released in MXFP4; converting an ordinary checkpoint into it with round-to-nearest is lossier than NVFP4 or INT4 with group scales (9.3% against 6.2% and 7.8% output error on this page's layer) and should be calibrated or fine-tuned.
"Quantize from whatever file you have"
Always quantize from the full-precision original: errors from an already-quantized file compound and cannot be recovered ({{Model formats|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81ea815aedfa052e4919}}).
