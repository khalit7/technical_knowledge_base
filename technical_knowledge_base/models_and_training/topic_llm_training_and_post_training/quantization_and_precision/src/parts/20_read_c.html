Reading a quantisation name

Everything above is the method; this section is the notation: how to decode a quant string on a Hub repo without opening config.json.

GGUF names, in one paragraph

A GGUF name is the letter Q, a nominal bit count and a variant tag. _0 and _1 are the legacy flat formats (32 weights, one fp16 scale, plus a minimum for _1); K marks the K-quants (a 256-weight super-block whose sub-block scales are themselves quantised: the group scaling of GPTQ and AWQ taken one level further, like QLoRA's double quantisation); IQ marks codebook types steered by an importance matrix (imatrix), the same activation-weighted idea as AWQ and GPTQ in the {{layer tab|#t-layer}}; and _S, _M, _L name a per-tensor mixing recipe, not a block format. So the bits per weight are never the nominal number: Q4_K's block is 4.5 bits per weight and a whole Q4_K_M file about 4.9. Every type's block layout (decoded from real files), the mixing recipes, importance matrices and a quality ladder measured as KL divergence against F16 on two Qwen3 models are owned by {{Local and on-device inference|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f7a144fe25d799e811}} (sections 3 and 4, and its Quant ladder tab), with llama.cpp's own perplexity lists and the corrections this page used to carry; the file container is section 4 of {{Model formats: GGUF, safetensors, ONNX, and the rest|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81ea815aedfa052e4919}}. The short version: Q4_K_M with an imatrix is the default, I-quants buy size rather than quality per byte, and dense models fall off a cliff below about 3 bits.

Names you will meet outside GGUF

Name you seeWhat it means
gptq-4bit-128g-actorder_TrueGPTQ, 4-bit weight-only. Group size 128: one scale and zero point per 128 consecutive weights along the input dimension. Smaller g is finer and more accurate but adds scale overhead (4 + 32/128 = 4.25 bits at g128 with fp16 scale and zero derived); 32g is the quality end, 128g the usual compromise, no g means per channel. act-order (desc_act in the config) quantises columns in decreasing order of activation size so accumulated error lands on the columns that matter least. Better accuracy, historically incompatible with some fused kernels: "Some GPTQ clients have had issues with models that use Act Order plus Group Size, but this is generally resolved now" (TheBloke's card)
awq-4bit-128g, AWQ-INT4AWQ, 4-bit weight-only, group size 128. Instead of reordering, it rescales the roughly 1% of salient channels (chosen by activation magnitude) before quantising. No reordering means simpler, faster kernels, which is why AWQ often wins on throughput at similar accuracy unconfirmed as a general rule. Qwen ships official AWQ checkpoints (for example Qwen3-8B-AWQ)
nf4, fp4, load_in_4bit, bnb-4bitbitsandbytes. A runtime flag, not a checkpoint format: the fp16 weights are quantised on load. NF4 is a 16-level codebook fitted to a normal distribution over 64-weight blocks; "double quant" additionally quantises the block scales (4.127 bits per weight 4 + 8/64 + 32/(64 × 256)). This is the QLoRA base. Convenient, not competitive on serving throughput
W4A16compressed-tensors and llm-compressor (the vLLM naming): "Quantizes weights to 4 or 8-bit integer precision, retaining activations in 16-bit", per group, "optimally compressed using non-RTN algorithms (GPTQ, AWQ) which require a dataset" (llm-compressor). Memory-bound win: right for low concurrency where you are streaming weights
W8A8 (-INT8)Weights and activations both int8, so the matmul itself runs in int8. Needs calibration for static activation scales (or GPTQ/AWQ for the weights). The high-throughput, compute-bound choice on pre-Hopper hardware
FP8-dynamicFP8 W8A8 with per-channel weight scales and per-token activation scales computed at runtime. No calibration set required. The low-effort near-lossless default on Hopper and Blackwell (vLLM). vLLM can also do it online from a bf16 checkpoint with a per-tensor scale
FP8-staticSame, but the activation scales are frozen from a calibration pass. Marginally faster (no per-token reduction), and marginally more brittle: a distribution shift away from the calibration data shows up as clipping. llm-compressor lists it as an option of its FP8 scheme rather than a separate name. FP8_BLOCK uses 128 × 128 weight tiles and per-128 activation groups, DeepSeek-V3's recipe
NVFP4, MXFP4 checkpointsBlackwell-native 4-bit. NVFP4: a global scale per tensor and FP8 E4M3 scales per 16, and it "requires a calibration dataset to calibrate activation global scales"; MXFP4: E8M0 scales per 32, "no calibration data required if using RTN", "though RTN accuracy may be lower" (llm-compressor). See the 4-bit frontier
Trailing -KV8, -FP8-KVNot about the weights at all: the KV cache is stored quantised (vLLM: kv_cache_dtype="fp8", per-tensor scales of 1.0 without calibration, per-head scales with it; docs). Orthogonal to the weight scheme, and often the bigger memory win at long context

On the dual 5090s: GGUF or a native checkpoint

Which model and which GGUF type fit two 32 GB cards, and the rule that a bigger model at Q4_K_M beats a smaller one at Q8_0 (checked by measurement), are in {{Local and on-device inference|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f7a144fe25d799e811}} (sections 4 and 8). The precision side: MoE experts tolerate much harder quantisation than the attention and shared layers; gpt-oss ships only its MoE weights, "90+% of the total parameter count", in MXFP4 (gpt-oss card), and llama.cpp's MXFP4_MOE type keeps "other tensors" at Q8_0. And the two 5090s are Blackwell SM120 with native FP8 and NVFP4 tensor cores, while GGUF's K-quants are a CPU-friendly design that dequantises to fp16 before the matmul. For single-stream interactive work the difference is small and llama.cpp's flexibility wins; once concurrency matters, an FP8 or NVFP4 checkpoint under vLLM uses hardware the GGUF path never touches, and the gap becomes large.
