The idea in one sentenceA low-precision number is a small code times a scale shared with its neighbours, so how much a model loses depends less on the bit count than on how many numbers share each scale and whether an outlier sets it; every method on this page (block formats, GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, rotations, quantisation-aware training, FP8 and 4-bit training) is a way to choose that scale, or to move the outlier, so the model's outputs barely change.

Scale (and zero point): what a stored code is multiplied by (and shifted by) to get the value back.
Granularity: how many numbers share one scale: a tensor, a row (per channel), or a block (group) of 16 to 128.
Bits per weight: element bits plus the scale's bits spread over its block; "4-bit" is 4.25 to 4.9 in practice.
E4M3, E5M2, E2M1: floats with 4, 5 or 2 exponent bits and 3, 2 or 1 mantissa bits; E8M0 is a pure power of two.
PTQ: quantising a trained model with no retraining; QAT: training with the rounding in the forward pass.
Calibration: running a few hundred tokens of text through the model to set scales or guide the rounding.
W4A16, W8A8: weights in 4 or 8 bits, activations in 16 or 8; only the second runs the matmul itself in 8 bits.
Outlier channel: an input channel whose activations are many times the rest, on every token.

1. Number formats: range against precision

A gradient of 10−8 stored in fp16 becomes 0; in bf16 it survives. The value 500 in fp8 E4M3 saturates at 448, while E5M2 stores 512. And 1 + 2−8 is exact in fp16 but rounds to 1 in bf16. All three follow from two fields of the bit layout: the exponent bits set the range (how many powers of two, or binades, the format spans), the mantissa bits set the precision (the relative step between neighbouring values, 2−m). How the bits encode a value, subnormals and rounding are built bit by bit in {{Numerical computing for ML|n:3f05c17b0d0d81d6af0be4a9a2edba8d}} (sections 1 to 3); this page starts from the consequence.

Fewer exponent bits leave fewer binades: E4M3 spans under 18, E2M1 under 4, bf16 the same 2−126 to 2128 as fp32

Any number through every format: its bits, what comes back, the error and the step at that size

Rounding is round-to-nearest-even with subnormals, as in hardware conversion. Formats with infinities (fp32, tf32, bf16, fp16, E5M2) overflow to infinity once past the largest value by half a step; E4M3 and E2M1 have none, and conversion kernels saturate. "Step here" is the gap between neighbouring values at this magnitude (one unit in the last place).

So bf16 is less precise than fp16 (7 mantissa bits against 10, a relative step of 0.0078 against 0.00098); it won training because of range: its exponent is fp32's, so small gradients do not underflow and no loss scaling is needed (section 6).
