One real layer, every scheme

Seventeen weight formats and six W8A8 recipes applied to one real layer, the query projection of layer 8 in Qwen2.5-0.5B (896 × 896 weights, bf16 in the checkpoint), and measured on 511 tokens of real text the calibration never saw. It answers "across all the formats, what does each bit of scale overhead buy?", and extends Reading: PTQ and Outliers. Everything here is measured here by src/quant_lab.py: one layer of one small model, so read it as a mechanism made visible, not as a ranking that will hold on your model.

Try: switch the metric to weight error: GPTQ and AWQ jump from best to worst among the 4.25-bit schemes. They minimise the output error, which is what the model feels.

Output error: ‖XŴT − XWT‖ / ‖XWT‖ on the evaluation tokens (activations unquantised); weight error: ‖Ŵ − W‖ / ‖W‖. Bits per weight include scales: per-channel overhead is one fp16 scale per 896 weights. Q4_K* is a simplified Q4_K (min-max per block of 32, 6-bit scales and mins against fp16 super-values, without llama.cpp's search). NF4 + DQ stores the same values as NF4 in fewer bits (scale error not modelled). GPTQ: the paper's algorithm without lazy batching, 1% damping, calibration Hessian from 511 tokens; act-order uses static groups. AWQ: grid search of α on the calibration text.

Weights and activations in 8 bits (W8A8)

Output error when the activations are quantised too. One per-tensor activation scale is the cheapest kernel and the most exposed to outlier channels; per-token scales or FP8's range fix most of it; SmoothQuant fixes it while keeping the cheap kernel.

Weights INT8 or FP8 per output channel unless named. "Static" uses the calibration text's maximum; "dynamic" recomputes it per batch or per token.
