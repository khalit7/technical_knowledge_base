The NanoGPT speedrun, record by record

The task is fixed: train a network to at most 3.28 cross-entropy loss on the FineWeb validation set, on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, without changing the training or validation token streams; the target matches Karpathy's GPT-2 (124M) reproduction in llm.c, which needed 45 minutes and 10B tokens ({{modded-nanogpt README|https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt}}, fetched 3 October 2026). Because the data, tokenizer and hardware are held still, every record is an ablation that the whole community re-runs: a change that claims a gain must show it, with enough runs for p < 0.01 that the mean loss is at most 3.28. That makes it the cleanest public evidence of which training tricks actually save compute, at one small scale.

Which kinds of change bought the speed

Each record's speed-up is ln(previous time / new time), so the speed-ups add up to the whole history: ln(45 / 0.665) + ln(3.014 / 2.933) = 4.24, a factor of 69.5 derived including the re-timing loss. Summed by kind:

What graduated, and what to discount

  • Muon is the clearest graduate: introduced in record 3 (31.4 to 24.9 minutes, 21% faster) and refined in records 4 and 6, it became MuonClip, which carried Moonshot's 1T-parameter Kimi K2 through 15.5T tokens with no loss spikes (see {{Moonshot AI: Kimi|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f5abf5dbdbac821a12}} and {{Optimisers and learning-rate schedulers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817080bbc90954681d09}}). Its later forms in the speedrun (Polar Express orthogonalisation, NorMuon, the ANVIL stack) have not been reported at scale unconfirmed.
  • The old page also listed untied embeddings, careful initialisation, FlexAttention with sliding windows and value-embedding skip connections as having "graduated to real training runs". No source for that was found for any named frontier run, so it is marked unconfirmed; sliding-window attention itself is older than the speedrun (Gemma 2 uses it, and the README credits it as the inspiration).
  • Some gains belong to the benchmark rather than to training: record 91 masks logits for impossible continuations during validation; record 92's hashed n-gram table holds 65B parameters by the README's count, a lookup that helps at a 40-second budget and says little about a 15T-token run. A tokenizer change (the 2.77-minute TokenMonster run) is listed by the README as outside the rules.
  • The README's own list of what produced the speed-up reads like a 2026 architecture checklist: rotary embeddings, QK-norm, ReLU², zero-initialised projections (muP-like), value embeddings, long-short sliding windows with window warmup and YaRN, FP8 matmuls, multi-token prediction, a batch-size schedule.
