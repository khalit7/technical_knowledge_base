The idea in one sentencePretraining spends one compute budget, C ≈ 6ND, on lowering next-token loss: a scaling law splits it between parameters N and tokens D, the data mix improves as the learning rate is annealed at the end, and the rest of a run exists to keep it stable long enough to finish.

Compute budget C: total training FLOPs; about 6 × parameters × tokens for a dense model (active parameters for a mixture of experts).
Tokens per parameter (D/N): Chinchilla's compute-optimal ratio is about 20; served models now use hundreds to thousands.
Scaling law: a formula for loss as a function of N and D, fitted on small runs and used to plan a large one.
IsoFLOP curve: loss against model size at one fixed budget; its minimum is the compute-optimal size.
Over-training: training a smaller model past the compute-optimal ratio, paying more training compute for a cheaper model to serve.
WSD (warmup, stable, decay): a flat learning rate with a short decay at the end, which can start at any point.
Anneal (mid-training): the final phase where the learning rate decays to zero, usually on a better data mix.
Bits per byte: loss divided over the bytes of text, not tokens, so it compares models with different tokenizers.
