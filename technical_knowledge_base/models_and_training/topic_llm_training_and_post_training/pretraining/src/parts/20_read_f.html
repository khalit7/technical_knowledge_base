6 · Running it at scale: schedule, batch, stability, evaluation

Learning-rate schedule. A cosine schedule must know the run's length before it starts; WSD (warmup, stable, decay; {{MiniCPM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06395}}) holds the rate flat and decays over the last 10 to 20% whenever you decide to stop, so anneals can branch off the plateau without committing to a token count. That costs nothing at the end: "the constant learning rate with a short cooldown (< 20% of total training steps) can achieve the same final loss as cosine" ({{Hägele et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18392}}). The runs above show the variants: GPT-3 one cosine; OLMo 2 a cosine cut early plus a linear anneal; SmolLM3 WSD with a 10% decay; Kimi K2 WSD with 500 warmup steps, 2 × 10−4 constant for 10T tokens, cosine to 2 × 10−5 over 5.5T, then an anneal to 7 × 10−6 over 400B + 60B. The decay can even be dropped: {{WSM|n:3c75c17b0d0d8105b452d2c1bc1d087a}} merges checkpoints instead. Shapes, cooldown length and the animation of one run serving many lengths are section 7 of {{Optimisers and learning-rate schedulers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817080bbc90954681d09}} (models at three lengths cost 1,750 steps with cosine and 1,150 with WSD).

Batch size. Below the critical batch size a larger batch buys nearly proportional speed; above it, it wastes compute, and it grows as the loss falls ({{McCandlish et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06162}}; Kaplan's form is plotted on the {{Scaling Laws page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b08a1debb0c15cd252}}). So runs ramp it: GPT-3 linearly from 32K tokens to the full batch over the first 4 to 12B tokens ({{GPT-3, Appendix B|https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165}}); Llama 3 405B from 4M to 8M tokens after 252M tokens and to 16M after 2.87T ({{section 3.4.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}); the speedrun's record 46 was a batch-size schedule.

Stability. A run that diverges at 60% has wasted 60% of the budget, so every large run stacks cheap guards: bf16 with fp32 master weights; gradient clipping at 1.0 (GPT-3, OLMo 2, SmolLM3 and this page's toy all clip at 1.0); z-loss or logit soft-capping on the output softmax; QK-norm on the attention logits (OLMo 2 moved to QK-norm plus reordered norms specifically for stability; its ablations are replayed on the {{OLMo 2 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fb9857fb956165ae1c}}); and a watch for loss spikes that correlate with bad data shards. Llama 3 lowered its early batch size for stability and reports few spikes and no interventions. Why each guard works, and the spikes themselves, are on {{Normalisation, residuals and stability|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8163b50dc5e9d9240e89}} (its Stability explorer); precision formats on {{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}; parallelism on {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}.

Failures are routine. Over a 54-day snapshot of Llama 3 405B's pretraining on 16K H100s there were 466 interruptions, 419 of them unexpected (78% hardware), yet the job kept "higher than 90% effective training time" ({{Llama 3 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}). Checkpointing and restart design are on {{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}}.

Evaluation during training. Track a fixed held-out loss suite plus few-shot benchmarks at checkpoints; held-out loss on high-quality sets predicts downstream better than training loss. Small models need easier formats to give signal (OLMo 2 uses cloze-form OLMES for exactly this), and an anneal is the cheapest way to ask "how good would this checkpoint be if I stopped now" (Llama 3's 40B-token test anneals; WSM's merges).

Corrections
The old page said the speedrun record "fell to under 2.5 minutes by late 2025"; it was 1.894 minutes on 31 December 2025 and 0.665 minutes on 30 August 2026 (record 92). It also described K2 Horizon as "one architecture across the whole fleet: diffusion distillation generating token blocks in parallel for a claimed 3x speedup, and a mixture-of-value-attention design". The model cards say otherwise: Mixture-of-Values attention (MoVA) is the design of one member, the sparse 36B-A4B ({{card|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B}}), and the diffusion pathway ("Uno") is a separately released LoRA adapter for the 7B and 0.9B, whose card reports 2.46 to 3.00 tokens per forward pass on its agentic benchmarks ({{K2-Horizon-7B-Uno|https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-7B-Uno}}).

How numbers on this page are labelled

Numbers are published, with the source linked, unless marked derived (formula shown, recomputed in src/recompute.py and checked against the page by src/check_data.py), illustrative, reported (the maker's own figure, not independently checked), toy (this page's own small run) or unconfirmed.

What SmolLM3's budget implies about utilisation

6ND gives 2.07 × 1023 FLOPs. 384 H100s for 24 days is 221,184 GPU-hours; at the H100's dense BF16 peak of 989.5 TFLOP/s that is 7.9 × 1023 FLOPs of capacity, so the run sustained about 26% of peak by 6ND derived. Wall-clock days include restarts and evaluation, and a 3B model is small for 384 GPUs. The root's {{Scaling calculator|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}} makes the same check for twelve published runs, with the attention term and each lab's stated MFU.

Design question: Chinchilla's budget, 5.76 × 1023 FLOPs, for a model that will serve a large product. What N and D?

Start from the compute-optimal split (72.2B parameters on 1.33T tokens under the refit), then move along the same-loss curve towards a smaller model until the training premium is repaid by expected serving volume. From the slider in section 3: at 100 tokens per parameter, 35.2B parameters for 1.29 times the training compute, repaid after about 2.2T served tokens; at Llama 3 8B's 1,868, 17.0B for 5.64 times, repaid after about 24T derived. Then check the data: at thousands of tokens per parameter you need tens of trillions of good tokens, and repeats past about 4 epochs lose value. Refit the law on your own small runs and tokenizer before trusting any constant, use WSD so the stopping point can move, and choose the anneal's data from small anneal ablations.
