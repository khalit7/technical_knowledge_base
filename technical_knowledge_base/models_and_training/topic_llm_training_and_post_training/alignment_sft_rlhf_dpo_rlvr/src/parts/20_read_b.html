1. Supervised fine-tuning: which tokens it learns from

SFT is the pretraining loss on a different text: conversations written in a chat template, with the loss switched off on every token the model should not learn to produce. The model reads the whole conversation; only the assistant's tokens are graded. Llama 3: "a standard cross entropy loss on the target tokens (while masking loss on prompt tokens)" ({{Llama 3, §4.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}). Ai2's open-instruct, the Tulu 3 code, sets the label of every non-assistant token to −100, the value the cross-entropy ignores, with the comment "mask the non-assistant part for avoiding loss" ({{open-instruct finetune.py at the Tulu 3 commit|https://github.com/allenai/open-instruct/blob/ba11286e5b9eb00d4ce5b40ef4cac1389888416a/open_instruct/finetune.py}}).

Which rule is right is not settled. Grading the prompt tokens too ("instruction modelling", template tokens excluded) helped "in many scenarios" across 21 benchmarks, most when instructions are long and answers short or examples few ({{Shi et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14394}}); with short answers, a small prompt-loss weight between 0.01 and 0.5 did best on multiple-choice and short-generation benchmarks, a weight near 1 on long-generation ones ({{Huerta-Enochian and Ko 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13586}}). It should still be a choice, and frameworks make it for you: Hugging Face TRL computes the loss on the completion only for prompt-completion datasets, but its assistant_only_loss is off by default, so a plain conversational dataset trains on the user's turns too unless the template marks the assistant spans ({{TRL SFTTrainer|https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/sft_trainer}}).

Quality beats quantity, and the template is part of the model. LIMA fine-tuned a 65B LLaMa on 1,000 curated examples, with no RL, and people rated it equivalent to or better than GPT-4 in 43% of cases ({{LIMA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11206}}): its "superficial alignment hypothesis" is that knowledge comes from pretraining and alignment selects the format. Production recipes still use far more, mostly curated and synthetic: Tulu 3 trained on 939,344 prompts for 2 epochs at a learning rate of 5e-6 (8B), an effective batch of 128 and sequences up to 4,096 tokens ({{Tulu 3, Tables 7 and 11|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}). The template moved Tulu 3's average from 51.6 with the Llama 3 template to 53.0 with an end-of-text token in place of the newline after each answer, 1.4 points from formatting alone ({{Tulu 3, Table 13|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}); a model served with a different template from the one it was trained on breaks.

Depth: epochs, and SFT from other sources (1 min)

InstructGPT's SFT set was about 13,000 prompts; its models "overfit on validation loss after 1 epoch", yet 16 epochs still raised the RM score and human preference ({{InstructGPT, §3.5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155}}), so SFT validation loss is a poor stopping rule.

Production traffic as the dataset. NeoHorse-1 is the online variant of this step: a router over a pool of models predicts each request's capability demand, those predictions order a three-stage supervised curriculum, and served interactions become training examples after structural validation, a semantic evaluation and labels for sub-scenes within a conversation, finer than one label per conversation, which is what makes supervising a partial trajectory work. Its gains, the ten benchmarks and the caveat that the loop ran once are on the parent page's Axis 2 and the {{NeoHorse-1 paper page|n:3db5c17b0d0d81e3b105e033a359ad24}} (live curriculum scheduler in its "Schedule the curriculum" tab).

2. Batching SFT: padding, packing, and how much each token counts

Conversations differ wildly in length. Padded to its longest member, a batch is mostly padding: of the computed positions are real tokens at 8 conversations per micro-batch, at 16. Tulu 3 itself did neither: its reproduction command runs one conversation per GPU per step with gradient accumulation, so it pads nothing and packs nothing (docs/tulu3.md, {{open-instruct|https://github.com/allenai/open-instruct/blob/ba11286e5b9eb00d4ce5b40ef4cac1389888416a/docs/tulu3.md}}).

How the drawing simplifies (under 1 min)

In a real multi-turn row the graded spans interleave with the prompts. Packing is best-fit decreasing, as TRL's default bfd; the hatched end of a packed row is drawn as padding to a fixed 4,096, whereas padding-free packing (TRL, the HF collator) flattens the batch and computes no padding at all.

The fix is to restart the position ids at each conversation and pass the boundaries to a variable-length attention kernel, so each conversation sees only itself; Hugging Face's DataCollatorWithFlattening does this with FlashAttention 2 and reported up to 2 times the throughput and 20% less peak memory on FLAN ({{Hugging Face, 2024|https://huggingface.co/blog/packing-with-FA2}}). Without a variable-length kernel, keep padding.

Sum or mean: how much each token counts. If the loss is averaged within each conversation and the conversations are then averaged (what gradient accumulation over one conversation at a time does), every conversation counts equally, so a token of a short answer weighs as much as many tokens of a long one. Tulu 3 traced a gap between its own SFT runs and TPU runs to exactly this ("averaging the loss across padding tokens without taking into account gradient accumulation or distributed training setups") and switched to summing the loss, which "effectively weights all tokens equally", with a retuned learning rate ({{Tulu 3, §4.3.2 "Batch Aggregation"|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}); the open-instruct code comments that sum reduction "can result in > 5 point improvements in AlpacaEval". Sum the loss over tokens, or normalise by the total token count of the whole accumulated batch.

Depth: the evidence on padding and cross-document attention (1 min)

Krell et al. found padding was 50% of all tokens in Wikipedia BERT pretraining at length 512, and packing doubled throughput ({{Krell et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02027}}). Masking within documents gave lower perplexity than plain causal masking across packed documents in pretraining ({{Zhao et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13991}}); Llama 3 found the cross-document mask of "limited impact" in standard pretraining but important for very long sequences ({{Llama 3, §3.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}). TRL's "wrapped" packing, which cuts mid-conversation, "may mix unrelated examples" and its documentation says it hurts performance ({{TRL|https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/sft_trainer}}).
