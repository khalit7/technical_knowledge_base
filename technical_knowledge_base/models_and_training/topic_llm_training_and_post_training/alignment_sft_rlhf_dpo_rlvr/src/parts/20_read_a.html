The pipeline, and what this page owns

A pretrained model continues text. Post-training turns it into an assistant in up to four moves, each trained on a narrower signal than the last:

  1. Pretraining: next-token prediction on trillions of tokens; produces a base model.
  2. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT), also called instruction tuning: train on (prompt, response) pairs with the language-model loss on the response tokens only, so the model answers instead of continuing the text. It sets the format, the persona and the chat template.
  3. Reward-model (RM) training, skipped by the direct methods: people rank two or more sampled responses per prompt, and a model learns to predict which one they prefer. The RM lets optimisation continue offline, without a person in the loop.
  4. Policy optimisation: use the RM, or a program that checks the answer, to update the model. Either with reinforcement learning (PPO, GRPO), or directly on preference pairs (DPO and its family).

1. Supervised fine-tuning: which tokens it learns from

SFT is the pretraining loss on a different text: conversations written in a chat template, with the loss switched off on every token the model should not learn to produce. The model reads the whole conversation, but only the assistant's tokens are graded. Llama 3 states it plainly: "a standard cross entropy loss on the target tokens (while masking loss on prompt tokens)" ({{Llama 3, §4.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}). Ai2's open-instruct code, used for Tulu 3, sets the labels of every non-assistant token to −100, the value the cross-entropy ignores, with the comment "mask the non-assistant part for avoiding loss" ({{open-instruct finetune.py at the Tulu 3 commit|https://github.com/allenai/open-instruct/blob/ba11286e5b9eb00d4ce5b40ef4cac1389888416a/open_instruct/finetune.py}}).

Which rule is right is not settled. Grading the prompt tokens too ("instruction modelling", template tokens excluded) helped "in many scenarios" across 21 benchmarks, most when instructions are long and answers short or when there are few examples ({{Shi et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14394}}). With short answers, a small prompt-loss weight between 0.01 and 0.5 did best on multiple-choice and short-generation benchmarks, and a weight near 1 on long-generation ones ({{Huerta-Enochian and Ko 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13586}}). Frameworks pick a default for you: Hugging Face TRL computes the loss on the completion only for prompt-completion datasets, but its assistant_only_loss is off by default, so a plain conversational dataset trains on the user's turns too unless the template marks the assistant spans ({{TRL SFTTrainer|https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/sft_trainer}}).

Quality beats quantity. LIMA fine-tuned a 65B LLaMa on 1,000 curated examples, with no RL, and people rated it equivalent to or better than GPT-4 in 43% of cases ({{LIMA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11206}}); its "superficial alignment hypothesis" is that knowledge comes from pretraining and alignment only selects the format. Production recipes still use many more examples, mostly curated and synthetic: Tulu 3, the open reference recipe, trained on 939,344 prompts for 2 epochs at a learning rate of 5e-6 (8B), an effective batch of 128 and sequences up to 4,096 tokens ({{Tulu 3, Table 7 and Table 11|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}). InstructGPT's SFT set was about 13,000 prompts; its models "overfit on validation loss after 1 epoch", but 16 epochs still raised the RM score and human preference ({{InstructGPT, §3.5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155}}). The template itself moves scores: Tulu 3's averages ranged from 51.6 with the Llama 3 template to 53.0 with an end-of-text token in place of the newline after each answer ({{Tulu 3, Table 13|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}), and a model served with a different template from the one it was trained on breaks.

Padding, packing, and how tokens are weighted

Conversations differ wildly in length. In 1,000 rows sampled from the released {{Tulu 3 SFT mixture|https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/tulu-3-sft-mixture}} the median is tokens and the mean ; of the 1,000 exceed the 4,096-token limit derived. A batch padded to its longest member wastes most of its compute on padding: of the computed positions are real tokens at 8 conversations per micro-batch, at 16. Krell et al. found padding was 50% of all tokens in Wikipedia BERT pretraining at length 512 and packing doubled throughput ({{Krell et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02027}}). Packing concatenates conversations into full rows instead; best-fit packing of the same 1,000 into 4,096-token rows fills of each row.

Plain packing has a cost: with an ordinary causal mask, a token attends to every earlier conversation in its row. On the same sample, of the causal attention pairs in packed rows cross a document boundary derived. The fix is to restart the position ids at each conversation and pass the boundaries to a variable-length attention kernel, so each conversation sees only itself; Hugging Face's DataCollatorWithFlattening does this with FlashAttention 2 and reported up to 2 times the throughput and 20% less peak memory on FLAN ({{Hugging Face, 2024|https://huggingface.co/blog/packing-with-FA2}}). Masking within documents also gave lower perplexity than plain causal masking across packed documents in pretraining ({{Zhao et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13991}}); Llama 3 found the cross-document mask of "limited impact" in standard pretraining but important for very long sequences ({{Llama 3, §3.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}). TRL's "wrapped" packing, which cuts mid-conversation, "may mix unrelated examples" and its documentation says it hurts performance ({{TRL|https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/sft_trainer}}).

After: packed into full rows, first with a plain causal mask, then with boundaries. Watch the counters for wasted positions and for attention that leaks between conversations.

Lengths are the conversations' real token counts (Tulu 3 tokenizer and template), the faint start of each block = tokens without loss, the solid rest = graded tokens, drawn as two blocks for clarity (in a real multi-turn row the graded spans interleave with the prompts). Packing is best-fit decreasing, as TRL's default bfd; the hatched end of a packed row is drawn as padding to a fixed 4,096, whereas padding-free packing (TRL, the HF collator) flattens the batch and computes no padding at all. Tulu 3 itself did neither: its reproduction command runs one conversation per GPU per step with gradient accumulation, so it pads nothing and packs nothing (docs/tulu3.md, {{open-instruct|https://github.com/allenai/open-instruct/blob/ba11286e5b9eb00d4ce5b40ef4cac1389888416a/docs/tulu3.md}}).

Sum or mean. How the per-token losses are combined decides how much each token counts. If the loss is averaged per conversation and conversations are then averaged (what happens with gradient accumulation over one conversation at a time), every conversation counts equally, so a token in a short answer weighs as much as many tokens of a long one. Tulu 3 traced a performance gap between its own SFT runs and TPU runs to exactly this ("averaging the loss across padding tokens without taking into account gradient accumulation or distributed training setups") and switched to summing the loss, which "effectively weights all tokens equally", with a retuned learning rate ({{Tulu 3, §4.3.2 "Batch Aggregation"|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}); the open-instruct code comments that sum reduction "can result in > 5 point improvements in AlpacaEval". In the 1,000-row sample, the answers at the 10th and 90th percentiles carry and graded tokens: under per-conversation averaging, a token of the short one weighs as much as a token of the long one derived, / .

Production traffic as the dataset. NeoHorse-1 is the online variant of this step rather than an offline curation pass: a router over a heterogeneous pool of models predicts each request's capability demand, those predictions order a three-stage supervised curriculum, and served interactions become training examples after structural validation, a semantic evaluation and labels for sub-scenes within a conversation, finer than one label per conversation, which is what makes supervising a partial trajectory work. Its measured gains, the ten benchmarks and the caveat that the loop ran once are on the parent page's Axis 2 and the {{NeoHorse-1 paper page|n:3db5c17b0d0d81e3b105e033a359ad24}} (with a live curriculum scheduler in its "Schedule the curriculum" tab).
