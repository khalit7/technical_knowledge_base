5. Online or offline

Vanilla DPO optimises preferences on a fixed dataset: pairs written before training, by other models or by an earlier version of this one, so the data is stale and off-policy. Online methods (PPO, GRPO, online or iterative DPO) sample from the current policy and score those samples, so the model learns from its own mistakes. At equal data they have been consistently stronger, at a higher cost in compute and complexity:

  • Well-tuned PPO "consistently outperforms DPO across all experiments", including code, where a 34B PPO model raised CodeContests 10@1k from 16.4% to 22.4% ({{Xu et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10719}}).
  • At the same KL budget online methods hold a clear advantage; offline-trained policies classify pairs better but generate worse, and neither data coverage nor a larger policy closes the gap ({{Tang et al. 2024, Google DeepMind|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08448}}); on-policy samples and a negative gradient both help ({{Tajwar et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14367}}).
  • The same loss run online wins: online DPO with an AI labeller beat offline DPO in human evaluation 63.7% to 7.7% (ties 28.6%) on TL;DR summaries ({{OAIF, Guo et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04792}}); three rounds of iterative DPO reached 24.17% length-controlled on AlpacaEval 2 ({{Xiong et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11456}}).
  • The cost side: in Tulu 3's controlled test PPO scored comparably to DPO, "albeit slightly lower", after "roughly 28 hours using two nodes" against "about 4 hours using a single node" for DPO, 14 times the node-hours derived, 28 × 2 / 4 ({{Tulu 3, §5.4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}).

The consensus: DPO-family losses for cheap, broad preference shaping; online RL (GRPO and PPO variants) where it matters most, for reasoning, agentic behaviour and the final alignment polish. Llama 3 used SFT, rejection sampling (10 to 30 samples per prompt, the reward model picks the best) and DPO, repeated over six rounds, choosing DPO because it "required less compute for large-scale models and performed better" ({{Llama 3, §4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}); each round's fresh samples make the recipe partly on-policy. Frontier reasoning models all use large-scale online RL ({{DeepSeek-R1|n:3c65c17b0d0d813faca4f7a51eaa0c65}}, {{Qwen3|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a19006e6b096a6e14b}}).

6. RLVR: reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards

RL needs some reward. When the answer is checkable (a maths answer checker, unit tests, a compiler, a rule-based format check, an instruction constraint) a program can give it, and the learned reward model, with its biases and its hackability, is bypassed. The checker becomes the new proxy: narrower, harder to flatter, still gameable (hard-coded outputs, edited tests, exiting before the assertions), which is the subject of {{Reward Hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}}.

Lineage

  • 2022, execution as a filter. AlphaCode sampled huge numbers of programs and kept those that passed the example tests ("filter them based on execution results on example tests from the problem description") at inference time; training was pretraining and fine-tuning, not RL ({{AlphaCode|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07814}}).
  • Nov 2024, the name. Tulu 3 introduced "Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)", which "leverages the existing RLHF objective but replaces the reward model with a verification function": a reward of 10 when a verifier accepts the answer, 0 otherwise, optimised with PPO with a KL penalty, on 29,946 prompts for GSM8K, MATH and verifiable instruction following (IFEval) ({{Tulu 3, §6 and Table 22|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}).
  • Jan 2025, from a base model. DeepSeek-R1-Zero ran GRPO with rule rewards (answer correctness and a format check) directly on DeepSeek-V3-Base, with no SFT; long chains of thought with self-checking emerged and AIME 2024 pass@1 rose from 15.6% to 77.9% (71.0% in arXiv v1). DeepSeek-R1 added a cold start of "thousands" of curated long reasoning examples before large-scale RLVR, for readability and to stop language mixing ({{arXiv 2501.12948|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948}}; the {{paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d813faca4f7a51eaa0c65}} trains a tiny R1-Zero).
  • 2025 on, standard. Qwen3's reasoning RL ran GRPO on 3,995 query-verifier pairs, lifting Qwen3-235B-A22B's AIME'24 from 70.1 to 85.1 in 170 steps ({{Qwen3, §4.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388}}). Ai2's Olmo 3 RL-Zero runs RLVR from a fully open base over maths, code, instruction following, general chat and a mix, with RL data decontaminated against the pretraining and midtraining data, so that RL-from-base results can be trusted as a benchmark ({{Olmo 3, §6|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}; {{Ai2: OLMo|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e08697df661f83c3ac}}).

Open problems

  • Does RLVR teach or elicit? RLVR-trained models beat their base at pass@1, but the base models score higher at large k: "the observed reasoning abilities originate from and are bounded by the base model" ({{Yue et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837}}, NeurIPS 2025 best paper runner-up). Random rewards lifted Qwen2.5-Math-7B on MATH-500 by 21.4 points against 29.1 for true rewards, an effect traced to the clipping term that mostly fails on Llama 3 and OLMo 2: ablate on more than one base family ({{Spurious Rewards|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947}}). Against that, judging the reasoning chain and not just the answer (CoT-Pass@k) finds RLVR does extend the reasoning boundary ({{Wen et al., ICLR 2026|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14245}}), and prolonged RL with reference resets "can uncover novel reasoning strategies that are inaccessible to base models" ({{ProRL, NVIDIA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24864}}).
  • Rewards where nothing can be checked. LLM judges and rubrics: rubric-based rewards gave up to 31% relative improvement on HealthBench over Likert-style judge rewards ({{Rubrics as Rewards, Scale AI|https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17746}}).
  • Credit over many turns. One reward at the end of a long agentic episode says little about which step mattered; GiGPO adds step-level advantages from states that recur across a group, over 12% better than GRPO on ALFWorld and 9% on WebShop ({{arXiv 2505.10978|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10978}}).
  • Entropy collapse. In long runs the policy's entropy falls and performance follows the fitted curve R = −a eH + b, so it is capped at −a + b once entropy reaches 0 ({{Cui et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617}}); clip-higher and covariance-based clipping keep entropy alive. Several rewards at once raise a related budget question: {{Learn What's Left, Not What's Mastered|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e38b0fe72f3daf44bc}}.

Infrastructure. {{verl|https://github.com/verl-project/verl}} (ByteDance's HybridFlow; it colocates the trainer and the rollout engine "for fast weight transfer using nccl", {{docs|https://verl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/hybrid_flow.html}}), {{OpenRLHF|https://github.com/OpenRLHF/OpenRLHF}}, {{TRL|https://github.com/huggingface/trl}}, {{SkyRL|https://github.com/NovaSky-AI/SkyRL}} and {{NeMo RL|https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/RL}}; rollouts are generated by {{vLLM|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm}} or {{SGLang|https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang}} with the new weights synced from the trainer after each update. The sandboxes and environments that agentic RL now spends most of its money on are on the parent page (Axis 3, "The environment is now the expensive part").

Corrections
The old page listed "AlphaCode-style execution feedback" as RLVR's first step without qualification: AlphaCode used execution results to filter samples at inference, not as an RL reward. It listed Tulu 3 among later users of RLVR; Tulu 3 is where the name was introduced (November 2024, with PPO). And R1-Zero's AIME 2024 gain depends on the version of the paper: 15.6% to 71.0% in arXiv v1, 77.9% in v2 and the Nature version.

Common mistakes

Training on the user's turns without meaning to
A conversational dataset in TRL trains on every token unless assistant_only_loss is on and the template marks the assistant spans. It is a choice, not a bug (instruction modelling sometimes helps), but it should be a choice: see the token chips in section 1.
Averaging the loss per conversation and then over the batch
With gradient accumulation over one conversation at a time, a token of a 21-token answer weighs about 57 times a token of a 1,187-token one (section 1). Sum the loss over tokens, as Tulu 3 does, or normalise by the total token count of the whole accumulated batch.
Packing without boundaries
A plain causal mask over a packed row lets each conversation attend to the ones before it. Reset position ids and use a variable-length attention kernel, or keep padding.
Serving with a different chat template
The template is part of what SFT trains. One newline less, a missing end-of-text token, or another model's markers shift the output distribution (Tulu 3 measured 1.4 points between templates).
Comparing β across methods
DPO's β = 0.1 multiplies a summed log-ratio; SimPO's β = 2 and length-normalised DPO's β = 5 multiply per-token averages; IPO's τ sets a target margin. Same letter, different units.
Reading a rising implicit reward margin as a better model
DPO's margin grows by pushing the rejected answer down, often faster than the chosen goes up, and both can fall together (likelihood displacement). In the pair animation, DPO ends with the rejected answer's log-probability far below where it started while the chosen moves much less. Evaluate generations, not margins.
"DPO is RLHF without the downsides"
It drops the RM and the sampling loop, and with them on-policy learning; the parent page's "Common mistakes" has the evidence. Online and iterative variants recover much of the gap (section 5).
"RLAIF means no people"
People still write the constitution, the judge's prompt, the rubrics and the evaluation; Constitutional AI kept human helpfulness labels. RLAIF moves human effort from labelling to specification.
"A verifier cannot be hacked"
An answer extractor that accepts malformed output or a test suite with gaps is a reward to hack, and the policy will find it ({{Reward Hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}}).
Reading a pass@1 gain as a new capability
RLVR reliably converts pass@k into pass@1; whether it expands what the base can solve at all is contested (section 6). Measure pass@k at large k before claiming it.
