5. Direct preference optimisation and its family

DPO removes the reward model and the RL loop: the policy becomes its own reward model. The step that makes it possible: the KL-constrained RLHF objective, maximise E[r(x, y)] − β KL(π ‖ πref), has a closed-form optimum, π*(y | x) = πref(y | x) exp(r(x, y)/β) / Z(x). Solved for the reward, any reward is β log(π*/πref) plus a term that depends only on the prompt (Eq. 5). Put that into the Bradley-Terry loss of section 3 and the prompt term cancels, because only differences of rewards appear. What is left trains the policy directly on (chosen, rejected) pairs ({{DPO, Eq. 7|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290}}):

LDPO = −E [ log σ( β log πθ(yw|x) / πref(yw|x) − β log πθ(yl|x) / πref(yl|x) ) ]

One trained model plus a frozen reference (whose log-probabilities can be computed once and cached), no sampling, a classification loss: stable and cheap, which is why it spread from Zephyr to Llama 3. The paper's headline, a 61% TL;DR win rate against PPO's 57%, rests on single runs about one standard error apart (the {{DPO paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d818bb1d8cafd30e20f9e}} works out the errors and trains DPO against RLHF on an enumerable toy with an exact optimum). Its successors each change one thing; the animation runs one pair through all of them.

How the toy is built (under 1 min)

Each token's log-probability is log σ(θ) of its own parameter, the shared prefix sharing one, so the bars move independently; a real model shares parameters across answers, which is where likelihood displacement comes from. Hyperparameters are each paper's defaults: DPO β = 0.1; cDPO ε = 0.1 illustrative, the note gives no default; IPO τ = 0.1 illustrative; length-normalised DPO β = 5 (Tulu 3); SimPO β = 2, γ = 1 (its Llama-3-Base row); ORPO λ = 0.1 (its Mistral setting); KTO β = 0.1, λD = λU = 1, with its reference point z0 taken as the clipped mean log-ratio of the two answers (the paper estimates it from mismatched pairs in the batch). Training: 400 steps of plain gradient descent, the step size fixed so that every method's largest first step is the same, so differences afterwards come from each loss's shape. Recomputed in src/recompute.py; the page's JavaScript matches it (check_pair.mjs).

The push on a pair against its margin: only a target or a hinge lets it reach zero

Why some losses stop and others do not. Almost every successor keeps DPO's frame (a margin between the chosen and the rejected answer, measured against a reference) and changes one thing: how hard a pair is pushed as a function of its margin. That one curve decides whether training stops. The figure draws the push −∂ℒ/∂h on one pair against its margin h = log πθ/πref(yw) − log πθ/πref(yl) in nats (before any β), one loss per step, with what each did when trained for 4,000 steps on the same deterministic labels on the {{DPO paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d818bb1d8cafd30e20f9e}}'s enumerable toy (its Run tab trains them).

Predict before stepping: which losses still push a pair that is already ranked right by 10 nats?

  1. Unlikelihood: push every pair at full strength (2019, the DPO paper's baseline). −log π(yw) + α log π(yl) (DPO paper, App. C.3). The push does not depend on the margin at all, and nothing ties the policy to πref. On the toy the grammar is gone by 4,000 steps (0.3% grammatical), the paper's "when when when" in miniature.
  2. DPO: push fades as the margin grows, but never stops (May 2023). −log σ(βh). The weight σ(−βh) is the paper's key ingredient; but it only reaches zero at an infinite margin, so with near-deterministic preferences the margins keep growing and the KL penalty is effectively ignored (Azar et al.'s critique). At β = 0.1 the push is still 75% of its starting value at a margin of 5 nats. On the toy the KL keeps climbing: 2.06 at 1,200 steps, 7.47 at 4,000.
  3. SLiC-HF's hinge: full push until a fixed margin, then none (May 2023, concurrent). max(0, δ − βh), here with δ = 1 and β = 0.5, so the push stops at h = 2 (SLiC-HF calibrates sequence likelihoods this way, with an extra SFT term not run here). A pair that is ordered well enough is left alone.
  4. IPO: pull every margin to a target, from either side (October 2023). (h − 1/(2τ))². The push is linear and changes sign at the target margin 1/(2τ) = 5 nats, so a pair can be pushed back. Azar et al. derive it as the identity case of their ΨPO objective, which needs no Bradley-Terry assumption. On the toy it is the only loss whose KL nearly levels off: 1.26 at 1,200 steps, 1.52 at 4,000, against DPO's climb from 2.06 to 7.47.
  5. Conservative DPO: assume ε of the labels are flipped (November 2023 note; rDPO March 2024). (1 − ε) ℒDPO(yw, yl) + ε ℒDPO(yl, yw): the gradient is zero when σ(βh) = 1 − ε (Mitchell's note), at h = ln 9 / β ≈ 22 nats for ε = 0.1, β = 0.1. A finite stopping point, but far away; the toy is still drifting at 4,000 steps.
  6. SimPO: no reference model, a length-averaged margin and a target gap (May 2024). −log σ(β/|yw| log π(yw) − β/|yl| log π(yl) − γ). Dropping πref saves memory and aligns the reward with how text is generated (average log-probability), but nothing anchors the policy except early stopping: on the toy, where every answer has 4 words so the length fix cannot help, it degenerates fastest (0.2% grammatical).

Curves at the toy's settings: β = 0.1 for DPO and cDPO (ε = 0.1), β = 0.5 for the hinge, τ = 0.1 for IPO, β = 2 and γ = 1 for SimPO (whose margin is the reference-free, length-averaged log-probability gap), each push scaled to 1 at h = 0. Same letter, different units. DPO's β = 0.1 multiplies a summed log-ratio; SimPO's β = 2 and length-normalised DPO's β = 5 multiply per-token averages; IPO's τ sets a target margin. Never carry a β from one loss to another.

A rising margin is not a better model. DPO's margin grows by pushing the rejected answer down, often faster than the chosen goes up, and both can fall together, moving probability mass to a third answer (likelihood displacement): DPO on refusals cut Llama-3-8B-Instruct's refusal rate from 74.4% to 33.4% ({{Razin et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08847}}). In the animation, DPO ends with the rejected answer's log-probability far below where it started while the chosen moves much less. Evaluate generations, not margins.

In practice the data matters more than the loss. DPO-family results are sensitive to label noise and to how far apart the chosen and rejected answers are, the failure PLC-DPO targets. Recipes add small guards: Llama 3 masked the header and termination tokens out of both answers (otherwise "tail repetition or abruptly generating termination tokens") and added an NLL term of weight 0.2 on the chosen answers ({{Llama 3, §4.1.4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}). And regenerating the pairs from the current policy, judging them and retraining (iterative or online DPO) recovers much of the gap to PPO (next section).
