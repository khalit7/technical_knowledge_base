The idea in one screen

A training step can be cut along six dimensions: the batch, the stored state, each weight matrix, the layers, the experts or the sequence. Each cut lowers what one GPU holds or computes and pays for it in bytes that a collective must move between GPUs, so a layout puts the chattiest cut on the fastest wire: NVLink inside a server, about nine times faster per GPU than the network between servers on an H100 cluster.

Six ways to cut one step over four GPUs: who holds what, and what has to cross the wire

Data (DP)

Cuts the batch; every GPU holds the whole model. Sends an all-reduce of the gradients: 24.1 GB. Any link.

Sharded data (ZeRO-3, FSDP)

Cuts the stored state as well. Sends each layer's weights twice and the gradients once: 36.1 GB. A fast link.

Tensor (TP)

Cuts every matrix; every GPU sees the whole batch. Sends four all-reduces inside every layer: 51.5 GB. NVLink only.

Pipeline (PP)

Cuts the layers. Sends one activation and one gradient per micro-batch: 0.54 GB. Any link, but stages idle in the bubble.

Expert (EP)

Cuts the experts of an MoE model. Sends tokens to their experts and back by all-to-all: 13.6 GB. NVLink, or a few servers.

Context (CP)

Cuts the sequence. Sends keys and values in every layer, plus the gradient all-reduce: 30.5 GB. A fast link.

Collective
an operation every GPU in a group calls together; NCCL implements them on NVIDIA hardware (section 2).
Model states
weights, gradients and optimiser state, the part of memory that does not depend on the batch.
Micro-batch
the slice of the batch one GPU processes in one forward and backward; gradient accumulation sums several before the update.
Bubble
the time pipeline stages sit idle while the pipeline fills and drains.
ZeRO stage
how much of the model states is sharded over the data-parallel GPUs: 1 optimiser state, 2 plus gradients, 3 plus weights (FSDP).
MFU
model FLOPs utilisation: useful FLOPs per second over the GPU's peak.
