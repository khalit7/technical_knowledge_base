Six ways to split one training step

A training step is a forward pass over a batch, a backward pass that produces one gradient per parameter, and an optimizer update. When the model, its optimizer state or its activations do not fit on one GPU, or one GPU is too slow, the step is cut along one of five dimensions: the batch (data parallelism), the stored state (sharded data parallelism: ZeRO, FSDP), each weight matrix (tensor parallelism), the layers (pipeline parallelism), the experts of a mixture-of-experts model (expert parallelism) or the sequence (context parallelism). Every cut buys memory or speed with communication, and the communication has a shape (a collective) and a wire it has to cross: NVLink inside a server, InfiniBand or Ethernet between servers, about nine times slower per GPU on an H100 cluster.

The memory every split is fighting

Mixed-precision training with Adam holds about 16 bytes per parameter before activations: 2 for bf16 weights, 2 for bf16 gradients, 12 for the fp32 master weights and the two Adam moments ({{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}} §3.1). A 7.5B model needs 120 GB on every GPU under plain data parallelism. Sixteen is one convention, not a constant: MT-NLG counts fp32 gradient copies and gets 20 (Smith et al. 2022 §2.1.1); Llama 3 accumulates and reduces gradients in FP32 (§3.3.2), about 18 on our reading derived; DeepSeek-V3 keeps fp32 master weights and gradients but bf16 Adam moments (§3.3), about 14 with a bf16 working copy derived. Activations come on top and grow with sequence length and micro-batch: about 34sbh bytes per layer for a GPT-style layer once the attention score matrix is not stored (Korthikanti et al. 2022), 36.5 GB for one 8,192-token sequence through Llama 3 8B's 32 layers derived. The parent page's {{bytes-per-parameter bars|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}} (Machinery) compare training with fine-tuning and serving; the {{Layout calculator|#t-calc}} here splits that memory across a layout.

One training step, nine ways

The same model, the same batch and the same four GPUs, split nine ways. The model is Llama 3 8B (32 layers, hidden size 4,096, 8.03B parameters recounted from its config.json (unsloth mirror of the gated repository)), drawn as 8 rows of 4 layers, each with its attention block (A) and feed-forward block (F) cut into four slices. The batch is four sequences of 8,192 tokens, or one sequence of 32,768 to see which splits can cut a single sequence. Each GPU's bar shows its memory to scale against an 80 GB H100: weights, gradients and optimizer state (16 bytes per parameter) and activations (34sbh per layer, derived). Expert parallelism needs an MoE model, so its mode uses an MoE twin illustrative: every feed-forward block becomes four experts the size of the original, each token routed to one (Switch-style top-1), so each token costs the same compute and the model grows to 24.9B parameters.

Try: step through DP, then switch to ZeRO-3 at the same step and watch the bars shrink while the bytes counter grows by half. TP sends the most bytes of all, 51.5 GB per GPU, which is why it stays on NVLink. Switch the batch to one 32K sequence: DP and ZeRO leave three GPUs idle, PP idles 75% of the time, and only TP and CP split the sequence's 146 GB of activations.
