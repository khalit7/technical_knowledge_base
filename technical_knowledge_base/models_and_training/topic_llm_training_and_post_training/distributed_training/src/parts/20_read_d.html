7. Expert parallelism

In a mixture-of-experts model each token visits only k of E feed-forward experts, so the experts can live on different GPUs. Tokens travel to their experts in an all-to-all dispatch and come back in an all-to-all combine, around every MoE layer, forward and backward; attention and the other dense parts stay replicated (data parallel) or are split another way. The step uses an MoE twin of Llama 3 8B illustrative: every feed-forward block becomes four experts the size of the original, each token routed to one (Switch-style top-1), so each token costs the same compute and the model grows to 24.9B parameters.

Per GPU the twin needs what the dense model needed, 128.5 GB, while holding three times the parameters: expert parallelism spreads the experts but does not shrink the dense state, so it is combined with FSDP, ZeRO-1 or TP for that. As Mixtral's paper puts it, tokens "are routed to the corresponding GPU for processing, and the expert's output is returned to the original token location", and EP "introduces challenges in load balancing" ({{Mixtral|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba72af0ac91ddc6b3}} §2.1): the busiest GPU sets the pace (switch the step to one sequence to see a hot spot).

At scale. DeepSeek-V3's layout (256 fine-grained routed experts per layer, 8 per token, 64-way EP over 8 nodes, each token capped at 4 nodes so it crosses InfiniBand once and NVLink forwards it, dispatch in FP8) is worked through on {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}, section 7, and on the {{DeepSeek-V3 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} (§3.2). What it means for the training layout: 20 of each H800's SMs drive the communication, and even so cross-node EP gave "an inefficient computation-to-communication ratio of approximately 1:1", the reason DualPipe (section 6) overlaps the two. The {{Layout calculator|#t-calc}}'s DeepSeek-V3 preset recomputes that ratio independently as 1.39 : 1.

Lineage, Qwen3's practice, and MoE training memory (depth)

Experts were sharded across devices with all-to-all routing by GShard (Lepikhin et al. 2020, the work DeepSeek-V3 cites for EP); {{Switch Transformers|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a8b541c9babbf40c94}} simplified routing to one expert and laid out how expert, data and model parallelism combine (§5, its Figure 9 redrawn on that page). The memory side of long-context MoE training (dispatch buffers, the vocabulary projection, checkpoints, optimiser state) is worked through on the {{MoE training memory bottlenecks|n:3e95c17b0d0d8148a1f3d412918234fc}} paper page.

8. Context parallelism

Context parallelism shards the sequence dimension of the activations across GPUs, for long-context training where activation memory, which grows with sequence length, is what no longer fits. Token-wise layers (the feed-forward block, norms) need nothing from other GPUs; attention does, because every query needs the keys and values of earlier tokens. Causal attention makes late tokens more expensive than early ones, so the sequence is cut into 2 × CP chunks and rank i takes chunks i and 2 × CP − 1 − i (Llama 3):

Each GPU holds a quarter of the activations whether the batch is four sequences or one, but the weights stay whole (128.5 GB), so CP is combined with FSDP or TP. There are three ways to bring the keys and values to the queries:

Ring, all-gather or all-to-all: the same attention, three traffic patterns

Ring Attention

K,V blocks travel round the ring, n − 1 passes, each overlapped with attention on the block already received.

All-gather (Llama 3)

Every GPU gathers all keys and values once, then attends its own queries. Exposed, but small with GQA.

Ulysses (DeepSpeed)

An all-to-all swaps the split: tokens by GPU become heads by GPU, ordinary attention runs, a second all-to-all swaps back.

  • Ring Attention (Liu et al. 2023): each GPU merges the partial outputs from each block with their log-sum-exp, the online softmax FlashAttention uses inside one GPU, so no GPU ever holds all the keys or a full score matrix. The cost is n sequential steps whose transfers must hide behind compute; a plain causal ring leaves GPU 0 with work in 1 of 8 steps, which the chunk ordering above fixes. The JAX page measured 123 MB of temporary memory per device for the ring against 273 MB for the all-gather at 8,192 tokens.
  • DeepSpeed Ulysses (Jacobs et al. 2023): its communication volume stays constant when sequence length and GPU count grow together, and it trained "2.5x faster with 4x longer sequence length" than the baseline it compared with reported; because it splits by heads, its degree is bounded by the head count derived.
  • All-gather, Llama 3's choice: the gather is exposed rather than overlapped, but with grouped-query attention keys and values are much smaller than queries and the gather is O(S) against attention's O(S²); it also handles document masks easily (§3.3.2).

Llama 3 used CP = 16 for its 128K-context phase ({{Table 4|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}). PyTorch's context parallel, shipped in torchtitan, implements Ring Attention with two rotation methods, ring passing and all-gather, defaulting to all-gather, and trained Llama 3 8B on 1M-token sequences on 32 H100s (PyTorch).

Correction
The old page said torchtitan "ships both" Ring Attention and Ulysses. Its context parallel is Ring Attention (with an all-gather rotation as the default); Ulysses is DeepSpeed's. It also implied Llama 3 used Ring Attention: Llama 3 chose an all-gather variant instead, for document masks and simplicity (§3.3.2).
