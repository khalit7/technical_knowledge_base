1. The memory every split is fighting

Take Llama 3 8B (8.03B parameters recounted from its config.json, an unsloth mirror of the gated repository) and train it with Adam in mixed precision on one 80 GB H100. Fig. 2 stacks what one GPU must hold, to scale, against its 80 GB: Llama 3 8B does not fit on one H100 before a single activation is stored.

  1. bf16 weights. 8.03B parameters at 2 bytes each: bf16 weights 16.1 GB, a fifth of the GPU.
  2. bf16 gradients. Backward produces one gradient per weight, the same size again: bf16 gradients 16.1 GB.
  3. Optimiser state. Mixed-precision Adam keeps an fp32 master copy of the weights and two fp32 moments, 12 bytes per parameter: fp32 master weights and two Adam moments 96.4 GB. Model states 128.5 GB, 16 bytes per parameter, are now well past the 80 GB line.
  4. Activations. Backward also needs what forward computed: activations of one 8,192-token sequence 36.5 GB, from the GPT-style estimate below derived (src/recompute.py). About 165 GB in all, twice what the GPU holds, before a second sequence.

The rule behind the bar: mixed-precision Adam holds about 16 bytes per parameter before activations, 2 for bf16 weights, 2 for bf16 gradients and 12 for the fp32 master weights and the two Adam moments ({{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}} §3.1). So plain data parallelism stops fitting above about 5B parameters on an 80 GB GPU (80 / 16 derived), and a 7.5B model needs 120 GB on every GPU. Activations come on top and grow with sequence length and micro-batch: about 34sbh bytes per layer for a GPT-style layer once the attention score matrix is not stored (Korthikanti et al. 2022), 36.5 GB for one 8,192-token sequence through Llama 3 8B's 32 layers derived. Every split below removes one of these four blocks from each GPU, or a share of it.

Sixteen bytes is one convention, not a constant (depth)

MT-NLG counts fp32 gradient copies and gets 20 (Smith et al. 2022 §2.1.1); Llama 3 accumulates and reduces gradients in FP32 (§3.3.2), about 18 on our reading derived; DeepSeek-V3 keeps fp32 master weights and gradients but bf16 Adam moments (§3.3), about 14 with a bf16 working copy derived; an 8-bit optimiser is less. The {{Layout calculator|#t-calc}} offers all four. Training against LoRA and QLoRA fine-tuning, model by model, is {{PEFT|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba08eef56b3e3b4eb}}'s memory calculator (section "Memory: full fine-tuning, LoRA and QLoRA").

2. Collectives: the five shapes communication takes

Every split talks through five operations that all GPUs in a group call together. Fig. 3 runs them on four GPUs, each holding four chunks of a buffer:

  1. Four GPUs, four chunks each. Each GPU holds one buffer cut into four chunks, coloured by the GPU that computed them. Every GPU in the group calls the collective together.
  2. Reduce-scatter. Chunk j of every GPU travels to GPU j and is summed there: each GPU ends with the sum of one chunk only (Σ). Sends (n − 1)/n of the buffer. ZeRO and FSDP gradients.
  3. All-gather, and so all-reduce. Each GPU contributes its chunk and ends with all of them. Sends (n − 1)/n. FSDP weights; context-parallel keys and values. A reduce-scatter followed by an all-gather leaves every GPU with the element-wise sum: an all-reduce, which sends 2(n − 1)/n of the buffer. Data-parallel gradients; tensor-parallel partial sums.
  4. All-to-all. Each GPU sends a different chunk to every other: GPU i's chunk j lands on GPU j. Sends (n − 1)/n, nothing summed. Expert dispatch and combine; DeepSpeed Ulysses.
  5. Send and receive. One GPU to its neighbour. Pipeline stages pass activations forward and gradients back.

The ring factors are nccl-tests'; since the bytes per GPU barely change with the group size, the slowest link sets the time. Because a ring all-reduce is exactly a reduce-scatter followed by an all-gather, ZeRO-1 and ZeRO-2 cost what DDP costs (Ultra-Scale Playbook). Inside an H100 server NVLink gives each GPU 450 GB/s each way; between servers each GPU has its own 400 Gb/s card, 50 GB/s, nine times less (Llama 3 used RoCE and InfiniBand at 400 Gb/s per GPU, {{§3.3.1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}); DeepSeek's export-limited H800s have NVLink at 160 GB/s, "roughly 3.2 times that of IB (50 GB/s)" ({{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} §3.2.2). That ratio pins each split to a level of the network.

Per GPU per step, for Llama 3.1 8B on 8 GPUs (BF16): DDP all-reduces 28.1 GB of gradients; FSDP moves 42.2 GB (1.5 × DDP: two weight gathers and a gradient reduce-scatter); tensor parallelism 0.47 GB per layer, on the critical path; a pipeline boundary 67 MB per micro-batch; expert parallelism at most 229 KB per token per MoE layer (DeepSeek-V3). The formulas behind each number and the time on each link: {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}, section 8.
