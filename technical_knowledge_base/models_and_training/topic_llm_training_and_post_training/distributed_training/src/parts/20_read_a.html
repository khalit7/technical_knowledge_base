1. The memory every split is fighting

Take Llama 3 8B (8.03B parameters recounted from its config.json, an unsloth mirror of the gated repository) and train it with Adam in mixed precision on one 80 GB H100:

Llama 3 8B does not fit on one H100 before a single activation is stored

bf16 weights 16.1 GBbf16 gradients 16.1 GBfp32 master weights and two Adam moments 96.4 GBactivations of one 8,192-token sequence 36.5 GB80 GB

Bar to scale, full width 180 GB. Model states 128.5 GB, 16 bytes per parameter; activations from the GPT-style estimate below derived (src/recompute.py).

The rule behind the bar: mixed-precision Adam holds about 16 bytes per parameter before activations, 2 for bf16 weights, 2 for bf16 gradients and 12 for the fp32 master weights and the two Adam moments ({{ZeRO|n:3c65c17b0d0d81879a7ed90bca007699}} §3.1). So plain data parallelism stops fitting above about 5B parameters on an 80 GB GPU (80 / 16 derived), and a 7.5B model needs 120 GB on every GPU. Activations come on top and grow with sequence length and micro-batch: about 34sbh bytes per layer for a GPT-style layer once the attention score matrix is not stored (Korthikanti et al. 2022), 36.5 GB for one 8,192-token sequence through Llama 3 8B's 32 layers derived. Every split below removes one of these four blocks from each GPU, or a share of it.

Sixteen bytes is one convention, not a constant (depth)

MT-NLG counts fp32 gradient copies and gets 20 (Smith et al. 2022 §2.1.1); Llama 3 accumulates and reduces gradients in FP32 (§3.3.2), about 18 on our reading derived; DeepSeek-V3 keeps fp32 master weights and gradients but bf16 Adam moments (§3.3), about 14 with a bf16 working copy derived; an 8-bit optimiser is less. The {{Layout calculator|#t-calc}} offers all four. The parent page's {{bytes-per-parameter bars|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b6876ee72b7056793b}} (Machinery) compare training with fine-tuning and serving.

2. Collectives: the five shapes communication takes

Every split talks through five operations that all GPUs in a group call together. Four GPUs, each holding four chunks of a buffer:

What each GPU holds before and after: an all-reduce is a reduce-scatter followed by an all-gather

All-reduce

Every GPU ends with the element-wise sum. Sends 2(n − 1)/n of the buffer. Data-parallel gradients; tensor-parallel partial sums.

Reduce-scatter

Each GPU ends with the sum of one chunk only. Sends (n − 1)/n. ZeRO and FSDP gradients.

All-gather

Each GPU contributes its chunk and ends with all of them. Sends (n − 1)/n. FSDP weights; context-parallel keys and values.

All-to-all

Each GPU sends a different chunk to every other. Sends (n − 1)/n, nothing summed. Expert dispatch and combine; DeepSpeed Ulysses.

Send and receive

One GPU to its neighbour. Pipeline stages pass activations forward and gradients back.

a chunk as GPU 0, 1, 2 or 3 computed itΣ a chunk summed over all four GPUs

The ring factors are nccl-tests'; since the bytes per GPU barely change with the group size, the slowest link sets the time. Because a ring all-reduce is exactly a reduce-scatter followed by an all-gather, ZeRO-1 and ZeRO-2 cost what DDP costs (Ultra-Scale Playbook). Inside an H100 server NVLink gives each GPU 450 GB/s each way; between servers each GPU has its own 400 Gb/s card, 50 GB/s, nine times less (Llama 3 used RoCE and InfiniBand at 400 Gb/s per GPU, {{§3.3.1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}); DeepSeek's export-limited H800s have NVLink at 160 GB/s, "roughly 3.2 times that of IB (50 GB/s)" ({{DeepSeek-V3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} §3.2.2). That ratio pins each split to a level of the network.
