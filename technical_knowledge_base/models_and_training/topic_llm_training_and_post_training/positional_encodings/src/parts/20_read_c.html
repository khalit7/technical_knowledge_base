What breaks past the trained length

A model trained on sequences of length L has only seen relative distances below L. For pair i of RoPE that means angles from 0 to Lθi. A fast pair (wavelength shorter than L) has turned through the full circle many times, so every angle it meets later is familiar. A slow pair (wavelength longer than L) has only covered a wedge of its circle; the first distance beyond L pushes it into angles that no training example produced, and the attention scores it feeds are untested. Position Interpolation's authors describe the result as "catastrophically high attention scores that completely ruin the self-attention mechanism" ({{Chen et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595}}); ALiBi's authors measured rotary models gaining for only 100 to 200 tokens past their training length ({{Press et al. §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}).

Correction to the old page
It said RoPE fails past L "because high-frequency dims see unseen rotation angles". It is the other way round: the low-frequency pairs, whose wavelength exceeds L, are the ones pushed into unseen angles, which is why YaRN leaves high frequencies alone and interpolates the low ones fully ({{YaRN §3.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}). High frequencies matter for the opposite reason: squeezing them (as Position Interpolation does) blurs neighbouring tokens.

"Seen" counts every relative distance below L, and each pair is judged alone, on its angle modulo one turn: a slow pair whose angle has wrapped back into its wedge has seen that angle, but never together with the other pairs' angles at this distance, so the counts are a lower bound on what is new to the model. Nor do they say how often an angle was trained: a pair just past its wedge is less of a problem than one far past it. Eight steps: a quarter of L, half, the edge, then five steps growing geometrically to s × L.

Stretching RoPE: the context-extension methods

Every method rewrites the frequencies θi (or, equivalently, the positions they multiply) of a model that already uses RoPE, then usually fine-tunes briefly at the new length. With s = Lnew / Ltrain, in historical order:

  • Position Interpolation (PI), Meta, June 2023: divide every position by s, f′(x, m) = f(x, mL/L′), so all angles stay inside the trained range. Its interpolation bound is "at least ∼600× smaller" than extrapolation's (Theorem 2.1); LLaMA 7B to 65B reached 32,768 tokens "within 1000 steps" of fine-tuning, with "minor degradation" on the original 2,048-token window, regressions of up to 2% on standard benchmarks ({{Chen et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595}}). The cost is that every pair is slowed by s, including the fast ones that resolve neighbouring tokens. Gemma 3 uses it: pretraining at 32K, then "a scaling factor of 8" on the global layers for 128K ({{Gemma 3 §5.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19786}}).
  • NTK-aware scaling, bloc97 on Reddit, June 2023: instead of the positions, raise the base, b′ = b · s|D|/(|D|−2) ({{YaRN Eq. 16|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}), "scaling high frequencies less and low frequencies more". Pair 0 is untouched and the slowest pair is slowed by exactly s: for base 10,000, d = 128 and s = 4, b′ = 40,890 (recompute.py). It works surprisingly well with no fine-tuning, but some pairs end up slightly past their trained angles (try it in the animation). Dynamic NTK (emozilla, also on Reddit) sets s = max(1, l′/L) from the current length l′ at every step, so short inputs run unchanged ({{YaRN §3.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}). The original posts: {{NTK-aware|https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/14lz7j5/ntkaware_scaled_rope_allows_llama_models_to_have/}}, {{dynamic|https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/14mrgpr/dynamically_scaled_rope_further_increases/}}.
  • YaRN, Peng et al., August 2023: "NTK-by-parts" plus a temperature. With r(i) = L/λi, the number of turns pair i makes within the trained length, pairs with r > β (32 for Llama) are not interpolated, pairs with r < α (1) are divided by s, and a linear ramp blends between; then the logits are scaled by 1/t with √(1/t) = 0.1 ln s + 1 (Eqs. 17, 18, 22). It needed "10x less tokens and 2.5x less training steps than previous methods": Llama 2 7B and 13B were fine-tuned 400 steps at s = 16 (64K) and 200 more at s = 32, which then extrapolated to 128K ({{Peng et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00071}}). DeepSeek-V2 and V3, Qwen3, gpt-oss and SmolLM3 use it.
  • Llama 3 scaling (the llama3 rope type, July 2024): the same idea with the boundaries set by wavelength. Pairs with a wavelength under L/4 = 2,048 tokens are kept, those over L = 8,192 are divided by 8, and a smooth blend covers the six in between: 29 kept, 6 blended, 29 divided in Llama 3.1 8B (recompute.py from its {{config|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct/blob/main/config.json}} and {{the code|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/modeling_rope_utils.py}}). The Llama 3 paper itself does not describe this scaling; it describes the long-context stage (below).
  • LongRoPE, Microsoft, February 2024: one rescaling factor per pair, found by evolutionary search, which "extends the context window of pre-trained LLMs to an impressive 2048k tokens, with up to only 1k fine-tuning steps at within 256k training lengths", progressively, then readjusted at 8K to recover short-context quality; shown on LLaMA2 and Mistral ({{Ding et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13753}}). Phi-3-mini's 128K version uses it ("a long context version via LongRope", {{Phi-3 report §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14219}}): its config carries 48 "long" factors from 1.07 to 64.76 and 48 "short" ones (1.10 to 3.05), switched by sequence length. LongRoPE2 (February 2025) blames the remaining failures on "insufficient training in higher RoPE dimensions", searches the factors with a "needle-driven" perplexity, and adds "mixed context window training" that keeps the original RoPE for short sequences; it extends LLaMA3-8B to a 128K effective length "while retaining over 98.5% of short-context performance, using only 10B tokens", 80 times fewer than Meta's own extension ({{Shang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20082}}).

Recipes in production

No current model trains its whole run at its final window. The pattern: pretrain short with a base chosen for the long target, extend in a short continued-pretraining stage on long documents with the positions rescaled, and check that short-context quality recovers. Beyond RoPE

  • NoPE. A decoder-only model with no positional encoding still learns order from the causal mask: a token at position m can see m + 1 tokens, and that count is recoverable. Kazemnejad et al. found NoPE "outperforms other explicit positional encoding methods" on their length-generalisation tasks, and showed it "can represent both absolute and relative PEs, but when trained with SGD, it mostly resembles T5's relative PE attention patterns" ({{NeurIPS 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19466}}). At scale, NoPE alone "lags behind" RoPE on standard benchmarks, although in needle tests it put the most attention on the needle ({{Yang et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18795}}). Hence the hybrids in the config table above: Cohere's RNoPE-SWA (RoPE in sliding-window layers, NoPE in one full-attention layer of four) reached 256K with "almost no loss" on needle tests and trains "nearly 2x faster at 128k", shipped in Command A; Llama 4 adds a position-dependent temperature on its NoPE layers ("inference time temperature scaling", from {{Scalable-Softmax|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19399}}); SmolLM3 found that removing RoPE from every 4th layer "improves long context performance without affecting short context capabilities".
  • CoPE (Contextual Position Encoding, Meta, 2024). Positions are counted, not indexed: each key gets a gate gij = σ(qi·kj), the position of key j from query i is the sum of the gates between them, and fractional positions interpolate between learned integer embeddings (§4). A head can therefore measure "the i-th sentence" or "the i-th verb" instead of tokens. It solves the selective copy, counting and Flip-Flop tasks where standard encodings fail and improves perplexity on language modelling and code ({{Golovneva et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18719}}); not used at scale yet.
  • Fixing the spectrum. {{FoPE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17739}} (Fourier Position Embedding, ICML 2025) treats each dimension as a Fourier series and "zero-outs the destructive frequency components", those insufficiently trained because of truncation. {{HoPE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21216}} (2024) argues "long-term decay is outdated" and keeps "only high-frequency signals", replacing the others with position-independent components. {{Wavelet-based positions|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02004}} (ICLR 2025) capture several window sizes at once "without limiting the model's attention field". And {{Barbero et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06205}} found that Gemma uses RoPE's highest frequencies for positional attention patterns and "greatly prefers to use the lowest frequencies", which they suspect carry semantic information; this is one argument for partial RoPE.
  • Long context is not only positions. Sliding windows with a few global layers, chunked attention and sparse attention (DeepSeek's NSA and DSA) decide what a long context costs to serve; that side lives in {{Topic: inference-and-serving|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c08b3bc95ff45c7b13}}, and the architecture comparisons in {{Topic: llms|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}} and its {{Architecture Gallery|n:3c65c17b0d0d81be8a07f2562fa2030a}}.

Common mistakes

"RoPE breaks past L because the high-frequency dimensions see new angles"
The low-frequency pairs do: fast pairs have seen every angle. Shown in the animation above; this was the old version of this page.
"NTK scaling means a base like 500,000"
NTK-aware scaling for s = 4 at d = 128 takes 10,000 to 40,890. 500,000 is Llama 3's pretraining base, chosen before training (ABF, Xiong et al.), not a scaling applied to a trained model.
"LongRoPE is behind Phi-3's 2M-token context"
The 2,048K result is the LongRoPE paper's, on LLaMA2 and Mistral. Phi-3-mini uses LongRoPE for 128K.
"factor × original length = the context window"
Llama 3.1's 8 × 8,192 = 65,536 is half its configured 131,072; the model was trained at 128K in its long-context stage, so the rescaled spectrum does not have to cover the window on its own. Gemma 3's 8 × 32,768 = 262,144 is twice its 131,072. DeepSeek-V3's 40 × 4,096 = 163,840 and Phi-3's 32 × 4,096 = 131,072 do match their configs' max_position_embeddings.
"RoPE extrapolates because it is relative"
Relative does not mean unbounded: the score at distance 10,000 uses angles a model trained at 4,096 never saw. Rotary models gained only 100 to 200 tokens past their training length in ALiBi's measurement; ALiBi, whose penalty is linear and has nothing to go out of range, is the relative method that extrapolates.
"Turn on YaRN for every request"
Static YaRN slows the low frequencies for short inputs too; Qwen advises enabling it only when long contexts are needed, with the factor set to the length required.
"Weights from one codebase load into another's RoPE"
Interleaved and split-half layouts need the query and key projections permuted; without it the model runs and produces garbage.
"NoPE layers are worse at retrieval"
The old version of this page said so. In Yang et al.'s needle tests the NoPE variant put the most attention on the needle; its weakness was on standard benchmarks, which is why it is used in one layer of four rather than everywhere.
