The relative family

  • Shaw, Uszkoreit and Vaswani (2018). Add a learnable vector for each relative distance, clipped at a maximum k, directly into the attention computation: to the keys when scores are formed, and optionally to the values when they are summed. On WMT 2014 translation it beat absolute positions by 1.3 BLEU (English to German) and 0.3 BLEU (English to French), and "combining relative and absolute position representations yields no further improvement" ({{Shaw et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02155}}).
  • T5's relative bias (2019). Simplify Shaw to one learned scalar per distance bucket, added to the logit. There are 32 buckets per head, shared across layers: the first half are exact offsets, the rest grow logarithmically up to an offset of 128, and everything further shares the last bucket. In the decoder, which looks back only, offsets 0 to 15 get a bucket each, then 16 to 18, 19 to 20, and so on, with 113 onwards in bucket 31 (recomputed with T5's {{bucket function|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/t5/modeling_t5.py}}). Cheap, per-head, and learned; one layer is blind beyond 128, though stacked layers can pass information further. Its extrapolation was the best of the pre-ALiBi baselines (600 to 800 extra tokens), at the price of training at least twice as slowly ({{ALiBi §2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}). Details on the {{T5 page|n:3d45c17b0d0d81ec8ebfec02e953a7fd}}.
  • ALiBi (2021). No embeddings at all: add a fixed, non-learned penalty −m · distance to each attention score, with a different slope m per head. For 8 heads the slopes are 1/2, 1/4, ..., 1/256; for 16, the geometric sequence from 1/√2 with ratio 1/√2 ({{Press et al. §3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12409}}). MPT-7B's config generalises this as 2−8h/n over its 32 heads (alibi_bias_max 8: slopes from 0.84 to 1/256). Closer tokens score higher, steeply in some heads and gently in others. Its strength is extrapolation: a 1.3B model trained on 1,024 tokens "extrapolates to input sequences of length 2048, achieving the same perplexity as a sinusoidal position embedding model trained on inputs of length 2048 but training 11% faster and using 11% less memory". Used by {{BLOOM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100}} and MPT; every open model family since 2023 in this page's {{config table|#t-spec}} uses RoPE instead, with scaling for long contexts.
  • DeBERTa's disentangled attention (2020). Summing content and position into one vector mixes the two from the first layer. DeBERTa keeps them apart: "each word is represented using two vectors that encode its content and position, respectively, and the attention weights among words are computed using disentangled matrices on their contents and relative positions" ({{He et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03654}}): content-to-content, content-to-position and position-to-content terms. Absolute positions enter only in an "enhanced mask decoder" near the output, for masked-token prediction.

RoPE, the modern default

Rotary position embedding ({{RoFormer|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cfa5e9f54459720098}}, Su et al. 2021) rotates instead of adding. Each query and key vector of a head is split into d/2 pairs of dimensions; pair i of the vector at position m is rotated by the angle mθi, with

θi = base−2i/d,   wavelength λi = 2π / θi = 2π · base2i/d tokens

10,000 in the paper; 500,000 in Llama 3; 1,000,000 in Qwen3 and Gemma 3's global layers; 5,000,000 in SmolLM3 (configs in src/inputs/)

Why it works: a rotation preserves lengths, and the dot product of two rotated vectors depends only on the difference of their angles, so the score depends on m − n only. RoPE is absolute in form (each vector is rotated by its own position) and relative in effect, with no parameters, applied to queries and keys (not values) in every attention layer; a key is rotated once, when it enters the cache. The pairs form a geometric spectrum: in Llama 3.1 8B (base 500,000, d = 128) pair 0 turns once every 6.3 tokens and pair 63 once every 2.56 million; 35 of the 64 pairs complete at least one turn within the 8,192-token pretraining length (recompute.py). Fast pairs resolve neighbouring tokens, slow pairs measure long distances. The paper's long-term decay of scores with distance holds for its bound and for aligned vectors, not in expectation for random ones; the RoFormer page rebuilds that figure, shows the 2D dial on many pairs and runs trained toy models past their length, and the {{Architecture Gallery|n:3c65c17b0d0d81be8a07f2562fa2030a}} has the one-pair dial. Since 2023, Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma, Mistral ({{Mistral 7B config|https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-v0.1/blob/main/config.json}}: rope_theta 10000), gpt-oss and most other open families use RoPE or a variant of it.

Choosing the base. A larger base slows every pair except pair 0, so more pairs stay informative across a long context. Xiong et al. found Llama 2's base of 10,000 decayed attention too strongly beyond 4,000 to 6,000 tokens and proposed "increasing the 'base frequency b' of RoPE from 10,000 to 500,000", called adjusted base frequency, ABF ({{Xiong et al. §4.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16039}}); Llama 3 pretrains with 500,000 ({{Llama 3 page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}), and Qwen3 raises 10,000 to 1,000,000 for its long-context stage ({{Qwen3 page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a19006e6b096a6e14b}}). This is a pretraining choice, not the NTK-aware scaling below, though both change the base.

Variants, read from the configs

Four variants matter in practice. Partial RoPE rotates only a fraction of each head's dimensions and leaves the rest position-free. Decoupled RoPE (DeepSeek's multi-head latent attention, MLA) keeps position out of the compressed keys entirely and carries it in a small separate key: RoPE "is position-sensitive for both keys and queries", so a rotated key would block absorbing the key up-projection into the query, and every prefix key would have to be recomputed; the fix uses "additional multi-head queries and a shared key to carry RoPE" of 64 dimensions per head ({{DeepSeek-V2 §2.1.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434}}; {{DeepSeek-V3 page|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}). Multimodal RoPE gives images and video several position indices: Qwen2-VL splits the rotation into "temporal, height, and width" components, and for text "these components utilize identical position IDs, making M-RoPE functionally equivalent to 1D-RoPE" ({{Qwen2-VL §2.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12191}}). Interleaved layers mix RoPE layers with layers that have no positions (NoPE) or a different base. The layer strips are drawn from each config.
