Positional Encodings

How a Transformer knows where each token is: learned and sinusoidal positions, the relative family (Shaw, T5, ALiBi, DeBERTa), rotary embeddings (RoPE) and their variants, what breaks past the trained length, the RoPE scaling methods that stretch a context window (PI, NTK, YaRN, Llama 3, LongRoPE), and layers with no positions at all (NoPE). In one screen

Attention compares every query with every key by a dot product, and a dot product does not care about order: shuffle the tokens and each one gets the same scores. Something has to tell the model where each token sits. There are three broad answers: add a position vector to the input once (absolute: learned or sinusoidal), change the attention score by the distance between the two tokens (relative: Shaw, T5, ALiBi, DeBERTa, and RoPE, which does it by rotating queries and keys), or add nothing and let the causal mask carry the order (NoPE). Since 2023 almost every open LLM uses RoPE, and the practical questions are about RoPE: which base, how many dimensions to rotate, and how to stretch it past the length it was trained on.

Absolute or relative, embedding or encoding

Two distinctions organise the whole field.

  • Learned or fixed. A positional embedding in the strict sense is a learned table: row p is a trained vector added to the token at position p (GPT-2, BERT). It is simple, but the table has as many rows as the training length, so there is nothing to look up beyond it and no extrapolation at all. A positional encoding is a fixed function of position. The 2017 Transformer's sinusoidal encoding was chosen because it "may allow the model to extrapolate to sequence lengths longer than the ones encountered during training" (Vaswani et al. §3.5); learned embeddings gave "nearly identical results" there (Table 3, row E). In practice it interpolates well and extrapolates poorly: ALiBi's authors measured its perplexity stop improving about 20 tokens past a 512-token training length and degrade from about 50 (Press et al. 2021 §2). The naming is loose in the literature: RoPE is "rotary position embedding" although nothing in it is learned.
  • Absolute or relative. Absolute methods answer "where is this token?" and are added to the input once, before the first layer. Relative methods answer "how far apart are these two tokens?" and act inside attention, at every layer, which matches what attention uses: the score between a query and a key. A relative method gives the same score to the same pair of tokens wherever the pair sits in the sequence.

The relative family

  • Shaw, Uszkoreit and Vaswani (2018). Add a learnable vector for each relative distance, clipped at a maximum k, directly into the attention computation: to the keys when scores are formed, and optionally to the values when they are summed. On WMT 2014 translation it beat absolute positions by 1.3 BLEU (English to German) and 0.3 BLEU (English to French), and "combining relative and absolute position representations yields no further improvement" (Shaw et al.).
  • T5's relative bias (2019). Simplify Shaw to one learned scalar per distance bucket, added to the logit. There are 32 buckets per head, shared across layers: the first half are exact offsets, the rest grow logarithmically up to an offset of 128, and everything further shares the last bucket. In the decoder, which looks back only, offsets 0 to 15 get a bucket each, then 16 to 18, 19 to 20, and so on, with 113 onwards in bucket 31 (recomputed with T5's bucket function). Cheap, per-head, and learned; one layer is blind beyond 128, though stacked layers can pass information further. Its extrapolation was the best of the pre-ALiBi baselines (600 to 800 extra tokens), at the price of training at least twice as slowly (ALiBi §2). Details on the T5 page.
  • ALiBi (2021). No embeddings at all: add a fixed, non-learned penalty −m · distance to each attention score, with a different slope m per head. For 8 heads the slopes are 1/2, 1/4, ..., 1/256; for 16, the geometric sequence from 1/√2 with ratio 1/√2 (Press et al. §3). MPT-7B's config generalises this as 2−8h/n over its 32 heads (alibi_bias_max 8: slopes from 0.84 to 1/256). Closer tokens score higher, steeply in some heads and gently in others. Its strength is extrapolation: a 1.3B model trained on 1,024 tokens "extrapolates to input sequences of length 2048, achieving the same perplexity as a sinusoidal position embedding model trained on inputs of length 2048 but training 11% faster and using 11% less memory". Used by BLOOM and MPT; every open model family since 2023 in this page's config table uses RoPE instead, with scaling for long contexts.
  • DeBERTa's disentangled attention (2020). Summing content and position into one vector mixes the two from the first layer. DeBERTa keeps them apart: "each word is represented using two vectors that encode its content and position, respectively, and the attention weights among words are computed using disentangled matrices on their contents and relative positions" (He et al.): content-to-content, content-to-position and position-to-content terms. Absolute positions enter only in an "enhanced mask decoder" near the output, for masked-token prediction.

RoPE, the modern default

Rotary position embedding (RoFormer, Su et al. 2021) rotates instead of adding. Each query and key vector of a head is split into d/2 pairs of dimensions; pair i of the vector at position m is rotated by the angle mθi, with

10,000 in the paper; 500,000 in Llama 3; 1,000,000 in Qwen3 and Gemma 3's global layers; 5,000,000 in SmolLM3 (configs in src/inputs/)

Why it works: a rotation preserves lengths, and the dot product of two rotated vectors depends only on the difference of their angles, so the score depends on m − n only. RoPE is absolute in form (each vector is rotated by its own position) and relative in effect, with no parameters, applied to queries and keys (not values) in every attention layer; a key is rotated once, when it enters the cache. The pairs form a geometric spectrum: in Llama 3.1 8B (base 500,000, d = 128) pair 0 turns once every 6.3 tokens and pair 63 once every 2.56 million; 35 of the 64 pairs complete at least one turn within the 8,192-token pretraining length (recompute.py). Fast pairs resolve neighbouring tokens, slow pairs measure long distances. The paper's long-term decay of scores with distance holds for its bound and for aligned vectors, not in expectation for random ones; the RoFormer page rebuilds that figure, shows the 2D dial on many pairs and runs trained toy models past their length, and the Architecture Gallery has the one-pair dial. Since 2023, Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma, Mistral (Mistral 7B config: rope_theta 10000), gpt-oss and most other open families use RoPE or a variant of it.

Choosing the base. A larger base slows every pair except pair 0, so more pairs stay informative across a long context. Xiong et al. found Llama 2's base of 10,000 decayed attention too strongly beyond 4,000 to 6,000 tokens and proposed "increasing the 'base frequency b' of RoPE from 10,000 to 500,000", called adjusted base frequency, ABF (Xiong et al. §4.1); Llama 3 pretrains with 500,000 (Llama 3 page), and Qwen3 raises 10,000 to 1,000,000 for its long-context stage (Qwen3 page). This is a pretraining choice, not the NTK-aware scaling below, though both change the base.

Variants, read from the configs

Four variants matter in practice. Partial RoPE rotates only a fraction of each head's dimensions and leaves the rest position-free. Decoupled RoPE (DeepSeek's multi-head latent attention, MLA) keeps position out of the compressed keys entirely and carries it in a small separate key: RoPE "is position-sensitive for both keys and queries", so a rotated key would block absorbing the key up-projection into the query, and every prefix key would have to be recomputed; the fix uses "additional multi-head queries and a shared key to carry RoPE" of 64 dimensions per head (DeepSeek-V2 §2.1.3; DeepSeek-V3 page). Multimodal RoPE gives images and video several position indices: Qwen2-VL splits the rotation into "temporal, height, and width" components, and for text "these components utilize identical position IDs, making M-RoPE functionally equivalent to 1D-RoPE" (Qwen2-VL §2.1). Interleaved layers mix RoPE layers with layers that have no positions (NoPE) or a different base. The layer strips are drawn from each config.

What breaks past the trained length

A model trained on sequences of length L has only seen relative distances below L. For pair i of RoPE that means angles from 0 to Lθi. A fast pair (wavelength shorter than L) has turned through the full circle many times, so every angle it meets later is familiar. A slow pair (wavelength longer than L) has only covered a wedge of its circle; the first distance beyond L pushes it into angles that no training example produced, and the attention scores it feeds are untested. Position Interpolation's authors describe the result as "catastrophically high attention scores that completely ruin the self-attention mechanism" (Chen et al. 2023); ALiBi's authors measured rotary models gaining for only 100 to 200 tokens past their training length (Press et al. §2).

Correction to the old page
It said RoPE fails past L "because high-frequency dims see unseen rotation angles". It is the other way round: the low-frequency pairs, whose wavelength exceeds L, are the ones pushed into unseen angles, which is why YaRN leaves high frequencies alone and interpolates the low ones fully (YaRN §3.2). High frequencies matter for the opposite reason: squeezing them (as Position Interpolation does) blurs neighbouring tokens.

"Seen" counts every relative distance below L, and each pair is judged alone, on its angle modulo one turn: a slow pair whose angle has wrapped back into its wedge has seen that angle, but never together with the other pairs' angles at this distance, so the counts are a lower bound on what is new to the model. Stretching RoPE: the context-extension methods

Every method rewrites the frequencies θi (or, equivalently, the positions they multiply) of a model that already uses RoPE, then usually fine-tunes briefly at the new length. With s = Lnew / Ltrain, in historical order:

  • Position Interpolation (PI), Meta, June 2023: divide every position by s, f′(x, m) = f(x, mL/L′), so all angles stay inside the trained range. Its interpolation bound is "at least ∼600× smaller" than extrapolation's (Theorem 2.1); LLaMA 7B to 65B reached 32,768 tokens "within 1000 steps" of fine-tuning, with "minor degradation" on the original 2,048-token window, regressions of up to 2% on standard benchmarks (Chen et al.). The cost is that every pair is slowed by s, including the fast ones that resolve neighbouring tokens. Gemma 3 uses it: pretraining at 32K, then "a scaling factor of 8" on the global layers for 128K (Gemma 3 §5.3).
  • NTK-aware scaling, bloc97 on Reddit, June 2023: instead of the positions, raise the base, b′ = b · s|D|/(|D|−2) (YaRN Eq. 16), "scaling high frequencies less and low frequencies more". Pair 0 is untouched and the slowest pair is slowed by exactly s: for base 10,000, d = 128 and s = 4, b′ = 40,890 (recompute.py). It works surprisingly well with no fine-tuning, but some pairs end up slightly past their trained angles (try it in the animation). Dynamic NTK (emozilla, also on Reddit) sets s = max(1, l′/L) from the current length l′ at every step, so short inputs run unchanged (YaRN §3.3). The original posts: NTK-aware, dynamic.
  • YaRN, Peng et al., August 2023: "NTK-by-parts" plus a temperature. With r(i) = L/λi, the number of turns pair i makes within the trained length, pairs with r > β (32 for Llama) are not interpolated, pairs with r < α (1) are divided by s, and a linear ramp blends between; then the logits are scaled by 1/t with √(1/t) = 0.1 ln s + 1 (Eqs. 17, 18, 22). It needed "10x less tokens and 2.5x less training steps than previous methods": Llama 2 7B and 13B were fine-tuned 400 steps at s = 16 (64K) and 200 more at s = 32, which then extrapolated to 128K (Peng et al.). DeepSeek-V2 and V3, Qwen3, gpt-oss and SmolLM3 use it.
  • Llama 3 scaling (the llama3 rope type, July 2024): the same idea with the boundaries set by wavelength. Pairs with a wavelength under L/4 = 2,048 tokens are kept, those over L = 8,192 are divided by 8, and a smooth blend covers the six in between: 29 kept, 6 blended, 29 divided in Llama 3.1 8B (recompute.py from its config and the code). The Llama 3 paper itself does not describe this scaling; it describes the long-context stage (below).
  • LongRoPE, Microsoft, February 2024: one rescaling factor per pair, found by evolutionary search, which "extends the context window of pre-trained LLMs to an impressive 2048k tokens, with up to only 1k fine-tuning steps at within 256k training lengths", progressively, then readjusted at 8K to recover short-context quality; shown on LLaMA2 and Mistral (Ding et al.). Phi-3-mini's 128K version uses it ("a long context version via LongRope", Phi-3 report §2): its config carries 48 "long" factors from 1.07 to 64.76 and 48 "short" ones (1.10 to 3.05), switched by sequence length. LongRoPE2 (February 2025) blames the remaining failures on "insufficient training in higher RoPE dimensions", searches the factors with a "needle-driven" perplexity, and adds "mixed context window training" that keeps the original RoPE for short sequences; it extends LLaMA3-8B to a 128K effective length "while retaining over 98.5% of short-context performance, using only 10B tokens", 80 times fewer than Meta's own extension (Shang et al.).

Recipes in production

No current model trains its whole run at its final window. The pattern: pretrain short with a base chosen for the long target, extend in a short continued-pretraining stage on long documents with the positions rescaled, and check that short-context quality recovers. Static scaling costs short inputs
Qwen's card: the open-source frameworks "implement static YaRN, which means the scaling factor remains constant regardless of input length, potentially impacting performance on shorter texts", so it advises adding rope_scaling "only when processing long contexts is required", and setting the factor to the need (2.0 for 65,536 tokens) (Qwen3-8B card). Its hosted endpoint uses dynamic YaRN instead.

Beyond RoPE

  • NoPE. A decoder-only model with no positional encoding still learns order from the causal mask: a token at position m can see m + 1 tokens, and that count is recoverable. Kazemnejad et al. found NoPE "outperforms other explicit positional encoding methods" on their length-generalisation tasks, and showed it "can represent both absolute and relative PEs, but when trained with SGD, it mostly resembles T5's relative PE attention patterns" (NeurIPS 2023). At scale, NoPE alone "lags behind" RoPE on standard benchmarks, although in needle tests it put the most attention on the needle (Yang et al. 2025). Hence the hybrids in the config table above: Cohere's RNoPE-SWA (RoPE in sliding-window layers, NoPE in one full-attention layer of four) reached 256K with "almost no loss" on needle tests and trains "nearly 2x faster at 128k", shipped in Command A; Llama 4 adds a position-dependent temperature on its NoPE layers ("inference time temperature scaling", from Scalable-Softmax); SmolLM3 found that removing RoPE from every 4th layer "improves long context performance without affecting short context capabilities".
  • CoPE (Contextual Position Encoding, Meta, 2024). Positions are counted, not indexed: each key gets a gate gij = σ(qi·kj), the position of key j from query i is the sum of the gates between them, and fractional positions interpolate between learned integer embeddings (§4). A head can therefore measure "the i-th sentence" or "the i-th verb" instead of tokens. It solves the selective copy, counting and Flip-Flop tasks where standard encodings fail and improves perplexity on language modelling and code (Golovneva et al.); not used at scale yet.
  • Fixing the spectrum. FoPE (Fourier Position Embedding, ICML 2025) treats each dimension as a Fourier series and "zero-outs the destructive frequency components", those insufficiently trained because of truncation. HoPE (2024) argues "long-term decay is outdated" and keeps "only high-frequency signals", replacing the others with position-independent components. Wavelet-based positions (ICLR 2025) capture several window sizes at once "without limiting the model's attention field". And Barbero et al. found that Gemma uses RoPE's highest frequencies for positional attention patterns and "greatly prefers to use the lowest frequencies", which they suspect carry semantic information; this is one argument for partial RoPE.
  • Long context is not only positions. Sliding windows with a few global layers, chunked attention and sparse attention (DeepSeek's NSA and DSA) decide what a long context costs to serve; that side lives in Topic: inference-and-serving, and the architecture comparisons in Topic: llms and its Architecture Gallery.

Common mistakes

"RoPE breaks past L because the high-frequency dimensions see new angles"
The low-frequency pairs do: fast pairs have seen every angle. Shown in the animation above; this was the old version of this page.
"NTK scaling means a base like 500,000"
NTK-aware scaling for s = 4 at d = 128 takes 10,000 to 40,890. 500,000 is Llama 3's pretraining base, chosen before training (ABF, Xiong et al.), not a scaling applied to a trained model.
"LongRoPE is behind Phi-3's 2M-token context"
The 2,048K result is the LongRoPE paper's, on LLaMA2 and Mistral. Phi-3-mini uses LongRoPE for 128K.
"factor × original length = the context window"
Llama 3.1's 8 × 8,192 = 65,536 is half its configured 131,072; the model was trained at 128K in its long-context stage, so the rescaled spectrum does not have to cover the window on its own. Gemma 3's 8 × 32,768 = 262,144 is twice its 131,072. DeepSeek-V3's 40 × 4,096 = 163,840 and Phi-3's 32 × 4,096 = 131,072 do match their configs' max_position_embeddings.
"RoPE extrapolates because it is relative"
Relative does not mean unbounded: the score at distance 10,000 uses angles a model trained at 4,096 never saw. Rotary models gained only 100 to 200 tokens past their training length in ALiBi's measurement; ALiBi, whose penalty is linear and has nothing to go out of range, is the relative method that extrapolates.
"Turn on YaRN for every request"
Static YaRN slows the low frequencies for short inputs too; Qwen advises enabling it only when long contexts are needed, with the factor set to the length required.
"Weights from one codebase load into another's RoPE"
Interleaved and split-half layouts need the query and key projections permuted; without it the model runs and produces garbage.
"NoPE layers are worse at retrieval"
The old version of this page said so. In Yang et al.'s needle tests the NoPE variant put the most attention on the needle; its weakness was on standard benchmarks, which is why it is used in one layer of four rather than everywhere.

Stretch the spectrum

Extends Reading's Stretching RoPE section: the same methods, every pair at once, on the real configs. Each line shows how much a method slows each rotation pair (θ / θ′, log scale) against the pair's wavelength. A pair at ÷1 keeps its trained speed; a pair at ÷s is fully interpolated.

Try: on Llama 3.1, YaRN and Llama 3 scaling agree at both ends and differ only in where the blend sits (YaRN by turns per pair, from L/32; Llama 3 by wavelength, from L/4). NTK-aware is a straight line on this chart: the base change slows pair i by si/(d/2 − 1). Pick Phi-3: LongRoPE's searched factors rise unevenly, in jumps rather than a smooth curve, and reach ÷64.8 on its slowest pair, twice its factor of 32, while its "short" factors stay near ÷1 to ÷3. Llama 4's config sets low_freq_factor and high_freq_factor both to 1, which collapses the blend into a single step at 8,192 tokens.
