2. Counting a corpus: which tokens, and how many unique

Ask five questions of every card; most mistakes with corpora come from skipping one.

  1. Source: which crawls (by id) and other sources, up to which date. A corpus built from crawls up to 2022 knows nothing of 2024.
  2. Size, in what unit: documents, bytes or tokens, and whose tokenizer; total or unique (below).
  3. Recipe: extraction (WET, or WARC HTML through trafilatura, resiliparse or jusText), language ID, heuristic filters, deduplication and its scope, model-based filter, decontamination, PII, synthetic data.
  4. Licence and access: the card's field, the terms beneath it, and any click-through or approval gate (section 7).
  5. Version and evidence: corpora change after release (FineWeb grew from 15T to 18.5T tokens over five versions and dropped domains after a takedown notice), and the evidence is usually the authors' own ablation (section 5).

Total tokens and unique tokens

FineWeb and DCLM deduplicate within each crawl (or shard), not across crawls, on purpose: FineWeb's ablations found global deduplication kept mostly worse documents ({{Deduplication|n:3f95c17b0d0d8171ac17e43ab5ea276f}} has the 2013-48 experiment). So a popular page captured in many crawls survives many times. NVIDIA estimated unique tokens after a global fuzzy dedup ({{Nemotron-CC Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02595}}, independent of HF and DCLM, vendor for its own row):

Hence its remark that both "contain roughly 80% fuzzy duplicates" (Zyphra's recount of DCLM: 750B unique tokens). A 100B-token run never sees most copies; a 15T-token run sees each popular page many times, which is repetition you did not plan ({{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}} measures its cost).
