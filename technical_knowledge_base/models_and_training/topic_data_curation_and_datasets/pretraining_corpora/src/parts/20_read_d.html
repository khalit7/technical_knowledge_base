3. The lineage: four generations from one crawl

The open corpora form one family tree rooted in Common Crawl. Each generation changed one or two things about turning the crawl into training text, each with an experiment behind it. The tree draws all of them; tap a row for its full card.

Generation 1 (2019 to 2021): rules on one crawl, hand-built mixtures

C4 (Google, October 2019) took one month of WET text (April 2019) and applied a handful of rules (terminal punctuation, at least three sentences, a bad-words list, no curly brackets, one copy of any repeated three-sentence span): 364,868,892 English documents, 156B words by spaCy's count ({{T5 paper, section 2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683}}). Its experiment was the first clean filtering test: filtered C4 beat the 8 times larger unfiltered set on all seven benchmark columns (83.28 against 81.46 GLUE). An independent audit ({{Dodge et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08758}}) found the bad-words rule removed African American English and Hispanic-aligned English at 42% and 32%, against 6.2% for white-aligned English: a filter that looks neutral can select dialects ({{Extraction and filtering|n:3c65c17b0d0d81579b32c07c92b7472b}} owns the rules).

The Pile (EleutherAI, December 2020) took the opposite view, diversity by hand: 22 sources each given a hand-set number of epochs (Wikipedia 3, PubMed 2, Pile-CC 1), 825 GiB in all ({{Pile Table 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00027}}); the hero's first bar. Its legacy is double: mixing domains helps, and a licence lesson, because Books3 (about 200,000 pirated books) was taken down in 2023 after a Danish Rights Alliance notice and sits at the centre of several lawsuits (section 7).

Generation 2 (2022 to 2023): reproduce the closed recipes, then "web only"

First the open world rebuilt closed recipes: RedPajama v1 reproduced LLaMA 1's mix (1.2T tokens), Dolma documented a 3T-token multi-source corpus. Then RefinedWeb (TII, June 2023) made the claim that defined the next generation: web data alone, filtered and deduplicated hard enough (trafilatura on WARC, Gopher rules, MinHash and exact-substring dedup, no learned filter), matches curated mixtures. Read its margins, though: +2.8 points over the Pile at 1B but only +0.5 over C4, and C4 put through RefinedWeb's own pipeline scored above RefinedWeb. The robust lesson is "filter and deduplicate the web well".

Generation 3 (2024): every step ablated, and learned quality filters

FineWeb (Hugging Face, April 2024; same lead author as RefinedWeb) rebuilt that approach in the open {{datatrove|https://github.com/huggingface/datatrove}} library on every crawl and published an ablation per step (1.71B parameters, 28B tokens per test). Three findings changed practice: extract from WARC, not WET; deduplicate each crawl separately, not globally; and drop C4's terminal-punctuation rule (it helped but threw away about 30% of tokens) for three custom rules ({{FineWeb paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17557}}). It now covers 110 crawls (v1.4.0, July 2025), "more than 18.5T" GPT-2 tokens.

Then learned quality filters won. FineWeb-Edu had Llama-3-70B-Instruct grade 460,000 pages for educational value, trained a small regressor on the grades, and kept scores of 3 or more: 8% of FineWeb, 1.3T tokens. DCLM (June 2024), a benchmark for data with fixed models, found that a cheap fastText classifier trained to tell instruction-like text (OpenHermes 2.5, top r/ExplainLikeImFive answers) from ordinary web text, keeping the top 10%, beat every other filter it tried (30.2 Core against 27.5 with no model-based filter, DCLM Table 4; {{Quality classifiers and data selection|n:3f95c17b0d0d810baaabe41da6c9c50f}} has every row and why they work). Nemotron-CC (NVIDIA, December 2024) argued the other side: 10% of the web is fine for a 1T-token run and runs out for 15T. It scored pages with three classifiers into 20 buckets, kept the medium ones, rewrote the low ones with an LLM and turned high ones into question-answer pairs: 4.4T real plus 1.9T synthetic tokens. Its high-quality subset beat DCLM by 5.6 MMLU points (59.0 against 53.4, 8B, 1T tokens; {{Nemotron-CC Table 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02595}}).

Generation 4 (2025 to 2026): labelled pools, synthetic text, PDFs and licences

The newest corpora are pools with labels from which a mix is drawn. Dolma 3 (Ai2, October 2025): a 9.31T-token pool from 104 crawls, deduplicated three ways (exact, MinHash, suffix arrays for repeats of 500 bytes or more) and bucketed by topic and quality; Olmo 3's 5.93T mix up-samples the best buckets and adds 805B tokens of OCR-read science PDFs. Nemotron-CC v2 and v2.1 (August and December 2025) add 2024 to 2025 crawls and several trillion LLM-written tokens, behind a training-only agreement. FinePDFs (September 2025) mines 3T tokens from PDFs; FineWeb2 takes FineWeb's recipe to 1,868 language-script pairs (section 6). At the other end Common Pile v0.1 (June 2025) keeps only openly licensed text and shows how little web that leaves: 15.2B tokens (section 7).

The other corpora of each generation (ROOTS to Essential-Web)

Generation 1. The Pile's evidence was perplexity: a 1.3B model trained on it scored 5.59 WikiText perplexity against 8.27 for CC-100 and 11.75 for raw Common Crawl.

Generation 2. ROOTS (BigScience, 2022) built BLOOM's 1.6 TB in 46 languages with per-language thresholds set by native speakers, a governance model more than a recipe. RedPajama v1 (Together, April 2023) rebuilt LLaMA 1's undisclosed mix from its paper (1.2T tokens); SlimPajama (Cerebras) deduplicated it globally to 627B. Dolma (Ai2, from August 2023) released a documented 3T-token multi-source corpus with its toolkit.

Generation 3. Alongside FineWeb, DCLM and Nemotron-CC: RedPajama v2 (a raw 30T-token pool with 46 quality signals per document), Zyda and Zyda-2 (cross-deduplicated open sets), TxT360 (global dedup keeping each page's duplicate count, so popular pages can be up-weighted). The full Nemotron-CC, built for long runs, scores 0.4 below DCLM on MMLU (53.0) and slightly above on the 10-task average (57.8 against 57.0) at 8B and 1T tokens.

Generation 4. Essential-Web (June 2025) labels 24T tokens on 12 taxonomy dimensions so that a subset is a query.

The Pile's size is often quoted as "300B tokens", which is Pythia's training budget; Pythia's paper gives 334B GPT-NeoX tokens, and the Pile paper gives no total. The closed models of the time published mixture tables without the data: GPT-3 drew 60% of its 300B tokens from filtered Common Crawl (410B available, so 0.44 epochs) and 22% from WebText2 (19B, about 3 epochs); LLaMA 1 drew 67% from Common Crawl through CCNet, 15% from C4, 4.5% each from GitHub, Wikipedia and books. GPT-3's table does not add up internally: weight times 300B divided by tokens gives 3.5 epochs for WebText2 and 3.0 for Wikipedia, against 2.9 and 3.4 printed. ROOTS kept a data card per source.

Generation 3 in detail (FineWeb's versions, DCLM's 7B check)

FineWeb's global-dedup finding: in its 2013-48 study, the 31B tokens kept by global dedup trained a worse model than the 171B tokens it had removed, once those were deduplicated per crawl (sections 3.2 to 3.5 of the paper; the plots have no tables). Its card says "more than 18.5T" tokens, but its per-crawl table sums to 18,306.0B, because the printed total of 18,527.0B counts CC-MAIN-2021-43 twice (two identical rows of 221.0B; checked 2026-10-14). DCLM's "+3.5 Core over conventional choices" is Table 5 at 7B (41.0 against 37.5 for a GPT-3-style classifier) and checks.
