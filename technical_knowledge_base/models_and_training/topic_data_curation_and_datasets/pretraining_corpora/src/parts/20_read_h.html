7. Licences: four layers, read from the bottom

Not legal advice. What the dataset cards and terms say, verbatim, and what courts decided as of 2026-10-14, from published summaries. The governance side (opt-outs, data cards, provenance) is {{Decontamination, PII and data governance|n:3f95c17b0d0d8100b7e4e0f96074c309}}.

"What licence is FineWeb under?" has four answers, one per layer, and a corpus is only as usable as its most restrictive layer. A field on a higher layer cannot remove an obligation from the layer below.

The licence field covers only the third layer

Pick a corpus to fill its four layers from its card (src/data/corpora.json).

  1. The content. Each page, book, paper or file belongs to its author; a crawl does not change that. Some content carries an open licence (Wikipedia's CC BY-SA, a repository's MIT licence, a CC BY paper); most web pages carry none, which means all rights reserved.
  2. The crawler's or archive's terms. Common Crawl's terms of use; Software Heritage's agreement for The Stack v2; the Internet Archive's terms for HPLT.
  3. The dataset licence, covering the compilation (selection, cleaning, metadata). ODC-By 1.0 (FineWeb, C4, Dolma, RefinedWeb) and CC BY 4.0 (DCLM) allow redistribution with attribution; CC0 (HPLT's packaging) waives even that. NVIDIA's Data Agreement for Model Training (Nemotron-CC v2 and later, Nemotron-CC-Math) allows training your own models and forbids redistributing, reselling or relicensing the data. Some fields are wrong: Ultra-FineWeb is tagged Apache 2.0 though it is a filtered subset of ODC-By FineWeb.
  4. The access gate. None (most), a click-through (The Stack, Nemotron-CC-Math, CulturaX), or manual approval (Nemotron-CC v2 and v2.1). Gates attach terms that bind what you do afterwards.

Two corpora mark the extremes. Common Pile v0.1 (June 2025) keeps only public-domain text and licences meeting the Open Definition (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, permissive software licences), checks against "licence laundering" (sites relabelling others' content), and keeps the licence per document: 8 TB in all, but only 15.2B tokens of licence-verified web pages, because almost no web page states an open licence. Its 7B Comma models are, by its authors' account, competitive with LLaMA 1 and 2 7B at similar budgets (vendor). At the other end, Darwin-CC (2026) is tagged CC BY 4.0 but derived from Nemotron-CC: if from v2 or later, NVIDIA's agreement forbids exactly that redistribution; which version it used is not stated.

What the courts add, without a legal opinion: how the data was acquired mattered as much as what was done with it (pirated books cost Anthropic a US$1.5B settlement, while training on lawfully acquired books was held fair use in Bartz v. Anthropic); memorisation of whole works is the risk European courts picked up (GEMA v. OpenAI); and keeping per-document provenance (source, crawl, licence field, date) is what lets you remove a source later.

Where the courts were on 2026-10-14 (six cases, with sources)
CaseDateWhat was decided
Bartz v. Anthropic (N.D. Cal.)2025-06-23; settlement approved 2026-07Training on lawfully acquired books was fair use; building a library from pirated copies (LibGen, PiLiMi) was not. Settled for US$1.5B, about US$3,000 per work for about 482,460 books.
Kadrey v. Meta (N.D. Cal.)2025-06-25Partial summary judgment for Meta on training, because these plaintiffs did not show market harm; the opinion says "market dilution" could succeed in other cases. Claims about torrent seeding continue.
In re OpenAI copyright litigation (S.D.N.Y., includes NYT v. Microsoft and OpenAI)summary judgment briefing from 2026-09-04No fair-use ruling yet; motion to dismiss denied in key part (2025-10-27).
Thomson Reuters v. Ross (3rd Cir.)2026-09-29First US appellate ruling: using Westlaw headnotes to train a non-generative legal search tool was not fair use, including harm to a market for licensing training data. Generative AI expressly not decided.
GEMA v. OpenAI (Munich Regional Court)2025-11-11Memorised song lyrics in model weights and outputs were reproductions; the EU text-and-data-mining exception covers building a corpus, not memorisation in the model.
Getty Images v. Stability AI (UK High Court)2025-11-04; appeal pendingModel weights that do not store the works are not an "infringing copy"; Getty won only narrow trade-mark points.
