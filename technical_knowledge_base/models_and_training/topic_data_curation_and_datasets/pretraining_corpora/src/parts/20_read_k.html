9. What to use, by budget and goal (October 2026)

There is no best corpus, only a best corpus for a budget, a goal and a licence. Four questions decide most cases, and the {{What to train on|#t-pcpick}} tab turns them into a starting mix with an epochs check.

SituationStart fromWhy, and what to watch
Small model, up to about 1B parameters, budget up to a few hundred billion tokensFineWeb-Edu, or a 60/40 FineWeb-Edu / DCLM-Baseline mix; 5 to 10% Stack-Edu, a few % FineMathThe 1.3T of FineWeb-Edu is never exhausted at this size, so the strictest filter is affordable. The 60/40 mix is what SmolLM2 chose after finding FineWeb-Edu alone strong on knowledge tests and weaker on commonsense (Table 1 of its paper; section 5 here). (The old page said nanochat and the nanoGPT speed runs "standardized" on FineWeb-Edu: at nanochat commit 92d63d4, nanochat/dataset.py lines 23 and 36 show it switched from FineWeb-Edu-100B to NVIDIA's ClimbMix-400B, and modded-nanogpt at 4ea6b93 downloads FineWeb 10B, not FineWeb-Edu, in data/cached_fineweb10B.py line 9.)

DCLM is about 80% fuzzy duplicates, so at 3T tokens you are already repeating popular pages.

Correction to the old page's 150M to 350M recipe
The old page recommended "FineWeb-Edu (the 1.3T score>=3 cut)" for a 150M to 350M model, said SmolLM2-135M and 360M trained on 2T and 4T tokens (correct: SmolLM2 paper), and that "SmolLM2 converged to" a 50/50 FineWeb-Edu and DCLM mix. SmolLM2's paper chose 60/40 FineWeb-Edu to DCLM for its first stage and moved to 40/60 in stage 3 (Table 1 and section 4); its Chinchilla figure ("only about 3 to 7B") is 20 tokens per parameter, so 3B tokens at 150M and 7B at 350M, as stated. The rest of that recipe holds: you will not exhaust FineWeb-Edu; add 5 to 10% code (Stack-Edu) and about 5% maths (FineMath 4+), weighted towards the end; decontaminate against your evaluation suite before training (see {{Decontamination, PII and data governance|n:3f95c17b0d0d8100b7e4e0f96074c309}}); train the tokenizer on the same distribution ({{Tokenizers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817187bcd94a1b0f15ea}}).

Whatever you choose, record the corpus version and revision hash, the files or configs you sampled, and the tokenizer you counted with: the second run of a project is usually a comparison with the first, and corpora change under their names (FineWeb v1.0 to v1.4, The Stack's opt-out refreshes, the redacted Olmo 3 PDFs).
