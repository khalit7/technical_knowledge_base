6. Beside the web: code, maths, papers and books, other languages

Modern open recipes add code (about 7 to 24% of tokens), maths (about 3 to 14%), scientific text and other languages to the web, often weighted up near the end of training (the hero shows stage-1 shares; {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}} owns how much and when). Each has its own corpora, extraction problems and licence questions.

Code and maths. Code licences follow the file: The Stack v1 kept only permissively licensed repositories (excluding the roughly 81% with no detectable licence); The Stack v2 keeps permissive and unlicensed files and drops copyleft and commercial ones ({{StarCoder2 paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19173}}); Stack-Edu applied the FineWeb-Edu idea per language and lifted MultiPL-E Python from 20.7 to 25.6 in a 1.7B annealing test. Maths corpora improved by better extraction: at 100B tokens of mid-training on an 8B model, Nemotron-CC-Math-4+ (pages re-rendered and rewritten into clean LaTeX by an LLM) scored 40.60 on MATH against 35.80 for FineMath-4+ and 29.20 for OpenWebMath (vendor; table below).

Papers, PDFs and books. {{FinePDFs|https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceFW/finepdfs}} (September 2025) extracted 2.9T tokens from PDFs in 105 crawls, routing each between a text-layer extractor and an OCR model, and recommends keeping PDFs under a quarter of a mix; Olmo 3 put 805B tokens of OCR-read science PDFs into its mix (13.6%). Books3 (pirated, in the Pile and LLaMA 1) is gone and is the centre of the litigation in section 7.

Other languages. English is 41.86% of CC-MAIN-2026-39's pages by Common Crawl's own {{language statistics|https://commoncrawl.github.io/cc-crawl-statistics/plots/languages}} (Russian 6.7%, German 5.7%, Japanese 5.2%, Spanish 4.7%, French 4.7%); the often-quoted "45%" is CulturaX's English share of tokens, a different measure. FineWeb2 (Hugging Face, December 2024) counts words, not model tokens, because no single tokenizer is fair across scripts. Its French ablation shows what helps outside English: language ID alone 18.3, plus deduplication 18.0 (no gain), plus per-language filters 21.9, plus "rehydration" (re-adding documents in proportion to how often they were duplicated) 23.6 ({{FineWeb2 paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20920}}, Table 30). For 70% of its language-script pairs, more than half the documents come from Bible- or Wikipedia-related domains.

Per-domain detail (The Stack's sizes, the maths table, papers and books, the multilingual lineage, Wikipedia)

Code in full. The Stack v1 kept only files whose repository carried a permissive licence (excluding the roughly 81% of repositories with no detectable one), deduplicated near-exactly (about 40% were duplicates) and introduced an opt-out. The Stack v2 moved to the Software Heritage archive and changed the rule: permissive and unlicensed files kept, copyleft and commercial dropped ({{StarCoder2 paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19173}}). Stack-Edu applied the FineWeb-Edu idea to code with one classifier per language and lifted MultiPL-E pass rates by 4 to 8 points in a 1.7B annealing test (Python 20.7 to 25.6).

Maths in full. The lineage is better recall and better extraction: OpenWebMath wrote a maths-aware extractor; DeepSeekMath (2024, not released) iterated a fastText classifier four times to 120B tokens; FineMath re-extracted FineWeb's candidates from WARC and kept LLM-graded pages; Nemotron-CC-Math re-rendered pages with a text browser and had an LLM rewrite them into clean LaTeX.

The Stack v2: its "900B tokens" is StarCoder2-15B's training set with extras such as pull requests, notebooks and arXiv, not the archive's size. Its content is not on Hugging Face: the dataset lists file ids, and the files come from Software Heritage's S3 bucket under an agreement. It removed files containing HumanEval, MBPP and APPS problems. Stack-Edu's card and paper say 125B tokens while its own per-language table sums to 156.8B.

The deltas in the prose recompute; its headline "+12.6 on MATH" is against the weakest baseline at 300B tokens (MegaMath-Web), and +9.6 against FineMath-3+. Independently, at 2.3B ({{Essential-Web Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14111}}), FineMath 3+ beats OpenWebMath on GSM8K (26.4 against 14.6). At a fixed token budget the small corpora repeat more (100B tokens is about seven epochs of OpenWebMath), so part of each gap is repetition. MegaMath (2025) reached its 371.6B with synthetic data; OpenWebMath (2023) filtered all of Common Crawl; DeepSeekMath seeded its classifier with OpenWebMath and added whole domains with many maths pages.

Papers. peS2o (Ai2) cleaned 39M open-access papers and abstracts from Semantic Scholar (42B whitespace words, not model tokens). arXiv LaTeX sources appear in RedPajama, Proof-Pile-2 and Dolma, but arXiv's default licence does not grant reuse to third parties, so only a minority of arXiv full texts is openly licensed. Olmo 3 later redacted some PDFs ("[REMOVED]" in the 7B mix). Books. PG-19 is Gutenberg books before 1919; Common Pile adds pre-1929 books and Library of Congress scans. Institutional Books 1.0 (Harvard, 2025, 983,004 public-domain volumes) shows the difference between public-domain text and release terms: its early-access terms forbid commercial use, including training, without an agreement.

Multilingual lineage. mC4 (2020, C4's rules in 101 languages), CC-100 (XLM-R, 2019), OSCAR (access suspended as of October 2026), CulturaX (2023, mC4 plus OSCAR cleaned), MADLAD-400 (Google, 419 languages, audited per language; its card's licence field says ODC-By while its text says CC BY 4.0), HPLT v2 and v3 (EU, adding Internet Archive crawls). Wikipedia is in nearly every mix and small (Olmo 3 uses 2.51B tokens of English Wikipedia); it is CC BY-SA, share-alike, so a redistributed processed copy must stay CC BY-SA, which is why mixes bundling it under ODC-By still say the source licence applies.
