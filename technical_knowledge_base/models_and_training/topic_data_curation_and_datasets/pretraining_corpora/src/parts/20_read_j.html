8. Measured here: @@ms_n@@ corpus samples on the same metrics

Published comparisons train models; this page cannot. What it can do on a laptop CPU is open the corpora and measure the same things in each: how long the documents are, how many characters a token holds, what language the language identifier says they are in, which domains they come from, how many near-duplicates a sample contains, what share of each corpus would pass other corpora's heuristic rules, and how the FineWeb-Edu classifier scores them. The table is the summary; the {{Corpus comparer|#t-pccmp}} tab has the distributions, the domains, the failing rules and short excerpts.

measured here on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), 2026-10-14. Samples: the first documents of one file per corpus, in file order, except FineWeb and FineWeb-Edu, which are every document they kept from the running example's WARC file (repositories, revisions and files in src/data/measured.json). Language: fastText lid.176 (datatrove's copy). Rules: datatrove 0.10.1 filters with their default settings. Classifier: the FineWeb-Edu classifier on up to 300 documents per sample (all of FineWeb's and FineWeb-Edu's). What transfers: the rule pass rates, token ratios and classifier scores are deterministic properties of these documents. What does not: a first-N sample of one file is not a random sample of a corpus (files are ordered by crawl, language or id), so treat differences of a few points as noise.

What the numbers say, read with that caveat:

  • Rules are not neutral across kinds of text. Gopher's quality rules, written for English web prose, pass @@ms_fw_gq@@ of FineWeb but @@ms_owm_gq@@ of OpenWebMath, @@ms_fm_gq@@ of FineMath-4+ and @@ms_fra_gq@@ of FineWeb2 French (its stop-word rule expects English words). Apply a web filter to maths, code or another language and it removes most of it; that is why FineWeb2 re-tuned thresholds per language and why maths corpora run their own pipelines.
  • Re-running a corpus's own rules on it is a consistency check. FineWeb's documents pass the C4 and FineWeb rules at @@ms_fw_c4@@ and @@ms_fw_fq@@, and Gopher quality at @@ms_fw_gq@@: the gap is worth chasing (a later text change, such as e-mail anonymisation, or a different library version) before trusting a reimplementation. Nemotron-CC's high bucket passes Gopher quality at only @@ms_nh_gq@@, as its paper says: heuristic filters were applied only to the non-high-quality buckets.
  • datatrove's C4 filter is not the T5 paper's C4 filter. C4's own documents pass datatrove's C4QualityFilter at only @@ms_c4_c4@@, because its default min_num_sentences is 5 (datatrove 0.10.1, pipeline/filters/c4_filters.py line 66), while the T5 paper and TensorFlow Datasets' code use 3. Defaults in a library are choices; check them against the paper you think you are reproducing.
  • The classifier tracks what it was trained for. Mean FineWeb-Edu score: @@ms_edu_means@@. Wikipedia and maths score high (higher than everything but FineWeb-Edu itself), paper abstracts sit in the middle, and PDFs score as low as the general web: "educational" here means explanatory prose for a school-level reader, not technical depth. Nemotron-CC's buckets order exactly as their names say, from @@ms_nh_edu@@ (high) to @@ms_nl_edu@@ (low), although NVIDIA did not use this classifier.
