7. Licences: four layers, read from the bottom

Not legal advice. This section reports what the dataset cards and terms say, verbatim, and what courts have decided as of 2026-10-14, from published summaries. Licence questions for a real model need a lawyer; the governance side (opt-outs, data cards, provenance) is {{Decontamination, PII and data governance|n:3f95c17b0d0d8100b7e4e0f96074c309}}.

"What licence is FineWeb under?" has four answers, one per layer, and a corpus is only as usable as its most restrictive layer. Pick a corpus to fill the layers from its card:

  1. The content. Each web page, book, paper or file belongs to its author. A crawl or a dataset does not change that; Common Crawl's terms say so explicitly. Some content comes with an open licence of its own (Wikipedia's CC BY-SA, a repository's MIT licence, a CC BY paper); most web pages come with none, which means all rights reserved.
  2. The crawler's or archive's terms. Common Crawl's terms of use (access to the service; respect third-party rights); Software Heritage's agreement for The Stack v2 content; the Internet Archive's terms for HPLT.
  3. The dataset licence. What the card's licence field covers is the compilation: the selection, cleaning and metadata. ODC-By 1.0 (FineWeb, C4, Dolma, RefinedWeb) and CC BY 4.0 (DCLM) let you redistribute the compilation with attribution; CC0 (HPLT's packaging) waives even that. NVIDIA's Data Agreement for Model Training (Nemotron-CC v2 and later, Nemotron-CC-Math) allows training your own models and forbids redistributing, reselling or relicensing the data. Some fields are wrong: Ultra-FineWeb is tagged Apache 2.0 though it is a filtered subset of ODC-By FineWeb, and MADLAD-400's field (ODC-By) disagrees with its card text (CC BY 4.0). A field cannot remove an obligation from the layer below.
  4. The access gate. Not gated (most), a click-through agreement (The Stack, Nemotron-CC-Math, CulturaX), or manual approval (Nemotron-CC v2 and v2.1). A gate is where extra terms are attached; read them before the download, because they bind what you do afterwards.

Two corpora show the extremes. Common Pile v0.1 (June 2025) keeps only public-domain text and text under licences meeting the Open Definition (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, permissive software licences), checks against "licence laundering" (sites that relabel others' content), and keeps the licence per document: 8 TB in all, but only 15.2B tokens of licence-verified web pages in the Comma training mix, because almost no web page states an open licence. Its 7B Comma models are, by its authors' account, competitive with LLaMA 1 and 2 7B at similar budgets (vendor). At the other end, a corpus like Darwin-CC (2026) is tagged CC BY 4.0 but derived from Nemotron-CC: if the source is v2 or later, NVIDIA's agreement forbids exactly that redistribution; which version it used is not stated.

Where the courts were on 2026-10-14

CaseDateWhat was decided
Bartz v. Anthropic (N.D. Cal.)2025-06-23; settlement approved 2026-07Training on lawfully acquired books was fair use; building a library from pirated copies (LibGen, PiLiMi) was not. Settled for US$1.5B, about US$3,000 per work for about 482,460 books.
Kadrey v. Meta (N.D. Cal.)2025-06-25Partial summary judgment for Meta on training, because these plaintiffs did not show market harm; the opinion says "market dilution" could succeed in other cases. Claims about torrent seeding continue.
In re OpenAI copyright litigation (S.D.N.Y., includes NYT v. Microsoft and OpenAI)summary judgment briefing from 2026-09-04No fair-use ruling yet; motion to dismiss denied in key part (2025-10-27).
Thomson Reuters v. Ross (3rd Cir.)2026-09-29First US appellate ruling: using Westlaw headnotes to train a non-generative legal search tool was not fair use, including harm to a market for licensing training data. Generative AI expressly not decided.
GEMA v. OpenAI (Munich Regional Court)2025-11-11Memorised song lyrics in model weights and outputs were reproductions; the EU text-and-data-mining exception covers building a corpus, not memorisation in the model.
Getty Images v. Stability AI (UK High Court)2025-11-04; appeal pendingModel weights that do not store the works are not an "infringing copy"; Getty won only narrow trade-mark points.
