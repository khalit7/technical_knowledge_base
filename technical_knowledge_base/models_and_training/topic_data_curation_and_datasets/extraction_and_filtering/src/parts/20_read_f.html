7. The heuristic rules, and what each one drops

The problem. After language ID most English pages are still not text worth training on: shop listings, link lists, login pages, spam, truncated teasers. Classifiers can judge this ({{Quality classifiers and data selection|n:3f95c17b0d0d810baaabe41da6c9c50f}}) but cost more and encode someone's taste, so cheap rules come first. Three families are in every modern English pipeline, in this order in FineWeb and in run0:

  • Gopher repetition (Rae et al. 2021, Appendix A, Table A1): the share of duplicated lines and paragraphs, of characters in them, of characters in the most frequent 2-, 3- and 4-gram, and in all repeated 5- to 10-grams; repetition "is often linked with uninformative content".
  • Gopher quality: 50 to 100,000 words, mean word length 3 to 10 characters, hash and ellipsis symbols at most 0.1 per word, at most 90% of lines starting with a bullet and at most 30% ending with an ellipsis, at least 80% of words containing a letter, and at least two of eight English stop words ("the", "be", "to", "of", "and", "that", "have", "with").
  • C4 (Raffel et al. 2020, section 2.2): keep only lines ending in terminal punctuation and with at least 5 words; drop pages with fewer than 3 sentences, any bad word, "lorem ipsum" or a curly bracket; drop lines mentioning JavaScript. FineWeb found the terminal-punctuation rule the strongest single C4 rule but too costly (about 30% of tokens) and left it off.
  • FineWeb's own (Penedo et al. 2024, section 3.6): drop a document when at most 12% of its lines end in punctuation, when at least 10% of its characters are in duplicated lines, or when at least 67% of its lines are 30 characters or shorter. Together about 22% of tokens removed and about 1% better aggregate score published.

How to check a rule: read what it drops. For every rule this page labelled up to 20 of run0's drops (seed 1) on top of the Pipeline lab's 8 (seed 0), plus 40 kept documents: R right drop, W wrong drop (coherent, informative text worth training on), U unclear hand-labelled, 495 new labels in all. Predict first: which rule wrongly drops the most good text, the stop-word rule, the bullet-line rule or the short-document rule?

Most rules drop junk; three misfire on formats (lists, code, a repeated heading), not on quality

The bar is the wrong-drop share with its 95% Wilson interval; the samples are small, so read the intervals, not the bars.

  • Safe: too few words, too few stop words, top 2-, 3- and 4-gram, lines ending in an ellipsis. Their drops are near-empty pages (login forms, "JavaScript required", empty carts), menus and lists of teasers: zero or one wrong drop in 28 each.
  • Misfiring on formats: the bullet-line rule counts any line starting with "-", and trafilatura writes every list item as "- item", so an article written as a list goes (8 of 27 wrong). The curly-bracket rule removes any page with a code sample (6 of 13). The FineWeb duplicated-line rule at datatrove's 0.01 removes articles whose only repetition is a heading or a date printed twice (10 of 28).
  • Kept is not clean: of 64 kept documents labelled across both samples, 17 are junk the rules missed (product pages, a sales page, spun SEO text, an Italian page that passed English language ID). The rules remove the worst; a classifier is still needed.

The {{Rule workbench|#t-rules}} moves every threshold at once and counts survivors, FineWeb overlap and labelled good documents lost; the {{Label browser|#t-labels}} shows every labelled document.

How FineWeb found its three rules, and two traps in labelling (provenance)

Gopher chose simple rules "to remove low-quality data while minimising potential for bias"; its repetition rules exist because repetitive training data may make a model repeat itself. FineWeb found its own by comparing histograms of many statistics on two versions of the 2013-48 crawl, the better-scoring per-crawl deduplicated one and the worse globally deduplicated one, putting thresholds where the worse version was denser (16 candidates), then ablating each with 28-billion-token runs on the 2019-18 crawl and keeping the three that helped most (section 3.6 and Figure 8). The paper's 0.1 for duplicated-line characters replaced MassiveText's 0.2. In datatrove the bullet test is gopher_quality_filter.py:101-105.

Reading the first lines can mislead the labeller too. Most line-punctuation drops look like articles in their first 200 characters but are one or two teaser lines cut mid-sentence by the site; the one real false drop found was an essay whose author hard-wrapped every line, so no line ended in punctuation. Look at the structure (line count, line lengths, where lines end) before deciding.

Where the C4 swap may come from (provenance)
The T5 paper and Google's own code say 3 sentences and 5 words per line (tensorflow_datasets/text/c4_utils.py:41-42, saved in this page's src/inputs/). Dodge et al.'s documentation of C4 (arXiv 2104.08758, section 2) describes it as "fewer than three words" and "less than five sentences", the same swap datatrove's docstring and defaults carry (c4_filters.py:31, 66-67). Whatever the history, an English C4 reproduction with datatrove's defaults is not C4.
