3. Measured: the same pages through six extractors

The experiment. The 20,280 pages that pass the URL filter, extracted six ways, then exactly the same filters on every result (run0's chain: lid.176 English above 0.65, Gopher repetition and quality, C4 without terminal punctuation, FineWeb's three rules). The chain on trafilatura's text reproduces run0 exactly: 2,692 kept, 2,404 of them in FineWeb, which kept 2,425 from this file measured.

WET has the most text and keeps the fewest documents; jusText keeps the most

Why. WET's text is three times trafilatura's, but its menus and widgets make the repetition and line rules fire. jusText with English stop words returns nothing for most non-English pages (a second language filter) yet keeps the most English documents, because it keeps short paragraphs that trafilatura's precision mode discards; only 1,946 of its 3,231 are documents FineWeb kept. Nemotron-CC saw the same at scale: jusText plus their filters gave 1,380 billion tokens against trafilatura's 994 billion on 13 snapshots (Table 1, arXiv 2412.02595 published).

What it does downstream published. DCLM (Table 3, 1B parameters) scored 24.1 Core with resiliparse, 24.5 with trafilatura and 20.7 with WET, and chose resiliparse for speed. FineWeb's 1.71-billion-parameter, 28-billion-token runs put trafilatura-on-WARC ahead of WET throughout (section 3.2). So the big step is WARC instead of WET; among main-content extractors the difference is small, and the choice is about cost, recall, and what happens to code, tables and maths (section 5).

4. Why extractors fail, and a cache that makes them order-dependent

Most failures are a class name or a layout habit; three real pages from the running example show the pattern, and the {{Extraction comparer|#t-ext}} puts any page through any two extractors side by side.

Three pages, three failures: a calendar widget, a layout table, a "dropped" data table (case studies)
The calendar widget. An advocacy site's sidebar holds a WordPress calendar (<table id="wp-calendar">, one cell per day). WET keeps every cell: "M", "T", "W", "1", "2" ... as lines of text. trafilatura and resiliparse both drop it, for the same reason: an ancestor's class or id contains "sidebar" (trafilatura's OVERALL_DISCARD_XPATH, xpaths.py:105-130; resiliparse's sidebar_cls_regex, html2text.pyx:476, matches the list's class "sidebar_widgets").
Menu lines ending in "|". A university weather page is laid out as one HTML <table>: a menu cell and a content cell. trafilatura keeps tables and writes each cell followed by " | " and each row on its own line (xml.py:320-343), so the whole menu ("Animate: ON | OFF Switch Channel: Visible True Color ...") becomes one long line ending in "|". resiliparse writes the same cells as separate lines. Layout tables are a 1990s habit, still common on university and government sites.
The "dropped" data table. A railway reference page's mileage list is not a table at all: it is a <pre> block. Both trafilatura and resiliparse keep its two data lines; what differs is that resiliparse also keeps the heading and the "Last update" line, and only resiliparse keeps the tab between mileage and place name. Correction to the Pipeline lab's caption ("resiliparse drops the data"): neither drops it; its own line masks show both keeping lines 20 and 21 measured.

The cache. datatrove's Trafilatura step calls extract(..., deduplicate=True) by default. That switches on a process-wide cache of 4,096 text segments: every paragraph over 100 characters is counted, and from its fourth sighting on it is dropped, as is a whole document whose body was seen before. It is a cheap within-site boilerplate remover (a disclaimer on every page of a site disappears after three pages), but the output now depends on which pages the worker saw before, in what order:

The same pages, the same code: the cache empties 310 pages, and page order changes more

On a cluster each task has its own cache, and datatrove restarts the sandbox after a timeout, which empties it; so a page's text depends on the task it lands in and on how loaded the node was. Small in aggregate (1.25% of characters here), but it breaks reproducibility and is a near-duplicate filter nobody configured: pass deduplicate=False and deduplicate later, on purpose ({{Deduplication|n:3f95c17b0d0d8171ac17e43ab5ea276f}}).
