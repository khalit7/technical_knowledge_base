9. Line-level edits against document-level decisions

The problem. Many pages are a good paragraph surrounded by debris the extractor let through: "Share", "3 likes", "Read more", a date, an address block. A document-level rule keeps or drops the whole page; a line-level rule deletes the debris and keeps the rest, but can leave fragments, and changes the statistics every later rule sees (in datatrove the FineWeb rules run on the text C4 already edited, c4_filters.py:135). Three schemes on the 6,971 English documents:

  • C4 as FineWeb runs it (no terminal-punctuation rule): delete lines of fewer than 3 words, lines mentioning JavaScript, cookie and terms-of-use lines. It deleted 71,012 of 193,197 lines (37%) but only 5.1% of characters, and edited 1,414 of the 2,692 documents that survive every rule measured.
  • C4 as C4 ran it: also delete every line not ending in ".", "!", "?" or a quote: 34.6% of all characters, close to the 30% of tokens FineWeb reports for this rule measured.
  • RefinedWeb's line-wise corrections (App. G.2): delete lines that are mostly upper case, only digits, a counter ("3 likes"), one word, or a short call to action ("sign in", "read more"), and drop the document if they hold more than 5% of its words. A reconstruction (RefinedWeb did not release this code): it would drop 1,526 of the 6,971 documents and 152 of the 2,692 our chain keeps reconstruction.

One page, three schemes: kept as a fragment, dropped by a swapped default, dropped for its flagged lines

An environmental organisation's volunteer event page from the running example, in FineWeb (the place names are a public reserve). Line decisions computed by this page's re-implementations, checked against datatrove for C4 measured.

FineWeb's C4 step keeps this page but turns "LOCATION / 176 Bells Pocket Road / Strathpine / QLD 4500" into a lone "176 Bells Pocket Road"; C4's terminal-punctuation rule cuts it to three sentences, which datatrove's default minimum of five rejects (C4's own minimum of three would keep it); the RefinedWeb-style rule throws it away. Each scheme trades keeping text against keeping it coherent: check line-level rules by reading edited documents, not only dropped ones.

How the line counts were checked (method)

A re-implementation of C4's line rules that records each line's reason matches datatrove's output on 6,971 of 6,971 documents. The RefinedWeb version is built from the paper's description with guessed pattern lists, so its counts show the mechanism, not RefinedWeb's numbers.

10. URL and domain filters

The problem. Some sites should never enter a corpus (adult content, malware, spam farms, file hosts), and the cheapest place to stop them is before extraction, from the URL alone. RefinedWeb filtered adult content only by URL, arguing that classifiers and word lists over content "overfilter" minority and medical text (App. G.1) published.

How it works. RefinedWeb aggregated about 4.6 million domains from ten categories of the UT1 blacklists (Université Toulouse 1 Capitole's lists for school proxies), removed fewer than 100 benign but prolific domains by hand, and added three word lists scored on the URL itself: "strict" subwords banned anywhere, "hard" whole words, and "soft" words needing two hits. datatrove ships this design as URLFilter, checking the registered domain, the full host, the exact URL, then the word lists published.

datatrove's blocklist is almost entirely UT1's adult category, frozen in 2023

  • Where it comes from. Of its 4,558,940 domains, 4,536,294 (99.5%) are in today's UT1 adult category and 21,839 in phishing (the categories overlap); only 390 are in no current UT1 category measured. It is RefinedWeb's list, frozen.
  • It ages. UT1's adult category, downloaded on 2026-10-15, has 62,986 domains datatrove's copy lacks. On our 19,424 pages a fresh list with RefinedWeb's categories would remove 42 more (7 adult, 13 gambling, 8 dating, 6 file hosting, 4 phishing, 4 mixed adult) measured.
  • Words in URLs are evidence, not proof. The root's Pipeline lab read the 77 word-based drops on this file: 10 were ordinary pages whose URL shared a word with the lists.
RefinedWeb's categories and curation, and a case bug in datatrove (provenance; file:line)

Categories (RefinedWeb Table 13): adult 4,516,478 entries, phishing 42,445, dating, gambling, file hosting, ddos, "agressif", chat, mixed adult, French regulated gambling. The benign domains were found by inspecting every URL matched more than 4,000 times in 832 million pages (6.04 million, 0.73%, matched); the word lists are App. G.1.2; Welbl et al. 2021 and Dodge et al. 2021 are the papers cited for over-filtering. datatrove: pipeline/filters/url_filter.py:33-132, lists inside the package. One quirk: for the hard and soft lists the URL is split into words without lower-casing (url_filter.py:120) while the lists are stored lower case (line 21), so an upper-case banned word in a URL path escapes them; only the subword check lower-cases the URL (line 128).

11. Toxicity and adult content

The problem. A URL list misses adult or hateful text on ordinary domains (forums, comments, fan fiction). Three approaches are in use, each with a measured cost:

  • A word list over the content. C4 dropped any page containing a word from the "List of Dirty, Naughty, Obscene, and Otherwise Bad Words" (LDNOOBW; 403 English entries today). Dodge et al. (2021) showed the cost: documents classified as African-American English and Hispanic-aligned English were removed at 42% and 32%, against 6.2% for White-aligned English, and documents mentioning LGBTQ+ identities far more often than average (sections 5.2 and 5.3, arXiv 2104.08758) published.
  • A classifier over sentences. Dolma trained two fastText classifiers on the Jigsaw Toxic Comments data ("hate" and "NSFW") and removed every sentence scoring above a threshold; the stricter τ = 0.0004 scored better downstream than τ = 0.4, but Dolma kept 0.4 to preserve scale published.
  • External signals. Gopher removed pages failing Google's SafeSearch, instead of word lists because of Dodge et al.'s finding; FineWeb filters adult content by URL only published.
