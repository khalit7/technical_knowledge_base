9. Line-level edits against document-level decisions

The problem. Many pages are a good paragraph surrounded by debris the extractor let through: "Share", "3 likes", "Read more", a date, an address block. A document-level rule keeps or drops the whole page; a line-level rule deletes the debris and keeps the rest. Line edits rescue text but can leave fragments, and they change the statistics every later rule sees (in datatrove the FineWeb rules run on the text C4 already edited, c4_filters.py:135).

Three line-level schemes, all measured on the 6,971 English documents:

  • C4 as FineWeb runs it (no terminal-punctuation rule): delete lines of fewer than 3 words, lines mentioning JavaScript, lines with cookie or terms-of-use phrases. It deleted 71,012 of 193,197 lines (37%) but only 5.1% of characters, and edited 1,414 of the 2,692 documents that survive every rule measured; a re-implementation that records each line's reason matches datatrove's output on 6,971 of 6,971 documents.
  • C4 as C4 ran it: also delete every line that does not end in ".", "!", "?" or a quote. That deletes 34.6% of all characters, close to the 30% of tokens FineWeb reports for this rule measured.
  • RefinedWeb's line-wise corrections (App. G.2): delete lines that are mostly upper case, only digits, a counter ("3 likes"), one word, or a short call to action ("sign in", "read more", "items in cart"), and drop the document if those lines hold more than 5% of its words. RefinedWeb did not release this code; this page's version is a reconstruction from the paper's description with guessed pattern lists, so its counts show the mechanism, not RefinedWeb's numbers: it would drop 1,526 of the 6,971 documents and 152 of the 2,692 our chain keeps reconstruction.

An environmental organisation's volunteer event page (the place names are a public reserve). Line decisions computed by this page's re-implementations, checked against datatrove for C4 measured.

The lesson. FineWeb's C4 step keeps this page but turns "LOCATION / 176 Bells Pocket Road / Strathpine / QLD 4500" into a lone "176 Bells Pocket Road"; C4's terminal-punctuation rule cuts it to three sentences, which datatrove's default minimum of five rejects (C4's own minimum of three would keep it); the RefinedWeb-style rule throws it away for having too many flagged lines. None of the three is wrong in general; each makes a different trade between keeping text and keeping text coherent. Check line-level rules by reading edited documents, not only dropped ones.

10. URL and domain filters

The problem. Some sites should never be fetched into a corpus (adult content, malware, spam farms, file hosts), and the cheapest place to stop them is before extraction, from the URL alone. RefinedWeb chose to filter adult content only by URL, arguing that classifiers and word lists over content "overfilter" minority and medical text (App. G.1, citing Welbl et al. 2021 and Dodge et al. 2021) published.

How it works. RefinedWeb aggregated about 4.6 million domains from the UT1 blacklists of the Université Toulouse 1 Capitole (a list maintained for school and university proxies), choosing ten categories (adult 4,516,478 entries, phishing 42,445, dating, gambling, file hosting, ddos, "agressif", chat, mixed adult, French regulated gambling: Table 13), then removed fewer than 100 benign but prolific domains found by inspecting every URL matched more than 4,000 times in 832 million pages (6.04 million, 0.73%, matched). Beside the list, three word lists score the URL itself: "strict" subwords banned anywhere (even split across dots and dashes), "hard" whole words, and "soft" words needing two hits (App. G.1.2). datatrove ships this design: URLFilter (pipeline/filters/url_filter.py:33-132) checks the registered domain, the full host, the exact URL, then the three word lists, with the lists inside the package published.

  • Where datatrove's list comes from. Of its 4,558,940 domains, 4,536,294 (99.5%) are in today's UT1 adult category and 21,839 in phishing (the categories overlap); only 390 are in no current UT1 category measured. It is RefinedWeb's list, frozen.
  • It ages. UT1's adult category, downloaded on 2026-10-15, has 62,986 domains datatrove's copy lacks. On our 19,424 pages a fresh list with RefinedWeb's categories would remove 42 more (7 adult, 13 gambling, 8 dating, 6 file hosting, 4 phishing, 4 mixed adult) measured.
  • Words in URLs are evidence, not proof. The root's Pipeline lab read the 77 word-based drops on this file: 10 were ordinary pages whose URL shared a word with the lists. One quirk in the code: for the hard and soft lists the URL is split into words without lower-casing (url_filter.py:120) while the lists are stored lower case (line 21), so an upper-case banned word in a URL path escapes them; only the subword check lower-cases the URL (line 128).
