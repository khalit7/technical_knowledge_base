7. Checklist for a CPT run

  1. Define target and guard metrics before training: domain evaluations, general evaluations and safety. Evaluate the base model on all of them first; that is the line forgetting is measured against.
  2. Pilot runs to pick the mixture ratio (CMR- or D-CPT-style: a few short runs, fit, predict) and the peak learning rate. Start the pilots well below the pretraining peak and include one near it, since the published sweeps (0.5× to 2×) trade adaptation against forgetting; use a WSD-style schedule if more stages will follow.
  3. Replay source as close to the original pretraining data as you can get; deduplicate the domain data against your evaluations; compute each source's epochs (section 1).
  4. Monitor both loss families during training, general and domain. Forgetting shows early: in the toy and in Ibrahim's runs the general loss jumps in the first few percent of steps, and "relative differences between [replay fractions] appear very early" (§2).
  5. Post-train afterwards, and compare against a no-CPT baseline fine-tuned the same way, to prove CPT was worth it.

Depth: common mistakes and interview rehearsal

Common mistakes (12, mixing and CPT)
Setting weights without computing epochs
A 10% weight on a 20B-token domain in a 2T-token run is 10 epochs. Compute w·D/U for every domain before training; above about 4, expect diminishing value, above about 16 near zero (section 2).
Copying a published mixture or mixture ratio
Weights depend on the domain definitions, the tokenizer, the model size and the budget: DoReMi's Pile weights changed from 60.6% Pile-CC (280M proxy, 256k vocabulary) to 13.8% (120M, 50k vocabulary), and AutoScale shows optima shifting with scale. In CPT, CMR's 30 to 48% and D-CPT's 92% domain share are both "optimal" under different tolerances, domains and budgets. Use a published mix as a starting point and fit your own on pilots.
Comparing against a strawman baseline
"Beats the Pile's default weights" meant beating a document-count weighting (section 3a), and "Pile-CC only" tied DoReMi in RegMix's own Table 4. Always include stratified sampling, natural shares and the single best domain among your baselines.
Optimising the loss of one domain and calling it quality
RegMix optimised Pile-CC validation loss because it correlated best with its downstream tasks; on other tasks (code, maths) a different target would win. Choose the target from the evaluation you care about, and check the correlation on your own runs.
Crediting the anneal's data for the learning-rate decay
A decay stage changes the data and the learning rate at once. OLMo 2's ablation found most of the knowledge gain came from the decay alone; only the maths gain was the data (Pretraining, section 5). Run the decay on the unchanged mix as a control.
Letting the loader change the epochs
Interleaving with an "all exhausted" strategy repeats small streams; restarting without the sampler's state replays or skips data. Log achieved token counts per domain, not just the configured weights.
Testing a mixture on a single seed
Small proxy runs are noisy: Aioli's differences are tenths of a perplexity point. Several seeds per mixture, or many mixtures and a regression (RegMix's answer), before trusting a ranking.
Continuing the chat model instead of the base
Instruction following erodes within a billion tokens (Jindal et al.: up to 10 points). Continue the base, then post-train, or add the instruction residual back.
No general evaluation until the end of a CPT run
Forgetting happens in the first percent of steps. Track a general held-out loss from step zero, next to the domain loss.
Blaming the learning rate for all the forgetting
Under a strong shift most of the damage is the data: Ibrahim's never-re-warmed run still lost 76% as much. Lowering the peak trades adaptation for retention; replay is the lever that is nearly free.
Comparing losses across tokenizers or scales
Loss per token depends on the tokenizer, and a 0.2 rise means different things at 405M and 10B. Compare relative changes against the same model's base, on the same validation set.
Reading a headline budget as a run cost
Thomson Reuters' $40M is a two-year programme including staff; the final run was $450K. Say which one you compare.
Interview rehearsal (6 questions)
Why not just train on the natural shares of your corpus?
Size is not value: the biggest pile (web) drowns small high-value ones (code, maths, encyclopedic). But upweighting a small pile repeats it, so the choice is a trade between value per token and repeats; compute epochs and use a cap (UniMax) or a fitted method.
Explain DoReMi in three sentences.
Train a small reference model on default weights. Train a small proxy with Group DRO: each step, upweight domains (multiplicatively) by the proxy's excess loss over the reference, clipped at zero, and weight the proxy's loss by them. Average the weights over the run and train the big model on that mixture.
What is the rank-invariance assumption and how would you test it?
That the order of mixtures by loss is the same for tiny and large models. Train a handful of large runs on mixtures the regression ranks differently and compute the Spearman correlation between predicted and true order (RegMix: 0.97 from 1M to 1B on its data; check yours).
You have 50B tokens of excellent maths and a 4T-token run. How do you use them?
Mostly in the decay stage, where they can be a large share of a short run; up to about 4 epochs costs little, so a 200B-token anneal could take 50% maths at 2 epochs or 25% at 1. Keep a small share in the stable phase if the model must not lose maths early. Validate with an anneal ablation and a decay-only control.
How do you pick the replay ratio for a legal-domain continued pretraining?
Run 3 or 4 short CPT runs at different domain shares, fit general and domain loss against share and tokens (CMR law), and take the largest share keeping general loss within your tolerance; replay data as close as possible to the base model's pretraining mix. Refit if the base model or the budget changes.
System design: plan the data mixture for a 3T-token pretraining run from six sources, with a fixed budget of 2% of the run's compute for mixture search.
Inventory: unique tokens per source after dedup and decontamination, with the tokenizer of the run. Constraints: max epochs per source (4 for anything that matters), minimum shares the product needs (code, languages). Search: RegMix-style, a few hundred proxy runs of about 1M non-embedding parameters on random Dirichlet mixtures inside the constraints, targets chosen by correlation with the eval suite; fit a nonlinear regressor and the exponential law, compare their recommendations; confirm the top 2 or 3 with a few 1B-scale runs (rank check). Stages: stable phase on the searched mix, a decay over the last 5 to 10% with the best sources upweighted to their epoch caps, chosen by anneal ablations from a mid-run checkpoint. Loader: per-source shards, deterministic blending, sampler state in checkpoints, per-source token logs. Risks: rank flips at scale, contamination through upweighted small sources, and the anneal hiding a weak base mix.
