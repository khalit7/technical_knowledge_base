3. Distillation and its terms of use

The problem. The cheapest good answer is a stronger model's answer. Almost every open SFT and preference set since Alpaca is distilled in this sense: prompts from people or templates, responses from a teacher model. Two things follow. The student inherits the teacher's style and errors (a filter for "As an AI developed by OpenAI" exists because students learned to say it: the root's {{Post-training data lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}} runs Ai2's identity and cutoff filters). And the teacher's terms of use travel with its outputs.

What the terms say. The generator-by-generator table (OpenAI, Anthropic, Llama 3.1, Gemma, Qwen, Mistral, NVIDIA; quoted, dated, not legal advice) is on the {{Synthetic data|n:3c65c17b0d0d8171b5d9e36e05b7a5d0}} page, section 8, and is not repeated here. What matters specifically for post-training sets:

  • Alpaca was released CC BY NC 4.0 for two stated reasons: LLaMA's non-commercial licence, and that text-davinci-003's "terms of use prohibit developing models that compete with OpenAI" ({{Alpaca blog|https://crfm.stanford.edu/2023/03/13/alpaca.html}}). Dolly 15k was written by employees so that it could be CC-BY-SA-3.0 and commercial.
  • Llama's licence changed between versions. Llama 3 (2024-04-18): "You will not use the Llama Materials or any output or results of the Llama Materials to improve any other large language model". Llama 3.1 (2024-07-23): allowed, but a model trained on its outputs must have "Llama" at the beginning of its name ({{3.1 licence, 1.b.i|https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meta-llama/llama-models/main/models/llama3_1/LICENSE}}). The same data has different terms depending on which Llama wrote it.
  • DeepSeek-R1 is MIT and says so for this use: "including, but not limited to, distillation for training other LLMs" ({{README, section 7|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1}}). Qwen3 is Apache-2.0; Qwen2.5-72B's licence asks for "Built with Qwen" on models trained on its outputs.
  • Mixtures carry many terms at once. Tulu 3's preference card lists them per generator: GPT-4 and GPT-4o outputs "subject to OpenAI's terms of use", Claude 3.5 Sonnet outputs "subject to Anthropic terms of service", Llama parts "Built with Llama", Gemma parts under the Gemma Terms of Use, Qwen2.5-72B under its licence ({{card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/llama-3.1-tulu-3-8b-preference-mixture}}).

4. Reasoning traces and how they are verified

The problem. A reasoning model writes a long chain of thought before its answer. To teach that to a smaller model by SFT, you need traces, and only correct ones: a trace that reasons well and ends in a wrong answer teaches the wrong habit, a trace that ends right after nonsense teaches that too. So reasoning SFT data is made by rejection sampling: generate several traces per problem with a strong model, keep the ones whose final answer a checker accepts.

Published recipes. DeepSeek-R1's SFT data: rejection sampling from its own RL checkpoint, "retain only the correct ones", filter "chain-of-thought with mixed languages, long paragraphs, and code blocks"; about 600k reasoning samples (Table 5: 626,933 math, code, STEM and logic) plus about 200k others (177,812). OpenR1-Math-220k: DeepSeek-R1 on 400k NuminaMath 1.5 problems, two (sometimes four) traces each, 16k-token limit; kept when Math-Verify accepts the answer, or, for 12% of samples, when Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct as a judge does ({{card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-r1/OpenR1-Math-220k}}). OLMo 3's thinking SFT reuses OpenThoughts3 traces, regenerates truncated ones with QwQ-32B and keeps code traces that pass tests written by GPT-4.1 ({{arXiv 2512.13961, 4.2.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961}}).
