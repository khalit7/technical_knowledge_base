8. Quality control: one prompt pool, three stages

The pretraining steps (dedup, decontamination, filtering) all apply, with three differences: rows are few, so each one matters; prompts are reused on purpose across stages; and the evaluations that will be reported are known in advance, so contamination is both easier to check and more damaging.

measured Every row of IF_multi_constraints_upto5 (OLMo 3's RL constraints) records the SFT row its base prompt came from: of @@if_multi_n@@ rows, @@if_from_tulu@@ come from Tulu 3 SFT subsets (Persona Hub @@if_persona@@, Evol CodeAlpaca @@if_code@@, Aya @@if_aya@@, ...), and all @@if_in_sample@@ whose id is in our 5,000-row SFT sample start with that SFT row's prompt.

That is by design: one pool, a different "what good looks like" per stage. It has two consequences. A prompt contaminated in one stage is contaminated in all of them, so decontaminate once, at the pool. And an RL prompt can be a jailbreak attempt with a formatting rule bolted on: @@if_safety@@ rows (@@if_safety_pct@@) come from the two adversarial safety subsets (WildJailbreak, WildGuardMix); rewarding "answer in lowercase" on a harmful request rewards compliance with the format, so the safety behaviour has to come from elsewhere.
