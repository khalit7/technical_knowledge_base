2. Distillation: the teacher's errors and terms travel with its answers

Almost every open SFT and preference set since Alpaca is distilled: prompts from people or templates, responses from a teacher model. The student inherits the teacher's style and errors (a filter for "As an AI developed by OpenAI" exists because students learned to say it; the root's {{Post-training data lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}} runs Ai2's identity and cutoff filters), and the teacher's terms of use travel with its outputs. 3. Reasoning traces: rejection sampling, and who checks the checker

To teach a long chain of thought by SFT you need traces that end in the right answer: a good-looking trace with a wrong answer teaches the wrong habit. So reasoning SFT data is made by rejection sampling: several traces per problem from a strong model, kept when a checker accepts the final answer. OpenR1-Math-220k is the open example: DeepSeek-R1 on 400k NuminaMath 1.5 problems, two (sometimes four) traces each, 16k-token limit, kept when Math-Verify accepts the answer or, for 12% of samples, when Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct as a judge does ({{card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-r1/OpenR1-Math-220k}}). measured on @@ck_n@@ R1 generations for @@ck_problems@@ of its problems:
