Common mistakes

Trusting a dataset's correctness flags
They were written by a checker at a version. Math-Verify today disagrees with OpenR1's February 2025 verdicts on @@ck_versions_dis@@ of @@ck_n@@ answers, and an LLM judge accepts @@ck_ll_true_pct@@ of what Math-Verify rejected (section 4). Re-run the checker you will train with on a sample, and keep its version with the verdict.
Checking the whole response instead of the final answer
Reasoning traces box intermediate results. Strip the thinking first (split("</think>")[-1], as open-instruct's IF verifier does), or a checker rewards a right guess abandoned halfway.
Counting rows instead of trained tokens
The share of tokens that get loss runs from @@mask_min_pct@@ (agent trajectories) to @@mask_max_pct@@ (reasoning traces) on the sets here (section 5). A mixture balanced by rows is not balanced by signal.
Reading "chosen" as "better"
It is "rated higher by this judge under this binarization rule". Ties, identical pairs and length effects are in the data (section 6, {{Pair inspector|#t-pdpair}}); check the margin and length distributions before training.
Assuming tests that the gold solution passes are good tests
A suite that passes the gold solution can also pass a mutant of it; @@mut_survive_pct@@ of mutants survived all tests on the sampled code problems (section 7). Add tests that kill mutants, or generate inputs.
Decontaminating per dataset with a percentage threshold only
A 2% per-benchmark rule lets 70 copied MMLU questions through because MMLU is large; remove matching rows, not only whole datasets, and check prompts reused across stages once, at the pool.
Ignoring the teacher's terms
The same kind of row has different terms depending on which model wrote it and when (Llama 3 against Llama 3.1). Keep the generator and its version in every row; mixtures that do (OLMo 2, Dolci) can be filtered later, mixtures that do not cannot.

Interview questions

Design the post-training data pipeline for a 7B reasoning model (system design).
Prompt pool first: sources per skill (math, code, science, chat, IF, safety, tool use), each with licence and origin recorded; exact and near dedup across sources; decontamination of prompts against every evaluation you will report, by 8-gram overlap plus an embedding pass for paraphrases, removing rows, not only datasets. SFT: generate several traces per prompt with a permissively licensed teacher; keep those whose answers pass a checker (math), tests (code) or constraint verifiers (IF); filter teacher artefacts, language mixing and truncation; budget by trained tokens. Preference: on-policy samples from the SFT model plus a stronger model; judge with a rubric and both orders, or use delta pairs; cap length differences; drop ties. RL: prompts with labels in canonical form; checkers tested on reference solutions and on model outputs before use; offline difficulty filter (pass rate between 0 and about 60% over 8 rollouts). Throughout: per-subset counts at every step, a read sample, the checker and generator versions stored per row, and ablations per subset, because quality is measured by the model trained on it. Scale: generation dominates cost (millions of long traces); checking and dedup are cheap CPU jobs.
Why can a verifiable reward still be wrong?
Extraction fails on correct answers in an unexpected format (false negatives), comparison is too lenient (false positives), labels are malformed, tests are weak, and checker versions change. Measure each checker on reference solutions and on real model outputs; read the disagreements.
What does on-policy preference data buy over UltraFeedback-style pairs?
Pairs drawn from the model being trained show it its own likely mistakes, so the gradient lands where the model actually puts probability; off-policy pairs from other models can be about mistakes it would never make. Tulu 3 mixes both; OLMo 3's delta learning uses two models of one family.
How would you detect length bias in a preference set, and what do you do about it?
Share of pairs where the chosen answer is longer, by length-gap bucket; the within-prompt correlation between rating and length; a length-only classifier's accuracy. Mitigate in the data (cap the length difference, as OLMo 3 does at 100 tokens for chat; balance pairs) or in the loss (length-normalised DPO, as Tulu 3 uses).
An SFT run on distilled data made the model claim to be another company's assistant. What happened and how do you prevent it?
The teacher's identity statements were in the responses. Filter identity and knowledge-cutoff patterns (Ai2's open-instruct scripts do), add hard-coded identity rows for your own model, and check by sampling identity questions after training.
What is the minimum you store per row?
Source dataset and row id, generator model and version, judge or checker and version, licence, the prompt's origin (to trace reuse across stages), decontamination result, and the date. Every filter later depends on these.

Method and sources

Measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), 2026-10-15, at low priority on a shared machine; counts and verdicts are deterministic and transfer to any machine, timings are not quoted. Samples (repository, commit, files or row groups, sha256) are in src/inputs/pd_manifest.jsonl and the root's src/data/manifest; full-set compositions are Hugging Face's dataset statistics (src/inputs/hf_statistics/, fetched 2026-10-15). Scripts in src/scripts/: pd_sft.py (lineage metrics, masks), pd_selfinst.py (ROUGE-L), pd_pref.py (pairs, binarization), pd_check.py and pd_check_sum.py (extractors on R1 answers), pd_rlcheck.py (OLMo 3 rollouts), pd_code.py (mutation testing, sandboxed), pd_env.py (tasks, trajectories), pd_contam.py (the Decontamination page's scanner), pd_quality.py. Checkers are the root lab's (src/post/pt_checkers.py) and open-instruct's at commit 72f46040. Lengths are Qwen3 tokens. Published facts with their quotes and table checks: src/notes/sources.md. Hand labels: src/notes/. Text generation with a local model ran only for the judge experiment (GPU, src/scripts/run_pending.sh).

One GPU run: Qwen3-4B (4-bit GGUF, llama.cpp on the M1 Pro GPU) as a judge on 300 UltraFeedback pairs in both orders (src/scripts/run_pending.sh, section 6). Two other runs were dropped as not worth their cost (local answers to OpenR1 problems; our own delta pairs); the page uses published model outputs and the delta-learning paper instead.
