5. Preference data: pairs, judges and the length trap

SFT can only copy; pairs teach a model to prefer the better of answers it can already write (DPO and relatives, or a reward model). Whatever separates chosen from rejected is what the model learns, intended or not.

Does on-policy help? Tulu 3's ablation (Figure 11, read from figure): 8B SFT model 58.2; DPO on 100k off-policy pairs 60.0; on 100k pairs with one on-policy answer 60.7; on the combined 120k 61.2 ({{arXiv 2411.15124, section 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}). On-policy pairs show the model its own likely mistakes; here that is worth less than a point, and the mixture is best.

Binarization: the same ratings, different pairs

UltraFeedback gives four rated answers per prompt; DPO needs one pair, and the rule that picks it is a data decision. Hugging Face's ultrafeedback_binarized card says it took the highest overall_score and a random other answer (the Zephyr paper, trained on it, says "highest mean score"); Argilla found overall_score is a critique score that "will give a 10 to responses that get a 1" in some rows, and rebuilt the pairs from the mean of the four aspect ratings, dropping ties. Length: the signal nobody asked for

Within each UltraFeedback prompt, GPT-4's mean rating correlates with the answer's length at @@ub_spear@@ on average (Spearman, @@ub_spear_n@@ prompts with untied ratings and lengths) measured; the longest of the four answers is (one of) the top-rated in @@ub_top_longest_pct@@ of prompts, against 25% by chance. Per aspect:

Longer answers are often more complete, so this is not proof of bias; but a model trained on these pairs can lower its loss by writing more. Published: always choosing the longer answer is right 55.7%, 59.6% and 63.1% of the time on WebGPT, Stack Exchange and RLCD preferences, and a reward of length alone reproduces most of RLHF's measured gains on two of them ({{Singhal et al., Table 5 and Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03716}}).
