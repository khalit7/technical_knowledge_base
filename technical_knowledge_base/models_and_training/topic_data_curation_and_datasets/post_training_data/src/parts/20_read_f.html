6. Preference data: pairs, judges and the length trap

The problem. SFT can only copy. To make a model prefer a better answer among the ones it can already write, you show it pairs: same prompt, a better and a worse answer. Preference optimisation (DPO and its relatives, or a reward model for RLHF; see {{Alignment|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}) then raises the probability of the chosen answer relative to the rejected one. Whatever separates chosen from rejected is what the model learns, intended or not.

Does on-policy help? Tulu 3's ablation (Figure 11, values read from the figure): aggregated score of the 8B SFT model 58.2; DPO on 100k off-policy pairs 60.0; on 100k pairs with one on-policy answer 60.7; on the combined 120k 61.2 ({{arXiv 2411.15124, section 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}). On-policy helps, by less than a point here, and the mixture is best.

Binarization: the same ratings, different pairs

How it works. UltraFeedback gives four rated answers per prompt; DPO needs one pair. The rule that picks the pair is a data decision, and the published ones differ. Hugging Face's card for ultrafeedback_binarized says it chose the highest overall_score and a random other answer; the Zephyr paper, trained on it, says "highest mean score"; Argilla found the overall_score field is a critique score that "will give a 10 to responses that get a 1" in some rows and rebuilt the pairs from the mean of the four aspect ratings, dropping ties. The animation takes real prompts from UltraFeedback's evol_instruct subset and builds the pair under each rule.

Across all @@ub_prompts@@ prompts measured: the two common rules pick the same chosen answer for @@ub_same_chosen@@ of the @@ub_same_n@@ prompts where both apply (@@ub_same_pct@@); Argilla reported "a different chosen response in ~30K examples (out of ~63K)" for the whole set. Under the overall_score rule @@ub_h4_ties@@ of the @@ub_h4_n@@ pairs are ties (no preference at all, since the rejected answer is drawn at random), against none under the mean rule, which drops them.

Is the judge rewarding length?

Within each UltraFeedback prompt, the rank correlation between GPT-4's mean aspect rating and the answer's length is @@ub_spear@@ on average (Spearman, @@ub_spear_n@@ prompts with untied ratings and lengths) measured; the longest of the four answers is (one of) the top-rated in @@ub_top_longest_pct@@ of prompts, against 25% by chance. Per aspect:

A positive correlation is not proof of bias: longer answers are often more complete. It means a model trained on these pairs can lower its loss by writing more, whether or not it writes better. Published: always choosing the longer answer is right 55.7%, 59.6% and 63.1% of the time on WebGPT, Stack Exchange and RLCD preferences, and a reward of length alone reproduces most of RLHF's measured gains on two of them ({{Singhal et al., Table 5 and Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03716}}).
