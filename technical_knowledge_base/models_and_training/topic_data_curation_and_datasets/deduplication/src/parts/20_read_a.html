The web repeats itself. The same cookie banner sits on thousands of sites, a news story is syndicated to hundreds, a shop renders the same template for every product, and Common Crawl recrawls the same pages every month. Deduplication finds those copies and decides which to keep. This page teaches it from zero: what the evidence says it buys, then every method a pretraining pipeline uses, each with the problem it solves, how it works, what it costs, when it matters and how to check it, measured on the topic's running example.

The running example, used on every tab of the {{root page|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}}: one WARC file (Common Crawl's raw archive of HTTP responses) from the July 2024 crawl CC-MAIN-2024-30, 20,991 responses, taken through a FineWeb-style datatrove pipeline (run0). Its stages: 19,241 extracted documents (text out of HTML), 2,692 filtered documents (English, passed the quality rules; this is what reaches dedup), 2,617 final. Everything labelled measured was run on an Apple M1 Pro, CPU only, in October 2026; published numbers are the authors' own, at their scale, with the table they come from; derived numbers show their formula; vendor marks a claim by someone selling the tool. The root's {{Dedup lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}} tab ran every method once on this file; this page goes deeper on each and reuses its scripts and numbers.

The three things to remember
  1. Duplication is about scope. One WARC file has 2.8% near-duplicate documents; a whole crawl, then all crawls, have far more. DCLM measured that a global MinHash would still remove 85% of its finished, already deduplicated DCLM-baseline (published Table 23). Every number on this page says over what scope it was measured.
  2. Removing copies cuts memorisation strongly and wasted compute clearly; its effect on benchmark scores is small and sometimes negative. The evidence in section 2 is consistent on the first and mixed on the second.
  3. Each method sees a different unit, so they are run in sequence, cheapest first: exact hashes, then MinHash on whole documents, then (optionally) suffix arrays on spans, then semantic dedup where paraphrase is the problem. The {{Method comparer|#t-cmp}} runs every combination on the same 2,692 documents.
