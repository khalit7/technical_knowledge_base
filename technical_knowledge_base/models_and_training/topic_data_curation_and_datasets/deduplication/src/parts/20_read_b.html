1. Does deduplication help? Three claims, three kinds of evidence

"Dedup makes models better" merges three claims. The evidence is strong for the first, clear for the second and mixed for the third.

  1. Duplicated text is memorised, and dedup cuts that tenfold. Memorisation means a model reproducing a stretch of its training text verbatim. Lee et al. (2021) trained 1.5B-parameter models on C4 and on two deduplicated versions: unprompted, the original emitted memorised text in 1.926% of its output tokens after one epoch, against 0.189% (NearDup, MinHash) and 0.138% (ExactSubstr, suffix arrays) ({{Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2107.06499}}). The cause is the copy count: a sequence present 10 times in training is generated about 1,000 times more often than one present once (Kandpal, Wallace and Raffel 2022, {{Figure 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2202.06539}}).
  2. Skewed repetition wastes compute and can hurt. Hernandez et al. (Anthropic, 2022): an 800M-parameter model "could be degraded to that of a 2x smaller model (400M params) by repeating 0.1% of the data 100 times" ({{arXiv 2205.10487|https://arxiv.org/html/2205.10487}}). Uniform repetition is not the problem: a whole dataset repeated up to about 4 epochs costs almost nothing (Muennighoff et al. 2023, see the {{data mixing page|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}}). The damage comes from a few heavily repeated items, exactly what MinHash's largest clusters are.
  3. Benchmark gains are small and sometimes negative. DCLM compared nine dedup recipes at 1B parameters: every one beat no dedup, by 0.3 to 2.1 Core points, and Exact+MinHash scored below either step alone, so the differences sit near the noise:

The memorisation and benchmark evidence, table by table (depth)
  • Lee et al. (2021, ACL 2022): NearDup is MinHash on space-separated 5-grams, 9,000 hashes, candidates confirmed by Jaccard > 0.8 and edit similarity > 0.8; ExactSubstr removes every repeated span of 50 or more tokens. Table 4 at two epochs: 1.571%, 0.264%, 0.168%.
  • Kandpal et al. (2022): slopes above 1 on a log-log plot of generation rate against copies. On Lee et al.'s models the deduplicated model generated 68,090 training sequences against 1,427,212 for the original, about 21 times fewer (Table 1), and two of three membership-inference attacks lost most of their power (AUROC 0.76 to 0.67 and 0.86 to 0.68; the third, 0.88 to 0.87, barely moved).
  • Carlini et al. (2022, ICLR 2023): memorisation grows log-linearly with model size, with the number of duplicates and with prompt length. On Lee's deduplicated C4 model, sequences repeated fewer than 35 times were memorised at 1.2% against 3.6% for the original; for sequences repeated more than about 100 times dedup "does not help", because the deduplication had missed them ({{Section 5.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2202.07646}}).
  • Hernandez et al.: 90% unique data plus 10% made of a small subset repeated many times. The body puts the loss "nearly as high as for the 340M" model; both figures are read from a figure.

Cross-crawl dedup: FineWeb's surprise

FineWeb first deduplicated all 96 crawls together, newest crawl first, so a page that appeared in a later crawl was removed from every earlier one. That removed "as much as 90%" of the oldest crawls, and models trained on the 4T tokens left improved little over no dedup. On the oldest crawl, 2013-48:

What survives global dedup is a sliver, and it trains a worse model than what was removed

Bars to scale. The about 31B tokens that survived scored worse than the about 460B tokens removed, deduplicated on their own to 171B. FineWeb's hypothesis: pages that never reappear in later crawls are disproportionately low quality, so global dedup "upsamples lower-quality data"; the gain of dedup comes from removing the huge clusters, and deduplicating clusters smaller than about 100 "can harm performance" (published {{FineWeb Section 3.4|https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17557}}).

Per-crawl MinHash gave 20T tokens and matched RefinedWeb; lighter global passes on top (URL dedup, which would remove 71.5% of tokens; line dedup 77.8%; 3-line dedup 80.9%) were all worse (Appendix E.3). FineWeb still deduplicates inside each crawl: what the experiment shows is that at this scale what survives global dedup is a biased sample, not that duplicates are good.

Read the arithmetic
"10% kept, 90% removed" is rounded: 31 / (31 + 460) = 6.3% kept. The paper's text points to Figure 4 for a comparison that is in Figure 3, and the figure values are not given as numbers.
Predict: FineWeb-Edu is about 80% duplicates by a global MinHash. A team deduplicates it globally and trains a 1.8B model on 350B tokens. What happens to the scores?
About the same. Loubna Ben Allal (Hugging Face) reported that cross-dump MinHash on FineWeb-Edu leaves about 200B unique tokens, and that their ablation at 1.8B parameters and 350B tokens had not "seen an improvement or performance degradation from deduplication" (June 2024) ({{discussion, HF hub|https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-edu/discussions/7}}). The duplicates in a quality-filtered set are mostly good pages that the web copies; repeating them a few times behaves like a few epochs of good data. Dedup matters most for memorisation and for budgets where the unique pool is not the limit.

Nemotron-CC took the other side: it deduplicated globally and still matched DCLM, so the FineWeb finding is an interaction with the rest of a pipeline, not a law. The working rule: dedup tells you the counts; mixing decides how often each document is seen (section 10, rehydration).

How much duplication is left in the corpora people train on (depth)
  • DCLM ran FineWeb-style global MinHash on finished datasets: it would remove 85% of DCLM-baseline (built with a 100-shard Bloom filter), 45% of DCLM's own RefinedWeb reproduction, 43% of Dolma v1 (36% without its about 600M empty documents) and 0% of the official RefinedWeb release; DCLM adds these leftover duplicates "do not seem to adversely effect downstream performance" ({{Table 23|https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11794}}).
  • Nemotron-CC's {{Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02595}} is a size table, not an ablation: after global fuzzy dedup, FineWeb-Edu goes from 1.3T to 0.2T unique tokens, FineWeb-Edu-2 from 5.4T to 1.1T, DCLM from 3.8T to 1.0T, Nemotron-CC from 4.4T real to 4.4T (it was deduplicated globally). Implied duplicate shares: 85%, 80%, 74%. The text rounds these to "around 80%".
  • Zyphra (Zyda-2, 2024): "we believe we have found 80% duplicates" in DCLM, and "about 80% for both" DCLM and FineWeb-Edu in the {{technical report|https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06068}}, with MinHash on character 25-grams, 128 hashes, 8 bands (about 85% Jaccard); "80%" is not defined further, and they did not deduplicate DCLM internally for the release because their first ablations showed "only a small effect".

measured Inside one FineWeb shard, and what crawl-wide dedup did to our file. The root lab ran FineWeb's own MinHash (14 × 8) over FineWeb's first CC-MAIN-2024-30 shard: 969,556 rows from 353 other WARC files, plus our 2,692 filtered documents. Inside FineWeb's output, already deduplicated across the whole crawl, it still finds 370 candidate pairs, 14 of them with true Jaccard ≥ 0.8: a MinHash setting misses some pairs by chance, and FineWeb filtered and edited text after dedup. Between our file and the shard it finds 424 candidate pairs, most between 0.5 and 0.8. Of the 214 documents our pipeline kept but FineWeb dropped, 15 have a partner at ≥ 0.8 in those 353 files alone (0.4% of the crawl's 90,000), and none of the documents FineWeb kept has one: consistent with FineWeb's crawl-wide MinHash removing them, partner by partner (root lab, src/dedup/dd_fwshard.py, about 2.5 hours on 4 processes).

Measure at the scope you will deduplicate. Duplicates are pairs: a random 1% sample keeps only 0.01% of the pairs. The running example's one WARC has 2.8% near-duplicate documents; DeepSeek LLM found 22.2% inside one dump and 89.8% across 91.
