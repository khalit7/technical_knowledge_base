5. SimHash: one 64-bit fingerprint per document

The idea (Charikar 2002; scaled by Manku, Jain and Das Sarma at Google, 2007). Give every shingle a 64-bit hash. For each bit position, count +1 for every shingle whose hash has that bit set and −1 otherwise; the fingerprint's bit is 1 where the count is positive. Similar documents share most shingles, so most counts keep their sign, and similarity is read as the Hamming distance (differing bits). Each bit is the sign of a random ±1 projection, so two documents disagree on a bit with probability close to θ/π, where cos θ = |A ∩ B| / √(|A| |B|): expected distance 64θ/π, 32 for unrelated documents, 0 for identical ones. Eight bytes per document against 896 for a 112-value MinHash signature.

At Manku's k = 3 SimHash only sees near-identical pages; pairs at Jaccard 0.8 or more spread up to 16 bits

measured On the running example's 2,692 filtered documents, with the same shingles MinHash uses: at Manku's k = 3, SimHash finds 147 of the 308 pairs with Jaccard ≥ 0.8 (48%), all near-identical; the 308 spread up to 16 bits, and it needs k = 8 to reach 91% (280), and makes its first candidate below Jaccard 0.5 at k = 13. Use it for near-identical detection with a tiny index (crawlers, recrawl detection) or as a cheap first pass; not for catching documents 75% to 90% the same, where MinHash's banding gives a sharp, tunable threshold. text-dedup's benchmark on the CORE dataset agrees (MinHash duplicate recall 0.94 against 0.73 for SimHash, README at commit 7538f3e), and in the {{Method comparer|#t-cmp}} SimHash at k = 3 removes a single document that exact hashing had not already removed.

Google's scale, the permuted-table index, and how well θ/π holds (depth)

"For a repository of 8 billion pages, 64-bit simhash fingerprints and k = 3 are reasonable", with precision and recall both "near 0.75" at k = 3 on their hand-tagged sample (Manku et al., Sections 3 and 4.1). Finding all fingerprints within 3 bits without comparing pairs uses permuted tables: split the 64 bits into blocks; any two fingerprints within 3 bits agree exactly on some choice of blocks, so each table sorts the fingerprints by one choice and only fingerprints sharing it are compared (their Example 3.1: 20 tables, at most 8 fingerprints per probe; or 4 tables, up to 256K per probe). The θ/π law is exact for Gaussian projections (Charikar, Section 3).

measured The 3,602,411 pairs with no shared shingle have mean Hamming distance 31.99 and standard deviation 4.00, exactly the 32 and 4 of a coin-flip model; the closest of them is 13 bits apart. Measured Hamming distance tracks 64θ/π well at the extremes and runs above it for cosine 0.6 to 0.9 (for example 13.7 measured vs 10.7 expected at cosine 0.8 to 0.9): SimHash uses ±1 hash bits on sets of 50 to 200 shingles, where the sign of a short sum ties and flips more often (a likely reason; not tested further here). ROOTS used SimHash and saw false positives on long documents.
