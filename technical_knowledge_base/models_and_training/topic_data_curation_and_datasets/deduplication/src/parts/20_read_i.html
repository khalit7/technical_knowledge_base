9. Semantic dedup: SemDeDup and D4

The problem. Two pages can say the same thing in different words and share no 5-word phrase: Jaccard near 0. Paraphrase-level redundancy is common in synthetic data, instruction sets and spam.

How it works. An encoder (a model that maps a text to a vector so that texts with similar meaning land close together; the {{RAG topic|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}} covers them) embeds every document. Comparing all pairs is again quadratic, so SemDeDup (Abbas et al. 2023) first clusters the vectors with k-means, then inside each cluster removes a document whose cosine similarity to a kept one is at least 1−ε. NeMo Curator 1.4.0 runs this on GPUs with embeddinggemma-300m, 100 clusters and ε = 0.01 by default, keeping the members farthest from the centroid first ("hard"; stages/text/deduplication/semantic.py:80-92).

What the papers report

  • SemDeDup ({{arXiv 2303.09540|https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09540}}): on LAION image-text pairs (k = 50,000 clusters), removing 37% caused no drop and removing 50% cost under 0.5% of zero-shot accuracy. On C4 with OPT-125M (k = 11,000), keeping 80% after SemDeDup matched the perplexity of keeping 96.1% after NearDup. The training-time saving is stated three ways: 15% (introduction), 10% to 15% (Section 5.2), 5% (Figure 8's example). Perplexity at 4% pruned (Table A12): baseline 38.95, NearDup 39.46, random 39.51, SemDeDup 39.35 on C4: pruning anything raised perplexity a little.
  • D4 (Tirumala et al. 2023, {{arXiv 2308.12284|https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12284}}): SemDeDup first (keep 75%), then "SSL prototypes": k-means again and drop the documents closest to their centroid, the most typical ones, until the target size. At 6.7B parameters and 100B tokens: about 20% fewer steps to reach the baseline's perplexity and up to 2% better average downstream accuracy. Table 1 (1.3B, 40B tokens): D4-selected 20B tokens repeated twice beat 40B random tokens once on non-web perplexity (16.10 vs 16.27) and instruction perplexity (13.85 vs 14.19). Selection always hurt perplexity on a held-out web snapshot: it trades web typicality for diversity.
  • DCLM Table 4: SemDedup used as the selection step scored 27.1 Core against 27.5 for no model-based selection: as a quality filter on its own it did not help.

Measured on the running example: does the encoder matter?

measured The 2,692 filtered documents embedded with three encoders on CPU (4 threads, low priority): arctic-embed-m (the FineWeb-Edu classifier's backbone, root lab), bge-small-en-v1.5 (440 s) and all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (100 s), then SemDeDup with 20 k-means clusters.

  • Similar counts, different documents. At ε = 0.05 the three encoders remove 62, 59 and 57 documents, but only 34 are removed by all three and 85 by at least one. At ε = 0.1: 72, 72 and 67; 38 by all three, 104 by any.
  • A fixed ε does not transfer between encoders. The median cosine between two unrelated documents is 0.21 for arctic, 0.49 for bge-small and 0.05 for MiniLM; the lowest cosine among pairs with Jaccard ≥ 0.8 is 0.71, 0.80 and 0.74. Calibrate ε on labelled pairs for each encoder before trusting NeMo Curator's default.
  • D4's prototypes are short and low-scoring here. After SemDeDup (ε 0.05, 2,630 left), keeping 80% by D4's rule prunes the 476 most typical documents: median length 1,057 characters against 2,156 for those kept, median FineWeb-Edu score 0.62 against 1.10, and only 19 of the 159 documents scoring ≥ 2.5 (FineWeb-Edu's keep line). The most typical ones are short corporate and shop pages. On this file, prototype pruning acts like a light quality filter, consistent with D4's diversity argument.

When it matters: synthetic and instruction data (many prompts that say the same thing), after exact and MinHash dedup. Cost: an encoder pass over every document (DCLM-scale pools need many GPU hours) plus k-means. How to check it: read the removed pairs at the chosen ε, especially pairs with low Jaccard: on this file they are cookie notices from different plugins and casino spam (root lab), but at looser ε real pages start to go.
