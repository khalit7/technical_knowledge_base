8. Semantic dedup: SemDeDup and D4

The problem. Two pages can say the same thing in different words and share no 5-word phrase: Jaccard near 0. Paraphrase-level redundancy is common in synthetic data, instruction sets and spam.

How it works. An encoder (a model that maps a text to a vector so that texts with similar meaning land close together) embeds every document. Comparing all pairs is again quadratic, so SemDeDup (Abbas et al. 2023) first clusters the vectors with k-means, then inside each cluster removes a document whose cosine similarity to a kept one is at least 1−ε. NeMo Curator 1.4.0 runs this on GPUs with embeddinggemma-300m, 100 clusters and ε = 0.01 by default, keeping the members farthest from the centroid first ("hard"; stages/text/deduplication/semantic.py:80-92). D4 (Tirumala et al. 2023) adds a second step: k-means again and drop the documents closest to their centroid, the most typical ones, until a target size.

measured Does the encoder matter? The 2,692 filtered documents embedded with three encoders on CPU (4 threads, low priority): arctic-embed-m (the FineWeb-Edu classifier's backbone, root lab), bge-small-en-v1.5 (440 s) and all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (100 s), then SemDeDup with 20 k-means clusters.

Three encoders remove similar counts but different documents

  • Similar counts, different documents. At ε = 0.05 the three encoders remove 62, 59 and 57 documents, but only 34 are removed by all three and 85 by at least one. At ε = 0.1: 72, 72 and 67; 38 by all three, 104 by any.
  • A fixed ε does not transfer between encoders. The median cosine between two unrelated documents is 0.21 for arctic, 0.49 for bge-small and 0.05 for MiniLM; the lowest cosine among pairs with Jaccard ≥ 0.8 is 0.71, 0.80 and 0.74. Calibrate ε on labelled pairs for each encoder before trusting NeMo Curator's default.
  • What D4 prunes depends on what came before. On the raw running example the most typical documents are short corporate and shop pages, so prototype pruning acts like a light quality filter. On 50,000 FineWeb-Edu rows, already selected by a quality classifier, "typical" means the classifier's favourite kind of page, and D4 removes it (details below).

Published results are modest (closed block below): no loss on LAION at 37% removed, a perplexity match for C4 with less data, small downstream gains for D4 at 6.7B, and no gain as DCLM's selection step. When it matters: synthetic and instruction data, after exact and MinHash dedup. Cost: an encoder pass over every document plus k-means. How to check it: read the removed pairs at the chosen ε, especially pairs with low Jaccard: on this file they are cookie notices from different plugins and casino spam (root lab), but at looser ε real pages start to go.

With arctic-embed-m and 100 clusters, SemDeDup removes 3 documents at ε 0.01 (NeMo Curator's default), 96 at 0.05, 302 at 0.1 and 866 at 0.15: an already deduplicated, quality-filtered set has little paraphrase redundancy until ε gets loose. What the semantic-dedup papers report, in full (depth)
  • SemDeDup ({{arXiv 2303.09540|https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09540}}): on LAION image-text pairs (k = 50,000 clusters), removing 37% caused no drop and removing 50% cost under 0.5% of zero-shot accuracy. On C4 with OPT-125M (k = 11,000), keeping 80% after SemDeDup matched the perplexity of keeping 96.1% after NearDup. The training-time saving is stated three ways: 15% (introduction), 10% to 15% (Section 5.2), 5% (Figure 8's example). Perplexity at 4% pruned (Table A12): baseline 38.95, NearDup 39.46, random 39.51, SemDeDup 39.35 on C4: pruning anything raised perplexity a little.
  • D4 ({{arXiv 2308.12284|https://arxiv.org/html/2308.12284}}): SemDeDup first (keep 75%), then "SSL prototypes". At 6.7B parameters and 100B tokens: about 20% fewer steps to reach the baseline's perplexity and up to 2% better average downstream accuracy. Table 1 (1.3B, 40B tokens): D4-selected 20B tokens repeated twice beat 40B random tokens once on non-web perplexity (16.10 vs 16.27) and instruction perplexity (13.85 vs 14.19). Selection always hurt perplexity on a held-out web snapshot: it trades web typicality for diversity.
  • DCLM Table 4: SemDedup used as the selection step scored 27.1 Core against 27.5 for no model-based selection; as a quality filter on its own it did not help.
