Staging: the same token budget before and after a mid-training stage

Modern runs change the mixture during training: a long first stage on broad data, then a short final stage on the best data (math, instruction-like and question-answer text, filtered web, often partly synthetic) while the learning rate decays to zero. Labs call it mid-training or annealing. How big is the final stage? It depends on the lab

published stage sizes, fraction of the run's tokens derived; log scale. Not a rule: from 40 million tokens to 40% of the run. OLMo 2's own text calls mid-training "5 to 10% of training FLOPs" because it counts every mid-training run averaged into the final model ("souping"): three runs of 50B for the 7B (), three of 100B and one of 300B for the 13B (). Any single path to the final 7B weights saw 50B mid-training tokens, 1.2% of its tokens. Llama 3 also has a long-context stage of about 800B tokens before its annealing.

Long horizons: where mixing meets dedup and quality filtering

The quality lab's classifiers keep the top slice of the web; dedup removes copies. Both shrink the pool of unique tokens, and a long run must then repeat it. Nemotron-CC made this argument with numbers: DCLM-baseline and FineWeb-Edu "contain around 80% near-duplicates", leaving about 1T and 0.2T unique tokens, while their filters remove 90% of the data; a 15T-token run (Llama 3.1's horizon) on either would see the same documents many times ({{Nemotron-CC, Section 1 and Table 4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02595}}). Its answer was to keep more of the crawl (an ensemble of classifiers sorted into quality buckets instead of one cut) and to add rephrased synthetic text.

Dataset sizes and unique tokens (after global fuzzy dedup of the real tokens): {{Nemotron-CC, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02595}}, published, trillions of tokens. Epochs and fresh-token equivalents are derived: the whole budget drawn from one corpus's unique tokens, valued with Muennighoff et al.'s law as in the calculator. Nemotron-CC's 1.9T synthetic tokens are left out of "unique"; counting them as fresh would raise its bar further. A thought experiment, not a training result.

What the labs did with it
Nemotron-CC's own 15T run was staged: a first phase of 9T tokens with 59% English Common Crawl (5.31T, the medium, medium-high and high buckets, real and synthetic) and a second phase of 6T with 31% (1.86T, high quality only), 7.17T in total; the 8B model scored MMLU 70.3 against 65.3 for Llama 3.1 8B in NVIDIA's own evaluation setup (Appendix E and Table 6, published). In a 1T-token ablation, swapping 4 of 8 epochs of the high-quality data for synthetic rephrasings improved most benchmarks (Section 3). So the quality tiers are mixing domains in their own right: the top tier is repeated, the middle tiers supply unique tokens, and synthetic data replaces repetitions.

Continued pretraining: how much domain data before the model forgets

Continued pretraining (CPT) is the same mixing decision made later: train a finished model further on one domain and mix general data back in (replay) or it forgets. The CMR scaling law reads the critical mixture ratio, the largest share of domain data that keeps general loss within a tolerance, off a few short probe runs; bigger models and domains closer to the general data tolerate more (29.8% to 47.8% on finance from 460M to 3.1B, {{Gu et al., arXiv 2407.17467|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17467}}). {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}}, section 6, owns CPT: forgetting, re-warming, schedules and a CMR calculator with these ratios; this lab's bar chart of them was retired on 2026-10-24.

Common mistakes

Mixing sizes counted with different tokenizers
Cards count tokens with their own tokenizer, or in words or bytes. Code is where it bites: on the topic's Stack v2 Edu sample GPT-2 needs tokens for every Comma v0.1 token. Convert everything to your tokenizer on a sample before computing epochs.
Temperature sampling at a large budget without counting epochs
Temperature knows sizes, not repetition. At 15T with T = 3.33, English Wikipedia would be repeated times. Cap epochs (UniMax) or accept that the extra passes are worth little.
Copying a published mixture
Optimal weights shift with model size and budget, and DoReMi's weights from the paper's 280M proxy and from its repository's 120M proxy differ widely on the same data (Pile-CC 60.6% against 13.8%, compared on the child page). Treat a published mix as a starting point and re-run a cheap search on your own pools.
Calling the late stage "the final 10 to 30%"
OLMo 2 7B's mid-training is about 1.2% of its tokens, SmolLM2's decay stage 9%, Llama 3's annealing 40M tokens; Nemotron-CC's high-quality phase is 40%. There is no standard size; the common element is a different, higher-quality mix while the learning rate decays.
Reading DoReMi's or RegMix's gains as gains over The Pile's weights
Their baseline is a document-count weighting from DoReMi's repository, not The Pile's Table 1. The gains are real but measured against that baseline.

Interview questions

You have 3T unique web tokens, 50B code, 20B math and a 10T-token budget. How do you set the mix?
Convert all pools to the training tokenizer. Start from capped epochs: code and math at no more than about 4 epochs over the run (200B and 80B), the rest web (about 3.2 passes over 3T, so even web repeats). Put most of the math and code repetitions in a final decay stage. Check with a cheap proxy search (RegMix-style small models, or an annealing test per source) and confirm the chosen mix against natural and stratified baselines; expect differences of a point or two.
Why does aggressive quality filtering become a problem at 15T tokens?
It shrinks the unique pool (FineWeb-Edu about 0.2T unique, DCLM about 1T by Nemotron-CC's estimate), so a long run repeats it 15 to 75 times, far past the 4-epoch point where repetition starts to cost. Keep lower tiers for unique tokens, repeat the top tier a few times, add rephrased data, and stage it.
How would you test whether a new 10B-token dataset is worth adding?
An annealing test: take a mid-run checkpoint, decay the learning rate over a short run with the candidate at a fixed share (Llama 3 used 30% of 40B tokens), compare with the same run without it. Cheaper than retraining and sensitive to small, high-quality sources; OLMo 2 ran 19 such "microanneals" for 130B tokens in total.
DoReMi or RegMix: which would you run, and what would you check?
RegMix if many tiny runs are cheap to parallelise (it found its mix with about a tenth of DoReMi's search compute); DoReMi if you can afford two mid-size runs and want weights tied to excess loss. Either way check transfer: rank a few mixtures at the small scale, then train two of them at a larger scale and see if the order holds (the proxy experiment's rank check), and compare with stratified sampling, which Aioli found hard to beat.
