7. Mixing and staging: how much of each kind, and when

A pretraining corpus is a mixture of domains (web text, code, maths, papers, books, encyclopedias, other languages, synthetic text), and the mixture weights say what fraction of the training tokens each supplies. At a fixed budget every point of code is a point not spent on web text, so the weights move abilities directly: Llama 3's final mix was roughly 50% general knowledge, 25% maths and reasoning, 17% code and 8% multilingual tokens ({{Llama 3, Section 3.1.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}). Natural shares drown small, valuable domains; multilingual models sample domain i with probability proportional to ni1/T, a temperature above 1 flattening the mix.

Repetition is the number every other stage feeds. Upsampling a small domain means repeating it. At large scale, up to about 4 epochs is nearly as good as fresh data and gains continue to about 16, after which extra repeats are worth almost nothing ({{Muennighoff et al., arXiv 2305.16264|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264}}); in the T5 experiments, repeating a truncated C4 1,024 times cost about 4 GLUE points and 4.6 SQuAD points ({{T5, Table 9|https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683}}). So a strict quality cut (section 5) is a mixing decision: it sets how many epochs the best data must take.

Choosing weights means training small proxies and transferring what they learn (DoReMi, RegMix, Data Mixing Laws: {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}}, section 3). The sobering check is Aioli: no existing method consistently beat simple stratified sampling on average test perplexity ({{Aioli|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05735}}, third-party), and optimal weights shift with model size and budget, so a published mix is a starting point. On the running example's five domains a 0.8M-parameter DoReMi proxy (4,000 steps, one seed) moved weight from natural shares of 17% web, 16% Wikipedia, 31% code, 21% maths and 15% papers to 2.6% web, 10.7% Wikipedia, 37.9% code, 38.5% maths and 10.3% papers, while the proxy itself ended 0.35 to 0.39 nats worse than its reference on every domain; whether those weights beat the natural shares was not run (measured, {{Mixing lab|#t-mix}}). It is a lesson in the method's sensitivity at small scale, not a recommended mix.

Staging: the mix changes during training. Modern recipes use a long first stage on broad data and a short final stage, mid-training or annealing, on the best data (maths, code, instruction-like and question-answer text, often synthetic) while the learning rate decays to zero. OLMo 2 7B's 50 billion Dolmino tokens, about 1.2% of the run, took GSM8K from 24.1 to 67.5 (section 1's table). Annealing is also the cheapest way to evaluate a dataset: decay a half-trained model over a short run where the candidate is a fixed share (Llama 3 used 40 billion tokens with 30% candidate data) and compare with the same run without it; Nemotron-CC ranked its quality buckets this way.

Continued pretraining (CPT) is the same split made later: train a finished model further on a domain, and mix general data back in (replay) or it forgets. The CMR scaling law fits general and domain loss on a few short probe runs and reads off the critical mixture ratio, the largest domain-data share that keeps general loss within a tolerance: 29.8% for a 460M model up to 47.8% for 3.1B on finance ({{Gu et al., arXiv 2407.17467|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17467}}). It is the method Khalid has used at work to set this ratio; {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}}, section 6, owns CPT (forgetting, re-warming, schedules, the CMR calculator) and section 7 is its checklist.

Predict: you have 20 billion tokens of high-quality maths and a 2-trillion-token run. Which uses it best?
The published recipes concentrate it at the end (OLMo 2, Llama 3, SmolLM2), where 20B tokens can be a quarter or more of the final stage instead of 1% of every batch, and the annealing evidence above shows the gains. Spreading it is not wrong (it still helps), and repeating it up to about 4 times across stages is cheap. What to avoid is placing it early only, where later training dilutes it.

8. Synthetic pretraining data: when the crawl runs out

The web is finite and the best of it is small: FineWeb-Edu's strict cut is 1.3 trillion tokens, and frontier runs train on 15 trillion or more. Past that point the choice is to repeat (section 7: cheap to about 4 times), keep lower-quality text, or manufacture tokens with a model. Two lineages: generate from seeds (Phi's textbooks; Cosmopedia's 30 million documents written by Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, each prompt seeded with a real page or topic, because diversity, not generation, is the hard part) and rephrase real documents (WRAP, Nemotron-CC's 1.9 trillion rewritten tokens): the real page supplies the information and its diversity, the model fixes the form. Synthetic text is now a standard ingredient: 57.5% of Olmo 3's 100-billion-token mid-training mix is labelled synthetic (derived from the {{Dolma 3 Dolmino card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/dolma3_dolmino_mix-100B-1025}}).

Generating is not rephrasing, measured. On Cosmopedia documents and the web pages that seeded them, a generated document keeps almost none of its seed's wording (a median 99% of its 4-word sequences are new) and is 3.6 times as long, and the FineWeb-Edu classifier passes 79 of 200 generated documents against 9 of their 200 seeds (measured, {{Post-training data lab|#t-post}}). Rephrasing changes the form far more than a classifier's verdict: Qwen3-4B's rewrites of 200 running-example pages in WRAP's four styles barely move the FineWeb-Edu pass rate except in the style that discards half the text ({{Synthetic data|n:3c65c17b0d0d8171b5d9e36e05b7a5d0}}, section 3).

Model collapse: models trained recursively on their own output lose the tails of the distribution ({{Shumailov et al., Nature 2024|https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y}}), but the experiment assumes each generation replaces the previous data; with synthetic data added to the real data, collapse disappeared across model sizes and architectures ({{Gerstgrasser et al., arXiv 2404.01413|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413}}: test loss 1.82 after the first iteration, 2.39 after four when replacing, 1.74 when accumulating). Real pipelines avoid collapse by construction (added, grounded, filtered); the live risks are a uniform style, the generator's biases, and crawls after 2023 that already hold model-written text nobody chose to add.

9. Post-training data: instructions, preferences, checkers and environments

After pretraining, a model trains on much smaller, structured data, and each stage of post-training ({{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO Family, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}) consumes a different shape of example: a prompt plus a signal of what good looks like. The shape has grown richer over time:

StageOne example isBuilt now by
SFTa prompt and a good responsecurated prompts, responses distilled from a strong teacher, then filtered, deduplicated, decontaminated (Tulu 3: 939,344 prompts)
Preferencea prompt, a chosen and a rejected responseseveral models answer, an LLM judge rates them, best and a worse one form the pair, often on-policy
RLVRa prompt and a program that checks the answermaths with known answers, code with tests, checkable constraints; prompts the model always or never solves are removed
Environmentsa task, a sandbox and a testverified answer first, question after; environments rebuilt from recorded agent trajectories (Terminal-Universe: 37.3k)

Three lessons carry across the rows. Quality beats quantity for style and coverage needs volume: LIMA's 1,000 examples teach format (its "superficial alignment" hypothesis), Tulu 3 needed hundreds of thousands to cover maths, code, safety and instruction following. Judges have biases the data inherits: a judge that prefers longer answers produces pairs that teach length. And every row must be checked against your evaluations, because distilled and synthetic data launder benchmark items (section 6). On the samples, Ai2's clean-up filters remove 30 of 5,000 Tulu 3 SFT rows while an "obvious" English-only rule would remove 1,277, and a last-number checker accepts only 4,334 of 7,500 correct MATH solutions where math-verify accepts 7,485 (measured, {{Post-training data lab|#t-post}}). Operational know-how is a third kind of data beside instructions and traces, such as the AREX skill library distilled from 1,000 machine-learning repositories ({{Repo-To-Skill|n:3d45c17b0d0d818bacfeda8a40caddb2}}).
