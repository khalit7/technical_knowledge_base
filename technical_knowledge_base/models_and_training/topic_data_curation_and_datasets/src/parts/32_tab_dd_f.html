Across files and across crawls: what per-crawl dedup leaves

FineWeb deduplicated each crawl on its own and found that deduplicating all 96 crawls against each other made the model worse (the Reading tab's {{section 4|#t-read}} has the numbers and what they do and do not show). Two measurements on our file make the two scopes concrete.

1. Inside one crawl: what FineWeb's crawl-wide MinHash removed from our file

measured Joined on WARC-Record-ID with the 2,425 documents FineWeb kept from the same WARC file. Of our 2,692 filtered documents, the that we kept and FineWeb did not are where FineWeb's MinHash over all 90,000 files of CC-MAIN-2024-30 is the main suspect (other causes: FineWeb ran MinHash before its C4 and FineWeb rules, on a larger set, and its extraction differed on a few pages). Looking for their partners directly is a needle hunt: MinHash signatures of 50,000 random FineWeb rows from the rest of the crawl (0.02% of it) gave candidate pairs with our documents and inside the sample, as expected when each partner sits in one of 89,999 other files.

2. Across crawls: the same URLs in older crawls

For every one of the 2,692 documents we looked its URL up in the URL index of four earlier crawls, fetched the capture, extracted it the same way and measured the true Jaccard similarity with our version. A pair at 0.8 or more would be removed by a global MinHash; per-crawl dedup keeps both.

measured URL index read from data.commoncrawl.org (cluster.idx, then one block of the sorted CDX index per lookup, the same lookup the index server performs), URLs in Common Crawl's SURT form; newest HTML capture with status 200; Trafilatura with run0's settings (5 s timeout); 5-word shingles as in datatrove.

What this shows, and what it does not
It does not show that removing those copies would hurt or help a model: FineWeb's ablation is the evidence for that, at its scale. It also counts only same-URL recaptures; the same text under a new URL (a syndicated article, a moved page) is extra duplication that a URL lookup cannot see.
