In one screen

  • A filter that looks safe can delete a skill. Ai2's own clean-up filters for the Tulu 3 SFT mixture remove of our 5,000 SFT rows. The two "obvious" extra rules would remove far more, and they would remove the wrong rows: an English-only rule takes rows, of them from the deliberately multilingual Aya subset. A rule against refusal phrases takes rows, of them from the two safety subsets whose whole job is to teach refusals.
  • One preference pair in nine carries no preference. In UltraFeedback binarized, of 5,000 pairs give the chosen and the rejected answer the same judge score: the "rejected" answer was drawn at random from the other completions, and ties were allowed. In more pairs the two texts are identical. Chosen answers are also longer more often than not ( pairs): a model trained on these pairs can learn length.
  • A reward checker can make a prompt impossible to win. In Tulu 3's RLVR set, GSM8K labels are written with a thousands comma ("3,380"), while Tulu 3's checker strips commas from the answer before an exact string match, so no answer can score. Reference solutions show how checkers disagree: the last-number rule accepts of correct MATH solutions, math-verify .
  • Contamination hides at the level of one subject. The same RLVR set contains 70 MMLU test questions word for word (71 rows), all high-school maths, which is 0.50% of MMLU and so passes Tulu 3's 2% rule (measured by the {{Decontamination child page|n:3f95c17b0d0d8100b7e4e0f96074c309}}; the checker below re-scores its pairs).
  • Generating from a seed is not rephrasing. A Cosmopedia document keeps almost none of its seed web page's wording (median of its 4-word sequences are new, as long) and the FineWeb-Edu classifier's pass rate goes from . WRAP-style rephrasing with Qwen3-4B keeps the content and changes the form (section 5).
