Data curation is everything done to text before a model trains on it: finding it, turning it into clean text, throwing most of it away, removing copies, removing test questions, and deciding how much of each kind to show the model and when. This page teaches it from zero by following one slice of the raw web through a modern pipeline, measuring what each stage keeps and drops, and comparing it with what the published corpora kept. Reading time about 47 minutes (about 9,400 words outside the widgets at 200 a minute; the answers folded under "Common mistakes" and "Interview questions" add about 8).

The running example. Every lab tab measures on the same public data on one laptop, so numbers can be compared across tabs. The raw slice is one Common Crawl WARC file, the first of the July 2024 crawl (CC-MAIN-2024-30, 718.7 MB, 20,991 responses of which 20,463 HTML pages reach the pipeline), with its WET and WAT files; the Pipeline lab widens it to four files (81,738 HTML pages). It was chosen because FineWeb and FineWeb-Edu record the source WARC of every document, so the documents each kept from this exact file are known: 2,425 in FineWeb and 159 in FineWeb-Edu. DCLM-baseline only covers crawls before 2023, so a 50,000-document DCLM sample stands in for it and DCLM's own classifier is run on our pages; Nemotron-CC is represented by samples of each of its quality buckets from the same crawl. Seven benchmark test sets (MMLU, GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, HumanEval, MATH-500, HellaSwag) are the decontamination targets, and samples of Tulu 3's SFT mixture, UltraFeedback and Tulu 3's RLVR prompts stand for post-training data. The models are fastText lid.176 for language ID, the FineWeb-Edu classifier and DCLM's fastText classifier, all run on an Apple M1 Pro CPU; the full definition, with revisions and checksums, is in src/data/README.md. Numbers are labelled: measured means produced here in October 2026 on an Apple M1 Pro (CPU), self-reported a dataset's or method's makers evaluating their own work, third-party a comparison by someone without a stake in the result, derived computed from other numbers with the formula shown, and illustrative an input made up for teaching.

Post-training data (instructions, preference pairs, problems with checkers, environments) is built by a second, smaller pipeline with the same steps plus generation and verification: sections 8 and 9 and the {{Post-training data lab|#t-post}}. The real order varies (FineWeb deduplicates before its last heuristic filters; DCLM runs its quality model after dedup), but the principle is fixed: cheap stages run on everything, expensive ones only on what survives.

Four questions run through the page, one per goal: how do I build it (the stages and the real tools: datatrove, the Dolma toolkit, NeMo Curator), how does each part work inside (MinHash, suffix arrays, Bloom filters, fastText, the educational-value classifier), how do I know it worked (what a filter wrongly drops, how to read a curation paper's ablation table), and how do I explain it in a design interview (the trade-offs, with numbers). Each section ends with the lab tab that measures it.
