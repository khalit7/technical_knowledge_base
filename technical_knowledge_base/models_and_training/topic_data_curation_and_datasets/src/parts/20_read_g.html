8. Synthetic pretraining data: when the crawl runs out

The problem. The web is finite and the best of it is small: FineWeb-Edu's strict cut is 1.3 trillion tokens, and frontier runs train on 15 trillion or more. Once the token budget outgrows the good data, the choice is to repeat it (section 7: cheap up to about 4 times), keep lower-quality text, or manufacture tokens with a language model. Two lineages exist.

Generate from scratch

Prompt a model to write textbooks, exercises and stories. Microsoft's Phi models showed small models trained this way score well above their size ({{Textbooks Are All You Need|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644}}); the common criticism is a narrow style and skills shaped toward benchmarks. The open replication, Cosmopedia, has over 30 million documents written by Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, each prompt seeded with a real web page or curriculum topic, because diversity, not generation, is the hard part: without seeds, the model writes the same textbook again and again ({{Cosmopedia dataset card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/cosmopedia}}).

Rephrase real documents

Ask a model to rewrite an existing web page in a cleaner form (an encyclopedia-style article, question and answer pairs, a dialogue). The information and its diversity come from the real page; the model fixes the form. WRAP reported reaching the same quality with 5 times less data or 3 times less compute at its scale ({{arXiv 2401.16380|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16380}}); Nemotron-CC generated 1.9 trillion tokens this way with Mistral NeMo 12B, 336 billion from rewriting low-quality pages and 1.5 trillion of question-answer and other variants of high-quality ones; BeyondWeb (DatologyAI, a data-curation vendor) reports beating Cosmopedia by up to 5.1 points on a 14-benchmark average (self-reported). Synthetic text is now a standard ingredient: 57.5% of Olmo 3's 100-billion-token mid-training mix is labelled synthetic math, code, question-answer, reasoning-trace and instruction text (derived: 100% minus the four non-synthetic rows of the card's table) ({{Dolma 3 Dolmino card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/dolma3_dolmino_mix-100B-1025}}).

Generating is not rephrasing, measured. On a sample of Cosmopedia documents and the web pages that seeded them, a generated document keeps almost none of its seed's wording (a median 99% of its 4-word sequences are new) and is 3.6 times as long, and the FineWeb-Edu classifier passes 79 of 200 generated documents against 9 of their 200 seeds (measured, {{Post-training data lab|#t-post}}). Rephrasing should keep the content and change the form; the lab shows two web documents rewritten by Qwen3-4B in WRAP's styles from a CPU smoke test. PENDING GPU RUN (owned by the Synthetic data child page, its scripts/run_pending.sh, about 1.5 to 2 hours): 200 documents of the running example rephrased in WRAP's four styles; then quote the measured change in length, kept content and classifier score here.

Model collapse. Shumailov et al. showed that models trained recursively on their own output lose the tails of the distribution: rare things become rarer each generation until the model produces a narrow caricature of the original data ({{Nature, 2024|https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y}}). The experiment assumes each generation replaces the previous data. Gerstgrasser et al. repeated it with each generation's synthetic data added to the real data instead, and collapse disappeared across model sizes and architectures ({{arXiv 2404.01413|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413}}): for their 9M-parameter GPT-2, test loss went from 1.82 after the first iteration to 2.39 after four when replacing, and to 1.74 when accumulating (Table 2). The simplest case shows the mechanism:

Replace against accumulate: fitting a bell curve to its own samples, generation after generation

illustrative simulation run in your browser (seeded, so it is the same every time): generation 0 is 20 samples from a standard normal distribution; each generation fits a mean and standard deviation (maximum likelihood) and draws 20 new samples from the fit. Lines are the median over 400 runs of the fitted standard deviation, the width of what the "model" can produce. This is the one-dimensional case Shumailov et al. analyse, not a language model.

Real pipelines avoid collapse by construction: synthetic data is added to real data, grounded in real documents (rephrasing), and filtered. The live risks are milder: a uniform style, the generator's biases, and a web that increasingly contains unlabelled model-written text, so every crawl after 2023 already holds some synthetic data nobody chose to add.

9. Post-training data: instructions, preferences, checkers and environments

After pretraining, a model is trained on much smaller, more structured data. Each stage of post-training (explained on {{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO Family, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}}) consumes a different shape of example, and the history of the field is the shape getting richer: documents, then pairs, then problems with checkers, then whole environments.

Three lessons carry across the rows. Quality beats quantity for style, coverage needs volume: LIMA's 1,000 examples teach format and persona (its "superficial alignment" hypothesis: the knowledge is already in the base model), but Tulu 3 needed hundreds of thousands to cover math, code, safety and precise instruction following. Judges have biases that the data inherits: an LLM judge that prefers longer answers produces preference pairs that teach length. Every row must be checked against your evaluations, because distilled and synthetic data launder benchmark items (section 6). A third kind of data sits beside instructions and traces: operational know-how, such as the AREX skill library distilled from 1,000 machine-learning repositories ({{Repo-To-Skill|n:3d45c17b0d0d818bacfeda8a40caddb2}}).

What the samples show (measured, {{Post-training data lab|#t-post}}, open-instruct's own filters and checkers): Ai2's clean-up filters for the Tulu 3 SFT mixture remove 30 of 5,000 sampled rows, while an "obvious" English-only rule would remove 1,277, 912 of them from the deliberately multilingual Aya subset. In UltraFeedback binarized, 592 of 5,000 pairs (11.8%) give both answers the same judge score, so they carry no preference, and the chosen answer is the longer one in 2,764 pairs. In Tulu 3's RLVR set, 79 GSM8K labels are written with a thousands comma that Tulu 3's checker strips from answers, so those prompts can never be rewarded; and on correct reference solutions to MATH, a last-number checker accepts only 4,334 of 7,500 where math-verify accepts 7,485.
