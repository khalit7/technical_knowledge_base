MinHash and LSH, measured on every pair

The Reading tab's {{section 4|#t-read}} explains the four steps (shingles, Jaccard, the min-hash trick, bands and rows) and draws the S-curve for FineWeb's setting. Here the curve meets real pairs. Among 2,692 documents there are 3,622,086 pairs. For every one of them we computed the true Jaccard similarity of the shingle sets (a sparse matrix product, no estimate) and compared datatrove's 112 MinHash values position by position.

measured Shingles and signatures recomputed with datatrove's own MinhashDedupSignature code; all 37,688 stored signature values (2,692 documents by 14 bands) equal run0's signature files, and union-find over the candidates reproduces run0's 31 clusters and its 75 removals. Only 19,675 pairs share even one shingle; 856 pairs have a Jaccard of at least 0.05 or at least 2 equal MinHash values, and every other pair has at most one equal value, so it can never become a candidate when a band has 2 or more rows. Those 856 pairs, with their 112-position equality masks, are embedded in this page.

Does the estimate match the truth?

measured Each dot is one of the 856 pairs: true Jaccard against the fraction of the 112 MinHash values that agree. The band is ±2 standard deviations of a binomial estimate, 2√(J(1−J)/112). datatrove computes (shingles * a + b) % _mersenne_prime in unsigned 64-bit arithmetic (minhash.py:182), so the product wraps around before the modulus and the functions are not exactly the textbook universal hash; the dots show it does not matter in practice.

Bands and rows over the measured pairs

Move b (bands) and r (rows per band); the page splits the first b×r of the 112 values into consecutive bands, exactly as datatrove's np.split does (minhash.py:187), and checks every embedded pair for a band where all r values agree. Dots are the measured share of pairs that become candidates in each 0.05-wide Jaccard bin (area shows how many pairs); the line is 1 − (1 − sr)b.

Settings that need more hashes, measured on the same pairs

measured Signatures with 286 and 9,000 hash functions computed with datatrove's code (same shingles, same permutation generator, seed 1) for the 397 documents that appear in the 856 pairs; every other pair has Jaccard below 0.05, where these settings give a candidate probability below 10−12. Settings: published Dolma 3 pool README (allenai/dolma3 at 1a9daced, read 2026-10-14): web text used 26 bands of 11 (286 hashes) and then kept only pairs whose true Jaccard is at least 0.8; the same README says its PDF data used "the same parameters as FineWeb ... 14 bands of size 5", which is not FineWeb's 14 of 8. RefinedWeb (Penedo et al. 2023) used 9,000 hashes; its text says "20 buckets of 450", and only 450 bands of 20 rows reproduce its own published probabilities (76% at 0.75, 99.4% at 0.8). Read as written, 20 bands of 450 rows matches only byte-identical shingle sets: on our pairs it finds the 143 pairs with Jaccard 1 and nothing else.

"Removed" says which of the two run0 dropped.

What the labels say
FineWeb's 14 × 8 rarely pairs two different documents directly; the one D pair it does (0.65) is two shops' pages dominated by the same terms-of-service template. The larger risk is chaining: clusters are connected components (union-find in MinhashDedupCluster, minhash.py:537-556), so a page with two sentences of real text joins the banner cluster through intermediate banner pages and is dropped, although its own pairs with its D partner were never candidates. Dolma 3 adds a check of the true Jaccard similarity (0.8) between documents inside each cluster, which can break such chains.
