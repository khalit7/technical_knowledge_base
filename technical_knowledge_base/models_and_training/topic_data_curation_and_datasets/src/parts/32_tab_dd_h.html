Check yourself

Predict. On our 2,692 filtered documents, which kind of cluster is the biggest MinHash finds?
The banner: 25 documents from 25 sites whose extracted text is the same consent notice. Dedup is also an extraction check: big clusters of short documents usually mean the extractor kept boilerplate.
Predict. RefinedWeb's text says "20 buckets of 450" hashes. If you read that as 20 bands of 450 rows, which pairs become candidates?
Only identical sets: a band of 450 rows matches with probability s450, which is 10−44 at s = 0.8. Measured on our pairs: 143 candidates, all with Jaccard 1. 450 bands of 20 finds all 308 pairs at 0.8 or more.
Predict. On raw extracted text, suffix-array dedup at 100 bytes marks 17% of all bytes as repeated. If you keep one copy of each repeated span instead of deleting every copy, how much of that remains to remove?
Most repeated bytes are repeated many times, so the choice between "remove every copy" (the tool's default output) and "keep one" (Dolma 3) changes the result fourfold.

Common mistakes

Trusting cluster membership as similarity
MinHash clusters are connected components of candidate pairs; two members can be far apart. Check the true Jaccard of each member against the kept one (Dolma 3 checks at 0.8) or at least look at the largest clusters.
Copying band settings from prose
RefinedWeb's "20 buckets of 450" and Dolma 3's "the same parameters as FineWeb ... 14 bands of size 5" both disagree with the numbers they sit next to. Recompute the S-curve and check it reproduces the published probabilities.
Reading suffix-array output as "remove duplicates"
The ranges cover every copy. Deleting them deletes the text everywhere, including its first occurrence; decide whether you want that.
Setting an option without reading the branch it sits in
In Dolma 1.2.1, --dedupe.min_words turns deduplication off. Always run a tool on a sample where you know the right answer (here, an exact Python count) before running it on everything.
Measuring dedup on a single file and calling it the corpus rate
One WARC file shows 2.8% near-duplicates; FineWeb's per-crawl MinHash removed far more of the same file's documents, and across crawls more again. Duplication grows with the scope, and so does the cost.

Interview questions

How do you choose bands and rows for MinHash LSH?
Pick the similarity you care about, then b and r so the S-curve 1 − (1 − sr)b is steep there (threshold about (1/b)1/r); more rows make it sharper and lower recall, more bands raise recall and cost (signature size b×r). Then measure on labelled pairs from your data.
Exact, fuzzy, substring, semantic: when each?
Exact hashes first (cheap, removes the bulk at web scale: two thirds of Dolma 3's pool). MinHash for near-duplicate documents. Suffix arrays for boilerplate spans inside unique documents, with a minimum span length and a keep-one policy. Semantic dedup for paraphrase-level redundancy, mostly in synthetic or instruction data, and with a quality check because it removes real pages at loose thresholds.
Design dedup for a 100-crawl, 1010-document corpus.
Exact dedup by content hash first, sharded by hash. MinHash per crawl (or globally with counts kept, so mixing can decide repeats), signatures computed in parallel and grouped by band value with a shuffle, union-find on candidate pairs, a true-Jaccard check inside large clusters, keep the newest copy. Optional suffix-array pass per shard for boilerplate. Log what each step removes and sample it.
Why is a Bloom filter enough for exact dedup at scale?
Fixed memory (about 14 bits per item for 0.1% false positives), one streaming pass, no false negatives. The cost of a false positive is one unique item wrongly dropped, which at a rate of 10−3 is acceptable for paragraphs; measured here, 1 MiB for 487,620 distinct lines wrongly dropped 11 to 14.
