A contamination checker over real rows

The problem: a training row that contains a test question makes the test score a memory test. How it works: both texts are normalised (lower-case, punctuation to spaces; datatrove's simplify_text, numbers kept), cut into word n-grams, and compared. GPT-3 flagged any shared 13-gram; Tulu 3 flags a test item when more than 50% of its tokens sit inside 8-grams shared with one training row, and removes a training set that overlaps more than 2% of a benchmark ({{Tulu 3 paper, decontamination|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124}}). The rows and items below are real pairs that some rule flagged, labelled by reading on the {{Decontamination child page|n:3f95c17b0d0d8100b7e4e0f96074c309}}; the matching runs live here with that page's normalisation, so its numbers and these agree.

Predict: Tulu 3's rule removes a training set that overlaps more than 2% of a benchmark's items. Would it remove the RLVR set for MMLU, given the 70 copied questions?
No. The threshold is per benchmark, and MMLU has 14,042 test items, so 70 copies are 0.50%. Inside the one subject they come from, high-school mathematics, they are 26% of the items. A per-row filter (drop any training row that covers a test item) would have removed all 71 rows; a per-dataset threshold keeps them all.
Counts from the Decontamination child page's scan (its scripts/dg_contam.py and hand labels), 29,615 benchmark items: MMLU, GSM8K, ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, HumanEval, MATH-500, HellaSwag. Labels are an agent's reading, not a human annotator's.
