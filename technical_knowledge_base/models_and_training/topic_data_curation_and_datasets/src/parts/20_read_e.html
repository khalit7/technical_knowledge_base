Interview rehearsal: the design question that spans every stage

Design the data pipeline for a 2-trillion-token pretraining run. Clarify: model size and token budget (2T unique or 2T seen?), languages, domains (code, maths), licence constraints, and which evaluations matter. Sources: all available Common Crawl crawls (WARC), plus code, papers or PDFs, and maths. Stages, cheapest first, each writing what it drops: URL blocklist, extraction (resiliparse for throughput or trafilatura for precision), language ID, Gopher, C4 and FineWeb rules, MinHash dedup per crawl (5-grams, 14 bands of 8), then a quality model (a fastText classifier is cheap; an encoder distilled from LLM grades is better and costs GPU hours), decontamination against the evaluation suite, PII. The numbers: the calculator in section 11 shows that a strict top-10% cut cannot supply 2T unique tokens from the crawls that exist, so either relax the threshold, add rephrased lower tiers (Nemotron-CC), add other sources, or repeat the best data up to about 4 times. Mixing and staging: fit domain weights on small proxies, keep the best data for a mid-training stage, check candidate datasets by annealing runs. Infrastructure: stateless per-shard tasks (datatrove on Slurm or Ray), object storage, Parquet with metadata so filters can be re-run, tokenized shards for training, and a held-out slice for loss monitoring. Validation: ablate each stage on a 1 to 2B proxy, read stratified samples of what each stage drops, and track contamination.

How do you know whether a new dataset helps? Ablate: train identical small proxies with and without it at a fixed budget, on benchmarks with low seed variance; or anneal a partly trained model on a short run where the dataset is a fixed share (Llama 3: 30% of 40B tokens) and compare against the same run without it.

The per-stage questions (MinHash bands and rows, choosing a dedup method, building a quality classifier, decontamination failure modes, CPT ratios, synthetic-data risk, a good RLVR prompt set) are rehearsed on the child that owns each stage.
