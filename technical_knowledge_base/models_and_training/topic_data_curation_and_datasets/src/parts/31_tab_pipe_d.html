4. Language ID: what sits on the 0.65 line

The problem. An English corpus must drop other languages, and the crawl does not label them reliably. How it works. fastText's lid.176 is a linear classifier over averaged character n-gram embeddings: it turns the text (newlines replaced by spaces, utils/lid.py:54) into a probability for each of 176 languages. datatrove keeps a document when the English probability exceeds 0.65 (filters/language_filter.py:61-65), FineWeb's threshold. Cost: microseconds per document on a CPU, which is why it runs before every rule. How to check it: read what lands near the threshold.

What the boundary is made of

Every page shown between 0.52 and 0.82 (a random sample of organisation pages, seed 0) is in English. What lowers the score is not another language but text that is not prose: menus, search facets, data tables, a PHP error log, OCR of a scanned newspaper. lid.176 was trained on running text (Wikipedia, Tatoeba and SETimes, per the fastText language-identification page), so a list of short labels looks less like any language. The 0.65 threshold therefore also acts as a weak quality filter, and the rule filters after it remove most of these pages anyway.

6. Right and wrong drops, labelled

A filter's drop count says nothing about whether it drops the right documents. The only check is to read a sample. For every rule that removed documents, 8 documents it removed were drawn at random (seed 0; all of them when fewer), plus 24 documents the whole chain kept, and each was labelled from its first 360 to 700 characters. Labels are committed in src/pipe/labels/drops.json. Eight per rule is enough to see a pattern, not to estimate a rate: treat one wrong drop in eight as "happens", six in eight as "the rule is broken at this threshold".
