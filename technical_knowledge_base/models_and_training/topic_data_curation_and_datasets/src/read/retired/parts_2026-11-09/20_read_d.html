10. Storage, loading and the tools that run it

A corpus has two lives in two formats. While curating, one record per document with its metadata (URL, crawl, language and quality scores, duplicate-cluster id) in compressed JSONL or Parquet (columnar: a reader loads only the columns it needs); FineWeb ships as Parquet, DCLM-baseline as zstd JSONL. For training, documents are tokenized once into flat arrays of token ids (2 bytes per token below a 65,536-entry vocabulary, 4 above: storing Qwen3's ids as 16-bit integers would corrupt 97% of FineWeb documents), memory-mapped or packed into streaming shards (MosaicML's MDS, WebDataset's tar files); the loader samples domains with the weights of section 7, packs documents into fixed-length windows, shuffles approximately, and must resume exactly after a crash. When the weights are applied at load time rather than baked into the files, changing the mix needs no reprocessing.

The tools. Three open frameworks run whole pipelines; teams with big-data infrastructure also write their own stages on Spark or Ray.

11. Choosing: what to do for the run in front of you

A small pretrain (up to about 2B parameters, a few hundred billion tokens)Use an existing corpus: FineWeb-Edu at threshold 3 (1.3 trillion tokens, so nothing repeats), or SmolLM2's 60/40 mix of FineWeb-Edu and DCLM-baseline if commonsense and everyday-language benchmarks matter. Add a few percent of code and maths, mostly in the last stage. Your remaining jobs are decontamination against your own evaluations and ablating any change on a small proxy.SmolLM2 trained its 135M and 360M models on 2 and 4 trillion tokens: small models are routinely trained far past the compute-optimal point.
A long run (trillions of tokens)The binding constraint is unique good tokens. Do not keep only the top 10%: bucket by quality, keep the middle tiers for bulk, rephrase the low ones, add PDFs and code, repeat the best data up to about 4 times, and save the best for a mid-training stage (Nemotron-CC, Dolma 3).
Continued pretraining on a domainMix general data back in; fit the replay ratio with a few short probe runs (CMR, section 7 and {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}}, section 6) instead of folklore; re-fit when the base model or the budget changes.
Your own pipeline from raw crawlStart from datatrove's FineWeb example (WARC, trafilatura with deduplicate=False, language ID with the language set explicitly, Gopher, C4 and FineWeb rules, MinHash per crawl, PII), check its thresholds against the paper (and swap in resiliparse, which datatrove v0.10.1 does not ship as an extractor, if extraction cost dominates), then ablate one change at a time on small proxies (FineWeb used 1.71B models on 28B tokens).
Post-training dataStart from an open mixture (Tulu 3, SmolTalk) for coverage; add targeted data for your weak skills; generate preference pairs on-policy; keep only RLVR prompts your model sometimes solves; decontaminate every source.
A new languagePer-language pipelines and thresholds (FineWeb2 tunes them per language), language ID with a model that covers it well, and temperature sampling so small languages are not drowned.

Mistakes that only show across stages (nine)

Training on Common Crawl's WET text. It keeps menus and footers, documents are about twice as long as the real content, and models score worse (DCLM Table 3: 20.7 against 24.1 Core). Re-extract from WARC.

Copying a paper's thresholds, or trusting a library's defaults, without reading the code. FineWeb's paper says 0.1 for the duplicated-line rule and datatrove's default is 0.01; RefinedWeb's prose says 20 buckets of 450 hashes and only 450 bands of 20 reproduce its probabilities; datatrove's C4 filter swaps C4's two numbers. Set every parameter explicitly, log it, and read what a sample run drops.

Reading an ablation without its setup. Check the model size, tokens, benchmarks and seeds before believing a gap. Small proxies can only use benchmarks that already move at small scale (FineWeb chose its eight for low seed variance and early signal), so effects on harder skills go unmeasured; without a seed-to-seed spread, a one-point gap may be noise; a dataset's makers choose its benchmarks.

Assuming more dedup is always better. FineWeb's global dedup kept the worst copies of the oldest crawl; Muennighoff et al. saw no downstream gain from dedup in their setting. Deduplicate to save compute and avoid memorisation, then decide how much to repeat on purpose.

Treating a quality score as ground truth. Each classifier encodes its reference set's taste. Read a stratified sample of what it drops.

Decontaminating too little, too much or too late. 13-grams miss paraphrases; short n-grams remove ordinary sentences; decontaminate before training against every evaluation you will report, and record what you removed.

Comparing token counts from different tokenizers. C4 is 175B tokens in the Dolma paper's count; The Pile is 387B there and about 300B in GPT-NeoX tokens. Say whose tokens, or compare bytes.

Over-filtering for a long run, or under-filtering for a short one. A strict cut is right when you will not exhaust it and wrong when you would repeat it 10 times; the calculator shows when that happens.

Reading the CMR ratio as the share of general data. The critical mixture ratio is the largest share of domain data (29.8% to 47.8% in the paper's finance runs), not the general share. An old version of this page had it backwards.
