With more documents, best-fit packing finds better fits: its extra windows over concatenation fall from 0.14% (2,000 documents, L = 2,048) to 0.06%; Ding et al. report at most 0.01% on RefinedWeb and The Stack at their scale.

Reading the attention numbers
"Cross-document pairs" is the share of the causal (query, key) pairs inside windows whose query and key belong to different documents. Without a mask the model computes and learns from them; with a document mask they are excluded, and a variable-length attention kernel does not compute them at all, so the same share is also the attention-score work the mask saves (the rest of the model's FLOPs are unchanged). Truncation and padding have no cross-document pairs, at the cost shown in the table.
