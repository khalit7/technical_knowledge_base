8. Opt-outs: how a site says "not for training"

Not legal advice. This section reports what the standards, vendor documents, statutes and court summaries say, with their dates. Which of them binds a given project is a question for a lawyer. The licence of each corpus, the share of robots.txt files blocking AI crawlers across crawls, and the court cases (Bartz v. Anthropic, Thomson Reuters v. Ross, GEMA v. OpenAI, Getty v. Stability) are on {{Pretraining corpora|n:3c65c17b0d0d81f382b6e75707d00663}}; this section is the mechanics a data engineer has to implement.

robots.txt. A site's /robots.txt lists groups of rules, each for one or more crawler names (user-agent tokens), each rule allowing or disallowing a path prefix. It was standardised as RFC 9309 in September 2022: the most specific (longest) matching rule wins, allow wins a tie, a missing file (4xx) means everything is allowed, an unreachable one (5xx) means "the crawler MUST assume complete disallow", and the rules "are not a form of access authorization". It has no notion of purpose; purpose is expressed by which token you block. In the illustrative file below, GPTBot may fetch nothing; CCBot matches no named group and falls to *, so it may fetch /private/press/x (the longer Allow wins) but not /private/x.

User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /

Disallow: /private/
Allow: /private/press/

The time problem. A robots.txt rule applies when the page is fetched. Common Crawl obeys CCBot rules at crawl time, so everything in a crawl was allowed for CCBot then. A site that later blocks GPTBot or ClaudeBot has expressed a wish about AI training, but the old crawls, and every corpus built from them (FineWeb, DCLM, Nemotron-CC), still contain its pages. Honouring that wish means applying today's robots.txt to yesterday's data, which Apertus did for 2013 to 2024 crawls with January 2025 files, at a cost of about 8% of English tokens; GPTBot was the most-blocked token, removing 1,772,197 FineWeb documents on its own (Apertus, Section 3.1.1 and Table B.2). Fan et al. trained 1.5B models on FineWeb-Edu with and without that filtering (8% fewer tokens) and found "close to 0%" difference on general knowledge, with a gap only in specialised domains such as biomedicine after long training (arXiv 2504.06219). Decide whether you apply current signals retroactively, and record the decision with the data version.

Other machine-readable signals exist because robots.txt can only say "do not fetch", not "fetch for search but do not train":

  • noai / noimageai meta tags and X-Robots-Tag headers, introduced by DeviantArt in November 2022; not part of any standard.
  • ai.txt (Spawning, May 2023), a robots.txt-like file read when media is downloaded; named by the EDPB as an example exclusion mechanism (Opinion 28/2024, para 105), not documented by the major crawlers.
  • TDMRep (W3C Community Group report, 10 May 2024): tdm-reservation: 1 as an HTTP header, an HTML meta tag or /.well-known/tdmrep.json, built for the EU text-and-data-mining opt-out (report).
  • IETF AI Preferences (working group since 7 January 2025): a vocabulary (train-ai, ai-use, search, each y or n) attached by a Content-Usage HTTP header or a Content-Usage: line in robots.txt. Both are still drafts on 2026-10-14 (vocab-08, attach-05); the August 2026 milestone passed (datatracker).
  • Cloudflare Content Signals (24 September 2025): Content-Signal: search=yes, ai-train=no in robots.txt, added to the managed robots.txt of over 3.8 million domains (announcement). RSL (Really Simple Licensing, 1.0 in December 2025) points robots.txt at a machine-readable licence with payment terms (spec).

Why the EU cares about machine-readable signals. The EU's text-and-data-mining exception lets anyone mine lawfully accessible content unless the rightholder has reserved it "in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means" (DSM Directive Art. 4(3)). The AI Act then requires providers of general-purpose models to "identify and comply with ... a reservation of rights" and to publish "a sufficiently detailed summary about the content used for training" (Art. 53(1)(c), (d)), applying from 2 August 2025 with fines from 2 August 2026. For the engineer the consequence is concrete: record, per document, which signals were present and which you honoured, because the summary you may have to publish is computed from that record.

The Commission's template, the Code of Practice and Kneschke v. LAION (what the summary asks for; one court's reading of "machine-readable")

The Commission's template (24 July 2025) asks for the top 10% of scraped domain names by content size and the opt-out protocols honoured, not item-level lists (template); the voluntary Code of Practice commits signatories to crawlers that follow RFC 9309 and to excluding listed piracy sites (Measures 1.2 and 1.3). In Germany the Hamburg courts held in Kneschke v. LAION that building a training dataset was permitted scientific mining; on appeal (10 December 2025) a reservation written only in natural language in a site's terms was not treated as machine-readable, with a further appeal allowed (secondary reports: DLA Piper).

9. Keeping records: documentation and provenance

Per-document provenance is what makes every other duty on this page possible. The minimum a curated row needs: a stable id from the source (the WARC record id), the source location (crawl, WARC file, URL), the fetch date, the licence or terms of the source, the pipeline version and the decisions taken on it (filter scores, dedup cluster, decontamination and PII actions, opt-out signals present and honoured). With those fields a removal request becomes a join; without them it becomes a re-crawl. The fields are a few dozen bytes per document against kilobytes of text. Public corpora keep these fields to different degrees: FineWeb rows carry id, dump, url, date and file_path; Nemotron-CC carries warc_record_id and url; Dolma stores tagger outputs as separate "attributes" files aligned row for row, so filters can be re-run without copying text; Common Pile rows carry a per-document licence (FineWeb card, Dolma data format). The Stack v2's opt-out removed 1,561 repositories from 91 owners, 22,066 files, by exactly such a join (StarCoder2, Section 3.5).

Why it matters is measurable: the Data Provenance Initiative audited 1,858 fine-tuning datasets and found licences "Unspecified" for 72% (GitHub), 69% (Hugging Face) and 70% (Papers with Code) of them on the aggregators, and 66% of the Hugging Face licences it analysed in a different use category than the authors intended, "often labeled as more permissive" (Longpre et al. 2023). A post-training mixture built from such sets inherits the most restrictive of them.
