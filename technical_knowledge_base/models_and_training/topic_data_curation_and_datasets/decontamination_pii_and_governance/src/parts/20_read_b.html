1. Contamination, and the six things a "match" can be

A benchmark's test split is meant to be unseen, so its score estimates how a model does on new questions. Benchmarks are published on the web, copied into study sites, forums and repositories, and from there reach Common Crawl; a model trained on a test item can answer it from memory. The data engineer controls the training set and can search it directly (this page); someone scoring a model they did not train can only probe the model ({{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}} owns that side, section 5 here covers what the training side borrows from it).

How much it moves a score ranges from a lot (Llama 2 13B fine-tuned on rephrased MMLU test items: 54.8 to 85.9) to nothing (DCLM removed its MMLU overlaps and its score went up slightly). You cannot know in advance which case you are in, which is why labs decontaminate anyway. The evidence, and why it varies with copies seen, model size and whether the answer was included, is on {{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}} (its "Contamination and score inflation" section), which also reads scores in its light.

Not all overlap is the same thing. What a detector reports as "contaminated" is one of six situations, and they need different responses. These categories label every flagged pair on this page, and they are the reason a rule's raw count means little.

KindWhat the training document containsExample measured hereHarm
copyThe test question itself, often with its answerAn MMLU high-school maths question, word for word, as a Tulu 3 RLVR prompt with its answer keyHigh: this is what decontamination is for
variantThe same problem with other numbers, or the same setup with a different questionA MATH training problem and a MATH-500 test problem sharing a diagram and an opening, asking different thingsSome: the method is learnt, not the answer
sourceThe text the item was built fromThe WikiHow article a HellaSwag item was cut from, including the true endingReal for completion tasks
passageA famous text the item quotesKennedy's moon speech, the Pearl Harbor address, a Dickens opening, in MMLU history itemsLittle: knowing the speech is not knowing the question
phraseA common phrase or instruction the item happens to use"which of the following is not a symptom of", "express your answer as a common fraction", a primality-test loopNone: a false positive
diagramShared plotting codeThe Asymptote axis preamble many MATH problems shareNone

"variant", "source" and "passage" follow the spirit of PaLM's four categories ("wholesale contamination", "constructed from web", "context on web, not contaminated", "no significant overlap"; PaLM, Section 8) and GPT-3's finding that most reading-comprehension overlap was the source passage, not the question (GPT-3, Section 4).

The published evidence for how much contamination inflates a score (deliberate rephrased copies, accidental overlap measured after the fact, and the many cases where it changed nothing, with why the size varies) is on {{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}, in its "Contamination and score inflation" section.
