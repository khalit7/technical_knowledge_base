1. Contamination, and the six things a "match" can be

A benchmark's test split is meant to be unseen, so its score estimates how a model does on new questions. Benchmarks are published on the web, copied into study sites, forums and repositories, and from there reach Common Crawl; a model trained on a test item can answer it from memory. The data engineer controls the training set and can search it directly (this page); someone scoring a model they did not train can only probe the model ({{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}} owns that side, section 5 here covers what the training side borrows from it).

How much it moves a score ranges from a lot (Llama 2 13B fine-tuned on rephrased MMLU test items: 54.8 to 85.9) to nothing (DCLM removed its MMLU overlaps and its score went up slightly). You cannot know in advance which case you are in, which is why labs decontaminate anyway. The evidence, and why it varies with copies seen, model size and whether the answer was included, is in the closed block below; how evaluators read scores in its light is the benchmarks topic's.

Not all overlap is the same thing. What a detector reports as "contaminated" is one of six situations, and they need different responses. These categories label every flagged pair on this page, and they are the reason a rule's raw count means little.

"variant", "source" and "passage" follow the spirit of PaLM's four categories ("wholesale contamination", "constructed from web", "context on web, not contaminated", "no significant overlap"; PaLM, Section 8) and GPT-3's finding that most reading-comprehension overlap was the source passage, not the question (GPT-3, Section 4).

How much contamination inflates a score: the published evidence (the benchmarks topic reads scores with it)
  • Deliberate, the upper bound. Yang et al. fine-tuned Llama 2 13B on rephrased copies of the MMLU test set (16 epochs) and it scored 85.9 on MMLU against 54.8 before; on rephrased GSM8K it went from 28.7 to 95.3 (arXiv 2311.04850, Tables 2 and 4, 2023). A 13-gram check found none of the rephrased items (Table 5: F1 0).
  • Accidental, measured after the fact. GPT-3's own analysis flagged 29% of PIQA and 45% of Winograd as overlapping its training data and found the clean subsets 3 and 2.6 points lower, so those two results carry an asterisk (GPT-3, Section 4). Llama 2 concluded that only HellaSwag and MMLU-Humanities had been boosted: the 70B model scored 92.2 on HellaSwag's "dirty" items against 80.0 on its clean ones (Llama 2, Appendix A.6, Table 51). Llama 3 estimated gains of 14 to 15 points on HellaSwag and 8 points on PiQA from contamination at all three sizes (Llama 3, Table 15).
  • Often none. GPT-3 also found that "in most cases performance changes only negligibly" even where overlap was high, and PaLM saw roughly as many positive as negative clean-minus-full deltas (PaLM, Section 8, Table 18). DCLM removed the MMLU and HellaSwag overlaps it detected and its 7B model's scores went up slightly (MMLU 51.8 to 52.7; DCLM, Table 7). Magar and Schwartz showed in a controlled BERT study that "memorization does not guarantee exploitation" (arXiv 2203.08242).

The size depends on how many copies the model saw, how big the model is (Singh et al. found larger models exploit it more; arXiv 2411.03923) and whether the copy includes the answer.
