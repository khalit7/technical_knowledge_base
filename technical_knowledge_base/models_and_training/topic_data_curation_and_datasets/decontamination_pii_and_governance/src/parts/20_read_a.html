The idea in one screen

A cleaned corpus owes three more checks before it trains a model: keep the test sets out, keep personal data out, and keep a record of every document (source, terms, what was done to it). The first two are filters with both errors at once, so a rule is judged by reading what it removed; the third is what makes opt-outs, removals and audits a join instead of a re-crawl.

Three checks between the cleaned corpus and the training set: each catches something real and brings a cost

Numbers measured on this page's running example (sections 3, 7 and 9). Orange: what the check caught or made possible (a test question in training data, an identifier found, a document traced to its source); grey: what comes with it (innocent text flagged, false alarms, kept pages whose sites now refuse AI training).

Contamination
evaluation data (or something close enough to help) inside the training data; decontamination finds and removes it before training.
n-gram, coverage
a run of n consecutive words; coverage is the share of a test item's tokens that sit inside n-grams a document shares.
Canary string
a unique string a benchmark ships so that corpus builders can drop any document containing it.
Membership inference
guessing from a model's losses whether a text was in its training data.
PII, personal data
information that identifies a person; direct identifiers do it alone (an email), quasi-identifiers only in combination (ZIP code, sex, birth date).
NER
named-entity recognition: a model that labels spans as PERSON, LOCATION and so on from context.
User-agent token
the crawler name a robots.txt rule addresses (CCBot, GPTBot, ClaudeBot).
Provenance
per-document fields linking a training row back to its source: id, URL, crawl, date, terms, decisions.
