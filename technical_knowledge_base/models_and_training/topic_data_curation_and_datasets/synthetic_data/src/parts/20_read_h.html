Depth: mistakes that teach, and a design question

Common mistakes the sections above do not already show (depth)
  • Comparing distinct-n across samples of different sizes or domains. The ratio falls as the sample grows, and math or code repeats symbols by nature. Fix the word budget (100,000 words here) and compare within a domain.
  • Skipping decontamination because "the text is new". Generators reproduce benchmark items they memorised, and exercise-style data resembles benchmarks by design (phi-1's own analysis; Cosmopedia removed hundreds of matching documents). Run n-gram matching against every evaluation set, and for skill-targeted data retrain without the nearest items as phi-1 did.
  • Citing model collapse against all synthetic data. The famous experiment fine-tunes a 125M model on its own beam-search output and throws the real data away each generation. Accumulating, grounding and verifying, which is what labs do, avoids it (Gerstgrasser et al., Feng et al.). The real risks are narrower: lost tails and style, and unlabelled model text in new crawls.
  • Using a bigger generator than needed. For rephrasing, gains flatten above 1B to 8B parameters in three studies (BeyondWeb, Kang et al., FinePhrase), and the cost per token grows with size. Spend the budget on prompts and seeds.
  • Losing track of which model wrote what. Without the generator, prompt and seed id on every row, a licence change or a bad prompt cannot be removed later.
Design questions: a synthetic stage for a 10-trillion-token run, and a suspicious GSM8K gain (interview rehearsal)

Build the synthetic-data stage for a 10-trillion-token pretraining run. Goal and budget: say 30% synthetic, 3T tokens, repeated at most 4 times, so about 750B unique synthetic tokens. Seeds: the quality buckets from the web pipeline (rewrite low and medium, transform high), deduplicated, plus topic lists for math and code. Prompts: five to ten formats, piloted on 1,000 outputs each and measured. Generation: a 4B to 8B Apache-2.0 model on an inference server; at about 10 million tokens per GPU-hour (between the WRAP and FinePhrase rates) 750B tokens is about 75,000 GPU-hours, so a few days on 1,000 GPUs, sharded by seed id and resumable. Post-process and verify: preambles, length, refusals; execution for code, answer checks for math, fidelity checks for rephrasing. Dedup and decontaminate against the real corpus and every eval. Mix: sweep the share on 1B proxies, keep all real data, more synthetic in the final stage. Provenance: generator, prompt, seed id on every row. Trade-offs to name: generator size against cost, diversity against quality filtering, synthetic share against knowledge benchmarks, licences.

A synthetic math set lifts GSM8K by 15 points. What do you check before believing it? Contamination: n-gram and embedding matches between the set and GSM8K test items, and the generator's own exposure to the test set; retrain without the nearest items (phi-1's method). Then a held-out or fresh benchmark (phi-4 used the November 2024 AMC), several seeds, and whether general benchmarks dropped.
