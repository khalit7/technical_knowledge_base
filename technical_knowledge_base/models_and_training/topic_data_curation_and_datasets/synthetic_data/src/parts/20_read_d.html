3. Rephrasing real documents

The idea. Instead of asking the generator to know things, give it a real web page and ask it to rewrite the page in a better form. The facts, topics and variety come from the web; the generator removes menus, fixes grammar and changes the register. This is called rephrasing (or rewriting); when the output has a new structure (questions and answers, a list of facts) it is a transformation.

WRAP: four styles, half and half with real text

Maini et al. (Apple and CMU, January 2024; ACL 2024) rephrased C4 with a frozen Mistral-7B-Instruct in four styles and trained 128M to 1.3B models on real and rephrased text sampled 1:1 ({{Rephrasing the Web, arXiv 2401.16380|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16380}}). The prompts (Appendix G, verbatim) are short:

StylePrompt (each preceded by a Vicuna-style system line)
Easy"For the following paragraph give me a paraphrase of the same using a very small vocabulary and extremely simple sentences that a toddler will understand:"
Medium"For the following paragraph give me a diverse paraphrase of the same in high quality English language as in sentences on Wikipedia:"
Hard"For the following paragraph give me a paraphrase of the same using very terse and abstruse language that only an erudite scholar will understand. Replace simple words and phrases with rare and complex ones:"
Q/A"Convert the following paragraph into a conversational format with multiple tags of "Question:" followed by "Answer:":"

Outputs were capped at 300 tokens because rephrasing longer text "often lost information". Results, read from the tables (averages of 3 runs, about 1.3B parameters, self-reported):

WRAP zero-shot averages, Tables 1 and 2 (1.3B models)

Half C4 is 85B tokens, Full C4 170B, RW is RefinedWeb, Synthetic is 85B tokens of Medium plus Q/A rephrases, Synthetic+C4 mixes them with real C4. On the specialised tasks MMLU sits at 24 to 25 for every row, which is chance for four options: these models are too small to read it.

The paper disagrees with itself; quote the tables
  • Pile perplexity: the abstract says it "improves perplexity by more than 10% on average", the introduction and Section 4 say "~50%". Both come from figures with no table, so neither can be checked to the decimal.
  • The prose credits "the smaller C4 (85B) dataset" with 43.5% on the specialised tasks; in Table 2, 43.5 is Full C4 (170B) and Half C4 is 43.1.
  • "3x faster" (abstract) and "5x lesser data, or 3x lesser compute" (introduction) are read off Figure 1, again without a table.

The finding that has held up since is in WRAP's own discussion: "synthetic data can not impart 'new knowledge'. It can only help pre-train faster". Two more: a 1.8B rephraser (Qwen-1.8B-chat) gave rephrases "of high quality" comparable to Mistral-7B at about three times the throughput, and synthetic-only training lost perplexity on domains whose style the rephrases removed (Philpapers, Gutenberg), so the real half of the mix is not optional.

Nemotron-CC: five prompts at 1.9 trillion tokens

NVIDIA's Nemotron-CC (December 2024) applied rephrasing at corpus scale ({{arXiv 2412.02595|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02595}}). Documents had already been sorted into quality buckets by an ensemble of classifiers (the {{Quality classifiers|n:3f95c17b0d0d810baaabe41da6c9c50f}} page). Low-quality documents were rewritten Wikipedia-style with WRAP's prompt, so text that would otherwise be thrown away becomes usable. High-quality documents were turned into four additional forms: diverse question-answer pairs, a condensed "distilled" version, an "extract knowledge" rewrite and a knowledge list. Generator: Mistral NeMo 12B Instruct in FP8, temperature 0.5, top-p 0.9. Medium-quality documents were not rephrased "due to time and resource constraints". Each prompt type gets its own segment length (footnote 11: "512 for Wikipedia, 2,000 for Distill, 1,400 for Extract Knowledge and 1,000 for Diverse QA Pairs and Knowledge List, including tokens from the prompt"), because longer inputs gave "over-simplified outputs with reduced detail". Post-processing drops incomplete outputs and anything under 50 tokens, strips Markdown and preambles such as "Here is a paraphrased version:", and for question-answer output shuffles the pairs and appends a number of them after the source segment.

NVIDIA published the synthetic rows with the URL and WARC record id of the page each came from, so the original can be fetched from Common Crawl and compared. Look at the question-answer row: the one appended question asks which event is "NOT a real campus event mentioned in the text" and offers a "Spring Fling" that the text never mentions either, so the keyed answer is not supported by the passage. Transformations make plausible-looking errors, and at 500 billion tokens of question-answer pairs nobody reads them. What the 5 pairs show in numbers is on the {{Rephrasing lab|#t-syre}}.

The paper's ablations (8B models, 1 trillion tokens, average of 10 tasks, self-reported, Table 10): rewriting the low-quality bucket lifted the average from 52.5 to 54.0 (MMLU fell from 48.2 to 47.1); replacing half of the repeats of high-quality data with its synthetic variants lifted it from 55.8 to 56.7 (MMLU 53.4 to 53.6). Table 3 counts the output: 336.3 billion tokens from low-quality pages, and from high-quality pages 372.9B Wikipedia-style, 499.5B diverse QA, 157.6B distill, 303.6B extract knowledge and 203.2B knowledge lists, about 1.87 trillion in total (the abstract rounds to 1.9). Nemotron-CC v2 (August 2025) switched the generator to Qwen3-30B-A3B and translated the question-answer data into 15 languages ({{card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-CC-v2}}).

Where the evidence agrees and where it does not

Agreed: rephrased text mixed with real text beats real text alone at equal tokens; rephrased text alone does not; small generators (1B to 8B) are enough, bigger ones buy little (BeyondWeb Figure 9: 47.3, 48.8, 49.2 for 1B, 3B, 8B generators; Kang et al.; FinePhrase). Not agreed: the best synthetic share. Kang et al. find about 30% by validation loss; FinePhrase finds 60 to 80% by benchmark average; WRAP used 50%. They differ in generator, prompts, metric and scale, so the share is something to sweep for your own run, not to copy.
