4. Math and code: synthesis with a checker

Math and code are where synthetic data is easiest to trust, because an executor or an answer checker can reject wrong output. The clearest evidence that the form matters, not only the facts, is MegaMath's ablation: the same math web pages, trained on as they are or rewritten into questions and answers.

Three patterns recur:

  • Generate, execute, keep what passes. phi-1's exercises (section 2); OpenCoder's annealing data (2.71 billion tokens of synthetic code snippets kept "only if they pass generated tests" and 0.91 billion of code textbooks, beside 96 billion real tokens, Table 3, {{arXiv 2411.04905|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04905}}); MegaMath's interleaved text and code blocks, generated, run and packed (50.3 billion tokens).
  • Rewrite the web into problems and solutions. MegaMath (LLM360, April 2025) did this with Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct; its Table 1 lists 64.5 billion synthetic tokens out of 371.6 billion (chart above). MAmmoTH2's WebInstruct harvested 10 million question-answer pairs that already existed on the web (recall, extract, refine with open models), taking Mistral-7B from 11% to 36.7% on MATH without in-domain training data ({{arXiv 2405.03548|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03548}}). self-reported
  • Translate and restyle code. NVIDIA rewrote all its Python with Qwen3 32B using style-guided and self-contained rewriting prompts, checked with syntax checks and Pylint, and transpiled Python into C++, which "improved downstream C++ code-generation accuracy" (Nemotron 3 Nano report, Section 2.2.2, {{arXiv 2512.20848|https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20848}}; data in {{Nemotron-Pretraining-Code-v2|https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-Pretraining-Code-v2}}, gated).

A caution for section 6: MegaMath's text-and-code blocks have the lowest distinct-bigram ratio (@@mm_tcb_d2@@) and the highest compression ratio (@@mm_tcb_gz@@) of all 24 sets measured here, and its question-answer set is next to them. Math and code repeat symbols and keywords by nature, so diversity numbers are only comparable within a domain.

5. How much synthetic text to mix in

Every published recipe keeps real text in the mix, and the shares vary widely. Keep apart what a recipe used (one team's choice for one run) and what an ablation found best (a sweep, under that paper's metric). Qwen3's report says Qwen2.5 models synthesised "trillions of text tokens" of textbooks, question-answering, instructions and code ({{arXiv 2505.09388|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388}}; no exact count), so not every lab can be placed on the chart.

Recipes use a third to a half; sweeps disagree on the best share

Blue: what a recipe used. Green: what an ablation found best. Sources and arithmetic:

  • phi-1.5 pretraining 80%: of 150B training tokens (Section 2.3, arXiv 2309.05463).
  • Olmo 3 mid-training (Dolmino 100B) 57.5%: derived, 100% minus the four non-synthetic rows of the Dolma 3 Dolmino card table (parent page, section 8); its mix is largely synthetic math, code and question-answer text.
  • phi-4 pretraining 55%: derived, synthetic 40% + web rewrites 15% of about 10 trillion tokens for a 14B model (Table 5, arXiv 2412.08905).
  • WRAP experiments 50%: real and rephrased sampled 1:1 (Section 3.1, arXiv 2401.16380).
  • Nemotron-CC v2 42.7%: derived, (1,257.3 + 692.9 + 558.2) / (3,359.8 + 2,508.4) billion tokens, card table (nvidia/Nemotron-CC-v2).
  • BeyondWeb experiments 40%: 60% RedPajama + 40% synthetic (arXiv 2508.10975).
  • Nemotron-CC 30.2%: derived, 1.9T synthetic / 6.3T total (Table 4, arXiv 2412.02595).
  • SmolLM2 final stage 4%: Cosmopedia v2 at 4% of stage 4 (Section 4.5, arXiv 2502.02737).
  • FinePhrase sweep 70%: 60 to 80% best, 1.2B model on 21B tokens, benchmark average (Table 22, arXiv 2604.13977); bar at the middle.
  • Kang et al. sweep 30%: "converging to ~30%" for rephrased data by validation loss (arXiv 2510.01631).

Synthetic text is also usually repeated more than web text: phi-4's 290 billion unique synthetic tokens were seen 13.8 times each, its 1.3 trillion web tokens 1.2 times (Table 5). Practical reading: a third to a half of a training stage is where the evidence sits, more in the last stage (mid-training or annealing, where small high-quality sets are upsampled; {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}}) than in the bulk phase, and the share is worth one sweep at small scale for your own data and generator. Expect the best share to grow with the budget: once the rewriter's compute is charged, the optimum rose from under 10% at 3 × 1018 FLOPs to 32% at 1022, and the fitted law predicted a 22B model's final loss to within 1.1% ({{Rewriting Scaling Laws (arXiv 2610.31867)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31867}}). When unique real data, not compute, is the limit, rewriting it pays even with a rewriter no larger than the student: same-size rewrites of a model's own pretraining data beat natural-only training at every size tested up to 3B.
