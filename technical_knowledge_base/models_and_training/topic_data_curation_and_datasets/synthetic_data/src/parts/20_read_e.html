6. Checking a synthetic set before training on it

The problem. A generator writes fluent text whatever it is asked, so reading a few samples tells you almost nothing about a set of millions. The failures are statistical: the set is narrow (the same openings, phrases, topics), it repeats itself, it contains benchmark questions the generator memorised, or it is confidently wrong. Each has a cheap measure; all of them were run here on 1,000-document samples of 24 sets (real, rephrased and generated). The table shows eight; the {{Diversity meter|#t-sydi}} has all 24 and measures your own text.

  • Distinct n-grams (above): TinyStories and the Khan Academy part of Cosmopedia are narrow by design (a small vocabulary; one lesson template).
  • Compression ratio: raw bytes divided by gzip bytes of the same 100,000 words; higher means more redundant. A compressor finds repetition at every length, and Shaib et al. found that fast compression ratios capture much of what slow n-gram homogeneity scores measure, recommending them as one of a small set of low-correlated scores to report ({{arXiv 2403.00553|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00553}}).
  • Openings and templates: the share of documents starting with the set's ten most common three-word openings (section 2). Catches template collapse that n-gram ratios over whole documents average away.
  • Embedding spread: the mean cosine similarity between all pairs of documents (here the encoder of the FineWeb-Edu classifier, Snowflake arctic-embed-m, 300 documents per set); higher means documents about the same things in the same way.
  • Near-duplicates: MinHash over 5-word shingles ({{Deduplication|n:3f95c17b0d0d8171ac17e43ab5ea276f}}). Necessary, never sufficient: the most templated sets here have almost none.
  • Benchmark overlap: the share of documents sharing a 13-word sequence with a test item (MMLU, ARC, HellaSwag, GSM8K: @@bench_grams@@ distinct 13-grams). What the numbers say. Rephrasing keeps the variety of its source: Nemotron-CC's Wikipedia-style rewrites of low-quality pages have distinct-2 @@nlw_d2@@ against @@nlr_d2@@ for the pages themselves, because the information is the pages'. Generated sets are as varied as the web when seeds are varied (Cosmopedia web_samples_v2 @@cw2_d2@@) and much less when the template is fixed. The diverse-QA rows look less varied (@@nqa_d2@@) because each row repeats its source and then asks about it: a measure only means something against what the format must repeat.

A quality classifier is not a judge of synthetic text

Pipelines filter synthetic output with the classifiers they use on the web (REWIRE kept the top 10% of rewrites by a fastText classifier). Those classifiers learned a style: FineWeb-Edu's from Llama-3-70B ratings of educational value, DCLM's with OpenHermes 2.5 (itself GPT-4 output) as positives. Share of 300 documents per set the published classifier (HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-edu-classifier, CPU, first 512 tokens) scores 3 or more. Cosmopedia: from @@cosmo_edu3_min@@ (stories) to @@cosmo_edu3_max@@ (Khan Academy units).

Two readings. First, the classifier reads content, not just tone: Nemotron-CC's Wikipedia-style rewrites of low-quality pages pass at @@nemo_lq_wiki_edu3@@, the same as the pages themselves (@@nemo_lq_real_edu3@@), and Cosmopedia's web-seeded textbooks pass far less often (@@cosmo_web_v2_edu3@@ for web_samples_v2) than those seeded with real course outlines (@@cosmo_khan_edu3@@ Khan Academy, @@cosmo_stanford_edu3@@ Stanford). Second, every transformation of high-quality pages scores above its sources (@@nemo_hq_real_edu3@@ for the real high-quality pages): restating facts as questions, lists or condensed prose moves documents over the cut. Filtering synthetic output by this score partly selects formats; that may be what you want, but it should be a decision. And a score says nothing about whether the facts are right (the shipping unit's emissions figure in section 2): check seeds and facts, not only scores.

Model-written text is already in the "real" data

FineWeb's authors tracked phrases typical of chat models ("delve", "as a large language model", "it's important to note", "rich tapestry", "intertwined", "certainly!", "dive into") across crawls and saw a "steep increase" after CC-MAIN-2023-14; they judged it did "not seem to drastically harm" quality and did not filter ({{FineWeb blog, section "Synthetic data"|https://huggingface.co/spaces/HuggingFaceFW/blogpost-fineweb-v1}}).
