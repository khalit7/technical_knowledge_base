3. Rephrasing real documents

The idea. Instead of asking the generator to know things, give it a real web page and ask it to rewrite the page in a better form. The facts, topics and variety come from the web; the generator removes menus, fixes grammar and changes the register. This is rephrasing; when the output has a new structure (questions and answers, a list of facts) it is a transformation. WRAP: half real, half rephrased

Maini et al. (Apple and CMU, January 2024; ACL 2024) rephrased C4 with a frozen Mistral-7B-Instruct in those four styles, capped outputs at 300 tokens because longer rephrasing "often lost information", and trained 128M to 1.3B models on real and rephrased text sampled 1:1 ({{Rephrasing the Web, arXiv 2401.16380|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16380}}).

Half real plus half rephrased beats more real text, at 1.3B self-reported

Zero-shot averages of 3 runs, Tables 1 and 2. Half C4 is 85B tokens, Full C4 170B, RW is RefinedWeb, Synthetic is 85B tokens of Medium plus Q/A rephrases, Synthetic + C4 mixes them with real C4: 49.4 general against 47.3 for Full C4. On the specialised tasks MMLU sits at 24 to 25 for every row, which is chance for four options: these models are too small to read it.

The finding that has held up is in WRAP's own discussion: "synthetic data can not impart 'new knowledge'. It can only help pre-train faster". A 1.8B rephraser (Qwen-1.8B-chat) gave rephrases "of high quality" comparable to Mistral-7B at about three times the throughput, and synthetic-only training lost perplexity on domains whose style the rephrases removed (Philpapers, Gutenberg), so the real half of the mix is not optional.

The paper disagrees with itself; quote the tables (depth)
  • Pile perplexity: the abstract says it "improves perplexity by more than 10% on average", the introduction and Section 4 say "~50%". Both come from figures with no table, so neither can be checked to the decimal.
  • The prose credits "the smaller C4 (85B) dataset" with 43.5% on the specialised tasks; in Table 2, 43.5 is Full C4 (170B) and Half C4 is 43.1.
  • "3x faster" (abstract) and "5x lesser data, or 3x lesser compute" (introduction) are read off Figure 1, again without a table.

Nemotron-CC: five prompts at 1.9 trillion tokens

NVIDIA's Nemotron-CC (December 2024) applied rephrasing at corpus scale ({{arXiv 2412.02595|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02595}}). Pages already sorted into quality buckets by a classifier ensemble ({{Quality classifiers|n:3f95c17b0d0d810baaabe41da6c9c50f}}) get different prompts: low-quality pages are rewritten Wikipedia-style with WRAP's prompt, so text that would be thrown away becomes usable; high-quality pages are also turned into four new forms (diverse question-answer pairs, a condensed "distilled" version, an "extract knowledge" rewrite, a knowledge list). Medium-quality pages were not rephrased "due to time and resource constraints". NVIDIA published every synthetic row with the URL and WARC record id of its source page, so the original can be fetched from Common Crawl: the pairs below were found that way (record ids checked), the "before" extracted with jusText as Nemotron-CC did.

    Nemotron-CC's generation settings (depth)

    The settings, from the paper: Mistral NeMo 12B Instruct in FP8, temperature 0.5, top-p 0.9; segment lengths per prompt (footnote 11: "512 for Wikipedia, 2,000 for Distill, 1,400 for Extract Knowledge and 1,000 for Diverse QA Pairs and Knowledge List, including tokens from the prompt") because longer inputs gave "over-simplified outputs with reduced detail"; post-processing that drops outputs under 50 tokens and strips Markdown and preambles such as "Here is a paraphrased version:".

    Look at the question-answer row: the appended question asks which event is "NOT a real campus event mentioned in the text" and offers a "Spring Fling" the text never mentions either, so the keyed answer is not supported. Transformations make plausible-looking errors, and at 500 billion tokens of question-answer pairs nobody reads them.

    What it bought (8B models, 1 trillion tokens, average of 10 tasks, self-reported, Table 10): rewriting the low-quality bucket lifted the average from 52.5 to 54.0 (MMLU fell from 48.2 to 47.1); replacing half of the repeats of high-quality data with its synthetic variants lifted it from 55.8 to 56.7 (MMLU 53.4 to 53.6). Table 3 counts the output: 336.3 billion tokens from low-quality pages, and from high-quality pages 372.9B Wikipedia-style, 499.5B diverse QA, 157.6B distill, 303.6B extract knowledge and 203.2B knowledge lists, about 1.87 trillion (the abstract rounds to 1.9). Nemotron-CC v2 (August 2025) switched the generator to Qwen3-30B-A3B and translated the question-answer data into 15 languages ({{card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-CC-v2}}).

    Where the evidence agrees, and where it does not

    Agreed: rephrased text mixed with real text beats real text alone at equal tokens; rephrased text alone does not; small generators (1B to 8B) are enough (BeyondWeb Figure 9: 47.3, 48.8, 49.2 for 1B, 3B, 8B generators; Kang et al.; FinePhrase); several different rephrasings beat repeating the original (Kimi K2). Not agreed: the best synthetic share. Kang et al. find about 30% by validation loss, FinePhrase 60 to 80% by benchmark average, WRAP used 50%; they differ in generator, prompts, metric and scale, so the share is something to sweep for your own run, not to copy.
