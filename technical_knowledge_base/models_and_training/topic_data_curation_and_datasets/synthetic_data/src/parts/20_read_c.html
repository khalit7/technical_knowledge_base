3. Rephrasing real documents

The idea. Instead of asking the generator to know things, give it a real web page and ask it to rewrite the page in a better form. The facts, topics and variety come from the web; the generator removes menus, fixes grammar and changes the register. This is rephrasing; when the output has a new structure (questions and answers, a list of facts) it is a transformation.   2. Four prompts. WRAP's Easy, Medium (Wikipedia-style), Hard and Q/A prompts, quoted in the closed block below, each sent with the page to the same model.

WRAP: half real, half rephrased

Maini et al. (Apple and CMU, January 2024; ACL 2024) rephrased C4 with a frozen Mistral-7B-Instruct in those four styles, capped outputs at 300 tokens because longer rephrasing "often lost information", and trained 128M to 1.3B models on real and rephrased text sampled 1:1 ({{Rephrasing the Web, arXiv 2401.16380|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16380}}).

The finding that has held up is in WRAP's own discussion: "synthetic data can not impart 'new knowledge'. It can only help pre-train faster". A 1.8B rephraser (Qwen-1.8B-chat) gave rephrases "of high quality" comparable to Mistral-7B at about three times the throughput, and synthetic-only training lost perplexity on domains whose style the rephrases removed (Philpapers, Gutenberg), so the real half of the mix is not optional.

The paper disagrees with itself; quote the tables (depth)
  • Pile perplexity: the abstract says it "improves perplexity by more than 10% on average", the introduction and Section 4 say "~50%". Both come from figures with no table, so neither can be checked to the decimal.
  • The prose credits "the smaller C4 (85B) dataset" with 43.5% on the specialised tasks; in Table 2, 43.5 is Full C4 (170B) and Half C4 is 43.1.
  • "3x faster" (abstract) and "5x lesser data, or 3x lesser compute" (introduction) are read off Figure 1, again without a table.

Nemotron-CC: five prompts at 1.9 trillion tokens

NVIDIA's Nemotron-CC (December 2024) applied rephrasing at corpus scale ({{arXiv 2412.02595|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02595}}). Pages already sorted into quality buckets by a classifier ensemble ({{Quality classifiers|n:3f95c17b0d0d810baaabe41da6c9c50f}}) get different prompts: low-quality pages are rewritten Wikipedia-style with WRAP's prompt, so text that would be thrown away becomes usable; high-quality pages are also turned into four new forms (diverse question-answer pairs, a condensed "distilled" version, an "extract knowledge" rewrite, a knowledge list). Medium-quality pages were not rephrased "due to time and resource constraints". NVIDIA published every synthetic row with the URL and WARC record id of its source page, so the original can be fetched from Common Crawl: the pairs in Fig. 3 were found that way (record ids checked), the "before" extracted with jusText as Nemotron-CC did.

  1. Crawled. A page captured in Common Crawl CC-MAIN-2024-30, its text extracted with jusText as Nemotron-CC did: the "before" (host, WARC record and word count in the card).
  2. Bucketed. Nemotron-CC's classifier ensemble put the page in the high-quality bucket, so it gets the four transformation prompts as well as Wikipedia-style rephrasing (low-quality pages get only the rephrasing).
  3. Segmented. The text is cut at line boundaries into segments of at most 512 tokens for Wikipedia-style, 2,000 for Distill, 1,400 for Extract knowledge and 1,000 for Diverse QA pairs and Knowledge list, prompt included (footnote 11: "including tokens from the prompt"), because longer inputs gave "over-simplified outputs with reduced detail". Lines longer than the limit are dropped.
  4. Prompted. The segment is wrapped in the chosen prompt, quoted in the card.
  5. Generated. Mistral NeMo 12B Instruct in FP8, temperature 0.5, top-p 0.9, run with TensorRT-LLM. The raw output (with any "Here is a paraphrased version:" preamble) was not published.
  6. Post-processed and published. Preambles and Markdown stripped, outputs under 50 tokens dropped; Diverse QA pairs are shuffled and some appended after the source segment, the other prompts' segments are joined back per document. Green words in the published row sit in a four-word sequence copied from the page.

Transformations make plausible-looking errors, and at 500 billion tokens of question-answer pairs nobody reads them.

    What it bought (8B models, 1 trillion tokens, average of 10 tasks, self-reported, Table 10): rewriting the low-quality bucket lifted the average from 52.5 to 54.0 (MMLU fell from 48.2 to 47.1); replacing half of the repeats of high-quality data with its synthetic variants lifted it from 55.8 to 56.7 (MMLU 53.4 to 53.6). Table 3 counts the output: 336.3 billion tokens from low-quality pages, and from high-quality pages 372.9B Wikipedia-style, 499.5B diverse QA, 157.6B distill, 303.6B extract knowledge and 203.2B knowledge lists, about 1.87 trillion (the abstract rounds to 1.9). Nemotron-CC v2 (August 2025) switched the generator to Qwen3-30B-A3B and translated the question-answer data into 15 languages ({{card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-CC-v2}}).

    Where the evidence agrees, and where it does not

    Agreed: rephrased text mixed with real text beats real text alone at equal tokens; rephrased text alone does not; small generators (1B to 8B) are enough (BeyondWeb Figure 9: 47.3, 48.8, 49.2 for 1B, 3B, 8B generators; Kang et al.; FinePhrase); several different rephrasings beat repeating the original (Kimi K2). Not agreed: the best synthetic share. Kang et al. find about 30% by validation loss, FinePhrase 60 to 80% by benchmark average, WRAP used 50%; they differ in generator, prompts, metric and scale. Scale alone moves it: a scaling law over model size, tokens, rewrite fraction and epochs that charges the rewriter's compute puts the compute-optimal share under 10% at 3 × 1018 FLOPs and 32% at 1022, so the share is something to fit for your own run, not to copy. The same law adds a result the earlier studies lacked: the student can pass its rewriter. The studies, this one included, are below.
