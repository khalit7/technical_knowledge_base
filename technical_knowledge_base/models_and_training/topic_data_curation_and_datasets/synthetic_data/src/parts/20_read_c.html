2. Generating from seeds: TinyStories, phi and Cosmopedia

The idea. Ask a strong model to write the kind of text you wish the web had more of (clear explanations, graded exercises, simple stories) and train a small model on it. The generator's knowledge does the work; the prompt only says what to write. The hard part, every team that did this reports, is diversity: a model asked a thousand times for "a story for children" writes nearly the same story a thousand times.

TinyStories: a vocabulary three-year-olds know

Eldan and Li (Microsoft Research, May 2023) asked GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 for short stories using only words "a typical 3-year-old child would understand" ({{arXiv 2305.07759, Section 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759}}). Their diversity trick is the template for everything since: from a list of about 1,500 basic words, "3 words are chosen randomly (one verb, one noun, and one adjective)" and must appear in the story, and a random subset of story features (a dialogue, a plot twist, a bad ending, a moral) is required. Without it, they write, generation "even if the temperature of generation is set to a high value, will still produce a very repetitive dataset". Models under 10 million parameters, or with a single transformer block, trained on it write fluent, consistent stories, graded by GPT-4 for grammar, creativity and consistency. The dataset card lists 2,141,709 stories, licence CDLA-Sharing-1.0, plus a GPT-4-only version ({{dataset card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/roneneldan/TinyStories}}).

phi: "textbook quality" data for small models

Microsoft's phi-1 (June 2023) is the case that made the field take synthetic pretraining data seriously ({{Textbooks Are All You Need, arXiv 2306.11644|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644}}). A 1.3-billion-parameter code model was trained on under 7 billion tokens: about 6 billion tokens of real code filtered for "educational value" (GPT-4 labelled about 100,000 files; a random forest on code-model embeddings labelled the rest), under 1 billion tokens of Python textbooks written by GPT-3.5, and about 180 million tokens of exercises with solutions (the paper also says "less than 180M" and "<200M" elsewhere). It reached 50.6% on HumanEval and 55.5% on MBPP after 4 days on 8 A100s. The filtering alone mattered: on unfiltered code a 350M model stalled at 12.19% HumanEval, on the filtered subset it reached 17.68% in fewer steps.

Because the exercises resemble HumanEval, the authors tested contamination themselves: removing between 42.5 thousand and 354 thousand of the 879.5 thousand exercises closest to HumanEval problems (by embedding and syntax-tree distance) and retraining left phi-1 at 45.1% to 50.6%, still above StarCoder-Prompted's 41.5% (Table 3). This is the right test: a synthetic set built to teach a skill will resemble the benchmark for that skill, so remove the nearest items and retrain.

phi-1.5 (September 2023) extended the recipe to general text: "roughly 20B tokens" of synthetic textbook data seeded by "20K topics", with web samples inside the prompts for variety; the model trained for 150 billion tokens, 80% synthetic ({{arXiv 2309.05463|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05463}}; Table 1 lists the data as 30B, the prose 7B plus 20B). A web-only variant trained on 95 billion tokens of filtered web text did much worse on common-sense benchmarks (ARC-Challenge 0.329 against 0.444 for phi-1.5, Table 2). phi-4 (December 2024) is the latest public recipe: 50 kinds of synthetic datasets totalling "about 400B unweighted tokens", made with multi-agent prompting, self-revision and instruction reversal ({{arXiv 2412.08905|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08905}}). Its warning is the one to remember: "Models trained only with synthetic data underperformed on the knowledge-heavy benchmarks and demonstrated increased hallucinations"; in its Table 3 the synthetic-only 13B ablation was 14.8 points worse on TriviaQA than phi-3-medium, and adding web rewrites halved that gap (to 7.7).

Cosmopedia: the open replication, and what a prompt looks like

Hugging Face's Cosmopedia (March 2024) set out to reproduce phi-1.5's data openly: over 30 million documents and 25 billion tokens written by Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct, Apache-2.0 ({{blog|https://huggingface.co/blog/cosmopedia}}, {{card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/HuggingFaceTB/cosmopedia}}). Curated seeds (course outlines from Stanford, Khan Academy, OpenStax, WikiHow) ran out quickly ("only 16,000 unique units from OpenStax and 250,000 from Stanford"), so over 80% of prompts are seeded with web pages: millions of web samples were clustered into 145 clusters, the educational ones kept (the blog says 112 topics, the card says 35 of 145 were discarded, which is 110), and each prompt quotes one page. Every prompt is crossed with an audience (young children, high school, college, researchers) and a style (textbook, blog post, WikiHow), "up to 12 times the number of prompts". Two lessons sit in these rows. First, the generator invents: the shipping unit adds a figure for shipping's share of CO2 emissions that the seed never gave, and the product listing becomes a unit on "materials used (cotton candy, glass, etc.)" because the brand name "Cotton Candy" was read as a material. Seeded generation imports the generator's knowledge and its mistakes; nobody checks a 30-million-document set by hand. Second, a junk seed still yields a fluent textbook: the quality filter has to act on the seed (Cosmopedia kept only educational clusters) or on the output, because fluency says nothing about value.

The diversity fix, measured. The Cosmopedia team found that changing only the audience line was "insufficient to prevent a high rate of duplicate content", and that stories kept opening with "Once upon a time" and "The sun hung low in the sky" until the prompts said not to. MinHash deduplication over Cosmopedia found "less than 1%" near-duplicates (card), and the {{Diversity meter|#t-sydi}} agrees at sample scale (at most @@max_neardup@@ of any sample here is in a near-duplicate pair). That is the trap: no two documents are copies, yet the set is narrow. Near-duplicate detection measures copies, not variety; openings, distinct n-grams and embedding spread measure variety (section 6).

Cosmopedia v2 (July 2024, used for SmolLM) replaced web clustering with a fixed list of 34,000 topics from the BISAC book classification; for each topic a search over FineWeb retrieved 1,000 seed pages, giving 39 million documents and 28 billion tokens. Its ablations on 1.8B models found curated-seed textbooks best on MMLU and ARC and a middle-school audience best on everything except MMLU ({{SmolLM blog|https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm}}). By SmolLM2 (2025) Cosmopedia v2 appears only in the last stage, at 4% of the mix (Section 4.5, {{arXiv 2502.02737|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02737}}, which gives it as 30B tokens).

Persona Hub (Tencent, 2024) attacks diversity from the other side: 1,015,863,523 "personas" inferred from web pages ("who would write or read this?") are put into prompts so the same request is written for a billion different people; 1.07 million persona-conditioned math problems took Qwen2-7B to 64.9% on MATH ({{arXiv 2406.20094|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.20094}}, released subset CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0). It is mostly used for post-training data.
