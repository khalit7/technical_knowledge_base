Common mistakes

Training on synthetic text alone
Every comparison that tried it lost something: WRAP's synthetic-only runs on Pile domains whose style was rewritten away, phi-4's synthetic-only model on TriviaQA (−14.8) with "increased hallucinations", REWIRE's rewritten-only below real at every scale, Kang et al.'s rephrased-only no faster than web text. Mix it with real text.
Reading "no near-duplicates" as "diverse"
MinHash finds copies. Generated sets have almost none and can still start @@ts_open10@@ of documents with ten openings (TinyStories here). Measure openings, distinct n-grams at a fixed budget, compression and embedding spread.
Comparing distinct-n across samples of different sizes or domains
The ratio falls as the sample grows, and math or code repeats symbols by nature. Fix the word budget (100,000 words here) and compare within a domain.
Filtering synthetic text with a quality classifier and calling it checked
Classifiers trained on model-rated or model-written positives can favour formats and styles: here every Nemotron-CC transformation of high-quality pages scored above its source pages. A score says nothing about whether the facts are right (the shipping textbook's unsourced emissions figure). Check seeds and facts, not only scores.
Skipping decontamination because "the text is new"
Generators reproduce benchmark items they memorised, and exercise-style data resembles benchmarks by design (phi-1's own analysis; Cosmopedia removed hundreds of matching documents). Run n-gram matching against every evaluation set, and for skill-targeted data retrain without the nearest items as phi-1 did.
Citing model collapse against all synthetic data
The famous experiment fine-tunes a 125M model on its own beam-search output and throws the real data away each generation. Accumulating, grounding and verifying, which is what labs do, avoids it (Gerstgrasser et al., Feng et al.). The real risks are narrower: lost tails and style, and unlabelled model text in new crawls.
Using a bigger generator than needed
For rephrasing, gains flatten above 1B to 8B parameters in three independent studies (BeyondWeb, Kang et al., FinePhrase), and the cost per token grows with size (33.1 million tokens per GPU-hour for a 1.7B generator against 1.1 million for a 70B one, FinePhrase Table 10). Spend the budget on prompts and seeds.
Losing track of which model wrote what
Generator terms differ (section 8). Without the generator, prompt and seed id on every row, a licence change or a bad prompt cannot be removed later.

Interview questions

Why does rephrasing help pretraining if it adds no new information?
The facts are the same, but the form is easier to learn from: no boilerplate, consistent grammar, explanations and question-answer formats that resemble how knowledge is used later. Models reach the same loss or accuracy in fewer tokens (WRAP's speed-up; Kang et al. 5 to 10 times to the same validation loss with a third rephrased). Multiple rephrasings of the same fact also act as better repetition than repeating the original (Kimi K2's SimpleQA table).
How would you make 10 million generated documents diverse?
Vary the inputs, not the temperature: seed each prompt with a different real document or topic (web pages clustered or retrieved by topic), cross with audiences, formats and personas, ban stock openings in the prompt, and check openings, distinct n-grams and embedding spread on a 1,000-document pilot per prompt before scaling. Deduplicate seeds first.
Explain model collapse in two sentences, and when it does not happen.
Refitting a model on a finite sample of its own output loses rare events each round (a word that is not sampled gets probability zero and never returns), so the distribution narrows and drifts. It is avoided when real data stays in the training pool (accumulate), when fresh real data arrives, or when a verifier selects good samples.
A synthetic math set lifts GSM8K by 15 points. What do you check before believing it?
Contamination: n-gram and embedding matches between the set and GSM8K test items, and the generator's own exposure to the test set; retrain without the nearest items (phi-1's method). Then a held-out or fresh benchmark (phi-4 used the November 2024 AMC), several seeds, and whether general benchmarks dropped.
Which generator would you pick for 500 billion tokens of rephrasing, and why?
A permissively licensed 1B to 8B instruction model (Apache-2.0 Qwen3 or similar): quality flattens above that size in the published comparisons, and throughput, so cost, falls roughly with size. Run a small ablation of two or three generators on 1B-model proxies before committing.
System design: build the synthetic-data stage for a 10-trillion-token pretraining run.
Goal and budget: say 30% synthetic, 3T tokens, repeated at most 4 times, so about 750B unique synthetic tokens. Seeds: the quality buckets from the web pipeline (rewrite low and medium, transform high), deduplicated, plus topic lists for math and code. Prompts: five to ten formats, piloted on 1,000 outputs each and measured. Generation: a 4B to 8B Apache-2.0 model on an inference server; at about 10 million tokens per GPU-hour (between the WRAP and FinePhrase rates) 750B tokens is about 75,000 GPU-hours, so a few days on 1,000 GPUs, sharded by seed id and resumable. Post-process and verify: preambles, length, refusals; execution for code, answer checks for math, fidelity checks for rephrasing. Dedup and decontaminate against the real corpus and every eval. Mix: sweep the share on 1B proxies, keep all real data, more synthetic in the final stage. Provenance: generator, prompt, seed id on every row. Trade-offs to name: generator size against cost, diversity against quality filtering, synthetic share against knowledge benchmarks, licences.
