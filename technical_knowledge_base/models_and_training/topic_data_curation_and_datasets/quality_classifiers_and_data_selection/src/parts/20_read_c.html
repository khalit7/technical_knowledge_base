How the classifiers were trained, and every departure from DCLM (provenance)

Hyperparameters read from DCLM's model file: 100 dimensions, learning rate 0.05, 5 epochs, word bigrams, 2,000,000 buckets (DCLM's training script defaults to a learning rate of 0.1 and its README says defaults were kept, so the file is the reliable source). One concession to the shared machine: with most memory held by another topic's GPU job, a 2,000,000-bucket model with a vocabulary of 1.4 million words was swapped out while scoring, so the classifiers below use 500,000 buckets and drop words seen fewer than 3 times. Measured on the same 50k OH-2.5 + ELI5 training set, the two settings rank run0 at Spearman @@param_sp@@ and share @@param_j@@ of their top 10%. Training took @@train_small_secs@@ s per 100,000-example classifier on 4 threads measured, rough. Departures from DCLM, said plainly: OpenWebText stands in for OpenWebText2 (no longer distributed in the Pile mirror), Project Gutenberg chunks for RedPajama books, a Hugging Face ELI5 mirror without vote scores for DCLM's vote-filtered Reddit dump, and real RefinedWeb for DCLM's reproduction of it; 50k + 50k training sets for the comparison (DCLM: 200k + 200k, rebuilt once at full size). Negatives came from RefinedWeb and from other WET files, never from run0's file: a classifier whose negatives overlap the pages it later scores has seen those pages labelled "crawl".

The reference sets disagree with each other about as much as FineWeb-Edu disagrees with DCLM: pairwise overlaps of the 50k classifiers' top 10% are @@ref_jacc_range@@, where random sets would share 5%. And the human-style reading points the opposite way to the training result (Fig. 2, steps 3 to 5): on the uniform hand-labelled sample, the Wikipedia, OpenWebText and GPT-3-approx classifiers keep documents a reader calls worth training on @@hp_other@@ of the time, against @@hp_oh@@ for the three OH-2.5 + ELI5 classifiers, and they rank Llama 3's educational grades worse (AUC @@auc_other@@ against @@auc_oh@@); yet DCLM's 7B training runs put OH-2.5 + ELI5 3.5 to 6.3 Core points ahead of them (Table 5). The Wikipedia-style classifiers keep tidy, encyclopaedic pages that look right to a reader; the OH-2.5 + ELI5 classifiers keep question-and-explanation pages that, by DCLM's measurement, train better. DCLM's own human study (section 11) found the same disconnect.

Each precision rests on @@hk_min@@ to @@hk_max@@ labelled documents, so treat a 20-point gap as real and a 5-point gap as noise. The GPT-3-style classifier, trained against raw crawl text, barely discriminates among pages that already passed heuristic filters: its median score on run0 is @@gpt3_median@@ and its stochastic rule would keep about a third of them.
