1. What "quality" means, operationally

A web crawl is mostly text you would not want a model to imitate: shop pages, cookie banners, spun spam, lists of links. The cheap heuristic filters of the {{Pipeline lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}} (word counts, punctuation, repeated lines; owned in depth by {{Extraction and filtering|n:3c65c17b0d0d81579b32c07c92b7472b}}) remove the obvious junk. What survives still varies enormously, and since about 2023 every competitive open corpus adds a model-based quality filter: a model gives each document a number, and a rule keeps the documents above a cut-off. Data selection is the wider name for any procedure that picks a subset of a pool to train on.

There is no definition of quality behind these filters other than what their scorer was built to prefer. Every method on this page is one of five answers to the question "quality according to what?":

Two consequences run through the whole page. First, the reference decides the bias: a classifier trained to tell Reddit explanations from the crawl will prefer whatever Reddit explanations look like, including their punctuation and their country (section 4 measures both). Second, a filter is judged by the model you train on its output, not by agreement with people: DCLM found that filters agreeing best with human judgements did not give the best models (section 12), so the only real test is a training run, and the evidence on this page separates what was measured here (who gets kept and dropped, on real documents) from what was published (what the kept data does to a model).

The running example
One Common Crawl file from CC-MAIN-2024-30 (July 2024), taken through a FineWeb-style datatrove pipeline by the root page: 20,991 responses become 2,617 English documents after extraction, language ID, heuristic filters and MinHash (the root's run0, see {{the root's Pipeline lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}}). Every classifier on this page scores those same 2,617 documents. Larger samples are used where a question needs them: 50,000 FineWeb rows of the same crawl, 232,000 pages graded by Llama 3 for FineWeb-Edu, 1,000 Nemotron-CC documents with their published quality levels, and 300 documents read and labelled by hand.

Everything labelled measured was run on an Apple M1 Pro, CPU only, 4 threads at low priority on a shared machine, on 2026-10-14. Counts and scores are deterministic and transfer to any machine; timings are rough and do not transfer to a GPU cluster (section 13 gives the cluster arithmetic).
