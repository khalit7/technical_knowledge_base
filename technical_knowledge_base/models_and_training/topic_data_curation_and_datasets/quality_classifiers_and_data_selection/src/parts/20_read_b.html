0. In one screen

After heuristic filters and deduplication, a web pipeline still has to decide which of the surviving pages are worth training on. Today that is done by a model that scores every page and a cut that keeps the top slice. This page takes the scorers apart on one real Common Crawl file and measures what each keeps, drops and gets wrong.

  • The reference set is the filter. DCLM's switch from Wikipedia-like positives to instruction data and ELI5 answers lifted Core from 35.7 to 41.0 at 7B (Table 5, published). Rebuilt here with DCLM's hyperparameters, classifiers that differ only in their positives keep different documents: pairwise overlaps of their top 10% are @@ref_jacc_range@@ measured. Read by a person, the Wikipedia- and WebText-style classifiers' picks look better (@@hp_other@@ judged worth training on, against @@hp_oh@@ for OH-2.5 + ELI5), the opposite of DCLM's training result: what looks good is not what trains well.
  • It learns style as well as content. DCLM's classifier pushes hardest toward "high quality" on code fences, "Why" and "Answer:", and toward "crawl" on bylines, years and typographic punctuation. Straightening the curly quotes in run0's 2,617 documents adds @@pt_quotes_in@@ to its keep set; British spellings lower its scores a little (section 4).
  • LLM-graded classifiers read content, not typography, and Nemotron-CC's three-grader ensemble can be rebuilt on CPU from released models: it reproduces Nemotron-CC's published levels for @@nemo_exact@@ of its documents exactly and @@nemo_within1@@ within one level (section 5).
  • A cut is a percentile of a particular pool. DCLM's 0.018112 is its pool's top 10%, but keeps @@dclm_rw_keep@@ of real RefinedWeb pages and @@fw50_dclm@@ of FineWeb pages (section 6). Stricter cuts won at DCLM's scale; at trillions of tokens they force repetition (section 10).
  • Other selectors behave differently. Perplexity favours predictable text, templates included; DSIR's weights depend strongly on document length; gradient methods are the most direct and the most expensive (sections 7 to 9).
  • Every filter has a demographic. FineWeb-Edu under-keeps entertainment, sport and commerce; DCLM keeps @@tld_uk@@ of .uk pages against @@tld_com@@ of .com (section 11). Published audits found filters favouring wealthier, urban and anglophone writing.
  • Only training says which filter is better. Agreement with people did not predict it in DCLM's study; controlled training or annealing runs do (section 12).
