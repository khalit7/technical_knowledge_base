9. Selection for small models and for large ones

The tension. A strict filter raises the value of each token; it also shrinks the number of unique tokens. A small model trained on a modest budget never runs out, so stricter is better. A large model trained on trillions of tokens runs out and must repeat its data, and repeated tokens are worth less each time. Predict, then read the third number below: with FineWeb's 15T-token pool and a 15T-token run, how much of what you train on is worth fresh data if you keep the top 10%?

How strict can you afford to be? Unique tokens, epochs and effective data

Pool defaults: FineWeb's 15T GPT-2 tokens after its heuristic filters and dedup. Effective data uses Muennighoff et al.'s fit (R*D = 15.39, App. A; formula below), the same constant the {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}} page uses. Derived, not measured: it says how much a filter's token budget is worth once repeated, not whether the filter's tokens are better.

  • Repetition decays published: Muennighoff et al. (arXiv 2305.16264) fit effective data D' = U + U R* (1 - e-R/R*) with R* = 15.4 for R repeats of U unique tokens; up to about 4 epochs repeated data is nearly as good as new (4 epochs are worth 3.7 fresh ones), after about 16 it is worth little (the {{Data mixing|n:3c65c17b0d0d819ea5d6c59471e66875}} child page owns this law).
  • The best filter depends on compute published: Goyal et al. (arXiv 2404.07177, CLIP on DataComp, not LLMs) found the top 10% pool best at low compute and the top 30% best at high compute, with the top 10% falling behind the 30 to 40% pool after its fourth epoch: "data curation cannot be compute agnostic".
  • Nemotron-CC's answer is to keep more and rank it: 6.3T tokens in five levels, with the high levels repeated and rephrased (the {{Synthetic data|n:3c65c17b0d0d8171b5d9e36e05b7a5d0}} page) rather than discarding 90%; the full 6.3T matches DCLM's MMLU with 4 times more unique real tokens. Its 8B model on 15T tokens used 7.2T Nemotron-CC tokens (published).
  • Small proxies still choose well. DCLM's rankings of curation methods at 400M, 1B and 3B correlate with their 7B-1x ranking at Pearson r = 0.838, 0.956 and 0.982 (Section 3.2), and a 125M model's perplexity prunes usefully for a 3B one (section 6). What transfers is the ranking of filters at a fixed keep rate; the right keep rate does not transfer, because it depends on how many tokens the large run needs.

10. Whom filters disadvantage: topic, format, dialect

A quality filter is a choice about which writing counts. The question for an engineer is not whether a filter is biased (every one is) but which kinds of page it under-keeps, by how much, and whether that matters for the model you want. Three ways to measure it, all used on this page: label the pool and compare keep rates per label (below); controlled edits that change one property of the same text (section 3: typography, spelling, layout, LLM rewrites); and proxies at scale, such as the top-level domain as a rough stand-in for country, on 50,000 pages.

Region and dialect. On 50,000 FineWeb pages DCLM keeps @@tld_us@@ of .us and @@tld_com@@ of .com pages but @@tld_uk@@ of .uk, @@tld_au@@ of .au and @@tld_nz@@ of .nz; .in pages are kept at @@tld_in@@. The spelling swap (section 3) shows a causal but small part of that gap. Published audits found larger, systematic effects with GPT-3-style filters published:

  • Gururangan et al. 2022: a GPT-3-style filter scores U.S. high-school newspaper articles higher in larger, wealthier, more educated and urban places (each of 14 points more urban, 17 points more college graduates, double the home value: about +1 point of score), and prefers news and sports topics over food and holidays.
  • Lucy et al. 2024 (AboutMe, 10.3M self-description pages): Wikipedia- and WebText-referenced filters keep news and media and drop real estate, home, fashion and cleaning businesses; lower-prestige occupations (realtor, quilter, crafter) are dropped more; English language ID removes Eastern Asian pages most.

What the hand labels add. In the uniform random sample of 200, @@lab_yes@@ documents were judged worth training a general model on, @@lab_unsure@@ unsure and @@lab_no@@ not (labelled by the agent that built this page, first 1,000 characters each, 2026-10-14). The root's stratified 100 labels found that about a third of the documents both FineWeb-Edu and DCLM drop are fine, half of them news.

What this does and does not show
Keep rates per category are measured; whether under-keeping a category hurts a model is not, and the published ablations suggest it often does not hurt benchmarks (FineWeb-Edu's gains come with heavy topic skew). It matters when the model's users write or ask about the dropped categories: a model trained on DCLM-baseline sees less British-spelled and typographically formatted text, a model trained on FineWeb-Edu sees little entertainment, sport or commerce. Decide which skew you accept and check it, rather than inheriting one.
