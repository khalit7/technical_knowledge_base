What published studies found

  • published WebOrganizer (Wettig et al. 2025, Table 9): FineWeb-Edu's selection raises Science & Tech. from 4.1% to 15.8% of the data and History from 2.3% to 9.0%, and cuts Entertainment from 8.1% to 1.6% and Sports & Fitness from 7.3% to 1.5%; DCLM's raises Comment Section (8.3% to 12.4%), Q&A Forum (0.8% to 2.6%) and Creative Writing (1.9% to 5.4%) and cuts Product Page (11.5% to 2.8%).
  • published Gururangan et al. 2022: a GPT-3-style filter scores U.S. high-school newspaper articles higher in larger, wealthier, more educated and urban places (each of 14 points more urban, 17 points more college graduates, double the home value: about +1 point of score), and prefers news and sports topics over food and holidays.
  • published Lucy et al. 2024 (AboutMe, 10.3M self-description pages): Wikipedia- and WebText-referenced filters keep news and media and drop real estate, home, fashion and cleaning businesses; lower-prestige occupations (realtor, quilter, crafter) are dropped more; English language ID removes Eastern Asian pages most.
