Interview rehearsal: design questions (four questions with answers)
Why do pipelines use fastText rather than a transformer for quality filtering at crawl scale?
Cost: a hash lookup and a 100-dimensional average per document, about @@cost_ft_ms@@ ms on one CPU core here, against about @@cost_enc_s@@ s for a 110M encoder on 4 threads. On 15 billion documents that is CPU-days against thousands of GPU hours. DCLM also found fastText beat embedding classifiers, AskLLM and perplexity filters on Core (Table 4). One reading of why its reference set wins: question-and-explanation text is closer to what benchmarks ask.
How would you choose the threshold for a new quality classifier?
Score a representative sample of the deduplicated pool, express candidate cuts as percentiles, compute tokens kept at each against the training budget and the epochs implied, read samples just above and below each cut, and run small controlled training or annealing tests at two or three keep rates. Record the cut with the model revision and preprocessing.
System design: design the quality-selection stage for a 100 TB, 15T-token English web pretraining corpus.
Inputs: deduplicated, language-filtered documents in Parquet shards with ids. (1) Score every document with a cheap model (fastText, CPU fleet, model loaded once per worker, scores written as columns), plus one encoder grader on GPUs if budget allows (a 110M encoder is about 6,000 H100 hours for 15T tokens). (2) Store scores, not decisions; compute percentile cuts per crawl on the deduplicated pool. (3) Bucket documents by score (or by an ensemble maximum) instead of a single keep/drop, so later stages choose how much of each bucket to train on. (4) Validate with annealing tests per bucket on a partly trained model; pick per-stage thresholds against the token budget and an epoch cap of about 4. (5) Audit: drop rates by topic, format, language variety and region on a labelled sample each release; pin model revisions and preprocessing. Trade-offs to state: strictness against unique tokens; one reference's bias against an ensemble's cost; GPU graders' accuracy against CPU fastText's throughput.
A teammate reports their new filter beats DCLM's by 0.4 Core at 1B-1x. What do you ask?
Same pool, same tokens, same training seed policy? How many seeds of the baseline, and what is their spread (DCLM publishes none, and dedup variants 0.2 apart were called noise)? Does it hold at 3B-1x? What keep rate, and is the comparison at equal tokens? Is any benchmark data leaking through the reference set?
