5. LLM-graded classifiers: FineWeb-Edu and the Nemotron-CC ensemble

The problem with reference sets is that you can only ask "does it look like X". The second family asks a question instead: write a rubric, have a large LLM grade a few hundred thousand pages against it, then train a small model to imitate the grades on the whole crawl. The LLM is the annotator; the small model is a distilled classifier.

FineWeb-Edu, step by step

  1. Rubric. An additive 0 to 5 "educational value" prompt aimed at primary to grade-school teaching, chosen "to avoid having the LLM favor highly technical pages like arXiv abstracts" (the verbatim prompt is on the root's {{Quality lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}}).
  2. Annotator. Llama-3-70B-Instruct graded 460,000 random pages of CC-MAIN-2024-10 (FineWeb paper, Section 4, published).
  3. Student. A linear regression head on the frozen Snowflake arctic-embed-m encoder (110M parameters), trained 20 epochs on 410,000 grades, checkpoint chosen on 50,000 held out. Input: the first 512 tokens.
  4. Cut. Round the predicted score; keep ≥ 3. The paper reports "F1 82%" for that binary decision. On 1,500 annotation rows the root measured a positive-class F1 of @@edu_f1_pos@@ and a macro F1 (average of both classes) of @@edu_f1_macro@@, so the published 82% is closest to the macro average measured.
  5. Cost. 6,000 H100 GPU hours to score FineWeb's 15T tokens published; here @@cost_enc_s@@ s per document on 4 CPU threads rough, which is why the root ran it over 50,000 FineWeb rows on the GPU instead (section 13).

Two properties of the student follow from its tokenizer, and the page measured both: it is uncased (lowercasing all @@perturb_n_edu@@ test documents changed no score by more than @@edu_lower_maxabs@@) and whitespace-blind (collapsing a page to one paragraph changed nothing), while turning every line into a bullet raised the score of @@edu_bullets_up@@ of documents (section 4). It reads content and some layout, not typography.

What it buys published: on 1.71B models trained on 350B tokens, MMLU 33% to 37% and ARC 46% to 57% against FineWeb, and FineWeb-Edu reaches FineWeb's final MMLU with about 10 times fewer tokens. The threshold 3 was chosen over 2 on aggregate benchmarks (the paper gives the comparison only as a plot, Fig. 17). WebOrganizer's analysis (section 11) shows what that choice means in content: Science & Tech. rises from 4.1% to 15.8% of the data and Entertainment falls from 8.1% to 1.6%.

Nemotron-CC: three graders, percentile buckets and a maximum

NVIDIA's argument (Nemotron-CC, 2024) is that FineWeb-Edu and DCLM each keep about 10% of the crawl, too few unique tokens for a 15T-token run, and that they keep different 10% (Table 8: 10.1% of the union kept by both, the root measured @@jacc_published@@ on our documents). So it trains two FineWeb-Edu-style regressors on other annotators (Mixtral 8x22B and Nemotron-4-340B; the FineWeb-Edu classifier itself was left out for licence reasons) and adds DCLM's fastText. Each classifier's score is turned into a rank bucket 0 to 19 (bucket 19 = its top 5%), a document gets the maximum of its three buckets, and buckets are grouped into five levels by annealing tests: 19 high, 18 medium-high, 12 to 17 medium, 7 to 11 medium-low, 0 to 6 low (Table 2). Taking the maximum is a deliberate union: a page any one grader loves is promoted.

Rebuilt here. NVIDIA released both regressors, so the whole ensemble runs on CPU. On 1,000 Nemotron-CC documents of CC-MAIN-2024-30 (200 per published level), with percentile cut-offs estimated from those documents re-weighted to the published level shares, the rebuilt ensemble puts @@nemo_exact@@ of documents in exactly their published level and @@nemo_within1@@ within one level measured. Each member alone does worse (@@nemo_single_range@@ exact), and the maximum is set by @@nemo_argmax@@. The two regressors agree with each other more than with DCLM (Spearman @@sp_mix_nem@@ between them, @@sp_mix_dclm@@ with DCLM, on run0).

Rows: the level Nemotron-CC published. Columns: the level the rebuilt ensemble assigns. Departures, said plainly: the real percentiles came from a whole crawl after jusText extraction; ours come from 1,000 documents weighted by Table 2's token shares as if they were document shares. Applied to run0 with the same cut-offs, the ensemble would place @@run0_nemo_high@@ of the 2,617 documents in "high" and @@run0_nemo_hq@@ in the top two levels, against @@keep_edu@@ kept by FineWeb-Edu and @@keep_dclm@@ by DCLM. That is the recall argument in numbers: the union of three graders keeps more, and Nemotron-CC then decides by annealing how much of each level to train on (its Table 9: the ensemble labels 25% of tokens high quality against 9% to 14% for single classifiers, at equal or better accuracy on 1T-token 8B runs, published).
