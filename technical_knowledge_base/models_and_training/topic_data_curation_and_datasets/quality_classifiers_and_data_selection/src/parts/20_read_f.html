5. Choosing the threshold

A classifier gives scores; the threshold (or cut-off) turns them into a decision, and it is a separate choice with its own failure modes.

Predict: DCLM's cut keeps the top 10% of DCLM's pool. Applied to 50,000 random FineWeb documents (the same crawl as run0, after FineWeb's own filters), what share passes?
  • A probability is not a quality level. fastText's P(hq) is a softmax trained on a balanced set, not a calibrated probability on the crawl. DCLM's cut, 0.018112, keeps documents the classifier thinks are 98% likely to be crawl. Cuts are therefore set as percentiles of a pool: "keep the top 10%". On @@rw_hold_n@@ held-out pages of the real RefinedWeb, the same classifier's 90th percentile is @@dclm_rw_p90@@, and DCLM's cut keeps @@dclm_rw_keep@@ of them, not 10% measured: DCLM's pool had been through DCLM's own extraction, heuristics and Bloom-filter dedup, and its percentile does not travel even to a close cousin.
  • A percentile depends on the pool. The same 0.018112 keeps @@fw50_dclm@@ of already-filtered FineWeb documents and @@keep_dclm_frac@@ of run0's, not 10%: the cleaner the pool, the more a fixed cut keeps. Re-derive cuts on the pool you are filtering.
  • Stricter was better at DCLM's scale. Keeping the top 10% beat 15% and 20% at 7B-1x: Core 41.0, 39.8, 38.7 (Table 5, published). FineWeb-Edu chose an integer score of 3 over 2 the same way.
  • But strictness costs tokens. A 10% filter of a 15T-token crawl leaves 1.5T tokens. A model that needs more must repeat them, and repeated data loses value (section 9). Nemotron-CC's buckets exist so that the threshold can be chosen per training stage instead of once.
  • Sample or cut? GPT-3 kept documents at random in proportion to a function of the score (section 1), DSIR samples by weight (section 7), QuRating found sampling with temperature 2 better balanced than top-k. Top-k maximises average score and minimises diversity.

6. Perplexity filters

Idea. Train a language model on text you trust and score documents by how surprised it is. A perplexity of 20 means the model was, on average, as unsure as if choosing among 20 equally likely tokens. CCNet (2019) used a 5-gram KenLM model trained on Wikipedia per language and split documents into head, middle and tail at the 30th and 60th percentiles; the root's {{Quality lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fa5ecda8d365ab00f}} runs CCNet's own English model on run0.

Measured here with a small transformer instead: Pythia-160M (trained on the Pile) over the first 512 tokens of every run0 document. Its median perplexity is @@ppl_median@@; its ranking agrees with CCNet's 5-gram model at Spearman @@sp_ppl_ccnet@@, and with the quality classifiers only weakly (@@sp_ppl_edu@@ with FineWeb-Edu, @@sp_ppl_dclm@@ with DCLM). Low perplexity selects predictable text, and on the web the most predictable text includes boilerplate, templates and lists: of the 10% lowest-perplexity documents, @@ppl_low_hand_no@@ of those hand-labelled were judged not worth training on, against @@ppl_mid_hand_no@@ in the middle band (small numbers: a direction, not a rate) measured.

A modern LM finds model-written text the most predictable of all. This page ran Qwen3-1.7B on the root's 2,000 random FineWeb rows: median perplexity @@qwen17_median@@, Spearman @@sp_qwen17_edu@@ with the FineWeb-Edu score, and FineWeb-Edu keeps @@qwen17_low30_edu@@ of the 30% lowest-perplexity rows against @@qwen17_high30_edu@@ of the 30% highest (@@fw2k_edu_keep@@ overall) measured. Its 12 lowest-perplexity rows (@@qwen17_low12@@) are all templated or machine-written-looking pages. So "keep low perplexity" now also means "keep text that looks generated"; that is the main reason not to filter on perplexity alone.

Evidence published: in DCLM's comparison, keeping low-perplexity documents under a 154M model trained on Wikipedia, books and peS2o scored 29.0 Core against 27.5 for no model-based filter and 30.2 for fastText (Table 4). Ankner et al. 2024 found the best band depends on the pool: with a 125M reference model, keeping the highest-perplexity half of the Pile and the middle half of Dolma helped 1B and 3B models (Pile 3B: 18.63 to 20.67 average), and the pruned data had higher held-out perplexity while scoring better downstream, a warning against judging a filter by upstream perplexity. Small reference models pruning for 30 times larger ones worked.
