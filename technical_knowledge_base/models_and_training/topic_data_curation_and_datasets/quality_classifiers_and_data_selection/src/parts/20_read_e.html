Second, and the most instructive result on this page: the human-style reading and the training result point in opposite directions. On the uniform hand-labelled sample, the Wikipedia, OpenWebText and GPT-3-approx classifiers keep documents a reader calls worth training on @@hp_other@@ of the time, against @@hp_oh@@ for the three OH-2.5 + ELI5 classifiers, and they rank Llama 3's educational grades worse (AUC @@auc_other@@ against @@auc_oh@@). Yet DCLM's 7B training runs put OH-2.5 + ELI5 5 to 6 Core points ahead of them (Table 5). The Wikipedia-style classifiers keep tidy, encyclopaedic pages that look right to a reader; the OH-2.5 + ELI5 classifiers keep question-and-explanation pages that, by DCLM's measurement, train better. Each precision rests on @@hk_min@@ to @@hk_max@@ labelled documents, so treat a 20-point gap as real and a 5-point gap as noise; DCLM's own human study (section 12) found the same disconnect. Third, the GPT-3-style classifier, trained against raw crawl text, barely discriminates among pages that already passed heuristic filters: its median score on run0 is @@gpt3_median@@ and its stochastic rule would keep about a third of them.
