DCLM's lists are OpenHermes and Reddit: code fences, "Answer:", "assistant.", "ELI5", "Edit:", "[deleted]" toward high quality. Toward crawl: web page furniture (Comment, Posted, ©, Read, Click) and three things that are not about content at all: years (2010 to 2017: the negatives are older pages, the positives were written in 2023), typographic punctuation (curly quotes, en and em dashes, the ellipsis character; chat models and Reddit posts type straight quotes and hyphens), and question titles ("How to" is the strongest negative bigram, SEO headlines). LLM rewrites of the same page: quality scores of synthetic and real text are not on the same scale

The {{Synthetic data|n:3c65c17b0d0d8171b5d9e36e05b7a5d0}} page found that Nemotron-CC's LLM rewrites of high-quality pages pass the FineWeb-Edu cut more often than real high-quality pages do, comparing different documents. Paired here with the page each rewrite was generated from (@@pairs_n@@ source pages, @@pairs_kinds@@ rewrite styles) measured, the rewrite scores higher than its own source for @@pairs_edu_up@@ of pages with the FineWeb-Edu encoder and for @@pairs_dc_up@@ with DCLM's fastText. "Extract knowledge" rewrites raise the FineWeb-Edu score for most pages and roughly double the share passing its cut; concise distillations, question-and-answer pairs and Wikipedia-style rewrites barely move it; DCLM's fastText rewards the rewritten style more broadly. Part of the sibling's gap therefore comes from which pages Nemotron-CC chose to rewrite, part from the format itself, and how much depends on the scorer.
