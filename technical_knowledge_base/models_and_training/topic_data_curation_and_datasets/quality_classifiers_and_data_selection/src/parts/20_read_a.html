The idea in one screen

A quality filter is a scorer plus a cut: a model rates every document by how much it resembles a chosen idea of good text (a reference corpus, an LLM's rubric, a reference language model, a target domain, a target loss), and a percentile of the pool decides how many survive. The idea of good decides what is kept and whom it disadvantages; only a training run says whether it was the right one.

Every filter on this page is the same machine; only the scorer's idea of "good" changes

Model-based quality filter
a model gives each document a number and a rule keeps those above a cut-off; every competitive open corpus has added one since about 2023. Data selection is the wider name for any procedure that picks a subset of a pool to train on.
Reference set, negatives
the text a classifier is trained to call "good" (the positives), against random crawl pages.
Push
one feature's contribution to a linear classifier's logit.
Cut, keep rate
the score threshold, set as a percentile of a pool ("keep the top 10%").
Annotator, distilled classifier
a large LLM grading a sample against a rubric; a small model trained to imitate its grades on the whole crawl.
Perplexity
exp of the mean negative log-likelihood per token under a reference language model.
Importance weight
how much more likely a target distribution makes a document than the pool does (DSIR).
Influence
how much training on an example would lower the loss on a target set.
Core
DCLM's benchmark score: the average over 22 tasks of centered accuracy (0 = random guessing, 1 = perfect).

The running example
One Common Crawl file from CC-MAIN-2024-30 (July 2024), taken through a FineWeb-style datatrove pipeline by the root page: 20,991 responses become 2,617 English documents after extraction, language ID, heuristic filters and MinHash (the root's run0). Every classifier on this page scores those same 2,617 documents. Larger samples where a question needs them: 50,000 FineWeb rows of the same crawl, 232,000 pages graded by Llama 3 for FineWeb-Edu, 1,000 Nemotron-CC documents with their published quality levels, and 300 documents read and labelled by hand. Everything labelled measured ran on an Apple M1 Pro, CPU only, 4 threads at low priority on a shared machine, on 2026-10-14; counts and scores are deterministic and transfer to any machine, timings are rough and do not transfer to a GPU cluster (section 12 gives the cluster arithmetic).
