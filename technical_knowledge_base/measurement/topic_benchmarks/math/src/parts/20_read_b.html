3. Grade school: GSM8K and what broke it

GSM8K ({{Cobbe et al., OpenAI, 27 Oct 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168}}) is "8.5K high quality grade school math problems created by human problem writers", each needing 2 to 8 steps of basic arithmetic; the released split is 7,473 training and 1,319 test problems ({{Hugging Face, MIT licence|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/gsm8k}}). Each solution ends in #### 14, a single number graded by exact match. It is where chain of thought was shown, and by September 2024 OpenAI wrote that "recent frontier models do so well on MATH and GSM8K that these benchmarks are no longer effective at differentiating models" ({{OpenAI, Learning to reason with LLMs|https://openai.com/index/learning-to-reason-with-llms/}}).

The live question since has been whether a high GSM8K score means the model can do the arithmetic or has learned this set. Apple's GSM-Symbolic ({{Mirzadeh et al., 7 Oct 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229}}) turned 100 GSM8K test questions into templates: names, family members and numbers become variables with ranges and conditions (the answer must stay a whole number). Fifty instances per template give 50 test sets of 100 questions; harder variants delete one clause (M1) or add one or two (P1, P2), and GSM-NoOp adds a clause that sounds relevant and changes nothing. Accuracies are 8-shot chain of thought with greedy decoding, mean and standard deviation over 50 sets of 100 ({{Table 1, Appendix A.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05229v2}}); one irrelevant clause cost Phi-3-mini 65.7 points. The grey dashed whisker is what binomial chance alone gives for 100 items, √(p(1−p)/100) derived.

Read the evidence in two parts. The dramatic part holds up: P2 and NoOp cut every model, even strong ones (o1-preview from 94.9% on the full GSM8K test to 77.4% on NoOp), and models "blindly convert" a statement such as "five of them were a bit smaller" into a subtraction. The other headline, that accuracy "varies" across the 50 renamed and renumbered sets, is weaker than it sounds: the reported spreads (about 2 to 3.5 points) are smaller than the roughly 4 points binomial chance alone gives at 100 items derived, and a re-test found only 3 of 25 models with a significant GSM8K to GSM-Symbolic drop, while NoOp was significant for all 25 ({{Ivanova et al., ICLR 2025 blog post|https://iclr-blogposts.github.io/2025/blog/towards-more-rigorous-llm-evals/}}). The lesson both sides accept: a single GSM8K accuracy cannot tell understanding from pattern matching, and irrelevant information is where pattern matching shows.

Correction to the old page: "now >95% for anything frontier, heavily contaminated"

Neither holds as stated. No 2026 frontier card reports GSM8K; the last frontier figures were mid-90s. Contamination is family by family: GSM1k, a private look-alike set of 1,205 human-written problems, found drops of up to 8% in several families, but "many models, especially those on the frontier, show minimal signs of overfitting" ({{Zhang et al., v4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332}}); its first version said "up to 13%" ({{v1, 1 May 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332v1}}), the figure the parent's Reading tab quotes. And part of the ceiling is the key: GSM8K-Platinum re-checked 219 flagged test questions, removed 110 and relabelled 10, leaving 1,209 ({{MadryLab, March 2025|https://gradientscience.org/gsm8k-platinum/}}).

Depth: GSM8K's construction and climb, GSM-Symbolic's data and the authors' reply, GSM-Plus, GSM1k

"A bright middle school student should be able to solve every problem." The paper rounds the split to 7.5K and 1K. Solutions carry calculator annotations such as <<62-48=14>>. The paper's own method was already a sampling protocol: generate 100 candidate solutions and let a trained verifier pick one. Its best, GPT-3 175B fine-tuned with a verifier, scored 55% (the figure {{PaLM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311}} later quoted as the prior best). Wei et al.'s chain-of-thought paper led with GSM8K: PaLM 540B prompted with eight worked examples reached state of the art, "surpassing even finetuned GPT-3 with a verifier" ({{arXiv 2201.11903|https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903}}). GPT-4 reported 92.0% five-shot in March 2023, with a footnote that part of the GSM8K training set was mixed into its training data ({{GPT-4 report, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774}}). Epoch's table tops at 94.5% because labs stopped reporting GSM8K before 2025 ({{Epoch AI hub|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks}}).

The last frontier GSM8K figures: GPT-4o 95.2% and o1-mini 95.1% on the full test set in {{GSM-Symbolic's Table 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05229v2}}.

GSM-Symbolic's templates and the Symbolic, P1 and P2 data are released ({{Hugging Face, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0|https://huggingface.co/datasets/apple/GSM-Symbolic}}); NoOp is not. Ivanova et al. used the Fisher exact test and called the growing spread on harder variants "a trivial consequence of the decrease in probabilities". The authors answered in their v2 appendix that a model that truly understood a question should not flip when only a name changes, so variance itself is the finding.

GSM-Plus ({{Li et al., 29 Feb 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19255}}) is the hand-and-GPT-4-built cousin: each of the 1,319 test questions gets eight variations (numerical substitution, digit expansion, integer-decimal-fraction conversion, adding an operation, reversing an operation, problem understanding, distractor insertion, critical thinking), 10,552 in all; across 25 models "even for problems that have been solved in GSM8K, LLMs can make mistakes when new statements are added or the question targets are altered". GSM1k prints 50 of its problems in its appendix and reports systematic overfitting in several families. The multilingual translation, MGSM, has its row in the parent's atlas.

What GSM8K is for now: a cheap regression smoke test for small models, ideally on GSM8K-Platinum, with a perturbed variant beside it. A frontier model's GSM8K number says nothing.

4. Competition math: MATH, MATH-500, Omni-MATH

MATH ({{Hendrycks et al., 5 Mar 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874}}) collects 12,500 problems from high-school competitions (AMC 10, AMC 12, AIME and similar), 7,500 for training and 5,000 for test, in seven subjects and five difficulty levels. Every problem has a full LaTeX solution ending in a \boxed{} answer, "unique after normalization", so it is graded by exact match after normalising the expression. At launch, models scored 3.0% to 6.9%; o1 reached 94.8% on MATH-500 in September 2024, and on MATH-500 o3 scores 99.2% with the top five between 98.2% and 99.2% ({{Akhtar et al., ICML 2026, Table 6|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16763}}).

Where MATH-500 comes from matters more than it looks. OpenAI's process-reward work needed more training problems, so it moved 4,500 of the 5,000 test problems into the PRM800K training set and evaluated only on the remaining 500, "selected uniformly at random" ({{Lightman et al., Let's Verify Step by Step|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050}}):

Nine tenths of MATH's test set has been public training data since May 2023

Drawn to scale from the two papers' counts. A full-MATH-test score after May 2023 is not clean; MATH-500 is the held-out remainder, and the saturation study above still flags it as contaminated.

Omni-MATH ({{Gao et al., 10 Oct 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07985}}) answered MATH's saturation with 4,428 olympiad-level problems in over 33 sub-domains and more than 10 difficulty levels. Its answers are too varied for string matching, so the main leaderboard is graded by GPT-4o, with an open judge model (Omni-Judge) and a rule-gradable subset beside it. At launch o1-mini led with 60.54% and o1-preview 52.55%; the official board still shows those September 2024 rows ({{omni-math.github.io|https://omni-math.github.io/}}, read 4 Oct 2026): a dormant board, so any newer figure is someone's own run with someone's own judge.

Depth: MATH's subjects, the human check, and the climb by protocol

The seven subjects: prealgebra, algebra, number theory, counting and probability, geometry with diagrams written in the Asymptote language, intermediate algebra, precalculus. The paper's human check was small: on 20 problems a computer-science PhD student who "does not especially like mathematics" got 8 (40%) and a three-time IMO gold medallist 18 (90%), missing two "exclusively due to small errors of arithmetic".

The climb mixes protocols: Minerva 540B 33.6% with one sample and 50.3% with a majority vote over samples on the same set ({{Lewkowycz et al., June 2022, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14858}}); GPT-4 42.5% four-shot (March 2023). Epoch AI's own runs on the hardest level (1,324 Level 5 test problems) go from 22.97% for GPT-4 (June 2023) to 98.13% for GPT-5 (high), run in October 2025 (the parent's {{Saturation timeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}} tab draws all three series).
