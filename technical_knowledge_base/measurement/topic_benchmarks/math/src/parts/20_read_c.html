Research math: FrontierMath

Construction. FrontierMath ({{Glazer et al., Epoch AI, 7 Nov 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872}}) is a set of new, unpublished problems written and vetted by more than 60 mathematicians (collectively 14 IMO gold medals and one Fields Medal; Terence Tao contributed several). "Solving a typical problem requires multiple hours of effort from a researcher in the relevant branch of mathematics, and for the upper end questions, multiple days." Each answer is a definite object checked by code, built to be guessproof. In Epoch's harness the model can run Python (sympy, numpy, mpmath and a few other libraries), must submit a Python function answer() that returns its answer within 30 seconds, and has a hard limit of 1,000,000 tokens, with forced submission from 660,000 ({{Epoch AI, methodology|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2}}). At launch "current state-of-the-art AI models solve under 2% of problems" (about 6% for the best model when any of eight runs counts, in the paper's appendix), and Tao, interviewed for the paper, expected the problems to "resist AIs for several years at least".

Tiers. The core set is graded into three tiers by difficulty, and Tier 4 was added in 2025 as "an expansion set of 50 problems designed to vastly exceed the difficulty of even the Tier 3 problems", written by professors and postdocs ({{Epoch AI, Tiers 1-4 about|https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/tiers-1-4/about}}). Versions. On 12 June 2026 Epoch released v2, "addressing errors in 42% of problems": it corrected 123 problems in Tiers 1-3 and 12 in Tier 4 and removed 5 and 7, leaving 295 and 43 (338 in all), of which ten and two are public ({{changelog|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2}}). The arithmetic: (123 + 12 + 5 + 7) / 350 = 42% derived, far above the "approximately 1 in 20 problems had errors" the about page still gives from earlier review. Scores rose with the fixes: on the same Epoch harness o4-mini (high) went from 24.83% on the v1 private set to 36.14% on v2, GPT-5 (high) from 32.41% to 55.44%, so v1 and v2 numbers are never placed on one line. Epoch's private Tiers 1-3 now have 285 problems and Tier 4 41, which is why 93.68% is 267 of 285 and Tier 4's 97.6% is 40 of 41 derived from Epoch's standard errors.

Governance. OpenAI funded the benchmark. The paper's acknowledgement "We gratefully acknowledge OpenAI for their support in creating the benchmark" appears first in version 5 of 20 December 2024, the day of the o3 announcement, and not in version 4 of 22 November ({{v5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872v5}}, {{v4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872v4}}). Epoch's statement of 23 January 2025 set out the terms: OpenAI commissioned the 300 core and 50 Tier 4 problems and has access to most statements and solutions, except 53 core solutions and 20 Tier 4 problems held out for Epoch's own evaluations ({{Epoch AI|https://epoch.ai/latest/openai-and-frontiermath}}). The current page says OpenAI "has exclusive access to a subset of the benchmark". How the holdout maps onto the v2 sets is not published unconfirmed. The case is the reference example of the trade-off the parent page describes: a private set resists contamination but cannot be audited by outsiders, and its funder's access has to be disclosed.

Formal proofs: miniF2F and PutnamBench

A formal benchmark asks for a proof that a proof assistant accepts, so grading is mechanical and a wrong proof cannot pass. miniF2F ({{Zheng, Han and Polu, OpenAI, 31 Aug 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00110}}) has 488 problem statements from the AIME, AMC and IMO and from school and undergraduate courses, split 244 validation and 244 test, formalised in Metamath and Lean, partly in Isabelle and HOL Light; its baseline was GPT-f. Prover systems drove the test split to 99.2% with Hilbert, which combines an informal reasoning model, a prover model, the Lean verifier and retrieval ({{Varambally et al., 26 Sept 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22819}}).

PutnamBench ({{Tsoukalas et al., UT Austin, 15 Jul 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11214}}) took the undergraduate Putnam competition: 1,692 hand-made formalisations of 640 theorems in Lean 4 and Isabelle, with a subset in Coq (now Rocq); at launch, provers solved "a handful". The current set has 672 Lean 4 problems (346 with an answer placeholder), 640 Isabelle and 412 Rocq. By September 2026 at least four systems (Aleph Prover, Humanfia, NEAR AI and Forall) had proved all 672 Lean problems on the track that supplies the answer, at mean costs from $0.17 to $74 per problem, and Midas Prover all 672 on the track where the answer must be found (12 Sept 2026); the board now ranks by cost ({{PutnamBench leaderboard|https://trishullab.github.io/PutnamBench/leaderboard.html}}, read 4 Oct 2026).

Correction to the old page: "PutnamBench still has headroom"

Both formal benchmarks are saturated. And "no grading ambiguity" needs one qualifier: the checker verifies the proof, not that the formal statement says what the problem says, results on the board are self-submitted, several perfect runs used fixed formalisations, and the with-answer track hands the prover the answer ({{leaderboard notes|https://trishullab.github.io/PutnamBench/leaderboard.html}}). The cleanest metric design in math still needs its statements audited.

Two lessons carry over to every benchmark. The 2026 harness run is the same-model, many-numbers problem in its purest form: one model, one harness, three effort settings, 28, 30 and 39 points before repair. And "gold" has meant three different things (certified by IMO coordinators, graded by the lab's chosen medallists, graded by an AI) that a headline does not distinguish. Beyond math: CritPt and the physics re-grading

Physics benchmarks share math's exact-answer design and inherit its weak point, the answer key. CritPt ({{Zhu et al., 30 Sept 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26574}}) has 71 research-scale challenges written by more than 50 active physicists, decomposed into 190 checkpoints, with private reference solutions; at launch the best base model, GPT-5 (high), averaged 5.7%, about 10% with coding tools. Artificial Analysis runs 70 of them in its Intelligence Index, where GPT-5.6 Sol (max) leads at 32.3% (113 of 350 attempts, mean of 5).

An expert re-grading ({{Re-grading six physics benchmarks|n:3e25c17b0d0d8165b0c8d15600421259}}, the paper page in this knowledge base) reviewed a 56-challenge subset: 21 had benchmark errors (19 repaired, 2 excluded) and 5 were model errors. On the 54 kept, GPT-5.6 Sol scores 87.50% (mean of 4) and 94.44% (any of 4); Claude Fable 5 78.24% and 90.74%; Gemini 3.1 Pro rises from 17.71% to 54.63%. The before and after differ in harness, tools, judge and effort as well as in the keys (Gemini, without tools on both sides, is the cleanest pair), and Anthropic's separate correction revised 31 of the 71 statements. Read a low CritPt score as an upper bound on headroom, not a floor on capability; the paper page has the full audit and its caveats.
