5. Contests as benchmarks: AIME, HMMT and MathArena

The fix for contamination was freshness. The AIME (American Invitational Mathematics Examination) is two papers (AIME I and II) of 15 problems, three hours each, every answer an integer from 0 to 999 ({{AoPS wiki, 2026 AIME|https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/2026_AIME_II}}), so each February brings a new 30-problem set no model trained before it can have seen. HMMT February (Harvard-MIT Mathematics Tournament) plays the same role with 30 to 33 answer problems, some of them expressions.

MathArena ({{Balunović, Dekoninck et al., ETH Zurich and INSAIT|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23281}}) made this a protocol: extract problems and answers as soon as a contest ends; run every model four times per problem with one fixed prompt ("Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}. The answer is an integer between 0 and 999 inclusive."); parse the boxed answer with a rule-based parser, reviewing flagged parses by hand; report pass@1, the mean over all answers, with no voting; publish cost and tokens; and flag every model released after the contest as possibly contaminated. Running it on older contests is how MathArena found "strong signs of contamination in AIME 2024". Freshness bought about a year per vintage:

Clean scores reached the ceiling within a year: the newest vintage was at 98% the day it was sat

Correction to the old page: "current-year AIME and HMMT via the MathArena protocol" as the thing to use

MathArena retired final-answer contests on 12 May 2026: when it screened 176 new contest problems for a harder set, Gemini 3.1 Pro solved every one at least once in four attempts ({{Farewell to final-answer competition problems|https://matharena.ai/no_final_answer/}}). The contest tables stay online as history. Its live signal is now research mathematics and proofs: ArXivMath (answers taken from new arXiv papers; top 95.31% for GPT-6.1 Sol at max effort over the May, June and August 2026 sets, {{table|https://matharena.ai/competition_tables/overall--arxivmath}}), BrokenArXiv, ArXivLean (formal) and proof-graded USAMO 2026, where proofs are graded by human judges.

Depth: the contest dates, the old best scores and why MathArena gave up on final answers

AIME 2025 was sat on 6 and 12 Feb 2025; the best models released before it were o1 (medium) at 81.67% and o3-mini (high) at 86.67%, and GPT-5.2 (high) reached 100% in December 2025, flagged as released after the contest. AIME 2026 was sat on 5 and 11 Feb 2026, and HMMT February 2026 had 33 problems. MathArena's two hardest final-answer sets, built in August 2025 from problems no model could solve, were nearly saturated by May 2026 (GPT-5.5 had just solved the last unsolved Apex problem, IMO 2025 Problem 6), and in the screen of 176 new problems Gemini 3.1 Pro solved 162 in all four attempts.

6. Error bars on 30 problems

On AIME one problem is worth 3.33 points, and the item count sets how good an estimate a score can be. The half-width of a 95% interval is 1.96·√(p(1−p)/n). Predict the AIME interval at 80% before you look, then move the accuracy:

On 30 problems, an 80% score is ±14 points; it takes MATH-500's 500 to get under ±4

Item counts: AIME 30, HMMT February 2026 33, FrontierMath v2 private sets 41 and 285 (Epoch's public counts minus the published problems), one GSM-Symbolic set 100, miniF2F test 244, MATH-500 500, GSM8K test 1,319, MATH Level 5 1,324 derived. Near 100% the binomial interval collapses to zero, which says nothing about the next problem: the Wilson and exact intervals in {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}} and the parent's calculator handle that end.

MathArena's ± counts answers, not problems. Its tables print accuracy "together with a 95% confidence interval obtained with the normal approximation": GPT-5.2 (high) on AIME 2026 is 98.33% ± 2.29, which is 1.96·√(p(1−p)/120), with 120 = 30 problems × 4 runs; the rule reproduces all 123 rows of the three tables used here derived. Four runs on the same problem are not independent, so this measures run-to-run noise on these 30 problems, not how the score would move on a new 30. Treating the problem as the unit (clustered by problem, with the per-problem solve rates from the released answers) gives ±10.75 instead of ±6.92 for o1 (medium) on AIME 2025 derived, and over the four-run rows the clustered interval is a median 1.7 times the published one. To rank two models, use a paired test on the same problems, as MathArena does for its rank intervals; the {{Sample lab|#t-lab}} runs it exactly on any pair. The general method is {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}'.

Depth: the three intervals side by side, and what the paper says

The paper describes N as "the number of questions", but the printed ± only reproduces with the answer count. For GPT-5.2 (high) on AIME 2026 the clustered interval is ±2.27, close to the published one; it differs most (up to 2.0 times) when a model solves some problems on every run and misses others on every run, as o1 (medium) does on AIME 2025. The binomial interval at n = 30 (±13.85 for o1) is wider still, because it ignores what the four runs reveal about each problem.
