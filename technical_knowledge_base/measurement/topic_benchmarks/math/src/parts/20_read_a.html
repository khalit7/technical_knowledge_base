Math is the cleanest family of AI benchmarks to grade and the fastest to saturate: an answer is a number or a formula that can be checked exactly, so there is no judge to argue with, and every benchmark built so far has been climbed. This page takes each one in turn: how it was built, how it is graded, what broke, what its score means today, and what to use now. How a math answer gets checked

Every math benchmark sits somewhere on one ladder, from checks that cannot be argued with to checks that are themselves a model or a person. Where a benchmark sits decides which errors it can make.

Proof checker (Lean 4, Isabelle, Rocq)miniF2F, PutnamBench, ArXivLean, AlphaProofThe formal statement can misstate the problem; a "with answer" track hands the prover the answer

FrontierMath adds a design rule worth stealing: a problem must not give more than a 1% chance of guessing the answer without doing most of the work ({{Glazer et al., section 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872}}), which is why its answers are large, unobvious objects rather than small integers.

Grade school: GSM8K and what broke it

Construction. GSM8K ({{Cobbe et al., OpenAI, 27 Oct 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168}}) is "8.5K high quality grade school math problems created by human problem writers", each needing 2 to 8 steps of basic arithmetic; "a bright middle school student should be able to solve every problem". The released split is 7,473 training and 1,319 test problems ({{Hugging Face, MIT licence|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/gsm8k}}); the paper rounds them to 7.5K and 1K. Each solution ends in #### 14, a single number, graded by exact match, and carries calculator annotations such as <<62-48=14>>. The paper's own method was already a sampling protocol: generate 100 candidate solutions and let a trained verifier pick one. Its best, GPT-3 175B fine-tuned with a verifier, scored 55% (the figure {{PaLM|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311}} later quoted as the prior best).

Where chain of thought was shown. Wei et al.'s chain-of-thought paper led with it: PaLM 540B prompted with eight worked examples reached state of the art on GSM8K, "surpassing even finetuned GPT-3 with a verifier" ({{arXiv 2201.11903|https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903}}). GPT-4 reported 92.0% five-shot in March 2023, with a footnote that part of the GSM8K training set was mixed into its training data ({{GPT-4 report, Table 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774}}). In September 2024 OpenAI wrote that "recent frontier models do so well on MATH and GSM8K that these benchmarks are no longer effective at differentiating models" ({{OpenAI, Learning to reason with LLMs|https://openai.com/index/learning-to-reason-with-llms/}}).

Correction to the old page: "now >95% for anything frontier, heavily contaminated"

Neither holds as stated. No 2026 frontier card reports GSM8K, so there is no current frontier figure; the last ones were mid-90s (GPT-4o 95.2% and o1-mini 95.1% on the full test set in {{GSM-Symbolic's Table 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05229v2}}). And the contamination evidence is family by family, not frontier-wide: GSM1k, a fresh look-alike set of 1,205 human-written problems kept private (50 are printed in its appendix), found drops of up to 8% and systematic overfitting in several families, but "many models, especially those on the frontier, show minimal signs of overfitting" ({{Zhang et al., v4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332}}). The first version of that paper said "up to 13%" ({{v1, 1 May 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332v1}}); the parent page's Reading tab quotes the v1 figure. Part of the ceiling is the key, not the models: GSM8K-Platinum re-checked 219 flagged test questions, removed 110 and relabelled 10, leaving 1,209 ({{MadryLab, March 2025|https://gradientscience.org/gsm8k-platinum/}}).

GSM-Symbolic: the same question, perturbed

Apple's GSM-Symbolic ({{Mirzadeh et al., 7 Oct 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229}}) turned 100 GSM8K test questions into templates: names, family members and numbers become variables with ranges and conditions (for instance, the answer must be a whole number). Fifty instances per template give 50 test sets of 100 questions; harder variants delete one clause (M1) or add one or two (P1, P2), and GSM-NoOp adds a clause that sounds relevant and changes nothing. Templates and the Symbolic, P1 and P2 data are released ({{Hugging Face, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0|https://huggingface.co/datasets/apple/GSM-Symbolic}}); NoOp is not. Accuracies are 8-shot chain of thought with greedy decoding, mean and standard deviation over 50 sets of 100 ({{Table 1, Appendix A.2|https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05229v2}}). The grey dashed whisker is what binomial chance alone gives for 100 items, √(p(1−p)/100) derived.

Read the evidence in two parts. The dramatic part holds up: P2 and NoOp cut every model, even strong ones (o1-preview from 94.9% on the full GSM8K test to 77.4% on NoOp), and models "blindly convert" a statement such as "five of them were a bit smaller" into a subtraction. The paper's other headline, that accuracy "varies" across the 50 sets of renamed and renumbered questions, is weaker than it sounds: the reported spreads (about 2 to 3.5 points) are smaller than the roughly 4 points binomial chance alone gives at 100 items derived. Ivanova et al. re-tested the paper's numbers and found only 3 of 25 models with a statistically significant GSM8K to GSM-Symbolic drop (Fisher exact test), and the growing spread on harder variants "a trivial consequence of the decrease in probabilities"; but NoOp was significant for all 25 ({{ICLR 2025 blog post|https://iclr-blogposts.github.io/2025/blog/towards-more-rigorous-llm-evals/}}). The authors answered in their v2 appendix that a model that truly understood a question should not flip when only a name changes, so variance itself is the finding. Both readings agree on the practical lesson: a single GSM8K accuracy cannot tell understanding from pattern matching, and irrelevant information is where pattern matching shows.

GSM-Plus ({{Li et al., 29 Feb 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19255}}) is the hand-and-GPT-4-built cousin: each of the 1,319 test questions gets eight variations (numerical substitution, digit expansion, integer-decimal-fraction conversion, adding an operation, reversing an operation, problem understanding, distractor insertion, critical thinking), 10,552 in all; across 25 models "even for problems that have been solved in GSM8K, LLMs can make mistakes when new statements are added or the question targets are altered". The multilingual translation, MGSM, has its row in the parent's atlas.

What GSM8K is for now: a cheap regression smoke test for small models, ideally on GSM8K-Platinum, with a perturbed variant beside it. A frontier model's GSM8K number says nothing.
