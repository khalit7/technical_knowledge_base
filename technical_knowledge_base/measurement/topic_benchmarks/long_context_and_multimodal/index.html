Two families share this page because they fail the same way. A long-context test asks whether a model can use a million tokens, not just accept them; a multimodal test asks whether it can use the image, chart or video, not just answer around it. In both, the first benchmarks could be passed by a shortcut: a needle that repeats the question's words, a question whose answer the text alone gives away. Each generation since has been built to close a shortcut, and each saturated anyway. This page takes the benchmarks in that order: how each was built and graded, what broke, what its score means today, and what to use now.

The parent page, Topic: benchmarks, has the cross-family view: its Benchmark atlas tab (filter Long context or Multimodal) holds a dated row for every benchmark below, and its Reading tab explains version, split, harness and contamination in general. Two tabs here go deeper: Effective length recomputes "advertised against effective" context for 39 published model rows under a rule you choose, and Grade a real MRCR item runs OpenAI's MRCR grader on a real item from the dataset.

What a long-context benchmark has to separate

A context window is a promise about what the API will accept. Whether the model uses what it accepted is a separate question, and long-context benchmarks ask it at three levels of difficulty:

  • Find: retrieve one stated fact (needle in a haystack, single-needle RULER tasks).
  • Connect: link facts that are far apart or only implied (multi-hop tracing, NoLiMa's latent hops, BABILong's chained facts, MRCR's "the second of two identical requests").
  • Aggregate: use the whole input at once (counting the most frequent words, summarising a book, reasoning over 100 PDFs).

Three protocol knobs decide what a long-context number means, and a score quoted without them carries little: the length (and whose tokenizer counted it: AA-LCR counts with cl100k_base, MRCR bins by o200k), the depth where the evidence sits, and the threshold that turns a curve into a single "effective length". The Effective length tab lets you change the third and watch the answer move.

Why a model degrades past its training length (position encodings, RoPE scaling, YaRN, what breaks on six real configs) is on Positional Encodings; how a model is trained to long context is on Continued Pretraining (CPT). This page is about measuring the result.

Needle in a haystack: the test that was solved by its own design

Greg Kamradt's open-source test (LLMTest_NeedleInAHaystack, November 2023) plants one sentence in a long run of essays and asks for it back, sweeping context length on one axis and needle depth on the other; the result is the familiar green-and-red heatmap. Its needle and question, as Anthropic later quoted them (Anthropic, 6 Dec 2023):

Two things made it a weak test. First, the question repeats the needle's words, so finding the needle is a string match, the operation attention does best. Second, it is sensitive to the prompt in ways unrelated to memory: Claude 2.1 scored 27% on it until Anthropic added one sentence to the start of the answer, "Here is the most relevant sentence in the context:", which raised it to 98% (Anthropic). The model had been declining to answer from a sentence that looked out of place in its essay, not failing to find it.

By 2024 single-needle retrieval was a solved row: the RULER authors note "nearly perfect accuracy in the vanilla NIAH test" for every model they tested (RULER abstract), and the Llama 3 report claims perfect single-needle retrieval at all depths and lengths (see the Llama 3 Herd paper page). It is retired as evidence; the parent's atlas row carries no current reading because no 2026 card reports it.

Position matters: lost in the middle

Before the synthetic suites, Liu et al. measured where in the context evidence can sit (Lost in the Middle, v3). Twenty retrieved Wikipedia passages, exactly one containing the answer, and the position of that passage moved from first to last. Accuracy traced a U: high when the evidence came first (primacy) or last (recency), lowest in the middle. For GPT-3.5-Turbo the middle positions fell below its closed-book score, the score with no passages at all, so twenty passages with the answer in the middle were worse than none.

Values from Table 6 (20 documents) and Table 1 (closed-book, oracle) of arXiv 2307.03172v3; positions are the gold passage's index 0, 4, 9, 14, 19 shown 1-based. Lines join measured points only.

The effect is not universal: Claude-1.3's curve moves by about 4 points, GPT-3.5-Turbo's by 22. And it shifts with length: NoLiMa finds that once the needle shares no words with the question, the context length matters more than the position, because the model can no longer find the needle wherever it is (Section 4.4.2 of NoLiMa). Position is a protocol knob: a benchmark that only places evidence at the edges overstates, and a "depth" chart is a statement about one model family at one length.

RULER: from one needle to thirteen tasks, and "effective length"

NVIDIA's RULER (Hsieh et al., v3, April 2024) kept the needle's convenience (synthetic, any length, exact grading) and widened the tasks to 13 in four categories:

  • Retrieval: eight needle variants: one or several keys, several values for one key, several queries, distractor needles, keys that are words, numbers or UUIDs.
  • Multi-hop tracing: variable tracking, a chain of assignments (X2 = X1, X3 = X2, ...) scattered through the input; return every name bound to the value.
  • Aggregation: the most common or most frequent words in a long list.
  • Question answering: SQuAD and HotpotQA questions with their paragraph hidden among distractor paragraphs.

Each task gets 500 examples per length (4K to 128K), and grading checks whether the target string appears in the answer. RULER's lasting contribution is a definition: the effective length is the largest length at which a model's average still beats Llama 2 7B at 4K (85.6%). Under that rule GPT-4, advertised at 128K, was effective to 64K, and of 17 models all claiming 32K or more, the paper says "only half" pass at 32K; Table 3 gives 10 of 17 derived. Every one of Table 3's 17 effective lengths reproduces from its per-length scores (recompute.py; the Effective length tab shows them).

The weakness is the threshold: an absolute 85.6% is a property of a 2023 model. A model that starts at 99% and falls to 86% passes; one that starts at 88% and stays flat looks almost as good. RULER also kept literal matching in its retrieval tasks. Its board stopped at 128K in October 2025 with Jamba 1.5 Large on top at 96.0% (README); frontier windows are now a million tokens, so vendors moved to MRCR. RULER survives inside papers as a quick regression test (the Qwen3 report and the Latent Context Language Models page both use it).

NoLiMa: take away the shared words

Modarressi et al. asked how much of long-context "retrieval" is string matching (NoLiMa, v3, ICML 2025). They measured how many of a question's words appear in the passage that answers it (ROUGE precision): 0.905 for vanilla NIAH, 0.571 for RULER's single-needle task, 0.966 for InfiniteBench's QA. Then they built needles where the question shares almost nothing with the needle and the link is world knowledge: the needle says a character lives next to the Semper Opera House; the question asks who has been to Dresden. Two-hop versions ask about Saxony, the state Dresden is in. Haystacks are snippets of ten open-licence books, filtered (by Llama 3.3 70B and a manual pass) so nothing else in them answers the question. Each of the 58 question-needle pairs is placed 26 times at equal intervals in each of 5 haystacks: 7,540 tests per length.

"Content words shared" is this page's count (question words minus a fixed list of function words, found in the needle), not the paper's ROUGE; both are in recompute.py.

The result is the paper's headline. Of 13 models claiming at least 128K, 11 score at 32K less than half their own short-context base score; GPT-4o, the strongest, goes from 99.3% to 69.7% (Table 3). Under NoLiMa's own rule (effective length is the largest length scoring above 85% of the model's base), GPT-4o is effective to 8K and most others to 2K or less. The later Table 10 adds GPT-4.1: claimed 1M, effective 16K, 64.7% at 128K. Literal matches also hurt when they point the wrong way: one distractor sentence that shares the question's words cuts GPT-4o's effective length to 1K.

Reasoning models do better but do not escape it. On NoLiMa-Hard (the 10 hardest pairs) o3 holds 58.5% at 32K from a base of 100%, Gemini 2.5 Pro 58.6% from 99.1%, o1 31.1% (NoLiMa README, update of 17 Jul 2025).

Beyond retrieval: BABILong, Michelangelo, HELMET, LongBench v2

Four suites from 2024 each push past "find the sentence", in different directions.

BABILong (Kuratov et al.)

The 20 bAbI reasoning tasks (fact chaining, induction, deduction, counting, lists) with their facts scattered through PG-19 book text, in splits up to 10M tokens. Finding: popular models "effectively utilize only 10-20% of the context"; retrieval-augmented generation reaches about 60% on single-fact questions regardless of length; fine-tuned recurrent memory transformers process up to 50M tokens (arXiv 2406.10149). Its effective-length rule is NoLiMa's: 85% of the 0K score.

Michelangelo (Google DeepMind)

Latent Structure Queries: the context hides a structure (a list built by a sequence of Python operations, a set of repeated requests, a story with an unanswerable question) and the model must "chisel away" the rest. Three tasks: Latent List, MRCR (multi-round co-reference) and IDK. On MRCR every frontier model it tested fell off before 32K; after that initial drop the Gemini models stayed flat to 1M while GPT and Claude kept falling at 128K (arXiv 2409.12640). MRCR became the industry's long-context number.

HELMET (Princeton)

Seven application categories (retrieval-augmented QA, generation with citations, re-ranking, many-shot in-context learning, long-document QA, summarisation, synthetic recall) at controllable lengths to 128K, with model-based grading where ROUGE fails. From 59 models: synthetic tasks like NIAH "do not reliably predict downstream performance", the categories correlate weakly with each other, and the open-closed gap widens with length (arXiv 2410.02694).

LongBench v2 (Tsinghua)

503 hard multiple-choice questions written by nearly 100 professionals over contexts of 8K to 2M words (single and multi-document QA, long in-context learning, dialogue history, code repositories, structured data). Experts under a 15-minute limit score 53.7%; at launch the best direct answer was 50.1% and o1-preview 57.7% (arXiv 2412.15204). The board's top is still Gemini 2.5 Pro at 63.3% (25 Mar 2025); no 2026 model has been added.

On 503 items a 63.3% score carries a 95% binomial half-width of about ±4.2 points derived (1.96·√(p(1−p)/n)); two LongBench v2 entries a few points apart are not separated.

The 2026 frontier: MRCR, Fiction.LiveBench, AA-LCR, GDP.pdf

OpenAI MRCR is the open version OpenAI released with GPT-4.1 in April 2025 (dataset card, MIT licence). A synthetic conversation asks for dozens of short pieces of writing ("write a poem about tapirs", "write a blog post about rocks"); hidden among them are 2, 4 or 8 identical requests, and the last turn asks the model to prepend a random string to, say, the 2nd poem about tapirs. Every reply was written by GPT-4o, so the needles look exactly like the hay. There are 2,400 rows: three needle counts, eight length bins from 4K to 1M tokens, 100 per bin. Grading is Python's difflib.SequenceMatcher ratio between the answer and the target, set to 0 if the random prefix is missing. The literal match is total here (both needles repeat the request word for word), so the difficulty is order and identity, not finding: the Grade a real MRCR item tab runs the grader on a real row.

Version trap: MRCR before and after 5 December 2025
A generation bug put too many target needles in about 10% of rows and wrong ground truth in about 5%; OpenAI replaced them on 5 December 2025 and added a date_added field (changelog). MRCR scores from before and after the fix are not comparable, and "MRCR" in a vendor table can also mean Google's original Michelangelo version.

The current readings show the gap that matters most in this family. OpenAI reports GPT-6 Astra at 96.3% on 8 needles in the 512K to 1M bin and 100.0% at 256K to 512K (OpenAI, 2 Sep 2026). The best independent 1M reading, Context Arena's run of the same 8-needle set, is 63.5% (Gemini 3.7 Flash high, 23 Aug 2026; Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 not yet listed; Context Arena). Saturated in the vendor's own number, not yet independently.

Fiction.LiveBench asks comprehension questions about long stories at fixed lengths from 0 to 192K tokens, with private stories and questions (Fiction.live). Its last table (4 April 2026) already had Gemini 3 Flash preview at 100.0% at 192K (table image); it is saturated and stopped. The short columns had gone earlier: GPT-5 (medium) scored 97.2% at 16K at its August 2025 release (Fiction.live leaderboard, via the Epoch AI hub); the long columns were where models still differed.

AA-LCR (Artificial Analysis Long Context Reasoning) is 100 questions over document sets of 10K to 100K tokens (counted with cl100k_base): company filings, consultations, legal and academic texts, needing reasoning across several documents; an LLM judges each answer equivalent or not (AA-LCR, v1.1). Mid-2024 frontier models were below 50%; Kimi K3 (max) now leads at 88.7% with Step 5 Preview 88.3% (read 1 Oct 2026). With 100 questions, ±6.2 points at 95% derived, the top dozen are statistically one group.

GDP.pdf is long-context and multimodal at once: 100 professional tasks over 100 PDFs, 4,592 pages of tables, charts and scans, with 1,275 expert-written criteria; a task passes only if every criterion passes (Garre et al., arXiv 2607.11192; Surge AI launch post). Items were kept only if at least two frontier models failed them, and the data are public. GPT-6 Astra leads under both harnesses, which do not agree: 34.2% on Surge's (leaderboard) and 32.2% on Artificial Analysis's five-attempt implementation (AA GDP.pdf), both read 4 Oct 2026. The 33.2% often quoted is AA's figure from the Index v4.2 launch on 4 September, before v4.3 changed image handling.

Advertised against effective context

Three generations of measurement say the same thing in different units. RULER (2024): GPT-4 claims 128K, effective 64K; Mistral 7B v0.2 claims 32K, effective 16K. NoLiMa (2025): GPT-4.1 claims 1M, effective 16K once the needle shares no words with the question. MRCR (2026): a vendor's 96.3% at 1M against 63.5% for the best independently measured model at 1M. An effective length is only defined with its threshold and its task, and the threshold is a choice: RULER's absolute 85.6% rewards a model that starts high and decays, NoLiMa's 85% of the model's own base rewards one that starts low and stays flat. On the Effective length tab you can move both rules and watch rankings reorder.

What to quote
A long-context claim needs four things: the benchmark and version, the length bin, the number of needles or the task, and who ran it. "1M context" on a model card is the API limit; "effective to 16K on NoLiMa" and "63.5% on 8-needle MRCR at 1M (Context Arena)" are measurements.

Multimodal: what is different about grading images

A multimodal question is only a test of seeing if the answer depends on what is seen. Three things make the family saturate faster and mislead more often than text benchmarks:

  • Blind answerability. The question text and options often give the answer away, or a language model has memorised the item. A score has to be read against the same model with the image removed.
  • Short answers. Chart, document and visual-maths sets have one-word or one-number answers that are quick to grade and quick to overfit; their human ceilings are reached within two or three years.
  • The input pipeline is part of the score. Image resolution, the number of video frames sampled, whether subtitles or audio are passed: each is a harness choice. For video the frame budget differs by API (Gemini at 1 frame per second, GPT-6 Astra 800 frames, Claude Opus 5.5 600, per Google's Gemini 4 Argon evaluation notes), so a cross-vendor video table compares pipelines as much as models.

MMMU, the blind baseline, and MMMU-Pro

MMMU (Yue et al., v4, November 2023) collected 11.5K questions from college exams, quizzes and textbooks: 30 subjects, 183 subfields, 30 image types (charts, diagrams, maps, music sheets, chemical structures). Splits: 150 development questions (5 per subject), 900 validation, 10.5K test with hidden answers. 90 senior students, three per subject, set the human reference on the validation questions: 76.2% (worst), 82.6% (median), 88.6% (best). At launch GPT-4V scored 56.8% on validation; random choice 22.1%, the most frequent option 26.8%, and GPT-4 given only the text 34.9%.

That last number is the problem. In March 2024 the MMStar authors ran language models with no image on six multimodal benchmarks (Chen et al., arXiv 2403.20330): Gemini Pro scored 42.9% on MMMU blind, and more than 20% of MMMU questions could be solved by most language models directly. Worse, multimodal training leaks items: Sphinx-X-MoE answered 43.6% of MMMU with the image removed, 17.9 points above its own language backbone. A question that a vision model answers without the image is a test of memory, not of sight.

MMMU-Pro (Yue et al., v3, September 2024) applied three fixes in order:

  1. Filter: four strong text-only models (Llama 3 70B, Qwen2 72B, Yi-1.5 34B, Mixtral 8x22B) each answered every question ten times without the image; a question was dropped if at least three of the four got it right in a majority of tries. 1,800 were sampled from the rest, 60 per subject.
  2. Augment: options grew from four to ten (GPT-4o proposed, Claude 3.5 filtered, two rounds of human review), which also removed 70 incoherent questions: 1,730 remain. Random choice falls from about 25% to 12.8%.
  3. Vision-only: each question was also photographed or screenshotted with its options, so the model has to read the text from the image. The MMMU-Pro score is the mean of the 10-option and vision-only settings.

GPT-4V reached 45.1% on MMMU validation with its image removed and 53.6% with it: the picture was worth 8.5 points, and 73% of its margin over random choice was there without it derived. On MMMU-Pro, GPT-4o went from 69.1% (MMMU validation) to 54.0% with ten options and 49.7% from screenshots; every model in the paper lost 13 to 27 points overall, and the printed differences all reproduce from the settings (recompute.py).

Both have since saturated. MMMU's best validation score is 85.4% (GPT-5.1, author-reported, 13 Nov 2025), inside the expert band; labs stopped reporting it in late 2025, and the team released the test answers on 12 February 2026, so later test-set scores can be trained on (MMMU leaderboard). MMMU-Pro's top is 86.9% (Chance Vision 1.5, author-reported, 1 Jul 2026; standard 87.6, vision 86.1), above the paper's high human estimate of 85.4%. That human figure is itself an estimate: MMMU-Pro was never sat by people; the authors re-scored the original MMMU expert answers, guessing among the ten options where an expert wrote no working.

Charts, documents and visual maths

MathVista (2023)

6,141 examples from 28 existing datasets plus three new ones (IQTest, FunctionQA, PaperQA); most reports use the 1,000-item testmini. At launch GPT-4V scored 49.9% against a human 60.3% (Lu et al.). The board's top is 85.2% (DreamPRM with o4-mini, 4 Jun 2025, leaderboard): 25 points above the human reference. Saturated.

ChartQA (2022)

9.6K human-written questions and 23.1K generated from chart summaries (Masry et al.); numeric answers are accepted within a small relative tolerance ("relaxed accuracy"). Templated charts and questions made it easy to overfit: on CharXiv's stress test, open models that beat proprietary ones on such sets dropped by up to 34.5% when the chart or question changed slightly.

CharXiv (2024)

2,323 real charts from arXiv papers, descriptive and reasoning questions, all hand-checked (Wang et al.). At launch GPT-4o scored 47.1% on reasoning questions against 80.5% for humans. 2026 cards moved chart understanding here and to newer sets.

DocVQA (2020)

50,000 questions on 12,000+ document images (Mathew et al.), graded by ANLS: one minus the normalised edit distance to the closest reference answer, set to zero when that distance is 0.5 or more, averaged over questions. Humans score 94.36; self-reported model scores of 96.4 exceed it, which means the remaining errors are mostly label noise. Saturated.

ZeroBench (2025)

100 questions adversarially filtered to be "impossible" for frontier models at release: 0% pass@1 and pass^5. After a year of tracking the authors report 6% pass^5 and 19% pass@5 (Roberts et al., v3). pass^5 (all five tries right) is the reliability metric, as on the parent's agentic family.

The pattern repeats MMMU's: short answers grade cleanly, so the set saturates within two or three years, and the successor (CharXiv, ZeroBench) is built by filtering for what current models fail. That buys headroom at the cost of a moving, model-defined target.

Video

Video-MME (Fu et al., May 2024) is 900 videos (254 hours, 11 seconds to an hour, six domains) with 2,700 hand-written four-option questions, scored with and without subtitles. Gemini 1.5 Pro led at launch. Its official board stopped in 2025 with video-SALMONN 2+ at 79.7% without subtitles (via the Epoch AI hub); vendor self-reports now reach 89.2% (ByteDance Seed 2.1 Pro, setting unstated, via llm-stats). Video-MMMU (Hu et al., January 2025) is 900 questions on 300 lecture videos in three stages (perception, comprehension, adaptation), with a knowledge-gain metric; self-reports near 88% are above its 74.4% human average. Both are saturating, and both share the frame-budget problem above.

Video-MME-v2 (arXiv 2604.05015, April 2026) is the successor built against the shortcuts. 800 recent, manually decontaminated videos, each with 4 linked questions of 8 options, in three levels (aggregating visual information, temporal dynamics, complex reasoning). A group-based non-linear score gives credit only when the linked questions are answered consistently, so lucky guesses do not add up. Every question was checked against a text-only baseline and blind testing, and re-checked after each correction. Human experts score 90.7 (94.9% plain accuracy); the best model, Gemini-3-Pro, 49.4 with audio and 38.2 on frames alone. TimeBlind (arXiv 2602.00288) uses minimal pairs, two videos with identical static content that differ only in what happens when: the best model distinguishes both in 48.2% of pairs against 98.2% for people, evidence that video scores have leaned on single frames.

Audio

MMAU (Sakshi et al., October 2024) pairs 10K clips of speech, environmental sound and music with human-written questions needing 27 skills. The paper's abstract reports Gemini Pro 1.5 at 52.97% and Qwen2-Audio at 52.50%; the project page now quotes 66.15% for "Gemini 1.5" and 55.4% for Qwen2-Audio, next to a revised test set (MMAU-v05.15.25). Those are different versions of the benchmark and should not be read as progress. Audio evaluation is younger and less standardised than vision: no audio set appears on 2026 frontier cards as a headline number, and the parent atlas has no audio row yet.

Common mistakes

Reading the context window as capability
The window is what the API accepts. RULER, NoLiMa and MRCR all find effective lengths far below it: GPT-4.1's 1M window was effective to 16K on NoLiMa.
Quoting a needle heatmap as long-context evidence
Single-needle retrieval is a string match and was near perfect by 2024; it also moved from 27% to 98% for Claude 2.1 with one prompt sentence. Use a test without lexical overlap or with several identical needles.
Comparing "effective lengths" under different rules
RULER's is absolute (85.6%), NoLiMa's and BABILong's relative (85% of the model's own short-context score). The same model can be effective to 64K under one and 2K under the other; the rule is part of the number.
Splicing MRCR versions or variants
Google's Michelangelo MRCR, OpenAI's MRCR before 5 December 2025 and after, 2, 4 or 8 needles, and each length bin are different numbers. A vendor's best bin is not a model's 1M score.
Ignoring who ran a long-context number
8-needle MRCR at 1M: 96.3% in OpenAI's own report against 63.5% for the best independently run model. GDP.pdf: 34.2% and 32.2% for the same model under two harnesses.
Assuming the image was used
Language models answer a fifth or more of MMMU without the image, and vision models trained on leaked items do better still. A multimodal score means little without the same model's blind score beside it.
Comparing MMMU validation with test, or MMMU with MMMU-Pro
Validation (900, answers always public) tops at 85.4%; the test top was 65.9% before its answers were released in February 2026. MMMU-Pro is a different, harder set scored as the mean of two settings.
Treating a video score as a model property
Frames sampled, resolution, subtitles and audio are harness choices. Video-MME-v2's best model moves from 38.2 to 49.4 just by adding audio.
Reading small gaps on small sets
AA-LCR and GDP.pdf have 100 items each: ±6 to ±9 points at 95%. A two-point lead is not a ranking.

Effective length: advertised against measured

An "effective context length" turns a score-by-length curve into one number: the longest tested length at which the model still clears a threshold. Two published rules use different thresholds. RULER's is absolute: beat Llama 2 7B at 4K, 85.6% (Hsieh et al., Table 3). NoLiMa's is relative: stay above 85% of the model's own short-context base score (Modarressi et al., Tables 3 and 10). Here are all 17 RULER rows and all 22 NoLiMa rows, with the claimed window beside the measured one. Change the rule and the threshold and watch the effective lengths, and the ranking, move.

Try: on NoLiMa, switch to the absolute rule at 85.6%: GPT-4o keeps 8K, but Claude 3.5 Sonnet falls below 1K and Gemini 2.0 Flash from 4K to 2K, because their base scores were lower. On RULER, the relative rule at 85% of each model's 4K score lifts the weak-but-flat LWM from under 4K to 16K and lets GLM4 clear every tested length. Click rows to compare their curves below.

RULER's "relative" rule uses each model's 4K score as its base (RULER defines no base score; this is this page's choice). Grade a real MRCR item

This is a real row of OpenAI's MRCR dataset (openai/mrcr, MIT licence; file 2needle/2needle_0.parquet, row 170, the shortest 2-needle row in that file), and the grader is OpenAI's own: Python's difflib.SequenceMatcher ratio, ported line by line to JavaScript and checked against Python on the presets below. It is 16,317 tokens of prompt plus answer (o200k tokenizer, counted here), so it sits in the 8K to 16K bin; longer rows add more requests, not a different task.

The final turn

Both needles repeat the request word for word ("write a short scene in a play about blueberries"), so finding them is easy; the task is telling the 1st from the 2nd and copying the right one exactly, with the random string in front.

The grader

score = 0 if the reply does not start with the prefix, else 2M ⁄ (|reply| + |target|)
M
characters in the matching blocks SequenceMatcher finds between the reply and the target, both with the prefix removed: it takes the longest common substring, then recurses on the pieces to its left and right
autojunk
when the target has 200 or more characters, any character occurring more than 1% of its length (plus one) cannot start a match; spaces and common letters are "popular" here, which is why near-copies still score close to 1 and unrelated texts close to 0

Try: the presets, then edit the reply: delete a line, change a name, drop the prefix's last character.

What the score means in the bins

A run averages this score over 100 rows per bin; the 8 bins run 4K to 8K, 8K to 16K, and doubling to 512K to 1M tokens of prompt plus answer (o200k tokenizer). Picking the wrong needle scores about 0.03 here, close to an unrelated text, so a bin's average is roughly the share of rows where the model copied the right one. Current readings, 8 needles: GPT-6 Astra 96.3% at 512K to 1M (OpenAI's own figure, 2 Sep 2026) and Gemini 3.7 Flash 63.5% at 1M (Context Arena, 23 Aug 2026); see the Reading tab for the December 2025 data fix.
