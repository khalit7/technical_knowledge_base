Multimodal: what is different about grading images

A multimodal question is only a test of seeing if the answer depends on what is seen. Three things make the family saturate faster and mislead more often than text benchmarks:

  • Blind answerability. The question text and options often give the answer away, or a language model has memorised the item. A score has to be read against the same model with the image removed.
  • Short answers. Chart, document and visual-maths sets have one-word or one-number answers that are quick to grade and quick to overfit; their human ceilings are reached within two or three years.
  • The input pipeline is part of the score. Image resolution, the number of video frames sampled, whether subtitles or audio are passed: each is a harness choice. For video the frame budget differs by API (Gemini at 1 frame per second, GPT-6 Astra 800 frames, Claude Opus 5.5 600, per {{Google's Gemini 4 Argon evaluation notes|https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/gemini/gemini_4_argon_model_evaluation.pdf}}), so a cross-vendor video table compares pipelines as much as models.

MMMU, the blind baseline, and MMMU-Pro

MMMU ({{Yue et al., v4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16502}}, November 2023) collected 11.5K questions from college exams, quizzes and textbooks: 30 subjects, 183 subfields, 30 image types (charts, diagrams, maps, music sheets, chemical structures). Splits: 150 development questions (5 per subject), 900 validation, 10.5K test with hidden answers. 90 senior students, three per subject, set the human reference on the validation questions: 76.2% (worst), 82.6% (median), 88.6% (best). At launch GPT-4V scored 56.8% on validation; random choice 22.1%, the most frequent option 26.8%, and GPT-4 given only the text 34.9%.

That last number is the problem. In March 2024 the MMStar authors ran language models with no image on six multimodal benchmarks ({{Chen et al., arXiv 2403.20330|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20330}}): Gemini Pro scored 42.9% on MMMU blind, and more than 20% of MMMU questions could be solved by most language models directly. Worse, multimodal training leaks items: Sphinx-X-MoE answered 43.6% of MMMU with the image removed, 17.9 points above its own language backbone. A question that a vision model answers without the image is a test of memory, not of sight.

MMMU-Pro ({{Yue et al., v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02813}}, September 2024) applied three fixes in order:

  1. Filter: four strong text-only models (Llama 3 70B, Qwen2 72B, Yi-1.5 34B, Mixtral 8x22B) each answered every question ten times without the image; a question was dropped if at least three of the four got it right in a majority of tries. 1,800 were sampled from the rest, 60 per subject.
  2. Augment: options grew from four to ten (GPT-4o proposed, Claude 3.5 filtered, two rounds of human review), which also removed 70 incoherent questions: 1,730 remain. Random choice falls from about 25% to 12.8%.
  3. Vision-only: each question was also photographed or screenshotted with its options, so the model has to read the text from the image. The MMMU-Pro score is the mean of the 10-option and vision-only settings.

Blind test: MMStar Table 3, MMMU validation column, 0-shot (the language model is the vision model's own base: GPT-4 Turbo for GPT-4V, Gemini Pro for Gemini Pro Vision). MMMU-Pro: Table 1 of arXiv 2409.02813v3 (each setting the better of direct and chain-of-thought prompting). The two halves come from different papers and models and are not one series.

GPT-4V reached 45.1% on MMMU validation with its image removed and 53.6% with it: the picture was worth 8.5 points, and 73% of its margin over random choice was there without it derived. On MMMU-Pro, GPT-4o went from 69.1% (MMMU validation) to 54.0% with ten options and 49.7% from screenshots; every model in the paper lost 13 to 27 points overall, and the printed differences all reproduce from the settings (recompute.py).

Both have since saturated. MMMU's best validation score is 85.4% (GPT-5.1, author-reported, 13 Nov 2025), inside the expert band; labs stopped reporting it in late 2025, and the team released the test answers on 12 February 2026, so later test-set scores can be trained on ({{MMMU leaderboard|https://mmmu-benchmark.github.io/}}). MMMU-Pro's top is 86.9% (Chance Vision 1.5, author-reported, 1 Jul 2026; standard 87.6, vision 86.1), above the paper's high human estimate of 85.4%. That human figure is itself an estimate: MMMU-Pro was never sat by people; the authors re-scored the original MMMU expert answers, guessing among the ten options where an expert wrote no working.

Charts, documents and visual maths

MathVista (2023)

6,141 examples from 28 existing datasets plus three new ones (IQTest, FunctionQA, PaperQA); most reports use the 1,000-item testmini. At launch GPT-4V scored 49.9% against a human 60.3% ({{Lu et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02255}}). The board's top is 85.2% (DreamPRM with o4-mini, 4 Jun 2025, {{leaderboard|https://mathvista.github.io/}}): 25 points above the human reference. Saturated.

ChartQA (2022)

9.6K human-written questions and 23.1K generated from chart summaries ({{Masry et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10244}}); numeric answers are accepted within a small relative tolerance ("relaxed accuracy"). Templated charts and questions made it easy to overfit: on CharXiv's stress test, open models that beat proprietary ones on such sets dropped by up to 34.5% when the chart or question changed slightly.

CharXiv (2024)

2,323 real charts from arXiv papers, descriptive and reasoning questions, all hand-checked ({{Wang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18521}}). At launch GPT-4o scored 47.1% on reasoning questions against 80.5% for humans. 2026 cards moved chart understanding here and to newer sets.

DocVQA (2020)

50,000 questions on 12,000+ document images ({{Mathew et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00398}}), graded by ANLS: one minus the normalised edit distance to the closest reference answer, set to zero when that distance is 0.5 or more, averaged over questions. Humans score 94.36; self-reported model scores of 96.4 exceed it, which means the remaining errors are mostly label noise. Saturated.

ZeroBench (2025)

100 questions adversarially filtered to be "impossible" for frontier models at release: 0% pass@1 and pass^5. After a year of tracking the authors report 6% pass^5 and 19% pass@5 ({{Roberts et al., v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09696}}). pass^5 (all five tries right) is the reliability metric, as on the parent's agentic family.

The pattern repeats MMMU's: short answers grade cleanly, so the set saturates within two or three years, and the successor (CharXiv, ZeroBench) is built by filtering for what current models fail. That buys headroom at the cost of a moving, model-defined target.

Video

Video-MME ({{Fu et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21075}}, May 2024) is 900 videos (254 hours, 11 seconds to an hour, six domains) with 2,700 hand-written four-option questions, scored with and without subtitles. Gemini 1.5 Pro led at launch. Its official board stopped in 2025 with video-SALMONN 2+ at 79.7% without subtitles (via the {{Epoch AI hub|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks}}); vendor self-reports now reach 89.2% (ByteDance Seed 2.1 Pro, setting unstated, via {{llm-stats|https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks}}). Video-MMMU ({{Hu et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13826}}, January 2025) is 900 questions on 300 lecture videos in three stages (perception, comprehension, adaptation), with a knowledge-gain metric; self-reports near 88% are above its 74.4% human average. Both are saturating, and both share the frame-budget problem above.

Video-MME-v2 ({{arXiv 2604.05015|https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05015}}, April 2026) is the successor built against the shortcuts. 800 recent, manually decontaminated videos, each with 4 linked questions of 8 options, in three levels (aggregating visual information, temporal dynamics, complex reasoning). A group-based non-linear score gives credit only when the linked questions are answered consistently, so lucky guesses do not add up. Every question was checked against a text-only baseline and blind testing, and re-checked after each correction. Human experts score 90.7 (94.9% plain accuracy); the best model, Gemini-3-Pro, 49.4 with audio and 38.2 on frames alone. TimeBlind ({{arXiv 2602.00288|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00288}}) uses minimal pairs, two videos with identical static content that differ only in what happens when: the best model distinguishes both in 48.2% of pairs against 98.2% for people, evidence that video scores have leaned on single frames.

Audio

MMAU ({{Sakshi et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19168}}, October 2024) pairs 10K clips of speech, environmental sound and music with human-written questions needing 27 skills. The paper's abstract reports Gemini Pro 1.5 at 52.97% and Qwen2-Audio at 52.50%; the {{project page|https://sakshi113.github.io/mmau_homepage/}} now quotes 66.15% for "Gemini 1.5" and 55.4% for Qwen2-Audio, next to a revised test set (MMAU-v05.15.25). Those are different versions of the benchmark and should not be read as progress. Audio evaluation is younger and less standardised than vision: no audio set appears on 2026 frontier cards as a headline number, and the parent atlas has no audio row yet.
