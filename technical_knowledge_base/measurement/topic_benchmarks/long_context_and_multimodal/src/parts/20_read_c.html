Beyond retrieval: connect and aggregate

Five research suites push past "find the sentence", each in a different direction; what each found is the reason to know it.

MRCR: every needle matches, so the task is order

OpenAI MRCR is the open version OpenAI released with GPT-4.1 in April 2025 ({{dataset card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/mrcr}}, MIT licence). A synthetic conversation asks for dozens of short pieces of writing ("write a poem about tapirs", "write a blog post about rocks"); hidden among them are 2, 4 or 8 identical requests, and the last turn asks the model to prepend a random string to, say, the 2nd poem about tapirs. Every reply was written by GPT-4o, so the needles look exactly like the hay. 2,400 rows: three needle counts, eight length bins from 4K to 1M tokens, 100 per bin. The literal match is total (the needles repeat the request word for word), so the difficulty is identity and order, not finding. Here is a real row and the real grader:

Picking the other needle scores about 0.03, as low as an unrelated text: a bin's average is roughly the share of rows copied from the right needle

Row 170 of {{openai/mrcr|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/mrcr}} (2needle/2needle_0.parquet, the shortest 2-needle row in that file): 16,317 tokens of prompt plus answer (o200k, counted here), so it sits in the 8K to 16K bin; longer rows add more requests, not a different task. Each block is one message, width proportional to its length; click one to see it.

Both needles repeat "write a short scene in a play about blueberries"; the task is telling the 1st from the 2nd and copying the right one exactly, with the random string in front.

characters in the matching blocks Python's difflib.SequenceMatcher finds between reply and target, prefix removed: the longest common substring, then recursively the pieces to its left and right

Predict, then try: what does the wrong needle score? Pick the presets, then edit the reply: delete a line, change a name, drop the prefix's last character.

Why near-copies score near 1 and unrelated texts near 0 (autojunk)
When the target has 200 or more characters, SequenceMatcher's autojunk rule stops any character occurring more than 1% of its length (plus one) from starting a match; spaces and common letters are "popular" here, so only longer shared runs count. The grader is ported line by line to JavaScript and equals Python's difflib on 64 random and mutated pairs and the six presets (check_seqmatch.py, recompute.py).
Version trap: MRCR before and after 5 December 2025
A generation bug put too many target needles in about 10% of rows and wrong ground truth in about 5%; OpenAI replaced them on 5 December 2025 and added a date_added field ({{changelog|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/mrcr}}). Scores from before and after are not comparable; "MRCR" in a vendor table can also mean Google's original Michelangelo version; and 2, 4 and 8 needles and each length bin are different numbers, so a vendor's best bin is not a model's 1M score.

The readings show the gap that matters most in this family: OpenAI reports GPT-6 Astra at 96.3% on 8 needles at 512K to 1M; the best independent run of the same set at 1M is 63.5% (Context Arena). Saturated in the vendor's own number, not yet independently (both dated in the readings table).

Real documents: Fiction.LiveBench, AA-LCR, GDP.pdf

Fiction.LiveBench asks comprehension questions about private long stories at fixed lengths from 0 to 192K tokens ({{Fiction.live|https://fiction.live/stories/Fiction-liveBench/oQdzQvKHw8JyXbN87}}). The short columns went first (GPT-5 medium 97.2% at 16K at its August 2025 release, via the {{Epoch AI hub|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks}}), then the long ones; its last table (4 April 2026, {{table image|https://cdn6.fiction.live/file/fictionlive/a7be0188-9a0a-4b19-a4b3-97e7deecf52e.png}}) had a model at 100.0% at 192K, and it stopped.

AA-LCR (Artificial Analysis Long Context Reasoning, {{v1.1|https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/artificial-analysis-long-context-reasoning}}) is 100 questions over document sets of 10K to 100K tokens (company filings, consultations, legal and academic texts) that need reasoning across several documents; an LLM judges each answer equivalent or not. Mid-2024 frontier models were below 50%. With 100 questions the 95% half-width at the top is about ±6.2 points derived, so the top dozen are statistically one group.

GDP.pdf is long-context and multimodal at once: 100 professional tasks over 100 PDFs, 4,592 pages of tables, charts and scans, with 1,275 expert-written criteria; a task passes only if every criterion passes ({{Garre et al., arXiv 2607.11192|https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11192}}; {{Surge AI launch post|https://surgehq.ai/blog/gdp-pdf-can-100b-ai-models-master-the-documents-that-run-the-world}}). Items were kept only if at least two frontier models failed them, and the data are public. The two harnesses that run it do not agree for the same model (Surge's and Artificial Analysis's five-attempt implementation); the 33.2% often quoted is Artificial Analysis's figure from the Index v4.2 launch on 4 September, before v4.3 changed image handling. Grading by LLM judge is taught on {{Topic: evaluation-and-llm-judges|n:3c65c17b0d0d8181b351e1b8fc6a1546}}.
