Multimodal: what is different about grading images

A multimodal question tests seeing only if the answer depends on what is seen. Three things make this family saturate faster and mislead more often than text benchmarks: blind answerability (the text and options give the answer away, or the item was memorised: read a score against the same model with the image removed); short answers (one word or number, quick to grade and quick to overfit: human ceilings fall within two or three years); and the input pipeline is part of the score (image resolution, frames sampled, subtitles or audio are harness choices; for video the frame budget differs by API, Gemini at 1 frame per second, GPT-6 Astra 800 frames, Claude Opus 5.5 600, per {{Google's Gemini 4 Argon evaluation notes|https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/gemini/gemini_4_argon_model_evaluation.pdf}}, so a cross-vendor video table compares pipelines as much as models).

MMMU, the blind baseline, and MMMU-Pro

MMMU ({{Yue et al., v4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16502}}, November 2023): 11.5K questions from college exams, quizzes and textbooks, 30 subjects, 183 subfields, 30 image types (charts, diagrams, maps, music sheets, chemical structures). At launch GPT-4V scored 56.8% on validation; random choice 22.1%, the most frequent option 26.8%, and GPT-4 given only the text 34.9%. That last number is the problem. The MMStar authors ran models with no image on six multimodal benchmarks ({{Chen et al., arXiv 2403.20330|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20330}}): Gemini Pro scored 42.9% on MMMU blind, more than 20% of MMMU questions could be solved by most language models directly, and multimodal training leaks items: Sphinx-X-MoE answered 43.6% with the image removed, 17.9 points above its own language backbone. Their fix, MMStar, is 1,500 human-checked samples that need the image.

MMMU-Pro ({{Yue et al., v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02813}}, September 2024) applied three fixes in order: filter (drop questions that text-only models answer), augment to ten options (random choice falls from about 25% to 12.8%; 1,730 questions remain), and vision-only (question and options arrive as one screenshot). The score is the mean of the 10-option and vision-only settings.

Predict, then step: how much of GPT-4V's score survives with no image? Step through "MMMU, blind test", then switch to "MMMU-Pro, the fix" and watch the three changes for GPT-4o and then for every model in the paper (LLaVA-OneVision-72B loses 14 points to the screenshot alone).

Both have since saturated (readings in the table); MMMU's validation answers were always public and the team released the test answers on 12 February 2026, so later test-set scores can be trained on ({{MMMU leaderboard|https://mmmu-benchmark.github.io/}}). Validation and test are different numbers: the test top was 65.9% before its answers were released.

How MMMU and MMMU-Pro were built (splits, human reference, filter details)

MMMU splits: 150 development questions (5 per subject), 900 validation, 10.5K test with hidden answers. 90 senior students, three per subject, set the human reference on validation: 76.2% (worst), 82.6% (median), 88.6% (best).

MMMU-Pro's filter: four text-only models (Llama 3 70B, Qwen2 72B, Yi-1.5 34B, Mixtral 8x22B) each answered every question ten times without the image; a question was dropped if at least three of the four got it right in a majority of tries; 1,800 were sampled from the rest, 60 per subject. Options grew to ten (GPT-4o proposed, Claude 3.5 filtered, two rounds of human review), which also removed 70 incoherent questions. In the vision-only setting each question was photographed or screenshotted with its options.

MMMU-Pro's human figure (85.4% high) is an estimate: MMMU-Pro was never sat by people; the authors re-scored the original MMMU expert answers, guessing among the ten options where an expert wrote no working.

Yes-bias and option bias: POPE and MMBench

Two more ways to score without looking, each with a benchmark built to expose it. POPE ({{Li et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10355}}) asks "Is there a <object> in the image?" for present objects and for absent ones drawn at random, from the most frequent objects (popular) or from objects that co-occur with what is there (adversarial), half yes and half no; it reports accuracy, F1 and the yes-rate. MMBench ({{Liu et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06281}}) attacks guessing and option-letter bias with CircularEval.

Report the yes-rate beside any yes/no accuracy and the circular score beside any multiple-choice one. How to measure and reduce a VLM's hallucination in practice (and the POPE-style probe run on SmolVLM) is on {{Vision-language models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81adbb7be5bc41c4d9d0}} and {{Evaluating generative models|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81c890f4f62543312faa}}.

Charts, documents, OCR and visual maths

Short answers grade cleanly, so these sets saturate within two or three years, and each successor (CharXiv, ZeroBench, OCRBench v2) is built by filtering for what current models fail: headroom at the cost of a moving, model-defined target.

MathVista (2023)

6,141 examples from 28 existing datasets plus three new ones (IQTest, FunctionQA, PaperQA); most reports use the 1,000-item testmini. At launch GPT-4V scored 49.9% against a human 60.3% ({{Lu et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02255}}). The board's top is now 25 points above the human reference. Saturated.

ChartQA (2022)

9.6K human-written questions and 23.1K generated from chart summaries ({{Masry et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10244}}); numeric answers accepted within a small relative tolerance ("relaxed accuracy"). Templated charts made it easy to overfit: on CharXiv's stress test, open models that beat proprietary ones on such sets dropped by up to 34.5% when the chart or question changed slightly.

CharXiv (2024)

2,323 real charts from arXiv papers, descriptive and reasoning questions, all hand-checked ({{Wang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18521}}). At launch GPT-4o scored 47.1% on reasoning questions against 80.5% for humans. 2026 cards moved chart understanding here.

OCRBench (2023)

1,000 questions from 29 datasets in five parts: text recognition, scene-text VQA, document VQA, key information extraction, handwritten maths expressions ({{Liu et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07895}}; {{repository|https://github.com/Yuliang-Liu/MultimodalOCR}}). Reading text was the weakest skill of early VLMs. OCRBench v2 (31 December 2024): 10,000 human-verified pairs across 31 scenarios.

ZeroBench (2025)

100 questions adversarially filtered to be "impossible" for frontier models at release: 0% pass@1 and pass^5. After a year of tracking the authors report 6% pass^5 and 19% pass@5 ({{Roberts et al., v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09696}}). pass^5 (all five tries right) is the reliability metric, as on the agentic family.

DocVQA ({{Mathew et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00398}}): 50,000 questions on 12,000+ document images, graded by ANLS: one minus the edit distance to the closest reference answer divided by the longer string's length, set to zero when that ratio is 0.5 or more, averaged over questions. Humans score 94.36; self-reported model scores of 96.4 exceed it, which means the remaining errors are mostly label noise. Saturated.

Video and audio

Video-MME ({{Fu et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21075}}, May 2024): 900 videos (254 hours, 11 seconds to an hour, six domains), 2,700 hand-written four-option questions, scored with and without subtitles; Gemini 1.5 Pro led at launch and the official board stopped in 2025. Video-MMMU ({{Hu et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13826}}, January 2025): 900 questions on 300 lecture videos in three stages (perception, comprehension, adaptation) with a knowledge-gain metric; self-reports near 88% are above its 74.4% human average. Both are saturating, and both share the frame-budget problem.

Video-MME-v2 ({{arXiv 2604.05015|https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05015}}, April 2026) is the successor built against the shortcuts: 800 recent, manually decontaminated videos, each with 4 linked questions of 8 options, in three levels (aggregating visual information, temporal dynamics, complex reasoning). A group-based non-linear score gives credit only when the linked questions are answered consistently, so lucky guesses do not add up; every question was checked against a text-only baseline and blind testing. Human experts score 90.7 (94.9% plain accuracy); the best model, Gemini-3-Pro, 49.4 with audio and 38.2 on frames alone, so adding audio alone moves it 11 points. TimeBlind ({{arXiv 2602.00288|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00288}}) uses minimal pairs, two videos with identical static content that differ only in what happens when: the best model distinguishes both in 48.2% of pairs against 98.2% for people, evidence that video scores have leaned on single frames.

MMAU ({{Sakshi et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19168}}, October 2024) pairs 10K clips of speech, environmental sound and music with human-written questions needing 27 skills. The paper's abstract reports Gemini Pro 1.5 at 52.97% and Qwen2-Audio at 52.50%; the {{project page|https://sakshi113.github.io/mmau_homepage/}} now quotes 66.15% for "Gemini 1.5" and 55.4% for Qwen2-Audio next to a revised test set (MMAU-v05.15.25): different versions, not progress. Audio evaluation is younger than vision: no audio set is a headline number on 2026 frontier cards, and the parent atlas has no audio row yet.
