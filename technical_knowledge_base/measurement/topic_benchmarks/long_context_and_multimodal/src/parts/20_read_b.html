Needle in a haystack: solved by its own design

Greg Kamradt's open-source test ({{LLMTest_NeedleInAHaystack|https://github.com/gkamradt/LLMTest_NeedleInAHaystack}}, November 2023) plants one sentence in a long run of essays and asks for it back, sweeping context length on one axis and needle depth on the other. Its needle and question, as Anthropic later quoted them ({{Anthropic, 6 Dec 2023|https://claude.com/blog/claude-2-1-prompting}}), are beside this text with the shared words marked, above the test's own output for one model.

Two things make it weak. The question repeats the needle's words, so finding it is a string match, the operation attention does best. And it measures the prompt as much as memory: Claude 2.1 scored 27% until Anthropic added "Here is the most relevant sentence in the context:" to the start of the answer, which raised it to 98% ({{Anthropic|https://claude.com/blog/claude-2-1-prompting}}); the model had been declining to answer from a sentence that looked out of place, not failing to find it. By 2024 RULER's authors note "nearly perfect accuracy in the vanilla NIAH test" for every model they tested ({{RULER abstract|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654}}), and the Llama 3 report claims perfect single-needle retrieval at all depths and lengths ({{Llama 3 Herd paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81aca58ccb9d720b474e}}). It is retired as evidence: no 2026 card reports it.

Position matters: lost in the middle

Liu et al. put twenty retrieved Wikipedia passages in the prompt, exactly one with the answer, and moved that passage from first to last ({{Lost in the Middle, v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172}}). Accuracy traces a U: high when the evidence comes first (primacy) or last (recency), lowest in the middle.

Fig. 1 · GPT-3.5-Turbo with the answer in the middle does worse than with no passages at all

  1. Twenty passages, one with the answer. Each tile is one retrieved passage, in prompt order; the blue one holds the answer, and the dots run from it to the model. With it first, GPT-3.5-Turbo answers 75.8% of questions correctly.
  2. Move it to the middle. At position 5 the score is 57.2%, at position 10 it is 53.8%: 22.0 points below the first position, with the same passages and the same question.
  3. Then to the end. Position 15 gives 55.4% and position 20 gives 63.2%: the end recovers part of the loss (recency), so the five columns trace a U.
  4. Against no passages, and against only the right one. The lower glass plane is the closed-book score, with no passages at all: 56.1%. The upper one is the oracle, the answer passage alone: 88.3%. At positions 10 and 15 (orange) the model does worse than with no passages at all.
  5. Six models, one row each. The same test for every model in Table 6: GPT-3.5-Turbo and its 16K version dip by about 22 points (orange: below the model's own closed-book score), MPT-30B-Instruct by 4.5 and LongChat-13B (16K) by 16.1, while Claude-1.3's row moves by about 4. How deep the U is depends on the model.

Values from Table 6 (20 documents) and Table 1 (closed-book, oracle) of arXiv 2307.03172v3; positions are the gold passage's index 0, 4, 9, 14, 19 shown 1-based. Columns stand only at the five measured positions.

The effect is not universal: Claude-1.3's curve moves by about 4 points, GPT-3.5-Turbo's by 22. And it changes with the test: once the needle shares no words with the question, length matters more than position, because the model can no longer find the needle wherever it is (Section 4.4.2 of {{NoLiMa|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167}}). A benchmark that places evidence only at the edges overstates; a depth chart describes one model family at one length.

RULER and effective length: a curve turned into one number

NVIDIA's RULER ({{Hsieh et al., v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654}}, April 2024) kept the needle's convenience (synthetic, any length, exact grading) and widened it to 13 tasks in four categories (retrieval, multi-hop tracing, aggregation, question answering), 500 examples per task and length from 4K to 128K; an answer counts if it contains the target string.

RULER's 13 tasks (what each category asks)
Retrieval: eight needle variants: one or several keys, several values per key, several queries, distractors, keys that are words, numbers or UUIDs. Multi-hop tracing: variable tracking, a chain X2 = X1, X3 = X2, ... scattered through the input; return every name bound to the value. Aggregation: the most common or most frequent words in a long list. Question answering: SQuAD and HotpotQA paragraphs hidden among distractor paragraphs.

Its lasting contribution is a definition: the effective length is the largest length at which a model's average still beats Llama 2 7B at 4K, 85.6%. Under that rule GPT-4, advertised at 128K, is effective to 64K; of 17 models all claiming 32K or more, the paper says "only half" pass at 32K, and Table 3 gives 10 of 17 derived. Examples from Table 3 (claimed / effective): GPT-4 128K / 64K; Llama 3.1 70B 128K / 64K; Qwen2 72B 128K / 32K; Mistral 7B v0.2 32K / 16K. NoLiMa later used a relative rule: above 85% of the model's own short-context score (BABILong uses the same). Which rule you pick changes the answer, and the chart beside this text lets you see by how much.

Predict, then try: on NoLiMa, switch to the absolute rule at 85.6%: which loses more, GPT-4o (base 99.3%) or Claude 3.5 Sonnet (base 87.5%)?
Answer
GPT-4o keeps 8K; Claude 3.5 Sonnet falls below 1K and Gemini 2.0 Flash from 4K to 2K, because their base scores were lower. On RULER, the relative rule at 85% of each model's 4K score lifts the weak-but-flat LWM from under 4K to 16K.
Click rows to compare curves.

The weakness is the threshold. An absolute 85.6% is a property of a 2023 model: a model that starts at 99% and falls to 86% passes, one that starts at 88% and stays flat looks almost as good. A relative rule rewards the reverse. RULER also kept literal matching in its retrieval tasks. Its board stopped at 128K in October 2025 ({{README|https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER}}); with frontier windows at a million tokens, vendors moved to MRCR, and RULER survives in papers as a quick regression test (the {{Qwen3 report|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a19006e6b096a6e14b}} and {{Latent Context Language Models|n:3d45c17b0d0d81eb90a8d70a5ce84d19}} use it).

NoLiMa: take away the shared words

Modarressi et al. measured how many of a question's words appear in the passage that answers it (ROUGE precision): 0.905 for vanilla NIAH, 0.571 for RULER's single-needle task, 0.966 for InfiniteBench's QA ({{NoLiMa, v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167}}, ICML 2025). Then they built needles whose link to the question is world knowledge: the needle says a character lives next to the Semper Opera House; the question asks who has been to Dresden. Two-hop versions ask about Saxony, the state Dresden is in.

Predict, then step: same model (Llama 3.3 70B), same needle, same haystacks; only the question changes. How much of the Direct score survives at 32K once the question shares no words with the needle?

Fig. 2 · Same needle, same model: remove the shared words and accuracy falls with length

  1. One needle in a haystack. The haystack is book snippets, drawn here as one block per 1K tokens; the needle, "Actually, Yuki lives next to the Semper Opera House.", is placed at 26 depths in turn (shown at mid depth) in each of 5 haystacks.
  2. Direct question: a literal match. "Which character lives next to the Semper Opera House?" 4 of its 5 content words are in the needle, so the dots go straight to it. 98.3% at 8K, 98.5% at 16K and 32K: with a literal match, length barely matters.
  3. One-hop: the shared words are gone. "Which character has been to Dresden?" shares 0 of 2 content words with the needle. The model has to bring a fact the context never states, the Semper Opera House is in Dresden (the glass plate), before the needle looks relevant. 84.1% at 8K.
  4. Make the haystack longer. The same one-hop question at 16K scores 73.2%, at 32K 56.2%: 42.3 points below the Direct question at the same length.
  5. Two-hop. "Which character has been to the state of Saxony?" needs two facts: Saxony contains Dresden, and Dresden contains the Semper Opera House. 57.4%, 42.7% and 25.9% at 8K, 16K and 32K: 72.6 points below Direct at 32K.
  6. Give the answer options. Listing four names in the question, Yuki and three other characters from the haystack, restores a literal match without changing the reasoning: one-hop 98.7%, 97.4%, 93.1% and two-hop 96.3%, 94.6%, 87.2%, only 5.4 and 11.3 points below Direct at 32K. The model searches for the names instead of reasoning its way to the needle.

Scores: NoLiMa v3 Table 6 (Llama 3.3 70B at 8K, 16K, 32K; the multiple-choice rows put three haystack characters and the answer in the question). The Direct question is worded here from the paper's definition; the one-hop and two-hop questions are quoted. "Content words shared" is this page's count (question words minus a fixed list of function words, found in the needle), not the paper's ROUGE; both are in recompute.py.

The headline: of 13 models claiming at least 128K, 11 score at 32K less than half their own short-context base; GPT-4o, the strongest, goes from 99.3% to 69.7% (Table 3). Under NoLiMa's rule GPT-4o is effective to 8K and most others to 2K or less; Table 10 adds GPT-4.1: claimed 1M, effective 16K, 64.7% at 128K. Literal matches also hurt when they point the wrong way: one distractor sentence that shares the question's words cuts GPT-4o's effective length to 1K. Reasoning models do better without escaping it: on NoLiMa-Hard (the 10 hardest pairs) o3 holds 58.5% at 32K from a base of 100%, Gemini 2.5 Pro 58.6% from 99.1%, o1 31.1% ({{NoLiMa README, update of 17 Jul 2025|https://github.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa}}).

How NoLiMa is built (haystacks, placements, licence)
Haystacks are snippets of ten open-licence books, filtered (by Llama 3.3 70B and a manual pass) so nothing else in them answers the question. Each of the 58 question-needle pairs is placed 26 times at equal intervals in each of 5 haystacks: 7,540 tests per length. The data are released under the Adobe Research licence (non-commercial research only), so this page quotes the paper's example rather than redistributing the needle set.
