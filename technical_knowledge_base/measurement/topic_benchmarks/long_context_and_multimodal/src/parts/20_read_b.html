RULER: from one needle to thirteen tasks, and "effective length"

NVIDIA's RULER ({{Hsieh et al., v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654}}, April 2024) kept the needle's convenience (synthetic, any length, exact grading) and widened the tasks to 13 in four categories:

  • Retrieval: eight needle variants: one or several keys, several values for one key, several queries, distractor needles, keys that are words, numbers or UUIDs.
  • Multi-hop tracing: variable tracking, a chain of assignments (X2 = X1, X3 = X2, ...) scattered through the input; return every name bound to the value.
  • Aggregation: the most common or most frequent words in a long list.
  • Question answering: SQuAD and HotpotQA questions with their paragraph hidden among distractor paragraphs.

Each task gets 500 examples per length (4K to 128K), and grading checks whether the target string appears in the answer. RULER's lasting contribution is a definition: the effective length is the largest length at which a model's average still beats Llama 2 7B at 4K (85.6%). Under that rule GPT-4, advertised at 128K, was effective to 64K, and of 17 models all claiming 32K or more, the paper says "only half" pass at 32K; Table 3 gives 10 of 17 derived. Every one of Table 3's 17 effective lengths reproduces from its per-length scores (recompute.py; the Effective length tab shows them).

The weakness is the threshold: an absolute 85.6% is a property of a 2023 model. A model that starts at 99% and falls to 86% passes; one that starts at 88% and stays flat looks almost as good. RULER also kept literal matching in its retrieval tasks. Its board stopped at 128K in October 2025 with Jamba 1.5 Large on top at 96.0% ({{README|https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER}}); frontier windows are now a million tokens, so vendors moved to MRCR. RULER survives inside papers as a quick regression test (the {{Qwen3 report|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a19006e6b096a6e14b}} and the {{Latent Context Language Models|n:3d45c17b0d0d81eb90a8d70a5ce84d19}} page both use it).

NoLiMa: take away the shared words

Modarressi et al. asked how much of long-context "retrieval" is string matching ({{NoLiMa, v3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167}}, ICML 2025). They measured how many of a question's words appear in the passage that answers it (ROUGE precision): 0.905 for vanilla NIAH, 0.571 for RULER's single-needle task, 0.966 for InfiniteBench's QA. Then they built needles where the question shares almost nothing with the needle and the link is world knowledge: the needle says a character lives next to the Semper Opera House; the question asks who has been to Dresden. Two-hop versions ask about Saxony, the state Dresden is in. Haystacks are snippets of ten open-licence books, filtered (by Llama 3.3 70B and a manual pass) so nothing else in them answers the question. Each of the 58 question-needle pairs is placed 26 times at equal intervals in each of 5 haystacks: 7,540 tests per length.

One needle, three questions animation

Try: run Direct, then One-hop, then Two-hop. Same model (Llama 3.3 70B), same needle, same haystacks; only the question changes. Tick "answer options" to put the four candidate names in the question, which restores a literal match without changing the reasoning.

Scores: NoLiMa v3 Table 6 (Llama 3.3 70B at 8K, 16K, 32K; the multiple-choice rows put three haystack characters and the answer in the question). The Direct question is worded here from the paper's definition (it states the needle's keyword); the one-hop and two-hop questions are quoted. "Content words shared" is this page's count (question words minus a fixed list of function words, found in the needle), not the paper's ROUGE; both are in recompute.py.

The result is the paper's headline. Of 13 models claiming at least 128K, 11 score at 32K less than half their own short-context base score; GPT-4o, the strongest, goes from 99.3% to 69.7% (Table 3). Under NoLiMa's own rule (effective length is the largest length scoring above 85% of the model's base), GPT-4o is effective to 8K and most others to 2K or less. The later Table 10 adds GPT-4.1: claimed 1M, effective 16K, 64.7% at 128K. Literal matches also hurt when they point the wrong way: one distractor sentence that shares the question's words cuts GPT-4o's effective length to 1K.

Reasoning models do better but do not escape it. On NoLiMa-Hard (the 10 hardest pairs) o3 holds 58.5% at 32K from a base of 100%, Gemini 2.5 Pro 58.6% from 99.1%, o1 31.1% ({{NoLiMa README, update of 17 Jul 2025|https://github.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa}}).

Corrections found while checking
  • The v3 text says Llama 3.1 70B falls to "42.7% vs. 94.3% base score" at 32K; Table 3 gives Llama 3.1 70B 43.2% from 94.5%, and 42.7% belongs to Llama 3.3 70B (base 97.3%). The tables are used here.
  • Eight rows of the arXiv v3 tables print an effective length of "1K" where the 1K score is already below the threshold (Gemini 1.5 Flash: 68.6% against 72.0%); the GitHub README prints "<1K" for them, which is what the rule gives. The Effective length tab shows both.
  • The README and v3 tables differ by 0.1 in three cells (Claude 3.5 Sonnet base 87.6 against 87.5; GPT-4o mini base 84.9 against 84.8 and 4K 44.1 against 44.2). The paper is used here.

Beyond retrieval: BABILong, Michelangelo, HELMET, LongBench v2

Four suites from 2024 each push past "find the sentence", in different directions.

BABILong (Kuratov et al.)

The 20 bAbI reasoning tasks (fact chaining, induction, deduction, counting, lists) with their facts scattered through PG-19 book text, in splits up to 10M tokens. Finding: popular models "effectively utilize only 10-20% of the context"; retrieval-augmented generation reaches about 60% on single-fact questions regardless of length; fine-tuned recurrent memory transformers process up to 50M tokens ({{arXiv 2406.10149|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10149}}). Its effective-length rule is NoLiMa's: 85% of the 0K score.

Michelangelo (Google DeepMind)

Latent Structure Queries: the context hides a structure (a list built by a sequence of Python operations, a set of repeated requests, a story with an unanswerable question) and the model must "chisel away" the rest. Three tasks: Latent List, MRCR (multi-round co-reference) and IDK. On MRCR every frontier model it tested fell off before 32K; after that initial drop the Gemini models stayed flat to 1M while GPT and Claude kept falling at 128K ({{arXiv 2409.12640|https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12640}}). MRCR became the industry's long-context number.

HELMET (Princeton)

Seven application categories (retrieval-augmented QA, generation with citations, re-ranking, many-shot in-context learning, long-document QA, summarisation, synthetic recall) at controllable lengths to 128K, with model-based grading where ROUGE fails. From 59 models: synthetic tasks like NIAH "do not reliably predict downstream performance", the categories correlate weakly with each other, and the open-closed gap widens with length ({{arXiv 2410.02694|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02694}}).

LongBench v2 (Tsinghua)

503 hard multiple-choice questions written by nearly 100 professionals over contexts of 8K to 2M words (single and multi-document QA, long in-context learning, dialogue history, code repositories, structured data). Experts under a 15-minute limit score 53.7%; at launch the best direct answer was 50.1% and o1-preview 57.7% ({{arXiv 2412.15204|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15204}}). The board's top is still Gemini 2.5 Pro at 63.3% (25 Mar 2025); no 2026 model has been added.

On 503 items a 63.3% score carries a 95% binomial half-width of about ±4.2 points derived (1.96·√(p(1−p)/n)); two LongBench v2 entries a few points apart are not separated.

The 2026 frontier: MRCR, Fiction.LiveBench, AA-LCR, GDP.pdf

OpenAI MRCR is the open version OpenAI released with GPT-4.1 in April 2025 ({{dataset card|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/mrcr}}, MIT licence). A synthetic conversation asks for dozens of short pieces of writing ("write a poem about tapirs", "write a blog post about rocks"); hidden among them are 2, 4 or 8 identical requests, and the last turn asks the model to prepend a random string to, say, the 2nd poem about tapirs. Every reply was written by GPT-4o, so the needles look exactly like the hay. There are 2,400 rows: three needle counts, eight length bins from 4K to 1M tokens, 100 per bin. Grading is Python's difflib.SequenceMatcher ratio between the answer and the target, set to 0 if the random prefix is missing. The literal match is total here (both needles repeat the request word for word), so the difficulty is order and identity, not finding: the Grade a real MRCR item tab runs the grader on a real row.

Version trap: MRCR before and after 5 December 2025
A generation bug put too many target needles in about 10% of rows and wrong ground truth in about 5%; OpenAI replaced them on 5 December 2025 and added a date_added field ({{changelog|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/mrcr}}). MRCR scores from before and after the fix are not comparable, and "MRCR" in a vendor table can also mean Google's original Michelangelo version.

The current readings show the gap that matters most in this family. OpenAI reports GPT-6 Astra at 96.3% on 8 needles in the 512K to 1M bin and 100.0% at 256K to 512K ({{OpenAI, 2 Sep 2026|https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/}}). The best independent 1M reading, Context Arena's run of the same 8-needle set, is 63.5% (Gemini 3.7 Flash high, 23 Aug 2026; Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 not yet listed; {{Context Arena|https://contextarena.ai/?needles=8}}). Saturated in the vendor's own number, not yet independently.

Fiction.LiveBench asks comprehension questions about long stories at fixed lengths from 0 to 192K tokens, with private stories and questions ({{Fiction.live|https://fiction.live/stories/Fiction-liveBench/oQdzQvKHw8JyXbN87}}). Its last table (4 April 2026) already had Gemini 3 Flash preview at 100.0% at 192K ({{table image|https://cdn6.fiction.live/file/fictionlive/a7be0188-9a0a-4b19-a4b3-97e7deecf52e.png}}); it is saturated and stopped. The short columns had gone earlier: GPT-5 (medium) scored 97.2% at 16K at its August 2025 release (Fiction.live leaderboard, via the {{Epoch AI hub|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks}}); the long columns were where models still differed.

AA-LCR (Artificial Analysis Long Context Reasoning) is 100 questions over document sets of 10K to 100K tokens (counted with cl100k_base): company filings, consultations, legal and academic texts, needing reasoning across several documents; an LLM judges each answer equivalent or not ({{AA-LCR|https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/artificial-analysis-long-context-reasoning}}, v1.1). Mid-2024 frontier models were below 50%; Kimi K3 (max) now leads at 88.7% with Step 5 Preview 88.3% (read 1 Oct 2026). With 100 questions, ±6.2 points at 95% derived, the top dozen are statistically one group.

GDP.pdf is long-context and multimodal at once: 100 professional tasks over 100 PDFs, 4,592 pages of tables, charts and scans, with 1,275 expert-written criteria; a task passes only if every criterion passes ({{Garre et al., arXiv 2607.11192|https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11192}}; {{Surge AI launch post|https://surgehq.ai/blog/gdp-pdf-can-100b-ai-models-master-the-documents-that-run-the-world}}). Items were kept only if at least two frontier models failed them, and the data are public. GPT-6 Astra leads under both harnesses, which do not agree: 34.2% on Surge's ({{leaderboard|https://surgehq.ai/benchmarks/gdp-pdf}}) and 32.2% on Artificial Analysis's five-attempt implementation ({{AA GDP.pdf|https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/gdp-pdf}}), both read 4 Oct 2026. The 33.2% often quoted is AA's figure from the Index v4.2 launch on 4 September, before v4.3 changed image handling.

Advertised against effective context

Three generations of measurement say the same thing in different units. RULER (2024): GPT-4 claims 128K, effective 64K; Mistral 7B v0.2 claims 32K, effective 16K. NoLiMa (2025): GPT-4.1 claims 1M, effective 16K once the needle shares no words with the question. MRCR (2026): a vendor's 96.3% at 1M against 63.5% for the best independently measured model at 1M. An effective length is only defined with its threshold and its task, and the threshold is a choice: RULER's absolute 85.6% rewards a model that starts high and decays, NoLiMa's 85% of the model's own base rewards one that starts low and stays flat. On the Effective length tab you can move both rules and watch rankings reorder.

What to quote
A long-context claim needs four things: the benchmark and version, the length bin, the number of needles or the task, and who ran it. "1M context" on a model card is the API limit; "effective to 16K on NoLiMa" and "63.5% on 8-needle MRCR at 1M (Context Arena)" are measurements.
