AA-Briefcase

Artificial Analysis's own benchmark of agentic knowledge work (launched 18 June 2026): 91 private tasks inside four multi-week project scenarios with about 2,000 source files, where the model produces analyst-style deliverables; a fifth, public scenario only demonstrates the format ({{AA-Briefcase|https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/aa-briefcase}}; {{launch|https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/aa-briefcase}}). Deliverables are compared head to head by an LLM judge panel (Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash since index v4.3.1), and the result is an Elo rating plus a rubric pass rate. It entered the Intelligence Index at v4.2 (4 September 2026) in the slot GPQA Diamond left and carries 15% of the weight in v4.3.2 ({{methodology|https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking}}).

Reading it. Elo is relative and re-anchored between versions, so an Elo figure needs its version; and the judges come from the labs being ranked. It is private, so it resists contamination but cannot be audited from outside. Agentic benchmarks in general are on the {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}} page.

How to read a reported score, and what to use now

Before trusting a knowledge or reasoning number, ask five questions; each one moved a score on this page by more than the gap between frontier models.

Common mistakes, one line each (each is taught in a section above)
  • "6.5% of MMLU's answers are wrong": 6.49% has a defect of some kind; wrong keys are 1.86%.
  • Comparing HLE numbers from different sets, tools or judges.
  • Reading a 2-point GPQA Diamond gap as a ranking: one question is 0.51 points; effort and sampling move a single model by 17 points.
  • Quoting an ARC-AGI-3 score without harness and effort: GPT-6 Astra spans 17.5% to 99.9% on the same games.
  • Mixing SimpleQA and SimpleQA Verified, or F-score and accuracy: F rewards guessing whenever a model is more than F/2 sure.
  • Comparing a public-set ARC score with a leaderboard score: the 67-cent 44% and Prime Agent's 95.5% are on public sets (ARC-AGI-3's public games are deliberately easier); test-time training and best-of-runs also differ.
  • Mixing 5-shot and chain-of-thought MMLU (Gemini Ultra's 90.04% against 83.7%), or CoT and direct MMLU-Pro.
  • Treating the unsolved residue of a hard benchmark as the capability gap: on HLE-Physics 95.2% of audited failures were benchmark or grader errors.
  • Quoting one BBEH average: the same run is 44.8% harmonic and 54.2% micro.
