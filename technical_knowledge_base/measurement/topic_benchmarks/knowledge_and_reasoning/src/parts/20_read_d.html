Fix the key: what a residual actually measures

A benchmark score is capped by its answer key, and the defects gather in exactly the hard tail people quote, because a question ambiguous enough to defeat a frontier model is often ambiguous rather than hard. Two published audits let us watch the same models when the key is fixed. Predict first: when MMLU-Redux removes virology's flawed items, does the leader stay the leader? Then step through.

Fix the key and the ordering near the top changes

On MMLU, Llama 3.1 405B moves from 16th to 1st on virology and GPT-4 (0613) falls from 0.91 to 0.43 on human sexuality. On HLE, the seven models gain 29.9 to 39.6 points on the text-only items whose problem or answer was revised, but only 7.8 to 11.5 on the full text-only set Table 2, because most items did not change.

What the two audits measure, and what they do not
MMLU-Redux re-grades by removing flawed items from a 100-question sample per subject; the "after" scores are on the remaining correct items, and ranks are among all models HELM listed. HLE-Verified repairs items (problem, answer or both) and re-runs seven models five times each on the text-only set. Neither tells you which model is best; both show that the ordering near the top can be set by the key. Squares are drawn to scale: one per question. Upper estimates, since only answers marked wrong were audited; the paper page's "Follow the audit" tab replays the pipeline count by count ({{Re-grading six physics benchmarks|n:3e25c17b0d0d8165b0c8d15600421259}}).

143 were benchmark errors, 95 grader errors and only 12 model errors, so 95.2% were not the model's fault. On HLE-Physics, 86 of 202 questions were removed as defective and the same model went from 47.3% to 78.7% on the 116 kept; on CritPt it went from 32.3% to 87.5% on the 54 repaired challenges.

The rule for every number on this page. The residual, the part of a suite a model fails, is a mixture of model failure and item defect that cannot be separated without expert re-grading. So an apparent ceiling below 100% may be the answer key, rankings can reorder on a cleaned set, and the residual is an upper bound on the capability gap, not a measure of it. Every benchmark here carries such a rate: 6.49% of sampled MMLU questions flawed (1.86% with a wrong key), about 29% of HLE's text-only chemistry and biology answers contradicted by the literature, a few percent of GPQA Diamond's keys disputed (experts reached 74% on the extended set even after discounting their own slips). The re-grading authors' conclusion: the next bar has to be harder, human-written exams with verified keys, not another leaderboard built on the same pipeline.
