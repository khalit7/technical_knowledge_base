Fix the key

A benchmark score is capped by its answer key. Two published audits let us watch what happens to the same models when the key is fixed: MMLU-Redux on MMLU, HLE-Verified on Humanity's Last Exam. Below them, where MMLU's defects sit, subject by subject.

Re-grade with a fixed key

Try: on MMLU, compare Virology (Llama 3.1 405B from 16th to 1st) with Human Sexuality (GPT-4 (0613) from 0.91 to 0.43). On HLE, step from the revised subset to the full set: the gain shrinks from about 30 to 40 points to about 8 to 11 because most items did not change.

What the two audits measure, and what they do not
MMLU-Redux re-grades by removing flawed items from a 100-question sample per subject; the "after" scores are on the remaining correct items, and ranks are among all models HELM listed. HLE-Verified repairs items (problem, answer or both) and re-runs seven models five times each on the text-only set. Neither tells you which model is best; both show that the ordering near the top can be set by the key. Squares are drawn to scale: one per question. In HLE mode the squares are grouped by subset, not in dataset order.

Where MMLU's defects are

MMLU-Redux 2.0 (CC BY 4.0, {{dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/edinburgh-dawg/mmlu-redux-2.0}}): 100 sampled questions per subject, counted by error type. The estimate multiplies each subject's flagged share by its test-set size ({{cais/mmlu|https://huggingface.co/datasets/cais/mmlu}}) derived; summed over subjects it gives 7.0% of the 14,042 test questions, against the paper's 6.49% for the unweighted sample.
