MMLU and its family

MMLU Hendrycks et al., UC Berkeley, September 2020

What it measures. Breadth of exam knowledge: 57 subjects from elementary mathematics to professional law and medicine, four options per question, 14,042 test questions. Unspecialised Mechanical Turk workers scored 34.5%; the largest GPT-3 (175B) scored 43.9% few-shot, almost 20 points above chance ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300}}). The "expert-level" 89.8% was estimated from the 95th-percentile human test-taker on each source exam and "an educated guess" where that was unknown, so it is a constructed figure, not a measured panel.

Protocol. Base models were scored 5-shot, the five development examples teaching the answer format, by the generated letter or by log-likelihood. Instruction-tuned and reasoning models are scored zero-shot with chain of thought. Headline numbers mixed all of these: Gemini Ultra's 90.04% "exceeding human experts" used 32 chain-of-thought samples routed by a confidence threshold, where its 5-shot score was 83.7% (the full case, with GPT-4 measured both ways, is in the root's {{Same model, many numbers|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}} tab).

How MMLU was built (sources, splits)

Collected by hand by graduate and undergraduate students from free online sources: practice questions for the GRE and the US Medical Licensing Examination, undergraduate course questions and questions for readers of Oxford University Press books. The paper reports 15,908 questions split into a few-shot development set (5 per subject), a 1,540-question validation set and a test set of 14,079; the released files hold 14,042 test, 1,531 validation and 285 development questions, and 14,042 is the number every harness uses ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300}}; {{cais/mmlu|https://huggingface.co/datasets/cais/mmlu}}).

The answer key (MMLU-Redux). Fourteen annotators re-checked 100 randomly sampled questions from each subject, 5,700 in all; 370 were flagged, the paper's 6.49% ({{MMLU-Redux|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04127}}; {{dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/edinburgh-dawg/mmlu-redux-2.0}}, CC BY 4.0). Read the composition before quoting it: only 106 of 5,700 (1.86%) are a wrong gold label; the rest are unclear questions (132), unclear options (25), no correct option (36), several correct options (39) and items needing an expert (32) counted from the dataset. 57 of 100 sampled virology questions are flawed, 33 of them with a wrong key, and 25 to 26% in logical fallacies and college chemistry. What the defects do to rankings is replayed in Fix the key below: Llama 3.1 405B moves from 16th to 1st on virology once flawed items are removed.

6.49% or 7.0%? (weighting by subject size)
The paper calls 6.49% a stratified estimate, but it equals the raw share of the sample; weighting each subject by its size in the test set gives 7.0%, because professional law (1,534 test questions, 18 flagged in its sample) and virology (166, 57 flagged) carry more weight derived.

Contamination evidence on MMLU (the general case is the parent's)

The questions sit on the public web. Testset Slot Guessing masks one wrong option and asks the model to fill it in; ChatGPT and GPT-4 reproduced the missing option exactly 52% and 57% of the time, which they could hardly do without having seen the test ({{Xu et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09783}}). MMLU-Redux found a second sign: on some subjects (professional law, formal logic) models score as well or better on items whose key is wrong, which they could only match by memory. How contamination inflates scores in general is taught on {{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}.

Status. Saturated and contaminated; the gap between frontier models is answer-key noise. Still a cheap smoke test during training. The root page animates its whole life, from launch to saturation (Reading tab, "The life of a benchmark").

MMLU-Pro TIGER-Lab (Wang et al.), June 2024

What changed. Drop the questions small models got right, add harder STEM questions, and expand every question from four options to ten, the six new distractors written by GPT-4-Turbo: 12,032 questions in 14 disciplines, chance 10% ({{MMLU-Pro|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01574}}). Two measurement effects follow. Across 24 prompt styles, scores moved 4 to 5 points on MMLU (up to 10.98) but about 2 on MMLU-Pro (up to 3.74). How MMLU-Pro was built

Start from MMLU, merge the 57 subjects into 14 disciplines, and drop 5,886 questions that more than four of eight small models (Llama-2 7B and 13B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-7B, Yi-6B and their chat versions) answered correctly. Add harder questions from a STEM problem site, TheoremQA and SciBench. Then expand to ten options and run two review rounds: experts first, then frontier LLMs flagging suspect items for annotators to check.

Status. Saturated and legacy since early 2026: the tops are 89.8 to 91.2%, and Artificial Analysis dropped MMLU-Pro from its Intelligence Index at v4.0 in January 2026 and runs no newer models on it ({{methodology|https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking}}). Ten options also mean at least six of every ten were written by a 2024 model, so a distractor that is accidentally correct is a key error the original could not have; we found no published audit of how often that happens unconfirmed.

Multilingual knowledge: MMMLU, Global-MMLU, INCLUDE

Translating MMLU tests translation as much as knowledge, and it carries MMLU's Western syllabus into every language: 28% of MMLU needs culturally sensitive knowledge, and 84.9% of its geography questions are about North America or Europe ({{Global-MMLU|https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03304}}). Translated MMLU inherits MMLU's ceiling (Claude Opus 5.5 averages 94.3% on Global-MMLU lab). INCLUDE, built from local exams with regional knowledge, is the one of the three with clear headroom.

The three sets (and MGSM)
  • MMMLU (OpenAI, September 2024): MMLU's test set translated by professional human translators into 14 languages (Arabic, Bengali, German, Spanish, French, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Brazilian Portuguese, Swahili, Yoruba, Simplified Chinese), MIT licence ({{dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/MMMLU}}).
  • Global-MMLU (Cohere For AI, December 2024): 42 languages, translations checked by paid professional and community annotators, and every question tagged; it ships "culturally sensitive" and "culturally agnostic" subsets because model rankings change between them.
  • INCLUDE (EPFL and others, November 2024): no translation at all. 197,243 multiple-choice questions from 1,926 local academic, professional and licensing exams in 44 languages, 15 scripts and 52 countries ({{INCLUDE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19799}}).
  • For maths in many languages, MGSM (250 GSM8K problems in ten languages) is saturated (93.7%, o4-mini, OpenAI simple-evals, which calls it saturated) and belongs to {{Math benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81ec942edf0040a2f596}}.
