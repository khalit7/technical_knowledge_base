GPQA and GPQA Diamond

GPQA Rein et al., NYU, Cohere and Anthropic, November 2023

Construction. "Graduate-level Google-proof Q&A": domain experts (people with or pursuing PhDs) wrote four-option questions in biology, physics and chemistry. Each question then went to two more experts in the same subdomain and three skilled non-experts from other fields, who had unrestricted web access and spent 37 minutes per question on average (median 30). Writers were paid by outcome: $10 per question, $20 for each expert who got it right, $15 for each non-expert who got it wrong, and $30 more when both experts were right and at least two of three non-experts wrong; the authors estimate about $95 an hour ({{GPQA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12022}}).

Baselines. Experts reach 65% on the extended set, 74% once their acknowledged mistakes are discounted, and the authors estimate 74% of questions have uncontroversially correct answers on that set (higher on the filtered subsets); non-experts reach 34% with the web; GPT-4 with few-shot chain of thought reached 39% (38.8% on Diamond). Validator accuracy on Diamond itself is biased upward, since Diamond keeps exactly the questions both experts got right.

Format traps. Four options, so chance is 25%; option order matters, and careful harnesses shuffle and average over permutations. 198 questions make one question worth 0.51 points 100/198, and the 95% binomial interval at 90% is about ±4.2 points (±2.7 at 96%); two models scored on the same 198 questions need a gap well above 2 points before it means anything 1.96·√(p(1−p)/198). The authors ask that items not be published in plain text or images, and ship a canary string, to keep them out of training data; this page respects that and shows none.

Trajectory (Epoch AI's own runs, same harness, dated by model release; {{Epoch|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/gpqa-diamond}}): GPT-4 (March 2023) 35.7%; Claude 3.5 Sonnet (June 2024) 54.0%; o1 (high, December 2024) 76.8%; Gemini 2.5 Pro (June 2025) 85.3%; Gemini 3 Pro Preview (November 2025) 92.6%; Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (February 2026) 94.4%; GPT-6 Astra (max, September 2026) 95.8%. The whole curve, with the expert line, is in the root's Saturation timeline tab.

Status. Saturated at the frontier: three frontier models sit within half a point near 96%, inside the noise of 198 questions and the disputed-item rate. Artificial Analysis removed it from its Intelligence Index at v4.2 (4 September 2026), replacing it with AA-Briefcase and GDP.pdf while private held-out sets rose to 40% of the index weight ({{v4.2|https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-2}}). It is still a good discriminator between 60% and 90%, so it stays on cards for smaller models. Effort and sampling move it a lot: Claude 3.7 Sonnet reads 68.0% to 84.8% on Anthropic's own card depending on thinking budget and parallel sampling (root, {{Same model, many numbers|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}).

Humanity's Last Exam (HLE)

HLE Center for AI Safety and Scale AI, January 2025

Construction. Nearly 1,000 subject experts from over 500 institutions in 50 countries submitted questions; each had to stump frontier models before it was accepted for review (exact-answer questions had to defeat every model tested: GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and o1, plus o1-mini and o1-preview for text-only questions; multiple-choice questions all but one). Over 70,000 such attempts produced about 13,000 questions for two rounds of expert review, and 2,500 made the public set ({{HLE paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249}}). A $500,000 prize pool paid $5,000 for each of the top 50 questions and $500 for each of the next 500, plus co-authorship. About 24% are multiple choice and 14% need an image; mathematics is the largest category, about 40% by the old page's reading of the paper's Figure 3 (an image, so the share is unconfirmed). A private held-out set measures overfitting to the public one, and a later round of late contributions formed a second private set.

Grading. Models answer with reasoning, a final answer and a confidence; o3-mini (2025-01-31) judges correctness against the key with a fixed prompt that tolerates equivalent forms and small numerical margins (the pre-submission difficulty checks used a GPT-4o judge). Because every model also states a confidence, HLE reports RMS calibration error: above 70% for every model at launch, meaning models were confidently wrong. At launch o1 scored 9.1%.

The answer key. The selection rule is the problem: a question kept because it defeats frontier models is often defeated by being wrong or ambiguous. Reviewers were not required to verify a rationale that took "more than 5 minutes".

  • FutureHouse (23 July 2025) ran its literature agent on the 321 text-only chemistry and biology questions, had experts check 150 of its verdicts, and estimated 29.3 ± 3.7% contradicted by peer-reviewed literature, 51.3 ± 4.1% supported and 19.3 ± 3.2% nuanced ({{FutureHouse|https://www.futurehouse.org/research-announcements/hle-exam}}). Its example: the key says the rarest noble gas on Earth in 2002 was oganesson, a synthetic element of which five atoms have been made and which is predicted to be a solid.
  • The HLE team's follow-up (revised paper, section B.3): before release, two 200-question audits gave an estimated expert disagreement rate of 15.4% on the public set; a targeted review of a biology, chemistry and health subset found about 18% problematic, and in a three-expert review at least one reviewer disagreed 25% of the time ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249}}; FutureHouse's update of 16 September 2025).
  • HLE-Verified (Qwen team, Alibaba, February 2026) checked all 2,500 items component by component: 668 verified as correct, 1,143 revised (problem, answer or rationale repaired by two independent expert teams and adjudicated) and 689 left as an uncertain set ({{HLE-Verified|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13964}}). On the text-only items whose problem or answer was revised, seven models gained 29.9 to 39.6 points; on the full text-only set, 7.8 to 11.5 Table 2. The {{Fix the key|#t-key}} tab animates it.
  • HLE-Diamond (CAIS and Scale, 22 September 2026): after a year of cleaning with community input ("HLE-Rolling"), a 1,000-question subset, 500 reasoning and 500 knowledge questions ({{HLE-Diamond|https://lastexam.ai/blog/hle-diamond}}).

SimpleQA and SimpleQA Verified

SimpleQA OpenAI, November 2024 and SimpleQA Verified Google DeepMind, September 2025

Construction. 4,326 short fact-seeking questions with a single indisputable answer (for example "Who received the IEEE Frank Rosenblatt Award in 2010?", answer Michio Sugeno). A trainer wrote each question and answer, a second trainer answered independently, and only matching pairs were kept; questions also had to defeat at least one of four OpenAI model responses. A third-trainer check put the benchmark's own error rate at about 3% ({{SimpleQA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04368}}).

Grading. A prompted ChatGPT grader labels each answer correct, incorrect or not attempted. Two rates follow: overall correct, and correct given attempted. Their harmonic mean is the F-score, which simplifies to F = 2C / (2C + 2I + N) for counts of correct C, incorrect I and not attempted N derived; reproduces the paper's Table 3. That form shows the incentive: guessing on one more question with probability p of being right raises the expected F whenever p > C/(2C+2I+N) = F/2 derived, so a model at F = 38% should guess whenever it is more than 19% sure. The paper's appendix says "at least 50% sure"; that is a sufficient condition, and the true threshold is lower.

Illustrative what-if on the paper's published rates; the arithmetic is exact (expected values over the guessed share).

Results at launch (Table 3): GPT-4o 38.2% correct with 1.0% not attempted (F 38.4); Claude 3.5 Sonnet 28.9% correct but 35.0% not attempted, so a similar F (35.0); o1-preview 42.7% (F 44.8). The table prints 38.0% for GPT-4o's correct-given-attempted; 38.2 / (38.2 + 60.8) gives 38.6%, which its own F-score of 38.4 requires derived. The original is unmaintained: OpenAI's simple-evals stopped updating in July 2025, its last frontier entry being GPT-4.5 preview at 62.5% ({{simple-evals|https://github.com/openai/simple-evals}}).

SimpleQA Verified rebuilt the set to 1,000 prompts: unique source documents, semantic and TF-IDF de-duplication, topic re-balancing and reconciliation of conflicting sources, plus a better autorater prompt, to fix noisy labels, topical bias and redundancy. At release Gemini 2.5 Pro led with F1 55.6, ahead of GPT-5 at 52.3 ({{SimpleQA Verified|https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07968}}). Today: Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview 77.5% ± 2.6 and GPT-6 Astra 75.8% on Kaggle's board, 30 September 2026 (whether its headline is F1 or accuracy is unconfirmed: {{Kaggle|https://www.kaggle.com/benchmarks/deepmind/simpleqa-verified}}); Epoch measured GPT-6 Astra at 75.6%. A SimpleQA number and a SimpleQA Verified number are different sets: name the version.
