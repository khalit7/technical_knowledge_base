GPQA and GPQA Diamond

GPQA Rein et al., NYU, Cohere and Anthropic, November 2023

What it measures. Graduate-level science that the web does not give away ("Google-proof"): PhD-level experts wrote four-option questions in biology, physics and chemistry, and a question survived only if two more experts in the same subdomain could answer it and three skilled non-experts from other fields, with unrestricted web access and 37 minutes per question on average (median 30), mostly could not ({{GPQA|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12022}}). Diamond, the subset every card reports, is the strictest cut:

Baselines. Experts reach 65% on the extended set, 74% once their acknowledged mistakes are discounted, and the authors estimate 74% of questions have uncontroversially correct answers on that set (higher on the filtered subsets); non-experts reach 34% with the web; GPT-4 with few-shot chain of thought reached 39% (38.8% on Diamond). Validator accuracy on Diamond itself is biased upward, since Diamond keeps exactly the questions both experts got right.

Reading a GPQA score. 198 questions make one question worth 0.51 points 100/198, and the 95% binomial interval at 90% is about ±4.2 points (±2.7 at 96%) 1.96·√(p(1−p)/198); two models scored on the same 198 questions need a gap well above 2 points before it means anything (paired tests and how many items you need: {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}). Option order matters, so careful harnesses shuffle and average over permutations. Effort and sampling move one model a lot: Claude 3.7 Sonnet reads 68.0% to 84.8% on Anthropic's own card depending on thinking budget and parallel sampling (root, {{Same model, many numbers|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}).

How the writers were paid, and why no items are shown

Writers were paid by outcome: $10 per question, $20 for each expert who got it right, $15 for each non-expert who got it wrong, and $30 more when both experts were right and at least two of three non-experts wrong; the authors estimate about $95 an hour. The authors ask that items not be published in plain text or images, and ship a canary string, to keep them out of training data; this page respects that and shows none.

Status. Saturated at the frontier: three frontier models sit within half a point near 96%, inside the noise of 198 questions and the disputed-item rate; Epoch's runs went from 35.7% (GPT-4, March 2023) to 95.8% (GPT-6 Astra, September 2026). Artificial Analysis removed it from its Intelligence Index at v4.2 (4 September 2026), replacing it with AA-Briefcase and GDP.pdf while private held-out sets rose to 40% of the index weight ({{v4.2|https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-2}}). It still discriminates between 60% and 90%, so it stays on cards for smaller models.

Humanity's Last Exam (HLE)

HLE Center for AI Safety and Scale AI, January 2025

What it measures. Expert knowledge at the edge of what frontier models know. Nearly 1,000 subject experts from over 500 institutions in 50 countries submitted questions, and each had to stump frontier models before review: over 70,000 such attempts produced about 13,000 questions for two rounds of expert review, and 2,500 made the public set ({{HLE paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249}}). About 24% are multiple choice and 14% need an image. At launch o1 scored 9.1%.

Grading. Models answer with reasoning, a final answer and a confidence; o3-mini (2025-01-31) judges correctness against the key with a fixed prompt that tolerates equivalent forms and small numerical margins. Because every model states a confidence, HLE reports RMS calibration error: above 70% for every model at launch, meaning models were confidently wrong.

Construction details (the stump rule, prizes, private sets)

Exact-answer questions had to defeat every model tested (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and o1, plus o1-mini and o1-preview for text-only questions); multiple-choice questions all but one. The pre-submission difficulty checks used a GPT-4o judge. A $500,000 prize pool paid $5,000 for each of the top 50 questions and $500 for each of the next 500, plus co-authorship. Mathematics is the largest category, about 40% by the old page's reading of the paper's Figure 3 (an image, so the share is unconfirmed). A private held-out set measures overfitting to the public one, and a later round of late contributions formed a second private set.

The answer key. The selection rule is the problem: a question kept because it defeats frontier models is often defeated by being wrong or ambiguous, and reviewers were not required to verify a rationale that took "more than 5 minutes". Four audits measure it:

  • FutureHouse (23 July 2025) ran its literature agent on the 321 text-only chemistry and biology questions, had experts check 150 of its verdicts, and estimated 29.3 ± 3.7% contradicted by peer-reviewed literature, 51.3 ± 4.1% supported and 19.3 ± 3.2% nuanced ({{FutureHouse|https://www.futurehouse.org/research-announcements/hle-exam}}). Its example: the key says the rarest noble gas on Earth in 2002 was oganesson, a synthetic element of which five atoms have been made and which is predicted to be a solid.
  • The HLE team's follow-up (revised paper, section B.3): two 200-question audits before release estimated 15.4% expert disagreement on the public set; a targeted review of a biology, chemistry and health subset found about 18% problematic, and in a three-expert review at least one reviewer disagreed 25% of the time ({{paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249}}; FutureHouse's update of 16 September 2025).
  • HLE-Verified (Qwen team, Alibaba, February 2026) checked all 2,500 items component by component: 668 verified as correct, 1,143 revised (problem, answer or rationale repaired by two independent expert teams and adjudicated) and 689 left as an uncertain set ({{HLE-Verified|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13964}}). What the repairs do to scores is replayed in Fix the key.
  • HLE-Diamond (CAIS and Scale, 22 September 2026): a cleaned 1,000-question subset, 500 reasoning and 500 knowledge ({{HLE-Diamond|https://lastexam.ai/blog/hle-diamond}}); it came out of a year of cleaning with community input ("HLE-Rolling").
