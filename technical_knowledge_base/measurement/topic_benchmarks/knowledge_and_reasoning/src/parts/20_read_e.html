AA-Briefcase

Artificial Analysis's own benchmark of agentic knowledge work (launched 18 June 2026): 91 private tasks inside four multi-week project scenarios with about 2,000 source files, where the model produces analyst-style deliverables; a fifth, public scenario only demonstrates the format ({{AA-Briefcase|https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/aa-briefcase}}; {{launch|https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/aa-briefcase}}). Deliverables are compared head to head by an LLM judge panel (Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol and Gemini 3.8 Flash since index v4.3.1), and the result is an Elo rating plus a rubric pass rate. It entered the Intelligence Index at v4.2 (4 September 2026) in the slot GPQA Diamond left and carries 15% of the weight in v4.3.2 ({{methodology|https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking}}).

Reading it. Claude Opus 5.5 (max) leads at 1822 Elo (95% interval 1810 to 1834; rubric pass rate 61.2%) on AA-Briefcase v1.1, read 1 October 2026 ({{AA|https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-opus-5-5}}). Elo is relative and re-anchored between versions, so an Elo figure needs its version; and the judges come from the labs being ranked. It is private, so it resists contamination but cannot be audited from outside. Agentic benchmarks in general are on the {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}} page.

Item defects, and what a residual actually measures

Every benchmark above carries an item-defect rate, and none is a rounding error: 6.49% of sampled MMLU questions flawed (1.86% with a wrong key), about 29% of HLE's text-only chemistry and biology answers contradicted by the literature, a few percent of GPQA Diamond's keys disputed (experts reached 74% on the extended set even after discounting their own slips). The general case was settled by an expert re-grading of six physics benchmarks (September 2026): of 250 answers marked wrong for GPT-5.6 Sol, 143 were benchmark errors, 95 grader errors and only 12 model errors, so 95.2% were not the model's fault; on HLE-Physics, 86 of 202 questions were removed as defective and the same model went from 47.3% to 78.7% on the 116 kept; on CritPt it went from 32.3% to 87.5% on the 54 repaired challenges ({{arXiv 2609.13009|https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13009}}). These are upper estimates, since only answers marked wrong were audited; the paper page's "Follow the audit" tab replays the pipeline count by count ({{Re-grading six physics benchmarks|n:3e25c17b0d0d8165b0c8d15600421259}}).

The rule that follows applies to every number on this page. The residual, the part of a suite a model fails, is a mixture of model failure and item defect that cannot be separated without expert re-grading, and the defects gather in exactly the hard tail people quote, because a question ambiguous enough to defeat a frontier model is often ambiguous rather than hard. So an apparent ceiling below 100% may be the answer key, rankings can reorder on a cleaned set (MMLU-Redux's virology re-grade, HLE-Verified's revised subset: both in {{Fix the key|#t-key}}), and HLE-Verified's 30 to 40 point gains on revised items are the same effect measured directly. The re-grading authors' conclusion: the next bar has to be harder, human-written exams with verified keys, not another leaderboard built on the same pipeline.

Common mistakes

"6.5% of MMLU's answers are wrong."
6.49% of MMLU-Redux's sample has a defect of some kind; wrong keys are 1.86%. The rest are unclear questions or options, missing or multiple correct options, or items needing an expert. Either way, the ceiling is below 100% and the defects concentrate by subject (virology 57%).
Comparing HLE numbers from different sets, tools or judges.
Full HLE, text-only HLE, HLE-Verified and HLE-Diamond are four different tests; tools add 3.3 to 7.0 points in Anthropic's blocklisted runs and 19 to 32 on HLE-Diamond in the maintainers'; Anthropic grades with Claude Opus 4.6, Artificial Analysis and Scale with their own setups.
Reading a 2-point GPQA Diamond gap as a ranking.
198 questions: one question is 0.51 points and the 95% interval is about ±4.2 at 90%. Effort and sampling move a single model by 17 points (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, 68.0% to 84.8%).
Quoting an ARC-AGI-3 score without harness and effort.
GPT-6 Astra spans 17.5% to 99.9% on the same games depending on harness and effort. Quote "62.7%, standard harness, max effort, semi-private".
Mixing SimpleQA and SimpleQA Verified, or F-score and accuracy.
4,326 questions against 1,000; F-score rewards guessing whenever a model is more than F/2 sure, so a higher F can mean less abstention, not more knowledge.
Comparing a public-set ARC score with a leaderboard score.
The 67-cent 44% and Prime Agent's 95.5% are on public sets (ARC-AGI-3's public games are deliberately easier); leaderboards use semi-private sets. Test-time training and best-of-runs also differ.
Mixing 5-shot and chain-of-thought MMLU, or CoT and direct MMLU-Pro.
Gemini Ultra's 90.04% (CoT@32, routed) and 83.7% (5-shot) are the same model; on MMLU-Pro CoT is worth up to 19 points. Same name, different measurement.
Treating the unsolved residue of a hard benchmark as the capability gap.
On HLE-Physics 95.2% of audited failures were benchmark or grader errors. The residual is an upper bound on the gap, not a measure of it.
Quoting one BBEH average.
The same o3-mini (high) run is 44.8% as a harmonic mean and 54.2% as a micro average.
