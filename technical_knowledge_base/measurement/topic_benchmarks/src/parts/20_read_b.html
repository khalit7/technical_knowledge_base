What a number leaves out

A score is the output of a procedure: sample the data (the benchmark's dataset and ideally a canonical prompt), run the model (zero- or few-shot, chain of thought, tools, reasoning effort, an agent scaffold) and score it (accuracy, exact match, pass@k, pass^k, an Elo rating, a judge's grade, task success). Every knob moves the number, so a result means something only with its protocol tuple:

Predict first: which knob moves a frontier model's published score the most, the harness or the answer key? Each row below is one model with two published scores that differ in one knob.

The small knobs: shots and answer extraction
Shots: MMLU was scored 5-shot in the base-model era because the examples taught the format; reasoning models are evaluated zero-shot, and OpenAI reported that few-shot prompts degraded o1 on GPQA. Answer extraction: parsing "The answer is (X)" and reading the model's probabilities over the options give different MMLU scores for the same model, so harnesses (lm-eval-harness, HELM, a vendor's own) legitimately disagree by points.

Version same name, different test

Revisions are made to discriminate, so the version is part of the name: two models 4.9 points apart on Terminal-Bench 2.1 were 12.7 apart on 3.0 ({{Terminal-Bench 3.0|https://www.tbench.ai/news/terminal-bench-3-0}}), and 4.0 calls itself a breaking change, so a 2.x score beside a 4.0 score is a false comparison. OSWorld (original, Verified, 2.0) and SWE-bench Pro (V1, V2) are not one scale either, and inside one version the scoring rule still matters (the OSWorld row). Version histories are on each child; Terminal-Bench's and OSWorld's on {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}.

Terminal-Bench's versions, dated
Terminal-Bench went 2.0, 2.1, 3.0 (23 July 2026: 74 tasks, best models about 34%) and 4.0 (26 August 2026) ({{releases|https://github.com/harbor-framework/terminal-bench/releases}}). Version 4.0 set a flat 8-hour agent timeout, recalibrated CPU and memory, fixed 19 tasks and removed 8 ({{Terminal-Bench 4.0|https://www.tbench.ai/news/terminal-bench-4-0}}): when the agent gets a shell and a budget, the limits are part of the task. Correction to the old pages: the Agentic child once said 4.0 came out in September and "there is no 3.x"; 3.0 was tagged on 23 July 2026.

Split which tasks were scored

Held-back tasks measure overfitting to the public part (the SWE-bench Pro row; its splits are drawn to scale on {{Coding benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81bfa09ad4ba5eef0c4e}}). Public sets die by leakage; fully private sets cannot be audited and concentrate trust (FrontierMath's late-disclosed OpenAI funding is the cautionary tale). Best practice is a hybrid: public development set, private held-out set, independent re-runners (Epoch AI, Princeton's HAL, Artificial Analysis) as auditors.

Harness what ran the model

On agentic benchmarks the score belongs to the model plus the program around it. The ARC-AGI-3 row is the extreme: three labs' models exceed 96% with a harness that keeps their hidden reasoning state, while the best standard score is 62.7%; ARC Prize's reading is that neutral conditions and best achievable are different questions, and stay different once a model reasons in hidden state ({{Knowledge and reasoning benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d810f8574da3ffb860be5}}). On Terminal-Bench 2.0's public board the same weights under different agents span 14 to 21 points ({{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}, section 3); controlled experiments are on {{Harnesses for training and evaluation|n:3f15c17b0d0d815ea579d6c80065aa36}}. Who ran it is the same knob from outside: lab tables use their own harnesses, in both directions (the Terminal-Bench-Science row; on Terminal-Bench 4.0 Anthropic reports 66.4% for Claude Opus 5.5, Artificial Analysis measured 59.6% and Vals AI, in one minimal harness for every model, 65.15%; xAI reports 38.0% for Grok 4.7 on Terminal-Bench 4.0, Artificial Analysis 25.8%: {{Topic: llm-landscape, Benchmarks tab|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}), so quote the board and say which.

Retrieval and cost: two more things the harness carries

Retrieval: on the Vals AI Legal Research Bench (200 questions) OpenAI reports 38.7% for GPT-6 Astra with web search and 54% for Astra for Law, the same weights with a 230-million-URL legal index and legal-analysis instructions, on OpenAI's private validation set ({{TNW|https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-astra-for-law-gpt-6-legal-search-index}}); if both ran on the same 200 questions the gap is about 3.1 standard errors derived, but index and instructions move together, so it does not isolate retrieval. Cost: on ordinary tasks the harness moves the bill more than the score (Arena's HarnessTax study, on {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}), and at the cheap extreme a small transformer trained for about 67 cents reaches 44% on ARC-AGI-1 ({{write-up|https://mvakde.github.io/blog/44-on-arc-1/}}).

Sampling how many tries, how many items

Sampling conventions differ by more than model gaps (the o1 row): a "93% on AIME" without convention, temperature, token budget and run count compares with nothing. The conventions on real contest data are on {{Math benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81ec942edf0040a2f596}}, pass^k on {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}. Even with the convention fixed, the item count sets a floor on the noise (and an interval must say what it counts: MathArena's printed ± counts answers, not problems; Math, section 6). Predict: on a 30-item contest at 80%, how far apart can two runs of the same model land? Then run it:

illustrative runs: each item solved independently at the chosen rate (real items differ in difficulty). The band and stats are exact binomial arithmetic derived: standard error √(p(1−p)/n), 95% interval ±1.96 standard errors, and two models scored on different items need a gap above 1.96·√2 standard errors. Scoring both on the same items with a paired test removes much of the variance ({{Anthropic, "Adding error bars to evals"|https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals}}).

Three rules of thumb fall out: at 80% on 30 AIME problems the standard error is 7.3 points; a 2-point gap on GPQA Diamond's 198 questions is noise (±4.2 at 90%), and so is one on SWE-bench Verified's 500 tasks (±4.0 at 70%). The inflation tricks live here too: best-of-N reported as one run, maj@k beside a rival's pass@1, "with tools" beside "without". Clustered items, paired tests and how many items you need are on {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}.

Answer key is the key right?

A score is capped by its answer key, and a judge-graded key (HLE's) makes the judge part of the instrument. In an expert re-grading of six physics benchmarks, 95.2% of the audited answers marked wrong were benchmark or grader errors (the HLE-Physics row). So the part of a suite a model fails mixes model failure and item defect, defects gather in the hard tail people quote, and differences between models on one harness mean more than absolute scores. The audits and the rule for reading a residual are on {{Knowledge and reasoning benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d810f8574da3ffb860be5}} (section "Fix the key").

The physics re-grade beyond HLE
The audit covered 250 answers marked wrong for GPT-5.6 Sol across six physics benchmarks (143 benchmark errors, 95 grader errors, 12 model errors); on the cleaned, smaller PRISM-Physics set its score rose from 13% to 95%, an upper estimate since only rejected answers were checked ({{Re-grading six physics benchmarks|n:3e25c17b0d0d8165b0c8d15600421259}}).

Index version the aggregator moved the target

Composite indices are versioned benchmarks too. The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index changed twice in four days: v4.2 (4 September 2026) and v4.3 (7 September: Terminal-Bench 2.1 upgraded to 4.0, τ³-Banking replaced by AutomationBench-AA, private weight 45%) ({{v4.3|https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-3}}); the current release is v4.3.2 ({{methodology|https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking}}). A score without its index version is a benchmark name without a version.

Older scales and the announced v5
Index scores quoted at launches before 4 September, such as the high-50s and low-60s for GLM-5.3-Flash, Gemini 3.8 Flash and Muse Spark 1.3, sit on an older scale. Artificial Analysis has announced v5, adding Terminal-Bench-Science and a private, unpublished coding set, and says existing scores will not be directly comparable once it ships (as reported by {{AlphaSignal, 8 Oct 2026|https://alphasignal.ai/news/artificial-analysis-rebuilds-intelligence-index-v5-with-private-coding-tests}}; AA's own article not read here).

Epoch AI's {{Capabilities Index|https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/eci}} (ECI) answers the same need differently: it links more than 50 benchmarks by difficulty inferred from models scored on several of them, on a scale fixed at Claude 3.5 Sonnet 130 and GPT-5 150, so a saturated benchmark stops contributing instead of capping the index. Its top five sit inside 2.3 points with intervals about 7 points wide, so the index does not order them, while Artificial Analysis puts the first of them 5 points above two of the others: composite indices separate differently because they weight different things.

The readings behind that comparison (19 October 2026)
ECI's scores file (updated 19 October 2026) puts Claude Opus 5.5 at 166.8 (interval 163.5 to 171.0), GPT-6 Astra 166.4, GPT-6.1 Sol 166.3, Claude Fable 5.1 164.7 and Gemini 4 Argon 164.6 read from the file. On Artificial Analysis v4.3.2 Claude Opus 5.5 (max) scores 58 against 53 for both GPT-6 Astra (max) and Gemini 4 Argon (high) (AA model pages, read 19 October 2026), a gap ECI does not show. What the Intelligence Index is, and how it relates to arena preference, is on {{Human preference and arenas|n:3ef5c17b0d0d814db49ff3b5c9fc3034}} (section 7).

Subject what is measured is not the model

A cluster of September 2026 benchmarks measures the system around the model, and each moves the number more than any model gap: a human partner (the Hyper-τ-bench row), a model-built harness (HarnessDev), the monitor (MOLE) and the duration of the run (Emergence World). They are on {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}} ("When the subject is the system, not the agent") and, for MOLE and Emergence World, {{Safety and honesty benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d818da9d5efda1a443166}}.

What nobody reports, and one more system benchmark
No major model card reports confidence calibration ({{arXiv 2609.26489|https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26489}}); and a model's stated reason is not a measurement of its choice, since WhatWorkedBench finds agents picking near-optimal settings for wrong reasons ({{arXiv 2609.27490|https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27490}}).
