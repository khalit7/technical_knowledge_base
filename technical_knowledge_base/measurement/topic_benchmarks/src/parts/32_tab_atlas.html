Benchmark atlas: every benchmark on one grid

The old page's master table, rebuilt and checked: 93 benchmarks and benchmark versions from ImageNet (2009) to Terminal-Bench-Science (August 2026), each with its first paper, what it measures, its size, how it is graded, what defends it against contamination, and its status as of 4 October 2026 with the reading that justifies the status.

Versions are separate rows and never share a number: OSWorld, OSWorld-Verified and OSWorld 2.0; Terminal-Bench 1.0, 2.x and 4.0; SWE-bench Pro and Pro v2; FrontierMath Tiers 1-3 and Tier 4. Every reading names who ran it (independent means the maintainer or a third party, lab the model's vendor), the split, harness or version, and the date. Statuses follow the definitions in the legend; where the status rests on absence (a benchmark no 2026 card reports) the detail panel says so. Rows link to the child page that owns the depth: {{Knowledge and reasoning|n:3c65c17b0d0d810f8574da3ffb860be5}}, {{Math and coding|n:3c65c17b0d0d8146b127fe43f5761bf0}}, {{Agentic|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}; rows marked "planned" have no child page yet. Scores of current models on a fixed set of benchmarks are on {{Topic: llms, Benchmarks tab|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}; harnesses and LLM judges on {{Topic: evaluation-and-llm-judges|n:3c65c17b0d0d8181b351e1b8fc6a1546}}.

Try: filter with the chips or the search box, sort by a column name, click any row for its sources, the old page's wording and any correction. Tick two or three rows to compare them below the grid. Further down: the families by status, what defends each benchmark against contamination, and an animation of one moving training cutoff against five kinds of test set.

Families by status

Each benchmark sits in its family's row and its status's column. The grid's filters dim what they exclude. Read down a column: the saturated and retired columns are mostly the older static families (classic, commonsense, school maths, function-level code); the active column is mostly agentic, tool-use and safety work, plus whatever refreshes itself.

What defends it against contamination

One lane per defence, one dot per benchmark at the year it first appeared, coloured by status. A benchmark with two defences (SWE-bench Pro has a private split and licensed commercial code) appears in both lanes. The picture has a confound worth seeing rather than hiding: the undefended lane is also the oldest, so "no defence" and "had years to saturate" arrive together.

Click a dot to open that benchmark in the grid.

One training cutoff, five kinds of test set

The same moving training cutoff, run against five real test sets that defend themselves differently. Each block is a share of the set's items placed at the date it became public; a block turns orange once that date is before the cutoff, which means it could be in the training data (not that it is). The counters show what each defence buys and what it costs: a set refreshed by date never scores a seen item, but it scores fewer items, so its error bar is wider.
