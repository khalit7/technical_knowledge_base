The idea in one screen

A benchmark score is a measurement of a model under a protocol on a date, and every family keeps replacing its benchmarks because the frontier reaches the ceiling, or has seen the questions, faster than the test was built to last.

A benchmark is a fixed set of tasks, a way of running a model on them, and a rule for scoring the result: one comparable number, whose item set, protocol and date each move it by more than the gap between the models compared. The figure beside this shows every family's chain of replacements, each benchmark coloured by where it stands now.

Three questions settle most headline numbers
When was it measured (Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 went from 30.0% to 64.6% in 26 days on the labs' figures). What ran the model (at the same effort, one model scores 62.7% or 98.6% on ARC-AGI-3 depending on the harness). Has anyone checked the answer key (roughly 29% of Humanity's Last Exam's text-only chemistry and biology answers are contradicted by the literature). A figure that survives none of the three is not yet a measurement.

You need nothing beyond basic statistics. Error bars and paired tests in depth are on {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}; the tools that run benchmarks (lm-eval-harness, Inspect, HELM) and LLM judges on {{Topic: evaluation-and-llm-judges|n:3c65c17b0d0d8181b351e1b8fc6a1546}}; current models compared on fixed benchmarks on {{Topic: llm-landscape, Benchmarks tab|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}.

Saturated
top models sit within noise of each other and of the answer key's error rate, so gaps stop meaning anything.
Contaminated
test items (or close paraphrases) were in the training data, so the score partly measures memory.
Protocol tuple
everything that ran the model besides its weights: version, split, shots, tools, effort, sampling, extraction, harness, judge, who ran it, date.
Harness (scaffold)
the program around the model on agentic tasks: prompts, tools, memory, retries, budget.
pass@1, maj@k, pass@k, pass^k
one sample; the majority answer of k; any of k right; all of k right (reliability). {{Agentic benchmarks, Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e#stg-pk}} draws pass@k against pass^k on 456 released trials.
Private, live, licensed set
the three ways to keep items unseen: held behind an API, mined after the training cutoff, or bought from owners who never published them.

The life of a benchmark

Every benchmark follows roughly the same arc, and knowing where a benchmark sits on it tells you how much its numbers can mean. Fig. 1 follows one benchmark through it, then puts every family on the same floor: time runs left to right, the score climbs a glass plate from 0 to 100%, and each lane in depth is one family, named by the child page that owns it.

Fig. 1 · the life of a benchmark, then every family

  1. Born. The best model scores low, so the test has headroom to separate models. MMLU, the 57-subject exam that defined model comparisons from 2020 to 2023, launched on 7 September 2020 with 15,908 four-option questions; the largest GPT-3 scored 43.9% few-shot against 25% for guessing ({{Hendrycks et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300}}).
  2. Discriminates. The useful years: scores spread out and gaps mean something. Chinchilla reached 67.5% in March 2022, more than 7 points above Gopher ({{Hoffmann et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556}}), and GPT-4 86.4% in March 2023, 918 days after launch ({{GPT-4 technical report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774}}). The dots streaming up the line are the frontier climbing.
  3. Contaminated. Questions leak into training data, deliberately or by web scrape, and the score decouples from capability. MMLU's questions come from the public web; asked to restore a masked wrong option, GPT-4 reproduced it exactly 57% of the time (the second sign in Contamination and score inflation below). Orange: items going out to the crawl; red: memory coming back as score.
  4. Saturates. Top models cluster within noise of each other and of the answer key's error rate. o1 scored 92.3% in September 2024 ({{OpenAI, 12 Sep 2024|https://openai.com/index/learning-to-reason-with-llms/}}); the red band is the roughly 6.5% of questions that {{MMLU-Redux|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04127}} found to contain an error of some kind, which is where saturation meets the answer key: the top of the plate is now the key, not the model.
  5. Retired, then replaced. Still quoted for continuity and marketing ("zombie" phase), then replaced by a harder sibling or a new design; cards moved to MMLU-Pro (June 2024), GPQA and HLE. In the same lane, GPQA Diamond (November 2023) climbed to 95.8% in Epoch AI's runs and was dropped as saturated from the Artificial Analysis index in September 2026; Humanity's Last Exam (HLE, January 2025) stands at 54.8%, still discriminating (best score so far in the external results Epoch AI compiles: {{Saturation timeline|#t-sat}}).
  6. Faster every generation. On one time axis, MMLU needed 918 days to gain 42.5 points. Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1, 70 research tasks in a terminal sandbox launched on 27 August 2026 to sit far below saturation, gained 22.6 points in its first five days and 34.6 in 26: Claude Opus 5 led at 30.0% ({{announcement|https://www.terminal-bench-science.ai/announcement}}), Claude Fable 5.1 scored 52.6% on Anthropic's own run on 1 September, and GPT-6 Astra stood at 64.6% on 22 September in the comparison table of Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 release ({{Topic: llm-landscape, Benchmarks tab|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}). That is about 29 times MMLU's pace derived: 39.92 points per 30 days against 1.39, where points per 30 days is (score minus launch score) divided by days since launch, times 30. Terminal-Bench itself went from 2.0 to 4.0 in 292 days (the faint plates behind it).
  7. Every family, every benchmark. Each tile is one benchmark of the {{Benchmark atlas|#t-atlas}} at its launch year, in the lane of the child page that owns it: green still discriminating, amber saturating, grey saturated or retired; the count on the right is how many still discriminate. Every lane is grey at the back and green at the front: each family has replaced its benchmarks at least twice, and the survivors are recent. Lanes, front to back: Knowledge and reasoning, Math, Coding, Agentic, Long context and multimodal, Human preference, Safety and honesty.

Status as the atlas records it (read 4 October 2026); six atlas rows that belong to no child (the classic era, the Artificial Analysis index, FLORES-200, the Vals AI legal bench) are left out. The two benchmarks of steps 1 to 4 and 6 are different tests, so compare their shapes and speeds, not their heights; the scores are best published scores at each date.

The useful period used to be two to four years; it is now often under eighteen months, and sometimes weeks. The Terminal-Bench-Science figures are the labs' own; Artificial Analysis's re-runs are lower (43.3% for Fable 5.1, 63.3% for GPT-6 Astra) but tell the same story. Nothing is wrong with the benchmark; the rate is the finding, and its maintainers already have 0.2 in development. The {{Saturation timeline|#t-sat}} shows the same arc for every major benchmark with dated points.

Replacement follows two patterns (the chains in the figure at the top): a harder sibling (MMLU, then MMLU-Pro, GPQA, Humanity's Last Exam), or a change of design: a live set pinned to dates (LiveCodeBench, the current year's AIME, SWE-rebench), an interactive environment (ARC-AGI-3), a private or rotating set (FrontierMath, HLE's private split), a paired human baseline (METR time horizons, GDPval, OSWorld). The designs that last share one property: the model cannot have seen the test.

Aggregators retire benchmarks in public. On 4 September 2026 the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.2 dropped GPQA Diamond as "an exceptional scientific reasoning evaluation that has now been saturated" (GPT-6 Astra scores 96.0%), added AA-Briefcase and GDP.pdf and doubled the weight of private held-out sets to 40% ({{Artificial Analysis|https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-2}}); v5 (27 October) retired GDPval-AA, SciCode and AA-LCR and raised the private share to 60%. The lesson for anyone running evaluations: pin versions, rotate the suite yearly, keep a private regression set that never touches the internet.

A saturated benchmark still holds signal in its transcripts
In a retrospective simulation, judging pairs of correct transcripts from saturated benchmarks predicted capability on held-out unsaturated items with 5.3 times less error than the saturated scores alone, though it underestimated the first reasoning models of early 2025 ({{Hamada et al., Oct 2026|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20667}}).
