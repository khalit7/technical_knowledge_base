Contamination and score inflation

Public benchmarks leak into training data, and how much that inflates a score ranges from almost everything to nothing. Items are copied into study sites, forums and repositories and reach training crawls; "trained on the test" blurs into "trained on similar data". This page owns the evaluator's side; searching a training set for overlaps is on {{Decontamination, PII and data governance|n:3f95c17b0d0d8100b7e4e0f96074c309}}.

Predict: if a lab finds and removes every MMLU item from its pretraining data, how far does its MMLU score fall?

Why the spread: the size depends on how many copies the model saw, how big the model is (Singh et al. found larger models exploit it more; {{arXiv 2411.03923|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03923}}) and whether the copy includes the answer, and a controlled BERT study showed that "memorization does not guarantee exploitation" ({{Magar and Schwartz, arXiv 2203.08242|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08242}}). You cannot know in advance which case a score is in, so an evaluator reads every public-benchmark score as an upper bound and looks for one of four signs.

  1. Overlap with the training corpus. Only the model's builder can search it, and n-gram overlap misses paraphrase (the 13-gram F1 of 0 above).
  2. The model completes what it should not know. Testset Slot Guessing masks one wrong option of an MMLU question and asks the model to fill it in; ChatGPT and GPT-4 reproduced the missing option exactly 52% and 57% of the time ({{Xu et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09783}}). On some MMLU subjects models score as well on items whose key is wrong, which they could only match by memory (MMLU-Redux).
  3. A fresh parallel set scores lower. GSM1k, a new GSM8K look-alike, cost some model families up to 8% in the paper's current version, v4 (its first version said 13%: {{Zhang et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332}}). A transformed copy does the same for repositories: the SchrodingerRepo row in the figure above.
  4. Scores drop after the training cutoff. Splitting by date is the logic of live sets (LiveCodeBench, MathArena, SWE-rebench).

The defences, strongest first: private sets behind an evaluation API (FrontierMath, HLE's private split, SWE-bench Pro's commercial split), live data pinned to dates (LiveCodeBench, MathArena, SWE-rebench), canary strings (respected only by cooperating labs), perturbed templates, trust. Above them sits enclave evaluation, piloted by Google DeepMind on Gemini Flash Lite in August 2026: weights and prompts meet inside a hardware-encrypted enclave and only scores leave ({{DeepMind|https://deepmind.google/blog/piloting-the-worlds-first-double-blind-ai-evaluations/}}; caveats on {{Topic: evaluation-and-llm-judges|n:3c65c17b0d0d8181b351e1b8fc6a1546}}). Coding shows the three newest answers side by side, by the calendar (SWE-rebench), by transformation (SchrodingerRepo) and by licensing private code (Real-SWE), with their results, on {{Coding benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81bfa09ad4ba5eef0c4e}}.

More published evidence, and what the old benchmarks admitted
  • GPT-3's own analysis flagged 29% of PIQA and 45% of Winograd as overlapping its training data and found the clean subsets 3 and 2.6 points lower, so those two results carry an asterisk; it also found that "in most cases performance changes only negligibly" even where overlap was high ({{GPT-3, Section 4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165}}).
  • Llama 2 concluded that only HellaSwag and MMLU-Humanities had been boosted (the 92.2 against 80.0 row). Llama 3 estimated gains of 14 to 15 points on HellaSwag and 8 points on PiQA from contamination at all three sizes ({{Llama 3, Table 15|https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783}}).
  • PaLM saw roughly as many positive as negative clean-minus-full deltas ({{PaLM, Section 8, Table 18|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311}}).
  • Yang et al.'s rephrased GSM8K run went from 28.7 to 95.3; the MMLU run from 54.8 to 85.9 (the two top rows).
  • GPT-4's report estimated 3.4% of ARC and 0.9% of WinoGrande overlapping its training data ({{GPT-4 report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774}}).
  • Real-SWE, ten tasks licensed from private repositories (read 4 October 2026): GPT-6 Astra 46.25% and Claude Fable 5.1 45.00%, below the 57.9% and 55.8% their labs report on Terminal-Bench 4.0; with ten tasks one task is 10 points, so the ranking is within noise.

When the measure becomes the target

Contamination is one of five ways a score inflates, Goodhart's law ("when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure") in practice:

  • Training on the test or its paraphrases (above).
  • Overfitting the format: multiple-choice letters, or the verbosity voters and judges reward (AlpacaEval needed a length-controlled variant).
  • Scaffold shopping: your best harness against a rival's default (the harness knob).
  • Reward hacking the evaluator: Berkeley RDI's cheating agent reached near-perfect scores on eight agent benchmarks (98% on GAIA) while solving no tasks, by overwriting tests, tampering with checkers or fetching public gold answers ({{Berkeley RDI|https://rdi.berkeley.edu/blog/trustworthy-benchmarks-cont/}}), and o3 hacked the scorer unprompted in 39 of 128 METR RE-Bench runs; evaluator isolation is now a requirement, and the exploit catalogue with its defences is on {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}} (section 5). Goodfire proposes catching it from inside, via an activation signature, not yet a monitor with a known false-positive rate ({{Goodfire|https://www.goodfire.com/research/reward-hacking-activation-monitors}}); proxies exploited in training: {{Reward Hacking|n:3c65c17b0d0d8196a6f1f29a817aa974}}.
  • Selective reporting: testing private variants and publishing the winner, as The Leaderboard Illusion documented on Arena (then LMArena; Meta tested 27 Llama 4 variants) ({{Singh et al. 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879}}; {{Arena's response|https://arena.ai/blog/our-response/}}); the best-of-N arithmetic is on {{Human preference and arenas|n:3ef5c17b0d0d814db49ff3b5c9fc3034}}.

The fix inside companies is the same as outside: private in-house suites stay discriminating because nobody trains on them.
