Attack matters: HarmBench, every model against every attack

An attack success rate (ASR) is a property of a model and an attack, not of the model. HarmBench's authors ran 16 attack configurations against 29 models on the same test behaviours and graded every completion with the same classifier ({{Mazeika et al., arXiv 2402.04249v2, appendix Tables 6 and 7|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249}}, February 2024, paper authors' runs). Every number below is transcribed from those tables; blank cells are attacks that were not run (white-box attacks need the weights, so closed models only face the black-box ones).

Try: rank by Direct request, then by worst attack: GPT-4 Turbo goes from 6.9% to 81.8%, Llama 2 7B Chat from 0.0% to 32.1%. Then switch to the copyright set, where the ranking is about something else entirely.
Columns: DR direct request (no attack); Human, human-written jailbreak templates; ZS zero-shot generated prompts; PAP persuasion (top 5); PAIR, TAP and TAP-T (transfer) attacker-LLM loops; AutoDAN genetic search; GCG (per behaviour), GCG-M (multi-behaviour), GCG-T (transfer), PEZ, GBDA, UAT, AP and SFS (stochastic few-shot) token or prompt optimisers. "Zephyr 7B + R2D2" is the authors' adversarially trained model.
