A safety benchmark answers one of four separate questions (does the model say what is true and what it believes; does it refuse what it should and only that; could it do serious harm if it tried; does it behave the same when it thinks no one is checking), and every number it reports means something only beside its mirror: the over-refusals beside the refusals, the wrong answers beside the right ones, the strongest attack beside the plain request, the unwatched behaviour beside the watched.

Measurement only: no attack method is described beyond what is needed to read a score.

Over-refusal: declining a safe request because it looks dangerous. Under-refusal: answering an unsafe one.
Attack success rate (ASR): share of harmful behaviours for which an attack got a harmful answer, as judged by a grader.
Abstention: the model says it does not know; scored 0, neither right nor wrong. Hallucination rate: wrong answers as a share of all answers that were not right.
Honesty (MASK): the statement matches the model's own elicited belief. Accuracy: the belief matches the truth.
Sycophancy: the answer moves towards what the user signals they want.
Propensity: what the model chooses to do with safeguards on. Capability: what it can do with refusals removed.
Uplift: how much better people do a task with the model than without it, in a controlled trial.
Evaluation awareness: the model says, or acts as if, it is being tested.
