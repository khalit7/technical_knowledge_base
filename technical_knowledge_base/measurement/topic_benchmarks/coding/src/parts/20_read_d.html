Other coding benchmarks worth knowing

Aider Polyglot (Aider, December 2024) replaced Aider's Python-only editing benchmark after it saturated (Claude 3.5 Sonnet 84.2%, "Models as old as GPT 3.5 Turbo were able to solve half of the 133 problems"). From Exercism's 697 exercises in C++, Go, Java, JavaScript, Python and Rust it kept the 225 "solved by 3 or fewer" of seven strong models; the model edits code through Aider's edit formats and gets a second attempt after seeing failing tests. o1 (high) launched it at 62% ({{Aider|https://aider.chat/2024/12/21/polyglot.html}}). It is cheap to run and tests the editing loop practitioners use, which made it popular with them; its top is 88.0% (GPT-5 high, diff format, August 2025) and the board has added few frontier entries since ({{leaderboard|https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/}}, via Epoch AI). Saturating.

DeepSWE (Datacurve, 2026) runs every model in one fixed minimal harness (mini-swe-agent) on 113 repository tasks (v1.1) and reports pass@1 averaged over 4 runs with a 95% interval; the top is Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) at 73.8% ± 1.4 at a mean $2.36 per task, board updated 22 September 2026 ({{DeepSWE|https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/}}, as read for the {{Topic: llms, Benchmarks tab|n:3c65c17b0d0d812d9e00f6ec89965286}}). A fixed harness is what makes its readings comparable across models; it trades away the vendor agents that Real-SWE keeps on purpose.

Phi-Bench (USTC, StepFun and others, September 2026) asks whether a model can engineer the infrastructure AI runs on: 85 open-ended tasks in nine categories (training systems, inference and serving, compression, kernels, I/O, hardware and edge, data infrastructure, system optimisation, system assurance) in three formats of rising scope: 55 single-file kernel completions, 20 multi-file repository implementations and 10 end-to-end optimisations where the model must first find the bottleneck. Tasks were reconstructed from pull requests and issues, mined with agent help and generated tests, or curated by experts where no history exists, grounded in 2,260 papers and 1,852 engineering artifacts. Claude Opus 5 leads at 36.53, then Kimi K3 28.12 and Qwen3.8 Max 27.73 ({{Ding et al. 2026|https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10226}}). The knowledge base's {{Phi-Bench paper page|n:3db5c17b0d0d81029236da6c38f5891f}} replays its MoE training task and simulates its scoring.

Corrections to the old page (from the Phi-Bench paper page)
36.53 is a mean reward in which matching the expert reference earns zero, not a percentage of tasks passed. "Hardware and edge tasks collapse to 5.4%" is Qwen3.7 Max, seventh overall, on a category of 3 tasks; Claude Opus 5 scores 3.9 there. "Longer reasoning budgets do not reliably help" is too strong: the effort figure covers 30 tasks, all three models tested are best at maximum effort, and Kimi K3 loses 45% at low effort.

Terminal-Bench (agents completing tasks in a terminal; version 4.0 current since September 2026, not comparable with 2.x) is often quoted beside SWE-bench Pro as a coding number. It belongs to the {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}} page, which owns its versions and harnesses.

What to use now (October 2026)

  • Repository-level engineering, the main question: SWE-Bench Pro V2's private split (Scale's runs; the public split is saturated), SWE-rebench's current window for fresh public issues, Real-SWE for private enterprise code (remember its ten tasks), and DeepSWE for a fixed-harness comparison. Quote each with its version, split, harness and date. Terminal-Bench 4.0 for terminal work (on the Agentic page).
  • SWE-bench Verified only for continuity with 2024 and 2025 reports, and never to rank frontier models: the top eight sit within 3.6 points on a memorised, years-old set.
  • Function-level and contest code: nothing current and independent remains. LiveCodeBench's board and LiveCodeBench Pro's ratings are frozen (April and November 2025), HumanEval and MBPP are saturated. Vendor Codeforces ratings are not comparable across labs; a live contest (ICPC, IOI) is the one clean result. HumanEval+ and MBPP+ stay useful as cheap regression tests for small models.
  • Infrastructure engineering: Phi-Bench, reading its score as a reward against an expert, not a pass rate.
Correction to the old page's advice
"Model-level codegen: LiveCodeBench (latest window) plus LiveCodeBench Pro rating" no longer works: neither board has new problems or ratings since 2025. "SWE-bench Pro (state the split and scaffold)" now also needs the major version, and on V2 the private split.

Common mistakes

Comparing pass@1 with pass@k, or the plug-in estimate with the Codex one
pass@100 (70.2% for Codex-12B) and pass@1 (28.8%) are different metrics. pass@k computed as 1 − (1 − p̂)k underestimates; use 1 − C(n−c,k)/C(n,k) with n ≥ k samples, and report n and the temperature. {{pass@k lab|#t-pk}}.
Treating "passes the tests" as "correct"
HumanEval graded a sample that calls every negative number prime as CORRECT; HumanEval+ caught it. Verified's median task has one fail-to-pass test. Ask how many tests, and whether anyone tried to break them.
Quoting a frozen leaderboard as current
LiveCodeBench (April 2025 problems), LiveCodeBench Pro (November 2025 ratings), BigCodeBench (April 2025 models), SWE-bench Multimodal (no 2026 entry) and swebench.com's Verified board (February 2026) are all frozen. "Unmaintained" and "saturated" are different reasons to stop using a benchmark; check the date of the last entry.
Calling "SWE-bench Pro" one benchmark
V1 public, V1 commercial, V2 public, V2 Hard and V2 private give 79.2% to 99.4% for one model. Name the version and split, and prefer the private one.
Comparing scores across harness columns
% resolved belongs to model plus scaffold, turn limit and cost cap. Bash-only, open-scaffold and vendor-agent figures are not comparable; on SWE-bench Pro V1 the same GPT-5 scored 23.3% (200 turns) and 41.8% (250 turns, uncapped cost) on the same split.
Reading a release date as a training cutoff
A rolling benchmark protects a model only on tasks newer than its training data. Tasks published weeks before a release are almost never in it, which is why SWE-rebench's before-and-after-release split shows nothing, while LiveCodeBench's split around DeepSeek-V3's marker shows a 35-point drop.
Ranking models on ten tasks
On Real-SWE one task is 10 points of a model's score; dropping a single task can change the leader. Read small private benchmarks as evidence of a gap, not as a ranking.
Ignoring answer leakage inside the task
Some SWE-bench issues contain the fix (32.67% of one agent's resolved tasks in SWE-Bench+). A resolved task can mean the model copied the issue's suggestion; transformed or private tasks remove that route.
