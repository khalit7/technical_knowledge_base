pass@k lab

pass@k is the chance that at least one of k sampled programs passes the tests. The Codex paper's estimator draws n ≥ k samples per problem, counts the c that pass, and computes 1 − C(n−c, k)/C(n, k), the chance that k samples drawn without replacement from those n all fail. The plug-in 1 − (1 − c/n)k looks equivalent and is biased low ({{Chen et al. 2021, Appendix A|https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374}}). Background and the step-by-step animation are in the {{Reading tab|#t-read}}.

One problem: n, c and k

Try: n = 10, c = 1, k = 10: the Codex estimator is 100% (one of the ten passed, and k = n takes all ten), the plug-in 65.1%. Then raise n with c/n fixed and watch the two converge slowly.

Bias, exactly: the average of each estimator over every possible draw

Fix a true pass rate p and a sample count n. c is then Binomial(n, p), and each estimator's average over c can be computed exactly. The true pass@k is 1 − (1 − p)k.

Real per-problem results: LiveCodeBench, ten models

LiveCodeBench's board publishes, for every problem and model, the share of samples that passed. For nine models that share comes in steps of 10% (10 samples per problem) and for EXAONE 4.0 in steps of 25% (4 samples), so c is known exactly derived ({{board data|https://livecodebench.github.io/performances_generation.json}}, 1,055 problems, May 2023 to April 2025). From those counts both estimators give a pass@k curve.
