LiveCodeBench by date

LiveCodeBench's defence against contamination is a date slider: every problem carries the date its contest ran, and a model is fairly scored only on problems from after its training data ended. This tab rebuilds that slider on the board's own per-problem data: 1,055 problems from LeetCode, AtCoder and Codeforces (May 2023 to April 2025) and 28 models ({{performances_generation.json|https://livecodebench.github.io/performances_generation.json}}, read 4 October 2026; {{Jain et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974}}). Scores are pass@1 averaged over each model's samples (10 for most non-reasoning models, 1 for reasoning models). The board froze in April 2025, so every 2026 model would sit entirely in the flagged zone.

The board for a window of your choice

Try: move the window start from May 2023 to August 2024. Every model loses points, because later problems are harder (o4-mini high: 87.3% to 80.2%); most lose 8 to 15. DeepSeek-V3 loses 21.3, the most of all 28, and its loss starts at its marker, as the monthly chart below shows derived. Then filter to medium problems.

"Marker" is the date LiveCodeBench stores for each model, which its own interface uses to flag possible contamination; for most API models it is a placeholder (30 April 2023), so the flag means little for them. The ± is one standard error from problem sampling, √(p(1−p)/n) on the per-problem scores treated as pass or fail derived, an approximation. The 80.2% for o4-mini (high) on 1 August 2024 to 7 April 2025 (454 problems) reproduces the parent page's reading derived. DeepSeek-V3's 2025 rows (zero even on easy problems, apparently failed runs) are excluded from its score. The board holds Codeforces problems only from August to October 2023 (9 problems); later problems are LeetCode and AtCoder.
