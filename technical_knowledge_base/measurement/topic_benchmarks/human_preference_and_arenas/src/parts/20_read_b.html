How sure is a rating? Intervals and rank spreads

A rating is an estimate from a sample of votes, so every score comes with a 95% interval. The method has changed twice. From December 2023 the intervals came from a bootstrap: refit the model on votes resampled with replacement, many times ({{LMSYS|https://lmsys.org/blog/2023-12-07-leaderboard/}}). On 23 July 2025 Arena switched to intervals from the central limit theorem for M-estimators and added a reweighting for the platform's unequal sampling of models ({{leaderboard changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}). That is the sandwich estimator, Var(θ̂) = H−1BH−1 with H the Hessian of the loss and B the covariance of its gradients, as Arena writes it out in its factuality post ({{Arena, 14 Jul 2026|https://arena.ai/blog/factuality-in-arena}}).

Two models whose intervals overlap may be in either order, so ranks are fuzzy too. Since 14 November 2025 each model shows a raw rank (its place by score, no ties) and a rank spread ({{Arena's ranking method|https://arena.ai/blog/ranking-method}}):

best = 1 + #{others with lower bound > my upper bound}   worst = 1 + #{others with upper bound > my lower bound}
spread
the ranks a model could hold if its true score were at the top of its interval and everyone else's at the bottom, and the reverse; two models are tied when their spreads overlap

Applied to the text board of 2 October 2026 (style control, all 413 models) derived: only the leader is alone. Gemini 4 Argon, a preliminary entry with 4,932 votes, sits at 1525 (1516 to 1534) with a spread of 1 to 1; the next twelve all have spreads that reach rank 2 and run to rank 7 or worse. Claude Opus 5.5 (high), fourth on raw rank, could be anywhere from 2nd to 13th. A preliminary tag, introduced on 18 September 2025, marks models tested anonymously before release ({{changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}).

Intervals cover sampling noise, not fragility. A 2025 study asked how many votes must be dropped, chosen adversarially, to change the board's top model: "dropping just 0.003% of human preferences can change the top-ranked model on Chatbot Arena", while MT-Bench's expert-graded rankings were far more stable ({{arXiv 2508.11847|https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11847}}). Intervals assume the votes are an honest random sample; the critiques below are about when they are not.

Style control: separating what is said from how

Voters like long, formatted answers. Is a model ahead because its answers are better, or because they are longer and bolder? Style control answers that inside the same regression: add columns for the difference in style between the two answers, so the style effect gets its own coefficient and each model's rating is what is left ({{Arena, 29 Aug 2024|https://arena.ai/blog/style-control}}).

logit P(A wins) = ln10 · (RA − RB) + γ · z
z
four style features, each the difference over the sum, (xA − xB) / (xA + xB + 1), standardised over all votes: answer tokens, markdown headers, list items, bold spans (FastChat's STYLE_CONTROL_ELEMENTS_V1); the ratio makes 500 against 520 tokens nearly equal and 20 against 40 very different
γ
the style coefficients, fitted with the ratings; a small ridge penalty (0.5) as in FastChat

Reproduced. On the 1.76 million votes of the August 2024 public log this page's fit gives γ = 0.249 for length, 0.024 for headers, 0.031 for lists and 0.019 for bold, the values LMArena published (0.249, 0.024, 0.031, 0.019), and 0.268 for length alone (published 0.267) reproduced. The ratings match LMArena's style-controlled board of 27 August 2024 to a mean of 1.1 points, and within 1.4 points for the top 20. Length dominates: one standard deviation of relative length is worth odds of e0.249 = 1.28, a 56.2% win chance between otherwise equal answers derived; markdown is second order.

Default since 16 May 2025. Style control became the default for the text and vision boards, with the offset adjusted so both views share a scale ({{leaderboard-dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset}}). Its effect is still large: on 2 October 2026 Claude Opus 5 (max) reads 1507.0 without style control and 1489.5 with it, and GPT-6 Astra (max) is 29th with it and 71st without ({{Today's board, three ways|#t-board}}). Style control is observational: a longer answer may also be a better one (a worked derivation is both), and LMArena's post says as much ("possible unobserved confounders such as positive correlation between length and substantive quality").
