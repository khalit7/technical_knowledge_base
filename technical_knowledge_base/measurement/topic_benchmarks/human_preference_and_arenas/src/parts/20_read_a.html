The idea in one screen

An arena turns millions of "A or B?" votes from real users into one Bradley-Terry score per model; that score measures what people prefer on the prompts they bring, with a statistical interval, and it bends wherever the votes stop being an honest random sample of taste: long or flattering answers, which models get shown, which private variants get kept.

A preference benchmark needs no answer key, so it can score open-ended chat, code that renders, images and search answers, and its prompts come fresh from users, so there is little to memorise. What it measures is preference, not correctness. Two families: live arenas (Arena, formerly Chatbot Arena and then LMArena, renamed Arena on 28 January 2026 per {{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_(AI_platform)}}) collect votes from people; judge-graded boards (MT-Bench, AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, WildBench) replace the voters with an LLM judge on a fixed prompt set, and are built to correlate with the arena.

One vote becomes a score, and four places where the number bends

  1. 1Who votes, on what. Voters reward length, markdown, citations and flattery; style control removes length and markdown only (section 3, section 4).
  2. 2Which models are shown and kept. Sampling rates differ by provider; testing 50 private variants and keeping the best is worth about +11 points (section 4).
  3. 3Which lens. The default text board has been style-controlled since 16 May 2025; a factuality toggle mixes in claim-checked votes (section 3).
  4. 4Intervals cover sampling noise only. Dropping 0.003% of votes, chosen adversarially, can change the top model (section 2).

Numbers on the figure: Arena Text board published 2 October 2026 (8,626,731 votes, 413 models, {{arena.ai|https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text}}, read 4 October 2026); the win probability and the length coefficient are explained in sections 1 and 3.

New terms

Battle: one prompt, two anonymous answers, one vote (A, B, tie, both bad).
Bradley-Terry score: one strength per model fitted to all votes at once; "Elo" in common speech.
Online Elo: chess's per-game update; the arena's method until January 2024.
Style control: style differences as extra regression columns, so length gets its own coefficient.
Rank spread: the best and worst rank a model could hold given everyone's intervals.
Preliminary: a model tested anonymously before release, few votes.
Factuality lens: a fit mixing 25% claim-checked "factuality votes" with 75% human votes.
Length-controlled win rate: a judge's win rate with the length term regressed out (AlpacaEval 2).
Null model: a constant, judge-targeted answer used to show a judge board can be gamed.
