Human preference benchmarks in one screen

A preference benchmark asks which of two answers a person (or a model standing in for one) likes better, and turns many such choices into a rating. It needs no answer key, so it can score open-ended chat, code that renders, images and search answers; and the prompts come fresh from users, so there is little to memorise. What it measures is preference, though, not correctness: a confident, long, well-formatted answer can win a vote it should lose.

Two families. Live arenas collect votes from real users on two anonymous models: Arena (Chatbot Arena from 2023, then LMArena, renamed Arena on 28 January 2026 per {{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_(AI_platform)}}) fits a Bradley-Terry model to millions of votes, now with style control on by default and a factuality toggle since July 2026. Judge-graded boards replace the voters with an LLM judge on a fixed prompt set (MT-Bench, AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, WildBench): cheap and repeatable, built to correlate with the arena, and all now dormant or gamed. How the judge itself is built, biased and calibrated belongs to {{LLM-as-judge: design, biases, calibration, reliability|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}}; this page covers the leaderboards.

From votes to a rating

The battle

A user types a prompt; two models chosen by the platform answer side by side without names; the user votes A is better, B is better, tie or both are bad, and only then are the names revealed. Only anonymous votes count: FastChat's rating code keeps battles[battles["anony"]] before fitting ({{elo_analysis.py|https://github.com/lm-sys/FastChat/blob/main/fastchat/serve/monitor/elo_analysis.py}}). The first vote in the public log is dated 24 April 2023 derived, and by the Chatbot Arena paper (March 2024) there were "over 240K votes" ({{Chiang et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132}}). The prompts are whatever users ask, which is the design's strength (fresh, varied, no fixed test set to leak) and its weakness (the mix of prompts, and of voters, is whoever turns up).

Bradley-Terry: one number per model

The Bradley-Terry model gives each model a strength and says the chance that one beats another depends only on the gap. On Arena's scale:

P(A beats B) = 1 / (1 + 10(RB − RA) / 400)
R
a model's Arena score; only differences mean anything, so the scale is pinned by an anchor (FastChat set Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct to 1114; {{rating_systems.py|https://github.com/lm-sys/FastChat/blob/main/fastchat/serve/monitor/rating_systems.py}})
400, 10
the chess Elo convention: a 100-point gap is a 64.0% expected win rate, 200 points 76.0% derived

The ratings are the maximum-likelihood fit to all votes at once: a logistic regression with one column per model (+1 for the left model, −1 for the right) and the vote as the label. A tie counts as half a win for each side ("this form naturally counts a draw as half a win and half a loss", in the same file). Because the fit uses every vote together, the order in which votes arrive does not matter.

It used to. Until late 2023 the board used online Elo, chess's update rule: after each vote, RA moves by K × (result − expected), with K = 4 in FastChat. That treats models as players whose strength drifts, but a released model's weights do not change, and the update makes the final rating depend on the order of the votes. LMSYS moved to Bradley-Terry with bootstrap confidence intervals, saying online Elo showed "considerable variability" and that "most models are static" ({{LMSYS, 7 Dec 2023|https://lmsys.org/blog/2023-12-07-leaderboard/}}); Arena's leaderboard dataset dates the switch to 9 January 2024 ({{leaderboard-dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset}}). The name "Elo" stayed in common use for what is now a Bradley-Terry score.

The fit reproduces the published board

Running FastChat's Bradley-Terry code, reimplemented here, on the 1,762,122 anonymous, de-duplicated votes of the {{public log of 26 August 2024|https://storage.googleapis.com/arena_external_data/public/clean_battle_20240826_public.json}} (up to the board's last vote) gives LMArena's published board of 27 August 2024 for all 136 models to within 0.48 points, mean gap 0.03 reproduced; the log of 14 August gives the board of 12 August within 2.97 points over 127 models. The de-duplication tag matters: without it the gaps reach 16.7 points. Ties were 34.7% of the votes used derived.
