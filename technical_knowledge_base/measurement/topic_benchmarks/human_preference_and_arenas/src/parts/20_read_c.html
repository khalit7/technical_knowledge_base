Category boards and the other arenas

Categories: the same votes, a subset at a time

The overall board mixes greetings, homework, code and fiction. A category board refits Bradley-Terry on the votes whose prompts carry a tag. The text board offers 29 views on 2 October 2026: languages, Hard Prompts, Coding, Math, Creative Writing, Instruction Following, Multi-Turn, Longer Query, Expert, and occupational ones such as Legal and Government or Medicine and Healthcare ({{leaderboard-dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset}}). The tags are themselves model-made. Hard Prompts (May 2024) asked Llama-3-70B-Instruct whether a prompt shows seven qualities (specificity, domain knowledge, complexity, problem-solving, creativity, technical accuracy, real-world application) and keeps those with six or more, about 20% of prompts ({{LMSYS, 20 May 2024|https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-05-17-category-hard/}}); in the public log of August 2024, 27.0% of the votes used carry six or more derived. The Expert and Occupational categories followed on 5 November 2025 ({{changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}). A category board has fewer votes, so wider intervals: read its rank spread, not its rank.

The other arenas

Each arena is its own fit on its own votes and its own scale, so scores do not compare across arenas: the Code Arena's leader stands at 1815 and the Vision leader at 1309 on the same day, which says nothing about one being "better".

  • Code Arena (WebDev). Both models build the same web app and the user votes on the result; since 12 November 2025 the WebDev board is "powered by Code Arena", which includes agentic, multi-step building ({{changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}), split into HTML and React in February 2026 and joined by Image-to-WebDev (April) and Fullstack (July 2026). Top on 1 October 2026: Claude Opus 5.5 (max) 1815 (±16, 2,062 votes), GPT-6 Astra (max) 1788, Claude Sonnet 5.5 (xhigh) 1786 ({{Code Arena|https://arena.ai/leaderboard/code}}). Coding benchmarks with tests are on {{Coding benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81bfa09ad4ba5eef0c4e}}.
  • Copilot Arena. Pairs of code completions shown inside the developer's editor: "over 4.5 million suggestions from 10 models" and "over 11k pairwise judgements"; its rankings "differ from those of existing evaluations", with preferences consistent across programming languages but varying by task category ({{Chi et al., Feb 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09328}}).
  • Search Arena. Models answer with web search; the leaderboard opened in August 2025. Its paper (about 24,000 conversations, 12,000 votes) found users prefer answers with more citations "even when the cited content does not directly support the attributed claims" ({{Miroyan et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05334}}). Style control matters most here: on the board of 24 August 2026 it moves Claude Opus 4.6 (search) from 1253 to 1223 while GPT-5.6 Sol (xhigh) moves from 1257 to 1256 derived from the {{leaderboard-dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset}}.
  • Vision, Document, Text-to-Image, Image Edit, Video. Image input from 2024, documents from March 2026, generated images and videos (Video Arena from January 2026) ({{March 2026 update|https://arena.ai/blog/march-2026-arena-updates}}; {{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_(AI_platform)}}). Text-to-Image had 6,478,738 votes on 24 September 2026. Multimodal benchmarks with answer keys are on {{Long-context and multimodal|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}.
  • Agent Arena (4 June 2026) is not a vote at all. Users run agents on real work; Arena randomises which model orchestrates and estimates each model's causal effect on signals such as confirmed task success, praise against complaint, steerability, recovery from failed shell commands and hallucinated tools, combined into a "net improvement" ({{Arena, 4 Jun 2026|https://arena.ai/blog/agent-arena-methodology}}). On 2 October 2026 Claude Fable 5.1 (max) led at 14.31% over 15,130 sessions, from 2,158,478 sessions in all ({{Agent Arena|https://arena.ai/leaderboard/agent}}). Agent benchmarks with checked end states are on {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}.
  • AutoEval (30 July 2026): a reward model trained on Arena's votes casts provisional votes for a new model within the hour, labelled "AutoEval" until human votes arrive; Arena says the model predicts human preference "8–10% more accurately than frontier LLM judges" ({{Arena|https://arena.ai/blog/autoeval-scores}}). A judge-made score shown on a human-preference board: check the label.

The critiques: who can game a vote

The Leaderboard Illusion, and the reply

In April 2025 Singh et al. argued that the arena's rules favoured a few providers ({{arXiv 2504.20879|https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879}}). Arena replied on 9 May 2025 ({{Arena's response|https://arena.ai/blog/our-response/}}). The two accounts, side by side:

The Leaderboard Illusion

Private testing: "undisclosed private testing practices benefit a handful of providers who are able to test multiple variants before public release and retract scores if desired"; "27 private LLM variants tested by Meta in the lead-up to the Llama-4 release".

Data access: Google and OpenAI received an estimated 19.2% and 20.4% of all arena data; 83 open-weight models together 29.7%. Proprietary models are sampled more and removed less; 205 of 243 public models were silently deprecated.

Overfitting: "even limited additional data can result in relative performance gains of up to 112% on the arena distribution".

Arena's response

Private testing was in a policy "published on March 1, 2024", open to any provider; Cohere ran more pre-release tests (9) than OpenAI or xAI. The paper's simulation of 100-point gains is "unrelated to Chatbot Arena"; the real effect is "around +11 Elo after 50 tests and 3000 votes".

Open models are 40.9% of battles, not 8.8%, once open-weight models such as Llama and Gemma are counted.

The 112% was measured on Arena-Hard, "a static benchmark with 500 data points". Changes: retired models marked clearly; scores "provisional" until 2,000 fresh votes when 10 or more variants were tested.

The 205 of 243 deprecations is from the paper's body as cited in the parent's {{Benchmark atlas|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}}; the quotations are from the abstract and the response. The two disagree on definitions (open-weight against open-source) as much as on facts.

Why picking the best of N variants inflates a score. If N variants are equally good and each is measured with noise σ, the best of the N measured scores beats the truth by about σ times the expected maximum of N standard normal draws, and the variant shipped then regresses. The widget computes that derived. With 50 variants and σ = 5.3 points (the standard error of a model with about 3,100 votes on the October 2026 board: Claude Sonnet 5.5 xhigh, ±10.4 on 3,145 votes) it gives about 12 points, the size of Arena's own "+11 after 50 tests and 3000 votes".

σ is scaled from the reference model as 5.3 × √(3,145 / votes); E[max] is computed exactly by numerical integration. All variants are assumed equally good, the worst case for selection (illustrative setting).

Llama 4 Maverick: the variant that was not the release

On 5 April 2025 Meta announced Llama 4 Maverick with "an experimental chat version scoring ELO of 1417 on LMArena" ({{Meta|https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-4-multimodal-intelligence/}}). The arena entry was "Llama-4-Maverick-03-26-Experimental", not the downloadable model. LMArena said "Meta should have made it clearer that Llama-4-Maverick-03-26-Experimental was a customized model to optimize for human preference", published more than 2,000 of its battles and changed its policy ({{The Register, 8 Apr 2025|https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/08/meta_llama4_cheating/}}). The released Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct first appears in Arena's published history on 9 April 2025 at rank 35 (1273, 2,662 votes); the experimental variant does not appear in that history at all, and neither does the experimental Gemini 2.5 Pro that Google announced on 25 March 2025 as debuting "at #1 on LMArena by a significant margin" ({{Google|https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/gemini-model-thinking-updates-march-2025/}}); the history's top entry on 9 April 2025 is chatgpt-4o-latest at 1406 derived from the {{leaderboard-dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset}}. The public record of the board is the board after cleaning.

Votes can be rigged

Answers betray their author: one study identified the model behind a reply "with more than 95% accuracy" and estimated that about a thousand votes could move the board; the arena added bot protection, rate limits, reCAPTCHA and logins in response ({{Huang et al., Jan 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07493}}). Another showed that votes on battles not involving the target model still move its rating through the shared fit, so "omnipresent" rigging improves a ranking with "only hundreds of new votes" (simulated on 1.7 million historical votes; {{Min et al., ICML 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17858}}). Arena's changelog records the defences: identity-leak detection and de-duplication (July 2025, January 2026) and an anomalous-voting filter (17 September 2025) ({{changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}).

Preference rewards flattery

What wins a vote is not always what is right. OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update shipped on 25 April 2025 after it became sycophantic, saying the change relied too much on short-term thumbs-up and thumbs-down feedback ({{OpenAI, 29 Apr 2025|https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/}}; {{follow-up|https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/}}; summary in {{TechCrunch|https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophant}}). That is a product, not the arena, but the signal is the same kind: a quick human judgement of one answer. Style control (above) removes length and formatting; it cannot see flattery or a confident error.

Factuality is Arena's direct answer (14 July 2026). It samples battles, extracts web-verifiable atomic claims from both answers, has search agents assign calibrated truth probabilities, and turns the gap in average truthfulness into a soft "factuality vote"; a composite Bradley-Terry fit weights those votes 25% and human votes 75%, as a non-default toggle on the Text and Search boards ({{Arena|https://arena.ai/blog/factuality-in-arena}}). Over 2 million claims labelled, it found a "weak positive correlation" between preference and factuality, OpenAI and xAI models rising with the factuality weight, Claude and Gemini falling or flat, and Muse Spark 1.1 and ERNIE 5.1 dropping heavily. The factuality judge is a model: its own calibration questions belong to {{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}}.
