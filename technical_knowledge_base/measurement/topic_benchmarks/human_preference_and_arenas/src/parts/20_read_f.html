6. Judge-graded proxies: the arena in a bottle

Votes are slow and live. From 2023 a family of offline boards replaced the crowd with a strong LLM judge on a fixed prompt set and validated themselves by how well they reproduced the arena's ranking. They are reproducible and cheap, and they inherit both the arena's taste and the judge's biases (position, verbosity, self-preference), which {{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}} treats as an instrument. All are now retired, dormant or gamed, but model cards still quote them and the same method lives on inside labs and in Arena's AutoEval.

Length control, concrete first. AlpacaEval 2 has 805 instructions; a GPT-4 Turbo judge compares each answer with GPT-4 Turbo's own. Judges prefer longer answers, so merely prompting GPT-4 to be verbose or concise moved its raw win rate from 22.9% to 64.3% ({{Dubois et al., 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04475}}).

Length control shrinks the swing from prompting alone, and agrees better with the arena

Bars span the win rates of GPT-4 Turbo (gpt4_1106_preview, the baseline itself) when prompted to be concise or verbose; Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena on the right. Values from Dubois et al. (2024), CC BY 4.0.

The fix is a regression: fit the judge's preference as a logistic model with a model term, a length term and an instruction-difficulty term, then report the win rate predicted with the length difference set to zero. Length control narrowed GPT-4 Turbo's swing to 41.9% to 51.6% (the normalised standard deviation across the three prompts fell from 25% to 10%) and raised the Spearman correlation with the arena from 0.94 to 0.98. It is the same idea as the arena's style control (section 3), applied to a judge.

P(judge prefers m over b on x) = logistic( θm − θb + φm,b · tanh(Δlen / std(Δlen)) + (ψm − ψb) γx )
θ
model strength (m evaluated, b the baseline)
φ
the length coefficient; tanh gives longer answers diminishing returns. LC win rate: the average over the 805 instructions with Δlen = 0
γx
instruction difficulty

The other three judge boards in one line each: MT-Bench (80 two-turn questions, GPT-4 grading 1 to 10) ran out of scale at 9.32; Arena-Hard-Auto (500 hard prompts mined from arena logs, judged against a baseline) and WildBench (1,024 real tasks with checklists) correlate with the arena at about 0.9 or better and are dormant.

MT-Bench, Arena-Hard-Auto and WildBench in detail (construction, agreement with the arena, errata, status)
MT-Bench
80 two-turn questions in 8 categories, each answer graded 1 to 10 by GPT-4. Its paper showed strong judges "achieving over 80% agreement" with human preferences, and released 3K expert votes and 30K conversations ({{Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685}}). Errata: 13 of 30 sampled GPT-4 reference answers for maths, coding and reasoning were wrong ({{FastChat PR 3158|https://github.com/lm-sys/FastChat/pull/3158}}). The last leaderboard table with the column tops out at 9.32 (GPT-4-1106-preview; {{leaderboard_table_20250804.csv|https://huggingface.co/spaces/lmarena-ai/arena-leaderboard}}): the scale ran out. Retired.
Arena-Hard-Auto
BenchBuilder selects hard, varied prompts from arena logs; v0.1 has 500, graded against GPT-4-0314 by GPT-4 Turbo, with "3x higher separation of model performances compared to MT-Bench" and "98.6% correlation with human preference rankings", "all at a cost of $20" ({{Li et al., 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11939}}). v2.0 (April 2025) has 500 new hard prompts plus 250 creative-writing prompts, Gemini 2.5 as judge, style control on; its top entry is o3 at 85.9% against o3-mini, and the board has not been updated since: newest entries April 2025, last commit June 2025 ({{arena-hard-auto|https://github.com/lmarena/arena-hard-auto}}). Dormant.
WildBench
1,024 tasks chosen from over a million real conversations, each with a checklist. WB-Reward compares pairs on five levels and turns a "slightly better" into a tie when the winner is more than K characters longer; WB-Score grades single answers. Against the arena: Pearson 0.98 (WB-Reward, top models) and 0.95 (WB-Score), against 0.91 for Arena-Hard and 0.89 for AlpacaEval 2 LC ({{Lin et al., 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04770}}). Last repository update August 2024 ({{WildBench|https://github.com/allenai/WildBench}}). Dormant.

The null model. A "model" that returns the same fixed, judge-targeted text whatever the instruction scored an 86.5% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0, 83.0 on Arena-Hard-Auto and 9.55 on MT-Bench ({{Zheng et al., Oct 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07137}}). On the AlpacaEval board it still ranks first, as a community entry (86.45%, August 2025), above the best real submission listed, GPT-4o at 57.45% ({{AlpacaEval|https://tatsu-lab.github.io/alpaca_eval/}}).

A constant answer beats every real model on two judge boards

Each board on its own scale (AlpacaEval and Arena-Hard as percentages, MT-Bench out of 10). "Best real" is the board's top non-null reading cited above. For Arena-Hard the null result is on v0.1, which has no maintained board now; v2.0's top is o3 at 85.9% against a different baseline and judge, so no bar is drawn for it.

Read any such number with its judge, its baseline and its version: a win rate against an unnamed baseline is not interpretable. A small checklist judge can rank like the arena at list level while agreeing with humans far less often per answer ({{RocketEval|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81ffa8e5f8fa5a5806c8}}). For a fast offline proxy during training, build a private prompt set from your own traffic, judge it with a validated judge and length control, and track it against occasional human votes.

Instruction following: checked by rule, not by taste (IFEval, IFBench, MultiChallenge)

The parent topic groups these with preference because both ask "did it do what I wanted?", but a verifier checks a constraint; no one votes.

  • IFEval (Google, 2023): 541 prompts with 25 kinds of verifiable instruction ("at least 300 words", "no commas") ({{Zhou et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911}}; {{dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/IFEval}}). Four scores (prompt or instruction level, strict or loose) are quoted interchangeably; self-reported tops cluster at 93 to 95% and the types are public and trainable. Saturated, and dropped from the Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon cards. Use it only as a floor.
  • IFBench (AI2, 2025): 300 prompts with 58 new out-of-domain constraints, to test whether instruction following generalises ({{Pyatkin et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02833}}). Artificial Analysis's run shows Grok 4.3 (medium) at 83.3% among its default models ({{AA|https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/ifbench}}).
  • MultiChallenge (Scale, 2025): 273 multi-turn conversations testing whether a model keeps earlier instructions, graded by a rubric-guided judge ({{Sirdeshmukh et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17399}}); Meta Muse Spark leads at 75.52% (±4.05), and the board has not moved since 3 February 2026 ({{Scale|https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/multichallenge}}).

Arena's "Instruction Following" category is a different thing: votes on prompts a model tagged as containing instructions.
