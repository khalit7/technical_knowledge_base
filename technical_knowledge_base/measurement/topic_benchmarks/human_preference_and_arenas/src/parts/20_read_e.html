5. Category boards and the specialised arenas

Categories are the same votes, a subset at a time. A category board refits Bradley-Terry on the votes whose prompts carry a tag. The text board offers 29 views on 2 October 2026: languages, Hard Prompts, Coding, Math, Creative Writing, Instruction Following, Multi-Turn, Longer Query, Expert, and occupational ones such as Legal and Government or Medicine and Healthcare ({{leaderboard-dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset}}). The tags are model-made: Hard Prompts (May 2024) asked Llama-3-70B-Instruct whether a prompt shows seven qualities (specificity, domain knowledge, complexity, problem-solving, creativity, technical accuracy, real-world application) and keeps those with six or more, about 20% of prompts ({{LMSYS, 20 May 2024|https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-05-17-category-hard/}}); in the public log of August 2024, 27.0% of the votes used carry six or more derived. Expert and Occupational followed on 5 November 2025 ({{changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}). Fewer votes mean wider intervals: read a category's rank spread, not its rank (try Coding in the board explorer of section 3).

The specialised arenas change one part of the battle. Each is its own fit on its own votes and its own scale, so scores do not compare across arenas: the Code Arena's leader stands at 1815 and the Vision leader at 1309 on the same day, which says nothing about one being "better".

Two of them are not human votes at all: Agent Arena estimates each model's causal effect on real agent sessions, and AutoEval fills in provisional votes from a reward model. Pair Code Arena with tested coding benchmarks and Agent Arena with checked end states.

Each arena in detail (Code, Copilot, Search, Vision and media, Agent Arena, AutoEval)
  • Code Arena (WebDev). Since 12 November 2025 the WebDev board is "powered by Code Arena", which includes agentic, multi-step building ({{changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}), split into HTML and React in February 2026 and joined by Image-to-WebDev (April) and Fullstack (July 2026). It measures the rendered result as users see it; correctness against tests is on {{Coding benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81bfa09ad4ba5eef0c4e}}.
  • Copilot Arena. "Over 4.5 million suggestions from 10 models" and "over 11k pairwise judgements"; its rankings "differ from those of existing evaluations", with preferences consistent across programming languages but varying by task category ({{Chi et al., Feb 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09328}}). A research deployment; its status after 2025 is unconfirmed.
  • Search Arena. The leaderboard opened in August 2025; its paper analysed about 24,000 conversations and 12,000 votes ({{Miroyan et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05334}}). Style control matters most here: on the board of 24 August 2026 it moves Claude Opus 4.6 (search) from 1253 to 1223 while GPT-5.6 Sol (xhigh) moves from 1257 to 1256 derived.
  • Vision, Document, Text-to-Image, Image Edit, Video. Image input from 2024, documents from March 2026, generated images and videos (Video Arena from January 2026) ({{March 2026 update|https://arena.ai/blog/march-2026-arena-updates}}; {{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_(AI_platform)}}). Benchmarks with answer keys for the same inputs are on {{Long-context and multimodal|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}.
  • Agent Arena (4 June 2026) is not a vote at all. Users run agents on real work; Arena randomises which model orchestrates and estimates each model's causal effect on signals such as confirmed task success, praise against complaint, steerability, recovery from failed shell commands and hallucinated tools, combined into a "net improvement" ({{Arena, 4 Jun 2026|https://arena.ai/blog/agent-arena-methodology}}). It is the only preference-style board that measures outcomes rather than votes; pair it with checked end states on {{Agentic benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb926d7b91ef8234e}}.
  • AutoEval (30 July 2026): a reward model trained on Arena's votes casts provisional votes for a new model within the hour, labelled "AutoEval" until human votes arrive; Arena says it predicts human preference "8–10% more accurately than frontier LLM judges" ({{Arena|https://arena.ai/blog/autoeval-scores}}). A judge-made score on a human-preference board: check the label.
