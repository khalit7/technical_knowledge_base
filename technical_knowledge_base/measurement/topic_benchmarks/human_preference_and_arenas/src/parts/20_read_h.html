8. How to read an arena number

Seven questions, each with the case on this page that shows why it matters.

  1. Which arena, category and lens?Each arena is a separate fit on its own scale, and the scale moves: the style-control offset was re-anchored on 16 May 2025 and the reweighting changed on 23 July 2025. A 1500 in Text and a 1500 in Code, or a 1400 in 2025 and a 1500 in 2026, are not comparable. Since May 2025 the default text and vision boards are style-controlled; Claude Opus 5 (max) reads 1507.0 without style control and 1489.5 with it on the same votes. Say which view you quote, and pick the category closest to your use (Hard Prompts, Coding, Multi-Turn, an occupational one).
  2. What is the rank spread, not the rank?Twelve models below the leader share overlapping rank spreads on the October 2026 text board; Claude Opus 5.5 (high) could be 2nd or 13th.
  3. How many votes, and is it labelled Preliminary or AutoEval?A preliminary entry with 4,932 votes led the board; an AutoEval score is a reward model's vote, not a person's.
  4. Is this the model you can use?Llama 4 Maverick's 1417 was an experimental chat variant; the release ranked 35th on its first appearance.
  5. Could selection explain the gap?Keeping the best of 50 private variants is worth about +11 points, the size of many top-ten gaps.
  6. Is it preference or capability you need?Among frontier models the arena and the capability index agree at Spearman 0.33, and voters reward length, formatting, citations and flattery. For truth, check the factuality lens; for capability, use the answer-keyed families; for agents, Agent Arena's net improvement plus checked agent benchmarks.
  7. For a judge-graded number: which judge, baseline and version?An AlpacaEval or Arena-Hard win rate is relative to one baseline under one judge, and a constant answer can top both.
