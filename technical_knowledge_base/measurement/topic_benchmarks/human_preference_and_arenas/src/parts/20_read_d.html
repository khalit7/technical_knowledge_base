4. Biases and gaming: when the votes stop being a fair sample

What voters reward besides quality. Length and markdown (section 3); citations, in Search Arena, "even when the cited content does not directly support the attributed claims" ({{Miroyan et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05334}}); and flattery. OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update shipped on 25 April 2025 after it became sycophantic, saying the change relied too much on short-term thumbs-up and thumbs-down feedback ({{OpenAI, 29 Apr 2025|https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/}}; {{follow-up|https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/}}; summary in {{TechCrunch|https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/29/openai-explains-why-chatgpt-became-too-sycophant}}). That is a product, not the arena, but the signal is the same kind: a quick human judgement of one answer. Sycophancy measured directly is on {{Safety and honesty benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d818da9d5efda1a443166}}.

The Leaderboard Illusion, and the reply

In April 2025 Singh et al. argued that the arena's rules favoured a few providers ({{arXiv 2504.20879|https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879}}); Arena replied on 9 May 2025 ({{Arena's response|https://arena.ai/blog/our-response/}}). The paper's own summary figure:

The Leaderboard Illusion

Private testing: "undisclosed private testing practices benefit a handful of providers who are able to test multiple variants before public release and retract scores if desired"; "27 private LLM variants tested by Meta in the lead-up to the Llama-4 release".

Data access: Google and OpenAI received an estimated 19.2% and 20.4% of all arena data; 83 open-weight models together 29.7%. Proprietary models are sampled more and removed less; 205 of 243 public models were silently deprecated.

Overfitting: "even limited additional data can result in relative performance gains of up to 112% on the arena distribution".

Arena's response

Private testing was in a policy "published on March 1, 2024", open to any provider; Cohere ran more pre-release tests (9) than OpenAI or xAI. The paper's simulation of 100-point gains is "unrelated to Chatbot Arena"; the real effect is "around +11 Elo after 50 tests and 3000 votes".

Open models are 40.9% of battles, not 8.8%, once open-weight models such as Llama and Gemma are counted.

The 112% was measured on Arena-Hard, "a static benchmark with 500 data points". Changes: retired models marked clearly; scores "provisional" until 2,000 fresh votes when 10 or more variants were tested.

The 205 of 243 deprecations is from the paper's body; the quotations are from the abstract and the response. The two disagree on definitions (open-weight against open-source) as much as on facts.

Why keeping the best of N variants inflates a score. If N variants are equally good and each is measured with noise σ, the best of the N measured scores beats the truth by about σ times the expected maximum of N standard normal draws, and the variant shipped then regresses. With 50 variants and σ = 5.3 points (the standard error of a model with about 3,100 votes on the October 2026 board: Claude Sonnet 5.5 xhigh, ±10.4 on 3,145 votes) it gives about 12 points, the size of Arena's own "+11 after 50 tests and 3000 votes" derived. Predict: does doubling the votes per variant halve the inflation?

Selection inflation grows with the number of variants and shrinks only as 1/√votes

Inflation = σ × E[max of N]; σ is scaled from the reference model as 5.3 × √(3,145 / votes); E[max] is computed exactly by numerical integration. All variants are assumed equally good, the worst case for selection (illustrative setting). Doubling the votes divides the inflation by √2, not 2.

Llama 4 Maverick: the variant that was not the release

On 5 April 2025 Meta announced Llama 4 Maverick with "an experimental chat version scoring ELO of 1417 on LMArena" ({{Meta|https://ai.meta.com/blog/llama-4-multimodal-intelligence/}}). The arena entry was "Llama-4-Maverick-03-26-Experimental", not the downloadable model. LMArena said "Meta should have made it clearer that Llama-4-Maverick-03-26-Experimental was a customized model to optimize for human preference", published more than 2,000 of its battles and changed its policy ({{The Register, 8 Apr 2025|https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/08/meta_llama4_cheating/}}). The released Llama-4-Maverick-17B-128E-Instruct first appears in Arena's published history on 9 April 2025 at rank 35 (1273, 2,662 votes) derived. The experimental Maverick variant does not appear in Arena's published history at all, and neither does the experimental Gemini 2.5 Pro that Google announced on 25 March 2025 as debuting "at #1 on LMArena by a significant margin" ({{Google|https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/gemini-model-thinking-updates-march-2025/}}); the history's top entry on 9 April 2025 is chatgpt-4o-latest at 1406 derived from the {{leaderboard-dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset}}.

Votes can be rigged

Answers betray their author: one study identified the model behind a reply "with more than 95% accuracy" and estimated that about a thousand votes could move the board; the arena added bot protection, rate limits, reCAPTCHA and logins in response ({{Huang et al., Jan 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07493}}). Another showed that votes on battles not involving the target model still move its rating through the shared fit, so "omnipresent" rigging improves a ranking with "only hundreds of new votes" (simulated on 1.7 million historical votes; {{Min et al., ICML 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17858}}). Arena's changelog records the defences: identity-leak detection and de-duplication (July 2025, January 2026) and an anomalous-voting filter (17 September 2025) ({{changelog|https://arena.ai/blog/leaderboard-changelog}}).
