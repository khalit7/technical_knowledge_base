3. The harness, the effort and the budget

Terminal-Bench 2.0's public board is the cleanest natural experiment: one fixed 89-task set, 5 trials per entry, and many agents submitted for the same model. Claude Opus 4.6 appears with nine agents, and they span about 18 points.

The provider's own harness is often not the best one for its model: Claude Code is the lowest of Opus 4.6's nine. How much the harness moves the number depends on the task:

  • On ordinary tasks, mostly the bill. {{Arena's HarnessTax study|https://arena.ai/blog/coding-agents-harness-tax}} (16 Sep 2026) ran 21 pairs, seven models in three harnesses (Claude Code, Codex CLI and Pi, a minimal open-source harness with four tools: read, write, edit, bash), on the same 30 sampled tasks each from SWE-bench Lite and Terminal-Bench 2.0, three attempts per task. The harness moved success by about ±2 points on SWE-bench Lite and about ±5 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, but Claude Code cost about 2.0× Pi and 1.6× Codex on SWE-bench Lite and 1.5× Pi on Terminal-Bench 2.0 (geometric means); its initial context was over 10× Pi's. Thirty tasks and three attempts cannot separate harnesses a few points apart, which the authors' bootstrap intervals reflect.
  • On long, hard tasks, the score. {{StateM|n:3c65c17b0d0d81899098d8153e100691}} (Aug 2026) wrapped a fixed command-line agent in a checked state-machine runbook and lifted GPT-5.5 (xhigh) from 83.1% to 92.1% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with the same weights (95.3% raw with GPT-5.6 Sol); the runbook was developed on the same 89 tasks it is scored on, and the leaderboard closed that submission without merging. On ARC-AGI-3, GPT-6 Astra at the same effort scored 62.7% in one harness and 98.6% in another ({{Knowledge and reasoning|n:3c65c17b0d0d810f8574da3ffb860be5}} owns that case).
  • Effort. On the official Terminal-Bench 4.0 board, GPT-6 Astra in Codex runs from 50.61% at low effort to 58.18% at max, and Claude Opus 5 scores 53.94% at xhigh but 51.82% at max. {{HAL|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977}} found higher reasoning effort reduced accuracy in the majority of its runs across 9 models and 9 benchmarks.
  • Budget. Time, steps and resources are part of the task. OSWorld-Verified reports scores per step budget (50 or 100 steps); Terminal-Bench 4.0 replaced per-task agent timeouts with a flat 8 hours, one reason it is a new major version.
  • Who ran it. Lab tables run their own harnesses and settings, in both directions: on Terminal-Bench-Science, Claude Fable 5.1 is 52.6% in Anthropic's own run and 40.0% on the official board, GPT-6 Astra 64.6% in Anthropic's table and 68.1% on the board ({{Terminal-Bench-Science board|https://www.terminal-bench-science.ai/}}, read 4 Oct 2026). Quote the board and say which.
  • One harness for everyone. {{Vals AI|https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-4}} runs Terminal-Bench 4.0 with every model in mini-swe-agent (a single bash tool, no step or cost limit), the average of three pass@1 runs in Daytona sandboxes on the upstream Harbor setup, no partial credit, the 8-hour limit. It is a separate board, not a new top of the official one: there Claude Opus 5.5 reads 65.15% ($13.20 per test) and GPT-6.1 Sol 55.05% at $1.72, about an eighth of the cost for 10 points less.

Cost and run-to-run variance are part of the result. A suite that reports only success cannot express that one harness charges twice another for the same answer. And reruns are expensive: {{HAL|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977}} (Princeton) reran 9 benchmarks with 21,730 rollouts for about $40,000, which is why most agent numbers rest on 1 to 5 trials. Controlled harness experiments, where only the harness changes, are on {{Harnesses for training and evaluation|n:3f15c17b0d0d815ea579d6c80065aa36}} (section 1) and {{Building a harness|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ca14fc32338707c2f}}.
