Best case or reliability: pass@k against pass^k

Agents are sampled, so the same task can pass on one try and fail on the next. Run every task n times and count its successes c. Two summaries answer opposite questions:

pass@k = mean over tasks of [ 1 − C(n−c, k) / C(n, k) ]     pass^k = mean over tasks of [ C(c, k) / C(n, k) ]
pass@k
chance that at least one of k tries succeeds: the best case, the metric of code benchmarks since Codex
pass^k
chance that all k tries succeed ("pass hat k"): reliability, introduced by {{τ-bench (Yao et al., 2024)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045}} for agents that must act correctly every time a customer calls
C(a, b)
binomial coefficient; both estimators are unbiased for k ≤ n. At k = 1 both equal the mean success rate.

The τ-bench paper's own example: gpt-4o as a function-calling agent passed 61% of retail tasks once, but pass^8 fell to about 25% ({{abstract and section 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045}}). The animation below runs the arithmetic on real released trials: GPT-5.2 (high) on τ²-bench's 114 retail tasks, four trials each, from the trajectory file Sierra published with its leaderboard submission (26 Feb 2026, user simulator GPT-5.2). Every value it shows is computed in the page from those 456 trials, and it lands on the leaderboard's published 81.58 / 69.59 / 59.87 / 51.75 exactly.

Each square is one task, placed in the column of how many of its 4 trials succeeded; its fill is the task's contribution to the metric at the current k (C(c,k)/C(4,k) for pass^k, 1 − C(4−c,k)/C(4,k) for pass@k). Only runs whose released file reproduces the published figures exactly are offered (27 of 36 files checked; the others are listed in pass^k on real trials).

Three things to take from it. First, the drop is set by the middle columns: a task solved 3 times in 4 counts 0.75 toward pass^1 and nothing toward pass^4, so a model whose failures are spread thinly across many tasks falls hardest. Second, rankings can change with k. On retail, GPT-5.2 (81.58), Claude Opus 4.5 (79.61) and Gemini 3 Flash (76.75) differ by five points at pass^1 and tie exactly at pass^4 (51.75: each solved 59 of 114 tasks four times out of four). On airline, Opus 4.5 leads at pass^1 (84.0 against 83.0) and GPT-5.2 at pass^4 (72.0 against 70.0); with 50 tasks that is one task either way, inside the noise. Third, pass@k flatters: the same GPT-5.2 run reaches 96.49% at pass@4, so "solved at least once" and "solved every time" differ by 45 points.

Other boards report the same split. {{MCPMark|https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24002}} launched with gpt-5 (medium) at 52.56% pass@1 and 33.86% pass^4; its board's last top entry is GPT-5.2 (high) at 57.5% pass@1 and 44.9% pass^4 ({{MCPMark leaderboard|https://mcpmark.ai/leaderboard}}, updated 15 Dec 2025). The {{Terminal-Bench 4.0 board|https://www.tbench.ai/leaderboard}} runs every pair 5 times per task and prints pass@5 beside the mean: GPT-6 Astra (max) in Codex 58.18% mean and 71.2% pass@5, Claude Fable 5.1 (max) in Claude Code 57.88% and 78.8%, so the order flips depending on which you read (chart below).

The harness, the effort and the budget

An agent's score belongs to the pair (model, harness) at a setting. The evidence on how much each part moves the number:

  • Harness, on ordinary tasks: mostly cost. {{Arena's HarnessTax study|https://arena.ai/blog/coding-agents-harness-tax}} (16 Sep 2026) ran 21 pairs, seven models in three harnesses (Claude Code, Codex CLI and Pi, a minimal open-source harness with four tools: read, write, edit, bash), on the same 30 sampled tasks each from SWE-bench Lite and Terminal-Bench 2.0, three attempts per task. The harness moved success by about ±2 points on SWE-bench Lite and about ±5 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, but Claude Code cost about 2.0× Pi and 1.6× Codex on SWE-bench Lite and 1.5× Pi on Terminal-Bench 2.0 (geometric means). Claude Fable 5 solved 97.8% in Claude Code, 96.7% in Codex and 96.7% in Pi, at $1.33 against $0.67 per attempt (Claude Code against Pi). In 9 of 12 comparisons a model's best observed score came from a harness other than its provider's; GPT-5.6 Sol reached 83.3% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 in Pi against 78.9% in Codex, at $0.42 against $0.76. Claude Code's initial context was over 10× Pi's. Thirty tasks and three attempts is a small sample, which the authors' bootstrap intervals reflect.
  • Harness, on some tasks: the score. {{StateM|n:3c65c17b0d0d81899098d8153e100691}} (Aug 2026) wrapped a fixed command-line agent in a checked state-machine runbook and lifted GPT-5.5 (xhigh) from 83.1% to 92.1% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 with the same weights, and reported 95.3% raw with GPT-5.6 Sol; the runbook was developed on the same 89 tasks it is scored on, and the leaderboard closed that submission without merging. On ARC-AGI-3, GPT-6 Astra at the same effort scored 62.7% in one harness and 98.6% in another ({{Knowledge and reasoning|n:3c65c17b0d0d810f8574da3ffb860be5}} owns that case).
  • Effort. On the official Terminal-Bench 4.0 board, GPT-6 Astra in Codex runs from 50.61% at low effort to 58.18% at max, and Claude Opus 5 scores 53.94% at xhigh but 51.82% at max (chart below). {{HAL|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977}} found higher reasoning effort reduced accuracy in the majority of its runs across 9 models and 9 benchmarks.
  • Budget. Time, steps and resources are part of the task. OSWorld-Verified reports scores per step budget (50 or 100 steps; see Computer use); Terminal-Bench 4.0 replaced per-task agent timeouts with a flat 8 hours, one reason it is a new major version.
  • Who ran it. Lab tables run their own harnesses and settings. On Terminal-Bench-Science, Claude Fable 5.1 is 52.6% in Anthropic's own run and 40.0% on the official board; GPT-6 Astra is 64.6% in Anthropic's table and 68.1% on the board ({{Terminal-Bench-Science board|https://www.terminal-bench-science.ai/}}, read 4 Oct 2026).
