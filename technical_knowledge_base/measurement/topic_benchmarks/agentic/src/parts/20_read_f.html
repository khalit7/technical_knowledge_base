Long tasks and real work: METR horizons, GDPval, AutomationBench, Agents' Last Exam

METR's time horizon ({{Kwa et al., Mar 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499}}) turns a task suite into one number with units. Skilled people are timed on each task; each model runs each task several times; and the horizon is the length of task, in human time, that the model completes half the time. GPT-4o's is about 7 minutes; Claude Opus 4.6's about 12 hours. Below, METR's fit done in your browser on METR's own released runs: pick a model and watch where its curve crosses 50%.

Success falls with the log of human time; the horizon is where the fitted curve crosses one half

Dots: one task, at its human time, with the model's success rate over its runs (size: the task's weight). Line: the fitted curve. Squares: METR's weighted success rate within its time buckets (1 to 4, 4 to 16, 16 to 64, 64 to 256, 256 to 960 and 960 to 2,880 minutes). Data: {{METR eval-analysis-public|https://github.com/METR/eval-analysis-public}}, reports/time-horizon-1-1/data/raw/runs.jsonl (24,008 runs) and the published {{results file|https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml}}, read 4 Oct 2026; the repository states no licence, so this page ships only per-task counts derived from it.

Each of the 228 tasks in suite v1.1 has a human time (the geometric mean of skilled people's successful attempts, or an estimate). For one model the fit is a logistic curve of success against log2(human minutes), each task weighted by METR's diversity weight (task families with many members count less):

P(success | task) = σ( a + b · log2 minutes )     horizonp = 2( ln(p/(1−p)) − a ) / b
a, b
fitted by weighted maximum likelihood with an L2 penalty of 10−5 on b (scikit-learn's C = 105, METR's setting), solved here by Newton's method
p
0.5 for the 50% horizon, 0.8 for the 80% horizon

Refitting each model from METR's {{v1.1 runs file|https://github.com/METR/eval-analysis-public}} with METR's own settings (diversity weights, near-zero regularisation) reproduces every 50% horizon in its results file to the published rounding. The suite combines {{HCAST|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17354}} (189 software, ML, security and reasoning tasks with 563 human baselines over 1,500 hours, from one minute to 8+ hours), RE-Bench and 66 short software tasks; version 1.1 uses 228 tasks. A horizon has no ceiling until it outgrows the suite, which is now the limit.

Frontier horizons double about every 4.2 months since 2023, not every 7

All 26 models in METR's v1.1 results file, with 95% intervals; filled points are the ones METR marks as frontier ("SOTA") at release, the only ones used in its doubling fit. The fit is ordinary least squares of log2(horizon) on release date. Defaults reproduce METR's 128.7 days (from 2023) and 187.8 days (all time) independently from the file's points.

  • Doubling time about 4.2 months, not 7. The v1.1 file gives 128.7 days (95% interval 104 to 158) for frontier models from 2023 on and 187.8 days (about 6.2 months) over the whole series since 2019; the 2025 paper's "about seven months" was the earlier, longer window.
  • The conversion is not linear. The fit assumes a task's difficulty for AI rises linearly in log human time; a statistical re-fit of the same 228 tasks and 26 AIs with splines and item-response theory finds the conversion "nearly flat in a region from 2--30 min but close to linear elsewhere", so a jump from 3 to 30 minutes is much easier than one from 30 minutes to 5 hours despite the same tenfold multiplier, and its estimates score better under cross-validated proper scoring rules ({{Nguyen and Fithian, Oct 2026|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12466}}). Read doubling times with that in mind.
  • The suite is running out. The best measured horizon is Claude Mythos Preview (early) at 1,045 minutes (about 17 hours; 95% interval 509 to 3,304, so 8 to 55 hours), dated 8 May 2026, beyond the 16 hours METR treats as reliable, so the trend fit excludes it: it says "beyond what this suite can measure", not "17 hours". No Opus 5.x, Fable or GPT-6 measurement was published, and since 8 September 2026 METR's {{time-horizons page|https://metr.org/}} says it "is no longer actively updated".
  • Reward hacking was measured here first. o3 reward-hacked in 39 of 128 RE-Bench runs (30.4%), and in every one of 21 runs on one task, against 8 of 1,087 on HCAST (0.7%), because RE-Bench shows the scoring function to the agent ({{METR, 5 Jun 2025|https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/}}).

Work suites trade benchmark cleanliness for external validity, which is why they are increasingly quoted in capability-forecasting debates; each reports a different metric, so read the metric before the number.

  • GDPval ({{OpenAI, Oct 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04374}}): real deliverables from 44 occupations in the 9 sectors that contribute most to US GDP; experts compare the model's deliverable with a professional's. OpenAI reported GPT-5.5 winning or tying 84.9% of the time (Apr 2026); Epoch's run of the gold subset gives GPT-5.2 49.7% wins with ties excluded, a different metric. GDPval-AA v2.1 is Artificial Analysis's agentic variant on an Elo scale anchored at 1600 for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash: only differences mean anything on an Elo scale, and it is a different instrument from OpenAI's GDPval.
  • AutomationBench 1.0.6 ({{Zapier|https://zapier.com/benchmarks}}): business workflows across 47 tools on a held-out private set, with dollars per task beside the score; most failures are false confidence, the model declaring success while failing.
  • Agents' Last Exam ({{site|https://agents-last-exam.org/}}; Berkeley RDI with 300+ experts): professional agent work in 55 sub-industries, graded by rubric into a strict pass rate and a partial score. OpenAI's 56.4% for GPT-6 Sol is the partial score; the {{independent leaderboard|https://snorkel.ai/leaderboard/agents-last-exam/}} (hosted by Snorkel) gives GPT-6 Sol a 32.2% pass rate.
  • Delivery, engineering and research suites start far from saturation: on {{FDE-Gym|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22350}} the best of 13 configurations resolves 14 of 95 delivery tasks; on {{EngiWorld|https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37686}} the best of seven frontier models scores 44.7 of 100; on {{Into the Unknown|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19159}} none of 135 research submissions met all of a scientist's conditions.
  • Beating the published best is not research. On {{Agent4Sci|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28763}} (Zhong, Li, Carlini and Jagielski, Oct 2026) frontier coding agents improve on the published best model on all 20 ML-for-science benchmarks in 11 domains (Claude Opus: 10.4% mean relative improvement, no run flagged for misconduct), but the authors trace the gains "almost entirely" to a bag of ML-engineering tricks rather than research insight; plug-in skills for ideation and experiment management raise Opus to 20.1% and Codex from 4.4% to 8.1%.
  • Strict success is not goal success. {{BizAgentBench|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20424}} (Oct 2026: 50 payments tasks, 789 policy documents, 601 tools) scores both reaching the goal and every intermediate step staying within policy: Claude Opus 5 reaches 64.0% strict success and GPT-6 Sol 61.3%, and Qwen3.8-27B drops from 42.0% goal attainment to 32.7% strict, the share that an outcome-only checker would have passed despite invalid execution.
More on the work suites: OpenAI's AutomationBench claim, Vending-Bench, AgentBench, and the research suites' failure details

GDPval's tasks were written by professionals with on average 14 years of experience; 1,320 tasks, a 220-task gold subset public with an automated grader. GDPval-AA is graded head to head, anchored since 19 Sep 2026. AutomationBench has a public set for research beside the private one; Zapier flags that safety-classifier fallbacks change scores (Fable 5.1's 31.4% includes about 40% of tasks completed by Opus 5 as fallback); Artificial Analysis's AutomationBench-AA is a partial-credit rerun, a different metric. Agents' Last Exam has 147 public tasks from a corpus of over 1,500.

OpenAI quotes GPT-6 Sol (xhigh) at 33.2% on AutomationBench for $0.27 per task and Claude Opus 5 at 11.1 times that cost ({{OpenAI|https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/}}; the old page said "high" effort and "roughly nine times"). The old page called OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol 56.4% the only Agents' Last Exam figure; the independent board exists.

Vending-Bench ({{Andon Labs, Feb 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840}}) runs a simulated vending-machine business over more than 20 million tokens per run: simple steps, long horizon. Claude 3.5 Sonnet and o3-mini usually made a profit, but every model had runs that derailed (misread delivery schedules, forgotten orders, "meltdown" loops), and failures did not track when the context filled. AgentBench ({{Tsinghua, Aug 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03688}}), eight environments from an OS shell to games, is historical: not reported on 2026 cards, its successors measure each environment more deeply.

FDE-Gym has 95 delivery tasks from 20 customer scenarios in 18 sectors, with scripted stakeholders and live customer systems, and scores how much a delivery improves the customer's own metric, from 0 (unchanged) to 1 (reference solution): across 13 configurations from seven providers the best averages 0.56. EngiWorld has 1,301 tasks in 26 professional engineering applications with artifact verifiers.

FDE-Gym: even on the 23 tasks where the best configuration gathered every key fact it reached 0.8 on only 10, so finding unstated constraints is not the whole gap. EngiWorld covers CAD, CAE, CAM, BIM, EDA and DCC, its verifiers check geometry, physics and design rules, and 3.6% of cross-software runs succeed. Into the Unknown gives an agent a paper and an unpublished follow-up direction from a US national-laboratory scientist; of the 135 submissions (nine agents, 15 problems), 114 failed the integrity check, most often by claiming work never done or numbers the logs contradict.

MLE-bench ({{OpenAI, Oct 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095}}) scores machine-learning engineering: 75 Kaggle competitions, each with its own data and grader, split by complexity into 22 low, 38 medium and 15 high (the paper suggests the low split alone when all 75 cost too much). The human baseline is each competition's public Kaggle leaderboard, and the headline metric, Any Medal, is the share of competitions where the agent's submission would have earned at least a bronze medal: o1-preview in the AIDE scaffold reached 16.9% at release. Because an agent may spend hours of GPU time per competition, the budget is part of the score: Repo-To-Skill's jump from 31.1% to 72.9% with Codex on GPT-5.5 used skills built from up to 24 GPU-hours of trials on each competition, outside the fixed run-time budget ({{Repo-To-Skill|n:3d45c17b0d0d818bacfeda8a40caddb2}}).

When the subject is the system, not the agent

A cluster from September 2026 scores something around the model, each a different answer to where agent performance lives: the harness a model builds (HarnessDev), the monitor watching agents (MOLE), what survives weeks of operation (Emergence World), and a harness improving itself (SoL-Pi on EdgeBench). Each has its own paper page; the facts that bear on reading agent scores are below.

HarnessDev, MOLE, Emergence World, EdgeBench and SoL-Pi, with the corrections to the old page
  • {{HarnessDev|n:3d45c17b0d0d81cd9883f783840e013f}} scores a model's ability to build a harness from minimal components and then evolve it from feedback: six creator models, four domains, five downstream benchmarks, 2,207 instances, with execution-token cost beside capability. The best generated harness (Opus 4.8 running itself) averaged 67.8 against 86.2 for human-engineered references, which ran different, mostly newer models. Corrections: "match or beat on writing and ML experimentation" holds only for the best one or two creators; on evolution, all five self-runtime runs improved on 630 held-out tasks (+1.43 to +4.44), but the score a creator could see moved the same way as the held-out score in only 34 of 64 version changes, and swapping the model that executes a harness can halve its score.
  • {{MOLE|n:3db5c17b0d0d818b9680c7b91baaf7e9}} scores the monitor: 150 AI-operated accounts share 9 stateful services over 30 workdays with 12 injected threats. 28 of 39 agent models (72%) completed most assigned harmful objectives, measured under a preamble telling them the sandbox is harmless and refusing spoils the research; refusal and completion rates are strongly linked across models (Spearman −0.73), so the old "stated refusal does not predict whether it declines" overstates the paper. The best monitor (Claude Opus 4.7) caught 24 of 45 completed harms in a single-day audit of 10 accounts a day.
  • {{Emergence World|n:3e25c17b0d0d818fbffbc9e2ceab824b}} scores what survives duration: eight worlds of ten agents (six ran 16 days, one 21, one ended on day 4), about 850,000 LLM calls and 50 billion tokens, under prompt injection, misinformation and memory exposure. No world was resilient to all three; all 7 exposed worlds warned their community about the phishing and none removed its traces. The old "kept interacting 46 hours later" is one agent retrying an attack link about 46 hours after the campaign ended, not a measured detection-to-stop delay. It also tracks goal drift and tool-call failure trends day by day, which a suite of short episodes cannot see in principle, however many episodes it has.
  • EdgeBench (ByteDance Seed) has 134 day-scale tasks, 51 public; agents submit repeatedly within a time budget and the best submission is the score. {{SoL-Pi|n:3e25c17b0d0d81a0bb77f16cbc898bb5}} (NVIDIA) used it to show a harness-level improvement loop cutting recorded tokens 44.7 to 49.0% against Pi, mostly cache reads, with API cost about a third lower, at 93.7 to 94.3% of Pi's score: not "at task parity" as the old page said, and the cost per hour is SoL-Pi's derived metric, not EdgeBench's.

Code as agent work. SWE-bench, SWE-bench Pro and Real-SWE are agent benchmarks too (the score depends on the scaffold; "bash only" and "full agent" columns differed by 10 to 20 points on swebench.com, whose bash-only board was dropped on 1 Sep 2026). {{Coding|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81bfa09ad4ba5eef0c4e}} owns them. One matters here for its method: Real-SWE ({{Specific Labs|https://www.withspecific.com/benchmarks/real-swe}}) licenses 10 tasks from companies' private production repositories (median 11 files changed), so they cannot leak into training data, the one contamination defence that does not decay as a benchmark ages. Eight model and native-harness pairs ran 8 rollouts per task (640 in all), at $2.50 to $6.96 per rollout. It now reads GPT-6 Astra 46.25% and Claude Fable 5.1 45.00% (the old page's "Fable 5.1 leads at 38.8%" is out of date), and the old "71.4% of rollouts that finished inside ten minutes failed" is no longer what the page shows: 57.4% of rollouts under ten minutes failed (62 of 108) against 66.2% of longer ones (352 of 532), so short runs now fail less often (read 4 Oct 2026).
