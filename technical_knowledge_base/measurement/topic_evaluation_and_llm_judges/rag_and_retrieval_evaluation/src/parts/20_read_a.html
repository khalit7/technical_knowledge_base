The idea in one sentenceA RAG answer can fail at finding the evidence or at using it, so grade the two halves apart: the retrieved passages against labels of which passages hold the answer, the answer against the passages it was actually given, and only then the answer against the truth; every RAG metric compares two of five things, and which two decides what it can see.

You need: the ranked-list metrics themselves, recall@k, MRR and nDCG with its two gains ({{Evaluation metrics|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a29acef722e842a29b}}, section 6, owns the definitions and a one-list calculator); what an LLM judge is and how it is calibrated ({{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}}); how a RAG system is built ({{Topic: rag-and-retrieval|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}}). Everything here is worked on released human labels: BEIR qrels, 1,584 RAGTruth answers with annotated spans, and MS MARCO's marks of which passage holds the answer.

qrels: the relevance labels of a test set, query to passage to grade.
hit@k: 1 if any labelled passage is in the top k; recall@k is the share of the labelled passages that are.
Faithfulness (also groundedness): the share of the answer's claims that the retrieved passages support.
Claim decomposition: splitting an answer into atomic claims before checking each; itself an LLM step.
NLI: natural-language inference, a classifier saying whether a premise entails, contradicts or is neutral to a hypothesis.
Context precision / recall: judge-graded stand-ins for retrieval metrics: are relevant passages ranked first; do the passages cover the reference answer.
Nugget: one atomic fact a good answer must contain (TREC RAG's unit of correctness).
Citation recall / precision: do a sentence's citations entail it; is each citation needed.

Each metric is one comparison; faithfulness never looks at the truth, correctness never looks at the passages

Upper arcs grade the passages (the retrieval half), lower arcs grade the answer (the generation half). Recall@k needs passage labels (qrels); context precision and context recall replace them with a reference answer and a judge; faithfulness and citation accuracy compare the answer with the passages only; correctness and nugget recall compare it with a reference only. No single arc says which half failed; the pair of halves does.

Split the halves: one score hides which one failed

End-to-end accuracy is a product of stage shares, A = rK · pk|K · g (found in the first stage's top K, kept in the k that reach the prompt, answered correctly), so the same accuracy can come from a weak retriever or a weak generator; {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}} measures that split on its running example (section 9). Here it is done on single answers, with human labels for each half.

The one habit
For every wrong answer, first ask whether a passage that holds the answer was in the prompt. That one bit gives the failure to the retriever (not in the prompt) or to the generator (in the prompt, and still wrong) before anybody touches a component.

Here it is on one claim, with a label for each half: SciFact's qrels say which abstract holds the evidence, SciFact's CONTRADICT label says what the right answer is. Fig. 1 runs it twice, first with BM25, then with retrieval fixed:

  1. A real claim, a real retriever. SciFact test claim 54, "AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) activation increases inflammation-related fibrosis in the lungs", goes to BM25 over the 5,183 SciFact abstracts (our run, k1 = 0.9, b = 0.4), which returns a ranked list.
  2. The top 3 reach the generator. Three abstracts about AMPK, none about the lung, go into the prompt of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct with RAGTruth's instruction to say "Unable to answer" if the passages do not settle it. It answers "Supported".
  3. Score end to end: wrong. SciFact's annotators label the claim CONTRADICT, from a paper showing that metformin, an AMPK activator, reverses lung fibrosis in mice. One more miss in the accuracy number; which half to fix is not in it.
  4. Score the retrieval half: failed. SciFact's qrels name one relevant abstract, "Metformin reverses established lung fibrosis in a bleomycin model"; BM25 ranked it at 5, below the cut. So hit@3 = 0, precision@3 = 0, RR@3 = 0: the evidence never reached the prompt.
  5. Score the generation half: failed too. The answer quotes passage 1 on JAK1 and 14-3-3 proteins in endothelial cells and fibroblasts and says it shows AMPK "leads to increased inflammation-related fibrosis"; none of the three passages mentions lung fibrosis, so the answer is unfaithful, and the instructed answer was "Unable to answer". Fix retrieval first: a better generator could at best refuse; it cannot find what it was not given.
  6. Fix the half that failed. Same claim, same generator, retrieval swapped for BM25 plus the MiniLM dense run fused by RRF (our hybrid). The labelled abstract rises to rank 2: hit@3 = 1, precision@3 = 1/3, RR = 1/2. Retrieval now passes.
  7. The bug moved. The answer still says "Supported", so a single accuracy number would say the retrieval change did nothing. Split, it did: the answer even cites passage 2 ("metformin, an AMPK activator, reverses established lung fibrosis"), then calls that support for a claim that AMPK activation increases fibrosis, an Evident Conflict in RAGTruth's terms. The next fix is the generator: a stronger model, or an instruction to check the direction of the effect.

Six generators, the same three passages

The same split on a question six generators answered from the same three passages. Before scores each answer end to end; After also scores the retrieval half against a human mark of which passage holds the answer, and the generation half against human-marked hallucinated spans. Predict first: for the "carry on cast" question, how many of the six generators fail because of retrieval?

  1. A real question. MS MARCO's "carry on cast still alive", one of RAGTruth's 989 question-answering items. The instruction: answer strictly from the three passages, or reply "Unable to answer based on given passages."
  2. Retrieve. The three passages RAGTruth kept from MS MARCO's ten Bing results. Nobody has looked at labels yet.
  3. Generate. The chosen generator writes its answer from the three passages (temperature 0.7, as released). Switch the generator to see the other five.
  4. Score end to end. Against MS MARCO's reference answer, which names six actors, the answer is correct or wrong: one bit. Our reading of the chosen answer is under the card.
  5. Score the retrieval half. MS MARCO's annotator marked passage 2 as the one the answer was written from (is_selected). So hit@3 = 1, precision@3 = 1/3, RR = 1/2: the evidence reached the prompt, whatever the answer says. This half is identical for all six generators.
  6. Score the generation half. RAGTruth's annotated spans light up in the answer; the reading under the card says what each span is, or what an answer without spans still gets wrong.
  7. Which half failed. For the three wrong answers, the generator: retrieval delivered the answer-bearing passage, and the answer contradicted it, ignored it or left half of it out. The fix is the prompt, the model or the instruction, not the retriever. For the three correct ones, neither half, against the passages and the reference.
  8. And the world. Both halves can pass and the answer still be wrong today: passage 2 dates from about 2014, and four of the six (June Whitfield, Barbara Windsor, Leslie Phillips, Julian Holloway) have died since, per Wikipedia on 4 October 2026. Faithfulness checks the answer against the passages, never the passages against the world; corpus freshness is a third thing to test.

Triage a wrong answer, cheapest check first

Each "no" names what to fix: a corpus gap is not a retriever bug; outside the first stage's top K is a retrieval miss (chunking, hybrid search, query rewriting); found at rank 40 when the prompt takes 10 is a ranking miss, visible only if recall is measured at the reranker's depth too; a wrong answer from the right passage is the generator's (prompt, model, context format, distraction by irrelevant passages); a refusal when the context sufficed, or an answer when it did not, is an abstention miss (instructions); both halves passing on a stale source is a corpus miss.
