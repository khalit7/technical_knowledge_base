A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system answers in two steps: a retriever puts a few passages in the prompt, and a generator writes an answer from them. Each step fails in its own way, and a single end-to-end score ("62% of answers were correct") cannot say which one did, so it cannot say what to fix. Evaluation therefore splits the system: classical retrieval metrics over labelled passages for the first step, faithfulness and relevance checks for the second, and an end-to-end check that ties them together. This page works every metric on released human labels: a BM25, dense and hybrid run on three BEIR test sets scored against their qrels, 1,584 RAGTruth responses whose hallucinated spans were marked by annotators, and MS MARCO's own marks of which passage holds the answer. About 30 minutes; the two tabs are optional.

The one habit
For every wrong answer, first ask whether a passage that holds the answer was in the prompt. That one bit gives the failure to the retriever (not in the prompt) or to the generator (in the prompt, and still wrong) before anybody touches a component.

Two halves, and why one score hides which failed

With one gold passage per question, end-to-end accuracy is a product: the share of questions whose gold passage the first stage returns among the K candidates, times the share the reranker keeps in the k that reach the prompt, times the share the generator then answers correctly, A = rK · pk|K · g. Any factor caps the whole, and the same 67.5% can come from a weak retriever or from a weak generator. {{Topic: rag-and-retrieval|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}} works the product through a 200-question example in its "where an answer gets lost" section; here is the same split done on real items.

The animation runs two real questions through retrieve, then generate. Before scores each answer end to end, the way a single accuracy number does. After scores the same answer twice more: the retrieval half against a human mark of which passage holds the answer, and the generation half against human-marked hallucinated spans. The first question comes from RAGTruth, whose three passages per question were taken from MS MARCO; the second from SciFact, retrieved by our own BM25 and hybrid runs.

Labels: which passage holds the answer is MS MARCO v1.1's is_selected flag (the MS MARCO annotator wrote the reference answer from it) and SciFact's qrels; hallucinated spans are RAGTruth's annotations; end-to-end correctness is our reading of each answer against MS MARCO's reference answer and SciFact's CONTRADICT label, shown in the caption so it can be checked. The SciFact answers were generated for this page by Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, greedy, with RAGTruth's QA instruction (src/gen_case.py).

The first question is not a lucky pick. RAGTruth built its question-answering set so that the retriever had already done its job: of its 989 MS MARCO questions, 276 can be matched by text to MS MARCO v1.1, and in all 264 whose three passages could be matched one by one, at least one passage carries MS MARCO's answer mark (exactly one passage in 200 questions, two in 51, all three in 13).
