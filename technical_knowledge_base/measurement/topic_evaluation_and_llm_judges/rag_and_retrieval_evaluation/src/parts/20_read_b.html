The retrieval half: what changes when the reader is a generator

The metrics are the ranked-list metrics of information retrieval (precision@k, recall@k, MRR, MAP and nDCG with its two gain definitions, worked on one list in section 6 of {{Evaluation metrics|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a29acef722e842a29b}}), computed per query and averaged; TREC's trec_eval and its Python binding pytrec_eval are the reference implementations, and BEIR and MTEB use them. What RAG changes is where to read them. We ran five retrievers over three BEIR test sets (Thakur et al., NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks): SciFact (5,183 abstracts, 300 scientific claims), NFCorpus (3,633 medical documents, 323 queries, graded labels 1 and 2) and FiQA-2018 (57,638 financial forum posts, 648 questions).

Fig. 2 reads the metrics on one query's real ranked lists, from our runs on NFCorpus (graded labels, many per query):

  1. One query, the top 100 as a field. NFCorpus query "grapes" has 196 labelled passages. BM25's top 100 is folded into rows of ten, rank 1 at the front left; the 15 labelled passages among them stand up.
  2. The prompt's cut, k = 10. 9 of the first ten are labelled: precision@10 = 0.9 and hit@10 = 1, yet recall@10 = 9/196 = 0.046. With 196 labels, recall@10 cannot exceed 10/196 (0.051): when a query has many labels, hit rate and recall tell different stories.
  3. nDCG@10 discounts each hit by its rank. A hit's gain is divided by log2(rank + 1): rank 1 keeps all of it, rank 10 keeps 0.29. Summed, DCG@10 = 4.113; the ideal list (ten labelled passages on top) scores 4.544, so nDCG@10 = 0.905.
  4. The reranker's depth, k = 100. All ten rows: 15 of 196, recall@100 = 0.077. The 6 labelled passages below rank 10 are what a reranker reading this list could still lift into the prompt; nothing else can get there.
  5. The dense run on the same query. MiniLM's top 100: only 2 labelled passages in its first ten, but 22 in the hundred (recall@100 0.112; nDCG@10 0.130).
  6. Read both depths. At the prompt BM25 wins, 9 against 2; at the reranker's depth the dense run wins, 22 against 15 (the RRF hybrid keeps 9 and reaches 24). Recall read at one depth hides either a ranking miss or a retrieval miss.

Predict: on FiQA, where forum questions paraphrase the posts, how much recall@100 does a 22M-parameter dense model add over BM25: about 2 points, 10, or 17? Pick FiQA below.
  • Measure recall twice, at the prompt's k and at the reranker's depth (often 50 to 150), or a retrieval miss and a ranking miss look the same.
  • Recall@k has no value for an unanswerable question (no relevant passage to divide by): leave those out of the average and score abstention on them instead.
  • hit@k (success@k, and "recall@k" in some papers) matches recall@k only when each query has one relevant passage; the naming is defined on {{Evaluation metrics|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a29acef722e842a29b}}, section 6, so check which one a paper means before comparing.
  • precision@k is capped by the labels: with one relevant passage per query, precision@10 cannot exceed 0.1; raising k can only raise recall and usually lowers precision.
  • nDCG@10 is the standard for comparing rerankers and BEIR's and MTEB's headline number; MRR fits single-answer lookups. Say the gain: linear (gain = grade) in trec_eval, BEIR and MTEB, exponential (2grade − 1) in some learning-to-rank libraries; they differ on graded labels.
  • ANN recall is not retrieval recall. ANN recall measures an approximate index against exact search over the same embeddings; retrieval recall measures the system against human labels. A perfect index cannot fix an embedding model that puts the right passage far from the query ({{Vector indexes|n:3f85c17b0d0d81f29707c130b2ebcfb1}} owns ANN recall).

Recall keeps rising past the prompt's k: read it at the prompt and at the reranker's depth

Recall@k for k = 1 to 100 against the released qrels, computed in your browser from the stored ranks of every labelled passage; measured on our runs (src/beir_run.py, src/paired.py). FiQA: BM25 recall@100 0.538, dense 0.706, hybrid 0.711, so the dense model adds about 17 points. On SciFact a reranker reading BM25's top 100 has 12.5 more points of recall to work with (0.800 to 0.925) than the prompt ever sees, and none beyond 0.925.

The five systems, exactly

The systems: BM25 reimplemented the way Anserini's "flat" regression runs it (title and text as one field, Lucene's English stopwords, Porter stemming, k1 = 0.9, b = 0.4), the same BM25 with k1 = 1.2 and b = 0.75, the rank_bm25 package as installed, a small dense retriever (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 22M parameters, cosine), and a hybrid fusing BM25 and dense with reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60). All runs took under 15 minutes on a laptop CPU, two threads. Code: src/beir_run.py.

Defaults reproduce published numbers, independently
The dense run matches MTEB's published all-MiniLM-L6-v2 results to the fourth decimal on all three sets; our BM25 lands within 0.004 of Anserini's flat regressions (a Python reimplementation, not Lucene's exact analyzer). The BEIR paper's own BM25 (0.665, 0.325, 0.236) indexed title and text as separate fields, a slightly different system.
  • The winner depends on the corpus. BM25 edges the dense model on SciFact (nDCG@10 0.676 against 0.645), where claims reuse the abstracts' terms, and loses badly on FiQA (0.239 against 0.369), where questions paraphrase. Test on your corpus (and the SciFact gap is within noise, as Comparing two systems shows).
  • Fusion wins everywhere here. The RRF hybrid is best on all three sets (0.719, 0.356, 0.373) and lifts SciFact recall@100 from 0.925 to 0.962: the two retrievers miss different passages.
  • "BM25" is not one system. rank_bm25 as installed (no stemming, no stopwords, k1 = 1.5, b = 0.75) scores 0.652 on SciFact and 0.217 on FiQA, against 0.676 and 0.239 for the Lucene-style run; moving k1 and b alone takes FiQA from 0.239 to 0.253. Quote the implementation and parameters with the score.
  • Recall and hit rate tell different stories when labels are many. NFCorpus has 38 labelled passages per query on average, so BM25's recall@10 is 0.152 while 68% of queries have at least one relevant passage in the top 10 (hit@10 0.684); precision@10 can never approach 1 on SciFact (1.1 labels per query).
