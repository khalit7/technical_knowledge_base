Same names, different instruments, and how well they track people

Every framework has a "faithfulness" and an "answer relevance", and they do not mean the same thing: name the instrument, its version and its judge model beside every score, as you would the harness beside a benchmark score ({{Eval harnesses|n:3c65c17b0d0d81ea9cd5eb65795ff5c9}}). Three disagreements change numbers on the same item: unsupported but uncontradicted claims (RAGAS fails them, DeepEval's default passes them: the Llama-2-13B case in the stepper above, 0 against 1; RAGTruth's annotators side with RAGAS); abstentions (TruLens counts a refusal as grounded, RAGAS has no claims to check, and an end-to-end check counts it wrong when the answer was in the passages: in RAGTruth's QA set, 36 bare refusals were made with an answer-bearing passage in context); and answer relevance run in opposite directions.

No head-to-head of the frameworks. No study we found runs RAGAS, DeepEval and TruLens on the same items and reports their agreement (unconfirmed gap as of October 2026); the nearest is a July 2026 preprint on 96 business questions (arXiv 2607.07302) finding claim recall tracking humans at about 0.7 and the faithfulness metrics only weakly. Measure your own: a few hundred hand-labelled items, the metric's agreement with them, and its error rates to correct the pass rate ({{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}}).

Agreement with people, in more detail: WikiEval, ARES, TREC 2024
  • On easy ground they look good. RAGAS reported agreement with human annotators on its 50-question WikiEval set of 0.95 for faithfulness, 0.78 for answer relevance and 0.70 for context relevance, against 0.72, 0.52 and 0.63 for a direct GPT score (Table 1).
  • Fine-tuned judges rank systems better. ARES reported Kendall's τ with true system rankings 0.065 higher than RAGAS for context relevance and 0.132 higher for answer relevance on KILT and SuperGLUE tasks.
  • Automatic evaluation ranks systems well and individual answers poorly. For TREC 2024, run-level τ between fully automatic and manual nugget scoring was 0.783 on the 21 topics first judged (Pradeep et al., Nov 2024); on support, GPT-4o and assessors agreed exactly on 56% of judgments made from scratch, 72% after post-editing (arXiv 2504.15205).
