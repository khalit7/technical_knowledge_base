Faithfulness: split into claims, verify each, apply a rule

RAGAS's own example: the passage says Einstein was born on 14 March 1879 in Germany; the answer says "Einstein was born in Germany on 20th March 1879". The judge splits it into "born in Germany" (supported) and "born on 20th March 1879" (not supported), so faithfulness = 1/2 = 0.5 ({{RAGAS docs|https://docs.ragas.io/en/stable/concepts/metrics/available_metrics/faithfulness/}}). Every faithfulness metric runs these steps; they differ in the checker and in the rule that turns verdicts into a score. faithfulness = claims in the answer supported by the passages / claims in the answer

Three kinds of checker

  • An LLM judge per claim (RAGAS, DeepEval, TruLens): flexible, but the split and the verdict are both judge calls, so the judge's biases enter twice.
  • An NLI or fact-checking model: the claim is the hypothesis and the passages the premise. ALCE used TRUE, a T5-11B fine-tuned for entailment; RAGAS's FaithfulnesswithHHEM uses Vectara's HHEM-2.1-Open, a flan-t5-base classifier; MiniCheck-FT5 (770M) scored 74.7% balanced accuracy on LLM-AggreFact against GPT-4's 75.3%, at about $0.24 against $107 for the benchmark (Tang et al., EMNLP 2024). Cheap and deterministic.
  • A trained span detector: marks where in the answer the unsupported text is, not only whether. RAGTruth's fine-tuned Llama-2-13B is the reference point (below).

Three things faithfulness is not

  • Not correctness. GPT-4's answer to the same question is perfectly faithful and four of its six "still alive" are now wrong, because the passage dates from about 2014 (June Whitfield died in 2018, Barbara Windsor in 2020, Leslie Phillips in 2022 and Julian Holloway in 2025, per Wikipedia as read on 4 October 2026). Freshness and source checks are a separate test.
  • Not completeness. Llama-2-7B's answer names three of the six and has no marked span, so faithfulness passes it; only a correctness or context-recall check catches the gap.
  • Not the same as "true". RAGTruth's annotators flagged whether each hallucinated span was nevertheless true in the world (implicit_true): 846 of the 2,927 question-answering spans were (28.9%; the model filled the gap from memory, correctly). A faithfulness metric fails them: right for a system that must answer from its sources, wrong if you only care about accuracy.

How a faithfulness judge fails

A faithfulness metric is an LLM judge with a fixed prompt, so it inherits position, verbosity and self-preference biases and drift across versions; {{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}} owns the catalogue and the calibration method. What is specific to faithfulness:

  • Decomposition decides the score. Claim-level factuality scores are "sensitive to the decomposition method used" (Wanner et al., 2024): a split that drops a qualifier turns a faithful sentence unfaithful and charges the generator for the judge's error.
  • Leniency is a setting. DeepEval's default passes unsupported claims and TruLens counts abstentions as grounded, by design. Read the prompt before reading the number.
  • Prompted judges over-flag. On RAGTruth's QA responses, prompted GPT-4-turbo found 90.6% of hallucinated answers but only 33.2% of the answers it flagged were hallucinated (F1 45.6; span F1 32.6). A 13B model fine-tuned on the labels reached 68.2 and 58.2.
  • Judges grade their own family up. FACTS Grounding uses three judges from three labs because, measured, each rated its own family's responses 3.23% higher on average (Jacovi et al., Jan 2025).
  • Long contexts. With 20 passages the judge's prompt is long, and judges degrade with length as generators do. We found no clean measurement of faithfulness-judge accuracy against context length (unconfirmed); measure it on your own lengths before trusting a single judge there.
  • Unpinned judges. Upgrading the judge model moves every generation metric; treat it as a new instrument and recalibrate on the gold slice.

Choosing the checker

Hallucination-detection benchmarks score the checkers themselves. On easy, synthetic perturbations (HaluBench) detectors look nearly solved; on natural errors of strong models (FaithBench) they were at chance until judges got examples. Your traffic is the second kind, so choose the checker on a benchmark of natural errors before trusting it. The practical answer in 2026 is a small trained detector for the bulk (cheap, deterministic, span-level) and a calibrated frontier judge, or a person, for the disputed slice.
