Pipeline lab

Build a guarded pipeline over the 450 released XSTest conversations and see, conversation by conversation, what each stage catches and what it wrongly blocks. Prompts and human labels are from the {{XSTest repository|https://github.com/paul-rottger/xstest}} (Röttger et al., NAACL 2024); answers are Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1's, plain and with Mistral's guardrail system prompt, as released; the guard verdicts and scores are this page's runs of Qwen3Guard-Gen-0.6B (greedy decoding, model card template) and Protect AI's DeBERTa injection classifier (scripts in src/runs/). The Reading tab's {{animation|#t-read}} walks four of these configurations.

2. The trade-off curve: every threshold at once

What this shows: for the rail chosen below, each point is one threshold on the guard's score (the probability that its first label token is not "Safe"). Across: share of the 250 safe prompts blocked. Up: harmful answers still delivered, of the 128 full compliances with unsafe prompts. The two marked points are the guard's own loose and strict labels; the ring is your threshold from section 1.

3. Choose the input-rail threshold with conformal risk control

What this does: splits the 250 safe prompts at random into 125 for calibration and 125 for testing, then picks the lowest threshold whose calibration over-block rate, inflated by the finite-sample correction, stays at or under your target. The guarantee is that the expected over-block rate on new traffic from the same distribution is at most the target. Try: press "New split" a few times and watch the test rate scatter around (and on average below) the target.
