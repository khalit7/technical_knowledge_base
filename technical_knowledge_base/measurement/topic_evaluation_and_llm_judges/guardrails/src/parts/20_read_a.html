The idea in one screen

A guardrail is a classifier with a threshold, put in the request path where a risk first becomes visible and allowed to act (block, rewrite, redact, regenerate, ask a human). Every one trades harm let through against legitimate work blocked and pays in latency; an attacker needs only one path through the stack, so in an agent the rails that hold are the ones that remove a capability rather than score text.

Three stages of rails around one call, and the boundary that does not depend on any of them

Left to right, blocking gets more expensive: an input rail stops a request before anything is generated; an output rail costs a second model pass; in an agent, a rail that reads the final answer arrives after the tool calls already happened. Rail
one check in the request path: a scoring function, a threshold, an action when the score crosses it.
Operating point
the (miss rate, over-block rate) pair one threshold fixes.
Over-block rate
share of benign traffic a rail blocks; users feel it as refusals.
Guard model
a small language model fine-tuned to label a prompt, a response or both against a hazard taxonomy or a written policy.
Action rail
a check on a proposed tool call (tool, arguments, provenance) before it executes.
Streaming rail
a check on a partial answer that can cut it off mid-stream.
Cascade
a cheap high-recall screen on all traffic that escalates only flagged items to an expensive classifier.
Conformal risk control
choosing a threshold on held-out data so an expected loss stays under a target, with a finite-sample guarantee.

1. A rail is a classifier with a threshold

Strip away the framework and every rail is the same object: a score over some text or action, a threshold, and an action when the score crosses it. So everything known about measuring binary classifiers applies, with two error rates that pull against each other:

miss rate = FN / (TP + FN)     over-block rate = FP / (FP + TN)
FN
harmful items the rail let through (a leak)
FP
benign items the rail blocked (an over-block, which users feel as a refusal)
threshold
moving it up lowers FP and raises FN; there is no setting that lowers both

Every threshold is one point: the same guard can be lenient or strict

Predict, then drag: Qwen3Guard-Gen-0.6B as an input rail on XSTest's 450 prompts (this page's run). How low must the threshold go before no harmful answer gets through, and what share of the 250 safe prompts does that block? Then switch to the output rail, which sees the answer too.

Each point is one threshold on the guard's score, the probability that its first label token is not "Safe". Across: share of the 250 safe prompts blocked. Up: harmful answers still delivered, of the 128 full compliances with unsafe prompts by Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.1 (XSTest's human labels). The marked points are the guard's own loose (block Unsafe) and strict (also block Controversial) labels; the ring is your threshold.

Two consequences decide how rails are designed. First, base rates: attacks are rare in real traffic, so even a small over-block rate produces more false blocks than true ones. Second, severity sets the stage: a risk that is cheap to detect and expensive to let through (a known jailbreak string, a credit card number) is caught early, where blocking costs nothing; a risk that only exists once the answer is written (an ungrounded medical claim) can only be caught late, where catching it costs a second model pass. The old version of this page put it as "severity determines where you pay the cost".

At a realistic attack share, most blocks are mistakes

The rule this page keeps returning to

A guardrail number means nothing without its operating point. Every guard has a threshold; every threshold fixes one pair (share of harm missed, share of benign traffic blocked). Report both, at your traffic's base rate, on your own policy, through the whole pipeline.

Error bars on a measured over-block rate, and how many labelled items you need to tell 2% from 4%, are on {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}; labelling your own benign and attack sets is {{Human evaluation and annotation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d819485bcc54a45d49420}}.
