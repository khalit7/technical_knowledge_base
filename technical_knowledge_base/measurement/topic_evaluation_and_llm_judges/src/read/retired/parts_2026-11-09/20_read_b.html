3. Calibration: checking the grader against people

A grader's number means nothing until it has been compared with human labels on your task. Which judge to use is therefore an empirical, per-task decision made on a human-labelled gold slice, never a default. Three definitions carry the rest of the topic:

  • The ceiling is human agreement, not 100%. People disagree on open-ended answers. On MT-Bench two experts agreed on 81% of non-tie votes and 63% with ties counted, and GPT-4 agreed with the experts 85% and 66% ({{Zheng et al. 2023, Table 5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685}}). So a judge that agrees 80% with your expert may already be at the ceiling, and one claiming 95% on open-ended work should make you suspect the labels. The ceiling moves with the test: LLMBar's curated pairs reach 94% human agreement, while DevAI's three experts disagreed on 10 to 30% of requirements (both in the {{Judge atlas|#t-judges}}).
  • Report kappa, not raw agreement. κ = (po − pe) / (1 − pe), with po the observed agreement and pe the agreement expected from the two pass rates alone. A judge that always says pass, on a slice where the expert passes 90%, agrees 90% of the time and has kappa 0. The worked matrix on real MT-Bench votes is on {{LLM-as-judge, Calibration|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b#rd-cal}}; agreement between people (kappa, alpha, AC1) on {{Human evaluation and annotation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d819485bcc54a45d49420#rd-agr}}.
  • Reliable is not valid. A judge that gives the same verdict every time is repeatable, not right: a 2026 study of 21 judges found test-retest reliability above 0.95 alongside position bias above 0.10 in two production-deployed judges ({{Reliability without Validity, arXiv:2606.19544|https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544}}). Only chance-corrected agreement with people measures validity.

The same gold slice then corrects the headline number: a judge with sensitivity TPR and false-positive rate FPR reports a biased pass rate, and the plug-in correction is true rate ≈ (judge rate − FPR) / (TPR − FPR), with an interval that carries the uncertainty of both the test set and the slice ({{How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations, arXiv:2511.21140|https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21140}}). Stepped on real data, with a slice-size lab: {{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b#rd-cal}}. Meta-evaluations (JudgeBench, RewardBench 2, LLMBar) shortlist judges before your own calibration; every judge and test of judges, dated and sourced, is in this page's {{Judge atlas|#t-judges}}.

The old page put inter-annotator agreement on open-ended tasks at "75 to 85%"; the sourced figures depend on whether ties count (81% and 63% on MT-Bench, above). It also said JudgeBench (hard pairs whose right answer is objectively checkable; random guessing scores 50%) put "even frontier judges at 60 to 70%": that was GPT-4o-era (56.6% with the Arena-Hard judge prompt); on the paper's own v2 table (April 2025) o1-preview scores 75.4%, DeepSeek-R1 73.1% and o3-mini (high) 80.9%.
