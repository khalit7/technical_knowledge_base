Choosing

Every harness runs a dataset through a model and scores it. They differ in the unit of work, and the dividing question is which of two things you are asking:

"Is this checkpoint good?"
A model-level question: fixed public tasks, comparable across labs and over time. Use a model-level harness and report its version and settings with the number.
"Is this configuration safe to ship?"
An application question: your prompt, retrieval, tools and model together, on your traffic. Use golden sets, regression gates and shadow traffic. A model swap is this kind of question, even though the trigger is a model.
SituationTool, and what it treats as the unit of work
Standard benchmark numbers for a checkpoint{{lm-evaluation-harness|https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness}}: a declarative task in YAML; the reproducibility standard behind most model-card numbers
Agentic, tool-use or safety evals, with full transcripts{{Inspect|https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/}} (UK AI Security Institute): an eval is a program (dataset, solver, scorer) with a sandbox; used by AI safety institutes, METR and Apollo Research
Evals inside a Hugging Face training loop{{lighteval|https://github.com/huggingface/lighteval}}: tasks run on checkpoints during pretraining
Multi-metric methodology (accuracy, calibration, robustness, fairness, bias, toxicity, efficiency){{HELM|https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm}}: read it, do not build on it: it entered maintenance mode on 1 June 2026 and its {{Maintenance Mode Policy|https://crfm-helm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/maintenance_mode/}} adds no new evaluations to the leaderboards, pointing to Inspect Evals, lighteval, lm-eval-harness, Evalchemy and Unitxt instead
Prompt and configuration regression tests in CIAn application config: promptfoo (open source) or Braintrust (platform), which add online scoring of live traces

Single verdicts or aggregates?

The second choice is what the grader's output will be used for. A grader that ranks models well can still be unreliable on any one response, and the cheapest graders are built that way. RocketEval's small checklist judge shows the shape ({{RocketEval paper page|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81ffa8e5f8fa5a5806c8}}): ranking 12 models on WildBench, Gemma-2-2B reaches a Spearman correlation of 0.965 with Chatbot Arena against GPT-4o's 0.979 (one more of the 66 pairs out of order), at $27.7 against $3,400 for 1,000 runs at batch prices; on single MT-Bench verdicts it agrees with humans 57.9% of the time, against 66.6% for GPT-4o and 64.7% for humans with each other.

  • Aggregates (leaderboards, drift monitoring, a gate that trips on a distribution): a cheap grader is fine, and cheap enough to run on every merge rather than once a quarter.
  • Single items (adjudicating one case, failing one pull request on one example, generating preference labels for training): use a calibrated strong judge or a person.
  • Checklist grading still needs a frontier model to read every query once to write its checklist (never the responses), which weakens the privacy case for local judging.

Before trusting a number

  1. Which model, harness version, prompt and judge (all pinned) produced it?
  2. Has the grader been checked against people on this task, with kappa, and is it below the human ceiling or suspiciously above it?
  3. Is the change larger than the minimum detectable effect of this set, on a paired comparison?
  4. Have the failing items been read: truncated, mis-extracted, mislabelled, or genuinely wrong?
  5. For agents: was the action graded, or only the final message?
