6. Choosing: which instrument for which question

Every harness runs a dataset through a model and scores it; the dividing question is which of two things you are asking.

"Is this checkpoint good?"
A model-level question: fixed public tasks, comparable across labs and over time. Use a model-level harness and report its version and settings with the number.
"Is this configuration safe to ship?"
An application question: your prompt, retrieval, tools and model together, on your traffic. Use golden sets, regression gates and shadow traffic. A model swap is this kind of question, even though the trigger is a model.
The questionInstrumentPage
What does this checkpoint score on public tasks?a model-level harness (lm-evaluation-harness, Inspect, lighteval), every setting pinned and reported{{Eval harnesses, section 7|n:3c65c17b0d0d81ea9cd5eb65795ff5c9#rd-choose}}
Did my change break my product?a golden set from real traffic, a CI gate with a stated MDE, then shadow and canary{{Production eval engineering|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b39ad9e96193d21adc}}
Is B better than A on open-ended answers?a pairwise judge in both orders, calibrated on a gold slice{{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}}
Is the difference real?a paired test with an interval{{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}
Are my labels right?agreement beyond chance, then an audit of the gold set{{Human evaluation and annotation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d819485bcc54a45d49420}}
Did the agent do the task, every time?state checks against a do-nothing baseline, pass^k over repeated trials{{Agent and trajectory evaluation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81fdbabaf3582bff37f2}}
Is the answer grounded in what was retrieved?retrieval metrics and faithfulness, scored separately{{RAG and retrieval evaluation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8194a914d1843ee26f13}}

Single verdicts or aggregates? The second choice is what the grader's output will be used for. A grader that ranks models well can still be unreliable on any one response, and the cheapest graders are built that way: RocketEval's small checklist judge ranks a field of models almost as well as GPT-4o for under 1% of its cost ($27.7 against $3,400 for 1,000 runs) while agreeing with people on single verdicts much less often (numbers on {{LLM-as-judge, List against instance|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b#rd-list}} and the {{RocketEval paper page|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81ffa8e5f8fa5a5806c8}}).

  • Aggregates (leaderboards, drift monitoring, a gate that trips on a distribution): a cheap grader is fine, and cheap enough to run on every merge rather than once a quarter.
  • Single items (adjudicating one case, failing one pull request on one example, generating preference labels for training): use a calibrated strong judge or a person.

Before trusting a number

  1. Which model, harness version, prompt and judge (all pinned) produced it? ({{Eval harnesses|n:3c65c17b0d0d81ea9cd5eb65795ff5c9#rd-repro}})
  2. Has the grader been checked against people on this task, with kappa, and is it below the human ceiling or suspiciously above it? ({{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b#rd-cal}})
  3. Is the change larger than the minimum detectable effect of this set, on a paired comparison? ({{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d#rd-power}})
  4. Have the failing items been read: truncated, mis-extracted, mislabelled, or genuinely wrong? ({{Production eval engineering, error analysis|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b39ad9e96193d21adc#rd-ea}})
  5. For agents: was the action graded, or only the final message? ({{Agent and trajectory evaluation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81fdbabaf3582bff37f2#rd-actions}})
