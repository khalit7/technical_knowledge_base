Judge atlas: the judges, and the tests that rate them

Every judge model, judging method and meta-evaluation this topic leans on, on one grid: 26 rows and 172 readings, each with its metric named, who ran it, the date and the source. Read date: 4 October 2026.

Three rules hold everywhere on this tab. A reading always names its metric, and readings on different metrics are never put on one axis: JudgeBench accuracy, a kappa on JudgeBench and agreement with MT-Bench experts are three different instruments. independent readings (run by someone other than the judge's or method's authors) come first; self-reported and vendor-run readings are kept and flagged; reference marks a human ceiling or a no-judge baseline. Status is relative to October 2026. The biases these tests probe are explained in the {{Judge bias lab|#t-bias}}; preference leaderboards (Arena, AlpacaEval, Arena-Hard, WildBench) belong to {{Human preference and arenas|n:3ef5c17b0d0d814db49ff3b5c9fc3034}}, and guard models to {{Guardrails|n:3c65c17b0d0d819bbfa5dffa9246c297}}.

Try: filter with the chips or the search box; sort by any column (the reading column sorts by metric first, then value, so different metrics never interleave); click a row for every reading, its sources and any correction. Tick two or three rows, or pick a preset, to compare them: only metrics they share are lined up. Further down, one metric at a time on a chart.

Side by side

Tick up to three rows, or start from a preset. Fields that differ are shaded. Below the fields, each metric two or more of the rows were measured on gets its own line; a metric only one row has is listed, not lined up.

One metric at a time

Pick a metric. Each dot is one judge's reading on that metric only, placed by the date it was published (or ranked, for a single study's snapshot). Filled dots are independent runs; hollow dots are self-reported or vendor-run. The dashed line is what a coin would score, where the metric has one.

Click or tap a dot for the reading and its source.

Sources read 4 October 2026: the arXiv HTML of each paper (tables parsed by script, never retyped), the JudgeBench leaderboard files on Hugging Face, and the knowledge base's RocketEval page.
