Interval lab: one comparison, every interval and test

Two real sets of per-item results, each with a natural cluster. Pick a comparison and the lab computes, on exactly the same items, the unpaired and paired standard errors, their clustered versions, a paired bootstrap, a sign-flip permutation test and, for pass/fail scores, McNemar's test. Shrink the set to see how intervals grow as items run out.

  • RACE-H, run for this page: reading comprehension from English exams for Chinese high-school students ({{Lai et al. 2017|https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04683}}); several questions share one passage, the clustering example in {{Miller 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640}}. Two small open models, , answered every question of 400 of the 1,045 test passages (the first 400 of a seeded shuffle; questions), zero-shot, scored from the next-token probabilities of the letters A to D (src/run_race.py, run on 4 October 2026). Capped at 400 passages to keep the offline run near 20 minutes. Small models were chosen because they run on a laptop; the statistics do not depend on the models being good.
  • MT-Bench: LMSYS's released GPT-4 grades (1 to 10) for 34 models on 80 questions of two turns each ({{gpt-4_single.jsonl|https://huggingface.co/spaces/lmsys/mt-bench/tree/main/data/mt_bench/model_judgment}}); the two turns of a question share its context, so the question is the cluster. A turn the judge failed to grade is dropped for both models.

Bootstrap: 2,000 resamples with replacement of items, or of whole clusters, percentile interval (seeded, so the page always shows the same numbers). Permutation: 2,000 random sign flips of the per-item differences (or of per-cluster sums), two-sided p with the +1 correction. McNemar exact: two-sided binomial test on the discordant items; χ² with continuity correction beside it. Normal-approximation p for the paired and clustered rows. All computed in the page; src/recompute.py recomputes the default rows in NumPy and SciPy.

Reading the lab
Pairing helps exactly as much as the two models agree on which items are hard (the correlation r in the table): Miller's identity is SEpaired² = SEA² + SEB² − 2·r·SEA·SEB. Clustering hurts as much as items in one cluster move together. The two effects are independent, so a comparison can be both sharper (paired) and wider (clustered) than the naive interval. Scoring by probability removes the sampling noise that a sampled answer adds; scoring by the most likely letter removes it too, but changes what is measured (Reading, {{"Where the variance comes from"|#t-read}}).
