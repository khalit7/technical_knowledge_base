4. Paired comparisons: why scoring both models on the same items shrinks the interval

A model's score rarely matters alone; the question is whether B beats A. Treating the two scores as independent gives SEA−B = √(SEA² + SEB²). But when both models answered the same items, much of each score's error is the same error: the luck of which items were drawn. Taking the difference item by item cancels it (Miller §4.2):

SEpaired² = SEA² + SEB² − 2·r·SEA·SEB    =    Var(sA,i − sB,i) / n
r
the correlation between the two models' item scores: how much they agree on which items are hard
0/1 scores
Var of the difference is d − Δ², with d the share of items where exactly one model passes: only the discordant items carry information about the difference

With equal variances the paired variance is (1 − r) times the unpaired one, and the items needed shrink by 1/(1 − r). Miller's uniform example with r = 0.5 cuts the variance by a third (1/6 to 1/9); Anthropic reports correlations "between 0.3 and 0.7" between frontier models on popular evals, which is 1.4 to 3.3 times fewer items derived. On the parent topic's example (70% on 200 items, a 2-point drop, 10% of items discordant) the 95% interval goes from ±9.0 points to ±4.4.

Correction to the old Production page: "typical correlations cut required n by 3-10x"
No source was given and Miller does not state it unconfirmed. The factor is 1/(1 − r): 3 to 10 times needs r between 0.67 and 0.9. The only published range we found, Anthropic's 0.3 to 0.7 for frontier models, gives 1.4 to 3.3 times. On this page's real data the measured factor is on RACE-H (two small models, pass/fail) and on MT-Bench (claude-v1 against claude-instant-v1, judge grades); across all 561 MT-Bench model pairs the paired standard error is a median of the unpaired one. Pairing is the cheapest precision there is, but how much it buys has to be measured, not assumed.

Two points the animation makes on real numbers. Pairing does not need more labels: it is a choice of analysis, free once both models were run on the same items. And clustering and pairing are separate corrections; leaving out either one misstates the interval in its own direction, so report the paired, clustered one (Miller Eq. 8). A score compared with last month's dashboard number, or with a rival's number from a different harness, cannot be paired at all.

5. Which test: McNemar, paired t, bootstrap, permutation

A confidence interval that excludes zero and a two-sided test at 5% are the same decision. The tests differ in what they assume about the per-item differences:

TestUse it forNotes
McNemarpass/fail, two models, same itemsOnly discordant items count: with b items only A passes and c only B passes, under "no difference" b is Binomial(b + c, ½). Use the exact binomial version when b + c is small; χ² = (|b − c| − 1)²/(b + c) otherwise. The standard classifier comparison ({{Dietterich 1998|https://doi.org/10.1162/089976698300017197}}; {{Dror et al. 2018|https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/}}).
Paired t (or z)any score averaged over items, same itemsThe test version of the paired standard error; at hundreds of items the normal approximation is fine. Replace the SE with the clustered one when items cluster.
Paired bootstrapany metric, including ones that are not means (F1 over the set, a ratio, a pass@k curve)Resample items with replacement, recompute the difference, read the 2.5% and 97.5% points; resample whole clusters when items cluster (Miller Appendix C's advice for subsampling). {{Koehn 2004|https://aclanthology.org/W04-3250/}} popularised it for machine translation.
Permutation (sign-flip)small sets, no distributional assumptionUnder "no difference" each item's difference is as likely to be +d as −d: flip signs at random and count how often the total is as large as observed. Flip cluster sums for clustered data.
Wilcoxon signed-rankordinal or skewed scores (Likert, 1 to 10 grades)The default single-comparison test of {{evalstats|https://statsforevals.com/which-method.html}} (Ian Arawjo's guide, updated August 2026), which also defaults to Wilson intervals for 0/1 means.
Bayesiantens of itemsPosterior of the difference from Beta-Bernoulli models; Bowyer et al. find it holds its coverage where CLT-based paired intervals do not, and it answers "what is the probability B is better" directly.
Fisher's exact / two-sample zscores on different itemsThe unpaired fallback; much weaker. The {{Topic: benchmarks|n:3c65c17b0d0d811fb43fece56e40041a}} "Same model, many numbers" calculator has it.
