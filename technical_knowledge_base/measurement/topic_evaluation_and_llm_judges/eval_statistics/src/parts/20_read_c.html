4. Paired comparisons: why scoring both models on the same items shrinks the interval

On the parent topic's example (70% on 200 items, a 2-point drop, 10% of items discordant) the 95% interval on the difference is ±9.0 points treating the two scores as independent and ±4.4 paired item by item: same labels, half the error bar. Both models drew the same items, so much of each score's error is the same luck, and the per-item difference cancels it.

One comparison, three intervals, same items

Before: the two scores treated as independent. After: the same items paired, then clustered. Every square is one real item; the interval at the bottom is recomputed at each step from those squares. Press play or step; it does not move by itself.

The formula (Miller §4.2), with the same names as the figure:

SEpaired² = SEA² + SEB² − 2·r·SEA·SEB    =    Var(sA,i − sB,i) / n
r
the correlation between the two models' item scores: how much they agree on which items are hard; r = 0 gives the unpaired formula, SEA−B = √(SEA² + SEB²)
0/1 scores
Var of the difference is d − Δ², with d the share of items where exactly one model passes: only the discordant items carry information about the difference

With equal variances the paired variance is (1 − r) times the unpaired one, and the items needed shrink by 1/(1 − r). Miller's uniform example with r = 0.5 cuts the variance by a third (1/6 to 1/9); Anthropic reports correlations "between 0.3 and 0.7" between frontier models on popular evals, which is 1.4 to 3.3 times fewer items derived. On this page's real data the factor is 1.55 on RACE-H (r = 0.35) and 2.27 on the MT-Bench pair (r = 0.56); across all 561 MT-Bench model pairs the paired standard error is a median 0.66 of the unpaired one (0.41 to 0.91) real data.

  • Pairing is free: a choice of analysis once both models ran on the same items, no new labels. A score compared with last month's dashboard number, or with a rival's number from a different harness, cannot be paired at all.
  • Pairing and clustering are separate corrections, each wrong in its own direction if left out; report the paired, clustered one (Miller Eq. 8).
Correction to the old Production page: "typical correlations cut required n by 3-10x"
No source was given and Miller does not state it unconfirmed. The factor is 1/(1 − r): 3 to 10 times needs r between 0.67 and 0.9. The only published range we found, Anthropic's 0.3 to 0.7 for frontier models, gives 1.4 to 3.3 times; the measured factors above are 1.55 and 2.27. How much pairing buys has to be measured, not assumed.

5. Which test: McNemar, paired t, bootstrap, permutation

On the RACE-H comparison, 226 questions are right only for model A and 186 only for model B; McNemar's exact test on those 412 discordant questions gives p = 0.055, the paired z test 0.049, the clustered one 0.051, the (wrong) unpaired one 0.11 real data. Three tests sit on either side of the 5% line, which is the argument for fixing the test, the clustering and the threshold before looking. A confidence interval that excludes zero and a two-sided test at 5% are the same decision; the tests differ in what they assume about the per-item differences. In short: pass/fail on the same items, McNemar; any mean, the paired t (z) test with the clustered SE; a metric that is not a mean, the paired bootstrap; tens of items, a permutation test or a Bayesian interval; different items, Fisher's exact test, much weaker.
