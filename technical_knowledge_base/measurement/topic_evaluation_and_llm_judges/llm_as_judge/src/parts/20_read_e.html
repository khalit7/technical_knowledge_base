Operational checklist

  1. Pin the instrument: judge model version and prompt hash in the eval config; any change is a new instrument and needs recalibrating.
  2. Both orders for pairwise; a disagreement is a tie; report the tie rate.
  3. Validate before judging: finish_reason is "stop", length within bounds, the answer parses; failures go to an infrastructure bucket, not a quality bucket.
  4. Canaries in every run: a null-model answer that must lose, an injected instruction that must not change the verdict, a padded copy of a passing answer that must not gain.
  5. Gold slice and kappa: a few hundred human-labelled items (about 100 per failure mode); kappa, sensitivity and specificity, recomputed on a schedule and on any distribution shift.
  6. Another family for judge and judged; for batch scoring, a jury pooled by vote or median, with per-juror agreement tracked.
  7. Report the corrected pass rate with its interval, never a raw judge rate; if the interval is wider than the gap you care about, label more. Compare systems only with paired tests ({{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}) and wire the gate into the promotion path ({{Production eval engineering|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b39ad9e96193d21adc}}).
  8. Run the five-step validation protocol (chance-correct, swap, replicate, cross-validate, audit the consistency-bias paradox) before a judge goes into production.

Corrections to the old version of this page (8)
  • "Best frontier judges reach only about 64% on JudgeBench" is stale: reasoning judges reach 80.86% (o3-mini high) in JudgeBench v2, and NVIDIA's own submission 81.4%. The 64% was Claude 3.5 Sonnet in October 2024.
  • "Debate buys a few points of accuracy": on JudgeBench, ChatEval's multi-agent debate with GPT-4o scored 34.0%, below a single GPT-4o (56.57%) and below a coin.
  • The ordering "frontier chat models over fine-tuned judges over small open models" misses reward models: on JudgeBench, the 27B reward model Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2 (64.29%) beats every fine-tuned judge (Prometheus 2 8x7B 40.29%, JudgeLM-33B 35.71%).
  • Inter-annotator agreement "often 75 to 85%": on MT-Bench two experts agree 63% when ties count and 81% when they do not; the range depends on ties.
  • RocketEval's checklists hold 3 to 25 questions in the released data (not 5 to 10), and the 70.3% "with GPT-4o's analysis" is agreement with GPT-4o itself.
  • Cohere North Small Translate's nearest competitor in its own table is Qwen 3.5 397B (81.56), not DeepL (81.37): the margin is 2.0 points, and the claim covers "50+ languages".
  • Chain of thought before the verdict is not a universal gain: it hurt the 1.5B judge in RocketEval's diagnosis and helped the larger ones by about 2 points.
  • Verbosity bias is smaller in 2026 judges than in 2023 ones: under one fixed rubric all 21 judges Norman et al. tested showed a verbosity bias below 0.011 on MT-Bench.
