On this page

{{Protocol lab|#t-proto}}

GPT-4 run five ways (one order, the other, both orders twice, pointwise) against 3,355 MT-Bench human votes: agreement, kappa, tie rate, confusion matrix, length preference, and list-level against instance-level reliability; reproduces Zheng et al. Table 5.

{{Calibration lab|#t-cal}}

A judge's win rate corrected with a seeded human gold slice (Rogan-Gladen with Lee et al.'s interval), against the same labels used alone; 200 redraws for coverage, and interval width against slice size.

Best resources

{{Zheng et al. 2023, Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685}} (45 min)

The paper that named the pattern and released the votes and verdicts both labs use.

{{Lee et al., How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations|https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21140}} (45 min)

The correction and its interval, adaptive allocation of gold labels, and when a judge beats human-only evaluation.

{{Norman et al., Reliability without Validity|https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19544}} (45 min)

21 judges, about 541,000 judgments: kappa deflation, rank instability, the consistency-bias paradox, the five-step validation protocol.

{{Hamel Husain, Creating an LLM-as-a-Judge That Drives Business Results|https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/}} (about 35 min)

Critique Shadowing: building a judge from one expert's pass/fail critiques.

{{Gu et al. 2024, A Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594}} (90 min, survey)

The standard taxonomy of judge designs and mitigations.

{{Ye et al., Justice or Prejudice? (CALM)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736}} (45 min)

Twelve biases injected one at a time into six judges.

{{X. Zheng et al., Cheating Automatic LLM Benchmarks|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07137}} (45 min)

Null models at 86.5% LC on AlpacaEval 2.0, and the defences that failed.

{{Verga et al., Replacing Judges with Juries (PoLL)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796}} (45 min)

Three small judges from different families against GPT-4.

{{RoPoLL: Robust Panel of LLM Judges|https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30931}} (45 min, preprint)

Why mean pooling is fragile and the geometric median is not.

{{Panickssery et al., LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076}} (45 min)

Self-recognition and self-preference, linearly related.

{{JudgeBench|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784}} (45 min)

Judges on hard pairs with objectively correct answers.

{{RewardBench 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01937}} (45 min)

Reward models and judges on best-of-4 over unseen prompts, checked against downstream use.

{{Tripathi et al., Pairwise or Pointwise?|https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14716}} (40 min)

Distractor features flip pairwise preferences far more often than absolute scores.

{{Zhang et al., Generative Verifiers (GenRM)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15240}} (45 min)

Verification as next-token prediction, with chain of thought and majority voting.

{{Prometheus 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01535}} (40 min)

The best-known open judge model and how it was trained.
