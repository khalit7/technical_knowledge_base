Definition: canarying is "a partial and time-limited deployment of a change in a service and its evaluation."
Population/duration: should be "sizeable and last long enough to be representative"; run one canary at a time; "If you release daily, you can't let your canary last for a week...If you deploy weekly, you have time to perform fairly long canaries."
Example: a 5% canary population used as a hypothetical in the error-budget calculation (no universal percentage prescribed).
Compare canary vs control: "fine-grained breakdowns that enable you to differentiate metrics between the canary and control populations."
Decision rule example: "If the error rate of the canary metric is too far from the control error rate, this signals the canary deployment is 'bad.' In response, we should pause and roll back the deployment"
Metrics: select metrics that "indicate problems in the service"; reuse SLIs; "Stack-rank the metrics"; "perhaps no more than a dozen"; metrics "clearly attributable to the change".
Before/after is risky: "time is one of the biggest sources of change in observed metrics".
