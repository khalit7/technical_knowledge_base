Q: Why is "error analysis" so important in AI evals, and how is it performed?
- "Start by annotating at least 30 traces yourself before reviewing suggestions from an agent."
- "Categorize the open-ended notes into a "failure taxonomy.""
- "Axial coding is the most important step."
- "A working pool of roughly 100 diverse traces is a useful guardrail for this human-agent loop."
- saturation: "meaning new reviews stop revealing failure modes or changing existing ones."

Q: Do I need a reference answer or rubric before annotating data?
- "Writing a rubric before you review examples can get in the way."
- "Reviewers can become so focused on checking each item that they overlook problems outside the rubric."

Q: How many examples do I need for an eval?
- "Continue until new traces stop revealing failure modes or changing existing ones."
- "We recommend reviewing at least 100 traces."
- "Qualitative researchers call this theoretical saturation."
- "This review produces a failure taxonomy, which is a list of the specific ways your application fails."

Q: Why do you recommend binary (pass/fail) evaluations instead of 1-5 ratings (Likert scales)?
- "Binary evaluations force clearer thinking and more consistent labeling."
- "annotators often default to middle values to avoid making hard decisions."

Q: How much of my development budget should I allocate to evals?
- "we've spent 60-80% of our development time on error analysis and evaluation"
