Title: Your AI Product Needs Evals
Author: Hamel Husain (case study: Rechat's assistant Lucy). Date: page metadata "Published Time: 2024-03-29"

Three levels: "Level 1: Unit Tests", "Level 2: Model & Human Eval (this includes debugging)", "Level 3: A/B testing"
"The cost of Level 3 > Level 2 > Level 1. This dictates the cadence and manner you execute them."
"I often run Level 1 evals on every code change, Level 2 on a set cadence and Level 3 only after significant product changes."
"Rechat has hundreds of these unit tests. We continuously update them based on new failures we observe in the data as users challenge the AI or the product evolves."
"We use a simple regex to assert that the LLM response doesn't include UUIDs."
"unlike traditional unit tests, you don't necessarily need a 100% pass rate. Your pass rate is a product decision, depending on the failures you are willing to tolerate."
"Rechat has been leveraging CI infrastructure (e.g., GitHub Actions, GitLab Pipelines, etc.) to execute these tests."
"Rechat uses Metabase to track their LLM test results over time."
"You should track the correlation between model-based and human evaluation to decide how much you can rely on automatic evaluation."
Phillip [Carter] grades "his critique, outcome, and desired response for 25-50 examples at a time"
Level 3: "This level of evaluation is usually only appropriate for more mature products."
Symptom before evals: "Addressing one failure mode led to the emergence of others, resembling a game of whack-a-mole."
