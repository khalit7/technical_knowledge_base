Title: An LLM-as-Judge Won't Save The Product, Fixing Your Process Will (original title uses an em-dash)
Author: Eugene Yan (Ziyou Yan). Date: page citation "(Apr 2025)"; metadata "Published Time: 2025-04-20"

"Building product evals is simply the scientific method in disguise."
"Ideally, we should have a 50:50 split of passes and fails that spans the distribution of inputs."
"Eval-driven development (EDD) helps us build better AI products. It's similar to test-driven development"
"From then on, every prompt tweak, every system update, every iteration, is evaluated."
"With enough high-quality annotations, we can calibrate automated evaluators to align with human judgment."
"having automated evaluators doesn't remove the need for human oversight. We still need to periodically sample and annotate data, and analyze user feedback."
"If we're not actively reviewing AI outputs and customer feedback, automated evaluators won't save our product."
Note: practitioner essay, no company-specific numbers.
