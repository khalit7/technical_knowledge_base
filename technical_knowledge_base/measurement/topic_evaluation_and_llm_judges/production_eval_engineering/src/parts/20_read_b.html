One model swap, two gates

A real swap, on real released grades. The champion is claude-v1; the challenger is the cheaper claude-instant-v1, the kind of cost-down swap every product team runs. The golden set is {{MT-Bench|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685}}: 80 two-turn questions in 8 categories of 10, so 160 graded turns in 8 tagged slices, each turn graded 1 to 10 by GPT-4. Every grade below is LMSYS's own released judgment ({{gpt-4_single.jsonl|https://huggingface.co/spaces/lmsys/mt-bench/tree/main/data/mt_bench/model_judgment}}, mid-2023), not a simulation. Run it twice: once through a gate with one aggregate threshold, then through the same gate with thresholds per slice.

Thresholds are illustrative and the same in both runs: the aggregate gate fails a mean paired drop of 0.25 points or more; the per-slice gate fails a slice whose mean paired drop is 0.5 points or more and whose 95% interval lies wholly below zero, and sends a drop of 0.5 or more with an interval that still reaches zero to review instead of passing it. Intervals are paired t intervals over graded turns (the two turns of one question are treated as independent here; that overstates precision on average, since across all 561 MT-Bench model pairs the clustered standard error is a median 1.10 times the paired one (range 0.90 to 1.26), but for this claude-v1 against claude-instant-v1 pair the ratio is 1.00, so the intervals shown stand; measured on {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}, which covers clustered errors). Change any of this, or pick any other pair of the 34 graded models, in the {{Gate designer|#t-gate}} tab.

What the two runs show. Averaged over all 159 turns both models have a grade for, the swap moves the score by points, and the aggregate gate waves it through. Split by the tags the golden set already carries, the same numbers say something else: writing drops by points (95% interval ), which the per-slice gate fails, and extraction drops by with an interval that still crosses zero, which goes to review. Math improves by about as much as writing falls (and coding a little), which is exactly why the average hid it. Per-slice thresholds cost nothing extra to compute; they only need the tags.

A flaky judge, in the released data
The failed grade comes from one of the 320 judge calls in this run: on question GPT-4 kept writing for 9,516 characters, repeating "The assistant's function does not correctly..." 63 times, and never emitted a [[rating]], so the grade was recorded as −1. Across all 34 models in the file, of 5,440 judgments failed this way, all on second turns, all between about 9,500 and 12,000 characters long. A gate must decide what an unparsable verdict means (here: drop the pair from both sides, and count it), never average it in as a score.
