Who annotates, and what it costs

The rater pool sets both the ceiling on quality and the bill. The published evidence:

PoolExampleAgreement evidenceEffort and cost evidence
Domain expertsGPQA's question writers and validators, PhD holders or students in the field ({{Rein et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12022}}); MT-Bench's graduate students ({{Zheng et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685}})GPQA: experts 65% on each other's questions, 74% after discounting slips they admitted afterwards; skilled non-experts 34% with unrestricted web accessGPQA writers: $10 per question plus bonuses ($20 per expert who answers correctly, $15 per non-expert who does not, $30 extra if both hold); non-experts spent over 30 minutes per question
Trained contractorsInstructGPT's about 40 contractors from Upwork and Scale AI, chosen by a screening test ({{Ouyang et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155}}); HelpSteer2's about 1,000 US raters via Scale AIInstructGPT: 72.6 ± 1.5% between training labelers, 77.3 ± 1.3% for held-out labelers, against 73 ± 4% researcher to researcher in earlier summarisation workHelpSteer2: at least 3 raters per response (3.41 on average), a fourth and fifth when helpfulness ratings spread by more than 2; about half of all annotations were excluded
CrowdAnthropic's HH crowdworkers ({{Bai et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862}}); Mechanical Turk, ProlificHH: about 63% agreement between crowdworkers and the researchers, on about 320 test comparisons, against the 73 to 77% aboveProlific's floor is £6 / $8 an hour and it recommends £9 / $12 ({{Prolific, July 2026|https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/article/2273bd}}); 33 to 46% of MTurk workers on one summarisation task used an LLM ({{Veselovsky et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07899}})
UsersArena voters; thumbs up and down in a productArena crowd against two experts: 72.8 to 83.1%; the experts with each other 79.4% and 89.8% (160 battles; {{Chiang et al. 2024, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132}})Free and plentiful, but the prompt mix and the voters are whoever shows up; how votes become a leaderboard is on {{Human preference and arenas|n:3ef5c17b0d0d814db49ff3b5c9fc3034}}
The team that built itEngineers labelling their own outputsNo independence: they share the system's blind spotsCheapest and fastest; fine for a pilot, never the only source of gold

The gap between pools is mostly expertise and attention, not honesty: Arena's authors attribute the 5 to 10 point gap between crowd and expert agreement mostly to users "overlooking factual errors" in a response.

A budget, worked out

Cost is items × labels per item × minutes per label × hourly rate, plus adjudication for the items that disagree. Choose a preset or move the inputs.
