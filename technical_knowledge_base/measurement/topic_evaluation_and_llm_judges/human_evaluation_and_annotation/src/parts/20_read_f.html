The gold slice: building it and keeping it true

A gold slice is a set of real inputs (with outputs, when the label is about an output) labelled by qualified people under a fixed rubric version. It is the reference every automatic grader is calibrated against, the answer key of your own regression sets, and the thing an audit audits. {{Production eval engineering|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b39ad9e96193d21adc}} owns the golden sets built on it (composition, sizes, promoting production failures into tests, CI gates); {{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}} owns calibrating a judge against it. This section is about the labels themselves.

Building it

  • Sample from real traffic, stratified by the slices you will report (intent, language, risk tier), oversampling rare slices that matter and including known hard and ambiguous cases on purpose. Record each item's sampling weight, so an aggregate can be reweighted to the traffic mix.
  • Size it from the interval you need, not from habit: a 200-item slice at 80% agreement has a 95% interval of roughly ±5.5 points (the binomial standard error, √(0.8 × 0.2 / 200) = 2.8 points, times 1.96). How many items a given decision needs is on {{Eval statistics|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8187a7b4e5b4ec4a546d}}.
  • Two independent labels per item, blind to each other and to which system produced the output, and a third person to adjudicate disagreements. The physics re-grading audit did exactly this: two PhD reviewers agreed on 140 of the 196 cases they both reviewed (71.4%) and a third settled the other 56 ({{Re-grading six physics benchmarks|n:3e25c17b0d0d8165b0c8d15600421259}}).
  • A one-line rationale per label. It makes adjudication fast, it is the raw material of a judge prompt (Critique Shadowing, on the LLM-as-judge page), and it shows later whether a label was a judgement or a slip.

Keeping it true

  • Version the slice and the rubric together, and re-measure agreement whenever either changes.
  • Refresh with new samples when traffic shifts, and retire items whose answer has changed with the world. MMLU-Redux found questions in Professional Law and Accounting that assume US jurisdiction and practice without saying so and "may become outdated if standards change" ({{Gema et al. 2024, §2.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04127}}).
  • Hold part of it out. If the same items are used to iterate a judge prompt and to report the judge's agreement, the report is an in-sample number.
  • Model-assisted labelling (an LLM pre-fills the label, a person confirms) is faster, but it anchors the rater on the model's answer, which is the answer you are trying to check. If you use it, measure on a sample how often people overturn the pre-label, and keep the double-blind protocol for the items that feed calibration.
  • Audit it on a schedule and on every surprise: next section.
