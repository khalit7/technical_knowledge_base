Agreement: telling consensus from chance

Two raters who label at random will still agree some of the time. Every agreement statistic worth reporting subtracts that chance agreement and rescales: 1 means perfect agreement, 0 means no better than chance, below 0 means systematic disagreement. They differ in what "chance" means and in what data they accept.

Most real annotation sets have a varying number of raters per item, some missing labels, and often ordered scales. Cohen's kappa does not apply to them at all, which is why alpha is the default here. Why ties matter

On turn 1 of MT-Bench, two experts agree on of pairs when ties count, and on of when any pair involving a tie is dropped: Zheng et al.'s 63% and 81% (Table 5, reproduced exactly from the released votes derived). Dropping ties throws away the hardest third of the comparisons and shrinks the label set from three to two, so chance agreement rises from about a third to about a half. Alpha goes from to : a real improvement, but not the 18 points the raw figures suggest. Treating a tie as halfway between "A wins" and "B wins" (an ordinal distance) gives alpha with ties kept: a tie against a win is a smaller disagreement than a win against the opposite win. Any agreement figure for pairwise votes is meaningless without saying which of these it is.

Why prevalence matters

When one label dominates, two raters agree often just by both saying it, and chance-corrected statistics collapse. This is the "high agreement but low kappa" paradox ({{Feinstein and Cicchetti 1990|https://doi.org/10.1016/0895-4356(90)90158-L}}). It is not a bug in kappa: when nearly everything is coherent, the labels carry little information about which responses are better, and the statistic says so. Reading the number

The widely quoted bands (0.41 to 0.60 "moderate", 0.61 to 0.80 "substantial", above 0.81 "almost perfect") come from {{Landis and Koch 1977|https://doi.org/10.2307/2529310}}, who offered them as opinion, not evidence. Krippendorff's working rule is stricter: rely on data with alpha of at least 0.800, draw only tentative conclusions between 0.667 and 0.800, and discard below (Content Analysis, 2004, pp. 241 to 243, as quoted on {{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krippendorff%27s_alpha}}; the book was not re-read here). By that rule, MT-Bench's expert votes, HelpSteer2's ratings and Llama 2's helpfulness ratings (Gwet's AC2 0.37 to 0.55 on a 7-point scale; safety ratings 0.70 to 0.95, {{Touvron et al. 2023, §3.4.2 and §4.4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288}}) are all below the bar for individual labels. That is normal for subjective judgements, and it is why gold labels are aggregated over several raters and why a judge that matches one human is not necessarily at the ceiling.

What to report with any human-labelled set: the statistic and its variant (nominal or weighted, ties in or out), raw agreement beside it, the label shares, the number of items and of raters per item, how disagreements were resolved, and whether low-agreement items were filtered out before measuring.
