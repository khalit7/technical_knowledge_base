Agreement: telling consensus from chance

Two raters who label at random still agree some of the time. Every agreement statistic worth reporting subtracts that chance agreement and rescales: 1 is perfect agreement, 0 no better than chance, below 0 systematic disagreement. The animation runs this on released votes; the formulas follow it with the same names.

κ = (po − pe) / (1 − pe)     α = 1 − Do / De
po
observed agreement: the share of rater pairs, on the same item, that gave the same label
pe
chance agreement: for Fleiss, the sum of the squared pooled label shares (two labels drawn at random match)
Do, De
observed and expected disagreement, from a coincidence matrix in which an item with m labels contributes each of its ordered pairs with weight 1/(m − 1), so items with more raters do not count more; a distance between labels (0 or 1 for nominal, squared difference for interval) lets near misses count as partly right

Most real annotation sets have a varying number of raters per item, missing labels and often ordered scales. Cohen's kappa does not apply to them at all (with varying raters use Fleiss' kappa for a fixed count, or alpha), which is why alpha is the default here. The parent page's worked example (a judge that always says "pass" gets 90% raw agreement and kappa 0) is the two-rater case.

Why ties matter

On turn 1 of MT-Bench, two experts agree on of pairs when ties count, and on of when any pair involving a tie is dropped: Zheng et al.'s 63% and 81% (Table 5, reproduced exactly from the released votes derived). Dropping ties throws away the hardest third of the comparisons and shrinks the label set from three to two, so chance agreement rises from about a third to about a half. Alpha goes from to : a real improvement, but not the 18 points the raw figures suggest. Treating a tie as halfway between "A wins" and "B wins" (an ordinal distance) gives alpha with ties kept: a tie against a win is a smaller disagreement than a win against the opposite win. An agreement figure for pairwise votes means nothing until it says which of these it is.

Why prevalence matters

When one label dominates, two raters agree often just by both saying it, and chance-corrected statistics collapse: the "high agreement but low kappa" paradox ({{Feinstein and Cicchetti 1990|https://doi.org/10.1016/0895-4356(90)90158-L}}).

Reading the number

The widely quoted bands (0.41 to 0.60 "moderate", 0.61 to 0.80 "substantial", above 0.81 "almost perfect") come from {{Landis and Koch 1977|https://doi.org/10.2307/2529310}}, who offered them as opinion, not evidence. Krippendorff's working rule is stricter: rely on data with alpha of at least 0.800, draw only tentative conclusions between 0.667 and 0.800, and discard below (Content Analysis, 2004, pp. 241 to 243, as quoted on {{Wikipedia|https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krippendorff%27s_alpha}}; the book was not re-read here). By that rule MT-Bench's expert votes, HelpSteer2's ratings and Llama 2's helpfulness ratings (Gwet's AC2 0.37 to 0.55 on a 7-point scale; safety ratings 0.70 to 0.95, {{Touvron et al. 2023, §3.4.2 and §4.4|https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288}}) are all below the bar for individual labels. That is normal for subjective judgements; it is why gold labels are aggregated over several raters, and why a judge that matches one human is not necessarily at the ceiling.

How each number is computed (and checked against src/recompute.py):

  • Raw agreement: matching rater pairs over all rater pairs on the same item (unweighted, as Zheng et al. count it; this reproduces their 63% and 81%).
  • Fleiss' kappa: (po − pe) / (1 − pe) with pe = Σ (share of label)². Strictly defined for a fixed number of raters per item; computed here on the pairs, as Scott's pi generalises.
  • Krippendorff's alpha: 1 − Do/De from the coincidence matrix; distances 0 or 1 (nominal), squared rank-sum distances (ordinal) and squared differences (interval). For MT-Bench, ordinal and interval place a tie halfway between the two wins.
  • Gwet's AC1: chance agreement Σ π(1 − π) / (q − 1) over the q labels used.
  • Checked against src/recompute.py, which computes the same quantities in Python from the same inputs.
