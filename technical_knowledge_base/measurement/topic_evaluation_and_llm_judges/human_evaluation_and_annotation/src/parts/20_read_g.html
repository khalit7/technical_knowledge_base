Gold-label auditing: your key is wrong somewhere

Gold labels are written by people and are wrong at rates that swamp the small deltas evaluations are used to detect. Against a wrong key, a better model fails for disagreeing with the label, ceilings look lower than they are, and a regression gate can block the better model. Every audit of a widely used test set has found this:

AuditWhat was checked, howWhat it found
{{Northcutt, Athalye and Mueller 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14749}}Test sets of 10 image, text and audio benchmarks; confident learning proposed candidates, five Mechanical Turk workers checked eachAt least 3.3% label errors on average (from 0.15% on MNIST to 10.12% on QuickDraw); 2,916 in the ImageNet validation set (5.83%, "at least 6%"); 51% of the flagged candidates were real errors. Corrections to the facts this page inherited
The old Production eval engineering page (its gold-label section moved here) said:
  • "About 5% of GSM8K items are wrong." PlatinumBench's introduction does say "e.g., 5% of GSM8K", but its own Table 2 counts 27 problems in 300 (9.0%), of which only 1 is a wrong answer (0.3%) and 26 are badly posed; the full-test GSM8K-Platinum changed 120 of 1,319 (9.1%), of which 10 relabelled (0.8%). Whether GSM8K is "5% wrong" depends on counting badly posed questions; no count in either source gives 5%.
  • "PlatinumBench found that after cleaning, a majority of residual model failures on many benchmarks were label noise." Reversed: it is before cleaning that more than half of model errors were caused by benchmark errors, on 8 of 14 benchmarks; after cleaning, every remaining failure was manually verified as a genuine model error. And "label noise" there is mostly badly posed questions, not wrong keys.
  • "Model rankings can flip by 10 to 15 points on cleaned sets." unconfirmed Not found in the cited sources. What they do report: ResNet-18 moves from 34th to 1st of 34 ImageNet models on the correctable set (Northcutt et al., Table S1); Llama 3.1 405B Instruct from 16th to 1st on MMLU-Redux Virology (Gema et al., Table 2); Claude 3.7 Sonnet (extended thinking) and Llama 3.1 405B tied at 45 errors on GSM8K, 2 against 17 on GSM8K-Platinum. The animation below reproduces a ranking change from released per-question data.
  • Northcutt et al.'s rate was not given: it is at least 3.3% on average across the 10 test sets, reproduced from the mean of their Table 1 (3.33% derived).
The MMLU-Redux figures (5,700 questions, 57 subjects, about 6.5% with an error of some kind, Virology 57%) were right, and the old text already separated "error of some kind" from wrong keys.

One test set, before and after the audit

Try: play the Virology run and watch Mixtral-8x7B and DeepSeek-67B climb while Falcon-40B and MPT-30B fall; then switch to ImageNet, where the models that fit the original (wrong) labels best are ranked first, and ResNet-18, last on those labels, comes first on the corrected ones.

Two things stand out in the MMLU runs. First, on the 33 Virology questions whose key the audit judged wrong, 16 of the 18 models picked the audit's answer at least four times as often as the official key (Mixtral-8x7B 26 times against 0, Yi-34B 26 against 2); only the two weakest, GPT-J and Falcon-7B, were near chance on them derived. The official key was costing the strongest models the most. Second, the reshuffle is real but the margins are small: on 43 to 76 items, a 95% interval on an accuracy near 80% is about ±9 to ±12 points, so most adjacent ranks are within noise either way. Northcutt et al. note the same of their ImageNet result: on the full corrected test set, rankings barely change; it is on the correctable subset, and in deployments whose data is noisier than a curated benchmark, that they flip. They state that ResNet-18 would overtake ResNet-50 if the share of originally mislabelled examples rose by just 6%; that crossing depends on per-model accuracies they do not tabulate, so it is not reproduced here.
