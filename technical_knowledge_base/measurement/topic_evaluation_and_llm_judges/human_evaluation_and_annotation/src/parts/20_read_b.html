What a usable rubric contains

One criterion, written so two raters answer the same question

Criterion: Does every factual claim in the response agree with the reference document?
1 Definition as a question, not an adjective ("accurate")
Pass, e.g. a reply saying a trial enrolled "about 400" when the reference says 412. Fail, e.g. a reply that gives the right dose in the wrong unit.
2 Anchors from borderline cases, one per level. HelpSteer2's guidelines define every attribute and level with examples (its Appendix G)
First: is there a factual claim at all? Then: is it in the reference? Then: does it agree?
3 Decision order when criteria interact
Cannot judge (reference missing) · Needs an expert · Skip (personal data)
4 Exits: forcing a label onto an item the rater cannot assess manufactures noise
Ignore length, formatting and confident tone. Check claims with a search engine.
5 What to ignore, unless it is the criterion
Show the reference beside the response; score both responses to a prompt one after the other.
6 Context the rater needs

An illustrative rubric; the evidence for each part is published. Decision order: MMLU-Redux's ordered protocol is why its kappa for "is there an error" (binary) and "which error type" were almost the same, 0.67 and 0.67 on Virology and 0.92 and 0.95 on College Chemistry (Table 1): raters who agreed something was wrong also agreed what. Exits: HelpSteer2 told raters to skip anything with personal data and flag unsafe content. What to ignore: Arena's experts fact-checked with a search engine, 3 to 5 minutes per label ({{Chiang et al. 2024, §6.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132}}). Context: showing AMT workers the human reference beside the model's story improved their ratings ({{Karpinska et al.|https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06835}}); HelpSteer2 had each rater score the two responses to a prompt one after the other for the same reason.

Pilot, measure, revise

Write the rubric, have two or three people label the same 50 to 100 items, measure agreement, read every disagreement, fix the guideline, repeat. HelpSteer2 published what this loop is worth:

Clarifying the guidelines moved agreement more than filtering did

Quadratic weighted Cohen's kappa by attribute and stage, {{HelpSteer2 Table 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08673}}: helpfulness went from 0.465 in the initial collection to 0.706 after clarifying with the vendor how the guidelines apply to edge cases, and to 0.791 after post-processing. The third stage is partly agreement by construction: it kept only responses whose helpfulness ratings were within 2 points of each other, which removed about 10% of samples, and about 50% of all annotations were excluded at some stage. On the released individual ratings, the same retention rule alone raises helpfulness alpha from to derived. Coherence stays low at every stage for a reason unrelated to the guidelines: prevalence.
