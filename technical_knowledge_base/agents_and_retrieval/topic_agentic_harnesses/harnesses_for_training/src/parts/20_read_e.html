4. Groups and advantages: which rollouts teach anything

GRPO samples a group of G rollouts of the same task and gives every token of rollout i the same advantage, its reward measured against the group:

Ai = (ri − mean(r)) / std(r)

The derivation, the clip around it, Dr. GRPO's and RLOO's variants and a live group widget are on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}} (section 3; biases and fixes in section 7). What changes for agents is the size and the shape of the groups. They are small, because every rollout is an episode in a container: DeepSWE ran 64 problems with 8 rollouts each per step, 512 containers in parallel ({{Together AI and Agentica, 2 Jul 2025|https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe}}); ours are 4 rollouts (local model) and 3 (Haiku) per task and harness. And many of them are flat.

The group Fig. 3 moves in on (steps 3 to 5), its reward and its normaliser:

Fig. 3, step by step: when every rollout scores the same, every advantage is zero

  1. 70 groups, pass/fail reward. One cluster per model, harness and task (rows: harnesses, the 4B model's three in front, Haiku's four behind; columns: tasks T01 to T10), one bar per rollout. No disagreement, no signal: a grey cluster is flat, every rollout scored the same, so every advantage is zero and the group gives no gradient, only cost. With the pass/fail reward that happened in of the local model's groups (mostly all-fail) and of Haiku's (mostly all-pass).
  2. The partial reward. Bars now show the fraction of the 20 checks passed. It rescues some of the local model's groups ( still flat), because its failures fail different numbers of checks; it rescues none of Haiku's ( still flat), because every Haiku rollout in a failing group failed the very same held-out check and scored the same 0.95.
  3. One group: rewards and their mean. The camera moves to the group chosen above (it opens on 4B, bash only, task T07: pass/fail rewards 0, 1, 0, 0). The mean, 0.25, is the baseline every rollout is measured against: the glass plane.
  4. Advantages. Reward minus the mean, over the standard deviation; for the T07 group, +1.73 for the one pass and −0.58 for each failure (GRPO). Dr. GRPO drops the division (+0.75, −0.25); RLOO takes each rollout's baseline from the other three (+1.00, −0.33). Green is made more likely, red less; a flat group shows no bars at all.
  5. The update. The trainer multiplies each sampled token's log-probability gradient by its rollout's advantage, so a longer rollout moves more tokens: for the T07 group, 434, 1,123, 445 and 1,816 tokens the model sampled (counted by the loss mask, section 5; for Haiku, the output tokens Claude reports, thinking included) stream into the policy.

DAPO's dynamic sampling handles the rest by dropping groups whose accuracy is exactly 0 or 1 and sampling more until the batch is full; in its ablation that step took Qwen2.5-32B from 42 to 50 on AIME 2024 ({{arXiv 2503.14476|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476}}). For agents the waste is larger than for maths, because a dropped group cost minutes of container time, so curricula that keep tasks near a 50% pass rate matter (in EnvHarness's released configs a reshaped SWE-bench environment is accepted only when its success rate over 5 rollouts lands between 0.4 and 0.6; ALFWorld instead asks for a rise of at least 0.2 over the baseline, and the other benchmarks leave it to the designer's judgement, {{EnvHarness|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e88ab9c396a160f813}}, Table 8 against the released configs).

Agent recipes change the normaliser and the failures they score. Dividing by the spread pushes rare successes and rare failures hardest, which Dr. GRPO calls a difficulty bias (the numbers are worked on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}, section 7; toggle the three normalisers above to see it on real groups). DeepSWE's recipe dropped the division, used a leave-one-out baseline (RLOO: each rollout's baseline is the mean of the other G−1, {{arXiv 2402.14740|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14740}}), and added compact filtering: trajectories that hit the maximum context, step count or time limit are masked from the loss rather than scored 0, so the model is not punished for running out of room. of our rollouts hit the 16-call cap, the case compact filtering is for.

Depth: every group and its advantages ( rollouts, 70 groups)

One group = one model, one harness, one task. Advantages are GRPO's (reward minus the group mean, over the population standard deviation; zero when every reward is equal).
