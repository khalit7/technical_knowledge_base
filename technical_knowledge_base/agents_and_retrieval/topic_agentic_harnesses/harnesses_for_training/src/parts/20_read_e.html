4. Groups and advantages: which rollouts teach anything

GRPO samples a group of G rollouts of the same task and gives every token of rollout i the same advantage, its reward measured against the group:

Ai = (ri − mean(r)) / std(r)

The derivation, the clip around it, Dr. GRPO's and RLOO's variants and a live group widget are on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}} (section 3; biases and fixes in section 7). What changes for agents is the size and the shape of the groups. They are small, because every rollout is an episode in a container: DeepSWE ran 64 problems with 8 rollouts each per step, 512 containers in parallel ({{Together AI and Agentica, 2 Jul 2025|https://www.together.ai/blog/deepswe}}); ours are 4 rollouts (local model) and 3 (Haiku) per task and harness. And many of them are flat.

Real groups: when every rollout scores the same, every advantage is zero

Groups are named model, harness, task, then the pass/fail rewards. Predict before you step a flat group: what does the partial reward do to it?

No disagreement, no signal. If every rollout in a group gets the same reward, every advantage is zero and the group contributes no gradient, only cost. With the pass/fail reward that happened in of the local model's groups (mostly all-fail) and of Haiku's (mostly all-pass). The partial reward rescues some of the local model's groups ( still flat), because its failures fail different numbers of checks; it rescues none of Haiku's ( still flat), because every Haiku rollout in a failing group failed the very same held-out check and scored the same 0.95. DAPO's dynamic sampling handles the rest by dropping groups whose accuracy is exactly 0 or 1 and sampling more until the batch is full; in its ablation that step took Qwen2.5-32B from 42 to 50 on AIME 2024 ({{arXiv 2503.14476|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14476}}). For agents the waste is larger than for maths, because a dropped group cost minutes of container time, so curricula that keep tasks near a 50% pass rate matter (EnvHarness accepts a reshaped environment only when its success rate lands between 0.4 and 0.6, {{EnvHarness|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e88ab9c396a160f813}}).

Agent recipes change the normaliser and the failures they score. Dividing by the spread pushes rare successes and rare failures hardest, which Dr. GRPO calls a difficulty bias (the numbers are worked on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}, section 7; toggle the three normalisers above to see it on real groups). DeepSWE's recipe dropped the division, used a leave-one-out baseline (RLOO: each rollout's baseline is the mean of the other G−1, {{arXiv 2402.14740|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14740}}), and added compact filtering: trajectories that hit the maximum context, step count or time limit are masked from the loss rather than scored 0, so the model is not punished for running out of room. of our rollouts hit the 16-call cap, the case compact filtering is for.

Depth: every group and its advantages ( rollouts, 70 groups)

One group = one model, one harness, one task. Advantages are GRPO's (reward minus the group mean, over the population standard deviation; zero when every reward is equal).
