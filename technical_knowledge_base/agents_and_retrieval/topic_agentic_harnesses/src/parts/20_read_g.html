8. Harnesses for training: environments, trajectories, self-improvement

A harness is an RL environment. In reinforcement learning terms (see {{Topic: rl|n:3c65c17b0d0d8195a6c6c11ad093c16e}}), the task and its repository are the starting state, the tool calls are the actions, tool results are the observations, and a verifier such as the test suite gives the reward. Training a model for agentic work means running it inside a harness thousands of times in sandboxes and updating it on the trajectories that passed. Three consequences matter for anyone training models:

The training harness shapes the policy

A model learns the tools, formats and context habits of the harness it was trained in. Prime Agent's authors report that "many harness capabilities remain underused because current models were not trained to operate them" ({{Prime Agent|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81579a02f0431f24c6cb}}). Train and deploy in the same, or deliberately varied, harnesses.

Environments are the bottleneck

Terminal-Universe rebuilds workspaces from recorded agent trajectories and grows tasks from them: 37,300 environments (judged task-sufficient by an LLM, not shown runnable), and +11.9 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1 after fine-tuning Qwen3.5-27B ({{Terminal-Universe|n:3d45c17b0d0d814abc07fde424e09a31}}). EnvHarness wraps existing environments instead ({{EnvHarness|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e88ab9c396a160f813}}); DeepSeek's DSec reports about 3 million sandboxes a day for this kind of training, self-reported ({{DSec|n:3e95c17b0d0d81bd927be047bb0d7b0b}}).

Trajectories are data

A recorded run is a supervised example (imitate the passing trajectories) and an evaluation record. That is why the recordings on this page keep every tool call and token count, and why redaction comes first: trajectories carry paths, ids and secrets.

Self-improving harnesses. If the harness moves the score and the bill, a loop can search over harnesses. The paper pages on this knowledge base, each with its numbers checked: {{JIT-Agent|n:3cd5c17b0d0d81148227fbd67dc4b3ee}} trains a model to write a harness per task (+7.7 and +8.8 points averaged over nine agent benchmarks for two models, at 14.9 to 54.1% lower cost than fixed harnesses); {{SoL-Pi|n:3e25c17b0d0d81a0bb77f16cbc898bb5}} points the loop at cost and cuts recorded token traffic 44.7 to 49.0% at parity; {{RRSI|n:3e95c17b0d0d81dbb1a5e660ed019c8a}} regularises the loop so gains survive outside the tuning set (its +4.7 is the best of five out-of-distribution gains, mean 3.6, and its own Table 2 gives 36% fewer tokens, not the 30% often quoted); {{HarnessDev|n:3d45c17b0d0d81cd9883f783840e013f}} asks whether models can build harnesses at all. The risk is the one section 7 named: a loop that tunes on the score it reports will overfit it, and in Prime Agent's Factorio run the self-refining harness found a cheating shortcut and saved it as a reusable skill: a self-modifying harness can make its own reward hack persistent.
