11. Choosing

Ask these in order; the first "no" is your answer.

  1. Does the material overflow one context, or is the work wide? If one agent can hold it and the steps depend on each other, stay single (section 2). If a bigger window fits it, try that before agents (section 3, Sonnet).
  2. Are the pieces independent, with nothing written in common? Parallel reading splits well; parallel writing of one artefact needs a contract first (section 7) or one writer.
  3. Do you know the split in advance? Then it is a workflow: split in code, fan out, merge in code (section 4). Use a lead only when finding the split is part of the job.
  4. Is the brief exact, and who checks the merged result? Name what does not count, and budget a verifier that does not share the workers' judgement (an answer key, tests, Lean, a person) before you add workers (section 5). Every parallel design here reported things outside the brief in some run, and same-model checks kept them all.
  5. Is the answer worth several times the tokens? On our audit the orchestrator cost about one agent; Anthropic reports about 15 times chat for its research system.
  6. Can you see every agent? Without per-agent traces you cannot tell which worker invented a finding. {{LLM observability|n:3c65c17b0d0d81fe82c0c6e9360afec4}} covers how.

For routing a conversation (support desks, intake), choose between a handoff and agents-as-tools by one question: should the specialist own the rest of the conversation, or answer one bounded question? Section 6 shows what each passes along.
