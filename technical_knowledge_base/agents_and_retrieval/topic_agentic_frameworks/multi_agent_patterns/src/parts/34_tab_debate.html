Debate lab: thirty puzzles, every call

The debate experiment of Reading section 8 in full MEASURED: knights-and-knaves puzzles with nine people each (generated with a fixed seed and checked by brute force to have exactly one solution; src/code/gen_puzzles.py), Claude Haiku 4.5 through claude -p --tools "" --system-prompt <solver>, default sampling and thinking, Claude Code 2.1.291, 6 October 2026. Per puzzle: five independent samples (s1 to s5); a second debate round in which solvers 1 to 3 each read the other two round-1 solutions and revise (d1 to d3, the protocol of Du et al.: three agents, two rounds); and one judge call that reads solutions 1 to 3 and picks (j). Every method below is computed from these same calls, so the comparison is paired.

Voting, one sample at a time

Expected accuracy of a majority vote over k of the five samples, averaged exactly over every subset of size k (ties go to the earliest of the most common answers). k = 1 is the average single sample. A vote of two can only break a disagreement by taking the first answer, so it is no better than one sample; odd numbers are what voting needs.
