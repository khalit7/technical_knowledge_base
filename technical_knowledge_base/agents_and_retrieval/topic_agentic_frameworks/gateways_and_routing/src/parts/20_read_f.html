9. OpenRouter: a gateway you rent, and a market of providers

What it is. OpenRouter is a hosted gateway: one OpenAI-compatible endpoint, one key and one bill in front of many model vendors and many inference providers. Its public catalogue listed models from model authors on 6 October 2026, of them free variants (GET /api/v1/models, no key needed). Money, from its FAQ the same day: it passes provider prices through with "no markup on inference pricing"; buying credits costs 5.5% ($0.80 minimum) by card and 5% in crypto; with your own provider keys (BYOK) "Pay-as-you-go includes $25,000 per month with no BYOK fee" and 5% above that; free models allow 50 requests a day, 1,000 once you have bought $10 of credits (FAQ). DOCS The parent page has its company news (the Stripe announcement); not repeated.

Two kinds of routing. Across models: send a models list and it tries them in order on errors, rate limits and downtime, billing the model that answered (the parent page quotes the details). Across providers of one model, the part with no LiteLLM equivalent: the same open-weights model is served by many companies at different prices, and by default OpenRouter will "Prioritize providers that have not seen significant outages in the last 30 seconds", then "For the stable providers, look at the lowest-cost candidates and select one weighted by inverse square of the price", then "Use the remaining providers as fallbacks" (provider routing). The provider object overrides it: order, only, ignore, allow_fallbacks (default true), require_parameters, data_collection, zdr, quantizations, sort (price, throughput or latency), max_price, preferred_min_throughput, preferred_max_latency; the suffixes :nitro and :floor mean sort by throughput and by price. Why it matters for an agent. "The same model" is not the same numbers. For Llama 3.3 70B Instruct, 11 endpoints on 6 October 2026 ranged from $0.10 to $1.04 per million input tokens, declared their weights as fp8, bf16, fp16 or nothing, and offered contexts from 12,288 to 131,072 tokens; under the rule above the two cheapest would take about two thirds of first picks. A benchmark or eval run through default routing mixes providers, and an agent's long prompt needs an endpoint whose context fits.
