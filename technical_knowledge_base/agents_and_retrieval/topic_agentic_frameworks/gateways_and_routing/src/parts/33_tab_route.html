1. Forty requests under each LiteLLM strategy

Section 2 of the Reading tab: one group, copy A answering in 0.2 s and copy B in 1.0 s, 40 requests four at a time. Each bar is one request, placed at the time it was sent and coloured by the copy that answered. Play to watch the strategy decide; switch strategy to compare on the same requests.

2. One model, many providers on OpenRouter

The endpoint lists OpenRouter published for four open-weights models on 6 October 2026 (GET /api/v1/models/{author}/{slug}/endpoints). The bars apply the documented default rule (inverse square of price) to whatever survives your filters, the way the provider options quantizations, a minimum context and max_price would narrow it. The price used is the input price; the docs do not say which price, nor how many lowest-cost candidates are kept, so read the shares as the rule's shape, not OpenRouter's exact numbers.

3. The router experiment, question by question

Section 10 of the Reading tab: 48 questions, the local 4B model and Claude Haiku 4.5, LiteLLM's complexity-router score for each question. Move the threshold: every question scoring above it goes to Haiku. The dot shows where that lands next to random routing and a perfect router.

Answers are the final "ANSWER:" line of each reply (click a row for the end of the full reply). Grading: a number is right if the first number in that line equals the answer ("2500 grams" counts for 2500); a word must match exactly. "no answer": the reply hit its 512-token allowance before an ANSWER line. Questions and answers were written for this page (src/code/r_tasks.py, seed 7).
