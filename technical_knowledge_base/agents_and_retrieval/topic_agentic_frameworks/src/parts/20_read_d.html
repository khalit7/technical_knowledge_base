10. How to choose

The default, argued by Anthropic's post and by HumanLayer's 12-factor agents SOURCE: start with direct API calls behind your gateway, and adopt a framework when a specific capability would otherwise be built by hand. A hand-built engine that runs a graph of model calls, such as the DAG engine Khalid wrote, is already the workflow half of this spectrum; what LangGraph would add to it is the checkpointer, interrupts and replay. Answer five questions; the rules behind the answer are printed with it.

Five answers pick a position on the axis; each rule names the section it comes from

The twelve factors, as titled in the repository, are a checklist for any position on the axis: 1 Natural language to tool calls; 2 Own your prompts; 3 Own your context window; 4 Tools are just structured outputs; 5 Unify execution state and business state; 6 Launch, pause, resume with simple APIs; 7 Contact humans with tool calls; 8 Own your control flow; 9 Compact errors into the context window; 10 Small, focused agents; 11 Trigger from anywhere, meet users where they are; 12 Make your agent a stateless reducer. Factor 4 is section 4's tool route, factor 6 is section 3's checkpoint, factor 10 is section 5's warning. A framework that hides your prompt (factor 2) or your loop (factor 8) must pay for it with something on this list.

Look at the request, whatever you choose
