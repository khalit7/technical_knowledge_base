One benchmark, seven numbers for the same product

LOCOMO (Maharana et al., {{arXiv 2402.17753|https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753}}, Feb 2024) is a set of long, two-person chats with questions; the score is the share an LLM judge marks correct (the "J score"). What the dispute shows
The same product, Zep, scored 58.44%, 65.99%, 75.14% and up to 80.32% on the same benchmark, depending on who configured it, which prompt and retrieval template they used, how many results were retrieved, and whether a category of questions with no reference answers was counted. Zep's first figure of 84% counted correct answers on the excluded category in the numerator but not in the denominator; Zep corrected it within a week. The full-context baseline (paste the whole chat in) scored 72.90% in Mem0's own paper, above both Mem0 variants: LOCOMO chats fit in a modern context window, so it tests retrieval less than its name suggests.

What the benchmarks test

LOCOMO

Paper: 50 conversations of about 300 turns and 9K tokens, up to 35 sessions. Public release: 10 conversations, 19 to 32 sessions and 369 to 689 turns each, 1,986 questions in five categories; 444 of the 446 adversarial questions have no reference answer, so every product evaluation drops that category (counted from {{locomo10.json|https://github.com/snap-research/locomo}}, 6 Oct 2026). No knowledge-update questions.

LongMemEval

500 questions over five abilities: information extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, knowledge updates, abstention (Wu et al., {{arXiv 2410.10813|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813}}, ICLR 2025). The S setting has about 115K tokens of history per question, M about 500 sessions and 1.5M tokens. Counts in the released file: temporal 133, multi-session 133, knowledge update 78, single-session user 70, assistant 56, preference 30; 30 are abstention variants.

DMR (Deep Memory Retrieval)

Introduced by MemGPT: 500 conversations of 5 sessions with up to a dozen messages each. Zep's own paper says each conversation "easily" fits in a context window, and the full-conversation baseline scores 94.4% with gpt-4-turbo, beside MemGPT 93.4% and Zep 94.8% ({{arXiv 2501.13956|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956}}).

MemoryAgentBench

Independent (UCSD, {{arXiv 2507.05257|https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257}}, v4 28 Jun 2026): feeds long inputs in chunks and tests accurate retrieval, test-time learning, long-range understanding and selective forgetting, all memory products on gpt-4o-mini. Mem0 21.1, Zep 24.0, MemGPT 28.3 overall, against plain BM25 41.5 and gpt-4o-mini with the whole input 42.2 (Table 3). The products ran as of 2025 and on document-style input, far from what they are tuned for; versions not stated.

How to read a memory benchmark claim
Ask five things: who ran it (vendor, rival, independent); does the whole history fit in the answer model's window (then full context is the baseline to beat, and it often wins); which answer and judge models (gpt-4o-mini in most rows above); how many memories were retrieved per question (Zep's own sweep went from 69.62% at 5 edges to 80.32% at 30); and which version of the product (Mem0's 2026 numbers are its managed platform, "which includes proprietary optimizations not available in the open-source SDK").
