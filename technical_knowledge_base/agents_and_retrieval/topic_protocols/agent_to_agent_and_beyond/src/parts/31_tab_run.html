The same evaluation job, recorded six ways. The first two are the before and after: Team A's agent driving Team B's tools over MCP, against Team A delegating the job to Team B's agent over A2A. The other four replay what happens to an A2A task when the stream drops, when updates go to a webhook, when the agent needs an authorization, and when the client cancels.

Try: play MCP, then A2A, and watch the last counter: what Team A's model has to read. Then play "stream dropped" and find the moment nobody is listening.

What to take from it


    

  • MCP against A2A: with MCP, every step of Team B's pipeline is a round trip that Team A's model plans and reads; with A2A, Team A sends the goal, answers one question, and reads the outcome. A2A moved more bytes (ids repeat in every event) but less text into the model.
    • 

  • Stream dropped: the task kept working with nobody listening; SubscribeToTask began with a snapshot that already held the missed shards.
    • 

  • Push: the client's request returned at once (submitted); seven POSTs to the webhook carried the rest.
    • 

  • In-task auth: the stream stayed open through auth-required; the grant was a separate request to the agent, not a message in the task.
    • 

  • Cancel: the cancel reply and a final canceled event on the still-open stream.
