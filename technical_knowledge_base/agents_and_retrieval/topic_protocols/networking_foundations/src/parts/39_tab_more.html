The best resources

{{High Performance Browser Networking: Building Blocks of TCP|https://hpbn.co/building-blocks-of-tcp/}}

Ilya Grigorik's free chapter: handshake cost, slow start, window over RTT, head-of-line blocking, with clear diagrams (2013, so before QUIC was standardised). About 35 min. Its {{UDP chapter|https://hpbn.co/building-blocks-of-udp/}} covers NAT traversal well. About 25 min.

{{ESnet fasterdata: Linux host tuning|https://fasterdata.es.net/host-tuning/linux/}}

Buffer sizing for long fat paths from the people who run a 100 Gbit/s science network. The reference for section 13. About 40 min.

{{Jacobson and Karels, Congestion Avoidance and Control (1988)|https://ee.lbl.gov/papers/congavoid.pdf}}

The paper that introduced slow start and RTT variance estimation after the 1986 collapses; still the best explanation of why. About 45 min.

{{man 7 tcp|https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/tcp.7.html}} and {{ip-sysctl|https://docs.kernel.org/networking/ip-sysctl.html}}

The exact meaning and default of every Linux knob in section 15. About 45 min, as a reference.

{{RFC 9293: TCP|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293}}

The 2022 consolidation of TCP's specification; sections 3.4 to 3.8 cover everything on this page. About 2 h 30 min.

{{RFC 9000: QUIC|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9000}} and {{RFC 9002|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9002}}

Readable for specifications; read sections 5 (connections), 8 (address validation), 9 (migration) and 12 to 14 of RFC 9000, then RFC 9002's loss detection. About 3 h.

{{EC2 instance network bandwidth|https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/ec2-instance-network-bandwidth.html}} and {{EC2 network MTU|https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/network_mtu.html}}

The per-flow caps and jumbo-frame rules that decide real transfer speeds on AWS. About 35 min.

{{NCCL environment variables|https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/nccl/user-guide/docs/env.html}}

What NCCL_SOCKET_IFNAME, the socket thread knobs and the IB settings do. About 30 min for the socket and network parts.

{{RFC 8985: RACK-TLP|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8985}}

How modern Linux detects loss by time and probes a silent tail, the mechanism behind section 4's streaming caveat. About 1 h.
