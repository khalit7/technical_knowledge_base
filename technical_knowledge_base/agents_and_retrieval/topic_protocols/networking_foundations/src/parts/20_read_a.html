Everything marked measured was run for this page on 5 October 2026 on an Apple M1 Pro laptop (macOS 27.0.1, Python 3.13.5, aioquic 1.3.0) on a home broadband line, either on loopback or against public test endpoints (read-only, a few tens of megabytes in all); illustrative marks a picture of a shape rather than data, and derived numbers show their formula.

Four protocols carry almost every byte you send: IP moves packets between machines, TCP turns them into a reliable byte stream, UDP hands them to programs with nothing added, and QUIC rebuilds TCP's guarantees (plus encryption) on top of UDP. When an LLM call is slow, a checkpoint upload crawls or a training job hangs at start-up, the cause is usually one of a dozen mechanisms in these four, and each leaves a fingerprint you can see with one command.

The running example. The root page follows one streaming request to an LLM API: a 277-byte HTTP request, answered with a stream of Server-Sent Events, one small write per token about 50 ms apart ({{the recorded request|#t-tcp}} is replayed segment by segment in the TCP timeline tab). Here we follow what TCP, UDP and IP do with those bytes, then scale up to the transfer that hurts most in ML work: moving a checkpoint of hundreds of gigabytes across a long path.
