TCP timeline: the running request, segment by segment

The root page's streaming request (277 bytes up; a 244-byte response head, then seven Server-Sent Events written at the times the root's recording measured: 2.4, 123.8, 175.1, 226.5, 278.0, 329.1 and 330.1 ms after the request arrived) replayed over a TCP connection with a round trip you choose. Time runs down the page, to scale. Each preset is a before/after pair: pick one, then flip between its two settings and watch the same input take a different path.

illustrative model with recorded inputs Rules: one-way delay is half the round trip; segments up to 1,448 bytes; the receiver ACKs every second segment or after 40 ms (Linux's minimum delayed ACK) and at once for out-of-order data ({{RFC 5681 s4.2|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5681#section-4.2}}); the sender retransmits on three duplicate ACKs ({{s3.2|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5681#section-3.2}}), after a tail-loss probe at two round trips ({{RFC 8985 s7|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8985#section-7}}), or when its timer fires at RTT + 200 ms, doubling after each expiry (Linux's 200 ms floor); Nagle holds a small write while data is unacknowledged ({{RFC 9293 s3.7.4|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293#section-3.7.4}}). No TLS (as on the root's plain port), no congestion window limits (seven small segments never reach the initial window of 10), no queueing. Real stacks differ in detail (Linux uses RACK time-based detection, SACK and more); the order of events and the size of each wait are the point.

Try. "Token 2 lost" with the timer only, then with fast retransmit: the duplicate ACKs from tokens 3, 4 and 5 trigger the repair, but tokens 3 onwards still wait in the client's buffer until it arrives (head-of-line blocking). "Last token lost": no later segments, no duplicate ACKs, so only the probe or the timer can notice. Nagle on at 100 ms: tokens leave in pairs, one pair per round trip. Set the round trip to 10 ms and Nagle barely matters, which is why it hides on fast networks and in tests.
