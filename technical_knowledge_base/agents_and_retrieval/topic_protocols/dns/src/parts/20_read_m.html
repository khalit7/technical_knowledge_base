11. Outages worth knowing

Each of these is a primary post-mortem, worth reading in full; the lesson is the part to remember.

WhenWhat happenedLesson
AWS us-east-1, 19 to 20 Oct 2025DynamoDB's DNS automation has a Planner that writes plans (which load balancers, which weights) and three Enactors, one per availability zone, that apply them to Route 53. One Enactor stalled while applying an old plan; a faster one applied the newest plan and then cleaned up old plans. The slow one woke, its "my plan is newer" check long stale, and overwrote the record with its old plan just as the clean-up deleted that plan, leaving dynamodb.us-east-1.amazonaws.com with an empty record and the automation unable to repair it. DNS impact from 11:48 PM PDT; all DNS information restored by 2:25 AM, clients recovering as cached records expired by 2:40 AM; other services, EC2 and load balancer health checks among them, took until 2:20 PM ({{AWS summary|https://aws.amazon.com/message/101925/}}).A control plane can race with itself; "no addresses" is a valid and catastrophic answer; recovery is floored by TTLs.
Cloudflare 1.1.1.1, 14 Jul 2025A configuration error made on 6 June tied the 1.1.1.1 prefixes to a service that was not yet in production. It did nothing for five weeks; on 14 July an unrelated change to that service triggered a global refresh that withdrew the resolver's addresses from the internet, from 21:52 to 22:54 UTC (62 minutes). A BGP hijack of 1.1.1.0/24 by another network became visible during the gap and was widely misreported as the cause ({{Cloudflare|https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-1-1-1-1-incident-on-july-14-2025/}}).A resolver is a single point of failure: configure two from different operators. Dormant configuration errors are the dangerous kind.
.de DNSSEC, 5 May 2026Signatures under the .de zone that validators could not check, for about three hours (section 9) ({{DENIC|https://blog.denic.de/en/analysis-of-the-dns-outage-on-5-may-2026/}}).DNSSEC turns a signing bug into a hierarchy-wide outage; negative trust anchors and serve-stale are the operator's tools.

corrected The old page gave the AWS DynamoDB impact as 14 h 32 min; that is closer to the whole event's span. The DNS problem itself ran 11:48 PM to about 2:25 AM PDT (2 h 37 min), with clients recovered by 2:40 AM as caches expired (2 h 52 min, the figure the root page uses).
