Long context instead of retrieval

If the model can read 32,768 tokens (Qwen3-1.7B and 4B natively, per their model cards; the 4B up to 131,072 with YaRN; frontier models a million or more), why retrieve at all? Two reasons, both measurable. Cost: every question pays to read the whole window, and prefill time grows faster than linearly with its length (on this laptop the 1.7B model takes about 0.26 s to start answering a 270-token prompt and about 9.3 s for a 7,600-token one, measured by the RAG in production child). Size: this small corpus is 1.34 million Qwen3 tokens; a window of 8,000 holds 0.6% of it. So "long context" below means the best case: the gold paragraphs are always placed in the window, with distractors around them.

Measured with Qwen3-4B: giving the model the question's own 10 paragraphs (2 gold, 8 distractors; about 1,470 prompt tokens) scores F1 0.576, level with the agent loop (0.574) at 60% of its tokens and 3.95 s against 6.15 s. That is the best case: the gold paragraphs are guaranteed to be there, which no real long-context deployment over a corpus can promise. Widen the window to about 8,000 tokens (the same gold paragraphs plus the corpus paragraphs nearest the question) and F1 falls to 0.475, below the five-paragraph pipeline, at 7,963 tokens and 28.8 s per question: more on-topic distractors made the same evidence harder to use. The 1.7B model shows the same fall (0.273 to 0.230), under the prompt caveat above.

Lost in the middle, on this model

Liu et al. (2023, "Lost in the Middle", arXiv 2307.03172) found that language models use information at the start and the end of a long prompt better than information in the middle. The test here: the same 20 paragraphs per question (the two gold paragraphs kept together as one block, plus the 18 corpus paragraphs bge-small ranks highest among the rest, so the distractors are on topic), with only the position of the gold block changing.

Predict: for the 4B model, where is accuracy lowest?
In the middle, for the 4B model (100 questions): F1 0.682 with the gold paragraphs first, 0.540 at positions 5 to 6, 0.554 at 10 to 11, 0.599 at 15 to 16 and 0.592 last. The start is clearly best; the dip and the partial recovery at the end are the "lost in the middle" shape, though the middle slots overlap in their intervals.

The 4B model reproduces the shape of Liu et al.'s curve at a smaller scale: a strong advantage for evidence at the very start (0.682) and a drop of 0.13 to 0.14 for evidence in the early middle, with only part of it recovered at the end (0.592). Liu et al. found GPT-3.5-Turbo's end position recovering more (63.2 against 75.8 at the start). The 1.7B model's curve (200 questions: 0.293, 0.260, 0.238, 0.294, 0.298) is flatter, with every interval overlapping, and it carries the answer-prompt failure, so it shows little either way. The practical rule is the same: when stuffing a window, put the best evidence first, and measure on your own model before trusting a long prompt to be read evenly.
