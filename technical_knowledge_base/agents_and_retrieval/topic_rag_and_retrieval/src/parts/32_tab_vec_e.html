Exact search, and why indexes approximate

Exact (flat) search compares the query with every stored vector and keeps the best k. It is always right and needs no index; its cost grows with the number of vectors times their size. Measured on this laptop on one core, it streams about 37 GB of vectors per second: SciFact's 21 MB takes about half a millisecond per claim, the 522,931 Quora questions (384 dims) 22 ms per query, and 1 million 1,024-dim vectors would take about 112 ms (child page, sections 2 and 12). Below roughly a million vectors, exact search is often fast enough and is the honest baseline.

Approximate nearest neighbour (ANN) indexes look at a small part of the data and accept that they will sometimes miss a true neighbour. Three ideas cover almost every index in use:

IVF: cluster, then search a few clusters

k-means splits the vectors into nlist cells; a query scans only the nprobe cells whose centres are nearest. Misses a neighbour that sits in a cell it did not open. Knob: nprobe.

PQ: compress, then compare codes

Each vector is cut into m chunks and each chunk replaced by the id of its nearest learned centroid, so 4,096 bytes become m bytes. Distances are estimated from the codes; the estimate reorders neighbours. Knobs: m, rescoring.

HNSW: walk a graph

Each vector is linked to a few near neighbours; a query starts somewhere and greedily walks toward closer vectors, keeping a frontier of efSearch candidates. Misses a neighbour the walk never reaches. Knobs: M, efSearch.

IVF-PQ combines the first two (the classic billion-scale design); DiskANN keeps a graph on SSD; filtered search, deletes and updates change all of them. Each is built, animated and measured on {{Vector indexes from the inside|n:3f85c17b0d0d81f29707c130b2ebcfb1}}.

The two recalls

Two different questions share one word. ANN recall@10 asks: of the 10 vectors exact search would return, how many did the index return? It needs no labels, measures only the index, and is what vector database benchmarks report. Retrieval recall@10 asks: of the documents a human marked relevant, how many are in the top 10? It needs labels and measures the whole system: the embedding model first, the index second. An index with ANN recall 1.0 cannot fix a model that puts the answer at rank 40.

Speed, memory, recall: the short version

Every index trades three things: how many true neighbours it returns, how many queries per second it serves, and how many bytes it holds per vector (plus build time).
