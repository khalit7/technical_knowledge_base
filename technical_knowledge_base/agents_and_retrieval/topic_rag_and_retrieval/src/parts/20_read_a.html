The idea in one screen

Retrieval-augmented generation answers from the passages a search puts in the prompt, so an answer can only use the evidence that survives every stage between the corpus and the model; each stage is a separate choice, measured on its own.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): before a language model answers, a search system finds the few passages of a document collection that bear on the question and pastes them into the prompt, so the model answers from text it can read and cite rather than from memory.

Fig. 1: one question's path, and the page that owns each stage

  1. Parse the documents. Index time, once per document: PDFs, HTML and office files become text, and the parser decides what can ever be found (MinerU keeps 94 of 107 evidence paragraphs intact; section 3). {{Document ingestion and multimodal retrieval|n:3f85c17b0d0d81279079f614f382f2e7}}.
  2. Cut them into chunks. A chunk is indexed on its own and is what retrieval returns; splitting on blank lines first keeps the evidence paragraph whole for 838 of 892 QASPER questions, fixed windows for 607. {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}}, section 1.
  3. Embed and count terms, then index. Each chunk becomes a vector (an embedding model) and a row of term counts (BM25's postings lists); the vectors go into an ANN index, where HNSW returns 0.991 of the exact top 10 on SciFact. {{Embedding models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81508f99e1fa78c2bb41}}; {{Vector indexes|n:3f85c17b0d0d81f29707c130b2ebcfb1}}.
  4. A question arrives; rewrite it if it needs it. Query time, once per question: a chat follow-up becomes a standalone question; HyDE lifts Qwen3-Embedding from 0.700 to 0.718 on SciFact. The tower is the 188 SciFact claims with annotated evidence that the next three steps follow. {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}}, section 5.
  5. First stage: search both indexes, fuse. BM25 and dense search run side by side with the filters inside each, and their lists merge by reciprocal rank fusion into a shortlist of 30: 186 of the 188 claims have their evidence in it. {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}}, sections 2 to 6.
  6. Rerank the top 30. A cross-encoder reads the question with each candidate and keeps the best 3: 178 kept. {{Rerankers and late interaction|n:3f85c17b0d0d813fba53fc883f8a2c8f}}.
  7. Prompt, generate, cite. The top 3 abstracts go into the prompt with their ids; the generator gives the right label and cites an evidence abstract for 96 of 188. The tallest drop is here, at generation (section 4). {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}}, sections 8 and 9.
  8. Around the path. Three axes the other children own: who decides what is read (the model in a loop, a router, nobody), on {{Agentic retrieval, long context and memory|n:3c65c17b0d0d8105a68cfb504ed4b0e8}}; structure built before the question (a graph, a table), on {{GraphRAG and structured retrieval|n:3f85c17b0d0d81e2bb4cdbb749e48a5a}}; and what changes once users arrive (freshness, access, latency, cost), on {{RAG in production|n:3f85c17b0d0d81aa9ba9c0ceac9e28c1}}. Sections 5 and 6.

The towers follow the 188 SciFact claims that have annotated evidence through one pipeline: BM25 and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B fused by reciprocal rank fusion, Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B on the top 30, the top 3 abstracts to Qwen3-1.7B, which must give the right label and cite an evidence abstract (a deterministic check, no judge) measured on Apple M1 Pro. The first two stages almost never lose the evidence (186 of 188 in the top 30, 178 kept in the top 3); the generator gets 96 right. New terms

first stage: the search over the whole corpus that keeps a shortlist of K candidates.
reranker: a slower model that re-scores only the shortlist.
generator: the language model that writes the answer from the top k passages.
chunk: a piece of a document indexed on its own; the unit retrieval returns.
BM25: the standard keyword score over an inverted index (each term's list of documents).
bi-encoder: an embedding model that encodes query and document separately, so document vectors are computed ahead of time (dense retrieval).
cross-encoder: a model that reads query and document together and outputs one score; accurate, nothing to precompute.
hybrid search: BM25 and dense search run side by side and their lists merged, usually by reciprocal rank fusion (RRF: each list adds 1 / (60 + rank)).
ANN index: approximate nearest-neighbour search that reads a small part of the vectors and sometimes misses a true neighbour.
recall@K: the share of a question's relevant passages in the top K; with one relevant passage, "found or not".
nDCG@10: a 0 to 1 score of how high the relevant passages sit in the top 10 (each earns its grade divided by log2(rank + 1), normalised by the best possible order).
golden set: real questions, each with the passages a human marked as answering it.

The running example. Every page of this topic measures on the same public data, models and machine, so numbers can be compared across pages. The main set is SciFact: 5,183 scientific abstracts and 300 test claims (short statements such as "Activation of PPM1D suppresses p53 function"), each with the abstracts human annotators marked as evidence. NFCorpus (3,633 medical documents, many relevant per query), QASPER (281 long NLP papers, for cutting documents into pieces) and HotpotQA (200 questions that need two Wikipedia paragraphs each, over 9,760 paragraphs, for multi-step search) cover what SciFact cannot. Everything runs locally on an Apple M1 Pro laptop with open models (four embedding models, three rerankers, Qwen3 1.7B and 4B as the answering model), with no paid APIs. Numbers are labelled: measured means produced on this topic's pages in October 2026, vendor a figure a company publishes about its own product, published a paper or independent benchmark, derived computed from other numbers with the formula shown, and illustrative an input chosen for teaching. The definition of the running example, with every model revision and its baseline scores against BEIR and MTEB, is in this page's src/data/README.md; the topic's shared runs, which the child pages build on, are recorded under src/pipe, src/vec and src/agent.
