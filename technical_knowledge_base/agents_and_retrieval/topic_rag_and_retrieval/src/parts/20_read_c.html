3. Vectors: embeddings, similarity, training

The problem. The claim "Mitochondria are uninvolved in apoptosis" should find an abstract about cytochrome c release triggering programmed cell death, which shares almost none of its words. Matching meaning needs a representation of meaning that can be compared fast.

How it works. An embedding model is a neural network that reads a text and outputs a fixed-length list of numbers, a vector (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B outputs 1,024; bge-small 384). It is trained so that a query and the passages that answer it land close together. Each number means nothing alone; only distances carry information. Dense retrieval embeds every document once, embeds the query at search time, and returns the nearest documents. Because query and document are encoded separately, document vectors are computed ahead of time: this kind of model is a bi-encoder. (A cross-encoder, section 5, reads the two together: more accurate, nothing to precompute.)

Similarity. Three ways to compare vectors q and d: the dot product Σ qidi (larger is closer, and it grows with the vectors' lengths), cosine (the dot product divided by both lengths: only the angle counts) and L2 distance |q − d| (smaller is closer). If every vector has length 1, then cosine = dot and |q − d|2 = 2 − 2 cos, so all three give the same order. Most models normalise their output; gte-modernbert-base does not. On its raw vectors, SciFact nDCG@10 is 0.767 by cosine, 0.767 by L2 and 0.740 by dot product: the dot product silently loses 0.027 because it favours long vectors measured, Vector search lab. Normalise once at write time, then any of the three is the same search.

Scores are not probabilities, and not comparable across models. On one batch of eight SciFact training claims and their abstracts, bge-small's cosines run from 0.43 to 0.79 and Qwen3-Embedding's from 0.14 to 0.70; the median cosine of random pairs is 0.563 for bge-small and 0.097 for MiniLM measured. "Keep results above 0.7" means something different for each model, and changes silently when you swap models. Thresholds are tuned per model on labelled data.

How embedding models are trained

Pairs of (query, passage that answers it) are collected: questions and answers, titles and bodies, search queries and clicked pages, and, in modern recipes, pairs written by a language model ({{Qwen3-Embedding's report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05176}} describes about 150 million synthetic pairs in a first stage vendor). The loss is InfoNCE: for each query, a softmax over its similarity to its own passage and to a line-up of wrong ones, and the loss is minus the log-probability of the right one. It is a classification where the classes are the candidates.

Li = − log [ exp(cos(qi, di+) / τ) / Σj exp(cos(qi, dj) / τ) ]
di+
the passage that answers query i
dj
every candidate: the positive, the other queries' positives in the batch (in-batch negatives, free because they are already embedded) and any hard negatives (mined passages that look relevant but are not)
τ
temperature: small values make small cosine gaps matter ({{SimCSE|https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821}} used 0.05)

Bigger batches mean a harder line-up: bge-small's positive outscores all in-batch negatives for 97.9% of SciFact training claims at batch size 8 and for 82.8% at 512 measured. Hard negatives teach fine distinctions but carry a trap, the false negative: for 38.8% of SciFact's training claims (bge-small) the corpus holds an unlabelled abstract that outscores the labelled one, and some of those are relevant abstracts nobody labelled. Training on them pushes right answers away. The general theory of contrastive losses is on {{Contrastive and self-supervised learning|n:3c65c17b0d0d811db6a7d02d0ee7215c}}; the loss computed live on a real batch is in the {{Vector search lab|#t-vec}}; fine-tuning a model on your own data belongs to the {{Embedding models|n:3c65c17b0d0d81508f99e1fa78c2bb41}} child.

Query instructions. Many models expect a prefix on queries only (bge-small: "Represent this sentence for searching relevant passages: "; Qwen3-Embedding: "Instruct: task\nQuery:"). Measure it: bge-small's prefix is within noise here (SciFact 0.720 without, 0.713 with; NFCorpus 0.337 without, 0.343 with), while dropping Qwen3-Embedding's instruction costs 2.1 points on SciFact and 6.6 on NFCorpus measured, Embedding models child.

Which model: what MTEB measures, and what it does not

MTEB (Massive Text Embedding Benchmark) scores embedding models on dozens of tasks; its retrieval tasks use nDCG@10, mostly on BEIR datasets used zero-shot (the model never trained on them). The headline is an average over task types, many models trained on the benchmarks' training splits (Qwen3-Embedding declares HotpotQA and FEVER, so its HotpotQA results here are in-domain, not zero-shot), and gaps under a point are noise. What transfers. Model size is not a ranking: the 149 M gte-modernbert beats the 0.6 B Qwen3-Embedding on SciFact (0.767 against 0.700), Qwen3 wins NFCorpus, and bge-small (33 M) wins the multi-hop set. Cost scales with size: per document, Qwen3-Embedding indexes about 20 times slower than bge-small here (6.4 against 144 documents per second on MPS) measured, roughly its parameter ratio, on any hardware. Shortlist on the retrieval tasks closest to your domain, then measure nDCG@10 and recall@100 on a few hundred of your own labelled queries.
