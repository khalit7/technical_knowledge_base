Common mistakes, and interview questions

Using dot product on vectors the model did not normalise
Choosing the embedding model from a leaderboard average
On SciFact the 149 M gte-modernbert beats the 0.6 B Qwen3-Embedding (0.767 against 0.700); on NFCorpus Qwen3 wins (0.363 against 0.343). An average over dozens of tasks hides both. Build a labelled set from your own queries (a few hundred is enough to separate models this far apart) and measure.
Reusing a similarity threshold across models
bge-small puts unrelated pairs near 0.5 and good matches near 0.75; Qwen3-Embedding puts unrelated pairs near 0.2. "Keep results above 0.7" means different things for each, and changes silently when you upgrade the model. Tune thresholds on labelled data, per model.
Mixing vectors from two models in one index
Vectors from different models live in unrelated spaces even when the dimension matches. The production child page measured a half-migrated index (MiniLM to bge-small, both 384-d): nDCG@10 fell to 0.374 to 0.379 from 0.645 and 0.713 (prd, measured). Re-embed everything, then switch.
Truncating a model that was not trained for it
Halving bge-small's 384 dimensions loses 6% of its nDCG on SciFact; a quarter loses 19%. Qwen3-Embedding, trained with Matryoshka, loses 2% at half and 5% at a quarter. Check the model card, then measure.
Binary quantization without rescoring
Sign bits alone keep only 67% of the full top 10 for Qwen3 on SciFact (nDCG 0.680 against 0.700). Rescoring the top 100 with the float vectors brings back 99% of it and the full nDCG. Keep the originals somewhere cheaper and rescore.
Reporting ANN recall as if it were retrieval quality
An index can return 100% of the exact top 10 while the system finds only 84.5% of the relevant abstracts, because the embedding model already ranks the rest below 10. Report both: ANN recall to tune the index, retrieval recall and nDCG to judge the system.
Reading clusters and distances off a 2-D picture
PCA kept 10.5% of the variance of these vectors and placed between 0 and 3 of each claim's 10 true neighbours among its 10 nearest dots; t-SNE kept neighbourhoods but invents the distances between clusters. Use maps to explore, never to evaluate.
Treating mined hard negatives as certainly negative
For about 39% of SciFact train claims an unlabelled abstract outscores the labelled positive. Some are real model errors, some are unlabelled positives. Filter (Qwen3-Embedding masks negatives that score more than 0.1 above the positive) or check a sample by hand before fine-tuning on them.

Interview questions, short answers

What is an embedding, and why does retrieval with it work?
A fixed-length vector a trained network assigns to a text. Contrastive training on (query, relevant text) pairs makes the cosine between a query and its relevant texts larger than with other texts, so nearest-neighbour search in that space returns relevant texts even when they share no words with the query.
Cosine, dot product or L2?
Use what the model was trained with. If vectors are normalised, all three give the same order, and the dot product is cheapest. If not, the dot product favours long vectors and L2 favours short ones; cosine is the safe default. Normalise once at write time.
Explain in-batch negatives and hard negatives.
InfoNCE asks each query to pick its positive among candidates. In-batch negatives reuse the other pairs' documents in the same batch (free; harder as the batch grows). Hard negatives are mined documents that look relevant but are not; they teach fine distinctions but include false negatives, which have to be filtered.
We have 100 million 1,024-dim vectors. How do you fit them in memory?
float32 is 410 GB. Options, combined: a Matryoshka model cut to 256 or 512 dims (measure the loss); int8 (4 times smaller, almost no loss with a float query) or binary codes (32 times smaller) with the float originals on SSD for rescoring the top 100 to 400; or IVF-PQ / DiskANN, which keep compressed codes in memory and full vectors on disk. Measure recall at the target size, not on a small sample.
Your vector search has 0.98 recall but users still get bad results. Where do you look?
That 0.98 is ANN recall against exact search. Measure retrieval recall@K on labelled queries with exact search: if it is low, the problem is the embedding model, chunking or query (prompt prefix, domain mismatch), not the index. Then check whether hybrid search or a reranker fixes the misses (Pipeline lab).
When is exact search enough?
When the scan fits the latency budget: on one core here, about 0.5 ms for 5,000 vectors of 1,024 dims and about 112 ms for a million. Exact search is also the right choice after a restrictive filter, and as the ground truth for every ANN benchmark.

Method and limits

  • Data and models: the root's running example (SciFact test, 300 claims; the setup's stored, normalised vectors of four models). The two recalls, the before/after lists and the Matryoshka and quantization grid are new runs on those vectors (FAISS 1.15.1 on CPU, one thread for builds, k-means seed fixed; deterministic, no lock). The model comparison comes from the setup's records of record (quality: pytrec_eval; speed: three runs on MPS, load average 1.5 to 11.8 on a shared machine).
  • Re-embedding: only the raw gte-modernbert vectors for the cosine, dot and L2 section (SciFact corpus and 300 claims, MPS, bench lock held).
  • Reused from the Vector indexes child: the Quora frontier (its results.json) and two cross-checks (SQ8 and binary agreement at 1,024 dims).
  • Not run here: a fine-tune of an embedding model (it belongs to the Embedding models child page); DiskANN, ScaNN and filtered search (on the child page); hosted embedding models (no API keys by design).
  • Projection: the 2-D map uses a 705-point sample (seed 0), so trustworthiness and neighbour counts are for that sample.
  • Contrastive section: cosines from the trained models; the loss is computed in the page from them, not from a training run.
  • Scripts: src/vec/ of this page (mrl_quant.py, two_recalls.py, proj.py, contrastive.py, metric.py, models.py, frontier.py, then make_data.py); check_numbers.py checks that the page embeds exactly these results and that the numbers written in the prose match them.
