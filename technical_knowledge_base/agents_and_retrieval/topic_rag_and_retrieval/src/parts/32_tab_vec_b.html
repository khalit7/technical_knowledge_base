Cosine, dot product, L2: comparing two vectors

The problem. "Nearest" needs a definition. Three are in common use, every vector database asks you to pick one, and picking the wrong one is silent: the search still returns ten results.

dot(q, d) = Σi qi di    cos(q, d) = dot(q, d) / (|q| |d|)    L2(q, d) = |q − d|
|q|
the length (norm) of q, √Σ qi2
dot
larger is closer; grows with the lengths of both vectors
cos
larger is closer; only the angle counts (from −1 to 1)
L2
smaller is closer; |q − d|2 = |q|2 + |d|2 − 2 dot(q, d)

Why normalising makes them agree. If every vector has length 1, then cos = dot and |q − d|2 = 2 − 2 cos, so all three put the documents in the same order. Most embedding models normalise their output (three of the four here have a Normalize step in their sentence-transformers configuration), and the setup stored all four normalised, which is why every other section of this page can use the dot product as cosine. The engine-side view of the same identity, and the pgvector operators for each metric, are on {{Search and vector databases|n:3ef5c17b0d0d815eb4caef9b4cd4d732}}.

When they disagree. gte-modernbert-base has no Normalize step: encode() returns vectors of different lengths. Below, the SciFact corpus and the 300 claims were embedded again without normalising, and ranked three ways. Toggle "normalise" to see the three lists become one.

Which embedding model: quality, size, speed

The problem. Leaderboards rank hundreds of models on averages over dozens of tasks. What matters is your corpus, your queries, your latency budget and your memory. The four models below span the common choices: a 22 M-parameter tutorial model, a 33 M classic, a 149 M modern encoder, and a 0.6 B decoder turned embedder.

How to read it. Quality is nDCG@10 (how high the relevant abstracts sit in the top 10; 1 is perfect) and recall@100 (share of relevant abstracts anywhere in the top 100) on the test queries. Size is what the index stores per document (dims × 4 bytes as float32) and the model's weights. Speed is documents per second when indexing (paid once per corpus) and milliseconds per query (paid on every request). Pick a column for the bars.

What transfers
The ordering of these four is a fact about SciFact and NFCorpus, not about the models: on HotpotQA the order changes again (bge-small 0.860 nDCG@10, gte 0.857, Qwen3 0.825 on the multi-hop set of the running example), and Qwen3-Embedding declares HotpotQA and FEVER among its training data, so it is not zero-shot there (Tool map, MTEB notes). The ratios of cost transfer better: per document, the 0.6 B decoder costs about 20 times a 33 M encoder here (6.4 against 144 documents per second), roughly in proportion to its parameter count (18 times), which holds on any hardware. Absolute milliseconds do not transfer: a server GPU is much faster and batches concurrent queries (not run here).
