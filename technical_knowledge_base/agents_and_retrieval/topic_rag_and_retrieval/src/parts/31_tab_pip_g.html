Common mistakes, and interview questions

Comparing chunkers at a fixed number of chunks
Bigger chunks send more text, so they win at a fixed k almost by definition (512-token chunks: 61.4% at the top 3 with BM25). Compare at the same token budget, where they lose (45.9% at 1,024 tokens against 64.5% for paragraph-aware chunks).
Cutting on token counts and ignoring the document's structure
Fixed 256-token windows keep an evidence paragraph intact for 68.0% of QASPER questions; splitting on paragraphs first (the recursive rule, or paragraphs packed within sections) keeps 93.3 to 93.9%. Overlap is a weak patch: 32 tokens of overlap move fixed windows from 61.2% to 68.0%.
Assuming hybrid always helps
Equal-weight RRF helped every model whose quality is close to BM25's and hurt the two that beat BM25 clearly on SciFact (gte-modernbert 0.767 to 0.763, ColBERT-small 0.743 to 0.731). Measure on your data; if one retriever dominates, weight it.
Tuning the fusion weight, k, or the rerank depth on the test set
It looks like progress and is a leak. Hold out queries (SciFact train here) to pick the knob, then report the test score once.
Filtering after the search
With a selective filter the post-filter quietly returns fewer results or none (1.73 of 10 on average at 10% visibility, 14 empty answers of 300). Filter inside the search, or over-fetch and check how many survive.
Adding a reranker without checking it beats the first stage
The 22 M MiniLM cross-encoder trained on MS MARCO adds nothing to Qwen3-Embedding on SciFact (0.700 at K 100) and pulls the hybrid list down as K grows (0.724 to 0.689 at K 100). Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B on the top 30 lifts the same lists to 0.765 (dense) and 0.778 (hybrid).
Reporting one accuracy number
A label can be right without the evidence: on SciFact two thirds of the labelled claims are SUPPORT, so a model that always says SUPPORT scores 66% "accuracy". Check the citation too, and split the failures into retrieval, ranking and generation.

Interview questions, short answers

Users say the RAG system gives wrong answers. How do you find the broken stage?
Build a golden set of questions with the passages that answer them. For each, log the first stage's top K, the reranked top k and the answer. Count: evidence not in top K (retrieval), in K but not in k (ranking), in the prompt but answered wrong (generation). Fix the biggest bucket first; give the generator the gold passages (an oracle run) to get the ceiling of g.
Should we add BM25 to our vector search?
Probably, if queries carry identifiers, rare names or domain terms, and the dense model is not far ahead of BM25 on your data. Start with RRF (k 60, no tuning); if one retriever dominates, use a weighted fusion with the weight chosen on held-out queries. Measure per query wins and losses, not just the mean.
How do you choose a chunk size?
Split on the document's own structure first (sections, paragraphs), then cap the size. Measure on questions with located evidence: share of evidence intact in one chunk, and retrieval at an equal token budget, not at an equal number of chunks. Smaller chunks rank more precisely; bigger chunks carry more context per hit.
How many candidates should the reranker see?
Sweep K on a golden set against latency. The first stage's recall@K is the ceiling: a reranker cannot recover what it is not given. Past the point where recall@K flattens, extra K costs time and gives a weak reranker more chances to promote wrong documents.
Pre-filter or post-filter for access control?
Inside the search (pre-filter), always for access control: a post-filter can return fewer results than asked, or none, and its behaviour depends on how selective the filter is for that user. With an ANN index, check the engine's filtered-search strategy and measure recall with a restrictive filter.
Our reranker made nDCG worse. How is that possible?
It is weaker than the first stage on this domain (a small model trained on web search reordering a strong embedder's list), or it was given a deep list it then reorders badly. Check reranker-alone quality, and sweep K.

How this was measured, and the limits

  • Machine: Apple M1 Pro (8 performance and 2 efficiency cores, 16 GB unified memory), PyTorch MPS and llama.cpp (Metal), October 2026. Shared with other agents: every timing carries its load average in the scripts' records; the ratios between stages transfer to a server, the milliseconds do not (a datacenter GPU runs the encoders and rerankers many times faster, and servers batch concurrent queries).
  • Relevance numbers (nDCG@10, recall) are deterministic: pytrec_eval over the recorded top-100 runs; the same scripts reproduce them exactly. Generator outputs are greedy (temperature 0) and cached by prompt.
  • Running example and baselines: datasets, models, revisions and the baseline table against BEIR and MTEB are defined once for the whole page (the root's src/data/README.md); every baseline here matches it.
  • Scripts: src/pipe/ in the repository (fusion, filters, chunking, query rewriting, reranking, end to end, latency); make_data.py builds everything this tab draws from their outputs.
  • Generator: a 1.7 B model. Its g is far below a frontier model's; the shape (which factor dominates, what a reranker changes) transfers, the absolute accuracy does not.
  • Not run here: see the notes under each section.
Not run here, and why
  • Late chunking (Günther et al. 2024, arXiv 2409.04701: embed the whole document once, then pool the token vectors of each chunk): it needs a long-context embedder trained with mean pooling. The running example's long-context embedders pool differently (gte-modernbert uses the CLS token, Qwen3-Embedding the last token of a causal model), so a late-chunking run would measure a pooling the models were never trained for.
  • Generator-written context on every paper: about 30 s of generation per paper here, so it ran on every fourth paper (71 of 281); the comparison uses the same papers with and without the context.
  • A larger generator: the 4 B model files were missing from the shared model store while this tab's generator runs were scheduled (a temporary-file cleaner had removed them; they were restored later), so every answer here is the 1.7 B model through llama.cpp. A 4 B or frontier model would move g, not r or p.
  • Judged answer quality (faithfulness, groundedness with an LLM judge): SciFact's labels and evidence ids allow an exact check, and judged metrics belong to {{RAG and retrieval evaluation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8194a914d1843ee26f13}}.
  • Filtered approximate search (HNSW with a restrictive filter): the {{Vector search lab|#t-vec}}.
