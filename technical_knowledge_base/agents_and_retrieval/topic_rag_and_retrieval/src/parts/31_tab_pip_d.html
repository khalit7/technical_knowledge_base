BM25, dense, and hybrid

The problem. BM25 matches words: it finds "PPM1D" when the claim says "PPM1D", and misses an abstract that says the same thing in other words. A dense retriever matches meaning: it finds the paraphrase, and can rank a topically similar abstract above the one with the exact gene name. Their mistakes are different, so running both and merging the two lists (hybrid search) often beats either. How BM25's inverted index and a vector index sit inside a database is on {{Search and vector databases|n:3ef5c17b0d0d815eb4caef9b4cd4d732}}; the defaults each framework ships with are in the Tool map's defaults section (LlamaIndex returns only 2 chunks unless told otherwise).

How the lists are merged. The two scores live on different scales (a BM25 score of 4.45 against a cosine of 0.71), so they cannot simply be added. Two families:

  • Reciprocal rank fusion (RRF) ignores scores and uses ranks: score(d) = Σlists 1 / (k + rank(d)), with k = 60 in the paper that proposed it (Cormack, Clarke and Büttcher, SIGIR 2009) and in most libraries. No tuning, no calibration.
  • Score fusion normalises each list's scores (min-max to [0, 1], or z-score: subtract the mean, divide by the standard deviation) and takes a weighted sum, α · dense + (1 − α) · BM25. It keeps how much better the first hit is than the second, but α must be chosen, and choosing it on the test set is a leak.

What the numbers say
  • Hybrid helps a dense model that is about as good as BM25 and disagrees with it. On SciFact, RRF lifts MiniLM-L6 from 0.645 to 0.713 nDCG@10 and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B from 0.700 to 0.724 (better on 69 claims, worse on 50).
  • It can hurt a dense model that is clearly better. gte-modernbert (0.767) drops to 0.763 with RRF k 60, ColBERT-small (0.743) to 0.731: equal-weight fusion gives BM25's weaker list the same say.
  • k barely matters (0.724 to 0.732 for Qwen3 across k from 1 to 1,000), which is why 60 is a safe default.
  • Weighted score fusion beats RRF when its weight is learned on other queries. With α chosen on SciFact's 809 training claims (0.6), z-score fusion scores 0.739 for Qwen3 on the test claims; the best α picked on the test set itself would claim 0.744, a 0.005 leak. On NFCorpus, RRF adds nothing to Qwen3 (0.363 to 0.361) while z-score with α tuned on the dev split gives 0.375.
