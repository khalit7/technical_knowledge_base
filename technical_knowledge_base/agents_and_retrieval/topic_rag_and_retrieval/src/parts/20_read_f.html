6. Where an answer gets lost, and how to measure it

The golden set. Every number above needs a golden set: real questions, each with the passages a human marked as answering it (and, for judging answers, a reference answer). A few hundred real, labelled questions separate systems that differ by a few points; synthetic questions written from a chunk are easy by construction, because they reuse the chunk's words. Version the set, re-label when the corpus is re-chunked, choose every knob (fusion weight, rerank depth, prompt) on a separate tuning split, and report the test split once. SciFact here: 809 training claims for tuning, 300 test claims for reporting.

Retrieval metrics, per question then averaged (computed here with pytrec_eval, the TREC tool BEIR uses):

  • recall@K: the share of a question's relevant passages in the top K. With one relevant passage (277 of 300 SciFact claims) it is simply "found or not".
  • precision@k: the share of the top k that is relevant; irrelevant chunks in the prompt distract the generator.
  • MRR (mean reciprocal rank): 1 / rank of the first relevant passage, averaged.
  • nDCG@10 (normalised discounted cumulative gain): each relevant passage in the top 10 earns its relevance grade divided by log2(rank + 1), and the sum is divided by the best possible sum, so 1 is a perfect order. It rewards putting the right passage first, which is what a reranker is for.

Measure the first stage's recall at the depth the reranker reads (K), not only at the k that reaches the prompt: a reranker cannot rescue what it was never given, and the two depths separate a retrieval miss from a ranking miss. The formulas, a calculator on real BEIR runs and the LLM-judged metrics (faithfulness, answer relevance, context precision and recall) belong to {{RAG and retrieval evaluation|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8194a914d1843ee26f13}}; judge design and bias to {{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}}.

The decomposition. A question is answered correctly for the right reason only if its evidence survives three stages in a row, so end-to-end accuracy over N questions factors as A = rK × pk|K × g (section 0), and the N(1 − A) failures split into three buckets that add up exactly:

Mretrieval = N(1 − r)   Mranking = N r (1 − p)   Mgeneration = N r p (1 − g)
Mretrieval
evidence never reached the reranker: fix chunking, hybrid search, query rewriting, or raise K
Mranking
reached the reranker, not the prompt: a better reranker, or a larger k
Mgeneration
in the prompt, answered wrongly: the model, the prompt, the context format, abstention instructions

It assumes the answer needs its evidence; an answer right from the model's own memory is not counted as right, which is why the SciFact check also requires a cited evidence abstract (4 claims got the right label with no evidence in the prompt and were refused measured). Without the citation check a label is cheap: two thirds of the labelled claims are SUPPORT, so always answering SUPPORT would score 66%.

On the measured run the generation bucket is 82 of 92 failures: no retrieval change can buy more than 10 answers, while the oracle run (gold abstracts only, 112 of 188) shows how much a better prompt or model could. On the old example the ranking bucket was the largest. The point of measuring the factors is that the instinct ("try a bigger model") is right in one case and wrong in the other. How to debug one bad answer: log, per question, the first stage's top K, the reranked top k and the answer; ask first "was the evidence in the prompt?", then "was it in the top K?"; that one or two bits assign the failure to a stage before anyone touches a component.
