5. A tiny fine-tune, measured

The question. Does in-domain contrastive training help a small general model on SciFact, and does the choice of negatives matter once false negatives are counted? bge-small-en-v1.5 (33 M parameters) trained on the @@n_pairs@@ SciFact train pairs for @@tr_epochs@@ epochs, InfoNCE at τ = 0.05, four arms at batch 32: in-batch negatives only; +BM25 one BM25-mined negative per pair; +dense one bge-small-mined negative; +dense, filtered the same after the 95% filter. Plus in-batch only at batch 8 and 128. Three seeds each; the spread across seeds is the noise floor. Deliberately tiny: it shows the mechanics and their size on one corpus, not a production recipe.

Every arm beats the starting model; the arms differ by about a point, the seeds by less

nDCG@10 per arm, every seed a dot, the starting model as a dashed line; SciFact above, NFCorpus below (a domain it never trained on).
@@tr_reading@@

How to read a small difference: one model's nDCG@10 on 300 claims carries a 95% interval about ±@@ci_half@@ wide (section 11), but two runs on the same claims are compared with a paired bootstrap, which is much tighter; seeds add their own spread, which only repeats reveal. A difference smaller than the spread across seeds is not a finding.

What training does to the vectors. Twelve SciFact test claims (never trained on), each with its labelled abstract, its three highest-scoring unlabelled abstracts under the starting model, and twelve random abstracts, re-encoded every 4 training steps. The picture is exact, not a projection: each abstract sits at distance 1 − cosine from the claim at the centre, at a fixed angle, so moving in means "more similar". Predict before stepping: does the labelled abstract move closer to the claim?

Training pushes everything apart; what retrieval needs is the gap, not the cosine

All twelve claims at this step. Margin = cosine to the labelled abstract minus the highest cosine to any other abstract in the set; positive means the labelled abstract wins.
