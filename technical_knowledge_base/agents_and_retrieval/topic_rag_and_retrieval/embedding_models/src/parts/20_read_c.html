5. A tiny fine-tune, measured

The question. Does in-domain contrastive training help a small general model on SciFact, and does the choice of negatives matter once false negatives are counted? bge-small-en-v1.5 (33 M parameters) trained on the @@n_pairs@@ SciFact train pairs for @@tr_epochs@@ epochs, InfoNCE at τ = 0.05, four arms at batch 32: in-batch negatives only; +BM25 one BM25-mined negative per pair; +dense one bge-small-mined negative; +dense, filtered the same after the 95% filter. Plus in-batch only at batch 8 and 128. Three seeds each; the spread across seeds is the noise floor. Deliberately tiny: it shows the mechanics and their size on one corpus, not a production recipe.

@@tr_reading@@

How to read a small difference: one model's nDCG@10 on 300 claims carries a 95% interval about ±@@ci_half@@ wide (section 11), but two runs on the same claims are compared with a paired bootstrap, which is much tighter; seeds add their own spread, which only repeats reveal. A difference smaller than the spread across seeds is not a finding.

What training does to the vectors. Twelve SciFact test claims (never trained on), each with its labelled abstract, its three highest-scoring unlabelled abstracts under the starting model, and twelve random abstracts, re-encoded every 4 training steps. Fig. 4 is exact, not a projection: each abstract sits at distance 1 − cosine from the claim at the centre, at a fixed angle (the angle only groups the kinds), so moving in means "more similar". Predict before it plays: does the labelled abstract move closer to the claim?

  1. Before training. The starting bge-small: the labelled abstract (green, mean cosine @@tl_pos_0@@ over the 12 claims) and the three hardest unlabelled ones (red, @@tl_neg_0@@) crowd the claim; the other claims' abstracts (blue) and random ones (grey, @@tl_rand_0@@) sit farther out.
  2. Steps 4 to @@tl_step4@@: everything moves out. The first updates spread the whole space: random abstracts fall to @@tl_rand_4@@, but the labelled abstract also falls, to @@tl_pos_4@@, and the hard ones to @@tl_neg_4@@. The far abstracts move furthest.
  3. Steps @@tl_step5@@ to @@tl_stepe@@: settling. The labelled abstract stops moving (@@tl_pos_e@@ at the end) while the hard negatives keep receding (@@tl_neg_e@@) and the random ones reach @@tl_rand_e@@.
  4. The gap, not the cosine. The green ring is the labelled abstract's cosine, the red ring the nearest other abstract's; retrieval needs green inside red. The mean gap between the labelled and the hard abstracts grows from @@tl_gap0@@ to @@tl_gape@@ (in-batch arm), although every cosine fell.
