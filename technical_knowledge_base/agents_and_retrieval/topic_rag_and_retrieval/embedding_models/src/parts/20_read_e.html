Why rank does not transfer, in order of size here: the task mix (the mean blends classification, clustering and similarity with retrieval: a model can climb on 31 non-retrieval tasks); your domain (the retrieval tasks are arguments, questions, fact-checking, finance, COVID literature; your corpus is none of them); contamination (@@decl_sf@@ of these @@mteb_n@@ models declare SciFact training data and @@decl_nf@@ NFCorpus, while @@decl_none@@ declare nothing at all, so their zero-shot status is unknown); noise (a few hundred queries per task; section 11); and setup (a model run without its query instruction or at a shorter maximum length scores below its leaderboard number; sections 6 and 8). The leaderboard is a shortlist generator: read the retrieval tasks nearest your domain, not the mean.

How each task type is scored (1 min)

Retrieval and reranking: nDCG@10 or MAP; classification: accuracy of a logistic regression on the vectors; clustering: V-measure; semantic similarity: Spearman correlation of cosines with human scores; pair classification: average precision. 11. Choosing a model on your own data

  1. Shortlist 3 to 6 models: licence, languages, maximum length against your chunk size, vector size against your memory, hosted or local budget, and the MTEB retrieval tasks nearest your domain.
  2. Label a few hundred real queries (from logs if you have them) with their relevant chunks; keep 20 to 30% aside as a confirmation set. Same chunking for every model.
  3. Run each model with its documented prompt and without; exact search (no index, so the index does not blur the comparison); BM25 and a hybrid as references.
  4. Compare paired: per-query nDCG@10 (or recall@k at your k), the difference between models on the same queries, bootstrap interval of the difference. Models within the interval are tied; break ties on cost (bytes per vector, query latency, indexing time, licence).
  5. Confirm the winner on the held-out queries, then on a sample of production traffic after launch.

12. Fine-tuning for real: when it pays and how

When. After the protocol above says the best off-the-shelf model is not good enough, and when you have a domain the general models have not seen (internal jargon, product codes, queries that are error messages), at least a few thousand (query, positive) pairs or a way to make them (a language model writing questions for your passages is the common route; Qwen3-Embedding's report describes synthetic pairs at scale), and a labelled evaluation set never used for training.

How, with sentence-transformers @@st_version@@ (the tools this page's run mirrors): mine_hard_negatives(..., relative_margin=0.05) for positive-aware mining; MultipleNegativesRankingLoss, or CachedMultipleNegativesRankingLoss for large batches on a small GPU; GISTEmbedLoss, which uses a second "guide" model to mask in-batch negatives the guide scores above the positive; MatryoshkaLoss around any of them if you will truncate; a no-duplicates batch sampler; evaluation on held-out queries every epoch; three seeds.

The core of this page's training loop (code)


The costs people forget. A new model means re-embedding the whole corpus (bge-small: 36 s for SciFact here; Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B: 816 s), and vectors from the old and new model cannot share an index: a running system needs a second index and a switch-over ({{RAG in production|n:3f85c17b0d0d81aa9ba9c0ceac9e28c1}} measures what mixing them does: nDCG@10 0.379 at 50% re-embedded against 0.713 clean). Check the other domains for forgetting (section 5's NFCorpus panel).







Depth: two design questions


Switch a 50-million-chunk index to a better embedding model without downtime. Plan it. (1 min)
Evaluate offline on a labelled set first (paired, with intervals). Then build a second index: re-embed all chunks with the new model in a batch job (throughput-bound; cost = tokens × price or GPU-hours), while new and updated documents are written to both indexes. Shadow-query the new index and compare results and latency; switch reads with a flag per tenant or a percentage; keep the old index until the new one has been stable; then delete. Never mix vectors of two models in one index: a query vector from one model is meaningless against the other's documents. Budget memory for two indexes during the switch, or use Matryoshka truncation or quantisation (section 7) to fit.


Retrieval over internal documents is poor on product codes and jargon. What do you try, in what order? (1 min)
Measure first (golden set from search logs). Add BM25 and hybrid fusion (exact terms are BM25's strength); check chunking and the query prompt; try two or three stronger off-the-shelf models with the protocol of section 11; add a reranker; only then fine-tune on mined pairs from logs or LLM-generated questions, with positive-aware hard negatives, several seeds and a held-out set, and check general-domain queries for forgetting.
