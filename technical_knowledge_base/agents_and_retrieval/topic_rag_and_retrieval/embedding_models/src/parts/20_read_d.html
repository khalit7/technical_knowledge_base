The rule
Use the documented prompt as the default, then measure without it and with one or two task wordings on your own labelled queries: that costs one re-encoding of the queries, not of the corpus. A document prefix changes every stored vector, so changing it means re-embedding the corpus, and a query encoded with a mismatched setting silently loses quality.

7. Smaller vectors: fewer numbers (Matryoshka) or fewer bits (int8, binary)

Qwen3-Embedding stores 1,024 float32 numbers per text: 4,096 bytes, 4 GB for a million chunks (derived: 106 × 1,024 × 4 bytes). Two knobs shrink it: keep the first m numbers, or store fewer bits per number. Whether cutting works depends on training:

LMRL = Σm ∈ M cm  L(v1:m)
M
the nested sizes, for example {32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024}
v1:m
the first m numbers of the embedding, re-normalised
L
the contrastive loss of section 3, applied to the truncated vector
cm
a weight per size, often 1

Matryoshka Representation Learning (Kusupati et al. 2022, arXiv 2205.13147) adds the loss on every prefix, so the front of the vector carries the most; sentence-transformers wraps any loss this way (MatryoshkaLoss). Qwen3-Embedding was trained like this (its card: "user-defined output dimensions ranging from 32 to 1024"); bge-small was not.

Hosted models expose the same idea as a parameter: OpenAI's text-embedding-3 models take a dimensions argument (1,536 by default for -small, 3,072 for -large), and OpenAI states that "a text-embedding-3-large embedding can be shortened to a size of 256 while still outperforming an unshortened text-embedding-ada-002 embedding with a size of 1536" ({{OpenAI embeddings guide|https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/embeddings}}) published, vendor. 8. Long inputs: the silent cut

Every model reads at most a fixed number of tokens and silently drops the rest: all-MiniLM-L6 256 by default, bge-small and e5-small-v2 512 (the e5 card: "Long texts will be truncated to at most 512 tokens"), gte-modernbert 8,192 (run here at 1,024), Qwen3-Embedding 32K (run here at 1,024). The vector of a truncated text is still a vector.
