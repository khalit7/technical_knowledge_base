6. LLM rerankers: pointwise, pairwise, listwise

A decoder language model can rerank in three ways, which differ in what one call sees.

Pointwise: one call per candidate, "is this document relevant? yes or no", read the probability of "yes". Scores are independent, so calls run in parallel and the scores can be thresholded. K calls.
Pairwise: one call per pair of candidates, "which of A and B is more relevant?", used inside a sort. Robust to score scale, but many calls: all pairs is K(K-1), a sliding bubble sort is about 2 x passes x K with both orders asked.
Listwise: one call sees many candidates and writes an order, "[3] > [1] > [2]". Sees the competition, but long prompts, a parseable output to hope for, and position bias; long lists go through a sliding window.

Pointwise done properly: Qwen3-Reranker

Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B is a Qwen3 decoder fine-tuned to answer one question. Its chat template (shipped with the model, chat_template.jinja) puts the task in a fixed frame and pre-fills an empty thinking block, so the very next token must be the answer:

<|im_start|>system Judge whether the Document meets the requirements based on the Query and the Instruct provided. sentence-transformers 6.1.0, cross_encoder/modules/logit_score.py, lines 42-54 (abridged)

def forward(self, features, **kwargs): logits = features[self.module_input_name][:, -1] # next-token logits at the last position if self.false_token_id is None: scores = logits[:, self.true_token_id] else: scores = logits[:, self.true_token_id] - logits[:, self.false_token_id] # "yes" (9693) minus "no" (2152) features["scores"] = scores.unsqueeze(1)

Why the difference of two logits: a softmax restricted to the two tokens gives P(yes) = ey / (ey + en) = sigmoid(y - n), so the stored score is the log-odds of "yes" against "no", and sigmoid(score) is that probability. The idea is from monoT5 (Nogueira et al., 2020), which trained T5 to emit "true" or "false" and ranked by the softmax over only those two logits. The Qwen3 report says the reranker gets supervised fine-tuning and model merging, with no weakly supervised pre-training stage, unlike the embedding models (Qwen3 Embedding report: "the reranking model's training process does not include a first-stage weakly supervised training phase").

What it shows. A 4 B general model, about seven times the trained reranker's parameters, ranks worse than the 0.6 B model fine-tuned for the job, in every style, and costs several times more time per claim. Listwise with a 10-candidate window did not beat BM25 here; pointwise yes/no, the trained reranker's own format, gained little without the training; pairwise comparisons were the best prompted style but disagreed with themselves when the two passages swapped places in about 40% of comparisons (position bias), which is why PRP asks both orders. The training, not the architecture or the size, is what makes Qwen3-Reranker good.

Published at scale. In RankGPT's Table 1, GPT-4 listwise reranking of BM25's top 100 reached 75.59 nDCG@10 on TREC DL 2019 against 71.83 for the trained monoT5-3B and 50.58 for BM25; gpt-3.5-turbo reached 65.80, below monoT5 (published, academic, 2023). PRP with the open FLAN-UL2 (20 B) reached 72.42 to 72.65 depending on the variant. So a frontier LLM can beat a trained reranker, and a mid-size one usually does not; the trained small reranker is far cheaper per pair either way. Two later ideas cut LLM reranking cost: Setwise (Zhuang et al.) picks the best of three per call inside a sort (about 62% fewer inferences than pairwise heapsort in their Table 2), and FIRST (Reddy et al., 2024) reads the order from the logits of the first generated identifier instead of generating the whole permutation ("accelerates inference by 50%").

7. Distillation: how small rerankers get good

The problem. The best judge (a large cross-encoder ensemble, GPT-4) is too slow for production; labelled data is sparse and full of false negatives. Distillation trains a small student to reproduce a strong teacher's scores or orderings on many unlabelled (query, candidate) pairs, which also smooths over missing labels: the teacher says "this unmarked passage is quite relevant".
