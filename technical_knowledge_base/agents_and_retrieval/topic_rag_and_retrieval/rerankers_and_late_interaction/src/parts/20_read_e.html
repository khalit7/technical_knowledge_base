8. Late interaction: ColBERT and MaxSim

A bi-encoder squeezes a document into one vector; a cross-encoder precomputes nothing. Late interaction keeps the document side precomputable but much richer: the encoder outputs one small vector per token, stored offline, and query and document meet only at the end, through a cheap score. ColBERT (Khattab and Zaharia, 2020) defines it as MaxSim: each query token takes its highest dot product with any document token, and the maxima are added up. On claim 1180, "md ##a ##5" finds the same three word pieces in the abstract (similarities @@ms_md@@), "sensor" finds "sensing" (@@ms_sensor@@), and "rna", "virus" and "infection" find themselves. 9. PLAID: compressing and searching token vectors

One vector per token multiplies storage by the tokens per document (@@cb_tok_mean_r@@ here), and exhaustive MaxSim reads all of them. ColBERTv2 stores each token vector as the id of its nearest k-means centroid plus the residual (vector minus centroid) quantised to 1 or 2 bits per dimension: "this total of 20 or 36 bytes per vector" at 128 dimensions against 256 in float16, a 6 to 10 times smaller index. PLAID then searches without decompressing everything, the same IVF idea as {{Vector indexes|n:3f85c17b0d0d81f29707c130b2ebcfb1}}, applied per token:

  1. each query token picks its nearest centroids; documents with tokens in them become candidates;
  2. candidates are scored with centroid interaction: MaxSim with every token replaced by its centroid;
  3. centroids that score low for every query token are pruned, and the survivors rescored;
  4. only the best few are decompressed and scored exactly.

PLAID's Table 3 reports 39.8 MRR@10 on MS MARCO at k = 100 in 222 ms on one CPU core and 20 ms on a GPU, against 4,569 ms on 8 cores for vanilla ColBERTv2 (published, academic, 2022). Measured here with fast-plaid 1.4.6 (the engine pylate uses) on the stored SciFact vectors, CPU, seed 42 (e_plaid.py). 10. Learned sparse retrieval: SPLADE

The other middle point keeps BM25's machinery, an inverted index and a score summed over shared terms, but lets a model choose the terms and their weights. SPLADE (Formal, Piwowarski and Clinchant, SIGIR 2021) runs BERT's masked-language-model head over every input position i, which scores all 30,522 vocabulary entries j; the text's weight for term j is the largest log-saturated score over positions. Terms the text lacks get weight too: expansion terms, words a reader would expect. Here is a real document vector:

Model: opensearch-neural-sparse-encoding-doc-v3-distill (67 M, Apache 2.0) through sentence-transformers' SparseEncoder, @@sp_dps@@ documents per second on MPS. measured on the {{Retrieval pipeline page|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}} (its src/exp/sparse.py, 2026-10-14), copied to src/inputs/sparse_rp.json, not rerun here.

wij = transform(hi)T Ej + bj,   wj = maxi log(1 + ReLU(wij)),   score(q, d) = ∑j wj(q) · wj(d)

The log saturates so no term dominates; ReLU zeroes most of the vocabulary. SPLADE v1 summed over positions; v2 (Formal et al., 2021) switched to the max, "almost 2 points" better on MS MARCO and TREC. Sparsity is trained, not hoped for: the FLOPS regulariser penalises each term's average weight over the batch, squared, which pushes down terms that are active in many documents and keeps posting lists short and even, with separate strengths for queries (kept sparser) and documents:

ℓFLOPS = ∑j ∈ V ( (1/N) ∑i=1N wj(di) )2,   L = Lrank + λq ℓq + λd ℓd

An inference-free variant, the model above, runs the network on documents only; a query is tokenised and each word gets a fixed weight from a table, so a query costs a tokeniser call, like BM25. On SciFact it scores @@sp_ndcg@@ nDCG@10 against @@bm25_ndcg@@ for BM25 and @@de_ndcg@@ for Qwen3-Embedding, and fused with Qwen3-Embedding by RRF @@sp_rrf@@, level with BM25 + Qwen3 (@@hy_ndcg@@). A document keeps @@sp_nnz_d@@ non-zero terms on average and a query @@sp_nnz_q@@, so the index stays an inverted index, about @@sp_bytes_doc@@ bytes per abstract before compression (derived: @@sp_nnz_d@@ × (4-byte term id + 4-byte weight)), between the single vector and ColBERT. Caveat: the model card lists SciFact and NFCorpus among its training sets, so these scores are in-domain and flatter it against BM25 and the dense models.

Published (academic): SPLADE v1 reached MS MARCO dev MRR@10 0.322 against 0.184 for BM25 and 0.277 for doc2query-T5, close to dense ANCE (0.330), at an estimated 0.73 FLOPS per query-document pair against 0.13 for BM25 (Table 1, 2021). Distilled from a cross-encoder with Margin-MSE (section 3) on its own harder negatives, DistilSPLADE-max reached 0.368 (SPLADE v2, Table 1) and a BEIR zero-shot average nDCG@10 of 0.506 against 0.456 for BM25 and 0.457 for ColBERT (Table 2; SciFact 0.693 against 0.665 for BM25). Learned sparse is a first-stage retriever, or a third retriever beside BM25 and dense; how the lists are fused belongs to {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}}, and pgvector's sparsevec to {{Search and vector databases|n:3ef5c17b0d0d815eb4caef9b4cd4d732}}.
