11. Common mistakes

Reranking a list that does not contain the answer
The reranker's ceiling is the first stage's recall@K. Measure recall@K first; if it is low, fix retrieval (hybrid, query rewriting, chunking), not the reranker.
Assuming any reranker helps
The MiniLM cross-encoder lowered the hybrid list from @@hy_ndcg@@ to @@hy_ce100@@ here. Evaluate the reranker against your first stage on your data, at the K you will use.
Skipping in-domain fine-tuning, or mining hard negatives from sparse labels without care
Three minutes of fine-tuning on 809 SciFact training claims lifted the 22 M cross-encoder on the hybrid top 30 from @@hy_ce_k30@@ to 0.74 to 0.75 (section 3). In that run BM25-mined hard negatives did worse than random ones, plausibly because some of them were relevant abstracts nobody labelled: filter mined negatives with a stronger model, or distil from its scores.
Thresholding raw scores across models or corpora
MiniLM returns logits, bge-reranker a sigmoid, Qwen3-Reranker log-odds; the same model's relevant pairs sit at a median of @@sf_rel_med@@ on SciFact and @@nf_rel_med@@ on NFCorpus. Recalibrate on labelled target data.
Forgetting the instruction or template of an instruction-tuned reranker
Qwen3-Reranker expects its chat template with an instruction; a raw (query, document) pair through a generic causal-LM path gives different scores. Load it as its model card says (sentence-transformers CrossEncoder with prompts).
Silent truncation
@@trunc_ce@@% of SciFact pairs exceed MiniLM's 512 tokens and lose the end of the abstract. With long chunks, rerank shorter chunks or use a long-context reranker.
Timing one pair and multiplying
Per-query latency depends on batching (all K pairs in one batch), padding and length sorting. Measure a whole query's batch at your K, several times, under realistic load.
Parsing LLM listwise output without a fallback
Models skip or repeat identifiers. Deduplicate, append missing candidates in their original order, and count how often it happens (@@list_missing@@ here).
Storing ColBERT vectors uncompressed at scale
192 bytes per token in float16 is @@cb_x_bge@@ times a single 384-d vector per abstract; use residual compression (PLAID) or token pooling, and check the quality cost on your data.

12. Interview questions

Why not run the cross-encoder over the whole corpus?
It needs one forward pass per (query, document) pair and nothing can be precomputed: 5,183 passes per query here (@@ce_all_s@@ s with a 22 M model). A bi-encoder precomputes document vectors, so a query costs one pass plus dot products.
Bi-encoder, cross-encoder, late interaction: one line each on what is precomputed.
Bi-encoder: one vector per document. Cross-encoder: nothing. Late interaction: one vector per document token, scored with MaxSim at query time.
How do you pick the rerank depth K?
From first-stage recall@K (the ceiling), the reranker's quality at a few K on labelled queries, and the latency budget, which grows linearly in K. Here quality flattened at K about 20 to 30.
What does Qwen3-Reranker's score mean?
logit("yes") minus logit("no") at the answer position; sigmoid of it is P(yes) among {yes, no}.
Why is MaxSim cheaper than cross-attention but better than one vector?
It is only dot products between precomputed token vectors (about 1.4 MFLOP per abstract here against 8.9 GFLOP for a small cross-encoder), yet each query token can match a different part of the document instead of one pooled summary.
What is query augmentation in ColBERT?
Padding the query with [MASK] tokens to 32 and keeping their vectors: a learned expansion. Removing them cost 0.005 nDCG@10 here.
Pointwise, pairwise, listwise LLM reranking: costs?
Pointwise K calls (parallel, thresholdable); pairwise up to K(K-1) calls, or about 2 x passes x K with sliding bubble sort and both orders; listwise a few long calls with a sliding window, output to parse, position bias.
What is Margin-MSE?
A distillation loss: the student's score difference between a positive and a negative should equal the teacher's, so the student learns relative judgements without matching the teacher's scale.
System design: add reranking to a RAG service handling 200 queries per second, p95 under 300 ms end to end.
Budget each stage (query embedding about 10 to 50 ms, ANN search a few ms, generation's first token separately). Rerank the hybrid top 20 to 30 with a 0.6 B cross-encoder on GPUs: about 0.4 TFLOP per pair, so 200 × 30 × 0.4 = 2.4 PFLOP/s of work; at a realistic 100 to 200 TFLOP/s achieved per GPU that is 12 to 24 GPUs, so consider a smaller distilled reranker, fewer candidates (K 10 to 20 after measuring the quality curve), shorter chunks, int8 or fp8, and dynamic batching across concurrent queries with a batch timeout. Cache scores for repeated (query, chunk) pairs. Fall back to the first-stage order if the reranker times out. Log scores to recalibrate thresholds; evaluate offline on a golden set before each model change. Derived arithmetic; the throughput figure is an assumption to replace with a measurement.
