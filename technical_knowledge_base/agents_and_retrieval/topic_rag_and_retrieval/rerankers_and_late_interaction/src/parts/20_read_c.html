4. How deep to rerank: K against quality and time

The problem. The reranker sees only the first stage's top K. Too small a K and the relevant abstract is never shown to it; too large and you pay for pairs that almost never matter, and give a weak reranker more chances to promote a wrong abstract. Two curves decide K: the first stage's recall@K (the share of relevant abstracts inside the top K: the ceiling), and the reranker's quality as K grows.

Predict: the hybrid list (BM25 and Qwen3-Embedding fused) scores @@hy_ndcg@@ nDCG@10 on its own. You rerank its top 100 with the small MiniLM cross-encoder, which lifts BM25 from @@bm25_ndcg@@ to @@bm25_ce100@@. What happens?
It falls, to @@hy_ce100@@. The cross-encoder is a weaker judge of SciFact than the fused list it reorders: the deeper it reranks, the more wrong abstracts it promotes (@@hy_ce_k3@@ at K = 3, @@hy_ce_k30@@ at K = 30, @@hy_ce100@@ at K = 100). Qwen3-Reranker on the same list reaches @@hy_qw_best@@. A reranker is a model with its own errors, not a free improvement.

Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B rises fastest and highest on every first stage; bge-reranker-v2-m3 (568 M parameters, multilingual) lands between; the MiniLM cross-encoder helps BM25 a little, helps dense retrieval only at small K, and hurts the hybrid list. Past K = 20 the strong reranker gains nothing and the weaker ones lose.

Per claim, not on average. An average gain of a few points hides many individual losses, which is why the Before/after lab shows single claims.

How to choose K on your data. Plot recall@K of your first stage on a labelled sample (a few hundred queries is enough), pick the smallest K where it flattens, measure the reranker's quality at a few K around it, and check the latency at that K on your hardware (Depth chooser tab). 5. What a reranker score means: calibration, thresholds, abstaining

The problem. A RAG system often has to decide whether anything relevant was found (to say "I could not find it" instead of answering from the wrong abstract), and a natural idea is a threshold on the reranker score. That needs the score to mean the same thing across queries.

Three scales. The setup stored each model's default output, and they are different quantities: MiniLM's raw logit, bge's sigmoid of its logit, and Qwen3's log-odds of "yes" over "no". Mapped back to one logit scale (bge: log(p / (1 - p))), their distributions on the same @@pool_pairs@@ (claim, candidate) pairs look like this:

Calibration means that among pairs scored 0.7, about 70% are relevant. Reading each logit through a sigmoid, the expected calibration error (ECE: the average gap between predicted and observed relevance over ten score bins) is @@ece_ce@@, @@ece_bge@@ and @@ece_qw@@ for the three models; after Platt scaling (fitting p = sigmoid(a · score + b) on half the claims and testing on the other half) it drops to @@ece_ce_p@@, @@ece_bge_p@@ and @@ece_qw_p@@. Those ECEs look small because only @@base_rate@@% of the pairs are relevant; the top bin tells the real story: of the pairs whose raw sigmoid is above 0.9, only @@top_ce@@%, @@top_bge@@% and @@top_qw@@% are labelled relevant (some unlabelled ones are relevant, so the truth is somewhat higher). Raw scores are over-confident at the top; the reliability bars in the chart above show it bin by bin.

Thresholds do not transfer. The same MiniLM cross-encoder on NFCorpus (medical questions, graded labels) puts its relevant pairs at a median logit of @@nf_rel_med@@, against @@sf_rel_med@@ on SciFact: @@nf_above0@@% of NFCorpus relevant pairs score above 0, against @@sf_above0@@% on SciFact. A threshold tuned on one corpus, or on one model, is wrong on the next. Recalibrate on a labelled sample from the target data.

Abstaining when nothing relevant was retrieved

A constructed test, labelled as such: each test claim is ranked twice, once with its candidate pool as is (@@n_ans@@ claims have a relevant abstract in it) and once with every relevant abstract removed, so the best remaining candidate is a near miss. A good "nothing here" signal gives the top candidate a lower score in the second case.
