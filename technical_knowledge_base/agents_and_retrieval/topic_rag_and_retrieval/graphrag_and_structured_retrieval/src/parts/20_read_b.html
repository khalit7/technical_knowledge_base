Extraction without a language model

4. Entity resolution: is "Doyle" the same node as "Arthur Conan Doyle"?

Entity resolution sounds like housekeeping; it decides whether the graph connects at all. A bridge question's two paragraphs are joined only if the mention in one ("Temple", "Shirley Temple Black") and the subject of the other (the article "Shirley Temple") become the same node. Merge too strictly and the graph falls into islands; too loosely and unrelated things fuse ("James Bond" the character and "the James Bond series"; "Labrador" the region and "Labrador Retriever").

Gold without hand labels. HotpotQA gives two checks for free: every paragraph's title is its subject, so its extraction should contain a mention that resolves to that title (title recall); and every bridge pair is joined by a mention of one's title in the other, so a good graph should join them (link recall). Four rules, strictest first, applied to graphrag's noun phrases (s_entities.py): exact (upper-cased string equality, what graphrag's merge does); normalised (lower case, punctuation, articles and Wikipedia's parenthetical removed: "Kiss and Tell (1945 film)" = "kiss and tell"); token subset (the mention's words are a subset of a title's and cover at least half of them: "Kerensky" = "Alexander Kerensky"); embedding (nearest title by bge-small cosine, kept above 0.85).

The 30 hand-checked merges per rule (depth)

Every wrong merge adds edges between unrelated paragraphs, and graph algorithms amplify edges: a PageRank walk or a community will happily cross a false bridge. Production systems therefore resolve in stages: cheap normalisation for everything, blocking (compare only names that share a token or a sound), an embedding or LLM judgement on the candidate pairs, and, where it exists, linking to an external identifier (a Wikidata id, a customer number) that settles the question. graphrag does the first stage only; LightRAG 1.5.7 does not even case-fold names (utils.py lines 5391 to 5393, normalize_entity_name).
