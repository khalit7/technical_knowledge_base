6. Microsoft GraphRAG at query time: global, local, DRIFT, basic

After indexing, graphrag holds entities (with LLM-merged descriptions), relationships, text units, the community hierarchy and one community report per community: a JSON title, summary, an "impact severity rating" from 0 to 10 and a list of findings, written by the model from the community's entities and relationships (prompt @@pt_graphrag_community_report@@ tokens; reports default to 2,000 words from at most 8,000 tokens of input; claims extraction is off by default). Four query engines read them.

Global search: map-reduce over community reports

The question goes with batches of reports (every community at the chosen level, default 2) to a map prompt that returns key points, each scored 0 to 100 for how much it helps ("An 'I don't know' type of response should have a score of 0"); the reduce step keeps points scored above 0, sorts them, packs them up to 12,000 tokens and writes the answer in one call. The paper's own words: "Answers with score 0 are filtered out." ({{Edge et al., section 3.1.6|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130}}). Step through it on the subset:

What it costs and buys, published. One map call per batch of reports plus one reduce call per question: with hundreds of communities, hundreds of calls. On two corpora (podcast transcripts, about 1 M tokens; news, about 1.7 M), 125 generated "sensemaking" questions each, an LLM judge preferred the global approaches over vector RAG on comprehensiveness in 72 to 83% of pairs and on diversity in 62 to 82% ({{Edge et al., v2, section 5.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130}}) published; the range includes the text-summarisation baseline TS, which also beats vector RAG, and an LLM judge's preference for longer, broader answers is not correctness ({{LLM-as-judge|n:3c65c17b0d0d810ba2a2ecc08c4c233b}}). Indexing the podcast corpus took 281 minutes with gpt-4-turbo; the paper reports no token or dollar cost for indexing.

Context per query, and dynamic community selection (published and vendor, depth)

The LightRAG paper's back-of-envelope for its Legal corpus is 610 level-2 communities of about 1,000 tokens each read per query ({{Guo et al., section 4.5|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05779}}); the GraphRAG paper's Table 2 shows the other side, answering from the root level (C0) with 26,657 context tokens on its Podcast corpus against 1,014,611 for summarising the raw text (2.6%) published.

Microsoft's later dynamic community selection walks the hierarchy from the top, asking the model to rate each report 0 to 5 and descending only into relevant ones (query/context_builder/dynamic_community_selection.py, off by default); its blog reports about 77% lower cost than static global search at level 1 with no significant quality difference ({{Microsoft Research, 2024-11-15|https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/graphrag-improving-global-search-via-dynamic-community-selection/}}) vendor.

Local search: start from entities

For a question about something specific, graphrag embeds the question, finds the nearest entity descriptions (not the nearest chunks), and assembles a context from those entities' relationships, the text units they came from and their communities' reports, in fixed shares of a 12,000-token budget, top 10 entities and 10 relationships (config/defaults.py lines 265 to 270; mixed_context.py lines 175 to 208).

Local search's context window, to scale: half text, the rest graph

The GraphRAG paper does not evaluate local search; it is documented and shipped, not benchmarked there.

DRIFT: global first, then local follow-ups

DRIFT (Dynamic Reasoning and Inference with Flexible Traversal) combines the two, and is the mode Microsoft built for local questions:

DRIFT: a guessed report finds the right communities, then local searches chase follow-ups

Defaults from config/defaults.py lines 99 to 106 (drift_k_followups 20, primer_folds 5, n_depth 3); the primer is a HyDE-style query expansion (drift_search/primer.py lines 71 to 95).

Microsoft's blog reports DRIFT preferred over local search in 78% of comparisons on comprehensiveness and 81% on diversity, on 50 local questions over 5,000+ AP news articles, judged by an LLM ({{Microsoft Research, 2024-10-31|https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-drift-search-combining-global-and-local-search-methods-to-improve-quality-and-efficiency/}}) vendor; it gives no cost. Basic search is plain vector RAG over the text units, top 10 (basic_search/basic_context.py), so the same index answers lookups cheaply: the baseline every graph method has to beat.
