The idea in one screen

A top-k of similar chunks answers only questions whose evidence looks like the question. When the evidence is a chain of entities, the whole corpus or every row of a table, you need structure: GraphRAG pays at index time to turn text into entities, edges and community summaries; structured retrieval writes a SQL or Cypher query against data that already has structure. Each pays off only when its links are real.

1. What a top-k of chunks cannot answer

Everything on the parent page assumes the answer lives in a few passages that resemble the question. That assumption fails in three of these four ways:

Local

The answer is in one passage that shares words or meaning with the question. "What year was Goldfinger released?" Top-k works.

Multi-hop (relational)

Passage A names an entity, passage B says something about it; B may share nothing with the question. "What position did the woman who played Corliss Archer in Kiss and Tell hold?"

Global (sensemaking)

The answer is a property of the whole collection. "What are the main themes of these 10,000 reports?" Any top 10 samples ten fragments.

Aggregate

A count, sum, maximum or join over records. "How many singers are older than 40?" Retrieval returns k rows; the answer depends on all of them.

On the running example's 200 HotpotQA questions, one dense search (bge-small, the root agent runs' search tool) puts at least one gold paragraph in the top 10 for almost every question, but both for @@ppr_dense_10@@, and both in the top 5 for @@ppr_dense_5@@ measured: the second hop is what is missed. A graph attacks that: if paragraph A mentions an entity and paragraph B is about it, an edge joins them, and retrieval can walk from A to B without the question ever naming B.

Predict: 100 Spider questions over 20 real databases. How many are aggregates (COUNT, SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX or GROUP BY), and of those, how many depend on more than 10 rows?

Spider's databases are small (section 10); on real tables the gap is larger. A retriever that hands the model 10 rows cannot count more than 10 of them: retrieve the schema and let the database count.

When none of this matters. Most questions in most products are local. For those, hybrid search with a reranker is cheaper and at least as good as anything on this page; the GraphRAG paper evaluates only global questions, and Microsoft later built DRIFT search specifically for local ones (section 6).

2. Knowledge graphs, and the open graph GraphRAG builds

A knowledge graph stores facts as nodes (entities: a person, a film, a place) joined by edges (relations: starred in, held); one fact is a triple. Two families store them: property graphs (Neo4j, Kùzu, Memgraph, Amazon Neptune), where nodes and edges carry a label and key-value properties, queried with Cypher or its ISO successor GQL; and RDF stores (Wikidata, GraphDB, Apache Jena), where everything is a triple of URIs, queried with SPARQL, and the schema (an ontology) is itself data.

The question names the film and the character, never the actress: one shared node makes the second hop

The root's example bridge question from HotpotQA's dev set, drawn by hand from its two gold paragraphs (answer: Chief of Protocol). Once "Shirley Temple" is a node shared by both paragraphs, a graph walk reaches paragraph B in one hop.

A schema (which node labels and relation types are allowed) separates a curated knowledge graph from what GraphRAG builds. GraphRAG extracts an open graph: whatever entities and relation phrases a language model finds in each chunk, guided only by a short list of entity types. Cheap to start, messy to use: the same entity under five spellings, relations that are free text ("was the third film in the series of"), no constraints to check against.

HotpotQA is a fair test bed because it was built from exactly such links: its authors took the hyperlink graph of the first paragraphs of English Wikipedia and wrote bridge questions over pairs of articles joined by a hyperlink, the linked article's subject being the bridge entity ({{Yang et al. 2018, section 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09600}}) published. Keep that in mind in section 8: a graph built from title mentions recovers the dataset's own construction.
