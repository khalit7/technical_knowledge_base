LazyGraphRAG (Microsoft Research, November 2024) indexes with noun phrases and their co-occurrence (the fast path of section 3) and spends the language model only at query time: split the question into subqueries, rank chunks and communities by relevance, and let the model test candidates against a relevance test budget (100, 500 and 1,500 in the blog's configurations). Microsoft claims indexing at the cost of vector RAG and 0.1% of GraphRAG's, and "more than 700 times lower query cost" at comparable global-search quality vendor; the win rates exist only as chart images. It is not in the open graphrag package (3.3.0 mentions it only in the extractor comment of section 3) and ships in Microsoft Discovery and Azure Local; not run here.

LazyGraphRAG's claims in full (vendor, depth)
At the lowest budget it costs as much per query as vector RAG with an 8k context and beats every compared method except GraphRAG global search on global questions; at budget 500 it costs 4% of GraphRAG's level-2 global search and beats all eight compared conditions ({{blog, 2024-11-25, editor's note 2025-06-06|https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/}}); in the later BenchmarkQED study it won all 96 comparisons, all but one significant ({{2025-06-05|https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/benchmarkqed-automated-benchmarking-of-rag-systems/}}) vendor.

LightRAG (Guo et al., HKU, 2024; pip lightrag-hku 1.5.7) extracts like GraphRAG (1,200-token chunks, 100 overlap, one gleaning) but embeds entity and relation descriptions in their own vector stores and builds no hierarchy. At query time the model splits the question into low-level keywords (specific entities, searched against entity descriptions) and high-level keywords (themes, searched against relationships). One query is a keyword call plus an answer call instead of a map over hundreds of communities, which is the source of its cost claim. Run here with a recording stub instead of a model (s_lightrag.py stub), LightRAG made @@lr_calls@@ LLM calls to index @@lr_paras@@ paragraphs with @@lr_tok@@ prompt tokens, @@lr_tok_para@@ per paragraph, before any output measured.

Read the claims carefully. LightRAG's paper reports beating GraphRAG overall on three of four datasets by small margins (54.8, 52.0, 52.8 against 45.2, 48.0, 47.2) and losing on the fourth (49.6 against 50.4), judged by GPT-4o-mini ({{arXiv 2410.05779 v3, Table 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05779}}) published. HippoRAG 2's authors, re-running LightRAG with Llama-3.3-70B, got a QA F1 of 2.4 on HotpotQA against 75.3 for plain dense retrieval published: each paper runs its rivals with its own model, prompts and judge, so compare on your data with the same generator. The "70 to 90% of GraphRAG's quality at 1/100 of the cost" quoted around the web has no method or source and is not repeated here.

nano-graphrag (0.0.8, 2024) re-implements GraphRAG's shape in a few files: 1,200-token chunks, one gleaning, graspologic's hierarchical Leiden on the largest component with max_graph_cluster_size 10 and the same seed, community reports, and local, global and naive modes, default global at level 2 (nano_graphrag/graphrag.py lines 70 to 81; base.py lines 11 to 14). Last PyPI release 0.0.8.2, 2024-10-19; not run here because LightRAG covers the same shape and is maintained.

HippoRAG: personalised PageRank instead of summaries

HippoRAG (Gutiérrez et al., NeurIPS 2024) ignores communities and treats the graph as an associative memory. Indexing: an LLM extracts named entities, then triples, from each passage; phrases whose retrieval-encoder cosine exceeds 0.8 get a synonymy edge. Query: extract the question's entities, link each to its most similar graph node, and run personalised PageRank (PPR) from them; a passage scores the probability mass of its phrases.

Mass restarts at the question's entities and leaks one edge at a time: the bridge passage goes from 0 to a real score

Exact power iteration in the page, d = 0.5, reset weighted by specificity. Node area is the node's probability; bars are passage scores.

p = (1 − d) r + d W p
r
reset vector: mass on the question's nodes, each weighted by its node specificity 1 / (number of passages containing it), so rare names count more
W
the graph's edge weights, each column normalised to sum to 1
d
probability of following an edge rather than jumping back to r: 0.5 in the paper's experiments (run_hipporag_main_exps.sh line 4; the class default is 0.1)
p
the stationary probability of each node

The toy shows both sides of the measured result in section 8: the walk reaches the bridge passage, which shares no word with the question, but it still ranks below passages that merely repeat the question's names. Published: HippoRAG (ColBERTv2) recall@5 of 89.1 on 2WikiMultiHopQA against 68.2 for ColBERTv2 alone and 51.9 against 49.2 on MuSiQue, but 77.7 against 79.3 on HotpotQA, where it loses; online retrieval "10 to 30 times cheaper and 6 to 13 times faster" than iterative retrieval with IRCoT ({{arXiv 2405.14831 v3, Table 2 and appendix G|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14831}}) published. HippoRAG 2 (2025) adds passages as graph nodes seeded by dense similarity, the shape section 8 runs.

HippoRAG 2 in detail (published)
HippoRAG 2 seeds passages with reset weight 0.05 times the passage's dense score, links the query to triples rather than entities, and filters triples with the model; with NV-Embed-v2 and Llama-3.3-70B it reports recall@5 of 96.3 on HotpotQA against 94.5 for NV-Embed-v2 alone, and QA F1 averaged over seven datasets of 59.8 against 57.0, with GraphRAG at 49.6 ({{arXiv 2502.14802 v2, Tables 2 and 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802}}) published.
