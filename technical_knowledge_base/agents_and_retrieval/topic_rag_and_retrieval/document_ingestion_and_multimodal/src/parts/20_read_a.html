The idea in one sentenceA PDF stores glyphs at coordinates, not paragraphs, so every parser reconstructs reading order, tables and structure (by rules, by a layout model, by OCR, or not at all when the page image itself is embedded), and retrieval can only find what that reconstruction kept.

Retrieval only finds what ingestion kept: two routes from a file to an index

The parse route (top) turns the page into text elements, chunks and vectors; each box carries what that stage lost or cost on this page's runs (the topic's QASPER papers, an olmOCR-bench sample and three ViDoRe sets, Apple M1 Pro, October 2026). The page-image route (bottom) skips parsing and embeds the page picture as patch vectors. Tap a box to jump to its section. measured unless marked.

Ingestion: everything before the first chunk is embedded, including keeping the index in step when files change.
Text layer: the glyphs a born-digital PDF draws, decodable to Unicode; a scan has none.
Layout-aware parser: renders the page and detects text, title, table, figure, header and footer boxes before reading text.
Content hash: a fingerprint (SHA-256) of a file or chunk, used to re-embed only what changed.
