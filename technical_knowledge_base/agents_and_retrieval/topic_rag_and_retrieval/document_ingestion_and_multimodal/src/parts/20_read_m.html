12. Common mistakes

Evaluating retrieval on text someone else already cleaned
Public QA sets ship pre-parsed text (QASPER's comes from S2ORC's parse of each paper). Recall measured on it says nothing about your parser. Run the golden set through your own ingestion, from the original files, at least once.
Choosing a parser from a vendor's benchmark table
Marker's README puts Docling at 50.3 on olmOCR-bench; the olmOCR authors' table does not list Docling; this page's sample gives a third reading on another subset and settings. Scores move with the version, mode (OCR on or off, GPU or CPU path), categories included and who ran it. Run the candidates on fifty of your own pages.
Treating "the text is in there" as success
A paragraph split by a column jump, a footer inserted mid-sentence, or "classi- fication" with a hyphen still contains most of its words, and fuzzy checks pass. The retrieval check in section 3 is the one that matters: does the chunk with the evidence come back for the question.
Running OCR on every page
OCR on a born-digital page replaces exact text with a guess and costs seconds per page. Detect a missing or broken text layer and route only those pages.
Cutting tables into fixed-size chunks
Rows end up without their header and numbers without their meaning. Chunk a table as one unit (with its caption), or by rows with the header repeated.
Dropping page numbers and section paths
Without them a citation cannot be checked and a filter cannot be applied. They cost bytes; recovering them later means re-parsing everything.
Fixed-size windows in an index that is updated
One edit near the top changes every later chunk's hash, so incremental re-indexing silently becomes full re-indexing. Use structure-aware or content-defined boundaries.
Mixing vectors from two parser or model versions
After an upgrade, old and new chunks of the same document can both be live and both retrieved. Key every chunk by parser and model version and replace a document atomically.

13. Interview questions

Why can't you just call extract_text() on every PDF?
A PDF stores positioned glyphs, not paragraphs. Simple extractors follow the content stream or a top-to-bottom sort, so two columns interleave, headers and footers land in the middle of paragraphs, tables flatten and scans return nothing. Fine for clean single-column files; measure before trusting it on the rest.
How would you detect that a page needs OCR?
Too few characters for the page area, a high share of replacement or private-use characters, words not in a dictionary, or a page that is one full-page image. Route those pages to OCR and keep the text layer for the rest.
How do you make tables retrievable?
Detect and reconstruct the grid, keep the table whole with its caption, serialise rows with their headers ("column: value") or add a short generated summary for retrieval, and pass the full HTML or Markdown table to the generator.
When would you embed page images instead of parsing?
When answers live in charts, infographics, slides or scans with complex layout, and you can afford about 30 times the vector storage and a multi-vector index. For dense text, a good parser plus BM25 and a text embedder is as good and far cheaper (section 10). Many systems do both and fuse the results.
A user edits one paragraph of a 300-page manual. What gets re-embedded?
With file and chunk hashes and structure-aware or content-defined chunking: the one or two chunks around the edit. With fixed-size windows: everything after the edit. Delete chunks whose hashes vanished in the same transaction.
System design: ingest 5 million mixed documents (born-digital PDFs, scans, slides, spreadsheets) for an enterprise assistant with citations and per-user permissions.
  1. Intake: a queue of (document id, version, ACL) events from each source; a file hash skips unchanged files.
  2. Triage per page: text-layer quality check; born-digital pages to a fast layout-aware parser on CPU workers, broken or scanned pages to an OCR or VLM-OCR pool on GPUs; spreadsheets read natively, not as images.
  3. Elements, not strings: keep type, page, bounding box and heading path; tables as HTML with a row serialisation; figures with captions, and VLM descriptions for figure-heavy collections.
  4. Chunk by structure; hash chunks; embed only new hashes, keyed by model version; write vectors and metadata (ACL, tenant, dates) to a store that filters inside the search.
  5. Optional visual index for slide decks and reports with charts: page-image embeddings (ColPali family) fused with text results.
  6. Evaluate on a golden set built from the original files, per document type; track parse failures, OCR share and pages per second as production metrics; re-parse by parser version when a parser improves.
