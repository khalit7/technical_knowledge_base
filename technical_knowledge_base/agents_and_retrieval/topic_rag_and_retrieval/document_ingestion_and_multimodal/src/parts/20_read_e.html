4. OCR: when there is no text to extract

The problem. Scans, photos, faxes and some exported PDFs have no usable text layer: no layer at all, an invisible layer from an old OCR run, or glyphs without a Unicode mapping. Optical character recognition (OCR) reads the characters from pixels instead.

How it works, classic engines. Tesseract (5.4.1 here, Apache 2.0) binarises the page, finds text lines with its page-layout analysis (the --psm mode chooses how: 3, the default, assumes a full page of any layout), and since version 4 recognises each line with an LSTM (a recurrent network reading the line image left to right) trained per language (Tesseract documentation). It knows nothing of tables or reading order beyond its block finder. Layout-aware parsers run OCR only on regions their layout model marks as image text, or on pages whose text layer looks broken: Docling through whichever engine its OcrAutoOptions finds at start-up (docling/datamodel/pipeline_options.py line 312; here it chose RapidOCR with the PP-OCRv6 small models), Unstructured's hi_res through Tesseract, MinerU and Marker through their own models.

How it works, VLM OCR. Since 2025 the strongest systems on olmOCR-bench are vision-language models that read a whole page image and write Markdown directly, tables as HTML and maths as LaTeX: olmOCR 2 (7B), PaddleOCR-VL, Chandra, dots.ocr, DeepSeek-OCR. They handle reading order, tables and handwriting jointly, at the cost of a GPU and a few seconds per page, and they can hallucinate: produce fluent text that is not on the page, or repeat a line forever (olmOCR-bench's baseline test checks for runaway repetition for this reason). None ran here: their reference pipelines target NVIDIA GPUs with vLLM, and this page's budget on a shared 16 GB Mac went to the parsers and to page-image retrieval instead. Their published scores are in section 3.

Cost. On this machine Tesseract at 300 dpi took 2.18 s per page on the QASPER papers and 3.15 s on the olmOCR sample (CPU, at low priority, section 3b). When it matters: always run a check for a missing or broken text layer (characters per page, share of characters outside the expected scripts, the ratio of replacement characters) and route only those pages to OCR. How to check it: character error rate against a hand transcription of a sample, and the old-scans tests below.
