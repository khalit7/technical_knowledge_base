3b. Measured here: what each parser keeps

Two measurements, both on files whose content is known, every parser at its default settings.

A. Does the answer evidence survive? (24 QASPER papers)

The topic's long-document set is QASPER: questions about NLP papers whose annotators marked the paragraphs that answer them. The root page uses the text QASPER ships, already extracted by the S2ORC pipeline. Here the same papers start as their arXiv PDFs (latest version; 12 drawn at random from papers with a question answerable only from a table or figure, 12 from the rest, seed 0), go through each parser, and each evidence paragraph is searched in the output. Each paragraph is cut into overlapping word triples ("trigrams") and counted in the output: intact when 90% or more of its trigrams are there, damaged at 50 to 90% (there, but broken by hyphenated line ends, glued words, a column jump or a footer inside it), lost below 50%. Then the output is cut into the root's default chunks (256 tokens, 32 overlap) and BM25 with the root's settings ranks them for each question: recall@3 is the share of questions whose evidence is in the top 3 chunks.

Predict before looking at the bars: of the 107 evidence paragraphs, which tool loses the most? (All three read the same text layer; none uses OCR here.)
pdfplumber loses 71 and keeps 1 intact: on these two-column LaTeX pages it merges the line of the left column with the line beside it in the right column (section 1's animation) and its 3-point space tolerance glues words together (4,590 tokens of 25 or more letters across the 24 papers, against 0 for pypdf). pypdf keeps 60 intact and loses 2: pdfTeX draws columns in reading order, so the stream is nearly right, and the damage is hyphenated line ends (2,577 of them) and ligatures (1,676). Docling keeps 93 intact.

What the bars say. Three groups. The layout-aware parsers and the tools that join hyphenated line ends (MinerU flash 94 intact, Docling 93, Marker 92, MinerU basic and pymupdf4llm 91, pdftotext 86) keep almost every paragraph whole. The plain stream readers (pypdf 60, pdfminer.six 59, PyMuPDF 61, Unstructured 60 and 62, Tesseract on the rendered pages 64) keep the words but break about four paragraphs in ten at hyphenated line ends and ligatures. pdfplumber, which glues words and merges the two columns line by line, keeps 1. On these LaTeX papers almost nothing is lost outright except by pdfplumber, because pdfTeX draws each column in order.

And retrieval barely notices the middle group. Recall@3 of BM25 is 43 to 53% for every tool except pdfplumber (19%), and QASPER's own clean text scores 53%. BM25 matches words, and a paragraph that keeps 85% of its words intact still matches. What breaks retrieval is losing word boundaries, as pdfplumber does. Damage matters more where exact spans matter: quoting the evidence, citing it, or embedding a chunk whose sentences are cut by a column jump. At this sample size (58 questions) differences under about 15 points are noise, so read this as "a broken tokenizer input is fatal, a hyphen is not", not as a ranking.

B. olmOCR-bench sample: which failure each parser has

olmOCR-bench (Allen AI, 1,403 single-page PDFs, 7,010 pass/fail tests) checks facts rather than edit distance: a sentence must be present, a page number must be absent, sentence A must come before sentence B, a table cell must have a given neighbour. This page ran a seeded sample of five of its seven categories with the benchmark's own test code (olmocr/bench/tests.py at v0.4.27, lines 520 to 620 for presence, absence and order, 621 onward for tables); the two maths categories need a KaTeX renderer and were not run.

What the table says. Each category isolates one failure. Headers and footers separates the tools that know what a page header is: the layout models drop them (Marker 95, MinerU basic 95, Docling 93) while every text extractor keeps them (30 to 39), and so does Unstructured hi_res (28). Tables separates tools that emit a table at all: text extractors pass 1 of 108 tests, MinerU flash 28%, the other table-aware tools 44 to 73%. Multi column is where pdftotext's own column detection (69) beats pypdf (46) and PyMuPDF's sort=True collapses (12). Old scans defeats everything here (17 to 26): many of these Library of Congress pages are handwritten letters, on which Tesseract returns nothing at all, and the absence tests are the only ones a text extractor passes. Long tiny text (dictionary pages, reference lists) rewards OCR and good line grouping (MinerU 87, Docling 79, Tesseract 72). The means order the tools roughly as on the full benchmark, but this is a 110-page sample of five categories on CPU defaults, not a leaderboard.
