10. Measured here: page images against OCR text

The ColPali comparison rerun on this machine with open models small enough for a laptop: Tesseract OCR of every page then BM25, the same OCR text embedded by bge-small-en-v1.5 (the root's small embedder), and the page image embedded by colSmol-256M (vidore/colSmol-256M: the ColPali recipe on SmolVLM-256M, MIT licence, 128 dimensions per vector), on 100-query, 200-page subsets of three ViDoRe v1 sets: InfoVQA (infographics), ArxivQA (figures from papers) and Energy (text-heavy reports). colSmol ran on the CPU in float32 because the MPS runs failed on the shared machine (details under the table).

Predict: on the Energy set (text-heavy industrial reports), does the 256M page-image model beat plain BM25 over Tesseract OCR?
No clear winner: colSmol 97.7 against 95.0 for BM25, with intervals of about ±2.5 and ±3.8, so the two tie on text-heavy pages. The image model's lead appears where the text is not the content: ArxivQA (figures) 74.2 against 37.9, InfoVQA (infographics) 87.6 against 72.6.

What it shows. On figures, OCR text barely helps: the words inside a plot (axis labels, a legend) are not what the question asks about, and BM25 and the dense embedder over OCR both land near 38. The 256M page-image model doubles that. On infographics the image model leads by 15 points, beyond both intervals. On text-heavy reports the methods tie, and plain BM25 over OCR beats the dense text embedder (95.0 against 88.2): long pages of domain text with rare terms favour exact matching. The shape matches the ColPali paper's (dashed bars), at a smaller scale and on subsets.

What it costs here. colSmol produced 699 to 1,103 vectors of 128 numbers per page (mean per set), against one vector per page for bge-small: in float16 that is about 175 to 280 KB per page. Encoding took 4.3 to 5.9 s per page on this CPU in float32, against 0.3 to 1.6 s for Tesseract; on a GPU the paper's 3B ColPali took 0.39 s. What transfers: the ranking of approaches by document type, and the storage arithmetic. What does not: the absolute times (CPU, swapped memory) and the absolute nDCG on 200-page subsets, which are easier than the full sets (BM25 on the full InfoVQA set: 67.5 against 72.6 on the subset).
