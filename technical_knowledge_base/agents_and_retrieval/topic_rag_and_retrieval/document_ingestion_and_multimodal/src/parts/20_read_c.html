3. Layout-aware parsers: let a model look at the page

A person finds columns, headers and tables by looking. A layout-aware parser renders the page and runs a layout detection model, an object detector trained on labelled pages (the eleven classes of IBM's DocLayNet: Text, Title, Section-header, Table, Picture, Caption, Formula, List-item, Page-header, Page-footer, Footnote); it fills each box from the text layer where it is good and OCR where not, orders the boxes, sends tables to a table-structure model, and drops headers and footers by class, not by guessing.

Four open layout-aware parsers ran here on the CPU: Docling, Marker (fast mode, OCR off, so it reads scans like a text extractor), MinerU and Unstructured. Licences differ: Docling MIT, Unstructured Apache 2.0, MinerU its own licence with extra conditions, and Marker's model weights a modified OpenRAIL-M, free below $5M revenue.

4. Measured: what each parser keeps

Does the answer evidence survive? (24 QASPER papers)

QASPER, the topic's long-document set, marks the paragraphs that answer each question. The root uses the text QASPER ships (S2ORC's parse); here the same papers start as arXiv PDFs. Each evidence paragraph's word trigrams are looked for in each parser's output: intact at 90% or more, damaged at 50 to 90% (hyphens, glued words, a column jump or footer inside), lost below. Then BM25 with the root's settings ranks the root's chunks of each output (256 tokens, 32 overlap): recall@3 is the share of questions whose evidence is in the top 3.

Predict: of the 107 evidence paragraphs, which tool loses the most? (All three read the same text layer.)
pdfplumber loses 71 and keeps 1 intact: on these two-column LaTeX pages it merges a left-column line with the line beside it (section 1) and its 3-point space tolerance glues words (4,590 tokens of 25 or more letters across the 24 papers, against 0 for pypdf). pypdf keeps 60 intact and loses 2: pdfTeX draws columns in reading order, so the stream is nearly right, and the damage is hyphenated line ends (2,577) and ligatures (1,676). Docling keeps 93 intact.

Evidence survives in three groups; recall moves only when word boundaries break

Layout parsers and tools that join line-end hyphens keep almost every paragraph whole (MinerU flash 94 intact, Docling 93, Marker 92, MinerU basic and pymupdf4llm 91, pdftotext 86); plain stream readers break about four in ten at hyphens and ligatures (pypdf 60, pdfminer.six 59, PyMuPDF 61, Unstructured 60 and 62, Tesseract on the rendered pages 64); pdfplumber keeps 1. Little is lost outright, because pdfTeX draws each column in order. Top row: QASPER's own text.

Retrieval barely notices the middle group. Recall@3 of BM25 is 43 to 53% for every tool except pdfplumber (19%), and QASPER's own clean text scores 53%: a paragraph keeping 85% of its words still matches, and only lost word boundaries break it. Damage matters where exact spans matter (quoting, citing, embedding a chunk cut by a column jump). With 58 questions, differences under about 15 points are noise: a broken tokenizer input is fatal, a hyphen is not. And recall on text someone else cleaned says nothing about your parser: run the golden set through your own ingestion at least once.

How the evidence measurement was made

Measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), 2026-10-14: 24 QASPER validation papers (arXiv PDFs, latest version; 12 drawn at random from papers with a question answerable only from a table or figure, 12 from the rest, seed 0), 107 distinct evidence paragraphs behind 58 questions with text evidence. QASPER's BIBREF and INLINEFORM placeholders excluded. A recall hit is a top-3 chunk covering at least half of an evidence paragraph. Top row of the chart: QASPER's own S2ORC text of the same papers, the root's starting point. Script: src/scripts/score_qasper.py.

Which failure each parser has (olmOCR-bench sample)

olmOCR-bench (Allen AI, 1,403 single-page PDFs, 7,010 pass/fail tests) checks facts, not edit distance: a sentence present, a page number absent, sentence A before B, a table cell beside a given neighbour. Five of its seven categories ran here on a seeded sample with the benchmark's own tests.py (v0.4.27, lines 520 to 620 and 621 onward); the two maths categories need a KaTeX renderer. Cells: share of tests passed.

Each category isolates one failure. Headers and footers: layout models drop them (Marker 95, MinerU basic 95, Docling 93), every text extractor keeps them (30 to 39), and so does Unstructured hi_res (28). Tables: text extractors pass 1 of 108 tests, MinerU flash 28%, the other table-aware tools 44 to 73%. Multi column: pdftotext's own column detection (69) beats pypdf (46) and PyMuPDF's sort=True collapses (12). Old scans defeat everything (17 to 26): many are handwritten Library of Congress letters on which Tesseract returns nothing. Long tiny text rewards OCR and line grouping (MinerU 87, Docling 79, Tesseract 72). A 110-page sample on CPU defaults, not a leaderboard.

How the sample was drawn

Measured on Apple M1 Pro (CPU), 2026-10-14, every tool at its default settings. Seeded sample (seed 0) of olmOCR-bench at revision 54a96a6f: 25 PDFs each from multi column, headers and footers, tables and old scans, 10 from long tiny text; tests restricted to those PDFs (counts in the header: 97, 61, 108, 119 and 61). Score per category = share of its tests passed, as the benchmark computes it; Mean = mean of the five categories, with a 95% bootstrap interval over PDFs. The two maths categories and the baseline category are not included, so this mean is not comparable with the published Overall column. Script: src/scripts/score_olmocr.py.
