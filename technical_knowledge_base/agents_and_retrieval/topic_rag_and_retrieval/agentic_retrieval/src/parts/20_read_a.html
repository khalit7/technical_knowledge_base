A retrieval pipeline searches once, with the user's words, and hands the top results to a language model. This page is about what happens when that is not enough and something has to decide what to read: the model itself, in a loop (agentic retrieval); a fixed program that splits the question; a cheap classifier in front (a router); or nobody, because the whole corpus goes into the window (long context). The same machinery, pointed at an agent's own past, is agent memory.

The parent page, {{Topic: rag-and-retrieval|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}}, introduces these ideas in its section 7 and its {{Agentic lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}} tab measures five strategies on 200 HotpotQA questions with Qwen3 models running on this laptop, with a replay of single trajectories. This page reuses those recorded runs (nothing is re-generated) and goes further: what the second search needs, the iterative methods one by one, retrieval evaluators and routers measured on the same questions, where along a trajectory the answer gets lost, deep-research systems read in their source code, the real cost of long context on this machine, and retrieval over an agent's own history. GraphRAG has its own child page, {{GraphRAG and structured retrieval|n:3f85c17b0d0d81e2bb4cdbb749e48a5a}}; search quality itself (chunking, hybrid search, reranking) is on {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}} and {{Rerankers and late interaction|n:3f85c17b0d0d813fba53fc883f8a2c8f}}.

The running example, and how to read the numbers
The topic's multi-hop set: 200 questions from HotpotQA's distractor dev split (160 "bridge" questions, where the second paragraph can only be found once the first is read, and 40 "comparison" questions about two named things), each needing two gold paragraphs, searched over a corpus of 9,760 Wikipedia paragraphs (the gold and distractor paragraphs of 1,000 dev questions; {{the root's data README|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}}). The search tool is bge-small-en-v1.5 (a 33 M-parameter embedding model) with exact search; the generator is Qwen3-1.7B quantised to 4 bits (Q4_K_M) in llama.cpp's llama-server on the laptop's GPU, temperature 0. Answers are scored with HotpotQA's official exact match (EM) and token F1. Every measured number is labelled measured on Apple M1 Pro; small models answer worse than frontier ones, so read the absolute numbers as a small-model case and the gaps and costs as what the experiment shows. A number that needs a generator run this page has not made yet sits in a dashed pending box with the command that will fill it.

Four findings carry the page, all measured on the same 200 questions:

  1. The first hop is free; the second hop is a query-writing problem. The question finds one gold paragraph in its top 3 for @@h_first@@ of 200 questions; for the @@h_need@@ that still miss the second, searching again with the sentence a perfect reasoning step would have written finds it in the top 3 for @@h_sent_top3@@, the question plus that sentence for @@h_q_sent_top3@@, and the bridge entity's exact name for all @@h_title_top3@@. The 1.7B agent's own second queries found it for @@h_agent_top3@@ of @@h_agent_n@@ (section 2).
  2. The loop helps this small model more by reading less at a time than by finding more. The agent saw both gold paragraphs for only @@t_react_both_seen@@ questions against @@t_single_k10_both_seen@@ for a single top-10 search, but when both were in front of it, it answered right @@t_react_rgb@@ of the time against @@t_single_k10_rgb@@ for the top 10 and @@t_longctx_rgb@@ with the question's own 10 paragraphs in the window (section 6).
  3. On all-multi-hop traffic, routers and retrieval evaluators barely beat a coin. The best measured router reaches F1 @@p_crag_ce1_median_f1@@ against @@p_random_half_f1@@ for sending a random half to the agent, while a perfect chooser among the recorded strategies would reach @@p_oracle_all_f1@@ (sections 4 and 5).
  4. Long context is paid for in seconds here, and the attention term grows with the square of the window. A prefill model fitted to @@lc_n@@ recorded calls predicts @@lc_p7611@@ s for 7,611 tokens (the production child measured 9.34 s independently) and about @@lc_p32000@@ s for a full 32k window, two thirds of it attention (section 8).
