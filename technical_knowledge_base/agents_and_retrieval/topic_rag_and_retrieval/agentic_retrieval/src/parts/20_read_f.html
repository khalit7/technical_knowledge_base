6. Evaluating a trajectory: where the answer got lost

The problem. An agent's final answer is right or wrong, but the run that produced it is a sequence of decisions: which query, which results, when to stop. Scoring only the answer cannot tell a retrieval failure from a reading failure, and cannot tell a lucky guess from a grounded answer. The root page splits a pipeline's accuracy as A = rK × pk|K × g (retrieved, then placed in the prompt, then used by the generator); for a trajectory the same split applies to everything the model read along the way. Every question of every recorded strategy falls in one of four bins (measured; "right" = F1 at least 0.5):

rightboth gold read, answer wrong (reading failure)one gold read (retrieval failure, hop 2)no gold read

Read across the bars and the agent's advantage is not where one expects. The top-10 pipeline put both gold paragraphs in front of the model for @@t_single_k10_both_seen@@ questions, the agent for only @@t_react_both_seen@@ (it often stopped early, section 1). But given both, the agent answered right @@t_react_rgb@@ of the time, the top-10 pipeline @@t_single_k10_rgb@@, and the long-context strategy, with both gold paragraphs always present among the question's own 10, @@t_longctx_rgb@@ (and @@t_longctx_tokens8000_rgb@@ in an 8k window). For this small model the bottleneck is reading, and the loop helps reading: each observation is 3 paragraphs, and its own thoughts restate what it found before the next step. Two cautions. The agent was also right @@t_react_right_without_both@@ times without reading both gold paragraphs (comparison questions answered yes or no, or from memory; the closed-book model was right on @@t_closed_right@@): an answer metric alone would credit those. And whether the gain comes from the thoughts or from the smaller observations can be separated by answering from everything the agent read in one prompt, without its thoughts: pending: run_pending.sh core (memory, how=all).

7. Deep research systems: the same loop, for minutes, with parallel sub-agents

The problem. Some questions are reports, not facts: "compare the GPU export rules of three countries since 2022". They need dozens of searches across many sources, more text than fits in one window, and a cited write-up. Deep-research systems scale the agent loop with three additions: a planner that splits the task, parallel sub-agents that each research one part in their own context window, and compression of what each sub-agent found before it returns, so the lead agent's window holds summaries, not pages. Not run here: every one of them needs a web search API key, and this topic runs on the laptop without paid APIs. Three lessons that carry over to a retrieval system you build: the parallel sub-agent is a context-window device (each reads pages the lead never sees), so its value grows with how much must be read, and its cost is the 15 times tokens; the compression step is retrieval in reverse (decide what of a page to keep) and is where citations are most often lost (open_deep_research's citations are written by the model and never checked in code; GPT Researcher only tells the writer not to cite sources it was not given); and stopping is set by budgets in prompts and config, exactly as in section 1, only larger. {{Context engineering|n:3f15c17b0d0d811292cee46514370431}} covers sub-agents and compaction as harness techniques; {{Topic: agentic-frameworks|n:3c65c17b0d0d81ab8432c54d65b7f8c7}} and {{LangChain and LangGraph|n:3c65c17b0d0d81529358c996382224ac}} cover the graph frameworks two of these are built on.
