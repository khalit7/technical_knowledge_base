3. Loops and programs: ReAct, Self-Ask, IRCoT, FLARE, decomposition

Five published ways to interleave retrieval with generation. They differ in two things: where the next query comes from (the model writes it, the model's reasoning sentence is it, a planned sub-question is it) and who decides to stop (the model, a fixed number of steps, a marker string, a confidence threshold). All are measured or pending on the same 200 questions, same tool, same 1.7B generator; published results are the authors' own runs, on other models and corpora, and are not comparable to the numbers here.

ReAct: the model writes every query and decides when to stop

think→search(q1)→read→think→search(q2)→...finish[answer]

Yao et al. 2022 (the {{ReAct paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d816b9a24c28de8f08ea8}}): free-text "Thought", a tool "Action", an "Observation", repeated. Most flexible: any number of searches, any query. Cost: the quadratic token growth of section 1, and every failure mode of a model judging its own progress. With Qwen3-4B the root measured @@f1_4b_react@@ for the loop against @@f1_4b_single_k5@@ for the top 5: the larger model gets more out of the loop.

Self-Ask: explicit follow-up questions

Follow up: Who directed X?→search→Intermediate answer: Y→Follow up: Where is Y from?→search→So the final answer is: Z

Press et al. 2022 ({{arXiv 2210.03350|https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03350}}) named the compositionality gap: the share of two-hop questions a model gets wrong although it answers both sub-questions right; it stayed at roughly 40% across GPT-3 sizes. The prompt makes the model ask follow-ups ("Are follow up questions needed here: Yes." / "Follow up:" / "Intermediate answer:" / "So the final answer is:"), each sent to a search engine. Published (Table 1, Davinci-002): Bamboogle chain of thought 46.4%, Self-Ask 57.6%, Self-Ask with search 60.0%; 2WikiMultiHopQA 29.8, 30.0, 40.1 published. Why it may suit a small model: a follow-up question is a well-formed query that usually names the entity (section 2). Here (a chat-model version: each call writes the next line, a follow-up is searched with top 3 and answered from those paragraphs, and a reader answers at the step limit of 4): F1 @@pd_selfask_f1@@ (95% interval @@pd_selfask_ci@@) at @@pd_selfask_tok@@ tokens and @@pd_selfask_sec@@ s measured. The 1.7B model rarely decided it was done: it wrote "So the final answer is" by itself in @@pd_selfask_self_stop@@ of 200 runs and used all 4 follow-ups in @@pd_selfask_max_search@@, often repeating the first follow-up word for word. On 60 held-out questions the original completion-style prompt scored @@pd_selfask_v1_heldout_f1@@ (the chat model restarted the question instead of continuing the trace); the chat version @@pd_selfask_heldout_f1@@.

IRCoT: the last reasoning sentence is the next query

search(question)→sentence 1→search(sentence 1)→sentence 2→..."So the answer is: ..."

Trivedi et al. 2022 ({{arXiv 2212.10509|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10509}}): retrieve with the question, generate one chain-of-thought sentence from everything retrieved so far, search with that sentence, repeat until a sentence contains "answer is:" or 8 steps, keeping at most 15 paragraphs (BM25, K of 2 to 8 per step, tuned on dev). Published answer F1 (Table 4), one retrieval against IRCoT: HotpotQA 49.7 to 59.1 with Flan-T5-XXL and 53.6 to 60.7 with GPT-3 (code-davinci-002); 2WikiMultihopQA 54.8 to 68.0 with GPT-3 published. The oracle "sent" bar of section 2 is IRCoT's best case on this set: the sentence that states the fact read finds the second paragraph for @@h_sent_top3@@ of @@h_need@@. Here (bge-small top 3 per step, at most 4 steps, then a reader over everything collected, as IRCoT separates retrieval from reading; the prompt chosen on 60 held-out questions, @@pd_ircot_heldout_f1@@ against @@pd_ircot_i2_heldout_f1@@ for a variant that forbade answering early): F1 @@pd_ircot_f1@@ (95% interval @@pd_ircot_ci@@) at @@pd_ircot_tok@@ tokens and @@pd_ircot_sec@@ s measured, the best of the iterative methods here. But it barely iterated: the 1.7B wrote "So the answer is" in its first sentence for @@pd_ircot_one_search@@ of 200 questions, so the gain over the top-5 pipeline comes from a reasoning sentence plus a reader over 3 paragraphs, not from a second retrieval. Given both gold paragraphs it was right @@pd_ircot_rgb@@ of the time, the highest of any strategy.

FLARE: retrieve only when the model is unsure

search(question)→draft answer, token probabilities→any token < θ?→ yes →search(question + confident part of draft)→answer again

Jiang et al. 2023 ({{arXiv 2305.06983|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06983}}): generate the next sentence; if any of its tokens has probability below a threshold θ, use the sentence (with tokens below a second threshold β masked out) as a query and regenerate. The decision to retrieve comes from the model's own confidence, which costs nothing extra to compute (the probabilities come with the tokens). Published (Table 1, text-davinci-003, 2WikiMultihopQA EM / F1): single retrieval 39.4 / 48.8, question decomposition 47.8 / 56.4, FLARE 51.0 / 59.7 (θ 0.8, β 0.4, Table 9) published. Here (token probabilities from llama-server's OpenAI-compatible logprobs field; θ 0.8, β 0.4 as the paper's 2WikiMultihopQA setting; a draft from the top 3, and on low confidence 3 more paragraphs found with the question plus the confident part of the draft): F1 @@pd_flare_f1@@ (95% interval @@pd_flare_ci@@) at @@pd_flare_tok@@ tokens measured, level with the top-5 pipeline at about the same cost. The draft was confident enough to skip the second retrieval for @@pd_flare_one_search@@ of 200 questions, and a confident draft is not a right one: confidence in a short answer says little about whether the second hop was found.

Decomposition (plan, then execute): sub-questions written in advance

split into 1. and 2. (#1)→search(1.)→answer 1→search(2. with #1 filled)→answer

A fixed program: one call plans, the program executes, the model never decides to stop or search again. Cheaper to evaluate and to bound (always 4 calls here), and it cannot loop. Its weakness shows in section 2: a sub-question written before reading cannot name the bridge entity unless "#1" is filled correctly, and the 1.7B's second sub-question found the missing paragraph for @@h_decomp_top3@@ of @@h_decomp_n@@. Here: F1 @@f1_decomp@@ at @@tok_decomp@@ tokens measured (root's run). LangGraph's plan-and-execute template and most deep-research planners are this shape (section 7).

Training the loop: Search-R1 and RL-trained search agents

Everything above is prompting. Jin et al. 2025 ({{Search-R1, arXiv 2503.09516|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516}}) train the loop with reinforcement learning: Qwen2.5-3B and 7B emit <search> queries inside their reasoning, a retriever (E5, top 3, 2018 Wikipedia) inserts results, and PPO (GRPO also tested) optimises only the final answer's exact match, with no format reward. One detail matters for anyone training this: the retrieved tokens are masked out of the loss, so the policy is not trained to predict text it did not write. Published: about 24% (7B) and 20% (3B) average improvement over RAG baselines across seven QA sets; average EM 0.304 for RAG against 0.431 for Search-R1 with Qwen2.5-7B base (Table 2) published. The policy learns when and what to search, which is exactly the query-writing gap section 2 measured. RL training itself is covered on the {{Topic: rl|n:3c65c17b0d0d8195a6c6c11ad093c16e}} page; not run here (a training run of this kind needs GPU-days).
