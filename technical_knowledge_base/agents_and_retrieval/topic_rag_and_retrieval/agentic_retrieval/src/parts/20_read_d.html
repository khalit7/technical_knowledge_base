3. Loops and programs: ReAct, Self-Ask, IRCoT, FLARE, decomposition

Five published ways to interleave retrieval with generation differ in two things: where the next query comes from and who decides to stop. All are run here on the same 200 questions, tool and generators; the papers' own results are on other models and corpora and sit in the closed blocks.

The model's size moves every method more than the choice of method does

F1 with Qwen3-1.7B (open dot) and Qwen3-4B (filled dot), same 200 questions, tool and answer prompt (s3; the root's one-call 4B runs used prompt s2, which did not hurt the 4B: 0 wrongly typed yes/no answers, root README) measured. Every method gains between 0.09 and 0.17 F1 from the larger model (the top 5 the least, Self-Ask the most), about as much as separates the best and the worst method at either size. ReAct: the model writes every query and decides when to stop

think→search(q1)→read→think→search(q2)→...finish[answer]

Most flexible, and it pays the quadratic tokens of section 1 and every failure of a model judging its own progress. Self-Ask: explicit follow-up questions

Follow up: Who directed X?→search→Intermediate answer: Y→So the final answer is: Z

Press et al. 2022 named the compositionality gap: the share of two-hop questions a model gets wrong although it answers both sub-questions right, roughly 40% across GPT-3 sizes. A follow-up question is a well-formed query that usually names the entity. Here F1 @@pd_selfask_f1@@ (95% interval @@pd_selfask_ci@@) at @@pd_selfask_tok@@ tokens: the 1.7B rarely decided it was done, ending by itself in @@pd_selfask_self_stop@@ of 200 runs and using all 4 follow-ups in @@pd_selfask_max_search@@, often repeating the first one word for word. The 4B ended by itself in @@pd_selfask_4b_self_stop@@ of 200.

IRCoT: the last reasoning sentence is the next query

search(question)→sentence 1→search(sentence 1)→..."So the answer is: ..."

Trivedi et al. 2022: retrieve with the question, write one chain-of-thought sentence, search with that sentence, repeat until a sentence contains "answer is:". The oracle "sentence" bar of section 2 is IRCoT's best case here (@@h_sent_top3@@ of @@h_need@@). Here, with a separate reader over everything collected, F1 @@pd_ircot_f1@@ (95% interval @@pd_ircot_ci@@) at @@pd_ircot_tok@@ tokens, the best iterative method; but it barely iterated: the 1.7B wrote "So the answer is" in its first sentence for @@pd_ircot_one_search@@ of 200, so the gain comes from a reasoning sentence plus a reader, not from a second retrieval. Given both gold paragraphs it was right @@pd_ircot_rgb@@ of the time, the highest of any strategy. The 4B comes within a few points of its ReAct loop at less than half the tokens.

FLARE: retrieve only when the model is unsure

search(question)→draft, token probabilities→any token < θ?→ yes →search(question + confident part)

Jiang et al. 2023: if any token of the draft sentence has probability below θ, search with the sentence (tokens below β masked) and regenerate; the decision costs nothing extra, since the probabilities come with the tokens. Here (θ 0.8, β 0.4, the paper's 2WikiMultihopQA setting) F1 @@pd_flare_f1@@ (95% interval @@pd_flare_ci@@) at @@pd_flare_tok@@ tokens, level with the top 5 at about its cost. The draft was confident enough to skip the second search for @@pd_flare_one_search@@ of 200, and a confident draft is not a right one: confidence in a short answer says little about whether the second hop was found.

Decomposition: sub-questions written in advance

split into 1. and 2. (#1)→search(1.)→answer 1→search(2. with #1 filled)→answer

A fixed program: one call plans, the program executes, the model never decides to stop or search again. Easy to bound (always 4 calls here), and it cannot loop; but a sub-question written before reading cannot name the bridge entity unless "#1" is filled correctly (@@h_decomp_top3@@ of @@h_decomp_n@@ second sub-questions found it). Here F1 @@f1_decomp@@ at @@tok_decomp@@ tokens (root's run). LangGraph's plan-and-execute template and most deep-research planners have this shape (section 7).

Search-R1: training the loop instead of prompting it

Jin et al. 2025 train Qwen2.5-3B and 7B with reinforcement learning (PPO; GRPO also tested) to emit <search> queries inside their reasoning; a retriever inserts the results, and only the final answer's exact match is rewarded. The retrieved tokens are masked out of the loss, so the policy is not trained to predict text it did not write. The policy learns when and what to search, which is the query-writing gap of section 2.
