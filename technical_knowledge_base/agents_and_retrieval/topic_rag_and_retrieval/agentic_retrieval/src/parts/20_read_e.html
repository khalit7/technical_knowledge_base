4. Should I search again? Self-RAG, CRAG and retrieval evaluators

The problem. A pipeline answers from whatever came back, even when it is useless. A loop searches again, but only when the model notices. A retrieval evaluator is a separate judgment of the results: good enough, useless, or in between. If it works, the system can spend extra searches only where they are needed.

Self-RAG: the model is trained to judge itself

Asai et al. 2023 ({{arXiv 2310.11511|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511}}) teach the generator four kinds of special reflection tokens: Retrieve (yes, no, continue: should I search now?), ISREL (is this passage relevant?), ISSUP (is my sentence fully, partially or not supported by it?), ISUSE (how useful is the answer, 5 to 1). A critic model (Llama2-7B trained on 4k to 20k GPT-4 judgments per token type) labels about 150k training examples offline (145,619), and the generator (Llama2 7B or 13B) is fine-tuned on them; at inference the tokens drive retrieval and a beam search over segments. Published (Table 2): PopQA 54.9 / 55.8% for Self-RAG 7B / 13B against 51.8% for Llama2-chat-13B with retrieval; PubHealth 72.4 / 74.5 against 52.1 published. It needs training; the untrained imitation here asks the same 1.7B model one yes/no question ("do the paragraphs support this answer?") and falls back to the agent loop on "no": pending: run_pending.sh core (selfcheck).

CRAG: a small evaluator decides correct, incorrect or ambiguous

Yan et al. 2024, Corrective RAG ({{arXiv 2401.15884|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15884}}): a fine-tuned T5-large (0.77 B) scores each retrieved document from -1 to 1. If any score is above an upper threshold, the action is Correct (keep, refine); if all are below a lower threshold, Incorrect (discard, rewrite the query into keywords and search the web); otherwise Ambiguous (both). Kept documents are cut into strips, scored and filtered ("decompose then recompose"). Thresholds are set per dataset (for PopQA upper 0.59, lower -0.99). Published: on the Self-RAG 7B generator, PopQA 52.8 for plain RAG against 59.8 for CRAG, PubHealth 39.0 against 75.6 (Table 1); the evaluator's accuracy at telling relevant from irrelevant, 84.3% against 58.0% for ChatGPT prompted to judge (Table 4) published.

Measured here: can a cheap signal tell a good search from a bad one?

5. Routers: deciding once, before anything runs

The problem. The agent loop costs @@tok_react@@ tokens per question against @@tok_single_k5@@ for the pipeline (3.3 times), and most real traffic is easy. A router looks at the question once and sends it to the cheapest strategy likely to answer it. {{Adaptive-RAG|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14403}} (Jeong et al. 2024) trains a T5-Large classifier to predict a question's complexity and route to no retrieval, one search or the multi-step loop: with FLAN-T5-XL it reached 46.94 F1 against 48.85 for always multi-step, at 3.60 against 8.81 times the single-step time (Table 1), although the classifier was right only 54.52% of the time (Table 4); a perfect router would have reached 56.28 published. Their per-query times show why routing matters: 0.35 s with no retrieval, 3.08 s with one search, 27.18 s multi-step (Table 3) published.
