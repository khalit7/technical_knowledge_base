4. Should I search again? Retrieval evaluators

A pipeline answers from whatever came back; a loop searches again only when the model notices. A retrieval evaluator judges the results separately (good enough, useless, in between), so extra searches are spent only where needed. Two published designs: Self-RAG (Asai et al. 2023) trains the generator itself to emit reflection tokens (Retrieve: search now?; ISREL: is this passage relevant?; ISSUP: is my sentence supported by it?; ISUSE: how useful is the answer); CRAG (Yan et al. 2024) puts a small fine-tuned T5-large in front that scores each document and picks Correct (keep and refine), Incorrect (discard, rewrite the query, search the web) or Ambiguous (both).

Self-RAG and CRAG in detail, with published results; the AUROC table

Self-RAG ({{arXiv 2310.11511|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511}}): Retrieve takes yes, no or continue; ISSUP fully, partially or not; ISUSE 5 to 1. A critic model (Llama2-7B trained on 4k to 20k GPT-4 judgments per token type) labels about 150k training examples offline (145,619), and the generator (Llama2 7B or 13B) is fine-tuned on them; at inference the tokens drive retrieval and a beam search over segments. Published (Table 2): PopQA 54.9 / 55.8% for Self-RAG 7B / 13B against 51.8% for Llama2-chat-13B with retrieval; PubHealth 72.4 / 74.5 against 52.1 published.

CRAG ({{arXiv 2401.15884|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15884}}): the T5-large (0.77 B) scores each document from -1 to 1; any above an upper threshold means Correct, all below a lower threshold Incorrect, otherwise Ambiguous. Kept documents are cut into strips, scored and filtered ("decompose then recompose"). Thresholds are set per dataset (for PopQA upper 0.59, lower -0.99). Published: on the Self-RAG 7B generator, PopQA 52.8 for plain RAG against 59.8 for CRAG, PubHealth 39.0 against 75.6 (Table 1); the evaluator tells relevant from irrelevant with 84.3% accuracy against 58.0% for ChatGPT prompted to judge (Table 4) published.

5. Routers: deciding once, before anything runs

The agent loop costs @@tok_react@@ tokens per question against @@tok_single_k5@@ for the pipeline, and most real traffic is easy. A router looks at the question once and sends it to the cheapest strategy likely to answer it. Adaptive-RAG (Jeong et al. 2024) trains a T5-Large classifier on question complexity to choose no retrieval, one search or the multi-step loop, and gets close to always-multi-step accuracy at well under half its time, although its classifier is right only about half the time (details below). Here every router chooses between two recorded strategies per question, so each policy is exact and costs no new generation (the router's own cost is not counted: the signals come from the search the pipeline makes anyway; the cross-encoder adds 58 ms for 10 pairs on this laptop, root pipeline runs).
