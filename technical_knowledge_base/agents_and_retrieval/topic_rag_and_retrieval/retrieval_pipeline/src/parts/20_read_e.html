5. Understanding and rewriting the query

The problem. The text a user types is often not the best text to search with. It may need a different retriever (an error code wants BM25), contain a condition that should be a filter ("papers from 2019"), lean on earlier turns of a chat ("does it help?"), be phrased unlike the documents (a question against statements), or ask two things at once. Query understanding is everything done to the query before the search: routing, extracting filters, and rewriting. Each costs time before retrieval can start, so each has to earn its place on measured queries.

5.1 Routing: which retriever for which query?

If some queries are served better by BM25 and others by dense search, a router that sends each query to the right one beats always fusing. The ceiling is large. On SciFact, BM25 beats Qwen3-Embedding on @@rt_sf_bm_better@@ claims, loses on @@rt_sf_de_better@@ and ties on @@rt_sf_tie@@; a perfect router choosing per claim among BM25, dense and hybrid would score @@rt_sf_oracle3@@, against @@rt_sf_hybrid@@ for always-hybrid measured. The question is whether anything visible before the answer predicts the winner.

A router was trained on SciFact's training claims (logistic regression on nine features computable at query time: query length, the highest and mean idf of its terms, how identifier-like it is, each retriever's top score and its margin over the second, and how much the two top 10s overlap), with its two thresholds (send to BM25 above one, to dense below the other, fuse in between) chosen on the same training claims. On test it separates "BM25 wins" from "dense wins" only weakly (area under the ROC curve @@rt_sf_auc@@) and scores @@rt_sf_router@@, slightly below always-hybrid. On NFCorpus: @@rt_nf_router@@ against @@rt_nf_hybrid@@ (hybrid) and @@rt_nf_dense@@ (dense alone).

The folk rule "identifiers go to BM25" fails here too: the @@rt_sf_ident_n@@ claims containing a digit or an inner capital (gene and protein names) score @@rt_sf_ident_de@@ with dense search and @@rt_sf_ident_bm@@ with BM25. So: fuse by default; route only between query types that really differ, such as exact lookups of part numbers against natural questions, and check on labelled queries that the router beats fusion. Routing between whole sources or tools (a vector index, a SQL database, web search) by a classifier or an LLM is measured on {{Agentic retrieval, long context and memory|n:3c65c17b0d0d8105a68cfb504ed4b0e8}}.

5.2 Self-query: turning words in the question into filters

"In the CoVoST paper, how many languages..." contains a condition on metadata (which paper) mixed into the question. Embedded as is, the title words only nudge the vector; as a filter, they restrict the search to the right document. Self-query retrieval asks a language model to split the request into a cleaned query and a structured filter over known metadata fields (LangChain's SelfQueryRetriever pattern). Measured with the local generator (Qwen3-1.7B, greedy) on @@sq_n@@ constructed requests: real QASPER questions with a reference to their paper added by template, a third by title, a third by month and year of the arXiv posting, a third by year only; search over all 6,463 default chunks of all 281 papers with bge-small; found = the root's rule within 1,024 tokens.

The filter must be applied inside the search (section 6), and an extracted filter must be checked against the schema: a wrong year silently removes the right document, which is worse than no filter. Field values extracted from free text (titles, names) need fuzzy matching against the real values; here the title was matched to the best word overlap among the papers that survive the year and month filters.

5.3 Rewriting: HyDE, multi-query, step-back, decomposition, conversation

The root lab measured three rewrites on SciFact with the same generator root lab: HyDE (write a hypothetical answer passage and search with it; {{Gao et al., arXiv 2212.10496|https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10496}}) lifts Qwen3-Embedding from 0.700 to 0.718 when the passage replaces the claim, and helps bge-small only when the passage vector is averaged with the claim's (0.713 → 0.733; the passage alone gives 0.692); multi-query (three rewordings, fused with RRF) buys nothing on these already specific claims (0.699 for Qwen3); conversational rewrite turns a follow-up that is meaningless alone into a standalone question and repairs most of eight constructed chats (the relevant abstract at rank 1 for 6 of 8 with bge-small, against 2 of 8 for the follow-up alone; one chat got worse). The old version of this page said HyDE is "largely superseded by instruction-aware embedders": on this set it still helped the instruction-aware Qwen3-Embedding by 1.8 points, so that claim is dropped.

What it costs.
