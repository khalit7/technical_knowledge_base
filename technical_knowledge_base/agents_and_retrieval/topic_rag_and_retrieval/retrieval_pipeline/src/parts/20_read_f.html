6. Metadata filters: inside the search, never after it

Real queries carry conditions: this customer's documents, what this user may read, papers after 2020, the current version. A condition on metadata (structured fields stored next to each chunk) is a filter. The root lab measured the central fact: applied after the search (a post-filter: fetch the top 10, drop what is not allowed), a filter that lets a user see 10% of SciFact returns 1.73 results of 10 on average and none at all for 14 of 300 claims; applied inside the search (a pre-filter: only allowed documents compete) it always returns 10 root lab.

What to store on every chunk

  • Identity and provenance: document id, chunk index, character span or page, section path (the Markdown splitter's heading metadata, §1), source URL. Needed for citations (§8) and for deleting or replacing a document's chunks.
  • Version and time: document version or content hash, published and indexed dates. Needed for "current only" and for re-indexing (the ingestion page's Re-indexing lab).
  • Access: tenant, owner, groups allowed. Needed for access control, which must be a filter inside the search, never an instruction in the prompt; measured with PostgreSQL row-level security on {{RAG in production|n:3f85c17b0d0d81aa9ba9c0ceac9e28c1}}.
  • Facets users filter on: language, document type, product, year. Store as typed fields (integer year, date), not inside the text, so the engine can index them.

Every chunk inherits its document's fields at index time; a metadata change (a permission revoked) must update every chunk of the document, in both the BM25 and the vector index.

The arithmetic of post-filtering

If an index returns its top K and then a filter keeps a share s of documents (the filter's selectivity), the expected number of survivors is about K · s, assuming the allowed documents are spread evenly through the ranking. The {{pgvector README|https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector/blob/v0.8.7/README.md}} gives the same arithmetic for its HNSW index: "If a condition matches 10% of rows, with HNSW and the default hnsw.ef_search of 40, only 4 rows will match on average" (README line 450 at tag v0.8.7, verified). Against the root lab's measurement:

A post-filter keeps about K · s of the K results it fetched

Lines: K · s capped at 10. Dots: results returned of 10 asked, post-filter over the exact top K, mean over 300 SciFact claims (root lab, src/pipe/out/filter.json; each claim asked by a user who may see its evidence abstract, the constructed setting described there).

The measured counts sit above K · s at small K because the user may always see the claim's own evidence abstract, which ranks high; real access patterns are correlated in both directions, so measure the survivors on your own data. The fix puts the filter inside the search (engines rescan or filter during graph traversal; closed block below), measured on {{Vector indexes from the inside|n:3f85c17b0d0d81f29707c130b2ebcfb1}} (its filtered-search lab).

Correlated access, and how engines filter inside the search (depth)

Correlation cuts both ways: a user's own documents are often exactly what they search for; a tenant's documents may all sit far from a generic query. pgvector from 0.8.0 rescans more of the index until enough rows pass (SET hnsw.iterative_scan = strict_order or relaxed_order, stopping at hnsw.max_scan_tuples, 20,000 by default; README lines 480 and 527); Qdrant and Weaviate filter during graph traversal and switch to an exact scan when few points match.

Predict: hybrid search, with the filter applied to the fused list after RRF (each retriever returns its top 100 unfiltered). A tenant owns 1% of the corpus. How many results come back when you ask for 10?
About 2: the fused list holds at most 200 distinct documents, of which about 1% belong to the tenant. Filtering after fusion is a post-filter. Put the filter into each retriever's own search (a filter clause on BM25's posting lists, a filtered vector search), then fuse.

Filters in hybrid search

BM25 filters cheaply: the engine intersects the query terms' posting lists with the set of allowed documents (a bitmap), so a selective filter makes BM25 faster. A vector index pays for a selective filter instead, because its graph or clusters were built over every document. So the two halves of a hybrid search behave differently under the same filter, and both must receive it before fusion. The filters a user never typed (tenant, permissions) come from the session; the ones they typed come from query understanding (§5).

7. Duplicates and diversity

Ten retrieved chunks that say the same thing waste nine slots of the prompt. Duplicates come from the corpus (the same abstract under two ids, a page crawled twice, mirrored documentation), from overlapping chunks (neighbours share their overlap), and from near-identical versions. Two remedies: remove duplicates, or penalise similarity among the chosen results.

NFCorpus has @@dup_nf_995@@ pairs above cosine 0.995 with bge-small, and they are true duplicates: for example "@@dup_ex_title@@" appears as @@dup_ex_ids@@ with identical text measured. Exact duplicates are best removed at ingestion with a content hash, before they reach any index (the ingestion page covers content hashing). How much near-duplication each set has:

MMR ({{Carbonell and Goldstein, SIGIR 1998|https://doi.org/10.1145/290941.291025}}) builds the list greedily, each time taking the candidate most similar to the query and least similar to anything already taken. LangChain's max_marginal_relevance_search uses it with fetch_k 20 candidates and lambda_mult 0.5 by default (langchain-core 1.6.10, vectorstores/base.py lines 663 and 664). On HotpotQA, where diversity sounds like exactly what a two-hop question needs, the questions with both gold paragraphs in the top 5 fall from @@mmr_hp_10_5@@ (λ 1) to @@mmr_hp_08_5@@ at 0.8 and @@mmr_hp_05_5@@ at 0.5 (bge-small) measured. The two gold paragraphs are not near-duplicates of each other, so MMR has nothing to remove, and the penalty pushes in loosely related paragraphs instead. MMR pays when a corpus has many near-duplicates crowding out other relevant material and when the reader wants coverage of several aspects; check that your corpus is like that before turning it on, and keep λ high.

Overlapping chunks: merge, do not drop

With overlapping fixed windows, a chunk and its neighbour often both rank high, because both hold part of the same paragraph. Treating the neighbour as a duplicate and dropping it is wrong: on QASPER (bge-small, within the paper) dropping any chunk that overlaps one already chosen cuts the questions found within 1,024 tokens from @@ov_plain@@ to @@ov_drop@@, because the neighbour is what completes a paragraph the window boundary cut. Merging overlapping or touching chunks into one span and paying for the shared tokens once gives @@ov_merge@@; MMR at λ 0.7 gives @@ov_mmr07@@ measured.
