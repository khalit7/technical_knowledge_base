The retrieval pipeline is everything between a pile of documents and the handful of passages a language model reads before it answers. At index time (before any question arrives) documents are cut into chunks, each chunk keeps its metadata (source, date, owner), and the chunks go into two indexes: a word index scored by BM25 and a vector index of embeddings. At query time the question may be rewritten, both indexes are searched with any filter applied inside the search, the two ranked lists are fused, a reranker reorders the best few dozen, and the top few chunks are written into the prompt with their sources.

documents→chunk (§1)→BM25 index (§2), learned sparse (§3)vector index→rewrite the query (§5)→filter inside each search (§6)→fuse (§4)→rerank→deduplicate, diversify (§7)→assemble the prompt (§8)

The root page, {{Topic: rag-and-retrieval|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}}, gives the intuition for each stage and its {{Pipeline lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b89145c37dfe8a3b0b}} tab measures the main results on the running example (chunking strategies at equal token budgets, RRF and score fusion, pre- against post-filtering, HyDE and multi-query, reranking depth, and where an answer gets lost, A = r × p × g). This page is the depth behind those results: what the libraries actually do (read in their source at pinned versions), every knob swept, and the questions the root only names. Rerankers have their own page, {{Rerankers and late interaction|n:3f85c17b0d0d813fba53fc883f8a2c8f}}; turning PDFs into clean text, and how chunk boundaries move when a document is revised, are on {{Document ingestion and multimodal retrieval|n:3f85c17b0d0d81279079f614f382f2e7}} (its Re-indexing lab).

The running example (defined in the root's data README): SciFact, 5,183 scientific abstracts and 300 test claims, most with exactly one relevant abstract; NFCorpus, 3,633 medical documents and 323 test questions with many graded relevant documents each; QASPER, 281 NLP papers with 892 questions whose evidence paragraphs annotators located in the text (the set for chunking); HotpotQA, 200 two-hop questions over 9,760 Wikipedia paragraphs. Metrics: nDCG@10 (normalised discounted cumulative gain over the top 10: 1.0 when the relevant documents fill the top ranks, less the lower they sit) and recall@k (the share of relevant documents found in the top k). Everything marked measured ran on an Apple M1 Pro in October 2026; numbers marked published come from the cited source; constructed marks inputs written for this page.

The one-line lesson
Most knobs move the result by one or two points; three move it by ten or more: cutting documents along their own structure, putting a strong reranker after a recall-oriented first stage, and (for chat) rewriting the follow-up into a standalone question. Measure every knob on your own labelled queries, tuned on one split and reported on another.
