The idea in one screen

An answer can use only the evidence that survives every stage: cut into a chunk that holds it whole, found by words or vectors under the user's filters, kept through fusion and reranking into the few chunks the prompt holds, then read and cited. So the pipeline is built for recall first, and judged by where, on your own labelled questions, the evidence is lost.

Each stage, the knob that matters, and what it did on the running example

Index-time stages run once per document, query-time stages once per question. In blue under each stage, the largest measured effect of its main knob; the blue counts along the query stages follow the evidence of 188 SciFact claims through the root's measured pipeline (hybrid RRF, Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B over the top 30, the top 3 in the prompt, Qwen3-1.7B answering). Most knobs move a result by one or two points; three move it by ten or more: cutting along the document's structure, a strong reranker after a recall-oriented first stage, and rewriting a chat follow-up into a standalone question. measured

Chunk
a piece of a document indexed on its own; the unit retrieval returns.
BM25
the standard word-matching score over an inverted index.
Hybrid search, fusion
run BM25 and a dense retriever, then merge the two ranked lists into one.
RRF
reciprocal rank fusion: each list adds 1 / (k + rank) for each document.
Pre-, post-filter
a metadata condition applied inside the search, or to its results afterwards.
Small to big
search small units (sentences), hand over the larger unit (paragraph) that contains the match.
HyDE, multi-query
search with a generated answer passage, or with several rewordings fused.
Self-query
a model turns conditions in the question into a structured filter.
MMR
maximal marginal relevance: choose each next result for relevance minus similarity to those already chosen.
r, p, g
found in the first stage's top K, kept in the top k that reach the prompt, answered correctly: A = r × p × g.

The running example (defined in the root's data README): SciFact, 5,183 scientific abstracts and 300 test claims, most with exactly one relevant abstract; NFCorpus, 3,633 medical documents and 323 test questions with many graded relevant documents each; QASPER, 281 NLP papers with 892 questions whose evidence paragraphs annotators located in the text (the set for chunking); HotpotQA, 200 two-hop questions over 9,760 Wikipedia paragraphs. Scores are nDCG@10 (1.0 when the relevant documents fill the top ranks) and recall@k (share of relevant documents in the top k), defined in section 9. measured = run on an Apple M1 Pro in October 2026; published = from the cited source; constructed = inputs written for this page.
