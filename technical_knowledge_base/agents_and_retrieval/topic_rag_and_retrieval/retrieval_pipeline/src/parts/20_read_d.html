4. Fusion: merging two ranked lists

Hybrid search runs BM25 and a dense retriever on the same query and gets two ranked lists whose scores live on unrelated scales: a BM25 score of 8.96 and a cosine of 0.71. The root's shared runs measured the two common answers (RRF lifts Qwen3-Embedding on SciFact from 0.700 to 0.724; z-score fusion with a weight tuned on training claims reaches 0.739) root runs. This section is why each works and what each throws away.

Why raw scores cannot simply be added. BM25 is unbounded above (a SciFact claim's top-1 score ranges from @@bm_top1_lo@@ to @@bm_top1_hi@@, 5th to 95th percentile); Qwen3-Embedding's top-1 cosines range from @@de_top1_lo@@ to @@de_top1_hi@@ measured. Add them with equal weight and BM25 decides almost alone: 0.5 · BM25 + 0.5 · cosine scores @@raw05_sf_q@@, close to BM25 alone (@@bm_sf_def@@). The trap is the equal weight, not the addition: give the cosine @@raw_best_sf_q_w@@ of the weight (chosen on training claims) and the raw sum scores @@raw_best_sf_q@@, as good as any normalised method. The weight absorbs the scale difference, so it must be learned on labelled queries for each pair of retrievers, and again whenever either changes.

RRF(d) = Σlists r 1 / (k + rankr(d))
rankr(d)
position of d in list r, from 1; a list that does not contain d adds 0
k
60 in the paper ({{Cormack, Clarke and Büttcher, SIGIR 2009|https://doi.org/10.1145/1571941.1572114}}: "fixed during a pilot investigation"), the default rank_constant in Elasticsearch and OpenSearch and in most libraries: it damps the top ranks, so being in both lists counts for more than being first in one
Predict (the old page's worked example): dense returns A, B, C, D; BM25 returns C, D, E, A. With k = 60, what is the fused order?
C, A, D, B, E. C: 1/63 + 1/61 = 0.032266; A: 1/61 + 1/64 = 0.032018; D: 1/64 + 1/62 = 0.031754; B: 1/62 = 0.016129; E: 1/63 = 0.015873 (recomputed for this page; the old page's arithmetic is right). Anything in both lists beats anything in one, whatever its rank there.

RRF uses only ranks, so it needs no calibration and no tuning, and the root's runs found k barely matters (0.724 to 0.732 across k from 1 to 1,000). Watch it work on real lists: the card fuses one query's BM25 and dense top 20 with method A, then re-sorts the same candidates into method B's order.

What RRF throws away is how sure each retriever is. Scores carry that: on SciFact, when a retriever's first result beats its second by a wide relative margin, the first result is relevant far more often.

The whole family, tuned honestly

The score-combination family is older than RRF ({{Fox and Shaw, TREC-2, 1994|https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec2/papers/txt/23.txt}}): normalise each list (min-max or z-score), then add with a weight (CombSUM), or multiply that sum by the number of lists containing the document (CombMNZ, an explicit agreement bonus). Each knob was chosen on the tuning split (SciFact train, NFCorpus dev) and scored once on test; the root's RRF, min-max and z-score numbers are reproduced exactly.

CombMAX keeps each document's best normalised score and is the weak member (it ignores agreement); weighted RRF gives each list its own weight; Borda count adds positions. Fusing three lists (BM25, Qwen3 and gte with RRF) scores @@three_sf@@ on SciFact, against @@two_dense_sf@@ for the two embedders alone. In products the choice is a setting: Elasticsearch, OpenSearch and Weaviate let you weight the lists or fuse normalised scores instead of ranks (OpenSearch's normalization processor, {{docs|https://docs.opensearch.org/latest/search-plugins/search-pipelines/normalization-processor/}}); tune the weights on labelled queries, never by eye. Hybrid search measured inside Postgres (BM25 over Postgres lexemes, pgvector, and RRF in one SQL statement on 522,931 Quora questions and SciFact) is on {{Search and vector databases|n:3ef5c17b0d0d815eb4caef9b4cd4d732}}, section 11.

After a reranker, RRF is enough

The old page claimed weighted fusion "rarely beats plain RRF plus a reranker". Tested with the recorded Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B scores, reranking the top K of each fused list on SciFact test:

Before the reranker z-score fusion is ahead (@@ftr_z_before@@ against @@ftr_rrf_before@@); after it the gap closes or flips: RRF's top 20, reranked, scores @@ftr_rrf20@@, z-score's @@ftr_z20@@; at K 30 they score @@ftr_rrf30@@ and @@ftr_z30@@. The reranker reorders the candidates itself, so what matters is which documents are in the top K, not their order. So the claim holds here: tune the fusion weight when there is no reranker; with one, RRF is enough and the effort is better spent on the depth K (measured on {{Rerankers and late interaction|n:3f85c17b0d0d813fba53fc883f8a2c8f}}).
