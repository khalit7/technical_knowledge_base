4. Pods: a torus of chips, seen from the chip

A GPU cluster joins 8 or 72 GPUs through NVLink switches and everything else through network cards. A TPU instead has its own inter-chip interconnect (ICI) ports wired straight to its neighbours, 4 of them on a 2D torus (v2, v3, v5e, v6e) or 6 on a 3D one (v4, v5p, Ironwood), with no switch between chips; TPU v4 and later build the pod from 4 × 4 × 4 cubes of 64 chips whose faces are joined by optical circuit switches, mirrors that set which cube is plugged into which. The topology, the switches, twisted tori, Ironwood's pod and the data-centre network beyond it are taught on {{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}} (sections 6 and 7, with the torus collective cost model); this section keeps what the chip count and the slice shape change for one job.

What a slice costs a collective. The scaling book's rule for an axis with a wraparound: an all-reduce of V bytes takes about 2V / Wbidi, independent of the number of chips on the axis; several axes at once divide the time; an axis without the wraparound about doubles it ({{scaling book, TPUs|https://jax-ml.github.io/scaling-book/tpus/}}). For the root's 8B model, all-reducing its 16.06 GB of bf16 gradients along one 16-chip v5p axis takes about 0.178 s, 0.059 s over all three axes, and 0.357 s without wraparound derived, against 62.5 ms over 8 H100s' NVLink in the root. Only whole cubes get the optical wraparound, so the shape you ask for matters. Ironwood keeps the scheme at 9,216 chips: 144 cubes of 64, 1.77 PB of HBM in one pod.

Rules and sources for the slice builder

Wraparound: "Any multiple of a full cube (e.g. 4x4x4 or 4x4x8) will have wraparounds provided by the optical switches"; "a 2x2x4 won't have any wraparounds"; without one "typically doubles the time of most communication" ({{How To Scale Your Model, TPUs|https://jax-ml.github.io/scaling-book/tpus/}}). Here an axis wraps when every dimension is a multiple of 4. Per-link bandwidth one way / both ways: v4 45 / 90 GB/s, v5p and TPU7x 90 / 180 GB/s (scaling book; the TPU v4 paper gives "6 links @ 50 GB/s"). All-reduce time = 2V / Σ W over the chosen axes, W = both-way bandwidth with wraparound and one-way without, and at least 1 μs per hop along the axes (the book's Tmin) derived; real collectives reach about 95% of the link figure (the book's measurement on v5e). Hosts hold 4 chips (2 × 2 × 1). Optical links: 96 per cube (16 per face, TPU v4 paper). Largest pods: v4 4,096 chips (16 × 16 × 16), v5p 8,960, TPU7x 9,216.

The mesh is the machine
Because the pod is a grid, TPU programs describe parallelism as a grid too: a JAX Mesh with named axes ('data', 'model') laid onto the physical torus, and every array annotated with which axes split it. Choosing which model axis goes on which physical axis is choosing which links carry which traffic.
