PyTorch runs as a native device on TPUs and Trainium. Google's earlier path, PyTorch/XLA, built on lazy tensors and needed intrusive source changes; on 20 October 2026 Google and Meta released TorchTPU (open source) and AWS put TorchNeuron into public beta. Both take the same shape: an out-of-tree backend registered through PyTorch's PrivateUse1 dispatch key (how that works: {{Extending PyTorch|n:3f85c17b0d0d8158a7addcd3d601d72a}}), a real eager mode, and torch.compile captured by Dynamo but lowered to StableHLO for the vendor's compiler instead of through Inductor. TorchTPU implements about 450 ATen ops, launches one process per chip (JAX runs one per host) and turns torch.distributed collectives into XLA collective ops. Google reports, as vendor numbers, 98% (4,821 of 4,917) of popular Hugging Face models running, Qwen3 MoE 235B in TorchTitan on 64 TPU v7 chips at 1,828 tokens/s against 1,937 for JAX's MaxText, and 27.1% MFU on 8,192 chips ({{PyTorch blog, Oct 2026|https://pytorch.org/blog/introducing-torchtpu/}}). vLLM and SGLang TPU engines are built on it, and Meta's PyTorch-TPU lead says "SGLang-TorchTPU is running in production at Meta today" ({{Google Developers Blog, Oct 20 2026|https://developers.googleblog.com/announcing-new-vllm-and-sglang-inference-engines-on-tpu/}}). The hardware underneath is unchanged: compiled shapes are still static, and the mesh still has to fit the torus.

The checklist before you port a training job
  1. Does your model's arithmetic land on the matrix units? Matmul dimensions multiples of 128 (or 256), batch per chip well above the array's side; elementwise-heavy or custom ops run on the slow vector units.
  2. Are your shapes static? Variable sequence lengths mean padding or bucketing on TPU and Trainium, or recompiling every step.
  3. Which custom ops do you rely on? FlashAttention variants, fused optimizers, MoE routing kernels: each must exist for the target (Pallas, NKI, HIP or Triton on AMD) or be written.
  4. How big is the scale-up domain, and what shape? 8 GPUs on an AMD board, 72 on an NVL72 rack, 16 to 144 Trainium chips, thousands of TPUs on one torus: this decides how far tensor and expert parallelism can stretch (the root's "Joining chips" section).
  5. Measure, then cost. Spec sheets do not rank chips: compare achieved throughput on your model at the same precision, then price per hour (the root's Performance calculator and Chip atlas).
Interview rehearsal (one design question)
When would you not use a TPU?
When the model needs dynamic shapes or control flow, custom kernels that exist only in CUDA, or ops that do not map to big matmuls. PyTorch itself is no longer the obstacle (TorchTPU, above); CUDA-only libraries and kernels still are.
