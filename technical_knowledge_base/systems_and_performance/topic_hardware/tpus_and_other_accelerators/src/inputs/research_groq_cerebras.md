# Groq, Cerebras, energy: research notes (fetched 2026-10-05)

## 1. Groq first-gen TSP, ISCA 2020 "Think Fast" (Abts et al.)
Source: Wayback copy of Groq-hosted PDF, https://web.archive.org/web/2021/https://groq.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/ISCA-TSP.pdf (paper: 2020 ACM/IEEE 47th ISCA, June 2020). Live groq.com URL now 404. Fetched 2026-10-05. PRIMARY.
- Abstract: "guaranteeing determinism by eliminating all reactive elements in the hardware (e.g. arbiters, and caches)."
- Abstract: "computational density of more than 1 TeraOp/s per square mm of silicon for its 25×29 mm 14nm chip operating at a nominal clock frequency of 900 MHz." (25 x 29 mm = 725 mm2, my arithmetic)
- Abstract: ResNet50 "20.4K processed images per second (IPS) with a batch-size of one".
- Sec. I-B: "220 MiBytes of globally shared SRAM that delivers 32 bytes per lane of stream bandwidth"; "the MEM can read and MXM can install 400K weights into all four 320×320 arrays in less than 40 cycles". "144 independent instruction queues (ICUs)". maxVL "20×16=320 elements".
- Sec. II functional slices: VXM "a 4×4 mesh of ALUs in each lane for point-wise arithmetic"; MXM "four (4) independent 2D MACC (multiply-accumulate) arrays that operate on int8 or fp16 data types"; SXM "for intra-superlane and inter-lane switching by rearranging elements of vectors"; MEM: "East and West hemisphere ... composed of 44 parallel slices of SRAM ... for a total of 220 MiBytes of on-chip SRAM." Sec. III-B: "Each MEM slice comprises 20 tiles ... yielding a 2.5 Mibyte per-slice capacity, or 220 MiBytes for all 88 slices on-chip."
- C2C: "sixteen (16) ×4 links operating at 30 Gbps each"; "16 ×4 ×30Gb/s ×2 directions = 3.84 Tb/s of off-chip pin bandwidth that can be flexibly partitioned to support high-radix ... interconnection networks of TSPs". Host interface PCIe Gen4.
- Bandwidth (at an assumed 1 GHz, "For the sake of exposition"): stream registers "20 TiB/s"; SRAM "= 55 TiB/s on-chip memory bandwidth, or 27.5 TiB/s of SRAM bandwidth in each hemisphere."
- Sec. V-F: "The TSP's hardware eliminates arbiters and other reactive elements in the data path, making performance deterministic and precisely predictable from run-to-run execution."
- Conclusion: "four (4) MXM arrays of 320×320 MACC ... cores"; "5,120 vector ALUs on-chip"; "Supporting both int8 and fp16 native data types allows a single-chip solution for both quantized inference models and model training with floating point."; "The first-generation Groq TSP (at 1 GHz) in 14nm ASIC and packaged as a PCIe CEM form factor, yields a peak performance of 820 TeraOps/sec from 26.8B transistors on-chip".
- NOTE disagreement: abstract says nominal 900 MHz; the 820 TOPS peak is stated "at 1 GHz". The paper gives no separate FP16 TFLOPS figure (grep found none). Paper says MiB (220 MiB); Groq marketing usually says "230 MB" (220 MiB = 230.7 MB, so both are the same quantity). FP16 TFLOPS ("188 TFLOPS") and 1,000 TOPS marketing figures: UNCONFIRMED here.
- ECC: "SECDED"; 9-bit ECC on 128-bit word.

## 9b. TPU v1 paper, Jouppi et al., arXiv 1704.04760 (ISCA 2017, June 2017)
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.04760 (arXiv v1 April 2017). Fetched 2026-10-05. PRIMARY.
- Abstract: "The heart of the TPU is a 65,536 8-bit MAC matrix multiply unit that offers a peak throughput of 92 TeraOps/second (TOPS) and a large (28 MiB) software-managed on-chip memory." / "the TPU is on average about 15X - 30X faster than its contemporary GPU or CPU, with TOPS/Watt about 30X - 80X higher." / "using the GPU's GDDR5 memory in the TPU would triple achieved TOPS and raise TOPS/Watt to nearly 70X the GPU and 200X the CPU." / deterministic execution: "a better match to the 99th-percentile response-time requirement ... than are the time-varying optimizations of CPUs and GPUs (caches, out-of-order execution, multithreading, multiprocessing, prefetching, ...)".
- Sec. 1: "Eight-bit integer multiplies can be 6X less energy and 6X less area than IEEE 754 16-bit floating-point multiplies, and the advantage for integer addition is 13X in energy and 38X in area [Dal16]." (No Horowitz pJ table in this paper; grep for pJ found nothing.)
- Sec. 2: "It contains 256x256 MACs that can perform 8-bit multiply-and-adds"; "4 MiB of 32-bit Accumulators ... 4096, 256-element, 32-bit accumulators"; "8-bit weights and 16-bit activations ... half-speed ... quarter-speed when both are 16 bits"; "holds one 64 KiB tile of weights plus one for double-buffering (to hide the 256 cycles it takes to shift a tile in)"; "off-chip 8 GiB DRAM called Weight Memory"; "24 MiB on-chip Unified Buffer ... almost a third of the die and the Matrix Multiply Unit is a quarter"; "Control is just 2%"; PCIe Gen3 x16; designed, verified, built, deployed "in just 15 months".
- Sec. 2, weight-stationary systolic description (verbatim): "As reading a large SRAM uses much more power than arithmetic, the matrix unit uses systolic execution to save energy by reducing reads and writes of the Unified Buffer [Kun80][Ram91][Ovt15b]. It relies on data from different directions arriving at cells in an array at regular intervals where they are combined. Figure 4 shows that data flows in from the left, and the weights are loaded from the top. A given 256-element multiply-accumulate operation moves through the matrix as a diagonal wavefront. The weights are preloaded, and take effect with the advancing wave alongside the first data of a new block. Control and data are pipelined to give the illusion that the 256 inputs are read at once, and that they instantly update one location of each of 256 accumulators. From a correctness perspective, software is unaware of the systolic nature of the matrix unit".
- Table 2 (TPU row): process 28 nm, 700 MHz, TDP 75W, measured idle 28W / busy 40W, 92 TOPS (8b), 34 GB/s, 28 MiB on-chip; die "≤ half the Haswell die size" (die mm2 given as NA). Server with 4 TPUs: TDP 861W, measured 290W idle / 384W busy. K80 row: 28 nm, 560 MHz, 150W TDP, 2.8 TOPS (FP), 160 GB/s, 8 MiB.
- Sec. 7/Related: "The TPU has a 700 MHz clock, 65,536 8-bit MACs, 28 MiB, 34 GB/s, and typically uses 40 Watts." DDR3: "Replacing just the DDR3 Weight Memory with the equivalent GDDR5 memory requires doubling the number of memory channels to four."
- Sec. 6: "the TPU has the lowest power, 118W per die total (TPU+Haswell/2) and 40W per die incremental"; "at 10% load, the TPU uses 88% of the power it uses at 100%."
- Note: the 28 MiB total = 24 MiB UB + 4 MiB accumulators (my sum; consistent with paper's figures).

## 9a. Horowitz, "Computing's Energy Problem (and what we can do about it)", ISSCC 2014, paper 1.1 (Feb 10, 2014)
Source: https://gwern.net/doc/cs/hardware/2014-horowitz-2.pdf (mirror of the ISSCC 2014 Digest of Technical Papers pages 10-14, IEEE ©2014). Fetched 2026-10-05. Primary text (mirror, not IEEE Xplore; IEEE doc 6757323 blocked).
Figure 1.1.9 "Rough energy costs for various operations in 45nm 0.9V." (figure text was font-encoded; decoded by me, values below):
- Integer Add: 8 bit 0.03 pJ; 32 bit 0.1 pJ
- Integer Mult: 8 bit 0.2 pJ; 32 bit 3.1 pJ
- FP FAdd: 16 bit 0.4 pJ; 32 bit 0.9 pJ
- FP FMult: 16 bit 1.1 pJ; 32 bit 3.7 pJ
- Memory, Cache (64 bit): 8 KB 10 pJ; 32 KB 20 pJ; 1 MB 100 pJ
- DRAM (64 bit): 1.3-2.6 nJ
- Instruction energy breakdown (same figure): I-cache access 25 pJ, register file access 6 pJ, control, total "70pJ" per instruction vs "Add" a fraction of a pJ.
Text: "the programmable nature of a processor has high energy overhead, 70pJ/instruction, vs. a few pJ for an operation." "The energy cost of a DRAM access (1 to 2nJ) is a couple of orders-of-magnitude higher than the cost of an internal cache access or functional operation (10pJ)." "even when the I/O is improved, the energy cost of a DRAM access will still be large (10pJ/bit, 0.6nJ/8B)". "a cache fetch is 20pJ". "1000 MOPS/mW = 1pJ/operation."
Note: DRAM figure is a range 1.3-2.6 nJ in the table vs "1 to 2nJ" in text (minor disagreement inside the paper).

## 1b. Groq ISCA 2022, "A Software-defined Tensor Streaming Multiprocessor for Large-scale Machine Learning" (Abts, Kimmell, et al.; ISCA '22, June 18-22, 2022; doi 10.1145/3470496.3527405)
Source: Wayback copy of Groq-hosted PDF, https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://groq.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/GroqISCAPaper2022_ASoftwareDefinedTensorStreamingMultiprocessorForLargeScaleMachineLearning-1.pdf (live URL now 404). Fetched 2026-10-05. PRIMARY.
- Abstract: "a software-defined Dragonfly topology, ultimately yielding a parallel machine learning system with elasticity to support a variety of workloads, both training and inference." "Each TSP contributes 220 MiBytes to the global memory capacity". "The TSP acts as both a processing element (endpoint) and network switch". "a large-scale parallel machine learning system with up to 10,440 TSPs and more than 2 TeraBytes of global memory accessible in less than 3 microseconds of end-to-end system latency."
- Sec. 1: "systems up to several hundred TSPs can take advantage of about 50 GB/sec of global (bisection) bandwidth per TSP. As system size grows beyond 264 TSPs, the available global bandwidth flattens to about 14 GB/sec of global bandwidth per TSP endpoint. The Dragonfly network delivers flat global bandwidth up to the maximum system configuration of 145 cabinets for a total of 10,440 TSPs."
- Packaging: "a 4U chassis enclosure which houses eight TSPs ... referred to as a 'node.'" "The pin-bandwidth from each TSP is partitioned into 7 'local' links and 4 'global' links." "up to 33 nodes for total of 33×8 = 264 TSPs ... combined 56 GiBytes of global SRAM". Rack = 9 nodes = 72 TSPs; "145 (racks) ×72 (TSPs per rack) for a total of 10,440 TSPs in the largest system with at-most 5 hop diameter using minimal routing".
- Sec. 2.3 C2C: "low-swing differential signaling operating up to 30 Gbps over 34 AWG cables"; footnote: "in general we operate all the links at the same data rate of 25 Gbps ... with a combined bandwidth of 100 Gbps across the four lanes in a link."
- Software-scheduled network: "a novel software-controlled networking approach that avoids the latency variation introduced by dynamic contention for network links." Synchronization via per-chip hardware aligned counter (HAC) and a DESKEW instruction.
- Matmul: "K=[160,320] i.e. the vector lengths of the hardware for FP16 and int8 respectively. Additionally, a TSP can run two FP16 or four int8 sub-operations each cycle." Measured: "14.9 FP16 TFlops on 4 TSPs to 22.4 FP16 TFlops on 8 TSPs" (one workload). ">= 80% utilization" on [2304x4096]x[4096xN] vs A100.
- Peak FP16 TFLOPS per chip: not stated in either paper. Commonly quoted "188 TFLOPS FP16 / 750 TOPS INT8 at 900 MHz" is UNCONFIRMED (not found in a primary source in this session). Note 820 TOPS (1 GHz) x 0.9 = 738 TOPS at 900 MHz, my arithmetic.

## 5. Cerebras weight streaming (MemoryX, SwarmX)
a) Cerebras training docs, "Weight Streaming Execution", release 2.5.0, https://training-docs.cerebras.ai/rel-2.5.0/concepts/weight-streaming-execution.md (undated page; doc release 2.5.0). Fetched 2026-10-05. PRIMARY.
- "In this particular mode, the neural network model is loaded one layer at a time. This layer-by-layer approach is employed when working with expansive models, specifically those in which the weight parameters of a single layer can fit within memory, yet the entirety of the model as a whole cannot."
- "At each step, one layer is loaded onto the Cerebras Wafer-Scale Engine (WSE). The weight data flows from the MemoryX server to the WSE during the forward propagation phase. In the subsequent backpropagation phase, the weights are once again streamed from MemoryX to the WSE. Here on the WSE, weight gradients are computed, and then they are streamed from the WSE to MemoryX for storage."
- Forward: "MemoryX streams the weights of layer 1 into the WSE. If there's a request for multiple CS-X in the training job, SwarmX broadcasts the weights from MemoryX to all the WSEs. The batch of training samples is divided into equally sized subsets, or shards, with each shard directed to a respective CS-X. This technique is known as data parallelism." "The calculated activations for layer 1 are retained in the WSE's memory."
- Backward: "The gradients for layer three are then streamed out of the WSEs to SwarmX. SwarmX aggregates (sums together) the gradients from multiple WSEs and presents this sum to MemoryX. MemoryX employs this aggregate gradient within the learning algorithm to update its stored copy of the layer weights." (i.e. the optimizer step runs in MemoryX, not on the wafer.) "in practice, the weights of this output layer can be retained to save time."
- Compilation: "The Cerebras compiler strategically plans the mapping of one kernel or layer at a time onto the entire Cerebras Wafer-Scale Engine (WSE)"; "If multiple CS-X systems are requested ... the same mapping is employed for every layer across all CS-X systems."
- Checkpoints: "at specified intervals, all the weights can be retrieved from MemoryX and transferred to the user node".
b) Same docs, "Cerebras Wafer Scale Cluster", https://training-docs.cerebras.ai/rel-2.5.0/concepts/cerebras-wafer-scale-cluster.md . Fetched 2026-10-05. PRIMARY.
- "MemoryX technology serves as the storage and intelligent streaming solution for a model's weights". "SwarmX handles the broadcast of weights from MemoryX to the entire cluster and effectively reduces (sums) gradients in the opposite direction".
- CS-3 vs CS-2: "introduces a novel instruction set architecture and boosts the SIMD ... width ... expands the core count by 20% and enhances the ingress/egress bandwidth by 30%".
c) Cerebras blog "Announcing the Cerebras Architecture for Extreme-Scale AI", August 24, 2021, https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/announcing-the-cerebras-architecture-for-extreme-scale-ai/ (cerebras.net redirects here). Fetched 2026-10-05. VENDOR CLAIMS (WSE-2 era).

## 6. WSE core architecture and yield
a) Cerebras blog, Sean Lie, "Cerebras Architecture Deep Dive: First Look Inside the HW/SW Co-Design for Deep Learning [Updated]", August 29, 2022 (Hot Chips 34 talk write-up), https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/cerebras-architecture-deep-dive-first-look-inside-the-hw-sw-co-design-for-deep-learning . Fetched 2026-10-05. PRIMARY (vendor), WSE-2.
- "each small core has 48 kilobytes of local SRAM dedicated to the core", "eight single-ported banks that are each 32-bit wide."
- WSE-2: "850,000 cores", each with "four FP16 FMAC units" on a 64-bit datapath.
- Per-core memory bandwidth: "two full 64-bit reads, and one full 64-bit write per cycle"; claim "200 times more memory bandwidth within the same GPU area."
- Fabric: "a 2D mesh topology"; "fabric routers" with "a simple 5-port design with 32-bit bi-directional interfaces"; packet = "a single FP16 data element" plus "16 bits of control information making up a 32-bit ultra-fine-grained packet."
- Dataflow: "All the computation is triggered by the data"; "native sparsity acceleration because it only performs work on non-zero data. We filter out all the zero data at the sender."
- Cross-die: die-to-die interfaces with "training and auto-correction state machines" give "a fully uniform fabric across the entire wafer".
- Weight streaming: "All the model weights externally in a device called MemoryX, and we stream all those weights onto the CS-2 system as they're needed to compute each layer of the network, one layer at a time."
b) Cerebras blog, James Wang, "100x Defect Tolerance: How Cerebras Solved the Yield Problem", January 13, 2025, https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/100x-defect-tolerance-how-cerebras-solved-the-yield-problem . Fetched 2026-10-05. VENDOR.
- "Each AI core in the Wafer Scale Engine 3 is approximately 0.05mm2"; physical cores 970,000, active 900,000 ("93%" silicon utilization); H100 SM about 6 mm2, so WSE-3 core about 1/100 the size; "~100x more fault tolerant than a GPU when considering the silicon area affected by each defect."
- Mechanism: a "small reserve of spare cores" plus a routing architecture with "redundant communication pathways" that can "dynamically reconfigure connections between cores" around defects.
- WSE-3 per-core SRAM: 44 GB / 900,000 = about 48.9 KB per active core (my arithmetic; roughly unchanged 48 KB per core). A primary per-core WSE-3 SRAM figure was not found: UNCONFIRMED.
c) Cerebras blog, "Ultrafast frontier inference: Cerebras deep dive at Hot Chips 2026", Aug 25, 2026, https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/ultrafast-frontier-inference-cerebras-deep-dive-at-hot-chips-2026 . Fetched 2026-10-05. VENDOR.
- "A single Cerebras WSE-3T provides 53.5 petabytes per second of aggregate on-wafer fabric bandwidth"
- "When a model spans multiple Cerebras systems, execution is pipelined to keep high-volume tensor and expert communication within each wafer. Only lower-volume data, primarily activations, moves between wafers."
- Roadmap: "CS-5, targeted for 2027, is designed to generate up to 10,000 output tokens per second per user on leading open-source models"; "For the largest frontier models ... CS-5 targets up to 5,000 output tokens per second per user".

## 7. Programming Cerebras
a) PyTorch path: Cerebras training docs rel-2.5.0, "Intro to Model Zoo", https://training-docs.cerebras.ai/rel-2.5.0/model-zoo/model-zoo-overview.md , and "Writing a Custom Training Loop", https://training-docs.cerebras.ai/rel-2.5.0/cs-torch/writing-a-custom-training-loop.md . Fetched 2026-10-05. PRIMARY.
- Model Zoo: "a comprehensive repository offering a range of deep learning models optimized for the Cerebras hardware ... With a focus on models developed in PyTorch". Includes LLaMA/LLaMA-2/LLaMA-3, Mistral, Mixtral, GPT-2/3, GPT-J, Falcon, BERT, T5, LLaVA, DiT, DPO, etc. (github.com/Cerebras/modelzoo). Config is YAML-driven via a Trainer.
- Custom loop: "import cerebras.pytorch as cstorch"; build `cstorch.distributed.ClusterConfig(...)` and `cstorch.backend(...)`; "compiled_model = cstorch.compile(model, backend)"; "The call to cstorch.compile doesn't actually compile the model. Similar to torch.compile it only prepares the model for compilation. Compilation only happens after the first iteration, once the input shapes are known."
