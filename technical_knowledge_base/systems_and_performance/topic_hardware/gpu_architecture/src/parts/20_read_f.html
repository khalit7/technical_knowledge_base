7. Tensor cores, generation by generation

The problem. Ordinary lanes do one multiply-add per thread per instruction. An H100 SM has 128 of them, so our tile's million FMAs would take 8,192 cycles of perfect issue. Matrix multiplication has far more structure than that: every number of A meets a whole row of B. A tensor core exploits it.

How it works. A tensor core is a small fixed-function array that takes a tile of A, a tile of B and an accumulator tile, and returns A·B + C in one instruction for a whole warp (or more). On the A100 each of the 4 tensor cores per SM does 256 FP16 multiply-adds per clock, 1,024 per SM, sixteen times the A100 SM's 64 FP32 lanes ({{A100 in depth|https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-ampere-architecture-in-depth/}}). The H100's do twice that per SM on the same types and four times with FP8 ({{Hopper in depth|https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-hopper-architecture-in-depth/}}), so 2,048 FP16 FMAs per SM per clock; that rate reproduces the H100's 989.5 TFLOPS at a 1.83 GHz clock 989.5e12 / (132 × 2,048 × 2), a clock NVIDIA's blog leaves "not finalized". NVIDIA publishes no per-SM rate for Blackwell; Rubin "doubles the Tensor Core throughput per clock by doubling the amount of data it can process along the K dimension" ({{Inside Rubin|https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-nvidia-rubin-gpu-architecture-powering-the-era-of-agentic-ai/}}).

The bigger change over the generations is not the rate but who issues the multiply, where its operands come from, and where its results go. The animation follows our tile through the three instruction families.

What it shows. Ampere needs 512 warp instructions and every operand passes through registers; Hopper needs 8 warpgroup instructions reading straight from shared memory, but its accumulators still fill a quarter of the register file; Blackwell needs 4 instructions, issued by a single thread, and the accumulators leave the register file for tensor memory. Each step frees the threads for something else: computing addresses, the epilogue of the previous tile, softmax in attention.

Independent evidence that the instruction family matters. On an H800, Luo et al. found that the Ampere-style mma instructions reach on average only 62.9% of the tensor cores' peak, while wgmma reaches 729.3 of 756.5 TFLOPS (96.4%) in FP16 ({{Luo et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13499v1}}, Table VIII) independent. So a kernel written for the A100 runs on an H100 but leaves a third of its tensor-core peak unused.
