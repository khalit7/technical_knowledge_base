The idea in one sentenceA chip computes only on bytes that have arrived, and moving a byte costs more time and energy than computing with it, so memory is built as a ladder (small and fast beside the arithmetic, large and slow further away) and the speed of decoding is simply the bytes read per token divided by the bandwidth of the rung they live on.

New terms

DRAM: one transistor and one capacitor per bit; dense, must be refreshed.
SRAM: six transistors per bit, on the logic die; fast, large per bit.
Row buffer, bank: DRAM opens a whole row into sense amplifiers; banks work in parallel.
HBM: DRAM dies stacked and wired by TSVs, beside the processor on an interposer.
GDDR7, LPDDR5X: board-mounted fast-pin DRAM; low-power DRAM in or beside the package.
CXL: Compute Express Link, load/store memory attached over PCIe wires.
