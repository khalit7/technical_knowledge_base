7. Costing a run, and planning capacity

Llama 3 405B. 6 × 405.85e9 parameters × 15.6e12 tokens = 3.80 × 1025 FLOPs, the figure the paper states (3.8 × 1025). At Table 4's 400 TFLOP/s per GPU on 16,384 H100s (40.4% MFU; the table prints 41%) that is 26.4M GPU-hours: 67 days on 16,384 GPUs, $105M at the $3.99 list price. The model card lists 30.84M H100-hours, 1.17 times the estimate.

Headline GPU-hours are a budget, not a measurement of efficiency. Llama 3.1 8B on 15T tokens is 7.2 × 1023 FLOPs, 507K H100-hours at 40% MFU; its model card lists 1.46M H100-hours, 2.9 times our 507K: at list price $5.8M. Either the steady-state MFU was far below 40%, or (more likely) the hours include more than one clean pass: experiments, restarts, other stages ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B}}). DeepSeek-V3's $5.576M is 2.788M H800-hours at the $2 per hour DeepSeek assumes, and excludes "prior research and ablation experiments" by its own statement (the {{DeepSeek-V3 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} checks the bill).

Smaller jobs and serving. Fine-tuning the 8B on 1 billion tokens with FSDP on one 8-GPU H100 server takes 4.2 hours at 40% MFU: about $135 at list price (the parent's worked example). To produce one million output tokens per minute (16,667 per second) from Llama 3.1 8B at 4,096 tokens of context takes about 4 H100s at batch 64, or 83 at batch 1, at the bandwidth ceiling; real deployments reach a fraction of it and must also run prefill, so plan from measured throughput once you have it. 8. Communication: the same division, for bytes on the wire

On many GPUs every step also moves data, and the arithmetic is section 2's again: count the bytes each GPU sends, divide by the bandwidth of the link it crosses, and ask whether it overlaps with compute. For Llama 3.1 8B on 8 GPUs, data parallelism all-reduces 28.1 GB of BF16 gradients per step (62.5 ms over NVLink 4), once per step and overlapped with backward, so it can cross the slower network between servers; tensor parallelism moves 0.47 GB per layer on the critical path, so it stays inside the NVLink domain. The layouts are {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}'s subject and the links and collectives {{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}}'s; the parent calculator's "Why tensor parallelism stays inside the NVLink domain" chart puts numbers on the placement.
