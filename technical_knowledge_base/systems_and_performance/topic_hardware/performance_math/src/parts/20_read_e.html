6. Serving: prefill, decode, and tokens per second

A served request has two phases on opposite sides of section 2's ridge. Prefill runs the whole prompt through the model at once: thousands of tokens per weight read, compute-bound. Decode then produces one token per step, and each step must read every weight it uses plus the conversation's KV cache (the keys and values of every earlier token, which attention needs): one token per weight read, memory-bound.

Batching is the whole economics of serving. Each step reads the weights once whatever the batch, so 64 users share the 16 GB read and cost 21 times less per token, at the price of each user waiting 3 times longer per token. The cache does not share: at long contexts each conversation's KV reads dominate and batching stops helping, the reason grouped-query attention, latent attention and FP8 caches exist. Cache sizing is {{Long context|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81059ee1ce20c0326dc5}}'s subject; the step at batch 1 and 64 is animated in the parent's {{Performance calculator|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}}, and tokens per second against cost for any model, chip and batch is the {{Run and serving planner|#t-plan}} tab.

Mixture of experts. At batch 1 a decode step reads only the routed experts: DeepSeek-V3 in FP8 reads 37.6 GB of its 671 GB. But 64 tokens with 8 of 256 experts each touch about 222 experts per layer (uniform routing, 256 × (1 − (1 − 8/256)64)), so the step reads 585 GB: an MoE's bandwidth advantage disappears at the batch sizes that make serving cheap, while its FLOP advantage stays derived.

Long prompts: prefill grows faster than the prompt (under a minute)

Llama 3.1 70B with a 32,768-token prompt on 8 H100s at 50% of peak: 1.52 s before the first token, and attention is 23% of those FLOPs (causal count). Prefill time grows faster than linearly with the prompt. Scheduling, paged caches and speculative decoding are {{Topic: inference-and-serving|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c08b3bc95ff45c7b13}}'s.
