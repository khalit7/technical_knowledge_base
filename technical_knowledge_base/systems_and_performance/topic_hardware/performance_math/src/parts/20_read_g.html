Common mistakes the sections do not already show (1 min)
Dividing by the sparse peak
Datasheets print 2:4 structured-sparsity figures, twice the dense ones (H100 BF16: 1,979 sparse, 989.5 dense). Training never uses them; MFU against a sparse peak is half what it should be. The parent's Chip atlas marks which is which.
Using 6N at long context
At 32,768 tokens the attention core more than doubles the per-token cost of Llama 3.1 8B (105 against 48 GFLOP, full-square count). State the convention (full or causal) whenever you add it.
Counting the input embedding as compute
It is a lookup: 6.5% of Llama 3.1 8B's parameters do no FLOPs. Labs differ on whether "N" includes it (gpt-oss-120b's card leaves it out of the active count).
Forgetting the logits
With a 128K vocabulary the FP32 logits kept for the loss cost 4.47 GB per 8,192-token sequence. Chunked or fused cross-entropy kernels exist for this reason.
Leaving out goodput and price terms
A cost estimate without failures, restarts and the price's date and terms is a lower bound. Say which.
Drills: commit to an answer, then reveal (4 min)
1. One training step of Llama 3.1 8B on one 8,192-token sequence. How many FLOPs, counting attention?
6N × 8,192 = 395 TFLOP; counted, 492 TFLOP (60.06 GFLOP per token × 8,192). At 40% MFU on one H100 that is 1.24 s.
2. With FlashAttention and no recomputation, how much activation memory does that step keep?
49 bytes × 4,096 × 8,192 tokens per layer = 1.65 GB, × 32 layers = 52.7 GB, plus 4.47 GB of FP32 logits: 57.1 GB. 470 GB is the same step with plain attention.
3. Why can plain (non-flash) attention not train this model at 8,192 tokens on an H100?
32 heads × 8,192 × 8,192 scores, kept in FP32 (softmax output) and BF16 (for the next matmul): 6 bytes each, 12.9 GB per layer, 13.0 GB counted with the repeated K and V. The FLOPs are the same as with FlashAttention's forward.
4. Full activation checkpointing in PyTorch: by how much does the hardware's work exceed the model's?
Counted 1.24 times, not 8/6 = 1.33: the head is not checkpointed, FlashAttention's backward is already 2.5 times its forward, and the non-reentrant checkpoint skips each layer's final down projection.
5. Llama 3 405B ran at 430 TFLOP/s per GPU on 8,192 H100s with 16.8M tokens per step. How long was a step?
16,777,216 × 6 × 405.85e9 / (8,192 × 430e12) = 11.6 s (12.6 s if Meta's FLOP count includes attention). 177 tokens per second per GPU.
6. How many H100s pretrain Llama 3.1 8B on 15T tokens in 30 days at 40% MFU and 90% goodput?
7.2e23 / (989.5e12 × 0.4) / 3,600 = 507K GPU-hours; / 0.9 / 720 hours = 783 GPUs, about $2.25M at $3.99 per hour.
7. Serving Llama 3.1 8B on one H100 at 4,096 tokens of context: how much cheaper per token is batch 64 than batch 1, at the ceilings?
$5.49 against $0.26 per million tokens: 21 times, not 64, because each of the 64 conversations still reads its own 0.54 GB cache every step.
8. DeepSeek-V3 in FP8 decoding a batch of 64: what share of its 671 GB of weights does one step read?
64 tokens touch about 222 of 256 experts per layer: 585 GB, 87% of the weights, against 37.6 GB (5.6%) at batch 1.
10. Your 8B run reaches 25% MFU on 64 H100s. Where do you look, in order?
Single-GPU MFU first (matmul efficiency, memory-bound ops, fusion); then communication that does not overlap (FSDP across servers, TP outside NVLink); then data loading and stragglers. Each has a number from this page to compare with the profiler.
