Every performance question about a model is three counts divided by three numbers of the chip: FLOPs over peak FLOP/s gives the time when arithmetic is the limit, bytes moved over memory bandwidth gives the time when memory is the limit, and bytes held against memory capacity says whether it fits. MFU is the share of the peak a real run reaches, and the price per hour turns the time into money.

The running example in four divisions: a training step, what it holds, a decoded token, a whole run

Llama 3.1 8B (8.03 billion parameters, 32 layers, hidden size 4,096) on one H100 SXM (989.5 TFLOP/s dense BF16, 3.35 TB/s, 80 GB; vendor specifications from the parent's {{Chip atlas|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}}). Notice which chip number each row divides by: the training step is limited by arithmetic, the decoded token by bandwidth, and the step's memory only fits once the model state is sharded over 8 GPUs. Each row is derived in the section named on it.

You need: what a training step and a decoder layer are, and the chip numbers of the parent {{Topic: hardware|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}} (its Chip atlas and Roofline lab). Why backward costs twice forward is derived on {{Matrix calculus and backprop|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93}}; here it is used.

FLOP
one floating-point add or multiply; FLOP/s is the rate.
Arithmetic intensity
FLOPs done per byte moved to or from memory.
Ridge point
peak FLOP/s over bandwidth: the intensity above which a chip is compute-bound (295 FLOPs per byte for an H100 at BF16).
MFU, HFU
model (or hardware) FLOPs per second achieved, over the dense peak; HFU also counts recomputed FLOPs.
Prefill, decode
serving's two phases: the whole prompt at once, then one token per step.
Goodput
the share of wall-clock time spent on useful training.
Meta device
PyTorch's shapes-only device: every operator runs without data, so a full 8B or 70B step can be counted for free.

How the numbers were obtained. counted: PyTorch 2.14.1 ran the real Llama 3.1 8B (Transformers 5.18.0, config from Hugging Face) on the meta device, recording PyTorch's own FLOP formulas and every tensor autograd saved. measured: timed on this laptop's Apple M1 Pro GPU. derived: arithmetic on sourced inputs, formula shown. No NVIDIA GPU was available: nothing here ran on one.
