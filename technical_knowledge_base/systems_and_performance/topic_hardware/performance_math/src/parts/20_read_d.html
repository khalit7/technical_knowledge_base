4. Recomputation, and why the hardware does more FLOPs than the model needs

The problem. Section 3 showed recomputation turning 73 GB into 23 GB. The price is arithmetic: the forward pass runs again during backward. How much again, and how should utilisation count it?

The textbook answer is 8N. Recompute every layer's forward (2N) on top of 6N: 8/6 = 1.33 times the work. The counted answer for Llama 3.1 8B at 8,192 tokens is smaller: 1.24 times counted. Three reasons:

  • The output head is not recomputed (only the transformer layers are checkpointed).
  • PyTorch's non-reentrant checkpoint stops recomputing as soon as every saved tensor exists again. The last matmul of each layer (the MLP's down projection) produces nothing that the backward pass needs, so it is not rerun ("Non-reentrant checkpoint stops recomputation as soon as all needed intermediate activations have been recomputed", {{PyTorch 2.14 docs|https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/checkpoint.html}}): the recompute is 118.7 TFLOP, not the layers' full 149.5 TFLOP forward (30.8 TFLOP of down projections skipped).
  • Attention's backward already costs more than twice its forward with FlashAttention, so the recompute is a smaller share of the total.
MFU counts model FLOPs     HFU counts hardware FLOPs
model FLOPs
what the model needs for one forward and one backward pass, however it is implemented (6N plus attention)
hardware FLOPs
what the chips actually executed, including recomputation

Korthikanti et al. define them this way: "model FLOPs are hardware and implementation independent and only depend on the underlying model", while "if an implementation requires activation recomputation ... the hardware FLOPs are going to be larger" ({{section 6.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05198}}). HFU flatters: a run that recomputes everything can show high HFU while training no faster. Compare runs by MFU.

Megatron's selective recomputation recomputes only the attention-score part. Korthikanti et al. give the hardware-to-model ratio as about 1 + s/6h: for their 530B model (s = 2,048, h = 20,480) that is 1.017, and their Table 5 lists 56.0% MFU against 57.0% HFU (56.0 × 1.017 = 56.9) derived. Cheap recomputation is the point of the method: with sequence parallelism, a 29 to 32% throughput gain over full recomputation in their runs (Table 5).

Turning Llama 3's MFU back into a step time

Table 4's first row: 8,192 GPUs, data parallel 64, batch 32 sequences of 8,192 tokens per data-parallel group, so 64 × 32 × 8,192 = 16,777,216 tokens per step. At 430 TFLOP/s per GPU and 6N FLOPs per token, one step takes 16.78e6 × 6 × 405.85e9 / (8,192 × 430e12) = 11.6 s; counting attention in full (8.3% more FLOPs), 12.6 s. Meta does not say which FLOP count its 430 uses, so the step time is known only to within that 8%. Per GPU that is 177 tokens per second. derived

Where the other 55 to 60% goes

  • Matmuls below peak. Even a large BF16 GEMM reaches about 720 of the H100's 989.5 TFLOP/s in SemiAnalysis's independent measurement (73%) ({{SemiAnalysis, Dec 2024|https://semianalysis.com/2024/12/22/mi300x-vs-h100-vs-h200-benchmark-part-1-training/}}). That ceiling is reached before anything else goes wrong.
  • Memory-bound operators (norms, activations, residual adds, the loss): no matmul, so no MFU, and on an H100 each byte costs as much time as 295 FLOPs (section 6 counts them).
  • Communication that does not overlap (section 8), pipeline bubbles (idle stages at the start and end of each step), stragglers, data loading.
  • Failures and restarts, which MFU measured in steady state leaves out: compare Llama 3.1 8B's card, 1.46M H100-hours for about 15T tokens, an implied 13.9% if every hour were training.
