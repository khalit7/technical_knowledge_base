6. Joining chips: why the network picks your parallelism

The state does not fit one GPU and the arithmetic would take 64 s on one, so the step is split, and the GPUs must exchange at least the 16.06 GB of gradients. The links form a ladder, each rung roughly an order of magnitude slower than the one above:

Memory: one chipHBM3 / HBM3e: 3.35 / 8 TB/s.
Scale-up: one server or rackNVLink 4 (H100) / NVLink 5 (Blackwell) through NVSwitch chips: 450 / 900 GB/s per GPU each way. 8 GPUs in an HGX server; 72 in a GB200 or GB300 NVL72 rack.
Host link: one serverPCIe 5.0 x16, about 63 GB/s each way: CPU to GPU, NIC to GPU, and between consumer cards.
Scale-out: thousands of serversInfiniBand NDR (400 Gb/s) or RoCE Ethernet, one NIC per GPU: 50 GB/s each way; XDR is 800 Gb/s.

Vendor figures usually add both directions: NVLink 4 is "900 GB/s total" ({{Hopper in depth|https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-hopper-architecture-in-depth/}}), NVLink 5 "1.8 TB/s" per GPU ({{Blackwell|https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/blackwell-architecture/}}); halved here. InfiniBand: {{Quantum-X800|https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/networking/products/infiniband/quantum-x800/}}; an AWS p5 server has 400 Gb/s per GPU ({{AWS p5|https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/p5/}}). PCIe 5.0: 32 GT/s per lane derived. vendor spec

Definitions. Scale-up links (NVLink with NVSwitch, AMD Infinity Fabric, Google's ICI) join a small group of chips closely enough to read each other's memory; the group is the NVLink domain. Scale-out links (InfiniBand, or RoCE: RDMA over Ethernet) join servers through switches and reach tens of thousands of GPUs. An all-reduce leaves every GPU with the sum of everyone's data; a ring all-reduce sends 2(N−1)/N times the data per GPU, so the 8B's gradients over 8 H100s are 28.1 GB per GPU: 62.5 ms over NVLink 4, 0.56 s over one 400 Gb/s NIC per GPU derived.

The rule that spans this topic. With 8 GPUs the step's arithmetic takes 8.0 s, so even the InfiniBand all-reduce is small and overlaps the backward pass: data parallelism tolerates the slow network. Tensor parallelism all-reduces activations twice per layer forward and twice back, on the critical path between layers: for Llama 3.1 70B with 8-way tensor parallelism at 8,192 tokens that traffic is 16% of the layer's compute time over NVLink but 141% over a 400 Gb/s NIC (the {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}}). So tensor and expert parallelism stay inside the NVLink domain; pipeline and data parallelism cross servers. Llama 3 405B used tensor parallelism of 8, exactly one server, in every configuration ({{Llama 3, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3}}); DeepSeek-V3, with 160 GB/s NVLink and 50 GB/s InfiniBand on H800s, avoided tensor parallelism entirely ({{DeepSeek-V3 report|https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v2}}). The NVL72 rack exists to make the domain 72 GPUs instead of 8; TPUs instead join chips directly to their neighbours over ICI in a torus, with optical switches between 64-chip cubes.

Depth: two quick estimates

Two RTX 5090s in one workstation have no NVLink, only PCIe 5.0 (about 63 GB/s each way). Their ring all-reduce of the 8B's 16.06 GB of gradients sends 2 × 1/2 × 16.06 = 16.06 GB per GPU: at 63 GB/s that is 255 ms derived. Workable for data parallelism with a long step; tensor parallelism across PCIe pays that price on every layer.

Llama 3 trained on RoCE at 400 Gb/s per GPU in a three-layer network of 24K GPUs ({{Llama 3 report|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3}}); DeepSeek-V3 used 16-way pipeline, 64-way expert parallelism across 8 nodes and ZeRO-1 data parallelism, and its all-to-all kernels needed only 20 SMs to saturate both links.
