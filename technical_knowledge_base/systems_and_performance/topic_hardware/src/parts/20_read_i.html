10. Interview questions, a symptom table, and what the child pages will cover

Interview questions, short answers

Why is LLM decode memory-bound and training compute-bound?
Arithmetic intensity is about the number of tokens that share each weight read. Decode at batch 1 uses each 2-byte weight for one FMA, about 1 FLOP per byte, far below an H100's ridge of 295; a training step has thousands of tokens per weight read, far above it (section 5).
Estimate the batch-1 decode speed of an 8B bf16 model on one H100.
16 GB of weights per token at 3.35 TB/s: 4.8 ms, so at most about 209 tokens per second, before the KV cache and overheads (section 3).
How much memory does full fine-tuning of a 7 to 8B model need?
About 16 bytes per parameter for mixed-precision Adam (bf16 weights and gradients, fp32 master weights and two moments), so about 128 GB for 8B, plus activations: more than one 80 GB GPU, so shard (ZeRO, FSDP) or use LoRA.
What is MFU and what is a good value?
Useful model FLOPs per second (6N × tokens per second) over the chips' dense peak. About 40% is very good for a large dense run at scale (Llama 3 405B: 38 to 43%); under 20% points to a bottleneck. HFU also counts recomputation and is higher.
Why does tensor parallelism stay inside a node?
It all-reduces activations twice per layer forward and twice backward, on the critical path. For a 70B model with 8-way tensor parallelism that costs 16% of the compute time over NVLink and 141% over a 400 Gb/s NIC (section 6, from the Performance calculator).
What does a ring all-reduce cost?
Each GPU sends 2(N−1)/N times the data (a reduce-scatter then an all-gather), so time ≈ 2S / link bandwidth for large N, independent of N apart from latency.
What did Hopper and Blackwell add to the tensor core, physically?
Hopper: FP8, a per-SM copy engine for tiles (TMA), warpgroup-wide asynchronous matrix multiplies, clusters of SMs sharing shared memory. Blackwell: FP6, FP4 and block-scaled formats, 256 KB of tensor memory per SM for accumulators, two dies acting as one GPU.
GPU or TPU: what is the real difference?
Thousands of threads hiding latency with parallelism and caches, programmable at the kernel level, against large systolic arrays scheduled by a compiler with software-managed on-chip memory and switchless torus pods. Per-chip peaks are now comparable; the differences are flexibility, ecosystem, pod scale and price.
Why do vendors quote sparse FLOPS, and what should you use?
2:4 structured sparsity doubles the tensor-core rate if half of every four weights are zero. Dense training and most inference do not use it, so compare dense figures on both sides.
An H100 run is slow on B200s. What do you check first?
Whether the software uses the new hardware: kernels built for the new architecture, the matmul formats (FP8, FP4 paths), larger batches to stay above a ridge that did not move at BF16, and whether communication, not compute, became the limit.
