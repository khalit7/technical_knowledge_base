5. The roofline and MFU: which column limits you

The problem. Sections 1 to 3 gave each chip two speeds: how fast it computes and how fast it reads memory. A given operation needs a certain number of FLOPs per byte it moves; that number decides which of the two speeds it runs at. This is the most useful single idea on the page.

time ≈ max( FLOPs / peak FLOP/s , bytes / bandwidth )
AI
Arithmetic intensity: FLOPs performed per byte moved to or from memory, a property of the operation and its shapes
ridge
peak FLOP/s divided by bandwidth: the intensity at which the two terms are equal. Below it the operation is memory-bound, above it compute-bound

Plotting attainable FLOP/s against intensity gives a sloped line (bandwidth) that meets a flat line (peak) at the ridge: the roofline. Ridge points, dense, in FLOPs per byte derived:

Peaks and bandwidths as in sections 3 and 4 (A100 SXM 80 GB: 312 TFLOPS BF16, 2,039 GB/s, {{NVIDIA A100|https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/a100/}}). The M1 Pro point uses the measured fp16 peak (5.06 TFLOP/s) and stream copy (165 GB/s) from the {{Roofline lab|#t-roof}}.

Where real operations land. Multiplying activations of shape (tokens × 4,096) by a 4,096 × 4,096 bf16 weight does 2 × tokens × 4,0962 FLOPs and moves the 32 MB weight once, plus the activations. When the number of tokens is small next to 4,096, the intensity is about equal to the number of tokens. So the batch is the intensity: decode at batch 1 sits at 1 FLOP per byte, a training step with 8,192 tokens per sequence sits in the thousands, far right of every ridge. Elementwise operations (adding a residual, an activation function, a normalisation) do a few FLOPs per element they read and write: below 1, memory-bound on every chip ever made; that is why they are fused into neighbouring kernels (torch.compile, FlashAttention). A third regime exists: overhead-bound, when the GPU waits on Python and kernel launches and neither term explains the time.

Correction to the old page
The old notes said the ridge "rises every generation". At BF16 it doubled from A100 (153) to H100 (295) and then fell slightly on B200 (281), because Blackwell grew bandwidth (3.35 to 8 TB/s) as fast as BF16 FLOPS. It keeps rising only if you move to the new formats: B200 at FP8 is 562.5, at FP4 1,125. Running in lower precision makes an operation more compute-hungry relative to its bytes, which is why low precision and big batches go together.

The {{Roofline lab|#t-roof}} measured this on the M1 Pro GPU: peak FMA 5.01 TFLOP/s (fp32) and 5.06 (fp16, the same ALUs: no tensor cores), copy bandwidth 165 GB/s, ridge 30.7; an 8,192 × 8,192 fp16 linear layer at batch 1 ran at 168 GB/s (memory-bound, on the roof) and at batch 64 reached 2.4 TFLOP/s; a naive, a tiled and the library matmul reached 351, 640 and 4,070 GFLOP/s at the same intensity measured here. That last row is the gap the roofline cannot see: a kernel can sit far under the roof because it is badly written. Pick any chip, precision and shape there.

MFU: how far below the roof a training run sits

MFU = (tokens per second × 6N) / (number of chips × peak FLOP/s per chip)
6N
model FLOPs per token (section 1's rule), counted whether or not the run recomputes activations
peak
dense peak in the format the matmuls run in

Model FLOPs utilisation is the fraction of the hardware's peak that does useful model arithmetic. Counting recomputed FLOPs too gives hardware FLOPs utilisation (HFU), which flatters. Llama 3 405B on 16,384 H100s reported 400 TFLOP/s per GPU and 41% MFU; 400 / 989.5 = 40.4% recomputed, consistent with the printed figure after rounding. On 8,192 GPUs it reported 43%, and 38% at 131,072-token sequences ({{Llama 3, Table 4|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3}}). Around 40% is what a very well tuned dense run at scale reaches; the rest goes to communication that does not overlap, memory-bound operations, pipeline bubbles and failures.

Our step. At 40% MFU, the step's 2.53 × 1016 FLOPs take 64 s of arithmetic on one H100, 8.0 s on 8 and 1.0 s on 64 derived. The {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}} adds memory traffic and communication: 8.7 s on 8 GPUs, 1.17 s on 64 with hybrid sharding (34% MFU), 1.95 s with flat FSDP across all 64 (20%). The difference is the network (section 6).

Predict: Llama 3.1 8B's model card reports 1.46M H100 GPU-hours for "~15 trillion tokens". What MFU does that imply?
6 × 8.03 × 109 × 15 × 1012 = 7.2 × 1023 FLOPs, against 1.46M h × 3,600 s × 989.5 TFLOP/s = 5.2 × 1024 available: 13.9% derived ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B}}). The card does not say what the hours include (experiments, restarts, other phases, a smaller per-GPU batch for a small model), so this is not the run's MFU: headline GPU-hours are not a measurement of efficiency. Compare with the 405B's 38 to 43% measured in-run.
