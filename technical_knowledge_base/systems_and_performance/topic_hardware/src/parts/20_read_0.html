The idea

The idea. A training or serving step needs three things: arithmetic, bytes from memory and bytes between chips. Every part of an accelerator system exists to supply one of them, and the step runs at the speed of whichever runs out first; choosing hardware is finding that resource before you pay for the others.

The running example is one training step of Llama 3.1 8B, an ordinary dense transformer with 8,030,261,248 parameters (32 layers, hidden size 4,096, 8 key-value heads; counted from its {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/blob/main/config.json}}, an open mirror of Meta's gated repository): 64 sequences of 8,192 tokens, 524,288 tokens in all illustrative batch, the step the {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}} animates. Fig. 1 is the page: the step's hardware from the inside out, each part lit where it acts, with the child page that owns it.

  1. One step, three demands. The step needs 2.53 × 1016 FLOPs of arithmetic, 128.5 GB of state held in memory and 16.06 GB of gradients exchanged between chips, plus the building that powers and cools them. Drawn: two 8-GPU H100 servers joined by a network illustrative layout. Read down this list for the order the page takes; each step lights one part and links the child page whose own figure teaches it.
  2. Arithmetic: tensor cores inside the SMs. 6 × parameters × tokens. Nearly all of it is matrix multiplication, done by the tensor cores, four in each of an H100's 132 SMs (16 drawn, one lifted out). On one H100 at 40% of its 989.5 TFLOPS dense BF16 peak: 64 s derived: 2.53e16 / (989.5e12 × 0.4). Systolic arrays do the same job on a TPU (step 8). Owner: {{GPU architecture|n:3c65c17b0d0d810682aae39bd614ec64}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d810682aae39bd614ec64#stg-hero}} draws one H100 SXM to its counts and {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d810682aae39bd614ec64#stg-sm}} one SM. Sections 1, 2 and 4.
  3. Bytes from memory: HBM beside the die. 16 bytes per parameter for mixed-precision Adam, before activations. HBM stacked beside the die holds 80 GB on an H100 and feeds it at 3.35 TB/s; SRAM on the die is far smaller. Against the 128.5 GB of state, one H100 does not fit, so the state is sharded. Owner: {{Memory technology|n:3f05c17b0d0d818ab063e327e3d7009f}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3f05c17b0d0d818ab063e327e3d7009f#stg-hero}} walks one H100 server from the die outward and {{Fig. 2|n:3f05c17b0d0d818ab063e327e3d7009f#stg-hbm}} sets HBM against GDDR7; the state item by item is {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}'s {{Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08#stg-mem}}. Section 3.
  4. Bytes on the wire, inside the server: NVLink. Every GPU must end with the sum of everyone's bf16 gradients, every step. Inside one HGX server, NVLink through NVSwitch gives each H100 450 GB/s each way: a ring all-reduce over 8 GPUs sends 28.1 GB per GPU, 62.5 ms derived. Owner: {{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}}, whose {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0#stg-srv}} sets one 8-GPU server's switch against a mesh. Section 6.
  5. Across servers: the network. Between servers, InfiniBand or RoCE, one 400 Gb/s NIC per GPU, gives 50 GB/s each way: a factor of 9 below NVLink, and the same all-reduce takes 0.56 s. That gap decides which parallelism may cross it (section 6). Owner: {{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0#stg-hero}} is the whole ladder on two HGX H100 servers and {{Fig. 5|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0#stg-par}} shows where each parallelism sends its traffic.
  6. The building: watts and failures. Power in, heat out: 700 W per H100, about 1.2 kW per Blackwell GPU; liquid cooling above about a kilowatt; at 16K GPUs something fails every few hours, and one failure stops every GPU of the job. Owner: {{Power, cooling, reliability and the data centre|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22#stg-hero}} follows one gigawatt from the grid to the GPU and out as heat, {{Fig. 2|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22#stg-cool}} one second of coolant for a rack and {{Fig. 3|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22#stg-fail}} one failure stopping every GPU. Section 8.
  7. Across all of it: the arithmetic that predicts the step. Each resource takes its demand over its rate, and the largest wins: on 8 H100s at 40% MFU the arithmetic takes 8.0 s, the gradient all-reduce 62.5 ms over NVLink (0.56 s if it crossed the network), so here the arithmetic runs out first (roofline, MFU, memory and time budgets, cost). Owner: {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08#stg-hero}} lays three counts over one H100 and {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08#stg-flops}} draws the step's FLOPs as a volume. Sections 5 and 9; tools on this page: {{Chip atlas|#t-chips}}, {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}}, {{Roofline lab|#t-roof}}.
  8. Other bets beside it. The same three demands, answered differently: a TPU multiplies in a systolic array (128 × 128 cells on v2 to v5, 256 × 256 on v6e) whose operands pass from cell to cell on a compiler's schedule; Cerebras keeps the model on one wafer, 46,225 mm2 with 44 GB of SRAM at 21 PB/s. Owner: {{TPUs and other accelerators|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187}}, whose {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187#stg-sys}} runs 80 multiply-adds cycle by cycle, {{Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187#stg-pod}} builds a TPU cube and its rings and {{Fig. 4|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187#stg-wafer}} a wafer as one chip. Sections 1 and 7.

A map, not a mechanism: each step lights the part one child page owns and links that child's own figure. The three demands come from the {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}}'s count (16 bytes per parameter: bf16 weights and gradients, an fp32 master copy and two fp32 Adam moments; activations add about 57 GB more at micro-batch 1, counted on {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}). Counts drawn (16 SMs, two servers, one network switch, one failed GPU) are illustrative; the bar heights in steps 3 and 7 are the page's numbers to scale.

New terms on this page (13)
FMA
fused multiply-add, a × b + c in one instruction: 2 FLOPs
SM
streaming multiprocessor, NVIDIA's GPU core
tensor core
a unit that does a small matrix multiply per instruction
systolic array
a grid of multiply-accumulate cells that pass operands to their neighbours
SRAM, DRAM
on-die memory (fast, small) and off-die memory (dense, slower)
HBM
DRAM dies stacked beside the processor on an interposer
arithmetic intensity
FLOPs per byte moved
ridge point
peak FLOP/s over bandwidth; below it an operation is memory-bound
MFU
model FLOPs utilisation, useful FLOPs over peak
scale-up, scale-out
the fast fabric inside a server or rack, and the network between them
RDMA
a network card writing straight into another machine's memory
all-reduce
every GPU ends with the sum of everyone's data
MX, NVFP4
block-scaled 8-, 6- and 4-bit formats
How numbers are labelled
measured here on the Apple M1 Pro this page was built on (a real 16-core SIMT GPU; no NVIDIA GPU was available), scripts and raw runs in the {{Roofline lab|#t-roof}}; vendor spec, dated; independent third-party measurements, kept apart; derived with the formula shown; announced for parts not yet shipping. Tensor-core figures are dense unless they say otherwise: headline numbers often assume 2:4 structured sparsity, which doubles them and which training rarely uses. Vendor figures fetched on 2026-10-05 unless dated.
