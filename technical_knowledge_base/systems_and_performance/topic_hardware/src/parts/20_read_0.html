The idea. A training or serving step needs three things: arithmetic, bytes from memory and bytes between chips. Every part of an accelerator system exists to supply one of them, and the step runs at the speed of whichever runs out first; choosing hardware is finding that resource before you pay for the others.

The running example is one training step of Llama 3.1 8B, an ordinary dense transformer with 8,030,261,248 parameters (32 layers, hidden size 4,096, 8 key-value heads; counted from its {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/blob/main/config.json}}, an open mirror of Meta's gated repository): 64 sequences of 8,192 tokens, 524,288 tokens in all illustrative batch, the step the {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}} animates. The map below is the page: each row is one of the three demands (plus the building that powers them), the hardware that supplies it, and the child page that owns it.

One training step, and the hardware that serves each of its demands
Arithmetic2.53 × 1016 FLOPs
6 × parameters × tokens. Supplied by SMs and tensor cores on a GPU, systolic arrays on a TPU and most other accelerators. On one H100 at 40% of peak: 64 s derived: 2.53e16 / (989.5e12 × 0.4).
Sections 1, 2, 4, 7. Owners: {{GPU architecture|n:3c65c17b0d0d810682aae39bd614ec64}}, {{TPUs and other accelerators|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187}}
Bytes in memory128.5 GB of state
16 bytes per parameter for mixed-precision Adam, before activations. Supplied by HBM stacked beside the die (80 GB at 3.35 TB/s on an H100) and SRAM on it. One H100: does not fit.
Section 3. Owner: {{Memory technology|n:3f05c17b0d0d818ab063e327e3d7009f}}
Bytes on the wire16.06 GB of gradients
Every GPU must end with the sum of everyone's bf16 gradients, every step. Supplied by NVLink inside a server or rack (450 GB/s each way per H100) and InfiniBand or RoCE between servers (50 GB/s): a factor of 9 apart.
Section 6. Owner: {{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}}
The buildingwatts and failures
700 W per H100, about 1.2 kW per Blackwell GPU; liquid cooling above about a kilowatt; at 16K GPUs something fails every few hours.

New terms on this page (13)
FMA
fused multiply-add, a × b + c in one instruction: 2 FLOPs
SM
streaming multiprocessor, NVIDIA's GPU core
tensor core
a unit that does a small matrix multiply per instruction
systolic array
a grid of multiply-accumulate cells that pass operands to their neighbours
SRAM, DRAM
on-die memory (fast, small) and off-die memory (dense, slower)
HBM
DRAM dies stacked beside the processor on an interposer
arithmetic intensity
FLOPs per byte moved
ridge point
peak FLOP/s over bandwidth; below it an operation is memory-bound
MFU
model FLOPs utilisation, useful FLOPs over peak
scale-up, scale-out
the fast fabric inside a server or rack, and the network between them
RDMA
a network card writing straight into another machine's memory
all-reduce
every GPU ends with the sum of everyone's data
MX, NVFP4
block-scaled 8-, 6- and 4-bit formats
How numbers are labelled
measured here on the Apple M1 Pro this page was built on (a real 16-core SIMT GPU; no NVIDIA GPU was available), scripts and raw runs in the {{Roofline lab|#t-roof}}; vendor spec, dated; independent third-party measurements, kept apart; derived with the formula shown; announced for parts not yet shipping. Tensor-core figures are dense unless they say otherwise: headline numbers often assume 2:4 structured sparsity, which doubles them and which training rarely uses. Vendor figures fetched on 2026-10-05 unless dated.
