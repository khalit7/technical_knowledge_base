This page follows one training step down to the silicon. The model is Llama 3.1 8B, an ordinary dense transformer with 8,030,261,248 parameters (32 layers, hidden size 4,096, 8 key-value heads, vocabulary 128,256; counted from its {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B/blob/main/config.json}}, an open mirror of Meta's gated repository). One step processes a batch of 64 sequences of 8,192 tokens, 524,288 tokens in all illustrative batch, the same step the {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}} animates. Before any chip is named, ask what that step needs. There are only three things.

Arithmetic
2.53 × 1016 FLOPs

6 × parameters × tokens: each weight does one multiply and one add per token going forward, and about twice that going backward (the 6ND rule; section 5 says when it is wrong).

Bytes in memory
128.5 GB of state

16 bytes per parameter for mixed-precision Adam (bf16 weights and gradients, fp32 master copy and two Adam moments), before activations; about 165 GB with them (the {{Performance calculator|#t-calc}}'s count). One H100 holds 80 GB.

Bytes on the wire
16.06 GB of gradients

If the step is split across GPUs, every GPU must end up with the sum of everyone's bf16 gradients, every step (section 6).

Every piece of hardware on this page exists to serve one of those three columns: arithmetic units for the first, memory for the second, links between chips for the third. How fast a step runs is set by whichever of the three is slowest, and the art is to arrange the work so that it is the arithmetic. On one H100 at a typical 40% of peak, the arithmetic alone would take 64 seconds per step derived: 2.53e16 / (989.5e12 × 0.4); the state would not even fit. The sections walk down from there:

How numbers are labelled here
measured here on the Apple M1 Pro this page was built on (its GPU is a real 16-core SIMT GPU; there is no NVIDIA GPU in this build), with the {{Roofline lab|#t-roof}} holding the scripts and raw runs; vendor spec from a datasheet or whitepaper, dated; independent measurements by third parties, kept apart from vendor figures; derived with the formula shown; announced for parts not yet shipping. Every tensor-core figure is dense unless it says otherwise: NVIDIA and AMD headline numbers often assume 2:4 structured sparsity, which doubles them and which training rarely uses. Vendor figures were fetched on 2026-10-05.
