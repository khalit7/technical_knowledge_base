9. Choosing and costing hardware for a job

Five lines of arithmetic before you rent anything, each from a section above:

  1. Memory (section 3): bytes of state (16 per parameter for mixed-precision Adam, 2 per parameter for bf16 inference, plus activations or KV cache). Does it fit one GPU, one server, or does it need sharding?
  2. Compute (section 5): 6 × parameters × tokens, divided by chips × dense peak × an assumed MFU (30 to 40% for a tuned dense run) gives the wall-clock time.
  3. Roofline (section 5): is the batch per GPU large enough to be compute-bound? Decode at small batch is bandwidth-bound: tokens per second ≈ bandwidth / bytes per token.
  4. Communication (section 6): bytes per step for each kind of parallelism against the link it crosses. Tensor and expert parallelism must fit inside the NVLink domain.
  5. Cost: chip-hours × price, with the price's date and terms.
Worked example: fine-tune Llama 3.1 8B on 1 billion tokens derived
  • Memory: 128.5 GB of state; sharded over 8 GPUs that is 16 GB each, which fits an 80 GB H100 with room for activations.
  • Compute: 6 × 8.03 × 109 × 109 = 4.8 × 1019 FLOPs. On 8 H100s at 40% MFU: 4.2 hours. On 8 B200s (2,250 TFLOPS dense each): 1.9 hours.
  • Communication: everything inside one 8-GPU NVLink server; the 16 GB gradient all-reduce (62.5 ms on NVLink 4) is small next to each step.
  • Cost at on-demand list prices ({{Lambda|https://lambda.ai/pricing}}, fetched 2026-10-05: H100 SXM $3.99, B200 $6.69 per GPU-hour): about $135 on H100s, about $99 on B200s. The newer chip costs more per hour and less per job, if the software reaches the same MFU on it.
Peak per dollar-hour, on-demand list prices

GPUs: Lambda 8-GPU instances per GPU-hour; TPUs: Google Cloud per chip-hour on demand ({{TPU pricing|https://cloud.google.com/tpu/pricing}}); both fetched 2026-10-05. Peaks are vendor figures, not achieved throughput; reserved and committed prices are lower and differ by provider; a TPU also means JAX or PyTorch/XLA. A ranking, not a quote. derived

Choosing, by job. Pretraining or full fine-tuning of a large model: the biggest NVLink domain and the most HBM you can get, then the network (InfiniBand or tuned RoCE, one NIC per GPU). Fine-tuning a small model, or LoRA: one 8-GPU server, or even one GPU. Batch-1 latency (chat, agents): bandwidth per dollar, or SRAM-based systems. Throughput serving: FLOPs per dollar at high batch, and memory capacity for the KV cache. Local development: a consumer GPU (fast, 32 GB, no NVLink, half-rate FP32-accumulate training math) or a Mac (slow but large unified memory). In every case the first question is whether your software stack runs well on it: a cheaper peak that your kernels cannot reach is not cheaper.
