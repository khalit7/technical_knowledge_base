8. Power, cooling, reliability: the building is part of the machine

Three facts change how every other section is read. Watts per chip keep rising: A100 400 W, H100 700 W, Blackwell up to 1,200 W and Blackwell Ultra 1,400 W per GPU ({{NVIDIA, Aug 22 2025|https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/inside-nvidia-blackwell-ultra-the-chip-powering-the-ai-factory-era/}}), MI355X 1,400 W, MI455X up to 2,500 W; above about a kilowatt per chip air gives way to liquid, so a 9,216-chip Ironwood pod spans "nearly 10 MW" ({{Google, Apr 2025|https://blog.google/products/google-cloud/ironwood-tpu-age-of-inference/}}) and comparisons are now quoted per megawatt. Lockstep jobs swing the grid: Llama 3's cluster saw its power draw move by "tens of megawatts" as all GPUs paused and resumed together. Failures are a hardware number: a 54-day snapshot of Llama 3 405B training on up to 16K H100s had 419 unexpected interruptions, about 78% attributed to confirmed hardware issues ({{Llama 3 report|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3}}, Table 5), so checkpoint speed and automatic restart are part of the hardware budget, and MFU measured between failures overstates a run's real throughput.
