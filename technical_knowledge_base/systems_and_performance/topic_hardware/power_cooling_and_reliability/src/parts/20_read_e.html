7. Silent data corruption: the failure that raises no error

Everything in section 5 announces itself: a crash, an Xid, a timeout. A silent data corruption is a wrong answer with no error. ECC protects bits sitting in memory, not the arithmetic units that compute on them, so a marginal transistor in a multiplier can return a wrong product and nothing notices.

  • Real and systemic. Meta's fleet testing found "hundreds of CPUs" with SDCs across hundreds of thousands of machines over more than 18 months, and notes SDCs "are not captured by error reporting mechanisms" ({{Dixit et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11245}}) published. Google observed "on the order of a few mercurial cores per several thousand machines", with frequency, voltage, temperature and age all affecting whether a defective core misbehaves ({{Hochschild et al., HotOS 2021|https://sigops.org/s/conferences/hotos/2021/papers/hotos21-s01-hochschild.pdf}}) published. Both studies are of CPUs; the mechanism (manufacturing defects that show only under some conditions) applies to any chip.
  • At training scale, a weekly event. For Gemini, Google wrote that "we can expect SDC events to impact training every week or two", and found the faulty hardware with "deterministic replay to isolate incorrect computations, combined with proactive SDC scanners on idle machines and hot standbys" ({{Gemini report|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11805}}) published. Llama 3's Table 5 attributes 6 of its 419 interruptions to SDC: the ones that were caught.

What one flipped bit does to a data-parallel run, and which detector catches it (a corrupted gradient is averaged into every replica; a corrupted weight is caught by comparing replicas), is measured on {{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}}, section 7. Treat an unexplained loss spike as a possible hardware event, not only an optimiser problem.

All derived. Llama 3.1 405B used 30.84 million H100 GPU-hours ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B}}) on 15.6 trillion tokens ({{Llama 3 paper|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3}}): at the 700 W board limit at most 21.6 GWh of GPU energy and 4.98 J per training token. One token costs 2.43 × 1015 FLOPs; at 0.71 pJ per BF16 FLOP that is 1.72 J at 100% of peak and 4.19 J at 41% MFU. The GPU-hour figure sits about a fifth above the MFU figure, as it should: it includes restarts, evaluation and slower phases. A DGX H100 is 1.82 times its GPUs' power; with PUE 1.2 the facility spends roughly 10.9 J per training token, an upper bound because GPUs rarely sit at their limit.

Inference. Google measured the median Gemini Apps text prompt at 0.24 Wh (864 J), counting idle provisioned machines, host CPU and memory, and data-centre overhead; counting only the active accelerators gives 0.10 Wh; and the median prompt's energy fell 33 times over twelve months ({{Google Cloud, Aug 21 2025|https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/infrastructure/measuring-the-environmental-impact-of-ai-inference}}) published. The boundary moves the answer by more than two times, and software (batching, quantisation, better kernels) moves energy per token faster than hardware. Mistakes and interview rehearsal

Common mistakes the sections above do not spell out
  • Treating TDP as average draw. TDP is the ceiling the cooling must handle; average draw depends on the workload (communication phases run near idle) and on power caps.
  • Quoting MFU between failures as the run's throughput. Multiply by the useful fraction of wall-clock time: at Llama 3's failure rate with a 5-minute checkpoint and 15 minutes to recover, that fraction is about 73%; Meta reached over 90% by making all of these faster.
  • Keeping the checkpoint interval when the job grows. MTBF falls as 1/N and the interval as its square root: eight times the GPUs means checkpointing about 2.8 times as often.
Interview rehearsal: six questions with worked answers
  • How many H100s can a 100 MW site hold? At a DGX H100's 10.2 kW per 8 GPUs and PUE 1.2: 100,000 kW / (1.275 kW × 1.2) = about 65,000 GPUs. Say what you assumed: server maximum power, no network or storage, the PUE.
  • Why is a GB200 NVL72 rack liquid cooled? About 120 kW in one rack: air at a 15 °C rise would need about 6.6 m³/s, water at a 10 °C rise about 170 L/min, because water holds about 3,500 times more heat per litre.
  • Derive the optimal checkpoint interval. Waste per unit time is C/T plus (T/2 + D + R)/μ. Differentiate in T: −C/T² + 1/(2μ) = 0, so T = √(2μC) (Young adds C back for the checkpoint itself).
  • A 32,768-GPU job fails at Llama 3's rate and a checkpoint costs 2 minutes. How often should it checkpoint? MTBF halves from 185.6 to 92.8 minutes. Young: √(2 × 92.8 × 2) + 2 = about 21.3 minutes.
  • Why does synchronous training stress the grid, and what are the fixes? All GPUs compute and communicate in lockstep, so the site swings between near full and near idle power every step, by tens of megawatts. Filler work, GPU power floors and ramp limits (about 10% energy overhead at a 90% floor), energy storage at the rack.
  • What is silent data corruption, and how would you look for it? A wrong computation with no error. Compare checksums of weights across replicas that should be identical, replay suspicious steps deterministically on other hardware, and scan idle machines with known-answer tests.
