Checkpoint simulator

One synchronous training job, one day, failures arriving at random at the rate your cluster size and failure rate imply. The same failures hit two copies of the job: one checkpointing at an interval you choose, one at Young's interval. Below, the useful fraction of time against the interval: the exact formula, the first-order formula, and this page's own Monte Carlo. The model and the formulas are explained in {{Reading, section 7|#t-read}}; the engine is a port of src/recompute.py, which checks it against Aupy et al.'s Table 2 and a long Monte Carlo.

What reproduces what

  • Aupy et al.'s Table 2 (C = R = 600 s, D = 60 s, ten platform sizes): Young, Daly, refined first-order and exact-optimum periods, all forty reproduced within independently (src/recompute.py; the largest gaps are in the two smallest platforms, where the paper fixed a two-hour job).
  • Kokolis et al.'s projected 1.8 h and 0.23 h job MTBF at 6.50 per 1,000 node-days for 16,384 and 131,072 GPUs: the presets give them by construction (same formula).
  • Llama 3's rate, 3.79 per 1,000 server-days, is 419 failures / (54 days × 2,048 servers) derived.
  • Defaults illustrative: C = 5 min is Meta's assumed synchronous pause (Kokolis et al.); D = 10 and R = 5 min are chosen inside Meta's assumed 5 to 20 minutes of restart overhead; the 3-hour "your interval" is a deliberately poor choice. The default day is seed 54, chosen because its eight failures are spread evenly; press "Another day" for others.
