Gigawatt planner

Start from the power a site can draw from the grid and work inward: how much of it reaches the servers (PUE), how many accelerators that powers, what peak compute they add up to, and what the electricity costs per year and per accelerator-hour. Every system power is the vendor's maximum; real sites provision below nameplate and also power switches and storage, so read the results as an order of magnitude. The formulas are in {{Reading, section 3|#t-read}}; a whole run's cost is on {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}.

Average draw only changes energy and cost: the site must still be sized for the maximum. Default price: US industrial average, July 2026, 9.77 cents per kWh ({{EIA, released Sep 24 2026|https://www.eia.gov/electricity/monthly/epm_table_grapher.php?t=epmt_5_6_a}}). Site power uses a log scale from 1 MW to 5 GW.

Accelerators the site can power

Peaks are dense BF16 per accelerator (H100 989.5, HGX B200 and B300 2,250, GB200 2,500, TPU7x 2,307 TFLOPS). Rental prices on demand, fetched 2026-10-05: Lambda H100 $3.99 and B200 $6.69 per GPU-hour, Google Cloud Ironwood $12.00 per chip-hour; none listed here for the other systems.
