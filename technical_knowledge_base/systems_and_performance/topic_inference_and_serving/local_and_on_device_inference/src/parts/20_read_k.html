9. Very low bits: ternary and 1-bit models

The idea. If a model's weights could be only −1, 0 or +1 (ternary), each weight would need log23 = 1.58 bits, a 70B model would fit in about 14 GB, and multiplication would become addition and subtraction. The catch is that you cannot get there by rounding a normally trained model: section 4 shows what that does even at 2 bits. The working route is to train the model ternary (or nearly so) from the start, or to retrain it heavily, so it learns weights that survive.

Reading these claims

Ask four questions of any "N-bit model at M tokens per second" headline: was the model trained or retrained for that precision (if not, expect the cliff above); which benchmark gives the "98%" and against which baseline; what batch size, context and hardware produced the speed (decode at batch 1 on short context is the flattering case, section 1); and does stock software run it. The 1.58-bit idea is sound; the products are young.
