7. MLX and mlx-lm on Apple silicon

MLX is Apple's array framework (unified memory, lazy evaluation) and mlx-lm its language-model package; new architectures often reach it within days, and Ollama, LM Studio and Lily build on it for Macs.

What MLX is, and why it exists beside llama.cpp (depth)

MLX is Apple's open-source array framework for its own chips: NumPy-like arrays and automatic differentiation, with two design choices that matter for inference. Arrays live in unified memory, so CPU and GPU use the same bytes without copies; and evaluation is lazy: operations build a graph that runs only when a result is needed, which lets MLX fuse work and skip what is never used. mlx-lm is the language-model package on top: generation, a server, conversion and quantisation from Hugging Face checkpoints, and LoRA fine-tuning (MLX 0.32.3, mlx-lm 0.32.0 of 1 October 2026, {{PyPI|https://pypi.org/project/mlx-lm/}}). It exists beside llama.cpp because it is a framework researchers can read and change in Python (a model is a short Python file), so new architectures often appear in mlx-lm within days, and Ollama, LM Studio and Perplexity's Lily build on it for Macs.

Its quantisation is the per-group scale and bias laid out in section 3: by default 4-bit codes with a 16-bit scale and bias per 64 weights, w ≈ scale × q + bias, 4 + 32/64 = 4.5 bits per weight, the size of Q4_0 or Q4_K with an offset like Q4_1; group size, bit width and FP4 or FP8 modes are options (closed block below).

MLX's other quantisation modes (reference)

Other modes: mxfp4 (groups of 32 sharing an 8-bit power-of-two scale: 4.25 bits, since v0.29.0, 2025-08-29), nvfp4 (groups of 16 with an FP8 scale) and mxfp8 (both since v0.29.4, 2025-11-11; {{ops.cpp|https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/blob/v0.32.3/python/src/ops.cpp}}); affine allows 2 to 8 bits (and 1) with groups of 32, 64 or 128, and mixed recipes (--quant-predicate mixed_3_6: 3 bits for most layers, 6 for sensitive ones; mlx_lm/convert.py L20 to L80). mlx-lm also ships calibrated methods (awq, gptq, dwq distilled quantisation), not measured here.

mlx_lm.server: good for one person

mlx_lm.server is for one person: it batches, but its own docs say "not recommended for production", and under load on this laptop Metal ran out of memory. For more than a few users on a Mac, llama-server is the steadier server.

Its batching limits, and why it fell over here (depth)

It batches: up to 32 requests decode together and 8 prompts are read together (--decode-concurrency, --prompt-concurrency, server.py L1876 to L1887), since v0.28.4 (December 2025); a quantised KV cache (--kv-bits) or a draft model turns batching off (L365). Its own documentation says "not recommended for production" ({{SERVER.md|https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/v0.32.0/mlx_lm/SERVER.md}}), and the parent's {{Engine bench|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c08b3bc95ff45c7b13}} found why: past about 2 requests per second on Qwen3-0.6B its memory grew to 12 GB and Metal ran out of memory, killing the generation thread. SGLang's MLX backend fared worse on this machine (wrong text from cache hits, crashes on eviction: the {{SGLang sibling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e691e4ce9f9b5d1fad}}). For more than a few users on a Mac, llama-server is the steadier server.

Beyond one Mac, and other engines on MLX (depth, vendor claims)
  • Several Macs. mx.distributed splits a model over Macs (tensor or pipeline parallel, --pipeline in mlx-lm). From macOS 26.2, RDMA over Thunderbolt 5 lets Macs exchange data directly; MLX's JACCL back end uses it with "communication latency an order of magnitude lower than the ring backend" ({{MLX distributed docs|https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/usage/distributed.html}}). docs
  • Lily (Perplexity, open source, 2 September 2026): a Rust and Metal server for one model, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, on M5-class chips, loading MLX's 4-bit checkpoints. Its own report against mlx-lm on an M5 Max: decode 1.31 times faster at 256 tokens of context and 1.24 at 131K; prefill 1.13 times at 256 tokens but 0.80 (slower) at 131K ({{pplx-garden performance doc|https://github.com/perplexityai/pplx-garden/blob/main/lily/docs/2026-09-02-performance-mlx-0.32.2.md}}). vendor A specialised engine buys speed for one model on one chip.
  • Ollama on MLX: its blog reported Qwen3.5-35B-A3B in NVFP4 on MLX against Q4_K_M on llama.cpp at 1,810 against 1,154 tokens per second of prefill and 112 against 58 of decode, using the M5's GPU neural accelerators ({{Ollama blog, 30 March 2026|https://ollama.com/blog/mlx}}). vendor, one model, one chip, two different quantisations.
