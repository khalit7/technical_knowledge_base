8. Three workloads that change the rules

Long context

The cache grows with every token: 128K tokens of Llama 3.1 70B are 40 GiB 131,072 × 320 KiB, four fifths of the roughly 54 GB an H200 has left for the cache after FP8 weights (section 2), for one conversation. Every decode step reads all of it, so tokens slow down as the context grows: measured here, Qwen3-0.6B went from 198 tokens per second with an empty context to 56 at 16,384 tokens, because the cache read became larger than the weight read measured here. Prefill attention grows with the square of the prompt, so a long prompt can hold a GPU for seconds. The levers: chunked prefill (so a long prompt does not stall everyone), an FP8 cache, offloading cold blocks, splitting one sequence across GPUs (context parallelism, ring attention), and models built for it (sliding-window layers as in gpt-oss, MLA as in DeepSeek). How well models actually use long contexts is on {{Long-context and multimodal benchmarks|n:3ef5c17b0d0d810bb4dfc98919c9be8a}}.

Structured output

To guarantee valid JSON (or any grammar), the engine masks the vocabulary at every step: tokens that would break the grammar get probability zero before sampling. Computing that mask every step once slowed decoding; XGrammar precomputes it for most tokens and reports up to 100 times faster masks and near-zero end-to-end overhead ({{Dong et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15100}}, the authors); vLLM uses xgrammar or guidance as back ends. A constraint can also push the model away from what it would have written, so check quality, not only validity.

Many fine-tuned variants: multi-LoRA

A LoRA adapter is a small low-rank change to a base model, megabytes against gigabytes. One base model can serve many adapters in one batch, each request applying its own through a batched kernel, adapters paged in from CPU memory. Punica reported 12 times the throughput of serving systems of the time with 2 ms added per token ({{Chen et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18547}}); S-LoRA, thousands of adapters on one GPU ({{Sheng et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03285}}); both the authors' measurements. vLLM, SGLang and TensorRT-LLM support it.
