10. Cost and capacity

You pay per token to an API provider or per GPU-hour for your own servers. Self-hosted, a token costs the hour divided by the tokens the hour produced, which depends on everything above and on how busy the fleet is.

GPUs = ⌈ peak requests/s ÷ requests/s one copy serves within the SLO ⌉ × GPUs per copy
$ per million tokens = $ per GPU-hour × GPUs ÷ (millions of tokens per hour at average traffic)
within the SLO
the highest load at which p99 TTFT and the TPOT target both hold (the knee of section 3, not the peak throughput)
average traffic
a fleet sized for the peak is paid for all day, busy or not

All derived by the {{Capacity planner|#t-plan}} (worked example "Chat assistant"), whose step model is calibrated on MLPerf runs; prices as listed there.

For this workload the API is cheaper. Self-hosting wins with high and steady utilization, reserved or cheaper GPUs (DigitalOcean's 12-month H200 price is $3.40), traffic with heavy prefix reuse, a model or fine-tune no provider hosts, or data that cannot leave your network. The planner shows which knob moves the answer: in the running example the 50 ms TPOT target, not memory, sets the number of copies; relaxing it to 100 ms cuts the fleet from 10 GPUs to 8 ($35.76 an hour). Measured numbers from this laptop do not transfer to these dollar figures; ratios and the shapes of the curves do (the {{Engine bench|#t-bench}}'s "What transfers" section).

Predict: an API charges $0.40 per million tokens; your H200 at $4.47 an hour pushes 2,982 output tokens per second at full batch. Is the GPU cheaper per token?
About the same, and only on paper. 2,982 tokens per second is 10.7 million an hour: $0.42 per million output tokens (the prompts come with them). But that is the GPU fully loaded, every hour, at 47 ms per token. Sized for a peak with latency targets and paid for at half load, the planner's fleet comes to $44.70 an hour against $33.12 for the API. Utilization, not peak speed, decides self-host against API.
