Reading the log. Each decode batch prints a line such as #running-req: 233, #token: 370959, token usage: 0.82, gen throughput (token/s): 4594.01, #queue-req: 317 (the docs' example on an H100-class GPU). Token usage above 0.9 with a queue means the pool is the limit; a queue of 0 in an offline job means the client is not sending fast enough (the docs suggest 100 to 2,000 waiting for offline throughput); frequent retraction means the scheduler admitted more than fit. The prefill lines print #new-token and #cached-token, the radix tree's hit for that batch.

8. The router: keeping a conversation where its cache is

The problem. With several replicas of a model, each has its own radix tree. A round-robin load balancer sends a conversation's next turn to a replica that has never seen it, so the hit rate falls toward one over the number of replicas; sending every request to the replica holding its prefix piles popular prefixes onto one replica. The router has to trade cache affinity against load.

How the cache-aware policy picks a worker: balance first, then the longest prefix, then the least loaded

{{cache_aware.rs, select_worker L387|https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/blob/v0.5.21/sgl-model-gateway/src/policies/cache_aware.rs#L387}}, thresholds at the command-line defaults of v0.5.21 ({{main.rs L150-170|https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/blob/v0.5.21/sgl-model-gateway/src/main.rs#L150}}).

The router (now the SGLang Model Gateway, written in Rust) keeps an approximate radix tree per worker of the request texts it has sent there, in characters rather than tokens so it never tokenizes, and never asks the workers what they actually hold. Imbalance means the busiest worker has more than balance_abs_threshold requests more than the idlest and more than balance_rel_threshold times as many; a prefix match counts when the matched share exceeds cache_threshold. The file's header comment says a miss goes to the worker with the "smallest tree size"; the code picks the least loaded, ties broken at random. Trees are trimmed least recently used on a timer.

Defaults, and whether they act. The command-line defaults are a 0.3 cache threshold, 64 requests absolute and 1.5 times relative imbalance, eviction every 120 s and up to 226 tree nodes; the Rust struct's own Default (0.5, 32, 1.1, 30 s, 10,000) and the Python launcher (eviction 60 s) disagree with them, so check which entry point you use. At those defaults the imbalance rule may never act: replaying the Mooncake conversation trace on three real replicas, {{LLM serving in production|n:3f35c17b0d0d818aa406da1f7a784c62}} (section 2) never saw it fire, because no replica ever had more than 10 requests in flight, far below 64 measured there. The v0.4 post reported 1.9 times the throughput and a hit rate of 75% against 20% with 8 replicas on a workload of long shared-prefix groups ({{LMSYS, 2024-12-04|https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-12-04-sglang-v0-4/}}) vendor, one workload.

The gateway's other policies and features (reference)

The other policies are round_robin, random, power_of_two (pick the less loaded of two random workers), prefix_hash, consistent_hashing and bucket ({{policies/|https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/tree/v0.5.21/sgl-model-gateway/src/policies}}). The gateway also does prefill/decode routing, retries, circuit breakers, rate limits and tokenization in Rust ({{README|https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/tree/v0.5.21/sgl-model-gateway}}). A router in front of real replicas, measured against round robin and least-loaded, is on {{LLM serving in production|n:3f35c17b0d0d818aa406da1f7a784c62}}; its section 2 steps eight requests through three real replicas under each routing rule.
