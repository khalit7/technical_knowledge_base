Questions about a few long documents, all arriving at once in a shuffled order, and a KV pool that holds only some of the documents: the case where the order of the queue decides how much of the cache gets used (section 4). All four of SGLang's main policies run on the same queue; the animation shows the rounds one at a time.

Try: 96 questions and a 3,000-token pool (lpm and dfs-weight well ahead); raise the questions above 128 (lpm switches itself off until the queue drains); raise the pool to 9,000 (every order converges); change the arrival order (fcfs and random swing widely, the cache-aware orders hardly move).

On the real engine

The model, and how it was checked
A round: match every waiting question against the tree; order the queue by the policy; admit questions in that order while each one's new tokens plus its output fit in the free and evictable pool and the round's prefill stays under 16,384 tokens, stopping at the first that does not fit; run the admitted ones to completion and insert them. Real SGLang admits continuously, reserves output space by an estimate scaled by --schedule-conservativeness, and overlaps prefill with running decodes, so the numbers here are the policy's effect in isolation, not a throughput prediction. The orderings were checked against SGLang v0.5.21's own SchedulePolicy.calc_priority on a shadow RadixCache: . Arrival orders and the random policy use the root simulator's seeded generator.
