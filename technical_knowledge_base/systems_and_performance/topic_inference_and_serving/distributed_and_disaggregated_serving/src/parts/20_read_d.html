5. Expert parallelism at serve time: tokens fly to their experts

A mixture-of-experts (MoE) layer replaces the MLP with many smaller experts and a router that sends each token to a few of them (the layer itself, routing and its training-time balancing are on {{Mixture of experts|n:3fd5c17b0d0d81fda06bf955af7c2b58}}). DeepSeek-V3 (and R1) has 256 routed experts per MoE layer, picks 8 per token, and has 58 MoE layers: 671 billion parameters in all, 37.6 billion used per token as the hardware root's calculator counts them from the {{config.json|https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/blob/main/config.json}} (DeepSeek rounds to 671B and 37B). Most of the weights are experts, and any one token touches a few percent of them.

At serve time each GPU gets a few experts, and the attention layers usually run data-parallel (section 2), each GPU attending over its own users. Every MoE layer then needs two all-to-alls: dispatch (each token's hidden state to its 8 experts' GPUs) and combine (the 8 results back).

FP8 dispatch and BF16 combine are what DeepSeek uses ({{inference system overview|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/open-infra-index/blob/main/202502OpenSourceWeek/day_6_one_more_thing_deepseekV3R1_inference_system_overview.md}}). derived

Unlike tensor parallelism, much of this traffic can be overlapped: while one micro-batch's tokens are in flight, another micro-batch computes (DeepSeek's "dual-batch overlap"). That is why expert parallelism can cross servers where tensor parallelism cannot, and why it has its own communication libraries. DeepEP, DeepSeek's, had "normal" kernels for prefill (large batches, bandwidth-bound) and "low-latency" kernels for decode (pure RDMA); on H800s with 400 Gb/s cards its v1 README reported dispatch of 128 tokens in 77 µs at EP 8 and 194 µs at EP 256 ({{DeepEP v1.2.1 README|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepEP/blob/v1.2.1/README.md}}, 2025-09-16, vendor; the current README is a rewrite, "V2.5", without these tables). vLLM exposes the same split as --all2all-backend deepep_high_throughput or deepep_low_latency ({{config/parallel.py L44-56|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.31.0/vllm/config/parallel.py}}).

The catch: the busiest GPU sets the pace

The router decides where tokens go, not the scheduler, and some experts are far more popular than others. Every GPU must finish its experts before the combine, so a step lasts as long as the most loaded GPU. Measured here on a real MoE (IBM's Granite 3.1 1B-A400M, 32 experts, 8 per token, on WikiText-2):

Redundant experts are the fix DeepSeek published: copy the hottest experts onto extra slots and pack the copies so every GPU carries about the same load. Their {{EPLB|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/EPLB}} (Expert Parallelism Load Balancer) does it greedily from recent load statistics: replicate whichever expert has the highest load per copy until the slots run out, then fill GPUs heaviest-first. A hierarchical variant first balances groups of experts across servers (for prefill), a global one ignores groups (for decode). vLLM turns it on with --enable-eplb. The chart replays the real router's choices on 4 or 8 simulated GPUs; the balancer plans on the first half of the text (the history) and is judged on unseen batches from the second half (the traffic). What a production deployment looks like

DeepSeek's own V3/R1 service, as of February 2025 vendor: prefill units of 4 servers (32 H800s, expert parallelism 32, each GPU holding 9 routed experts and 1 shared expert, with 32 redundant experts) and decode units of 18 servers (144 GPUs, expert parallelism 144, 2 routed experts and 1 shared expert per GPU), so prefill and decode are also disaggregated (section 6). The open re-creations of this layout (SGLang on 96 H100s, llm-d's wide-EP path, TensorRT-LLM's wide-EP on GB200 NVL72) are on {{SGLang|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e691e4ce9f9b5d1fad}} (section 9, with its two-batch overlap, vendor figures) and in section 8. TensorRT-LLM's team adds a caution: "if the MoE GroupGEMM computation is not the bottleneck, then large-scale EP may not help much" ({{wide-EP blog, part 1|https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/blob/main/docs/source/blogs/tech_blog/blog04_Scaling_Expert_Parallelism_in_TensorRT-LLM.md}}, vendor).
