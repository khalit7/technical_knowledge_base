7. When disaggregation pays

Measure goodput, not throughput: the highest request rate at which a target share of requests (90% here) meets both the time-to-first-token and the time-per-output-token targets, per GPU (DistServe's measure; {{Batching, KV cache and scheduling in depth|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}, section 7, owns the definition and its scheduling side). The DistServe paper's own worked example shows why splitting can win on this measure ({{Zhong et al. 2024, section 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09670v3}}, a 13B model on A100s, the authors' figures):

The win comes from removing interference, so it is largest exactly when interference hurts most: strict per-token targets and long prompts. With loose targets, colocated engines with chunked prefill use every GPU for prompts and every GPU for decoding, and nothing is stranded in an idle pool.

What is simulated, and what is not

The model is Llama 3.1 70B with FP8 weights and a BF16 KV cache on H200s, one engine per GPU (no tensor parallelism): the root's running example. Weights and the KV pool per GPU come from the {{Capacity planner|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c08b3bc95ff45c7b13}}'s memory model (); every step's duration is the simulator's roofline, with compute and memory efficiencies and per-step overheads fitted on NVIDIA's published NIM measurements of Llama 3.1 8B on an H100 (vendor figures) and applied to the H200's peak and bandwidth. Scheduling (continuous batching, paged blocks, chunked prefill with an 8,192-token budget, recompute preemption) is the simulator's, which matched vLLM v0.31.0's own scheduler step for step on the traces the root compared. Requests arrive as a Poisson process; requests per run, the first 10% left out as warm-up.

Disaggregated mode: a request goes round robin to a prefill engine, which computes its prompt; the whole cache then travels to a decode engine (round robin) as one transfer of (prompt tokens × 320 KiB) at the link's full speed, and the decode engine produces every output token after the first. Not modelled: sending the cache layer by layer while prefill runs (real systems hide most of the transfer this way), link contention, routing by load, and the decode engine's prefix cache. What has been published

Every big number comes with long prompts, strict per-token targets, a large MoE model or a rack-scale system, and is measured by the people who built the system: DistServe's "7.4x more requests or 12.6x tighter SLO", Mooncake's "75% more requests" in Kimi's production. The modest numbers come from ordinary dense models on ordinary servers: Oracle's 10 to 30% on MI300X, and vLLM's own documentation, "Disaggregated prefill DOES NOT improve throughput". For a small fleet, chunked prefill with a tuned token budget gets much of the benefit ({{Batching, KV cache and scheduling in depth|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}, section 5).
