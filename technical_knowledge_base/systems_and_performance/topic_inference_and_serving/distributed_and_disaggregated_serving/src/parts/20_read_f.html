7. When disaggregation pays

Measure goodput, not throughput. DistServe's measure: the highest request rate at which a target share of requests (90% here) meets both the time-to-first-token and the time-per-output-token targets, per GPU ({{Batching, KV cache and scheduling in depth|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}, section 7, owns the definition and its scheduling side). Throughput that breaks the targets is not useful. The DistServe paper's own worked example shows why splitting can win on this measure ({{Zhong et al. 2024, section 1|https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09670v3}}, a 13B model on A100s, the authors' figures):

Colocated
1.6 req/s per GPU

the most one GPU serves while meeting both targets

Each phase alone

3.3 req/s per GPU

10 req/s on 3 GPUs: 2.1 times the colocated goodput

The win comes from removing interference, so it is largest exactly when interference hurts most: strict per-token targets and long prompts. With loose targets, colocated engines with chunked prefill use every GPU for prompts and every GPU for decoding, and nothing is stranded in an idle pool.

This is the pattern TaiChi reports: "aggregation is optimal for tight time-to-first-token (TTFT) and relaxed time-per-output-token (TPOT), while disaggregation excels for strict TPOT and relaxed TTFT" ({{arXiv 2508.01989|https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01989}}, the authors').
