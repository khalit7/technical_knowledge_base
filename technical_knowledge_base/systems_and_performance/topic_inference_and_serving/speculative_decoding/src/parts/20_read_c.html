4. How many tokens does a step give?

Say each guess is kept with probability α = 0.8, independently, and k = 4. The first guess survives with probability 0.8, the first two with 0.64, three with 0.512, all four with 0.4096, and the target always adds one token of its own. So a step yields on average 1 + 0.8 + 0.64 + 0.512 + 0.4096 = 3.36 tokens per target pass derived.

Tokens per pass saturate: long drafts stop paying, and α matters more than k

E[tokens per step] = 1 + α + α2 + … + αk = (1 − αk+1) / (1 − α)
α
acceptance probability of one guess, Σ min(p, q), averaged over positions
k
guesses per step

The yield is capped at 1/(1 − α) however long the draft: at α = 0.7 that is 3.33, and k = 4 already gets 2.77 of it. And it is very non-linear in α: at k = 4, α = 0.5 gives 1.94 tokens per step, α = 0.9 gives 4.10. "Acceptance rate 0.7" and "acceptance length 2.8" are different numbers; engines report one or the other (vLLM and SGLang acceptance length, llama.cpp drafted and accepted counts, from which a per-drafted-token rate follows but not α), and a per-drafted-token rate falls as k grows because tokens after the first rejection are wasted.

Real acceptance is anything but uniform: near 1 inside a word or a repeated pattern, near 0 where the text could go many ways. Click a token below to see the two distributions there.

α follows the kind of token, not its position. A long chain of thought does not wear a drafter down. Over 1.78 million verification rounds on MATH-500, with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B as target and two independent 1.5B drafters, acceptance did not fall with position out to 8K tokens under greedy decoding and 12K under sampling, once a greedy repetition pathology was removed; rejections clustered instead on reflection tokens such as "wait", "however" and "check" ({{Speculative Acceptance Does Not Decay with Position, arXiv 2610.30644|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30644}}). That is the pattern in the coloured text above: α drops at choices, wherever they fall.

Acceptance can be attacked (adversarial suffixes, 2026)

Because acceptance depends on the text, text can be written to lower it: an adversarial suffix appended to attacker-controlled content (a retrieved page, a shared document) makes drafter and target disagree more often, in some cases until speculative decoding is slower than plain decoding; the suffixes survive sampling and transfer across drafters, and regularising them to keep output quality gives up most of the slowdown ({{Speedbumps (arXiv 2610.10929)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10929}}). Per-request acceptance is worth monitoring for that reason too.

speed-up = E[tokens per step] × t(1) / ( k · tdraft + t(k + 1) )
t(n)
time of one target pass over n tokens per sequence (section 1's curve)
tdraft
time of one draft pass; Leviathan et al. call tdraft/t(1) the cost coefficient c

Leviathan et al. assume t(k + 1) = t(1), which gives their speed-up (1 − αk+1) / ((1 − α)(kc + 1)), with c below 0.05 for their T5 drafts (though their own Table 4 gives 0.11 for T5-large). Neither assumption holds on the laptop.

The batch-size crossover. Serve B users at once (how an engine forms those batches is on {{Batching, KV cache and scheduling in depth|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}) and the verification pass carries B × (k + 1) tokens. The weights are still read once per step, so plain decoding gets cheaper per token as B grows, but the flat part of section 1's curve is used up by the batch itself: once B tokens fill the arithmetic, every extra guessed token costs real compute, and rejected guesses are pure waste. Speculation trades idle arithmetic for latency, and a full batch has no idle arithmetic.

What the published GPU results say
The same crossover appears in every measured source, further out because an H100 stays memory-bound longer. EAGLE-3 in SGLang on one H100 (Llama 3.1 8B, MT-Bench): 1.81x throughput at batch 2, 1.48x at 16, 1.38x at 64; the older EAGLE fell below 1x from batch 24 ({{EAGLE-3, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01840}}, measured by the SGLang team). DFlash on one B200 with Qwen3-4B: 4.8x at concurrency 1, 2.9x at 32 ({{DFlash, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06036}}, authors). vLLM's own blog: 1.5x to 2.8x at QPS 1, but 1.4x to 1.8x slower at high QPS on Llama 3 70B, 4x H100 ({{2024-10-17|https://vllm.ai/blog/2024-10-17-spec-decode}}, vendor). On an L40S at QPS 16, even k = 1 cost 14% ({{TurboSpec|https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14066}}, vLLM authors). SGLang: up to 60% more output throughput for DeepSeek V3 with MTP at 2 concurrent requests per GPU, 14% at 128 ({{LMSYS, 2025-07-17|https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-07-17-mtp}}, vendor). No benchmark across batch sizes by a party that is neither vendor nor author was found. Every headline number in section 6 is at batch 1 unless stated: measure at your real concurrency.

Two things move the crossover out. Long contexts: every sequence's KV cache is also read each step, a read that grows with batch size times context length, so at long context a big batch is still memory-bound and verification is cheap again; MagicDec reports up to 2.51x for Llama 3.1 8B at batch 32 to 256 on 8x H100 with long contexts ({{Sadhukhan et al., ICLR 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11049}}, authors). Cheaper drafts and adaptive length: a drafter whose cost does not grow with k (block diffusion), and choosing k per step from the load (TurboSpec, SGLang's adaptive table, vLLM's batch-size schedule, DSpark's per-request verification length) keep the gain at low load and avoid the loss at high load.

Does the drafter need a long context of its own? (2026 result)

No. A drafter that attends over the full prefix inherits the target's context cost and its speed-up erodes, then reverses, at ultra-long context, but target-conditioned drafters (EAGLE-style heads) already read the target's hidden states over the whole prefix. Frozen short-context drafters with a bounded attention cache and rotary positions re-based into their trained range kept their short-context speed-up flat from 4k to two million tokens across three target-drafter pairs and five corpora, at a cost of at most 0.16x of speed-up at short length ({{Long-Context Drafting Does Not Require Long-Context Drafters (arXiv 2610.20865)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20865}}, authors).
