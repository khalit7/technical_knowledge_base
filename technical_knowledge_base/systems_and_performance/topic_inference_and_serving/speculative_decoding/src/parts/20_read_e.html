8. In production: the knobs, and what to watch

Draft length kThe main knob. vLLM's num_speculative_tokens has no default for a draft model (it must be set); llama.cpp's --spec-draft-n-max defaults to 3. Raise it while acceptance is high and verification is cheap; lower it as the batch grows.
Draft samplingvLLM drafts greedily by default (draft_sample_method="greedy"), so q is one-hot and a draft token is kept with probability p(token). llama.cpp also drafts greedily and can stop a draft early when the draft's top probability falls below --spec-draft-p-min (default 0).
Batch-size scheduleAll three datacenter engines can now shorten or switch off speculation as the batch grows (engine table below). SGLang's default adaptive table goes from candidate k of 1 to 7 at batch 1 down to 0 at batch 64.
What to watchAcceptance length (tokens per verification step) and draft acceptance rate per request, and TPOT and throughput against the same server without speculation, at your real concurrency. vLLM exports vllm:spec_decode_num_drafts_total, ..._num_draft_tokens_total, ..._num_accepted_tokens_total and accepted tokens per position on /metrics (acceptance length = 1 + accepted / drafts; {{v1/spec_decode/metrics.py|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.31.0/vllm/v1/spec_decode/metrics.py#L180}}); llama-server returns draft_n and draft_n_accepted per request.

How vLLM's scheduler counts draft tokens as ordinary tokens to compute (num_tokens_with_spec), which speculative methods its Model Runner V2 does not support yet, and its per-request acceptance metrics are on {{vLLM: inside the default engine|n:3c65c17b0d0d8113b976d8bcaf2c0722}}. SGLang's scheduler, including the overlap scheduler its speculative workers run inside, is explained on {{SGLang: radix cache, cache-aware scheduling, large-scale serving|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e691e4ce9f9b5d1fad}}.

Recent developments, dated (2025 to 2026; volatile)
  • 2025-03: EAGLE-3 (training-time test, fused features) became the drafter most engines ship; drafts for Llama 4, gpt-oss, Qwen3 and Gemma 4 followed from NVIDIA, Red Hat, Tencent's AngelSlim and LMSYS ({{arXiv 2503.01840|https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01840}}).
  • 2025-03 to 2025-04: Snowflake's Arctic Inference (suffix decoding, now imported by vLLM) and vLLM's speculators library for training and packaging drafts ({{speculators|https://github.com/vllm-project/speculators}}).
  • 2025-07 to 2026: MTP heads shipped inside open checkpoints became common (Qwen3-Next, GLM, MiMo, Gemma 4 among the 27 MTP model types in vLLM v0.31.0; the SGLang throughput result is in section 5).
  • 2026-01 to 2026-05: DFlash, block-diffusion drafting, published drafters for many open models and was accepted at ICML 2026 ({{arXiv 2602.06036|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06036}}).
  • 2026-03: vLLM's P-EAGLE drafts up to 10 tokens in one pass: 1.55x over EAGLE-3 at concurrency 1, 1.05x at 64, GPT-OSS 20B on one B200 ({{vLLM blog|https://vllm.ai/blog/p-eagle}}, vendor).
  • 2026-07: DeepSeek open-sourced DeepSpec and DSpark, which it runs in DeepSeek-V4 production; its argument is the batch-size crossover itself: verifying long blocks "wastes critical batch capacity", so it chooses per request how much of the block to verify ({{arXiv 2607.05147|https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05147}}).
  • 2026-08: the DFlash 2 drafter for Qwen3.8-27B reports 3.43x at concurrency 1 on GSM8K on one H200, 1.45x at 32, where the model's own seven-token MTP falls to between 0.77x and 1.04x ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/incoai/Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2}}, drafter's publisher). The parent page cites the 3.43x; the other two numbers are the more useful ones.
