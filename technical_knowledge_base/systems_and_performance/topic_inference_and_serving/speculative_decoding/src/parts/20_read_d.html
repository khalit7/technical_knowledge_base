6. Drafters: where the guesses come from

Everything so far works with any drafter that proposes tokens and (for sampling) says how likely it thought each one was. The drafters differ in three things: how well they guess (acceptance), what a guess costs (the cost ratio c), and what you have to train or ship. They form a ladder, from no model at all to heads trained into the target itself.

Why feature-level drafters guess better

A separate 0.6B model has to work out what the 4B model is thinking from the tokens alone. EAGLE's head is handed the 4B model's own hidden state (its summary of everything so far), so its job is much easier: predict what the next feature, and so the next token, will be. That is why a single extra layer can out-guess a separate model several times its size: on Qwen3-4B, the AngelSlim EAGLE-3 head (one layer) reports a mean accepted length of 2.08 with 2 draft tokens on one H20 in vLLM, where plain decoding gives 1 ({{model card|https://huggingface.co/AngelSlim/Qwen3-4B_eagle3}}, vendor). The cost: the head only works with the exact target it was trained for, and the engine must pass hidden states from the target to the drafter every step.

MTP takes this one step further: the prediction module is trained with the model, so the model's maker ships the draft. DeepSeek-V3 trained one MTP module (depth 1) to improve pre-training and noted it can be "repurposed ... for speculative decoding" ({{DeepSeek-V3 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} has a calculator for what 85 to 90% acceptance implies). With one MTP token, a step yields at most 2 tokens; engines chain the module to draft 2 to 4.

Block diffusion attacks the other term, the drafting cost. An autoregressive drafter needs k sequential passes for k tokens, so c grows with k; a diffusion drafter writes the block in one pass, so a 16-token draft costs about as much as a 1-token draft. Its weakness is that block positions are guessed without seeing each other (the "suffix decay" DSpark's Markov head tries to fix). The same idea used as the whole model rather than as a drafter is on the {{DiffusionGemma paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d818390bfd7969757211e}}.

Not every acceleration in this family is exact
Medusa-2's typical acceptance accepts a guessed token when the target gives it probability above min(ε, δ·e−H) (H the entropy of the target's distribution); it is faster but no longer samples from the target ({{Cai et al. 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774}}). TensorRT-LLM's "relaxed acceptance" for DeepSeek-R1's thinking phase is lossy in the same way (accept if in the target's top 10 within a probability gap; {{TensorRT-LLM blog|https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/blob/v1.2.1/docs/source/blogs/tech_blog/blog1_Pushing_Latency_Boundaries_Optimizing_DeepSeek-R1_Performance_on_NVIDIA_B200_GPUs.md}}). Exactness is a property of the verification rule, not of the drafter. And a lossy rule's acceptance overstates its speed: across twelve target-drafter pairs, at each training-free relaxed rule's most relaxed setting, verification accepted 1.34x as many tokens per round as the exact rule but completions came out 1.54x longer, mostly longer reasoning, which largely cancelled the gain; the rules that cut verifier rounds typically lost accuracy substantially ({{Acceptance Is Not Speed (arXiv 2610.23818)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23818}}). Under a lossy rule, report completion length and verifier rounds beside acceptance length.

7. Trees: checking several guesses in one pass

A chain draft dies at its first wrong token. If the draft was unsure between two words, it could propose both and let the target pick: a draft tree. All branches are checked in the same forward pass using a tree attention mask (each candidate token attends only to its own ancestors), introduced by {{SpecInfer|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09781}} and Medusa, and made adaptive in EAGLE-2, which grows the tree where the draft's confidence (the product of its probabilities along the path) is highest. The target keeps the longest root path it agrees with.

The trade: a tree spends its verification budget on breadth instead of depth. With a budget of M tokens checked per pass, a chain of M goes deep on one guess; a tree of M nodes covers the likely alternatives near the root, where most chains fail.
