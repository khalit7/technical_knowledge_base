5. Chunked prefill: the token budget as a latency dial

The problem. Continuous batching lets a new prompt join a step that is also decoding other users. If the whole prompt joins at once, that step lasts as long as the prompt's prefill, and every user mid-answer sees their stream freeze: a generation stall (the term is from {{Sarathi-Serve, Agrawal et al., OSDI 2024|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02310}}). The parent's Engine bench saw one: a gap of 803 ms in a stream whose median gap was 31.4 ms.

How it works. Cap the new tokens per step (the token budget of section 1), schedule the decodes first, and let a waiting prompt fill what is left, one chunk per step, until its prompt is done. Sarathi introduced chunked prefills with "decode-maximal batching" ({{Agrawal et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16369}}); Sarathi-Serve made them stall-free and reports 2.6 times the serving capacity of vLLM at the time for Mistral-7B on one A100, 3.7 times for Yi-34B on two A100s and 5.6 times for Falcon-180B with pipeline parallelism, under tail-latency targets (authors' measurement). The engines:

  • vLLM v0.31.0: on by default ({{config/scheduler.py L135|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.31.0/vllm/config/scheduler.py#L135}}); the scheduler walks the running requests first (their decodes, and the unfinished chunks of prompts already started), then admits waiting requests while budget remains ({{scheduler.py, schedule()|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.31.0/vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py}}).
  • llama-server: each step first adds every generating slot's sampled token, then pending prompt tokens until the batch holds -b tokens ({{server-context.cpp, update_slots L3100 to L3115 and L3524|https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/7fe450e19305b828c199d602c23a8337aaa1f03b/tools/server/server-context.cpp#L3100}}). So -b is its chunk size, decode-first, the same design.
  • SGLang v0.5.21: --chunked-prefill-size, set from GPU memory when not given (8,192 on H100 and H200, section 1); -1 turns it off.
  • TensorRT-LLM v1.2.1: enable_chunked_prefill is False by default ({{llm_args.py L2006|https://github.com/NVIDIA/TensorRT-LLM/blob/v1.2.1/tensorrt_llm/llmapi/llm_args.py#L2006}}).

The trade. A smaller budget means shorter stalls, but the long prompt needs more steps, each paying fixed costs and re-reading the weights, so its TTFT rises a little and total throughput falls a little. It was measured here on a real engine: four users decoding on llama-server while a fifth sends a 6,011-token prompt, at six budgets.

Stall against TTFT, one point per token budget

Measured here (Qwen3-1.7B, llama-server, three runs per budget): with a budget of 128 tokens per step the four decoding users never waited more than 242 ms, but the 6,008-token prompt took 9.55 s to its first token; at 512 tokens, 762 ms and 7.57 s; at 2,048, 2.66 s and 7.17 s; at 8,192, where the whole prompt fits in one step, the four users froze for 7.26 s and the prompt took 7.28 s. Alone, the same four streams had gaps of 26 ms (median) and 33 ms (p99). The three runs of each budget agreed within 2.5% on TTFT and 4% on the longest gap, although other work kept the machine's load average between 17 and 151. The simulator, given only its M1 Pro preset and Qwen3-1.7B's shape, comes within 4% to 11% of both numbers for budgets of 512 and above, and is 20% to 24% low at 128, where per-step costs it underestimates dominate. One more lesson from the same runs: at budgets of 2,048 and 8,192 the p99 of all gaps was 37 ms, because the stall is one gap among two thousand. Only the maximum shows it.

Predict: on the simulated H100, halving the budget from 2,048 to 1,024 tokens per step. What happens to the long prompt's TTFT?
About 2%: 0.431 s to 0.439 s, while the longest gap the eight decoding users see halves, from 79 ms to 42 ms (derived, the parent's simulator with the H100 preset fitted to NVIDIA's published measurements). Each chunk is still large enough to keep the GPU busy, so cutting it costs little; the cost grows below about 512 tokens (0.471 s at 512, 0.880 s at 128), where each step's fixed costs and weight reads dominate.

Choosing the budget

Pick the largest budget whose stall your inter-token latency target tolerates. Under steady traffic, with 20 chat requests per second and 2 long-document requests per second on the simulated H100, the p99 inter-token gap goes from 13 ms at a 512-token budget to 45 ms at 2,048 and 267 ms at 16,384, while p99 TTFT goes from 1.46 s to 1.07 s to 0.95 s and throughput does not move ({{Scheduling lab|#t-sch}}, section 1). vLLM's 8,192 default on an H100 is tuned for throughput; an interactive service with a 50 ms TPOT target wants about 2,048 on this model.

Cost, when it matters, how to check

Cost: slightly longer TTFT for long prompts and slightly lower peak throughput at small budgets. When it matters: any mix of long prompts (documents, retrieval, agents with big tool outputs) and streaming users. How to check: look at the maximum and p99 inter-token latency, not the mean (vllm:inter_token_latency_seconds is a histogram), during periods when long prompts arrive.
