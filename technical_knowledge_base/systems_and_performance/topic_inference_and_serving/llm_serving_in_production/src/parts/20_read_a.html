The idea in one sentenceA production LLM service is many copies of one engine behind a router and an autoscaler, and its job is to keep a latency promise (time to first token and time per token, at a percentile) for the fewest GPU-hours: send each request where its prefix is already cached without piling up a queue, add replicas before the queue forms (they take tens of seconds to start), and prove it with load that does not wait for the server.

Every decision on this page sits on one request's path

A request goes from the client through a gateway (keys, rate limits) to a router, which picks one replica (a running copy of the engine with its own KV cache). Replicas export metrics; an autoscaler reads them and adds or removes replicas, each of which serves only after its cold start. The section that owns each box is printed on it; the promise (section 1) is measured where the user is, at the client.

You need: what happens inside one engine (prefill, decode, the KV cache, continuous batching), from the parent {{Topic: inference-and-serving|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c08b3bc95ff45c7b13}}, and prefix caching from {{Batching, KV cache and scheduling in depth|n:3c65c17b0d0d819db53bd2c48909e5b2}}. Running example (the parent's): a chat assistant on Llama 3.1 70B in FP8, one H200 per replica, 2,000-token prompts of which 1,000 are a shared system prompt, 300-token answers. Evidence: real replicas, a real trace and real engines run on an Apple M1 Pro, the routers' and engines' own code at pinned versions (llama.cpp build 11146, vLLM v0.31.0, SGLang v0.5.21), and published datacenter results kept apart and labelled.

SLO (service level objective): a latency target at a percentile over a window, "99% of first tokens within 2 s".
Goodput: the request rate that still meets the SLO; throughput that breaks it does not count.
Replica: one running copy of the model; the router picks one for each request.
Prefix hit rate: the share of prompt tokens a replica found already in its cache.
Cold start: everything between "start a replica" and its first served request; headroom: spare capacity kept to cover it.
Closed / open loop: a load generator whose users wait for each answer / one that sends on a fixed schedule.

DecisionWhat the evidence here says
1. How many GPUs, at what price per tokenSize for goodput at the peak; at half the peak on average, 10 H200s cost $0.54 per million tokens against an API's $0.40. The fleet breaks even at 67% average load.
2. Which replica gets each requestFollowing the cache saves prefill only if the chosen replica still holds the prefix, and a router also needs a load signal: on a real multi-turn trace a cache-plus-queue score gave the lowest TTFT.
3. When to add and remove replicasNot on GPU utilization (96% at a tenth of capacity): queue depth, KV usage or a TTFT SLO, with headroom for the start-up time.
4. How long a new replica takesBytes over bandwidth plus engine initialization; where the weights come from can dominate.
5. Many fine-tunes on one modelAn engine must batch different adapters: llama-server cannot (352 tokens/s on one adapter, 83 to 127 on several); vLLM mixed four at no extra cost.
7. Which signals and alertsTTFT and TPOT percentiles, queue, KV, hit rate; burn-rate alerts on two SLOs.
8. How to load-test honestlyA 5-second freeze: 2 slow requests seen by a closed-loop generator, 94 by an open-loop one.
