3. Diversity against quality: twelve decoders on real text

Every truncation trades diversity for staying in the model's high-probability region, and human text sits in between. GPT-2 small (124M) is given the first 48 tokens of four paragraphs of {{Three Men in a Boat|https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/308}} and writes 48 more, 16 times per decoder; the book's real next 48 tokens are the human reference measured here.

Search sits far below human perplexity; top-p explodes with temperature, min-p does not

Perplexity: exp of minus the mean log-probability per token under GPT-2 small at T = 1 (human text is the target, not the minimum). Diversity: distinct-2 over the 16 samples of a passage (greedy and beam give one text 16 times, so their figure is divided by 16). Hover a dot for its decoder; the table in the closed block below has every value.

  • Search loops. Greedy and beam search sit far below human perplexity (2.3 and 2.4 against 31.0 for the real continuation), and 53% of greedy's 4-grams are repeats.
  • Top-p 0.95 at T 1 lands closest to human text (31.6 against 31.0), as nucleus sampling did in the paper (13.13 against 12.38); pure sampling overshoots (47.2).
  • Raise the temperature and top-p falls apart (perplexity about 3,900 at T 1.5) while min-p 0.1 stays near human text up to T 2 (16.1 at T 1.5, 40.0 at T 2): the mechanism of section 2, measured on text. This is likelihood under the same model, not quality as judged by people.

Defaults reproduce the ordering of Holtzman et al.'s Table 1 independently (GPT-2 small and 4 passages here; GPT-2 Large and 5,000 WebText passages there): search far below human perplexity (greedy 2.26, beam 2.37 against 31.0; theirs 1.50 and 1.48 against 12.38), pure sampling above it (47.2; theirs 22.73) and top-p 0.95 closest (31.6; theirs 13.13). The absolute values do not compare across the two studies: a smaller model is more surprised by everything. Perplexity near human is a necessary condition for human-like text, not a measure of quality.
