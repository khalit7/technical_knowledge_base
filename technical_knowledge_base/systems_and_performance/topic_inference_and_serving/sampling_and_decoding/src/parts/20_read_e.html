4. Logit processors: penalties, bias and masks

Step 2 of the hero is one interface in every framework: a logit processor takes the tokens so far and the logits, and returns edited logits. In {{transformers|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/generation/logits_process.py}} it is LogitsProcessor.__call__(input_ids, scores), and the repetition penalty, no-repeat n-gram, minimum length (suppress the end token) and token bans are all processors applied before the warpers. OpenAI-style APIs and open servers expose the simplest one as logit bias: a value from −100 to 100 added to a token's logit before sampling, where −100 effectively bans it ({{OpenAI API reference|https://platform.openai.com/docs/api-reference/chat/create}}; vLLM's logit_bias and allowed_token_ids in {{sampling_params.py|https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/vllm/sampling_params.py}}). A grammar mask (section 5) is a processor that adds −∞.

The repetition controls are processors that use counts of what is already in the context:

  • Repetition penalty ({{CTRL, Keskar et al., 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05858}}, who found θ ≈ 1.2 a good balance with greedy sampling): every token already in the context has its logit divided by θ if positive and multiplied by θ if negative; transformers counts the prompt too.
  • Presence and frequency penalties (the OpenAI pair): zj → zj − cj·αfrequency − [cj > 0]·αpresence, where cj counts how often token j was already sampled; "around 0.1 to 1" to reduce repetition somewhat, up to 2 to suppress it strongly at a cost in quality ({{OpenAI docs|https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/advanced-usage}}). Qwen3's card suggests presence_penalty 0 to 2 against endless repetition, warning of language mixing at high values ({{card|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B}}).
  • No-repeat n-gram bans any token that would complete an n-gram already generated (transformers' no_repeat_ngram_size).

A repetition penalty cannot tell a loop from a correct repeat

Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct continuing the pattern "red, blue, red, blue, red," at position 7 of its reply ("red, blue, red,"): the right next token " blue" is already in the context three times (the prompt counts) and was generated once. Repetition penalty 1.3 divides its logit 25.72 by 1.3, so the runner-up " and" (17.59) gains, and " blue" falls from 99.9% to 73.6%; a frequency penalty of 1 subtracts 1 per generated occurrence and barely moves it (99.7%) measured here. Penalties are blunt instruments, needed less as models improved and still useful for small models.
