The idea in one screen

A model only scores every token in its vocabulary; a separate rule, the decoder, edits those scores, reshapes them, cuts the unlikely tail and picks one token, and which tokens survive that rule decides whether the same model writes loops, sensible prose, noise or valid JSON.

Fig. 1 is one real next-token distribution through the decoder, 151,936 scores in and one token out: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct writing the opening of a lighthouse story, second token (after "The") measured here (src/reading_numbers.mjs).

  1. Logits. The model scores all 151,936 tokens. The 12 highest stand on the back plinth: " l" (the start of "lighthouse") leads with 14.11; the other 151,924 score from 11.26 down to about −19.7. Only differences between logits matter.
  2. Processors. Penalties, logit bias and a grammar mask would edit the logits here (sections 4 and 5). This request uses none, so the scores pass through unchanged.
  3. Temperature, then softmax. Dividing the logits by T 1.5 shrinks every gap: the top token " l" falls from 22.0% at T 1 to 5.4%, and the tall block on the right, the other 151,924 tokens together, grows from 37.3% to 77.3% of the mass outside the top 12.
  4. Truncate: min-p 0.1. The cut is 0.1 × 5.4% = 0.54%. Min-p 0.1 keeps 22 of 151,936 tokens, the 12 shown and 10 more; the rest of the tail is gone. Top-p 0.9 on the same distribution would keep 6,102 (section 2).
  5. Renormalise. The 22 survivors held 29.2% of the mass; rescaled to sum to 1, the top token rises to 18.6% and the 10 survivors from the tail hold 22.4%.
  6. Choose. Sampling draws one token from the result (this seeded draw picks " l"). Greedy skips steps 3 to 5 and takes the argmax; beam search keeps the B best whole sequences instead of one token (section 1).

You need: logits and softmax (assumed), and what a token is ({{Tokenizers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817187bcd94a1b0f15ea}}), which matters for constrained decoding. Where the sampler runs inside a serving engine's step is on {{vLLM: inside the default engine|n:3c65c17b0d0d8113b976d8bcaf2c0722}}.

Decoder
the rule that turns one next-token distribution into the next token.
Logit processor
a function that edits the logits before the choice: penalties, logit bias, a grammar mask.
Temperature T
divides the logits; below 1 sharpens, above 1 flattens.
Truncation
dropping the tail before sampling: top-k (a fixed count), top-p or nucleus (a probability mass), min-p (a share of the top token).
Beam search
keeps the B most probable partial sequences instead of one.
Constrained decoding
masking every token that would break a grammar (a JSON schema, a regular expression) at every step.
Perplexity
exp of minus the mean log-probability per token: how surprised a model is by a text.

Numbers are published, with the source linked, unless marked derived (formula shown), measured here (computed offline on a small open model, scripts in the page source) or unconfirmed. The formula behind steps 1 to 3: one forward pass ends in a vector of logits z, one per vocabulary entry (151,936 for {{Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct|https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct}}, 50,257 for {{GPT-2|https://huggingface.co/openai-community/gpt2}}), and the softmax with temperature turns them into probabilities:

pi = exp(zi / T) / Σj exp(zj / T)
zi
logit of token i (only differences between logits matter)
T
T = 1 is the model's own distribution; T → 0 puts all the mass on the top token (greedy)

Decoders fall into two families: search is deterministic and looks for a high-probability sequence (greedy, beam); sampling draws at random from a reshaped distribution. Constraints sit before either, as a logit processor.
