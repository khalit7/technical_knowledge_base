0. The map: one training step's life

The core idea
PyTorch runs your Python one operator call at a time, and every call walks down a table of kernels chosen by keys its tensors carry (autocast, autograd, device), with autograd recording on the way down the graph that backward walks back up; torch.compile replaces that walk with generated kernels, and torch.distributed runs the same program in several processes that must call the same collectives in the same order.

Fig. 1 follows one training step of the running example (below) from your loop down to the bytes, one station per layer; each layer has a child page, and each step links the child's own figure. This page is the map, the comparisons across the layers, and the one running example they all share.

  1. One step, seven layers. Your loop (or a library running it) calls the model; torch.compile may stand in front of it; every operator call walks the dispatcher's keys; autograd records a graph beside them; a kernel does the arithmetic on a tensor's bytes; the same step runs on every rank; the profiler measures it along the front. Drawn: one station per layer, left to right along the path a call takes; cubes, cells and nodes stand for the counts in the steps, not one for one illustrative layout.
  2. Your loop, or a library's. model(x, y), loss.backward(), opt.step(): the step every lever edits, on 3,186,944 parameters (12.75 MB of float32 weights). Libraries that run the loop for you (Hugging Face Trainer and Accelerate, Lightning, torchtitan, DeepSpeed) make the same calls below, plus defaults that change the run. Owners: {{Making training fast|n:3c65c17b0d0d810d9e1fdf9ec942c20d}}, section 7 (the loop); {{Libraries on top of PyTorch|n:3c65c17b0d0d81019d98f99978dc26f6}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81019d98f99978dc26f6#stg-hero}} gives eight duties of the loop to six libraries and {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81019d98f99978dc26f6#stg-step}} records one step call by call. Here: sections 4 and 9, the {{Ecosystem map|#t-map}}.
  3. Every call walks the dispatcher's keys. Autocast casts, Autograd records a node, Python hands the call to a subclass or mode, the backend key runs the kernel; keys with nothing to do are skipped. One step makes 923 calls: 142 in forward, 295 in backward, 486 in the default optimizer measured. Every extension plugs into one of these slots. Owner: {{Tensors, the dispatcher and autograd|n:3f85c17b0d0d81138595c1c6cd52f795}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3f85c17b0d0d81138595c1c6cd52f795#stg-hero}} takes one torch.mm through three machines and {{Fig. 3|n:3f85c17b0d0d81138595c1c6cd52f795#stg-walk}} walks one call down the keys in seven situations. Lab: {{Inside one step|#t-step}}. Section 2.
  4. Autograd records the graph on the way down. One node per differentiable call, keeping saved tensors: 187 nodes and 34.76 MB after one forward, against 12.75 MB of weights; backward walks the graph back from the loss and frees each node's saved tensors as it goes. Owner: Tensors, the dispatcher and autograd, whose {{Fig. 4|n:3f85c17b0d0d81138595c1c6cd52f795#stg-graph}} builds the graph of one block. Section 3.
  5. A kernel runs on a tensor's bytes. A tensor is a header (shape, strides, offset) over a flat storage: x of shape (3, 4) has strides (4, 1), and x.t() reads the same 12 cells with strides (1, 4), so a transpose or a slice copies nothing. Owner: Tensors, the dispatcher and autograd, whose {{Fig. 2|n:3f85c17b0d0d81138595c1c6cd52f795#stg-ten}} lays one storage under many headers. Section 1.
  6. torch.compile replaces the walk. Dynamo captures the forward behind guards (1 graph of 59 operations, 549 guards), AOTAutograd splits forward and backward, Inductor fuses: 142 calls become 35 launches per forward measured. Owner: {{torch.compile from the user's side|n:3c65c17b0d0d8123a232d75bd7339ecb}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d8123a232d75bd7339ecb#stg-call}} follows one call into a compiled function, {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d8123a232d75bd7339ecb#stg-brk}} a graph break frame by frame and {{Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d8123a232d75bd7339ecb#stg-cut}} the cut between forward and backward. Lab: {{Compile lab|#t-comp}}. Section 5.
  7. Your own code plugs into a slot. A torch.library.custom_op with a fake implementation is one opaque node to the compiler: a function calling tolist() gave 3 graphs and 2 breaks as plain Python, 1 graph and 0 breaks as a custom operator, with the same result measured. Owner: {{Extending PyTorch|n:3f85c17b0d0d8158a7addcd3d601d72a}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3f85c17b0d0d8158a7addcd3d601d72a#stg-hero}} walks one call down the keys and its slots, {{Fig. 2|n:3f85c17b0d0d8158a7addcd3d601d72a#stg-dyn}} shows one forward as Dynamo recorded it and {{Fig. 3|n:3f85c17b0d0d8158a7addcd3d601d72a#stg-sub}} one weight as a tensor subclass. Section 8.
  8. The same step on every rank. DDP keeps a full copy per rank and all-reduces gradients (2 all-reduces a step); FSDP2 shards every parameter across the ranks and gathers a block only to use it (14 collectives a step); on the 25.3M-parameter variant the peak per rank falls from 548 MB to 249 MB on 4 ranks measured, CPU, gloo. Owner: {{Distributed PyTorch|n:3c65c17b0d0d815e85aff8decd8f35ab}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d815e85aff8decd8f35ab#stg-hero}} shows what each rank holds, {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d815e85aff8decd8f35ab#stg-ddp}} DDP's buckets, {{Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d815e85aff8decd8f35ab#stg-fsdp}} one FSDP2 step and {{Fig. 4|n:3c65c17b0d0d815e85aff8decd8f35ab#stg-dt}} placements. Lab: {{Distributed lab|#t-dist}}. Section 7.
  9. The profiler finds what is slow. On the CPU the step splits into forward 6.57, backward 10.54 and optimizer 5.41 ms of 22.51; the default for-loop AdamW is a quarter of it, and fused=True takes the optimizer from 5.64 to 2.08 ms measured on Apple M1 Pro (section 6). Owner: {{Making training fast|n:3c65c17b0d0d810d9e1fdf9ec942c20d}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d810d9e1fdf9ec942c20d#stg-hero}} splits a step's time and memory, {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d810d9e1fdf9ec942c20d#stg-hunt}} fixes one bottleneck at a time, {{Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d810d9e1fdf9ec942c20d#stg-trace}} reads one MPS step's profiler trace and {{Fig. 4|n:3c65c17b0d0d810d9e1fdf9ec942c20d#stg-mem}} follows memory through a step.

The running example. A character-level GPT in plain PyTorch: 43 distinct characters as its vocabulary, 4 transformer blocks of width 256 with 4 attention heads, a context of 64 characters, 3,186,944 parameters, trained with AdamW on batches of 8 windows cut from the first three paragraphs of Alice's Adventures in Wonderland. Its first loss is 3.8014, just above ln 43 = 3.761 (a uniform guess), and after 300 steps it reaches 0.546 measured on Apple M1 Pro. Every tab of this page and every child page uses this model, these batches and this seed, which is why their numbers can be compared. Machine: an Apple M1 Pro laptop, its CPU (4 threads) and its GPU through Apple's MPS backend, PyTorch 2.14.1 (released 2026-09-30, {{PyPI|https://pypi.org/project/torch/#history}}). There is no NVIDIA GPU here: each page says what carries over to one, and published GPU numbers are labelled with who measured them.

You need: nothing beyond Python and the idea of a gradient; this topic starts from zero. The parallelism forms themselves (data, tensor, pipeline parallelism, ZeRO) are taught on {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}; the GPU underneath on {{Topic: cuda-and-gpu-programming|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c39f34d5e070d783c1}}.

New terms
Operator: a named operation such as aten::addmm; ATen is PyTorch's C++ tensor library where every operator is defined.
Kernel: one compiled function that does the arithmetic of an operator on one device.
Dispatch key: a label (AutogradCPU, AutocastCPU, Python, CPU) that selects one kernel from an operator's table.
Storage, strides, view: the flat bytes; how many elements to step per dimension; a new tensor over the same bytes.
Saved tensors: what autograd nodes keep from forward for backward; they, not the weights, fill memory in training.
Guard, graph break: a condition under which compiled code stays valid; a spot Dynamo cannot capture, run eagerly between graphs.
Rank, collective: one process of a job; an operation every rank calls together (all-reduce, all-gather, reduce-scatter).
Placement: how a DTensor is split over ranks: Shard(d), Replicate(), Partial().
