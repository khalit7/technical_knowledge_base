1. Tensors: a header over bytes

A tensor is two things: a storage, one flat block of bytes on one device, and a small header saying how to read it: a shape, one stride per dimension (how many elements to step in storage to move one along that dimension), an offset, a dtype (float32 at 4 bytes, bfloat16 at 2) and a device (cpu, mps, cuda).

address of x[i, j] = offset + i × stride0 + j × stride1

So a transpose, a slice or a reshape that keeps the element order writes a new header over the same storage, a view, and copies nothing. A tensor is contiguous when its elements, read in order, sit one after another in storage; kernels that need that copy first. Whether a copy happens depends on the device: in the running example, attention's .transpose(1, 2).contiguous() moves 0 bytes on the CPU, whose attention kernel already writes in the needed order, and 524,288 bytes per layer on MPS measured on Apple M1 Pro ({{Inside one step|#t-step}}, section 1).

The bytes every other section counts. The model's 3,186,944 parameters in float32 take 12,747,776 bytes (12.75 MB) 3,186,944 × 4; AdamW keeps two more tensors of the same size (25.5 MB) and the gradients another 12.75 MB. Moving data between devices is explicit (.to("mps")), and on a GPU a stray .item() or .cpu() makes the CPU wait for the GPU (section 6).
