7. DTensor and DeviceMesh: placements, and what changing them costs

FSDP2, tensor parallel and distributed checkpoints are all built on one abstraction. A DeviceMesh arranges the ranks in a grid: 4 ranks as a line of 4, or as 2 × 2 (say, data parallel along one axis and tensor parallel along the other). A DTensor is one logical tensor spread over a mesh, described by one placement per mesh dimension:

  • Shard(d): split along tensor dimension d; each rank holds one piece (like torch.chunk, so 6 rows over 4 ranks gives rows: the last rank can be empty).
  • Replicate(): every rank holds the whole tensor.
  • Partial(): every rank holds a full-shape tensor, and the real value is their sum (a pending reduction). This is what a row-split matrix multiply produces before its all-reduce.

Every operation on DTensors either works locally or needs a redistribute, which is a collective. Knowing which placement change needs which collective is most of what tensor parallelism is. Pick a mesh and two placements: the 4 × 4 tensor arange(16), which rank holds which elements before and after, and the collectives the real run issued.

Every combination was run (16 pairs on each 1-D mesh, 256 on the 2 × 2 mesh; CPU, gloo, torch 2.14.1): the collectives are the recorded ones, both as DTensor asked for them (CommDebugMode) and as gloo ran them (flight recorder). The local values are computed in the page from the placements and were checked against the recorded local tensors of every rank in every pair. A Partial source is built with integer pieces that sum to the tensor (element k on mesh coordinate k mod n).

Summary of the 1-D mesh of 4. Read across: Shard to Replicate is an all-gather, Partial to Replicate an all-reduce, Partial to Shard a reduce-scatter, Replicate to Shard is free (each rank keeps its piece), and Shard to Partial is free too (fill the rest with zeros). Two things the recording added: at 2.14.1 Shard(0) to Shard(1) is an all-gather followed by a local slice, not an all-to-all; and Replicate to Partial raises "only Shard to Partial(sum) redistribution is supported".

8. Tensor parallel: split the matrices, not the batch

Tensor parallelism (TP) splits each weight matrix across ranks that all see the same batch. The Megatron-LM recipe for an MLP, which PyTorch exposes as ColwiseParallel and RowwiseParallel in torch.distributed.tensor.parallel: split the first Linear by output columns (each rank computes half of the 1,024 hidden units, and GELU works on its half alone), split the second by input rows (each rank multiplies its half and gets a Partial sum), then one all-reduce adds the halves. Backward mirrors it: one all-reduce of the input gradient. The maths and its cost model are on {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}} and the {{Megatron-LM paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d8133ae92fbe90a1f308e}}.

The same plan on the fused attention projection fails
The model computes q, k and v with one Linear of 768 outputs laid out as [all of q | all of k | all of v]. ColwiseParallel on it gives rank 0 rows (all of q and half of k) and rank 1 the rest, and the forward fails at once: ValueError: not enough values to unpack (expected 3, got 2), because a 384-wide local output cannot be split into three 256-wide pieces. Column-splitting needs whole heads on each rank. torchtitan's fused projection is laid out head-group by head-group for exactly this reason ("Compatible with ColwiseParallel on the wqkv linear layer", torchtitan 0.3.0 attention.py L785), and its unfused version shards wq, wk, wv separately. The parallel plan is a property of the model's layout, not only of the module types.

Two dimensions at once. On 4 ranks as a 2 × 2 mesh, the MLPs were tensor-parallel over one axis and the whole model was fully_shard-ed over the other, the layout torchtitan calls 2-D parallelism. The first MLP weight's placements came out as : sharded on dimension 0 by both axes, the FSDP part as a "strided" shard so that each rank's rows are the right ones. 9. Pipelining: split the layers, stream micro-batches

Pipeline parallelism puts consecutive layers on different ranks: here rank 0 runs the embeddings and blocks 0 and 1, rank 1 runs blocks 2 and 3 and the output layer. The batch of 8 is cut into 4 micro-batches of 2 so both ranks can work at once; activations flow forward by send/recv, their gradients flow back the same way. GPipe runs all forwards, then all backwards: simple, but stage 0 holds the activations of all 4 micro-batches at once. 1F1B starts each backward as soon as it can and then alternates one forward with one backward, so stage 0 never holds more than 2. Same gradients, same communication (4 activations of 131,072 bytes sent forward and 4 gradients of 131,072 bytes sent back per step), lower peak. The idle gaps at the start and end are the pipeline bubble; the schedules that shrink it (interleaved 1F1B, zero-bubble, DualPipe) are in torch.distributed.pipelining too and their maths is on {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}.
