5. AOTAutograd: the backward graph, and what crosses to it

The problem. Dynamo captured only the forward. Training also runs a backward, and in eager mode that backward is decided op by op as the forward runs: each operation saves what its own derivative needs. A compiler wants the backward as a graph too, ahead of time, and it wants to choose what is saved rather than accept every op's default.

How it works. AOTAutograd runs the captured forward once more, on fake tensors, through the real autograd machinery, and records forward and backward together as one joint graph whose inputs are the forward inputs (primals_*) and the incoming gradients (tangents_*). Two rewrites happen on the way: functionalization replaces in-place and aliasing operations by pure ones (the block's joint graph has no in-place op, measured; the mechanism is section 2.7 of {{Tensors, the dispatcher and autograd|n:3f85c17b0d0d81138595c1c6cd52f795}}), and decomposition lowers composite ops to a smaller ATen set (F.linear becomes permute + addmm, GELU becomes erf, mul, add). Then a partitioner cuts the joint graph in two. Every value that crosses the cut is saved by the forward for the backward; everything else the backward needs, it recomputes.

default_partitionSave whatever the backward needs that the forward produced; recompute nothing. Close to eager. (partitioners.py L1595)
min_cut_rematerialization_partitionInductor's default. A max-flow min-cut over the joint graph where cutting an edge means saving that tensor (cost: its bytes) and cheap ops may be recomputed instead; matmuls, reductions and long chains are banned from recompute (ban_recompute_*, functorch config.py L159-L174). (partitioners.py L4109)
activation_memory_budgetBelow 1.0 (the default, L202) the partitioner may recompute expensive ops too, choosing which by a knapsack (solver "dp", L215) over runtime estimated from FLOPs (L209), until the saved bytes fit the fraction asked for.

One block of the running example (training, CPU, input (8, 64, 256)), cut every way, on the real Inductor path (its joint-graph passes, then the partitioner, wrapped to record what it received and returned):

One block: bytes saved for backward against ops the backward runs

Read it from the top. default_partition keeps 8,404,992 bytes, which is eager's number to within 4 bytes (8,404,996 live after one eager block forward, measured on the sibling page), a good check that the accounting is right. Min-cut at budget 1.0 keeps 7,880,704 bytes: it noticed that the 524,288-byte .contiguous() copy of the attention output (the input of proj) can be rebuilt in backward from the attention output it saves anyway, and that saving the parameters themselves is free where default saved transposed views of them. So 6% less memory and 10 more backward ops. Lower budgets buy memory with recomputation: at 0.0 nothing but inputs is saved, and the backward reruns the matrix multiplies and the attention, 134 ops instead of 88. The {{Partitioner explorer|#t-tcp-part}} tab draws the block's 144-op joint graph and colours each node by what each cut does with it.

The whole model behaves the same way: 36,862,468 bytes saved under default_partition (plus 12.63 MB of transposed parameter views, which cost nothing), 34,814,468 at budget 1.0 (the same figure the Compile lab measured), 17,301,504 at 0.5, 6,903,808 at 0.2 and 0 at 0.0, with 427, 578, 593 and 635 backward ops. On a larger configuration {{Making training fast|n:3c65c17b0d0d810d9e1fdf9ec942c20d}} (section 8) measured the same knob at 546.8, 273.0 and 0 MB for budgets 1.0, 0.5 and 0.0.

Predict: the running example's training step on the CPU with activation_memory_budget = 0.0 (save nothing, recompute everything) against the default budget: how much slower?

When it matters. When activations, not parameters, set the batch size you can fit: long sequences, many layers. It is the compiler's version of activation checkpointing (and composes with it: a checkpointed region is recomputed whatever the partitioner thinks). On this model the default min-cut barely moves memory; the budget is the lever. How to check: TORCH_LOGS=aot_graphs prints the forward graph (its return statement lists what is saved) and the backward graph; TORCH_LOGS=aot_joint_graph the joint graph.
