Correctness: measured on Apple M1 Pro, PyTorch 2.14.1, B = 2, 4 heads, T = 256, head size 64, float32; largest absolute difference from attention computed in float64 on the CPU with the same mask (src/exp/flex_lab.py). On the CPU, compiled FlexAttention is unavailable on macOS and FlexAttention ran eagerly; on MPS it ran compiled. Backward was attempted on both and raised.

What this lab does not show

GPU speed. The kernels that make FlexAttention fast on NVIDIA GPUs are Triton kernels generated by Inductor (and, since 2.11, an optional FlashAttention-4 backend, per the {{2.11 release notes|https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/releases/tag/v2.11.0}}); none of them run here. The authors' figure, 90% of FlashAttention-2's forward speed and 85% of its backward vendor, 2024, is from the {{FlexAttention blog|https://pytorch.org/blog/flexattention/}}. Training with FlexAttention needs a CUDA (or XPU or HPU) device at 2.14.1.
