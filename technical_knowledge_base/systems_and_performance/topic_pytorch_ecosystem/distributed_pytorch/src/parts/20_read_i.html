Common mistakes

Calling init_process_group() without a backend on a machine with an accelerator
c10d picks the backend for the detected accelerator only. On this Mac that was "mps:gloo", and every CPU collective then failed (section 1). Name the backend ("gloo", "nccl") or a device map such as "cpu:gloo,cuda:nccl".
Expecting an elastic restart to keep the job's state
torchrun restarts every worker from the top of the script. Without a checkpoint written by the script, a restart is a fresh run. With one, the losses after a restart matched an uninterrupted run exactly (section 1).
Leaving find_unused_parameters=True on "to be safe"
It costs a graph traversal every step and a bitmap all-reduce, and it removes the single-bucket first step. If the set of used parameters never changes, static_graph=True is cheaper; if a branch really is conditional, keep it on and accept the cost (section 3).
Splitting q, k and v with three ColwiseParallel styles and nothing else
Each wraps the shared input separately and sends its own gradient all-reduce: 24 all-reduces per step here instead of 16. Wrap the attention input once with PrepareModuleInput (section 6).
Running dcp.async_save on the default process group
The save's collectives run on a background thread while training issues its own on the same group: here gloo aborted. Give checkpoints their own group, dist.new_group(backend="gloo"), as torchtitan does (section 9).
A V pipeline schedule with tied embeddings
The tied weight sits in two stages on one rank and is divided by the number of micro-batches twice; the gradient was 0.37 of the right one with no error on the 8-block variant (section 8).
Issuing collectives from callbacks or threads
Order on every rank must be identical. PowerSGD's callback-issued all-reduces reached the ranks in different orders on 4 gloo processes and the job crashed (section 3). If a hook must chain collectives, keep their order fixed regardless of timing.
Declaring uneven local pieces with from_local and no shape
DTensor assumes even pieces and computes a wrong global shape; with a shape given, pieces must still follow torch.chunk. Wrong sizes surfaced as a gloo abort at full_tensor() (section 5).
Inspecting FSDP2 parameters after an evaluation forward
The root unit stays unsharded after forward, so its parameters are full plain tensors until backward or reshard() (section 4). Call model.reshard() after evaluation if code downstream expects shards.
Expecting gradient accumulation to remove FSDP2's all-gathers
set_requires_gradient_sync(False) removes reductions only (20 to 5 here); each micro-batch still gathers weights (36 all-gathers either way) unless you also keep them gathered after backward (section 4).

Interview questions, with short answers

What happens between torchrun --nproc_per_node 8 train.py and the first all-reduce?
torchrun starts 8 processes with RANK, LOCAL_RANK, WORLD_SIZE, MASTER_ADDR and MASTER_PORT set. init_process_group connects each to a TCPStore at the master address, every rank publishes its address under a key and reads the others' (rendezvous), and the backend (NCCL or gloo) builds its connections. Subgroups and DeviceMesh dimensions repeat this for their members.
find_unused_parameters against static_graph?
Both let DDP finish buckets whose parameters got no gradient. The first searches the autograd graph every step and all-reduces a used-parameter bitmap; the second learns the set once and assumes it never changes, then rebuilds buckets after step 1. Measured: 3 collectives per step with the first; 2, 1, then 4 in the first three steps with the second.
Gradient accumulation under DDP and under FSDP2?
DDP: no_sync() for all but the last micro-batch, one set of bucket all-reduces per optimizer step (4 collectives against 10 here). FSDP2: set_requires_gradient_sync(False) skips the reduce-scatters but every micro-batch still all-gathers weights, and full-size gradients are held between micro-batches.
FSDP against HSDP: when do you pick HSDP?
When the model's state fits in one node's worth of shards: shard inside a node (fast links carry the per-layer all-gathers), replicate across nodes (one gradient all-reduce per unit crosses the network). It trades memory (2 times the shard here) for less inter-node traffic.
What does Partial mean, and why does relu on it cost an all-reduce?
Each rank holds a full-shape tensor and the true value is their sum, as after a row-split matrix multiply. Linear operations can act on the pieces; relu of a sum is not the sum of relus, so DTensor reduces first. That is why Megatron puts the nonlinearity between a column split and a row split.
Tensor parallel against sequence parallel: what changes on the wire?
Each all-reduce after a row-split layer becomes a reduce-scatter, and an all-gather appears before the next column-split layer. Bytes in ring terms are about the same (an all-reduce is the two halves); LayerNorm, dropout and residual activations are split along the sequence, saving their memory and work.
Explain ring attention in two sentences.
Each rank keeps its own queries and passes blocks of keys and values around a ring, computing attention against each block and merging partial results with their log-sum-exp. After n − 1 passes every query has seen every key, and no rank ever held the whole sequence's attention.
Why do zero-bubble schedules have smaller bubbles than 1F1B?
They split backward into the input gradient, which the previous stage waits for, and the weight gradient, which only the optimizer needs, and schedule the weight gradients into the idle slots. Measured here at 4 micro-batches on 2 ranks: ZBVZeroBubble idled 0.08 and 0.07 of the step against 1F1B's 0.19 and 0.10.
In what order does torchtitan apply its parallelisms, and why is TP the innermost mesh dimension?
Pipeline split first, then per stage context parallel, TP, activation checkpointing, per-block compile, and FSDP2 last. TP is innermost because consecutive ranks share a server, and TP talks in every layer; pipelining, which talks least, spans the slowest links.
A 512-GPU job hangs. You have flight-recorder dumps. What do you look for?
The first collective sequence number at which ranks disagree on the operation or its sizes, or at which a rank is missing; then that rank's stack (in the dump, or with py-spy) shows the code that issued something else. torchfrtrace automates the comparison.
