2. Collectives: what each one leaves on every rank, and what it costs

Every collective c10d offers, called once with 1 MiB (262,144 float32 values) per rank on 4 ranks, as PyTorch's flight recorder (a per-process log of every collective: name, sizes, dtypes, sequence number) saw it. The five verbs on toy values are in the parent lab's {{collectives primer|n:3c65c17b0d0d8150aa65ca44b6ed2fd5}} (Distributed lab tab, section 2).

Measured, 4 CPU processes, gloo. "Bytes a ring sends per rank" is the textbook cost for S bytes on n ranks; backends pick their own algorithms ({{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}} owns them).

Two things the recording adds. reduce_scatter_tensor appears as an all_reduce of the whole tensor, because gloo has no reduce-scatter (it all-reduces and keeps a chunk; ProcessGroupGloo.cpp L1557): every gradient reduction on this page reads as an all-reduce. And all_to_all, the one the lab does not draw, sends rank r's j-th piece to rank j (recorded on 4 ranks, rank 1 sent ; every rank's piece number 1 landed on rank 1): it is what expert parallelism and Ulysses-style sequence parallelism are built on.

Cost per call: a fixed latency, then bytes over bandwidth

A collective's time is roughly α + S/B: a fixed latency α per call plus the bytes over an effective bandwidth. Small messages are all latency and big ones all bandwidth, which is why DDP groups gradients into buckets of megabytes and FSDP gathers a whole block at once. Predict first, then read the chart.

Predict: at 64 MiB on 2 processes, which is fastest: all-reduce, all-gather, or broadcast?
Broadcast, 10.7 ms against 25.7 ms for all-reduce and 26.9 ms for all-gather. A broadcast moves the data one way; an all-reduce must both combine and redistribute (in ring terms 2(n − 1)/n of S per rank, against (n − 1)/n for each half), and gloo's CPU reduction adds arithmetic. That factor of about two between an all-reduce and either half is the same one that makes FSDP's traffic about 1.5 times DDP's.

Up to about 256 KiB every collective costs only its latency; past a few hundred KiB, time grows with the bytes

Measured on Apple M1 Pro, CPU, gloo over loopback, one thread per process, under the shared bench lock: median of 15 timed calls after 3 warm-ups, time of the slowest rank, barrier before each call. Load average at the end . The fit is least squares on the medians; where its two terms are equal (α × B) lies between about 160 KiB and 1.3 MiB for these collectives (derived from the fit). On 4 processes the largest size stops at 16 MiB: a 64 MiB all_gather_into_tensor on 4 gloo ranks aborted every process (SIGABRT, no message).

Depth: reading these against a GPU cluster (1 min)

The shape transfers: a latency floor for small messages, then time growing with bytes, and all-reduce costing about two of its halves. The numbers do not. Loopback gloo here moves a 64 MiB all-reduce in 25.7 ms, about 2.6 GB/s; NVIDIA quotes 900 GB/s of NVLink bandwidth per H100 SXM GPU (NVIDIA, vendor). NCCL's benchmarks report a "bus bandwidth" that multiplies the measured rate by 2(n − 1)/n for all-reduce so that numbers on different n compare (nccl-tests PERFORMANCE.md); measuring that on real hardware belongs to {{Interconnects and scaling|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}}.
