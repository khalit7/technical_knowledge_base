7. Context parallelism: the API, run on a CPU

At long context the activations of attention grow with the sequence and can outgrow one GPU on their own, so context parallelism (CP) splits the sequence inside attention too: each rank keeps the queries of its own tokens and the keys and values travel, by ring passes or one all-gather, with partial outputs merged by their log-sum-exp (the form, its three traffic patterns and the causal load balancing: {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}, section 8; the online softmax it reuses: {{FlashAttention paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e4a3e8e7a2c4771381}}). PyTorch's API is a context manager that shards the named buffers along their sequence dimension and swaps the attention kernel for a ring version (with a causal mask it also reorders the tokens so that rank 0 gets the first and last chunk, balancing the work): with context_parallel(mesh, buffers=[q, k, v], buffer_seq_dims=[2, 2, 2]): F.scaled_dot_product_attention(...). Run here on the real q, k and v of the running example's block 0 (8 × 4 heads × 64 tokens × 64), split over 2 and 4 processes:

  • The public API fails on a CPU: Its handlers cover the CUDA flash, memory-efficient and cuDNN attention kernels only (_context_parallel/_attention.py L907-955); on a CPU, SDPA dispatches to a different kernel.
  • The ring itself works. PyTorch's ring template (_templated_ring_attention) called with the CPU flash kernel, load balancing off, matched full attention on every rank's tokens to . With the default rotation the "ring" is one all-gather of each rank's K and V (); with set_rotate_method("alltoall") it is n − 1 exchanges ().
  • A model must be written for CP. The running example numbers its positions arange(T) of the local length, so each rank would count its tokens from 0; torchtitan passes positions in. torchtitan applies CP to every attention module before TP and FSDP (models/llama3/parallelize.py L46-51, 0.3.0) and folds the cp axis into the FSDP mesh, because ranks that split one sequence still need their weight gradients summed (distributed/parallel_dims.py, docstring of build_mesh). Not run on the whole model here.

8. Pipelining: stages and schedules

torch.distributed.pipelining gives each rank a contiguous run of layers (a stage, PipelineStage(module, stage_index, num_stages, device)), cuts the batch into micro-batches and streams them through: forward activations go to the next stage by send/recv, their gradients come back. A schedule object (Schedule1F1B(stage, n_microbatches, loss_fn), then schedule.step(x, target=y)) decides each rank's order of forward (F) and backward (B) work. The idle fill and drain, the bubble, is (p − 1)/(m + p − 1) of the step for p equal stages and m micro-batches (derived on {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}, section 6). Every F, B, I and W action was timed by wrapping the stage's methods; the median of 5 timed steps is drawn. The ideal clock replays each rank's recorded order with F = 1, B = 2, I = 1, W = 1 units and no communication, respecting what each action waits for: the schedule's own bubble, apart from this machine's noise. Measured on Apple M1 Pro, CPU, gloo, under the bench lock.

Bubble = 1 − (time in compute actions) / (step time), per rank; formula column with p = 2 ranks. Every schedule's gradients matched one process (largest difference 1.3e-7 on the untied parameters).

What the schedules change. 1F1B runs a backward as soon as one is possible, so a stage holds at most p micro-batches of activations instead of m (memory, not bubble). Interleaved 1F1B gives each rank v stages spread along the model (rank 0 holds stages 0 and 2), which divides the fill and drain by v at the price of v times more send/recv. Zero-bubble schedules (Qi et al. 2023, arXiv 2401.10241) split each backward into the input gradient (I, needed by the previous stage now) and the weight gradient (W, needed only by the optimizer), and slot the Ws into what would have been idle time. The V layouts (ZBVZeroBubble, DualPipeV from DeepSeek-V3) give rank 0 the first and the last stage, so the pipeline turns round on the same rank. Measured: ZBVZeroBubble idled 0.08 and 0.07 of the step at m = 4 against 1F1B's 0.19 and 0.10.

A V schedule silently scales a tied weight twice
In the V layouts the token embedding (stage 0) and the output layer (stage 3) share one rank, and here one Parameter. But every stage divides its parameters' gradients by the number of micro-batches at the end of its backward (scale_grads, stage.py L857-869), and a parameter in two stages is divided twice, once after part of its gradient has arrived. Recorded: the tied gradient came out 0.37 of the right one, with no error. Loop-style schedules put the two copies on different ranks, where a manual all-reduce of the two copies fixes them (largest difference 1.6e-7 over these runs; 1.5e-7 in the parent lab). Untie the weights, or pass scale_grads=False and divide once yourself.
