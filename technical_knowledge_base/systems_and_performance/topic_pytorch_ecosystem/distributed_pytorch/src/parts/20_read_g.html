7. Context parallelism: ring attention, run on a CPU

The problem. Sequence parallelism splits the sequence everywhere except attention, where every token needs every earlier token's keys and values. At long context lengths the activations of attention grow with the sequence and can outgrow one GPU on their own. Context parallelism (CP) splits the sequence inside attention too. Each rank keeps the queries of its own tokens; the keys and values travel. In ring attention (Liu et al. 2023, arXiv 2310.01889) each rank computes attention of its queries against the block of keys it holds, passes that block to the next rank, and after n − 1 passes has seen them all; the partial outputs are merged with their log-sum-exp, the same online-softmax trick FlashAttention uses inside one GPU ({{FlashAttention paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81e4a3e8e7a2c4771381}}). With a causal mask the early ranks have less work, so PyTorch reorders the tokens (rank 0 gets the first and last chunk) to balance it.

Run here, at the attention level: the real q, k and v of the running example's block 0 (8 × 4 heads × 64 tokens × 64), the sequence split over 2 and 4 processes.

  • The public API fails on a CPU. with context_parallel(mesh, buffers=[q, k, v], buffer_seq_dims=[2, 2, 2]): F.scaled_dot_product_attention(...) raised Its handlers cover the CUDA flash, memory-efficient and cuDNN attention kernels only (_context_parallel/_attention.py L907-955); on a CPU, SDPA dispatches to a different kernel.
  • The ring itself works. Calling PyTorch's ring template (_templated_ring_attention) with the CPU flash kernel, load balancing off, every rank's output matched full attention on its tokens to . With the default rotation the "ring" is one all-gather of each rank's K and V (); with set_rotate_method("alltoall") it is n − 1 exchanges ().
  • Not run on the whole model: the running example computes its positions as arange(T) of the local length, so each rank would number its tokens from 0; a model must be written for CP (positions passed in, as torchtitan's are). torchtitan applies CP to every attention module before TP and FSDP (models/llama3/parallelize.py L46-51, 0.3.0) and folds the cp axis into the FSDP mesh, because ranks that split one sequence still need their weight gradients summed (distributed/parallel_dims.py, docstring of build_mesh).

8. Pipelining: every schedule, measured

Pipeline parallelism (torch.distributed.pipelining) gives each rank a contiguous run of layers (a stage), cuts the batch into micro-batches and streams them through: forward activations go to the next stage by send/recv, their gradients come back. A schedule decides each rank's order of forward (F) and backward (B) work; the idle time while the pipeline fills and drains is the bubble, (p − 1)/(m + p − 1) of the step for p stages and m micro-batches when every stage takes the same time. The lab tab (section 9) animates GPipe against 1F1B; at 2.14.1 the library has seven schedules, and all seven ran here:

Measured on Apple M1 Pro, CPU, 2 processes, under the bench lock, median step of 5, on the 8-block, dmodel 512 variant of the running example (25.3M parameters) so that compute outweighs Python overhead. Bubble = 1 − (time in compute actions) / (step time), per rank; the formula column is (p − 1)/(m + p − 1) with p = 2 ranks. Multi-stage schedules hold 2 stages per rank. Every schedule's gradients matched one process (largest difference 1.3e-7 on the untied parameters).

What the schedules change. 1F1B runs a backward as soon as one is possible, so a stage holds at most p micro-batches of activations instead of m (memory, not bubble). Interleaved 1F1B gives each rank v stages spread along the model (rank 0 holds stages 0 and 2), which divides the fill and drain by v at the price of v times more send/recv. Zero-bubble schedules (Qi et al. 2023, arXiv 2401.10241) split each backward into the input gradient (I, needed by the previous stage now) and the weight gradient (W, needed only by the optimizer), and slot the Ws into what would have been idle time. The V layouts (ZBVZeroBubble, DualPipeV from DeepSeek-V3) give rank 0 the first and the last stage, so the pipeline turns round on the same rank. On this machine the zero-bubble schedules had the smallest measured bubbles (ZBVZeroBubble 0.08 and 0.07 at m = 4); the {{Pipeline schedules|#t-dpt-pipe}} tab draws every recorded timeline and replays its order on an ideal clock.

A V schedule silently scales a tied weight twice
In the V layouts the token embedding (stage 0) and the output layer (stage 3) share one rank, and here one Parameter: a tied weight needs no extra all-reduce. But every stage divides its parameters' gradients by the number of micro-batches at the end of its backward (scale_grads, stage.py L857-869), and a parameter in two stages is divided twice, once after part of its gradient has arrived. Recorded: the tied gradient came out times the right one, with no error. Loop-style schedules put the two copies on different ranks, where a manual all-reduce of the two copies fixes them (largest difference 1.6e-7 over these runs; 1.5e-7 in the lab tab). Untie the weights, or pass scale_grads=False and divide once yourself.
