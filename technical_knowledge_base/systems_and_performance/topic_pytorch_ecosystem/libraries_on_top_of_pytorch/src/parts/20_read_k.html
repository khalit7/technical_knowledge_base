Common mistakes

Porting a loop to the Trainer and comparing curves
The Trainer shuffles, clips at 1.0, uses lr 5e-5 with betas (0.9, 0.999) and decays linearly to zero. Any one of them moves the curve (the {{Defaults lab|#t-dflt}} measures each). Set them explicitly before comparing, or the comparison measures the defaults.
Upgrading DeepSpeed and assuming nothing changed
From 0.19.3 a config without gradient_clipping clips at 1.0; before, it did not. The same script's third loss went from 3.16247 to on the running example. Pin versions and write every setting you rely on into the config.
Dividing each micro-batch's mean loss by the accumulation steps
With unequal real-token counts per micro-batch this is a mean of means: 59% gradient error on this page's example. Sum token losses and divide by the token count of the whole accumulated (and data-parallel) batch, or let a transformers model do it through num_items_in_batch.
Writing your own nn.Module for the Trainer without **kwargs
The Trainer only passes num_items_in_batch to a forward that accepts **kwargs; otherwise it silently falls back to the mean of means under accumulation.
Loading a v5 checkpoint and assuming float32
from_pretrained now defaults to dtype="auto": a bf16 checkpoint gives bf16 parameters, and full fine-tuning then updates bf16 weights directly. Pass dtype=torch.float32 (and use autocast) if you want float32 master weights.
Hooking the optimizer to read gradients under Lightning
Lightning runs forward and backward inside optimizer.step(closure), so a step pre-hook fires before the gradients exist. Use on_before_optimizer_step.
Expecting FSDPStrategy to be FSDP2
In Lightning 2.6.6 FSDPStrategy is the FSDP1 wrapper; FSDP2 is ModelParallelStrategy, where you call fully_shard yourself in configure_model.
Treating torchtitan as a pip framework
It is a reference codebase whose defaults (bf16 compute through FSDP2 even on one rank, clipping at 1.0, weight decay on every parameter, FlexAttention with document masking, chunked loss) are choices for large GPU runs. Read them, fork, and change them in code.
Installing a config tool into an existing environment
Axolotl 0.20.0 pins transformers==5.17.0 and torch at most 2.14.0; Unsloth 2026.10.3 caps transformers at 5.17.0. Give each its own environment, or you change the stack under your other code.

Interview questions, with short answers

You move a working PyTorch loop to the HF Trainer and the loss curve changes. What do you check first?
Sampler (random by default), clipping (1.0), optimizer (AdamW 5e-5, betas 0.9/0.999, weight decay 0), schedule (linear to zero), precision (v5 loads in the checkpoint's dtype), and loss averaging under accumulation. With those matched the curves are identical; this page shows it to five decimals.
Why can gradient accumulation give a different gradient from a big batch, and how do libraries fix it?
Averaging per micro-batch weights tokens by 1/(K nj) instead of 1/N. Fix: sum the token losses and divide by the total real tokens of the step, counted across micro-batches and ranks before backward. The Trainer does it via num_items_in_batch for models with **kwargs; torchtitan with a global token count and FSDP2's divide factor set to 1.
Does gradient clipping matter with Adam?
Less than with SGD, but yes: Adam's first update ignores the gradient's scale, later ones mix gradients scaled by different factors. On the running example clipping at 1.0 was active on 145 of 150 steps and changed the 150-step probe loss by about 0.09.
DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 or FSDP2?
Same idea (shard parameters, gradients, optimizer state; gather per layer). FSDP2 is PyTorch-native, composes with DTensor, TP, PP, compile and DCP, and keeps your loop. DeepSpeed brings an engine, CPU and NVMe offload and its own kernels. With clipping off both reproduce DDP's losses here; ZeRO-3 also keeps tensors under 100,000 elements unsharded.
What is torchtitan, and when would you use it?
PyTorch's reference training codebase: FSDP2, TP, PP, CP and EP on one DeviceMesh, compile, float8, DCP, with short model and loop files meant to be forked. Use it for pretraining at scale with full control and PyTorch-native parallelism; not for a quick fine-tune of a Hub model.
What does Accelerate actually do?
It decides the distributed type from the environment, wraps the model (DDP, FSDP2 via fully_shard per block, DeepSpeed or Megatron), shards the dataloader, and scales the loss for accumulation in backward. Your loop stays yours.
How does Unsloth get its speed, and what is the risk?
It replaces transformers' forward methods with its own and calls hand-written Triton kernels (cross-entropy, RMSNorm, RoPE, SwiGLU, LoRA MLP). The model you train is not the code you read, it needs a GPU, and it caps the transformers versions it supports.
Which PyTorch projects are dead, paused or alive for LLM training in 2026?
torchtune: no longer maintained (July 2025). torchforge: paused, consolidating into torchtitan. torchtitan: active (0.3.0, September 2026). Monarch: active. On the NVIDIA side, the NeMo repository is speech-only and LLM training goes through Megatron Core.
A Lightning DDP job is slower per step than your plain DDP script on GPUs. One thing to look for?
Per-step synchronisations the framework adds: Lightning broadcasts a SIGTERM flag tensor and reads it with .item() every step in distributed runs, and logging of tensors can add more. A profiler trace shows the CPU waiting there.
