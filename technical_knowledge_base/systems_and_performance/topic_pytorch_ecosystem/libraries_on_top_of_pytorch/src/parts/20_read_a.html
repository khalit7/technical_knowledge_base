The idea in one screen

A training library adds no maths to PyTorch: it takes over duties of the loop you would write (data order, loss scaling, clipping, optimiser, precision, spreading over processes) and fills each with a default. Choosing a library is choosing which duties to hand over; moving between two is matching their defaults.

Fig. 1, step by step: the same eight duties in every tower

  1. The plain loop: you hold every duty. Each tower is one library, cut into the same eight slabs: who runs the loop, data order, forward and loss, loss scaling under accumulation, clipping, optimiser and schedule, precision, spreading over processes. In your own loop every slab is yours (white), and the dots are its calls into PyTorch (the plate underneath): 5 per step in the recorded trace.
  2. The HF Trainer takes all eight. Tinted slabs are duties the library takes; blue ones are a default that changes the loss if you port a plain loop without setting it: it shuffles (seed 42), clips at 1.0, uses its own AdamW at lr 5e-5 with a linear decay, and since v5 trains in the checkpoint's saved dtype (often bf16). It spreads over processes through Accelerate.
  3. Each layer is built on the one below. TRL's SFT, DPO and GRPO trainers subclass the Trainer and add their own defaults (bf16, lr 1e-6 for GRPO); Axolotl turns a YAML file into a Trainer or a TRL trainer and adds a cosine schedule. The dots run down through every layer: whatever sits on top, the calls reach PyTorch through the Trainer, which runs on Accelerate, which calls DDP or fully_shard.
  4. Accelerate keeps your loop. It takes two duties: spreading over processes (DDP, FSDP2 or DeepSpeed) and dividing the loss by the accumulation steps. Everything else stays yours, so its calls per step are the plain loop's 5.
  5. Lightning runs the loop from hooks. Its Trainer runs the loop, divides by the accumulation steps, leaves clipping off and fixes precision at 32-true; your training_step and configure_optimizers keep the forward and the optimiser. Fabric, its other half, gives the loop back.
  6. DeepSpeed hands you an engine. The loop is yours, but engine.backward and engine.step replace yours; it shards with ZeRO stages 0 to 3 and, since 0.19.3, clips at 1.0 when the config says nothing.
  7. torchtitan brings everything. Its own model, data, chunked loss and loop, loss divided by the global token count, bf16 compute through FSDP2 even on one rank, clipping at 1.0, and FSDP2, TP, PP, CP and EP on one mesh. It is meant to be forked and edited.
  8. Side by side: every column ends in the same PyTorch calls. Matched to the plain loop, the Trainer, Accelerate and Lightning give its losses exactly. What differs is who calls, and how much: calls into PyTorch per step were 5 (plain loop), 91 (Trainer), 5 (Accelerate), 110 (Lightning), 583 (DeepSpeed ZeRO-0, 2 processes) and 3,787 (torchtitan, its debug model) in the recorded traces. Not drawn: Megatron Core (runs the loop, NVIDIA only), Unsloth and LLaMA-Factory (config tools on the same HF layers as Axolotl) and torchtune (unmaintained).

Running example
The topic's 3,186,944-parameter character-level GPT (4 layers, d_model 256, batches of 8 windows of 64 characters), defined once in the root's src/model/; seeded, so every run starts from the same weights and sees the same batches.
Matched
A library run with every setting set equal to the plain loop's.
Probe loss
The mean loss on four fixed batches (10,000 to 10,003 of the same text), read every 10 steps: a yardstick that does not depend on data order.
Mean of means
Under gradient accumulation, each micro-batch's mean loss divided by the number of micro-batches and added up (section 3). Its rival is the token-weighted loss: summed token losses over the real-token count of the whole step.

Three results the rest of the page explains
  • Matched settings, identical losses. The HF Trainer, Accelerate and Lightning give the plain loop's 150-step curve to five decimals; DeepSpeed ZeRO 0 to 3 give DDP's losses on 2 processes measured on Apple M1 Pro, CPU.
  • Defaults move the curve. The Trainer's default clip at 1.0 was active on 145 of 150 steps; DeepSpeed turned its own clip on (1.0) in 0.19.3, July 2026, so the same script trains differently after an upgrade.
  • Loss averaging is a choice. On micro-batches of 124, 10, 90 and 13 real tokens the mean of means is 59% away from the full-batch gradient (Accelerate, Lightning, the Trainer with a model without **kwargs); the Trainer with a Hugging Face model and torchtitan divide by the token count and match.
