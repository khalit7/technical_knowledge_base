3. Loss averaging under gradient accumulation

The problem: a language-model batch rarely has the same number of real tokens in every row. Padding positions get the label -100 and are ignored by the loss, and the loss is a mean over the real tokens. Split the batch into micro-batches, take each micro-batch's mean, divide by the number of micro-batches and add: that is a mean of means, and it is not the mean over the whole batch. Tokens in a short micro-batch count for more.

How it works. The full-batch loss is sum of token losses / N, with N the real tokens in the whole batch. Its gradient gives each token the weight 1/N. The mean of means gives a token in micro-batch j the weight 1/(K · nj), with K micro-batches and nj tokens in micro-batch j. The fix is to sum each micro-batch's token losses and divide by N, which must be known before the first backward (count the labels of all micro-batches first, and across ranks with one all-reduce of a single integer).

Why the Trainer has two behaviours. The Trainer counts the real tokens of all the micro-batches of one step (num_items_in_batch, {{trainer.py L2281 to L2342|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.19.0/src/transformers/trainer.py#L2281-L2342}}) and passes the count to the model, but only if the model's forward accepts **kwargs ({{L519 to L529|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.19.0/src/transformers/trainer.py#L519-L529}}). A transformers model does, and its loss is then the sum divided by that count (checked: ). Your own nn.Module usually does not, and the Trainer falls back to dividing by the number of micro-batches ({{L2089 to L2094|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/v5.19.0/src/transformers/trainer.py#L2089-L2094}}). The token-count path arrived in October 2024 ({{PR 34191|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/pull/34191}}), and its extension across data-parallel ranks, average_tokens_across_devices (default True at 5.19.0), answers {{issue 34242|https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/issues/34242}} (2024-10-18), which pointed out that averaging per rank has the same flaw. torchtitan sums labels != -100 over every micro-batch, all-reduces the count over the data-parallel ranks, and divides each micro-batch's summed loss by it ({{trainer.py L884 to L904|https://github.com/pytorch/torchtitan/blob/v0.3.0/torchtitan/trainer.py#L884-L904}}); it then sets FSDP2's own gradient division to 1.0 ({{fsdp.py L27 to L41|https://github.com/pytorch/torchtitan/blob/v0.3.0/torchtitan/distributed/fsdp.py#L27-L41}}), because averaging again over ranks would divide twice.

Within a few hundredths: against at step 100 measured on Apple M1 Pro, CPU, and the gap changes sign along the way. On a toy run AdamW absorbs much of a per-step reweighting, so the curves look alike; the gradient is still a different estimator, biased towards short sequences. It matters most when sequence lengths vary a lot and correlate with content (short chat turns against long documents), with SGD-like optimizers, and whenever you compare a run with accumulation to one without.

How to check it in your own setup. Run one step as one batch and as K micro-batches on the same rows with clipping off; compare gradients with torch.allclose or the relative error above. Two lines of code, and the only way to know what your library and model combination does.
