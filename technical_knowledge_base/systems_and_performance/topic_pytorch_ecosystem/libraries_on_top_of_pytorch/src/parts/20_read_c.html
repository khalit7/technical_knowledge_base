2. Defaults that change results

Porting a loop into a library, or upgrading one, swaps your decision for the library's wherever you did not set it. Clipping is the clearest case.

Predict: the running example's gradient norm, over 150 steps of the plain loop. On how many steps would a clip at 1.0 change anything?
Almost all: the norm was above 1.0 on of 150 steps (median , first step ) measured on Apple M1 Pro, CPU. Clipping at 1.0 is not a safety net here; it rescales nearly every update. With Adam that mostly changes the early steps: Adam divides by a running scale of the gradient, so a constant rescaling cancels once the running averages catch up.
