7. Priority: one number per job

The main loop walks jobs in priority order, so the whole policy of who goes first is how that number is computed. With PriorityType=priority/multifactor it is a weighted sum of factors, each between 0 and 1, then truncated to an integer. A concrete case first: for this page's gallery run the weights were age 1,000, fairshare 10,000, job size 1,000, partition 1,000, QOS 2,000 and GPU TRES 500 (the cluster's normal configuration uses only fairshare and age; the variant is src/scripts/sl_slurm.conf). Fairshare. Bob's blocker was charging him 15 billing units per second (12 GPUs at 1.0 plus 12 CPUs at 0.25). At 05:38:07 teamb's usage was still below teama's (EffectvUsage 0.31 against 0.69 in sshare), so bob ranked 3 of 4 users (factor 0.75: 7,500 points); by 05:38:58 teamb had passed teama (0.60 against 0.40) and bob dropped to rank 1 (0.25: 2,500 points), while alice rose to 0.5 and carol to 0.75. Fair Tree compares teams first, so carol, who ran nothing, moves with her team. That swing of 5,000 points dwarfs every other factor here.
priority = Wage·age + Wfs·fairshare + Wsize·jobsize + Wpart·partition + Wqos·qos + Σ Wtres·tres − nice
age
time eligible (since AccrueTime) over PriorityMaxAge, capped at 1. A job held by a dependency or a begin time does not accrue (jobs 84 and 85 showed AccrueTime=Unknown).
fairshare
with Fair Tree (the default), the user's rank in the account tree divided by the number of users: heavy recent use, decayed with PriorityDecayHalfLife, means a low factor. The root's Scheduling lab has a Fair Tree calculator.
jobsize
by default favours large jobs: (nodes / cluster nodes + CPUs / cluster CPUs) / 2.
partition
the partition's PriorityJobFactor over the largest one.
qos
the QOS's priority over the largest QOS priority.
tres
each TRES the job asks for over the partition's total, with its own weight (PriorityWeightTRES=GRES/gpu=500).
nice
subtracted as is (--nice=1500); users may only lower their own priority.

Two things that surprise people. sprio shows factors as of the last recalculation, not now: at 05:38:58 the age factor of jobs eligible since 05:37:48 read 0.0867, which is 52 s over 600, so they were computed at about 05:38:40 (one PriorityCalcPeriod after the previous one). And the printed parts need not add up: job 82's parts were 86.67 + 5,000 + 500 + 1,000 + 333.33 = 6,920.0, printed 6,920, while sprio -l rounds each part (87, 333). Defaults: PriorityDecayHalfLife 7 days and PriorityCalcPeriod 5 minutes (); this cluster uses 10 minutes and 1 minute so a lab can watch them move.
