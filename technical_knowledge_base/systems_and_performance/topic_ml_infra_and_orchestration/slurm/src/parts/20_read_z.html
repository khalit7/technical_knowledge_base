15. Common mistakes, with the messages they produce

Asking for far more time than the job needs
Backfill plans with your limit (section 6): a 2-hour job asking for 24 hours fits no gap and never jumps the queue, and before a maintenance window it shows ReqNodeNotAvail, Reserved for maintenance while nodes sit free (section 10). Ask for the expected time plus a margin, and checkpoint.
Reading (Priority) or the "DOWN, DRAINED" message as a broken cluster
Priority means a job ahead of yours is waiting. Nodes required for job are DOWN, DRAINED or reserved for jobs in higher priority partitions appeared here with every node healthy: a higher-priority job in an overlapping partition was waiting (section 6). Check sinfo and squeue --start first.
A job that can never start, accepted without complaint
QOSMaxGRESPerUser, AssocGrpGRES and PartitionTimeLimit jobs were all accepted here and would wait forever (section 5). A limit reason on a job that alone exceeds the limit is permanent: resubmit smaller, or ask for DenyOnLimit and EnforcePartLimits so such jobs are refused at submission.
Expecting the submit-time error to explain itself
Requested node configuration is not available covers 5 GPUs on a 4-GPU node, 4 nodes on a 3-node partition, 8,000 MB on a 4,000 MB node and 5 CPUs per task on a 4-CPU node alike (section 5). Compare your request with scontrol show node and the partition's limits; an administrator can read the cause in the controller log (never runnable in partition batch).
Side-by-side srun steps that run one after another
Without --exact a step may take every CPU of the job on its node (section 4); the measured case took 12 s instead of 6. Whether it happens depends on whether the job was submitted with --cpus-per-task. Write --exact, and -N1 for one-task steps in a multi-node job (srun: warning: can't run 1 processes on 2 nodes, setting nnodes to 1).
Stranding GPUs with CPU or memory requests
A job that takes all CPUs or all memory of a node leaves its other GPUs unusable (section 3). Tie CPUs and memory to GPUs (--gpus-per-task, --cpus-per-gpu, --mem-per-gpu) or take whole nodes with --exclusive.
Relying on --signal timing to the second
The signal "may be sent up to 60 seconds earlier than specified"; here it came 23 s early (section 8). And a requeued job waits about two minutes before it can start again. Both belong in the checkpoint budget.
Leaving dependency chains to rot
A job whose afterok parent failed sits as DependencyNeverSatisfied forever (section 9). Use --kill-on-invalid-dep=yes, or afterany plus a check in the script.
Reading sprio as live, or sreport as complete
sprio shows the factors of the last recalculation (every PriorityCalcPeriod, 5 minutes by default); sreport reads hourly rollups and showed nothing for the last few minutes (sections 7 and 11). Use sacct for recent jobs.
Assuming a job is fenced in
Without task/cgroup and ConstrainDevices=yes, a process can open every GPU on the node and run on any core (as on this lab cluster). On a shared node that is one user's job slowing another's. Check scontrol show config | grep -i -E 'TaskPlugin|ProctrackType' and cgroup.conf.

16. Interview questions

Why does Slurm not need gang scheduling as an add-on, while Kubernetes does?
A Slurm job is one allocation: the controller gives all its nodes at once or none, and only then starts processes. Kubernetes' default scheduler places pods one at a time and knows nothing of the job they belong to, so partial allocations (and deadlocks between two half-placed jobs) are possible without Kueue, Volcano or a coscheduling plugin; the root's Scheduling lab reproduces the deadlock.
A job has shown (Priority) for hours on a cluster with idle GPUs. Walk through what you check.
squeue --start and sprio for the jobs ahead in the same partition (the main loop stops a partition at its first blocked job); whether the idle GPUs come with free CPUs and memory (scontrol show node, AllocTRES); its time limit against the gap before the next reservation, including maintenance reservations; overlapping partitions; limits on its QOS or account (a limit reason would name them); fairshare (sshare).
Explain multifactor priority and what dominates it in practice.
A weighted sum of factors in [0, 1] (age, fairshare, job size, partition, QOS, TRES) minus nice, truncated to an integer. The weights decide; most sites make fairshare dominant, so a team's recent usage decides its place, as in this page's run where one team's 12-GPU job moved its users from first to last in 51 s. Age stops starvation; QOS gives fast lanes such as debug.
What is the difference between Resources and Priority as reasons?
Resources: the job is the first blocked job of its partition and waits for resources to free. Priority: a higher-priority job ahead of it is waiting, so the main loop did not try it. Only backfill can start a Priority job early, if its time limit fits a gap.
How do you run a 30-day training run on a cluster whose maximum job time is 2 days?
As a chain: --requeue plus --signal=B:USR1@600 and a trap that checkpoints and requeues, or a chain of afterany jobs; each start resumes from the latest checkpoint. Budget the dead time per hop (here about two minutes before a requeued job may start, plus queue wait and startup), and checkpoint often enough that a crash loses little.
Preemption: REQUEUE, CANCEL or SUSPEND for training jobs?
SUSPEND keeps GPU memory occupied and is rarely practical for GPU jobs; CANCEL loses the run; REQUEUE with a GraceTime long enough to write a checkpoint (the victim gets SIGTERM at once and SIGKILL after GraceTime) is the usual choice, with the low-priority work in a preemptible QOS or partition.
What happens to running jobs if slurmctld dies? If slurmdbd dies? If a node's slurmd stops answering?
Controller: running jobs continue (nothing new starts, srun cannot create steps) and its saved state is recovered on restart or by a backup controller. Database: the controller caches records and keeps scheduling, limits cannot be changed. slurmd: after SlurmdTimeout (300 s by default) the node is DOWN and its jobs are requeued (if requeueable) or killed; the root's Failure lab measured it.
Design: the scheduling setup for a 1,000-GPU lab (125 nodes of 8 GPUs) shared by three teams, with research jobs, production training runs and evaluation.
One controller with a backup on a second host and slurmdbd on its own host; configless nodes; cgroups with device and memory confinement; GPUs per node as GRES with NVML autodetection; a health-check prolog and HealthCheckProgram that drain bad nodes. Partitions: one main partition of all nodes (whole-node jobs, --exclusive default for multi-node), a small interactive or debug partition, and topology modelled (blocks or switches) so big jobs stay in one network domain. Accounts per team with fairshare shares matching what each funds, Fair Tree, decay half-life of a week; QOS normal, high (production runs, limited GPUs per team, can preempt), debug (1 node, 1 hour, high priority) and scavenger (any idle GPUs, preemptible with REQUEUE and a GraceTime of a few minutes). Reservations for a production run that needs 512 GPUs for a week, and for maintenance. Backfill on, with bf_max_job_user so one user's thousand jobs cannot fill the backfill budget. Accounting in GPU-hours per team, reported weekly. Trade-offs to say: whole-node allocation wastes capacity on small jobs (hence the shared debug partition), preemption costs the scavenger jobs their work since the last checkpoint, and strict fairshare can make a team with one huge run wait behind many small jobs of others (hence a reservation or high QOS for planned runs).
What does scontrol reconfigure do in Slurm 24.11?
It makes the controller save its state and start a fresh controller process that reads the new configuration and recovers the state (logged here: "Relinquishing control to new slurmctld process"); configless nodes fetch the new files. Running jobs are not touched. Test configuration changes on a copy first: a node declared with more than it has goes out of service at once (section 1).
