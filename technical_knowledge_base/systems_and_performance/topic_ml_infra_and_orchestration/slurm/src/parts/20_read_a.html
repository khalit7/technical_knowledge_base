The idea in one screen

Slurm is one controller that gives each job its whole allocation at once or nothing, tries waiting jobs in the order of one priority number, and lets a lower job start early (backfill) only when its time limit fits a gap; steps, limits, preemption and reservations are refinements of that one loop.

One job's path: queue, allocation, steps

The topic's lab cluster with the running example (job 79) as it ran: 1 sbatch queues it in slurmctld; 2 the main loop or backfill grants c1 and c2 together (gang scheduling comes free); 3 the script runs as step 79.batch on c1, and its srun starts step 79.0 on both nodes; 4 the order of waiting jobs is one integer per job, fed by usage that slurmdbd records. Section numbers below follow the same path.

You need: nothing beyond Linux processes and a training job. Why clusters need a scheduler at all, and Slurm against Kubernetes and Ray, are on the parent {{Topic: ml-infra-and-orchestration|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}}; how torchrun and NCCL start the ranks inside a step is on {{Launching distributed training|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81b3a8a5c58224bff9fa}}.

Allocation (job)
nodes, CPUs, memory and GPUs held for a time limit, granted all at once.
Job step
one srun inside an allocation: the processes (tasks) that do the work.
Partition
a named set of nodes with its own rules (a queue).
Association, QOS
a user in an account, and a named bundle of priority and limits; both live in the accounting database.
TRES, GRES
anything Slurm counts (cpu, mem, gres/gpu, billing); GRES are devices such as GPUs.
Backfill
starting a lower-priority job in a gap that its time limit fits, without delaying higher jobs.
Fairshare
a priority factor that falls as a user's or team's recent usage rises.
Reason
the one word squeue prints for a pending job: the first check it failed.

What "measured" means here. Every command output ran on the topic's lab cluster on 16 October 2026: Slurm 24.11.5 in four containers, 3 nodes of 4 CPUs and 4 fake GPUs each, which Slurm counts, allocates and bills but which do not exist (jobs get SLURM_GPUS_ON_NODE and no CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES) measured on Apple M1 Pro, Docker Desktop VM, shared machine. Which job ran where, in what order, with which reason and priority is deterministic Slurm behaviour and transfers to a real cluster; seconds and milliseconds are rough.

The running example. One small PyTorch job is used across the whole topic: a 112,577-parameter GPT trained with DDP over gloo, 2 nodes by 2 ranks, 200 steps, checkpoint every 50, submitted with sbatch train.sbatch. Its global loss at step 200 is 2.2638769149780273 on Slurm, Kubernetes and Ray alike (root page, src/data/runs/), and it was again in both runs of it on this page (jobs 79 and 125).

The cluster. A controller container ctl (slurmctld, slurmscriptd, slurmdbd with MariaDB, munged; 512 MB, 1 CPU) and three compute containers c1 to c3 (slurmd and munged only, configless; each declared 4 CPUs, 4,000 MB and gpu:4, four empty placeholder files, while Docker caps each container at 800 MB and 2 CPUs, which Slurm does not know). Users alice and carol are in account teama, bob in teamb. A Docker volume at /shared stands in for a cluster file system (jobs, output, checkpoints). Setup in the root's src/data/README.md.

Versions. Slurm here is the Debian 13 package 24.11.5; the current upstream release is 26.05.5 (13 October 2026, {{SchedMD releases|https://github.com/SchedMD/slurm/releases}}); source is quoted at tag slurm-24-11-5-1 so it matches what ran. Slurm has been open source (GPL-2.0) since 2002; NVIDIA bought SchedMD, its developer, in December 2025 and said it stays open source ({{NVIDIA, 2025-12-15|https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-acquires-schedmd/}}). Most large GPU training clusters run it: the root's {{Tool map|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} has the dated evidence.
