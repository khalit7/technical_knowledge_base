2. What fails: eleven published tables, read carefully

Llama 3's Table 5 is the failure table everyone quotes, and the {{Failure taxonomies|#t-tax}} tab shows it row by row with its misprint (148 interruptions printed as 30.1%; 148 / 419 is 35.3%). It is one of eleven. Read together, they say different things depending on what they count, and two of them disagree with their own text.

Predict: in the Shanghai AI Lab's LLM clusters (4,704 A100s, six months of 2023), user script errors (a missing file, a type error, a bad import) were what share of failed jobs, and what share of the GPU time lost to failures?
73.4% of the 2,575 failed jobs and 3.2% of the GPU time published (Hu et al., {{NSDI 2024, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07648}}; the table's arithmetic reproduces exactly). Script errors fail in the first minutes of small jobs. Infrastructure failures were 10.9% of failed jobs but 82.1% of the GPU time: they hit long, big jobs, after hours of work. Count failures to fix your users' tooling; weight them by GPU time to fix your platform.

The same company, two years apart: Llama 3 (2024) against a recurring 32K-GPU job (2026) published

Both are Meta's counts of unexpected interruptions of one job, so they compare directly; categories as printed, coloured by this page's common scheme our mapping. Sources: {{Llama 3, Table 5|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S3.SS3.SSS4}}; {{Training LLMs with Fault Tolerant HSDP on 100,000 GPUs, Table 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00277}} (arXiv v1, 30 Jan 2026).

Three findings across all eleven, each detailed in the {{Failure taxonomies|#t-tax}} tab:

  • Big jobs fail on hardware; clusters fail on code. In one long job on thousands of GPUs, GPUs and their memory dominate (64% of Llama 3's interruptions by count). In a multi-tenant cluster most failed jobs are user errors (Philly 2019, Acme 2024, ByteRobust 2025), while the hardware failures cost most of the GPU time: at Meta's research clusters hardware ended 0.2% of jobs but 18.7% of GPU runtime published (Kokolis et al., {{HPCA 2025, Fig. 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21680}}).
  • The leading cause moves. In Meta's 2026 table "'Faulty GPU Compute' used to be the top cause, but is now reduced to the fourth (7.4%) ... HBM issues rose to become the top issue" (22.9%), and PCIe devices are second (18.0%). Fleet work pays off cause by cause; the total does not go to zero.
  • A large share is never attributed. Watchdog timeouts and "unknown" are 10% of the 2026 table; job hangs 9.9% of ByteRobust's incidents; NODE_FAIL with no cause is the largest hardware signal at Meta's research clusters. For these the cost is mostly detection time (section 4).

The same order of magnitude in older and smaller records. Meta's OPT-175B ran on 992 80GB A100s, and "hardware failures contributed to at least 35 manual restarts and the cycling of over 100 hosts over the course of 2 months", plus an estimated "70+ automatic restarts" ({{OPT, section 2.5|https://arxiv.org/html/2205.01068v4#S2.SS5}}) published. Per GPU that is at least 1.8e-3 restarts per GPU-day against Llama 3's 4.7e-4 interruptions per GPU-day, roughly 3.7 times as often calculation; the two count different things (restarts against interruptions, "2 months" taken as 60 days) on different hardware two years apart, so read it as "the order of magnitude is stable", not as a trend. The Gemini checkpointing paper (Rice and AWS, SOSP 2023) puts OPT-175B's failures at about 178,000 GPU-hours wasted ({{Wang et al., section 1|https://www.cs.rice.edu/~eugeneng/papers/SOSP23.pdf}}) published, about 12% of 992 GPUs for 61 days calculation.

Real jobs fail more often than independent servers predict. If servers fail independently, a job on N servers fails N times as often as one: at Meta's research-cluster rate of 6.50 failures per thousand node-days, a 1,024-GPU job (128 servers) should run 28.8 hours between failures calculation, yet the same paper measured 7.9 hours for 1,024-GPU jobs ({{Kokolis et al., HPCA 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21680}}) published. Jobs also fail for reasons that are not one server breaking (software, the network between servers, the job's own code); the 1/N law is a floor on the failure rate, not a forecast.

Checks on the tables' own arithmetic calculation. Llama 3: one printed percentage is wrong (Faulty GPU), and the text's "58.7%" GPU share inherits it (64.0% by counts). ByteRobust (SOSP 2025): its text says 38,236 explicit and 5,948 implicit failures, its Table 1 sums to 39,687 and 6,096 (the implicit gap is exactly its 148 NaN incidents). Meta 2026: every percentage matches its count, but "with 100K GPUs, there is a failure every 18 minutes" does not follow from its own 2.3 interruptions per 1,000 servers per day, which gives one about every 50 minutes; 18 minutes matches Meta's older RSC-1 rate of 6.50. Microsoft Philly (ATC 2019): "top-8 cover 88.9%" computes to 87.6%. Acme, C4, Fire-Flyer and Lablup reproduce. Scripts: src/scripts/tax_build.py.
