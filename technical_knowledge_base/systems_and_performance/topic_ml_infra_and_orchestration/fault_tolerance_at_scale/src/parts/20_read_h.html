9. Goodput: name every lost second

Goodput is the share of a job's elapsed time spent computing useful new steps: Gemini's definition, "the time spent computing useful new steps over the elapsed time of the training job" ({{Gemini 1.0 sec. 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11805}}). Its complement, the time lost, is badput. One headline number is not enough to act on; accounting splits badput into named parts, each owned by someone.

The published numbers are not on one scale: "90% effective training time" (Llama 3), "97% goodput" (Gemini) and "97% ETTR" (ByteRobust) differ in whether queue time and MFU are inside the number, which changes it by more than the differences between them (definitions in the closed block below). Google's open-source ml-goodput-measurement library names the badput buckets a platform should log: TPU (accelerator) initialisation, training preparation, program start-up, synchronous data loading, unproductive checkpoint save and restore time, "wasted progress from disruption", "infrastructure recovery from disruption", and custom events; for asynchronous saves only the blocking copy counts as badput ({{AI-Hypercomputer/ml-goodput-measurement|https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/ml-goodput-measurement}}).

Recovery time matters more than the interval. At Llama 3's failure rate a 16,384-GPU job fails every 3.1 hours. With a 1-minute blocking checkpoint and 10 minutes to recover, the best interval is about 19 minutes of work and goodput is about 85% calculation. Cutting recovery from 10 minutes to 1 raises it to about 89%, more than the whole difference between a 10-minute and a 40-minute interval (84% and 83% against the optimum of 85%). Near the optimum the curve is flat in the interval (the reliability page's section 6 draws it); it is steep in the pause and in recovery. That is why Gemini kept model replicas in memory and spare cubes, why DeepSeek checkpoints "typically every 5 minutes" asynchronously, and why the measured recovery of the tiny job (seconds for an in-job restart, 2.5 minutes for a Slurm requeue) matters more at 16,000 GPUs than at 4.

What to log, so the accounting is possible. A per-step record with a timestamp (the running example's ts), a record at every start with the step it resumed from and the checkpoint it loaded, a record for every checkpoint save with its blocking time, and the scheduler's events (submitted, eligible, started, failed, requeued) with their times. With those, every second of a job's wall-clock falls into exactly one bucket, and a failure's cost can be split into its detection, waiting, start-up, load and rework, which is what section 5's model splits. The dashboards that carry it belong to the {{Observability and cost|n:3c65c17b0d0d818ebf79d1af4532f3ee}} child page; the root's {{Cost lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} turns lost GPU-hours into money.
