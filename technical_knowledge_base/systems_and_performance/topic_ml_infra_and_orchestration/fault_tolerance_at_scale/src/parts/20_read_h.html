9. Goodput accounting: name every lost second

Goodput is the share of a job's elapsed time spent computing useful new steps: Gemini's definition, "the time spent computing useful new steps over the elapsed time of the training job" ({{Gemini 1.0 sec. 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11805}}). Its complement, the time lost, is called badput. One headline number is not enough to act on; the point of accounting is to split badput into named parts, each owned by someone.

Four published definitions, and what each leaves out

NameDefinition, as publishedCounts queue time?
Goodput (Google, Gemini 1.0)"time spent computing useful new steps over the elapsed time of the training job"no (elapsed time of the job)
ML Productivity Goodput (Google Cloud, {{blog 2024-04-10|https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/goodput-metric-as-measure-of-ml-productivity}})three factors: Scheduling goodput, "the fraction of time that all the resources required to run the training job are available"; Runtime goodput, "the time spent to make forward progress as a fraction of time when all training resources are available"; Program goodput, "the fraction of peak hardware performance that the training job can extract" (MFU)yes, as its own factor
ETTR, effective training time ratio (Meta, Kokolis et al., {{HPCA 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21680}})"the ratio of productive runtime to available wallclock time of a job run", where wallclock W = Q (queued while eligible) + R (productive) + U (unproductive: catching up from the last checkpoint, restart overhead, checkpoint overhead)yes
Effective training time rate (ByteDance, MegaScale)"the number of iterations multiplied by the iteration training time, divided by the total training time"; ByteRobust adds a one-hour sliding-window versionno

So "90% effective training time" (Llama 3), "97% goodput" (Gemini) and "97% ETTR" (ByteRobust) are not on one scale: whether queue time and MFU are inside the number changes it by more than the differences between them. Google's open-source ml-goodput-measurement library names the badput buckets a platform should log: TPU (accelerator) initialisation, training preparation, program start-up, synchronous data loading, unproductive checkpoint save and restore time, "wasted progress from disruption", "infrastructure recovery from disruption", and custom events; for asynchronous saves only the blocking copy counts as badput ({{AI-Hypercomputer/ml-goodput-measurement|https://github.com/AI-Hypercomputer/ml-goodput-measurement}}).

From the root's Failure lab (src/fail/fl_recompute.json): three Slurm runs of the same 200-step job, wall-clock from rank 0's logs; useful time is the uninterrupted run's wall-clock; wasted progress is steps redone × the measured step time; recovery is from the failure to the first resumed step. Measured on Apple M1 Pro, Docker Desktop VM, shared machine. The remainder (negative when the parts overlap) is what the three-part model does not explain.

What to log, so the accounting is possible. A per-step record with a timestamp (the running example's ts), a record at every start with the step it resumed from and the checkpoint it loaded, a record for every checkpoint save with its blocking time, and the scheduler's events (submitted, eligible, started, failed, requeued) with their times. With those, every second of a job's wall-clock falls into exactly one bucket, and a failure's cost can be split into its detection, waiting, start-up, load and rework, which is what the {{Recovery designs|#t-rec}} tab models. The dashboards that carry it belong to the {{Observability and cost|n:3c65c17b0d0d818ebf79d1af4532f3ee}} child page; the root's {{Cost lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} turns lost GPU-hours into money.
