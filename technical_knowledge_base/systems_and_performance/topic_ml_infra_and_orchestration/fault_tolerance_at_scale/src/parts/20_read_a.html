The idea

A synchronous job on thousands of GPUs fails every few hours, so how much of it trains is decided less by the failure rate than by what each failure costs: the time until something notices, the time to get running again, and the work redone since the last checkpoint. Every design on this page shrinks one of those parts; slow and silent failures never start that clock and need detectors of their own.

One failure on a 16,384-GPU job: about 47 minutes lost with defaults, about 3 with the best designs

Both bars to the same scale, from the failure until the job is back where it was. Defaults (requeue, a 10-minute NCCL timeout, a new machine, a 5-minute blocking checkpoint at its best interval): about 24 min of recovery plus 22 min of rework, goodput 68.9% at Llama 3's failure rate (one failure every 3.1 h). In-memory peer checkpoints with hot spares and fast detection: about 1 min of recovery plus 2 min of rework, goodput 97.2%. MTBF
mean time between failures; for a whole job it falls as 1 / (number of parts).
Fail-stop, -hang, -slow, -silent
a process dies; everything waits forever; steps get slower; numbers go wrong. Section 2.
Rework
steps computed again after a restart, back to the last checkpoint.
Hot spare
a healthy machine kept inside the job's allocation, ready to replace a failed one.
Elastic training
continuing on fewer machines instead of stopping.
Replica group
one of many independent copies of a sharded job that average gradients together; a failed group restarts alone.
SDC
silent data corruption: a wrong result that raises no error.
Straggler
a rank that works, slowly, so every synchronous step waits for it.
Goodput, badput
the share of elapsed time spent on useful new steps; the time lost. Section 9.

1. What fails, and how often

Meta's Llama 3 405B pre-training on up to 16,384 H100s was interrupted 419 times unexpectedly in 54 days, once every 3.1 hours, yet kept "higher than 90% effective training time" published ({{Llama 3 sec. 3.3.4|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S3.SS3.SSS4}}). Meta's 2026 table for a recurring 32K-GPU job counts 678 interruptions and reports 95% to 97% effective training time published ({{arXiv 2602.00277|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00277}}). The failure rate barely moved (2.3 interruptions per 1,000 servers per day in 2026, against 3.8 for Llama 3 by our arithmetic) calculation; the recovery got faster.

Predict: in the Shanghai AI Lab's LLM clusters (4,704 A100s, six months of 2023), user script errors (a missing file, a type error, a bad import) were what share of failed jobs, and what share of the GPU time lost to failures?
73.4% of the 2,575 failed jobs and 3.2% of the GPU time published (Hu et al., {{NSDI 2024, Table 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07648}}; the table's arithmetic reproduces exactly). Script errors fail in the first minutes of small jobs. Infrastructure failures were 10.9% of failed jobs but 82.1% of the GPU time: they hit long, big jobs, after hours of work. Count failures to fix your users' tooling; weight them by GPU time to fix your platform.

Same company, two years apart: HBM overtook the GPU compute rows published

Both are Meta's counts of unexpected interruptions of one job, so they compare directly; categories as printed, coloured by this page's common scheme our mapping. Sources: {{Llama 3, Table 5|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3#S3.SS3.SSS4}}; {{Training LLMs with Fault Tolerant HSDP on 100,000 GPUs, Table 1|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00277}} (arXiv v1, 30 Jan 2026). All eleven tables are in the {{Failure taxonomies|#t-tax}} tab.

  • Big jobs fail on hardware; clusters fail on code. In one long job on thousands of GPUs, GPUs and their memory dominate (64% of Llama 3's interruptions by count). In a multi-tenant cluster most failed jobs are user errors (Philly 2019, Acme 2024, ByteRobust 2025), while the hardware failures cost most of the GPU time: at Meta's research clusters hardware ended 0.2% of jobs but 18.7% of GPU runtime published (Kokolis et al., {{HPCA 2025, Fig. 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21680}}).
  • The leading cause moves. In Meta's 2026 table "'Faulty GPU Compute' used to be the top cause, but is now reduced to the fourth (7.4%) ... HBM issues rose to become the top issue" (22.9%), and PCIe devices are second (18.0%). Fleet work pays off cause by cause; the total does not go to zero.
  • A large share is never attributed. Watchdog timeouts and "unknown" are 10% of the 2026 table; job hangs 9.9% of ByteRobust's incidents; NODE_FAIL with no cause is the largest hardware signal at Meta's research clusters. For these the cost is mostly detection time (section 3).
  • Real jobs fail more often than independent servers predict. If servers fail independently, a job on N servers fails N times as often as one (the {{reliability page|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22}}, section 5, draws it): at Meta's research-cluster rate of 6.50 failures per thousand node-days, a 1,024-GPU job (128 servers) should run 28.8 hours between failures calculation, yet the same paper measured 7.9 hours for 1,024-GPU jobs ({{Kokolis et al., HPCA 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21680}}) published. Software, the network and the job's own code fail too; the 1/N law is a floor on the failure rate, not a forecast.
Older records, and the tables' own arithmetic checked (depth)

The same order of magnitude in older and smaller records. Meta's OPT-175B ran on 992 80GB A100s, and "hardware failures contributed to at least 35 manual restarts and the cycling of over 100 hosts over the course of 2 months", plus an estimated "70+ automatic restarts" ({{OPT, section 2.5|https://arxiv.org/html/2205.01068v4#S2.SS5}}) published. Per GPU that is at least 1.8e-3 restarts per GPU-day against Llama 3's 4.7e-4 interruptions per GPU-day, roughly 3.7 times as often calculation; the two count different things (restarts against interruptions, "2 months" taken as 60 days) on different hardware two years apart, so read it as "the order of magnitude is stable", not as a trend. The Gemini checkpointing paper (Rice and AWS, SOSP 2023) puts OPT-175B's failures at about 178,000 GPU-hours wasted ({{Wang et al., section 1|https://www.cs.rice.edu/~eugeneng/papers/SOSP23.pdf}}) published, about 12% of 992 GPUs for 61 days calculation.

Checks on the tables' own arithmetic calculation. Llama 3: one printed percentage is wrong (148 interruptions printed as 30.1%; 148 / 419 is 35.3%), and the text's "58.7%" GPU share inherits it (64.0% by counts). ByteRobust (SOSP 2025): its text says 38,236 explicit and 5,948 implicit failures, its Table 1 sums to 39,687 and 6,096 (the implicit gap is exactly its 148 NaN incidents). Meta 2026: every percentage matches its count, but "with 100K GPUs, there is a failure every 18 minutes" does not follow from its own 2.3 interruptions per 1,000 servers per day, which gives one about every 50 minutes; 18 minutes matches Meta's older RSC-1 rate of 6.50. Microsoft Philly (ATC 2019): "top-8 cover 88.9%" computes to 87.6%. Acme, C4, Fire-Flyer and Lablup reproduce. Scripts: src/scripts/tax_build.py.
