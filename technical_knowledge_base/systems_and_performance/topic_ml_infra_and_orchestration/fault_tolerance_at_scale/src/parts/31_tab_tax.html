Failure taxonomies: every published failure table, on one scheme

Eleven organisations have published what broke in their training clusters or jobs. Each counts something different (interruptions of one job, failed jobs, incidents, driver error messages) and names causes its own way. This tab keeps every row exactly as printed, checks each table's arithmetic, and maps each row onto one common scheme so the reports can be compared, with the mapping marked as ours.

Before comparing: the units differ
A share of interruptions of one big job (Llama 3, Meta 32K, C4) is dominated by hardware, because only a long synchronous job on thousands of GPUs sees thousands of hardware faults. A share of failed jobs in a multi-tenant cluster (Philly, Acme, ByteRobust) is dominated by user code, because most jobs are small and most bugs show up in the first minutes. Both are true; they answer different questions. Where a report also gives the GPU time each cause cost, switch to it: Acme's script errors are 73.4% of its failed jobs and 3.2% of the GPU time lost. Xid events (Fire-Flyer) are driver messages, many per incident.

What the tables agree on

  • In big synchronous jobs, GPUs and their memory lead. GPU compute plus GPU memory is 64% of Llama 3's interruptions by count and 37% of the 2026 Meta 32K job's, where HBM is now the largest single row (22.9%) and PCIe devices second (18.0%); C4's one 4,096-GPU job: 40% (CUDA plus ECC/NVLink), with the network the larger share.
  • In multi-tenant clusters, users' code leads by count, hardware by cost. Philly and Acme: most failed jobs are programming errors that fail in minutes; the rare infrastructure failures hit long, big jobs and take most of the GPU time (Acme: infrastructure 10.9% of failed jobs, 82.1% of the GPU time).
  • Hangs and unknowns are a large, stubborn share. NCCL watchdog timeouts are 9.0% of the Meta 32K table; ByteRobust's job hangs 9.9%; NODE_FAIL with no attribution is the largest hardware signal at Meta's research clusters. Detection, not repair, is where those cost time.
  • Network faults are the largest share where the paper looks at the network (Unicron 48.6%, C4 47.5%), and small where it does not (Llama 3: switches and cables 8.4%, NICs 1.7%). A cause table reflects its authors' instrumentation as much as the cluster.
