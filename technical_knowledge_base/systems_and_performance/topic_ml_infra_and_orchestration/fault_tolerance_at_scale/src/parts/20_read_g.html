8. Stragglers: the slow rank waits least

A straggler costs no restart and raises no alarm, so it is easy to underestimate. Every synchronous step waits for the slowest rank, so a rank that is 10% slow makes the whole job about 10% slower. The one large published study says it is the common case: in ByteDance's clusters, "42.5% of the jobs are at least 10% slower" than they would be without stragglers, and "10.4% of the allocated GPU hours are wasted" published (Lin et al., {{arXiv 2505.05713v2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05713}}, five months of 2024, 3,079 jobs of 128 GPUs or more).

The root page's lab made rank 3 of the running example (2 nodes x 2 ranks, Slurm) sleep 50 ms or 200 ms after every step measured. Every rank then reported the same step time (22.1, 70.5 and 221.8 ms), so a step-time log cannot tell you who is slow. Time spent inside the gradient all-reduce can, but backwards: we wrapped loss.backward() (which contains DDP's all-reduce) with a timer on every rank:

measured: median backward() time per rank, steps 11 to 100 (runs fl-probe0, fl-probe50; probe in the root page's src/fail/scripts/fl_wrap.py, FL_PROBE=1).

With rank 3 sleeping 50 ms, ranks 0 to 2 spent about 62.6 ms in backward and rank 3 only 12.2 ms: it arrives last, so it never waits. That is the inversion every detector below works around.

Rank by compute, not by wait: try a detector on the same timings

planted (truth)flagged by the rule
  • The slow rank waits least. "Long waits in the all-reduce" flags the three innocent ranks hundreds of times and never rank 2 or rank 3: tools that rank collectives by duration find the victims. And the "Step time" rule flags the whole job, never a rank.
  • Compute time, compared across ranks, names it. With a 10-step median window, "Longest compute" and the relative score both flag rank 2 at step 84, four steps after its slowness began, and rank 3's mild slowness at step 204 or 205, with no false alarm on any of the three runs; with the median they ignore rank 0's periodic spike, which is a code path, not a slow machine. With the maximum, rank 0 is flagged on all twelve of its spikes: decide which of the two you want to page someone about.
  • Comparing a rank with its own past is noisier. The individual score raised 4 false alarms on the run with no straggler at the same settings, and with the maximum it raises dozens. A 1-step window reacts at once and flags noise; a longer one is quieter and later. The relative score's default cutoff of 0.75 here is NVIDIA Resiliency Extension's; real systems also require a minimum duration before acting.

Production detectors do the same: NVIDIA's NVRx scores each rank's compute against the fastest rank (relative) and against its own best (individual), flagging below 0.75; MegaScale times every rank's forward and backward with CUDA events and draws a heat map; FALCON times communication groups with CUDA events and confirms with GEMM and link tests. Lin et al. find stragglers mostly in compute, FALCON's cases at four nodes were mostly network congestion (42 of 43): different clusters and job mixes, and a job's own imbalance (pipeline stages, sequence lengths) looks like a straggler to a detector that only compares ranks. So a detector that names a rank still needs a second step that tells a slow machine (drain it) from a slow role in the job (fix the code). The dashboards and alerts that carry these scores belong to the {{Observability and cost|n:3c65c17b0d0d818ebf79d1af4532f3ee}} child page.
