5. Recovery designs: which part of the lost time each one removes

After a failure the job loses the time to detect it, to wait for the scheduler, to get a healthy machine, to start (processes, rendezvous, communicator set-up), to load the checkpoint, and then to redo the work since that checkpoint. On top, it pays a pause for every checkpoint it writes, failure or not. Every design below attacks some of these and leaves the rest. The {{Recovery designs|#t-rec}} tab puts them on one clock and runs the goodput model for any job size.

Predict: a 16,384-GPU job at Llama 3's failure rate (one failure every 3.1 hours). Which single change raises goodput most: cutting detection from 10 minutes to 10 seconds, adding hot spares so no machine is provisioned, or making checkpoints asynchronous (a 0.78 s pause instead of 5 minutes)?
Asynchronous checkpoints, by far, in this model calculation: from 68.9% (all defaults) to 72.7% with fast detection, 77.1% with spares as well, and 97.0% once checkpoints pause for 0.78 s instead of 5 minutes, because a cheap checkpoint can be taken every couple of minutes, which shrinks both the pauses and the rework. The other two only shorten the recovery itself, which here is a smaller part of the loss than the 40-minute optimal interval's pauses and rework. Just-in-time checkpoints (Microsoft, {{EuroSys 2024|https://doi.org/10.1145/3627703.3650085}})No periodic checkpoints: when a failure is detected, the surviving data-parallel replicas checkpoint on the spot, so only "a single minibatch iteration of work" is replayed.Pause and rework."from several minutes to a few seconds per GPU", "nearly zero steady state overhead" (abstract) authors' figures; scale and timings unconfirmed (the paper was not readable here).Needs a healthy replica of every shard at failure time.
Elastic resizing (torchrun --nnodes=min:max; Oobleck, Varuna, ReCycle)Continue on fewer machines: re-form the group (torchrun), switch to a precomputed pipeline template (Oobleck), or reroute micro-batches into pipeline bubbles of peers (ReCycle).Wait and replace; the job keeps going.Measured here: torchrun shrank in 44 s, with a waiting spare back to full size in 18.1 s (root Failure lab). {{Oobleck|https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08125}} up to 29.6 times the throughput of Bamboo and Varuna at a failure every 10 min (30 A40); {{ReCycle|https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14009}} 1.46 times Oobleck (32 A100, larger scales simulated) authors' figures.A smaller world changes the batch per GPU or the layout; most pre-training runs restart at full size instead (root Failure lab, section 8). Shown at small scale only.
Redundant computation (Bamboo, {{NSDI 2023|https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi23/presentation/thorpe}})Each node also computes its neighbour's pipeline stage in the pipeline's idle bubbles, so a lost node's work is already done elsewhere.Almost all recovery.3.7 times the throughput of checkpointing on preemptible V100s, needing about 1.5 times the nodes authors' figures.Built for spot instances that vanish often; the extra nodes rarely pay off on a reserved cluster.
Replica groups (torchft, {{pytorch/torchft|https://github.com/pytorch/torchft}})The job is many replica groups, each an ordinary sharded job; a fault-tolerant all-reduce averages across groups; a Lighthouse forms a quorum every step; each step commits only if every group succeeded; a failed group restarts alone and copies weights from a healthy peer.Everything except the failed group's share and the discarded step.300 L40S, 30 groups, Llama 3 1B, a failure every 60 s: 82.3% of steps committed, 81.2% training efficiency authors' figures; Meta's 2026 paper applies fault-tolerant HSDP at up to 100,000 GPUs ({{arXiv 2602.00277|https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00277}}).The batch shrinks while a group is away, which changes the optimisation slightly. At a failure every 15 s on average the same demo committed 268 of its first 888 steps (30.2%) with 18.9 of 30 groups taking part; the post prints 13.4% training efficiency, while its own two figures multiply to 19.0% calculation. The point is that the model still converged ({{PyTorch, June 2025|https://pytorch.org/blog/fault-tolerant-llama-training-with-2000-synthetic-failures-every-15-seconds-and-no-checkpoints-on-crusoe-l40s/}}).

Managed versions exist and package several rows: {{AWS SageMaker HyperPod|https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/latest/dg/sagemaker-hyperpod-resiliency-slurm-auto-resume.html}} replaces nodes found by its health agent and deep checks (DCGM level 4, EFA, NCCL tests) and resumes with srun --auto-resume=1; its December 2025 {{"checkpointless" training|https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/checkpointless-training-on-amazon-sagemaker-hyperpod-production-scale-training-with-faster-fault-recovery/}} reports recovery cut from "15 to 30+ min" to under 2 minutes (under 90 s at 2,304 GPUs) vendor. Google reports a 1,024-GPU (A3 Mega) job going from about 80% to over 90% goodput by stacking asynchronous checkpoints (+3 to 4 points), checkpoints every 20 minutes instead of hours (+2 to 3), recovery from 20 to about 2 minutes (+1 to 2) and elastic scale-down (+1 to 2) vendor ({{Google Cloud blog, May 2025|https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/elastic-training-and-optimized-checkpointing-improve-ml-goodput}}). The order of those gains is the same as the prediction above: make checkpoints cheap first.

Choosing, in one paragraph
Measure your failure rate and your current R and C first (section 9). Asynchronous checkpoints are the cheapest large win at almost any scale. Hot spares and fast detection matter once recovery is a large share of the MTBF, roughly from thousands of GPUs. In-memory checkpoints and replica groups matter when the MTBF falls to tens of minutes (about 30 minutes at 100,000 GPUs at Llama 3's rate), where even a fast storage restart is a large share of every interval. Elastic resizing and redundant computation fit preemptible capacity better than a reserved cluster. Every design needs its restart path exercised before the real run needs it.
