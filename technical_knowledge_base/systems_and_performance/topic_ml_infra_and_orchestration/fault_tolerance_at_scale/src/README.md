## The 2026-10-24 rebuild to the teaching contract: what changed and why
This page owns fault tolerance for training at scale: failure modes and their rates as they hit a job, checkpointing strategies, recovery designs, goodput, silent data corruption detection and what a flipped bit does to a run. Hardware failure rates, the checkpoint interval (Young, Daly, exact) and the CPU fleet SDC studies are the reliability page's (linked to its sections 5, 6 and 7). Editing only; no new runs.
- **Top**: the core sentence (what each failure costs decides goodput, not the rate) and a new hero (`36_js_ft_hero.js`): one failure on a 16,384-GPU job to scale, defaults (about 47 min, 68.9%) against in-memory peer checkpoints with spares and fast detection (about 3 min, 97.2%), with the part each design removes named on the legend. Then "You need" and new terms; the old "In one screen" block and provenance box became the hero and a closed provenance block.
- **Tabs whose content explained one mechanism moved into Reading**: Recovery designs (the one-failure animation and time-lost bars are section 5, goodput against job size and the Meta reproduction section 9, the inputs table a closed block) and Straggler replay (the measured animation and the detector bench are section 8). Failure taxonomies stays a tab: it answers "across all eleven reports".
- **New section 4, checkpointing strategies** (owned here): a static annotated diagram of what synchronous, asynchronous, in-memory/peer and just-in-time checkpoints stop training for, with the sharding rule and a prediction prompt; the with/without-checkpoint run moved here from section 9 and the atomic-write rules from section 6. The measured sync/async animation stays on Storage and data movement, section 7 (linked).
- **Removed visuals**: the Slurm straggler animation (its compute/wait split was illustrative; the replay shows the measured split; its step times stay in the text) and the goodput-against-interval calculator (the reliability page's section 6 owns the interval curve, with a day simulation and Monte Carlo; this page keeps the point that recovery time matters more than the interval, with its numbers).
- **Depth into closed blocks**: the timer table and NCCL messages, DCGM and Xid, the drain/cordon/health-check transcripts, older records and arithmetic checks, per-design published numbers, the model box and inputs, the rendezvous source, the bit-for-bit tables, the published SDC and straggler study tables, goodput definitions, managed services. Mistakes and interview answers were already closed.
- **No autoplay**: the skill templates replace `21_js_rd_common.js` and `99_js_tabs.js`; `16_js_ft_flh.js` no longer starts on scroll (`check/no_autoplay.mjs`). SVG labels raised to 11 px.
- **Links fixed**: the reliability page's removed checkpoint simulator is now "section 6", its time 23 min, its SDC section 7; the training root's time 22 min.

## Length judgement
| | Before (2026-10-23 audit) | After (2026-10-24) |
|---|---|---|
| Reading, core (230 words a minute, `html_utils/audit_pages.mjs`) | 43.3 min | 35.4 min |
| Reading, closed details | 5.5 min | 27.2 min |
| Other tabs, core (excluding Further reading) | taxonomies 3.2 + recovery 10.6 + stragglers 4.0 = 17.8 min | taxonomies 3.2 min |
| Core total, excluding Further reading | 61.1 min | 38.6 min |
| Visuals drawn in Reading | 8 (plus 8 in tabs) | 13 (plus 2 in the taxonomies tab) |

Why 35 minutes is right: the page teaches nine linked subjects (failure rates, failure shapes, detection timers, four checkpoint strategies, eleven recovery designs, restore traps, silent corruption, stragglers, goodput), each with its own measured or published evidence, and two of them (silent corruption and stragglers) rest on this page's own experiments, whose walk-throughs are the core. About a sixth of the core words are captions of the visuals that moved in from the tabs. What made the old page long in the wrong place (the reference tables, transcripts, evidence columns and the tab introductions that restated Reading) is closed or deleted; the closed time grew because the evidence moved there instead of being cut.
