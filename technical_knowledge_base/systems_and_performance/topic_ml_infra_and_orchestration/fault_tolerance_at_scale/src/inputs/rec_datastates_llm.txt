SOURCE: Maurya, Underwood, Rafique, Cappello, Nicolae. "DataStates-LLM: Lazy Asynchronous Checkpointing for Large Language Models". HPDC 2024. arXiv:2406.10707v1, 15 Jun 2024. RIT + Argonne National Laboratory.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10707
LABEL: vendor (authors of the system; academic/national lab, nothing sold). Read 2026-10-16.

MECHANISM (my words): Parameters and optimizer state do not change during the forward and backward pass (they change only at the optimizer step), so the GPU-to-host copy of a checkpoint can run lazily in the background during forward/backward of the next iteration instead of stalling training; the training only waits if the copy is not done by the next update. From host memory, shards are flushed in a multi-level, streamlined way to node SSD / parallel file system and consolidated asynchronously. Integrated into DeepSpeed and Megatron.

VERBATIM
- Abstract: "we introduce a lazy asynchronous multi-level approach that takes advantage of the fact that the tensors making up the model and optimizer state shards remain immutable for extended periods of time ... We evaluate our approach at scales of up to 180 GPUs using different model sizes, parallelism settings, and checkpointing frequencies. The results show up to 48x faster checkpointing and 2.2x faster end-to-end training runtime compared with the state-of-art checkpointing approaches."
- Results text: "compared to DeepSpeed, Asynchronous checkpointing, or TorchSnapshot, DataStates-LLM demonstrates at least 4x and up to 34x higher checkpointing throughput across various model sizes."
- Methods note: "In our experiments, we use up to 128 nodes (512 GPUs) to study" (the abstract says up to 180 GPUs for the evaluated runs; both appear).
- Platform: "ALCF's Polaris HPC testbed. It consists of 560 nodes, each equipped with 512 GB of DDR4 memory ... two 1.6 TB SSDs (2 GB/s) and four Nvidia A100 GPUs" (A100 40GB per Fig. 1), four NVLinks, PCIe Gen 4.
- Gap analysis of others' published throughput (useful independent-ish cross-check, as quoted by these authors): "Gemini [43] reports 3.13 GB/s checkpointing throughput (9.4 GB shard of GPT-100B takes about 3 seconds for checkpointing). REFT [42] reports 38% PCIe bandwidth utilization at 6 GB/s, while TRANSOM's checkpointing engine (TCE) [45] reports achieving a throughput of ~1.2 GB/s. Nebula [23], which is Microsoft's DeepSpeed closed-source implementation of asynchronous checkpointing and is only available on the Azure cloud, reports achieving 1-4 GB/s (GPT2-XL checkpoint of 20.6 GB takes 5 seconds to checkpoint)."

CAVEATS
- Small scale (up to 180 GPUs in evaluation, A100 40GB). "48x" is the best case (30B model) vs the slowest baseline; per-figure numbers vary by model and frequency.
