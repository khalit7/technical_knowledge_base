SOURCE: Jiang et al. "MegaScale: Scaling Large Language Model Training to More Than 10,000 GPUs". NSDI 2024. ByteDance + Peking University. arXiv:2402.15627v1, 23 Feb 2024.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15627 (also https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/jiang-ziheng)
LABEL: vendor (ByteDance on its own system and production run). Read 2026-10-16 from arXiv v1 PDF. Section 4 = robust training; 4.4 = fast checkpointing and recovery; 6.3 = production faults.

MECHANISM (my words): A driver process allocates pods via a custom Kubernetes; each node's executor runs a robust training daemon that heartbeats to the driver. On an abnormal status or a missed heartbeat window, the driver suspends training on all executors, runs lightweight self-check diagnostics (intra-host loopback/RNIC tests, NCCL tests), evicts faulty nodes and has Kubernetes replenish healthy ones, then resumes from the latest checkpoint. Checkpointing is two-stage: GPU to pinned host memory (seconds, blocking), then a background process writes to HDFS. On load, one worker per data-parallel group reads the shared partition from HDFS and broadcasts it to its peers.

VERBATIM
- Init time (affects restart): "the initialization time for Megatron-LM on 2,048 NVIDIA Ampere GPUs is approximately 1047 seconds." Replacing TCPStore with Redis: "reduces the initialization time to 361 seconds on 2,048 GPUs." With barrier reordering (O(n^2) to O(n)): "The initialization time is reduced to under 5 seconds on 2048 GPUs, and to under 30 seconds on more than 10,000 GPUs with those optimizations."
- Recovery loop: "When the driver process detects an abnormal status in a particular training process, or fails to receive a heartbeat from an executor within a predefined time window, it triggers the fault recovery procedure. The driver will suspend the ongoing training task across all executors and command them to run a series of self-check diagnostics ... the driver submits the IP addresses of the nodes to be blocked ... to Kubernetes, which evicts the faulty nodes and replenishes the cluster with an equivalent amount of healthy ones which pass our diagnostic tests ... the driver resumes training from the latest checkpoint." (heartbeat interval/window value not stated)
- Checkpoint stage 1: "each GPU worker writes its on-chip states to the host memory, and then continues the training process. After the optimization of Pytorch's serialization mechanism and the use of pinned memory, this process can be reduced to several seconds thanks to the high PCIe bandwidth". Stage 2: "a background process takes over, asynchronously transferring the state from the host memory to a distributed file system (HDFS in our deployment)".
- Load: "we designate a single worker in the group to read the shared state partition from HDFS, thereby reducing the load linearly. This worker then broadcasts the state partition to all other GPU workers that share the same data."
- Production: "This run uses more than 10,000 GPUs and lasts for several weeks ... we experience training restarts over 100 times. With the robust training framework, over 90% of software and hardware faults are automatically identified and fixed".
- Sec 6.3: "over 90% of the exceptions among them are automatically detected, located, and recovered using our robust training framework, such as CUDA error and segmentation fault. The average time required for detecting failure and executing diagnostic tests is less than 10 minutes. Moreover, the system can catch up to the training progress prior to the crash within 15 minutes from the latest checkpoints, maintaining over 90% effective training time rate, which is calculated as the number of iterations multiplied by the iteration training time, divided by the total training time."
- Hardware: NVIDIA Ampere GPUs (A100 class), >10,000 GPUs; headline MFU result 175B model on 12,288 GPUs.

CAVEATS
- "over 90% effective training time rate" is a floor, not a measured value; checkpoint interval not stated; no per-component timings beyond the <10 min and <15 min figures. Hot spares not described (replenishment comes from Kubernetes).
