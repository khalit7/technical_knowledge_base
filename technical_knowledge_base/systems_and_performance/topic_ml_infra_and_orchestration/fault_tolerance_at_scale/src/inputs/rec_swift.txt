SOURCE: Zhong, Sheng, Liu, Yuan, Wu. "SWIFT: Expedited Failure Recovery for Large-scale DNN Training". arXiv:2302.06173v1, 13 Feb 2023 (HKU, OneFlow). Venue not confirmed from the PDF (reportedly PPoPP 2023 poster / later journal: unconfirmed).

LABEL: vendor (authors). Read 2026-10-16.

MECHANISM (my words): No extra copy of model state. On failure, SWIFT repairs inconsistent state and copies it from a surviving data-parallel replica (replication-based recovery). When no replica exists (e.g., a pipeline stage with no DP peer), it logs intermediate data (activations/gradients crossing machine boundaries) during training and, after failure, replays the lost computation from the last checkpoint on the replacement machine, spreading the recomputation across machines; logging is asynchronous, in bubble time, and selective.

VERBATIM
- "Instead of making an additional copy of the model state, SWIFT resolves the inconsistencies of the model state caused by the failure and exploits the replicas of the model state in data parallelism for failure recovery. We propose a logging-based approach when replicas are unavailable, which records intermediate data and replays the computation to recover the lost state upon a failure."
- "For replication-based recovery in training Wide-ResNet-50, SWIFT reduces recovery time by 98.9%, 98.1%, and 98.1% compared to global checkpointing, CheckFreq and Elastic Horovod, respectively. For logging-based recovery in training BERT and ViT, SWIFT reduces recovery time by 57.3% and 76.3% compared to global checkpointing, respectively ... SWIFT can achieve up to 1.16x speedup in total training time".
- Hardware: machines "equipped with eight 32 GB Tesla V100 GPUs (NVLink)"; experiments use up to 16 workers (GPUs).

CAVEATS
- Small scale (16 V100) and smaller models; included as the log-and-replay design point.
