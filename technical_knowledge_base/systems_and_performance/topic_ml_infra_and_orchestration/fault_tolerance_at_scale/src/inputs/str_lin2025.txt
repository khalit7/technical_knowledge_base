Jinkun Lin (NYU), Ziheng Jiang, Zuquan Song, Sida Zhao, Menghan Yu, ... Haibin Lin, Xin Liu (ByteDance
Seed), Aurojit Panda, Jinyang Li (NYU). "Understanding Stragglers in Large Model Training Using What-if
Analysis", arXiv:2505.05713v2 (12 May 2025; document date June 11, 2025). OSDI 2025 per the brief (venue
not printed in the arXiv text I read: unconfirmed). https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05713
Project page: https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/StragglerAnalysis   (PDF read 2026-10-16)

Data: "traces collected from the LLM training cluster at ByteDance over a five-month period from January
to May 2024". "we only use traces for jobs that use at least 128 GPUs ... This produces 3079 jobs for our
analysis." "Of the 3079 jobs, 31.7% use >= 256 GPUs, 18.3% use >= 512 GPUs, and 3.6% use >= 5000 GPUs. In
total, the analyzed jobs cover around half of the total GPU-hours among all LLM training jobs." Framework:
customized Megatron-LM instrumented with NDTimeline: "By default, it samples 10% of the training steps".

Method (what-if simulation)
- Trace per profiled step the start/end of operations: forward-compute, backward-compute, forward/backward
  PP send/recv, grads-reduce-scatter, params-all-gather (per microbatch, per rank).
- Idealised durations (verbatim): "For the computation operation, we use the average value across the
  group of elements to be equalized. For communication operations, we use median instead of average."
  Reason: "computational straggling arises predominantly due to workload partitioning imbalance. Therefore,
  taking the average makes sense as doing so amounts to workload re-balancing." Communication straggles
  "due to external issues like switch/NIC flapping ... the affected operations tend to be very long, which
  significantly skews the average value and makes median a better choice."
- Replay on a dependency model: compute op starts when all dependencies finish; comm op ends at "the
  maximum launch time among the group plus the corresponding transfer-duration".
- Metrics (verbatim): "S = T / Tideal" (job slowdown); "St = Tideal^-t / Tideal" per operation type;
  resource waste "(T - Tideal)/T = 1 - 1/S"; worker slowdown "Sw = Tideal^-w / Tideal" where Tideal^-w fixes
  all operations except those on worker w. Traces with simulation error >= 5% are discarded.
- Straggling job threshold: "straggling jobs, aka those with slowdown S >= 1.1."

Key numbers (verbatim)
"42.5% of the jobs are at least 10% slower due to stragglers. At the tail, stragglers can result in jobs
wasting 45% of their allocated resources"
"> 10% of the jobs waste at least 21.3% of the GPU-hours it is allocated, while ~ 1% jobs waste at least
45.0% of its allocated GPUs. Across all our traces we found that 10.4% of the allocated GPU hours are
wasted due to stragglers." (Fig. 3 CDF: p50 7.8%, p90 21.3%, p99 45.0% waste)
Persistence: "Most steps incur similar slowdowns within a straggling job, suggesting that they are often
not caused by transient environmental issues but are rather caused by persistent problems" (Fig. 4
normalised per-step slowdown p50 1.00, p90 1.06, p99 1.26).
"Contrary to the observation in FALCON [40] we find that most slowdowns are caused by computation
operations, as opposed to communication. This is owing to our ample network bandwidth, the use of
dedicated LLM-training clusters, and several in-house ..."
"We did not notice a positive correlation between job size and its straggler-related slowdown"
Worker faults: "worker problems contribute to more than 50% of the observed slowdown for only 1.7% of
straggling jobs. This leads us to conclude that problematic workers are not the dominant factor".
(Method: MW = (T - Tideal^W)/(T - Tideal) with W = the slowest 3% of workers; worker slowdown approximated
by min of its DP-rank and PP-rank slowdowns, reducing simulations from DP x PP to DP + PP.)
Also: jobs with S > 3 "were large jobs, where fewer than 3% of the workers were responsible for the
problem".
Root causes:
- Pipeline stage imbalance: "forward-compute (backward-compute) of the last stage is 2.07 (1.41) times
  slower than an average stage's computation." "for 39.3% of jobs, a majority of the job slowdown (i.e.,
  MS >= 0.5) is because of the last pipeline stage." Manual re-partition gave "a speedup of 9.9%".
- Sequence length imbalance (long context): "21.4% jobs are affected by sequence" length imbalance [line
  wraps in the PDF; exact sentence not fully captured]; greedy re-balancing of microbatches by summed
  sequence length gave "a 23.9% improvement in throughput" on a 32K-max-length job.
- Python GC: planned GC "turns off Python's automatic GC mechanism, and instead manually schedules GC at a
  user-specified interval ... on a job that uses 128 DP ranks, using this optimization so that GC is run
  every 500 training steps results in a 12.6% improvement."
- Uncommon: PyTorch allocator segment_alloc/segment_free churn; false kernel dependency.
Online tool SMon (verbatim): "SMon estimates slowdown, per-step slowdown, and worker slowdown, and presents
the results on a webpage. ... we present worker slowdowns using a heatmap, where each cell represents a
worker with its x- and y-coordinate as its DP and PP rank". "jobs with worker issues, stage partitioning
imbalance and sequence length variance have distinct patterns". "Within a month of its deployment, SMon
has helped us identify and address several stragglers: it allowed us to locate faulty machines in three
cases where machine problems were responsible for stragglers, it helped identify a case where sequence
length variance was leading to large job slowdown, and a separate case where imbalanced compute
partitioning across pipeline stages was to blame."
Coverage caveat: after discarding traces, "total coverage of 38.2% jobs and 56.4% GPU hours."
