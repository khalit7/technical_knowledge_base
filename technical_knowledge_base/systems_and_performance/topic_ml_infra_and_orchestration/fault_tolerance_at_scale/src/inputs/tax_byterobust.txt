- Table 1: "Statistics of training incidents collected over a three-month span, encompassing 778,135 LLM training
  jobs." ("all LLM training jobs captured on our production platform over the last three months").
- Table 2: "incidents in the large-scale training jobs (on >2000 GPUs) over the last month".
- Deployment results: "a three-month job for training a dense model (Llama-like, 70+B), and a one-month job for
  training an MoE model (200+B). These pretraining jobs were run on a GPU cluster comprising 9,600 Hopper GPUs."
  Up to 1200 machines x 8 NVIDIA Hopper 80GB.
- Abstract: "achieving 97% ETTR for a three-month training job on 9,600 GPUs." "deployed for over one year".

UNIT OF COUNTING
Incidents (any training interruption or anomaly, including deliberate manual restarts), by symptom.
Three classes, verbatim: "(i) Explicit failures, which are characterized by clear diagnostic indicators, such as
error messages in stdout or stderr logs or specific exit codes; (ii) Implicit failures, which often manifest as job
hang-ups, performance degradation, or anomalous training trajectories, whose root causes are often elusive; and
(iii) Manual restart, which refers to proactive interruptions of training for the purposes of algorithm and
engineering improvement."

ARITHMETIC CHECKS (mine)
- Sum of all 17 counts = 55,365. Every printed percentage equals count / 55,365 rounded to 0.1 (e.g. CUDA
  19968/55365 = 36.07%, Job Hang 9.94%, Manual 17.31%). Percentages sum to 100.0%. Consistent.
- Class sums from the table: Explicit 39,687 (71.7%), Implicit 6,096 (11.0%), Manual 9,582 (17.3%).
- MISMATCH with the text: "ByteRobust identifies 38,236 explicit failures, 5,948 implicit failures in the
  three-month period". Implicit: 6,096 minus 5,948 = 148, exactly the NaN value row, so the text appears to exclude
  NaN from the implicit total. Explicit: 39,687 minus 38,236 = 1,451, which matches no row or small combination of
  rows (checked all subsets of up to 4 rows); unexplained. Manual restart 9,582 matches the text ("9582 manual
  interruptions occurred").
- Text: Hang is "covering over 10% of all incidents"; table shows Job Hang 9.9% (5506/55365 = 9.94%). Slight
  mismatch unless "Hang" is meant to include more than the Job Hang row. Text also says "more than 10% of incidents
  manifest as implicit failures": 6,096/55,365 = 11.0%, consistent.
- Share among failures only (excluding manual restarts, my computation): 45,783 failures; CUDA Error 43.6%, Job Hang
  12.0%, CPU Overload 13.3%, CPU OOM 12.2%.

OTHER TIMES (verbatim)
- Explicit failures "typically exhibit short detection times (around 60s, via error messages or other log-based
  indicators) and localization times (ranging from 2 minutes to 15 minutes)."
- "we encountered a communication hang issue caused by CUDA errors ... which took us more than one and a half
  hours for manual diagnosis."
- SDC: "Through more than 8 hours of offline stress testing, we successfully identified the faulty GPU machines."
- "ByteRobust maintained the cumulative ETTR at a plateau of up to 97% and kept the unproductive training time
  within a maximum of 50 minutes for both jobs." (sliding-window ETTR over a one-hour window)
- ETTR: "calculated as the ratio between the productive training time and the wall-clock time of a job".
- "warm standby and hot update mechanisms achieve up to 10.87x and 11.04x respectively in recovery";
  "every-step checkpointing with less than 0.9% overhead".
- Fig. 2: "training an LLM in a 1000-GPU cluster over a 10-day training span, during which a total of 28 runs were
  conducted (each run corresponds to a restart of the model training)".
- Cites Llama 3: "Meta reports that hardware failures occur approximately every 2.78 hours during the training of
  large models on 16,000 GPUs". (Note: 54 days x 24 / 466 total interruptions = 2.78 h; with only the 419
  unexpected ones it is 3.09 h. So "hardware failures" here is really all interruptions.)
