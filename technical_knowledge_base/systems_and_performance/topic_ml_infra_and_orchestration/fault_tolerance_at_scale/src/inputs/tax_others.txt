1. MINDER (ByteDance)
Yangtao Deng, Xiang Shi, Zhuo Jiang, Xingjian Zhang, Lei Zhang, Zhang Zhang, Bo Li, Zuquan Song, Hang Zhu, Gaohong
Liu, Fuliang Li, Shuguang Wang, Haibin Lin, Jianxi Ye, Minlan Yu (Tsinghua, ByteDance, Northeastern, Harvard).
"Minder: Faulty Machine Detection for Large-scale Distributed Model Training". NSDI 2025.
arXiv 2411.01791, version read: v2 (26 Apr 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01791
Unit: fault instances in ByteDance training tasks, "fault types that occurred over seven months" (2023).
Total count: not printed (unconfirmed).

CHECKS (mine): hardware rows 38.9+6.6+5.7+2.0+1.7+0.9 = 55.8 OK; software 14.6+7.7+5.7 = 28.0 OK; groups
55.8+28.0+6.0+10.3 = 100.1 (rounding). Metric columns imply small subsamples: ECC 35 instances (28/35 = 80.0,
23/35 = 65.7, 3/35 = 8.6, 16/35 = 45.7, 4/35 = 11.4, 20/35 = 57.1), PCIe 12, GPU card drop 20, NVLink 6, CUDA 21,

Freq is over a larger fault population than the metric study.
Quotes: "hardware faults make up the majority of faults (55.8%), in which ECC errors constitute a large proportion
(38.9%)." "From our experience, a training task can encounter two faults per day on average, possibly leading to a
halt for hours." "Minder can accurately and efficiently react to faults within 3.6 seconds on average, with a
precision of 0.904 and F1-score of 0.893." PCIe downgrading case: "The detection took 40 minutes in total and
involved multiple teams."

2. LABLUP 504 x B200 (small cluster, 2026)
Lablup Inc. "From Detection to Recovery: Operational Analysis on LLM Pre-training with 504 GPUs" (Lablup Technical
Report). arXiv 2605.09370, version read: v5 (15 Jun 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09370
Scale: "a 63-node NVIDIA B200 production cluster (504 GPUs), using 55 days of Prometheus time-series data and 73 days
of operational logs covering 224 multi-node training sessions." Unit: failure events mapped from XID codes.
TABLE 2 (verbatim): Failure distribution of our cluster, mapped to Minder categories
Failure Type (Minder Category)           XID Code    Count   Ratio (%)
NVLink errors                            145, 149      5      29.4
ECC errors                               94            2      11.8
GPU card dropout                         79            2      11.8
GPU execution errors                     119           1       5.9
Machine unreachable                      -             2      11.8
Others (e.g., performance degradation)   -             5      29.4
Total                                                 17     100.0
"63-node cluster (504 B200 GPUs), 55-day observation period." CHECK: counts sum to 17; 5/17 = 29.4, 2/17 = 11.8,
1/17 = 5.9. OK. Rate (mine): 17 / (63 x 55) = 4.9 per 1,000 node-days.
TABLE 3 (verbatim, XID by resolution): 79 "GPU fell off the bus" RESTART_BM, node reboot; 119 "GSP RPC timeout"
RESET_GPU; 145 "NVLink RLW error" RESET_GPU; 149 "NVLink NETIR error" RESET_GPU; 31 "GPU memory page fault"
RESTART_APP; 43 "GPU processing halted" RESTART_APP; 94 "Contained ECC error" RESTART_APP, auto-corrected.
It also reprints Minder Table 1 (matches the original above) and cites straggler studies: "59% of large-scale
training jobs (512-1,024 GPUs) experienced fail-slow stragglers and suffered an average job completion delay of
34.59%" (Wu et al.) and "42.5% of jobs in production LLM training clusters were affected by stragglers, wasting
10.4% of total GPU hours" (Lin et al.); secondary citations, originals not read here.

3. L4 (CUHK with a cloud vendor "Company-X")
Zhihan Jiang, Junjie Huang, Guangba Yu, Zhuangbin Chen, et al. "L4: Diagnosing Large-scale LLM Training Failures via
Automated Log Analysis". FSE Companion 2025 (Industry). arXiv 2503.20263, version read: v1 (26 Mar 2025).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20263
Unit: 428 failure reports of failed LLM training jobs, "collected between May 2023 and April 2024 from Platform-X";
jobs average "72.8 billion parameters" and "941 accelerators per job".
Symptoms (Fig. 3): Launching Failure 21.3%, Training Crash 57.5%, Abnormal Behavior 16.6%, Others 4.7%.
CHECK: sum 100.1%; 57.5 + 16.6 = 74.1% matches "Most LLM training failures (74.1%) occur during the iterative
training stage". Implied counts (mine): 91, 246, 71, 20 = 428.
Root causes: four categories, hardware fault, user fault, framework fault, platform fault, but "Due to privacy
concerns, specific distribution proportions are withheld." Ranking only: hardware "the most common failure root
cause in our study" (network faults most common within hardware, then accelerator), user fault "the second largest",
framework "a small proportion", platform "the least proportion". NOTE inconsistency: Finding 3 says "User faults
constitute the predominant cause of LLM training failures" while the text says hardware is the most common.
Diagnosis: "the average time to diagnose LLM training failures is 34.7 hours, with approximately 41.9% of failures
requiring more than 24 hours"; Table 1: Log-only 53.9%, Non-log 10.1%, Hybrid 36.0% (sum 100.0); average training
log size 16.92GB.

4. BLOOM (BigScience)
BigScience Workshop, "BLOOM: A 176B-Parameter Open-Access Multilingual Language Model". arXiv 2211.05100, version
read: v4 (27 Jun 2023). https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100 (sec 3.4.1 and 3.5).
Scale: Jean Zay, "48 nodes, each having 8 NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs (a total of 384 GPUs) ... a reserve of 4 spare
nodes"; "about 3.5 months"; "1,082,990 compute hours".
No cause table. Verbatim: "During training, we faced issues with hardware failures: on average, 1-2 GPU failures
occurred each week. As backup nodes were available and automatically used, and checkpoints were saved every three
hours, this did not affect training throughput significantly. A PyTorch deadlock bug in the data loader and disk
space issues led to 5-10h downtimes." Rate (mine): 1.5/week over 48 nodes = about 4.5 per 1,000 node-days.
(The detailed BigScience chronicles repository was not read here.)

5. GEMINI 1.0 (Google)
Gemini Team, Google. "Gemini: A Family of Highly Capable Multimodal Models". arXiv 2312.11805, version read: v5
(9 May 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11805 (sec 3, Training Infrastructure).
No cause table and no counts. Verbatim: "genuine machine failures are commonplace across all hardware accelerators at
such large scales." "on any unplanned hardware failures, we rapidly recover directly from an intact model replica
... the overall goodput for the largest-scale training job increased from 85% to 97%." "we can expect SDC events to
impact training every week or two." Goodput: "the time spent computing useful new steps over the elapsed time of
the training job." TPUv4 SuperPods of 4096 chips; OCS reconfigures cubes "in around 10 seconds".
