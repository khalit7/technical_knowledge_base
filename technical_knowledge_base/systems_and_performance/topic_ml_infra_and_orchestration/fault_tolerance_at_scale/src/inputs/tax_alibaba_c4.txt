3 Traffic collision in network
4 Storage hang or slow
5 Software issues: errors in users' code, CPU contention caused by other processes, e.g., python GC

KEY QUOTES (verbatim)
1. "the majority of them simply show themselves as 'NCCL Errors'. This is largely due to the synchronous nature of
   distributed training tasks. For instance, a hardware issue in a GPU could lead to the crash of one of the worker
   processes, causing its peer workers to fail to receive an acknowledgment ... this triggers an error code of 12
   in the communication library."
2. "In our experience, around 30% of our time was dedicated to error detection, system diagnosis, isolating
   defective nodes, and restarting processes, leaving only 70% for actual computing tasks".
3. "the majority of the downtime was due to extended periods of system diagnosis ... identifying the precise cause
   of job failure could take hours or even days in the absence of our diagnostic tools. The second major
   contributor to downtime was post-checkpoint overhead."
Other: "our experience has shown that high-end GPU products tend to have higher error rates"; automatic detection
expedites "the process from tens of minutes to mere seconds".

NUMBERS (verbatim)
- "In our operating clusters, as shown in Figure 5, 0.057% of NIC-ToR links fail each month, and about 0.051% of
  ToR switches encounter critical errors and crashes. Under this high failure rate, a single LLM training job would
  encounter 1-2 crashes each month. Furthermore, 5K-60K link flapping cases happen each day, introducing temporary
  performance degradation as well."
- Checkpoints "typically range from two to four hours, (even at these high intervals, the overhead introduced by
  checkpointing is still around 5%)"; checkpoint "e.g., 30GB per GPU" and "high overhead (e.g., 100s)".
- "Given that training costs are 20K dollars per hour for a training task utilizing 3K GPUs, a failure could lead to
  a financial loss of 30K dollars." CHECK: 30K / 20K = 1.5 h of lost work per failure (consistent with half of a
  roughly 3 h checkpoint interval). My inference.
- "our operational single-chip switches outnumber multi-chip switches by 32.6x. On the contrary, the total number of
  critical hardware failures in multi-chip switches is 3.77x higher than in single-chip switches." CHECK: per-switch
  critical failure rate ratio = 3.77 x 32.6 = about 123x for multi-chip vs single-chip. My computation.
- "over 40% of critical failures in our traditional data centers were caused by the abovementioned two categories of
  issues introduced by stacked dual-ToR in the past three years." "70% of upgrades in our ToRs do not meet this
  assumption of ISSU".
- Sanity check of "1-2 crashes each month" (mine): HPN hosts have "8 GPUs" and "9 NICs, each with 2x200Gbps" (one,
  NIC0, for the frontend), so a 3K-GPU job spans 375 hosts and about 3,000 backend NICs. With one NIC-ToR link per
  NIC (traditional single-ToR), 3,000 x 0.00057 = 1.7 link failures per month; ToR crashes add a little. Consistent
  with "1-2 crashes each month". (HPN's dual-ToR doubles the links but makes a single link failure non-fatal.)
