Shaobu Wang, Guangyan Zhang, Junyu Wei (Tsinghua), Yang Wang (Ohio State), Jiesheng Wu, Qingchao Luo
(Alibaba Cloud). "Understanding Silent Data Corruptions in a Large Production CPU Population".
SOSP '23, October 23-26, 2023, Koblenz. https://doi.org/10.1145/3600006.3613149
Author copy read 2026-10-16: https://yangwang83.github.io/papers/sosp2023-Wang.pdf
Note: the symbol ‱ below means "per ten thousand" (basis points of CPUs).

Scale (verbatim): "we conduct an extensive study on SDCs in a large production CPU population,
encompassing over one million processors."
Toolchain: "The toolchain includes 633 testcases and a framework." "We started such tests since January
2021, and so far, we have found hundreds of faulty processors." Deep study of 27 faulty CPUs: "we have run
tens of millions of tests and collected more than ten thousand SDC records from these tests".
Testing cadence: pre-production after factory delivery, datacenter delivery, re-installation; then
"in production, machines will be regularly tested in groups. Testing for each group lasts about 2 weeks,
and testing for the whole fleet needs months."

Rates (verbatim)
Observation 1. "In overall, 3.61‱ of the CPUs are identified to cause SDCs in our study."
  [3.61 per 10,000 CPUs, about 1 in 2,770]
Table 1 failure rate by test timing: Factory 0.776‱, Datacenter 0.18‱, Re-install 2.306‱, Regular
  0.348‱, Total 3.61‱.
Observation 2. "The failure rates observed during the pre-production testing period and the regular
  testing period amount to 3.262‱ and 0.348‱, respectively." "accounting for a significant proportion
  (90.36%) of all faulty processors we have identified."
Observation 3. "SDCs have been identified across all micro-architectures present within Alibaba Cloud
  fleet. The failure rate does not decrease with newer chips." Table 2 per micro-architecture M1..M9:
  4.619, 0.352, 2.649, 0.082, 0.759, 3.251, 1.599, 9.29, 4.646 (‱); "range from 0.082‱ to 9.29‱."
Comparison (verbatim): "Our results are consistent with but are more precise than those reported by
  Google ("the order of a few mercurial cores per several thousand machines") and Meta ("hundreds of CPUs
  detected for SDCs in hundreds of thousands of machines")."
Observation 4. "A single processor fault may exert its influence on an individual physical core or
  encompass all cores within the processor." "In about half of the faulty processors, there exists only
  one defective physical core."
Observation 5. "SDCs exhibit a substantial prevalence in particular workloads, exposing five vulnerable
  features, namely arithmetic logic computation, vector operations, floating point calculation, cache
  coherency and transactional memory."
Observation 6. "operations related to floating-point numbers demonstrate heightened vulnerability to SDCs."
Observation 7. "For floating-point numbers, bitflips predominantly occur in the fraction part, resulting
  in minor precision losses." "99.9% of the precision losses on double precision (64bit) floating-point
  numbers are less than 0.02%. 80.25% of the precision losses on single precision (32bit) floating-point
  numbers are less than 5%. On the other side, 40.2% of the precision losses on 32-bit integer data are
  bigger than 100%."
Observation 8. "bitflips tend to manifest at fixed position(s) within the number representations."
Observation 9. "Some SDCs are highly reproducible" "from as low as 0.01 times per minute to as high as
  hundreds of times per minute. In 51.2% of the settings, the occurrence frequency is higher than once per
  minute." Case: "a service in Alibaba Cloud falsely reported 26 invalid-data errors in approximately 4.5
  hours because of one faulty processor".
Observation 10. "Among those less reproducible SDCs, temperature serves as an important SDC triggering
  condition. In some settings, the occurrence frequency of SDCs demonstrates exponential growth in response
  to increasing temperatures." Example: "all the SDC records with testcase C on MIX1 are generated with
  their temperature above 59 C, which is much higher than its idle temperature (about 45 C)". One core
  "only produces errors when other cores are busy" (shared cooling).
Observation 11. "In a production environment with tens of thousands of CPUs, 560 out of the 633 testcases
  have not detected any errors."
Observation 12. "The effectiveness of existing fault tolerance techniques is diminished when confronted
  with CPU SDCs." (EC and CRC use vector ops, a vulnerable feature; "ECC and CRC assume the data is correct"
  when computed.)
Mitigation: "Farron, which relies on prioritized testing to detect highly reproducible SDCs and
  temperature control to mitigate less reproducible SDCs."

Relevance to ML training: Observation 7 is why SDC in floating-point training can hide: most flips land in
mantissa bits and change values slightly, while a flip in the exponent changes a value by orders of
magnitude (my note; the paper studies CPUs, not GPUs).
