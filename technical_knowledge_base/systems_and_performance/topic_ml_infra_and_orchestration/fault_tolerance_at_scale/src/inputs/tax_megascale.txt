IMPORTANT: MegaScale publishes NO failure-cause table and no per-category counts or percentages.
What it gives is aggregate restart and recovery numbers for one production run, plus named case studies.

SCALE AND PERIOD
- Production run: "a proprietary model with hundreds of billions of parameters on multi-trillion tokens. This run
  uses more than 10,000 GPUs and lasts for several weeks." (NVIDIA Ampere GPUs.)
- Benchmark: "55.2% Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) when training a 175B LLM model on 12,288 GPUs, improving the MFU
  by 1.34x compared to Megatron-LM."

UNIT OF COUNTING
Training restarts of one job; "exceptions" in the fault records of that job. No denominators are printed.

NUMBERS (verbatim)
- "Over the several weeks of this run, we experience training restarts over 100 times. With the robust training
  framework, over 90% of software and hardware faults are automatically identified and fixed by the techniques
  detailed in sec 4. The rest of the problems are handled with the help of the troubleshooting tools described in
  sec 5."
- "over 90% of the exceptions among them are automatically detected, located, and recovered using our robust
  training framework, such as CUDA error and segmentation fault. The average time required for detecting failure
  and executing diagnostic tests is less than 10 minutes. Moreover, the system can catch up to the training
  progress prior to the crash within 15 minutes from the latest checkpoints, maintaining over 90% effective
  training time rate, which is calculated as the number of iterations multiplied by the iteration training time,
  divided by the total training time."
- Initialization (restart cost): "for Megatron-LM on 2,048 NVIDIA Ampere GPUs is approximately 1047 seconds";
  after optimisations "361 seconds on 2,048 GPUs", then "under 5 seconds on 2048 GPUs, and to under 30 seconds on
  more than 10,000 GPUs".
- Stragglers: "a minor fraction of machines (approximately 0.5%) exhibit substantially slower performance during
  training"; "specific hosts took approximately 10% more time to execute the same forward computations ... After
  isolating and removing these problematic hosts from the cluster, we observed an approximate 0.7% improvement in
  MFU."

ARITHMETIC CHECK (mine, illustrative only, not printed)
- ">100 restarts over several weeks": at 3 weeks that is about 4.8 restarts/day (one per ~5 h); at 6 weeks about
  2.4/day. The paper does not state the exact duration, so the rate is unconfirmed.
- Lost time per incident upper bound implied: <10 min detect + diagnose, plus <15 min catch-up, so roughly
  <25 min of lost progress per auto-recovered fault (excluding reschedule and init time). My inference, not stated.
- ETTR definition here differs from Meta's: iterations x iteration time / total training time (no queue time).

MECHANISM (verbatim fragments)
- Detection: a robust training daemon "sends heartbeat to the driver periodically"; "When the driver process
  detects an abnormal status in a particular training process, or fails to receive a heartbeat from an executor
  within a predefined time window, it triggers the fault recovery procedure." RDMA traffic: "If the traffic ceases
  entirely, the driver will automatically initiate the fault recovery procedure."
- Diagnostics: intra-host loopback test (RNICs to memory nodes and GPUs), RNIC-to-RNIC test, NCCL all-to-all within a
  node and all-reduce with neighbours under the same ToR switch.
- Eviction: driver submits faulty node IPs to Kubernetes, "which evicts the faulty nodes and replenishes the cluster
  with an equivalent amount of healthy ones".
- Checkpoint: two-stage (GPU to host memory "several seconds", then async to HDFS); recovery reads one copy per
  data-parallel group from HDFS and broadcasts.

CASE STUDIES (failure kinds named, no counts)
- CUDA errors, segmentation faults (auto-recovered examples).
- Defective GPUs that "probabilistically cause blocking when executing NCCL communication operations. Such blocking
  can hang the entire machine, leading to cascading timeouts" (found via the 3D-parallel visualiser: hung nodes do
  not log, timed-out peers do).
- Computational stragglers (specific hosts, ~10% slower).
- MFU decreasing over time (irregular garbage collection, certain PyTorch ops on the critical path).
- "Frequent network interface flapping": "the root cause of this problem is the bad link quality between network
  card, AOC cable and switch"; lesson: set NCCL timeout threshold explicitly larger.
