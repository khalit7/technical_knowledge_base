Abstract (verbatim)
"we first present a characterization study on a shared production cluster with over 10,000 GPUs. We find
that fail-slows are caused by various CPU/GPU computation and cross-node networking issues, lasting from
tens of seconds to nearly ten hours, and collectively delaying the average job completion time by 1.34x."
[PDF prints "1.34×"] "We have applied FALCON to detect human-labeled fail-slows in a production cluster with
over 99% accuracy. Cluster deployment further demonstrates that FALCON effectively handles manually
injected fail-slows, mitigating the training slowdown by 60.1%."
Cluster: "over 10,000 GPUs and 4,000 nodes interconnected through a RoCE network with up to 400 Gbps NIC
capacity".

Single node: GPT2-11B on 4 H800, 10,000 iterations; "six out of 392 sampling jobs experienced computation
fail-slows ... these computation fail-slows persist for about 10 minutes, extending the job completion time
(JCT) by 11.79%". GPU case: "GPU0 was 20% slower than others ... and recorded an unusually high temperature
of nearly 70 C"; GPU degradation "may indicate a hardware problem, with an occurrence rate of about 0.5%,
consistent with ByteDance's report".
Four node: GPT2-7B on 8 A100 over 4 nodes; "43 out of 107 jobs experienced fail-slows. Among them, one job
was slowed due to CPU contention, while the remaining 42 encountered network congestion. The average
duration of these slowdowns was about 24 minutes, extending the average JCT by 15.45%."
Communication CoV (Table 2): intra-GPU A100 0.01, H800 0.01; intra-node NVL 0.02, PIX 0.09; inter-node RDMA
0.29.
At scale: "Among 27 jobs, 16 encountered fail-slows, delaying the average JCT by 34.59%. In particular, 20%
of these jobs were delayed more than 50% ... The mean fail-slow duration is 72 minutes" "13 slow jobs were
due to network congestion, while the remaining were attributed to both network and GPU degradation."
Takeaways (verbatim): "#1. Fail-slows are usually transient, primarily caused by degradation in computation
and communication; the former typically stem from slow GPUs or CPU contention, while the latter are mainly
due to network congestion." "#2. Computation fail-slows tend to be short-lived and less frequent ... cross-
node communication fail-slows are more common and tend to last longer" "#3. As training scales up, the
likelihood of simultaneously encountering multiple performance issues increases. The compounding effects
of these issues can lead to significant training slowdowns, potentially exceeding 90%."

FALCON-DETECT algorithm (precise)
Phase 1 Tracking: a shim layer intercepts NCCL calls "by hooking to NCCL functions using Linux's LD_PRELOAD
environment variable" and logs types and timestamps (framework agnostic, no model changes).
 - Iteration time from the NCCL call sequence: autocorrelation ACF(X)_k = Cov(X_t, X_{t+k}) / Var(X_t);
   "we can determine the recurring period of X by identifying the first k for which ACF(X)_k exceeds a
   predefined threshold M (set to 0.95 in our experiments)"; iteration time = time between a call and its
   occurrence one period earlier.
 - Slow-iteration detection: Bayesian Online Change-point Detection on the iteration-time series; "reports
   t as a change-point if the likelihood exceeds a certain threshold (set to 0.9 in our experiments)".
 - Verification: "compares the average iteration time before and after each identified change-point,
   treating it as a jitter if the performance difference is less than 10%."
Phase 2 Profiling: "inject CUDA Events into each NCCL call to measure the execution time of each
communication group" "a communication group that spent prolonged time in data transfer is likely
experiencing fail-slow degradation, while groups that eagerly wait for data (i.e., idling) suggest healthy
performance. In our implementation, communication groups with data transfer time longer than 1.1x median
value are classified as suspicious."
Phase 3 Validation: pause training by trapping NCCL calls in a wait loop (no checkpoint-restart); compute:
"dispatches standard GEMM tests to all GPUs in parallel"; communication: split ring/tree collectives into
non-overlapping P2P sends, O(1) passes (ring: 2 passes for even rank count, 3 for odd; tree: 4 passes);
"Since the transmission sizes are identical, slows link measures longer communication times".
Detection accuracy (Tables 4-5): computation BOCD+V 100.0% (392/392), FPR 0.0%, FNR 0.0% vs plain BOCD 77.8%
accuracy, FPR 18.39%; communication BOCD+V 99.1% (106/107), FPR 0.00%, FNR 2.3% vs BOCD 69.2%, FPR 34.0%.
Production cross-check: "FALCON-DETECT correctly diagnoses 498 jobs (99.8% accuracy), with less than 1%
overhead" (of 499).

FALCON-MITIGATE (ski-rental-like escalation): S1 do nothing; S2 "redistributing micro-batches across data
parallel groups to alleviate the load on slow GPUs"; S3 "adjusting the parallelization topology to move
congested links to light-traffic groups" (and consolidate stragglers into minimal PP stages); S4
checkpoint-and-restart. Results: "reduces the slowdown from computation fail-slows by up to 82.9% and from
communication fail-slows by up to 61.5%"; 64 H800 GPU job: slowdown mitigated by 60.1%.
