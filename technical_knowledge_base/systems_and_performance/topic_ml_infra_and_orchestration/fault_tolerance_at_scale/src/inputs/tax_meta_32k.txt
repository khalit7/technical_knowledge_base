ARITHMETIC CHECKS (mine)
- Counts sum to 678. Every percentage equals count / 678 rounded to 0.1 (HBM3 22.86, PCIe 17.99, Faulty GPU 7.37,
  Thermal 0.15 printed 0.2). Column sums to 100.2% (rounding). Consistent; unlike Llama 3 Table 5 there is no
  mismatched row.
- "78% of interruptions were due to hardware-related failures": not reproducible exactly from the rows since the
  paper does not say which rows count as hardware. Clear hardware rows (HBM3, PCIe, Faulty GPU Compute,
  Switch/Cable, SSD, SRAM, System Memory, Cooling, Thermal) = 406 = 59.9%; adding Host Maintenance and System Reboot
  = 486 = 71.7%; adding Kernel Fault as well = 525 = 77.4% (closest to 78%). So the 78% likely includes host
  maintenance, reboots and kernel faults. Flag as interpretation.
- Rate: "On average, training experienced 2.3 interruptions per 1,000 servers per day." 32K GPUs = 4,000 servers
  -> 9.2 interruptions/day, one every ~2.6 h.
- MISMATCH: the paper also says "our production data show that, with 100K GPUs, there is a failure every 18
  minutes". At 2.3 per 1,000 servers per day, 100K GPUs (12,500 servers) gives 28.75/day = one per 50 min, not 18.
  18 min implies about 6.4 per 1,000 servers per day (80/day), which matches Meta RSC-1's 6.50 per 1,000 node-days
  (Kokolis et al.): 12,500 x 6.5/1000 = 81/day = one per 17.7 min. Neither source says so; my observation.
- Effective training time arithmetic: "out of these 18 minutes, 10 minutes will be spent on failing over and
  restarting ... only 8 minutes for effective training. This results in an effective training time of 8/18 = 44%".
  8/18 = 44.4%. OK. With FT-HSDP stall 3 min: 15/18 = 83%; paper says "from 44% to 80%" (presumably other
  overheads; not explained). Flag minor.

KEY QUOTES (verbatim)
1. "'Faulty GPU Compute' used to be the top cause, but is now reduced to the fourth (7.4%), thanks to our prior
   effort to address specific failure modes and the aggressive isolation of misbehaving GPUs. As GPU compute issues
   declined, High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) issues rose to become the top issue in our current version."
2. "An increasing number of failures with SSD devices were due to a fatal NVMe firmware bug. This resulted in SSDs
   entering read-only mode with critical I/O errors. The other category of PCIe device issues involved GPUs, which
   reported uncorrectable errors, elevated correctable errors, and parity mismatch issues."
3. "training interruptions caused by numerical issues, such as Not-a-Number (NaN) errors or silent data corruption
   (SDC) detected through model evaluations, often required manual investigation ... The 37 interruptions in this
   category were traced to seven hosts, with root-cause investigations lasting from several hours to multiple days.
   In one SDC case, we traced the issue to 32 consecutive parameters (out of 2T total) in a single expert of a
   specific layer that abruptly spiked by over 1e7." [dashes replaced]
Also: kernel faults, lockups and spurious hangs "were typically transient and resolved by rebooting the affected
servers"; "synchronous recovery takes up to 10 minutes at 100K GPUs"; FT-HSDP cuts stall "from 10 minutes to 3
minutes".
