MECHANISM (my words): A per-pod Robust agent plus a control plane run real-time inspection (NIC, switch, GPU, host) and stack-trace aggregation to localise faults, then evict machines (often whole PP groups: over-eviction). Evicted machines are replaced from a pool of pre-validated warm standbys (self-checked, image installed, then sleeping), sized to the P99 of a binomial model of simultaneous failures, so only the failed machines change. Code changes are applied by in-place hot update (no pod teardown). Checkpoints are taken every step asynchronously to host CPU memory and SSD, with backups placed on peers in a different parallel group (outside the same failure domain), avoiding remote storage on restart.

VERBATIM KEY PASSAGES
- Abstract: "deployed on a production GPU platform with over 200,000 GPUs and advances the state of the art in training robustness by achieving 97% ETTR for a three-month training job on 9,600 GPUs."
- Intro: "ByteRobust identifies 38,236 explicit failures, 5,948 implicit failures in the three-month period ... Our micro-benchmark experiments on 16,384 GPUs show that the warm standby and hot update mechanisms achieve up to 10.87x and 11.04x respectively in recovery. The efficient checkpoint mechanism in ByteRobust achieves every-step checkpointing with less than 0.9% overhead to speed up failover."
- Failover cost before: "failover operations include: scheduling new machines ... reconstructing the pod environment, reloading the latest checkpoint from the remote storage [80], and recomputing the lost training progress." "relatively large checkpointing intervals (e.g., 30 minutes [20] or every 100 training steps [6, 80]) are required to reduce checkpoint stalls. We observed that the time cost of failover operations is often more than 10 minutes for the large model training at the scale of 10,000 GPUs."
- Hang detection without help: jobs "not produce any logs until timeout (i.e., 30 or 60 minutes ...)".
- Hot update: non-critical updates applied at next failure or "when a default triggering window (e.g., 24 hours) expires."
- Warm standby: "We set the number of warm standby instances to the 99th percentile (P99) of this distribution". Pool init: "machine self-checks ..., image installation, and library downloading, which then enters a low-power sleep mode." Eval: "4 backups for a 1,024-instance job".
- Detection, Table 3 (with inspection, seconds): NIC crash 30, Port flapping 30, Switch down 30x2, GPU driver hang 10, high temperature 10, GPU lost 10, OS kernel fault 2. Without inspection: T_timeout ("default timeout threshold of PyTorch Distributed (~10 minutes)") or T_monitor. Text: "baseline approach relying on only the timeout threshold (~30 minutes)". Network inspection interval 30 s; "We wait for two consecutive unresponsive switch events before raising an alert."
- Testbed: production 9,600 Hopper GPUs (1200 machines x 8 Hopper 80GB max) for deployment; eval on "1024 machines, each equipped with 16 NVIDIA L20 48GB GPUs connected via 30GB/s PCIe ... over 16,384 GPUs", eight 400 Gbps RDMA links, 2 TB DRAM per machine.
- Production jobs: three-month dense (Llama-like, 70+B) and one-month MoE (200+B) on 9,600 Hopper GPUs. "ByteRobust maintained the cumulative ETTR at a plateau of up to 97% and kept the unproductive training time within a maximum of 50 minutes for both jobs." Cumulative ETTR: "the ratio of the accumulated productive training time to the cumulated wall-clock time of a job run"; also sliding-window ETTR over one hour. MoE ETTR lower than dense.
- Table 7 (scheduling time, requeue vs hot update, s): 128x16: 454 vs 46; 256x16: 545 vs 51; 512x16: 635 vs 54; 1024x16: 768 vs 65. "our hot-update mechanism is 11.04x faster than full requeue".
- Warm standby (Fig. 12, weighted-average scheduling time from detection to resume, catastrophic failures fixed at 1%): "reduces recovery time by 10.87x versus requeue and 5.36x versus reschedule, and is within just 5.19% of the oracle upper bound."

CHECKS / CAVEATS
- 10.87x / 11.04x are scheduling-time ratios on L20 PCIe machines, not end-to-end ETTR. Table 8 MFU columns are relative to no checkpointing at every-iteration frequency (an extreme setting for Megatron save).
- Over-eviction trades false positives for faster localisation (whole PP group of 8 machines when 1-2 are faulty).
- Table 8 column mapping is my reconstruction from pdftotext; ratio claims (99.69%, 95.10%) match it.
