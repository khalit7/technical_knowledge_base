Departures from the child-page method: no GPU exists here, so GPU counters are derived from their definitions (labelled) and CPU time of the training pods stands in for the device meter; the dashboard is a replay of a recording, not a live Grafana (the page is a sandboxed iframe). The PodThrottled rule was corrected during the slow-node run (it first read the container cgroup); the page says so and the recorded alert times show it.

## The 2026-10-24 rebuild: what changed and why
Rebuilt to `.claude/skills/explainer-page/teaching/teaching-contract.md`; no new runs, every number from the existing recordings, inputs and `recompute.py`.
- **Top**: the core sentence ("each meter between the bill and the training log reads at most what the meter above it reads"), a new static hero (one GPU-hour read by seven meters, from allocation to kept model FLOPs, each rung labelled with who reports it and a published or measured reading), "You need" and a short list of new terms. The old "In one screen" cards and the 11-row word table were cut to the terms this page introduces; counters, gauges, exporters and cardinality are linked to the SWE Observability page.
- **Visuals inline beside their claims** (Reading visuals 1 before, 7 after):
  - the healthy-against-hung stepper moved from section 4 to section 1, next to the three-meter claim it proves; it no longer starts playing on a mode switch;
  - the Metrics explainer tab became section 2's figure (it explains one mechanism: what each counter reads), with its decoder table inline and its definitions table in a closed block; the tab was removed;
  - new: goodput bars of the two recorded runs (section 5), an alert timeline against the default timeouts (section 7), a fragmentation diagram of the Kueue and Slurm cases (section 8), and cost per useful GPU-hour against the busy share with one slider, owned, reserved, on demand and spot, with cost per million training tokens (section 10). The planner tab's own chart was removed so the concept is drawn once.
- **New content the brief asked for**: spot pricing (Azure H100 spot $2.27, read 2026-10-16, from the existing price data) and cost per token for training (derived in `recompute.py`: 500,258 tokens per paid GPU-hour for the default run; $2.80 per million owned, $11.93 Capacity Block, $13.75 on demand, $4.54 spot before evictions); the node and job dashboards are named.
- **Bug fixed**: Reading section 12's capacity numbers were written by `22_js_oc_read.js` before `33_js_oc_cap.js` defined `OCP`, so they rendered blank; the engine now loads first (`16_js_oc_plan.js`) and the numbers are static text checked against `recompute.json`.
- **Static instead of script-written**: the utilisation-studies, goodput, alert-latency, chargeback and decoder tables, and the MFU result, so the ledger and the prose checks can see them.
- **Ownership**: goodput definitions and the badput split are linked to Fault tolerance at scale (which carries them); MFU arithmetic to Performance math; Prometheus basics to SWE Observability; serving cost per token to LLM serving in production.
- **Closed blocks**: the DCGM field table, the five-fleet table, the MFU run on one CPU core, published goodput numbers, the scrape configuration, what the running example logs, accounting commands, the counter definitions and four design questions.
- **Deleted as fat**: the Common mistakes block (each item now sits in the section it belongs to, or restated it), four interview answers that restated sections, wind-ups, headings, an unsourced "92% allocated" illustration.
- **Further reading**: stale times updated from the pages' current headers (Making training fast 32 plus 19, Power 23 plus 3, GPU architecture 29 plus 8, and the siblings).
- **Images**: none. No openly licensed photo of a GPU monitoring dashboard or nvidia-smi output was found on Wikimedia Commons; the H100 photos there (Geekerwan, CC BY 3.0) show a PCIe card and teach nothing about metering.

Why 24 minutes is right: the page owns ten things a GPU-fleet operator needs (three meters, the DCGM counters, the fleet-scale gap, MFU logging, goodput metering, the exporters and their cost, alerts, scheduling efficiency, chargeback, and the cost of an hour and a token), each with its measured or published evidence; it absorbed the Metrics explainer and added spot and per-token cost, and still came out six minutes shorter in total. About a fifth of the core words are figure captions that carry the measured numbers. Reference material (field tables, fleet tables, configs, commands) is closed.
