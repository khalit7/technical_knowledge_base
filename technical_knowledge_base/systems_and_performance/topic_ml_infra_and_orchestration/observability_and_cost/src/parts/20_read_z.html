Common mistakes

Alerting on GPU utilisation
A hung NCCL collective reads 100%; a job with a fifth of its SMs busy reads 100%. Alert on the job's step counter and tokens per second; use DCGM's SM and tensor activity to explain, not to page.
Deploying dcgm-exporter with its defaults and expecting SM activity
# DCGM_FI_PROF_SM_ACTIVE, gauge, The ratio of cycles an SM has at least 1 warp assigned. is commented out in default-counters.csv at 4.8.4. Enable it (and occupancy) in your counters file, and run the exporter with the privileges profiling needs.
Looking for throttling in the wrong cgroup
The slow-node test's first rule read the container cgroup and returned 0 while the pod was throttled 70% of its periods: the cap was on the pod cgroup, and throttling is counted where the limit is hit. Measured here; check which cgroup (or, on GPUs, which clock reason) carries the limit before trusting a zero.
A progress alert without a start-up grace period
GpusHeldNoProgress went pending at the start of every job (admitted, no step yet) and was saved only by its 20-second for:. Real jobs take minutes to start: exclude the first steps or alert on "no step for N x the job's own median step time after the first step".
Expecting a gauge that is off by default
kueue_cluster_queue_resource_usage and kueue_cluster_queue_nominal_quota exist only with enableClusterQueueResources: true; the shipped config has it commented out. The same goes for Slurm: no metrics endpoint before 25.11.
Reading "GPU utilization" from a Slurm exporter as device utilisation
The community exporter's "GPU utilization" is "allocation ratio (alloc/total) per GPU type" (vpenso/prometheus-slurm-exporter README); it read 67% in the fragmentation test while a job waited and some allocated GPUs were idle.
Fragmentation hidden by GPU-only quotas
Kueue said insufficient unused quota for memory in flavor default-flavor, 528Mi more needed with 8 of 12 GPUs free. Size every quota resource to the job shape, or the cheapest resource decides how many GPUs you use.
Per-GPU series with per-job labels
Every job id on every GPU counter makes new series each run; over a 15-day retention that is the cardinality explosion the Observability page warns about. Put job identity on a few job-level series and join at query time.
Billing that silently truncates
Slurm stores billing as an integer: 4 GPUs and 1 CPU at weights 1.0 and 0.25 bill as 4, not 4.25. Use weights that give whole numbers for your job shapes, or bill from AllocTRES directly.

Interview questions

A 64-GPU job's dashboard shows 100% GPU utilisation everywhere, but no step was logged for 15 minutes. What is happening and how do you confirm it?
Most likely a hung collective: NCCL's kernel is resident and polling, which counts as a kernel executing. Confirm with SM and tensor activity (near zero), NVLink and network bytes (near zero), the step counter, then the stacks (py-spy) and the NCCL flight recorder to find the rank that never joined. Kill and drain that node rather than wait for the 10-minute NCCL timeout.
Why is SM activity a better meter than GPU utilisation, and why is it still not enough?
GPU utilisation is a duty cycle (any kernel running); SM activity averages over all SMs, so it sees a kernel that fills a fifth of the chip. But warps waiting on memory count as active, so a memory-bound job reads high SM activity with idle tensor cores. MFU from the training log is the measure of useful work; tensor activity is the nearest counter.
Define goodput. How do Google's and Meta's definitions differ?
The share of time that becomes kept training progress. Google multiplies scheduling goodput (all resources available), runtime goodput (checkpointed progress over allocated time) and program goodput (MFU). Meta's ETTR is productive runtime over available wall-clock time, including queueing, and leaves MFU out. Same idea, different boxes: always say which.
How do you find a straggler in a 2,048-GPU synchronous job?
Not with utilisation: everyone waits for the slowest, so all ranks look alike (measured here: the healthy pod dropped to the throttled pod's CPU). Time the same work per rank (CUDA events around forward and backward, as MegaScale and NVRx do) and compare each rank with the best; then look at that node's clock event reasons, power, temperature, Xid and link errors.
How big is the Prometheus for a 1,024-GPU cluster?
dcgm-exporter's 25 default series per GPU is about 25,600 series; container and node metrics add about 950 per node, so 128 nodes add about 122,000. At a 30-second scrape that is about 4,900 samples per second, and at 2 bytes per sample about 12.7 GB for 15 days. The section 7 calculator does it for any inputs. Then watch labels: per-job labels multiply it.
Charge teams for allocated or used GPU-hours?
Allocated. It is what the others could not use, the scheduler knows it exactly, and it gives teams a reason to release GPUs, fix hangs and size jobs well. Show utilisation next to it as a quality signal, not as the bill.
Design question: observability and cost controls for a 1,000-GPU training lab.
Meters: dcgm-exporter on every node with SM activity, tensor activity, clock reasons, Xid and NVLink errors enabled; node and container metrics; scheduler metrics (Kueue or Slurm 25.11's endpoint) with pending reasons and admission waits; every training job exporting step, tokens per second, MFU, last checkpoint and per-rank step time. Storage: Prometheus sharded or a long-term store, 30-second scrape for GPU counters, job labels on job-level series only. Alerts: no step for N step times after start-up, step rate below a fraction of the run's own best, a node with throttled clocks or Xid, GPUs admitted with no progress, work pending with GPUs free. Accounting: chargeback on allocated GPU-hours per team from sacct or Kueue Workloads, monthly; quotas per team with borrowing. Planning: quarterly forecast from the run plan (tokens, deadlines, failure rate) with the busy share of the fleet as the key efficiency number, and an own-versus-rent comparison redone whenever prices or depreciation assumptions change.
Your cluster is 95% allocated but throughput is down 30% this month. Where do you look?
Allocation is not work. Per job: tokens per second and MFU against last month; jobs holding GPUs with no steps (hangs); failure and restart counts (goodput); checkpoint stall time. Per node: low SM or tensor activity on a few GPUs (stragglers, throttling). Then the queue: did job shapes change and fragmentation rise?
