1. Observability and cost in one screen

A training cluster is paid for by the GPU-hour whether the GPUs do anything or not. Observability is the set of meters that tell you what those hours bought; cost is what you do with the answer. Three meters, read together, are the whole subject:

1. The scheduler's accounting

Who held which GPUs, from when to when. Slurm's sacct, Kueue's Workload conditions, Ray's state API. It is the bill. It cannot tell you whether anything ran.

2. The device's counters

What the hardware did: on NVIDIA GPUs, DCGM's counters (GPU utilisation, SM activity, tensor activity, memory, power, errors). They say how busy the chip was, not whether the work was useful.

3. The training job's log

What the model got: steps, tokens per second, loss, MFU, time since the last good checkpoint. Only this says whether the run advanced.

Every failure on this page is one meter looking healthy while another does not. A hung job holds every GPU (meter 1 full) at 100% GPU utilisation (meter 2 full) and makes no steps (meter 3 empty). A slow node shows nothing on either of the first two. A cluster whose scheduler reports 92% allocated can be running half its jobs at a fifth of the chip.

What this page measured on the topic's local clusters (Apple M1 Pro, Docker Desktop VM, shared machine; fake GPUs, real schedulers): a Prometheus scraping kind and Slurm while the running example (the topic's tiny GPT, 4 ranks over gloo) went through a healthy run, a slow node, a hang and two queues. The hang alert fired 33 s after a rank froze, while the process-group timeout took 83 s to end the job; the waiting ranks' CPU fell to 0.01 cores, the opposite of what a GPU reports in the same situation; the slow node made the healthy pod look exactly as idle as the slow one; Kueue left 8 of 12 GPUs idle with work waiting because memory quota, not GPUs, ran out. All of it replays on the {{Cluster dashboard|#t-dash}} tab.

What it takes from published work: the same LLM clusters read 97% to 99% median GPU utilisation and about 40% median SM activity (Shanghai AI Lab, section 4); goodput has three definitions that do not agree (section 6); and owning a GPU fleet costs about $1.40 per H100 per calendar hour on stated assumptions, against $3.99 to $12.29 per GPU-hour in published rental list prices (read 2026-10-16), with every assumption shown (section 12 and the {{Capacity planner|#t-cap}}).

2. The words, from zero

Metric, time seriesA named number sampled over time, with labels that say which thing it describes: container_cpu_usage_seconds_total{pod="oc-hang-node-0-1"}. Each distinct set of labels is its own time series.
Counter, gaugeA counter only goes up (CPU seconds used, steps done); you read its rate. A gauge goes up and down (memory in use, GPUs allocated); you read its value.
Exporter, scrapeAn exporter is a small program that serves current values over HTTP at /metrics; Prometheus fetches (scrapes) every exporter on a fixed interval and stores the samples. dcgm-exporter does this for GPUs, cAdvisor (inside the kubelet) for containers.
CardinalityThe number of distinct time series. Labels multiply it: one metric per GPU on 1,000 GPUs is 1,000 series; add a per-job label and every job makes new ones. It is what a metrics system's cost grows with.
SM, warp, occupancyA GPU is made of streaming multiprocessors (SMs: 132 on an H100 SXM). Threads run in groups of 32 called warps; an SM holds up to 64 resident warps. Occupancy is resident warps over that maximum. Taught on {{GPU architecture|n:3c65c17b0d0d810682aae39bd614ec64}}.
DCGMNVIDIA's Data Center GPU Manager: the library and daemon that read a GPU's counters, run health checks and diagnostics, and feed dcgm-exporter.
MFU, HFUModel FLOPs Utilisation: the FLOPs the model needs per step (about 6 per parameter per token) divided by what the GPUs could do at their datasheet peak in that time. Hardware FLOPs Utilisation also counts recomputed work, so it is never lower. Section 5.
Goodput, badputThe share of time (or chip-time) that becomes kept training progress; badput is the rest, broken into named causes. Three published definitions in section 6.
Scheduling efficiencyGPU-hours allocated to jobs over GPU-hours the cluster could have given out. The rest is idle or fragmented. Section 10.
Showback, chargebackShowback reports each team's usage; chargeback bills it against a budget. Both need the scheduler's accounting and an agreed rate. Section 11.
Capacity planningChoosing how many GPUs to have, and whether to own or rent them, from the work you must do and how well you can keep them busy. Section 12.

3. GPU counters: what each one measures

The problem. "Is the GPU busy?" has at least five different answers, and the one every dashboard shows by default is the weakest. How it works: the driver keeps one coarse duty-cycle counter, and DCGM reads finer profiling counters from the chip's performance monitors. Three facts that change what you deploy
The default exporter does not ship SM activity. Its two lines are commented out in default-counters.csv (lines 105 and 106 at 4.8.4); you must enable them, and profiling fields need the exporter to run with SYS_ADMIN.
The profiling fields were renamed. In DCGM 4.6.1, 1002 is DCGM_FI_PROF_SM_UTIL_RATIO and 1004 DCGM_FI_PROF_TENSOR_UTIL_RATIO; the old names stay as aliases (dcgm_fields.h lines 3821 to 3825). Dashboards written for one name may need both.
They are sampled, and multiplexed. DCGM samples at 1 Hz by default (100 ms minimum); counters that cannot be read together are sampled in turns, and "collection at higher frequencies will result in zeroes returned". dcgm-exporter asks DCGM for a new value every 30 s by default (--collect-interval 30000). A 2-second stall between steps is invisible at that resolution; the training log sees it.

How to read them together. SM activity says how much of the chip has work; occupancy says how full each busy SM is (low is normal for large matrix multiplies); tensor activity says how much matmul work is done, which is the closest counter to MFU; DRAM activity says whether the phase is memory-bound. NVIDIA's own rule of thumb for SM activity: "A value of 0.8 or greater is necessary, but not sufficient, for effective use of the GPU. A value less than 0.5 likely indicates ineffective GPU usage." The {{Metrics explainer|#t-met}} runs seven situations (healthy matmuls, too few blocks, launch gaps, an input stall, a memory-bound phase, an exposed all-reduce, a hung collective) through all the definitions at once, before and after each fix.

Predict: a kernel is launched with 26 thread blocks on a 132-SM GPU and runs for the whole one-second sample. What do GPU utilisation and SM activity read?
A kernel was executing the whole time, so the duty-cycle counter reads 100%. Only 26 of 132 SMs have a resident warp, so SM activity is 26/132 = 0.20. DCGM's documentation gives the same example with N/5 blocks. The opposite case, all SMs busy for a fifth of the window, also reads 0.2 SM activity, but 20% utilisation: SM activity alone cannot tell them apart; the pair can.
