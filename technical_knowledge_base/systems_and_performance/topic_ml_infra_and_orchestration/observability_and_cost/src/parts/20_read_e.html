10. The cost of a GPU-hour and of a token

What an owned GPU-hour costs. Owning one GPU costs its price (plus network and storage) spread over its depreciation life, plus energy (kW x average draw x PUE x hours x price per kWh), plus staff and space. Defaults: $33,600 per H100 (Epoch AI, from a 2023 analyst figure), 5.5 years (Meta's server life in its 10-K for 2025), 10.2 kW per 8-GPU DGX H100 (NVIDIA), PUE 1.54 (Uptime Institute 2025 survey average; Google's fleet is 1.09), 9.77 cents per kWh (EIA, US industrial, July 2026), 25% on top for network and storage, 75% average draw and 10% of the price a year for staff, space and repairs (assumptions). That is $12,257 per GPU-year, $1.40 per H100 per calendar hour derived. Rented H100s on 2026-10-16 list prices: $5.97 for an AWS Capacity Block (reserved), $6.88 AWS on demand, $2.27 Azure spot, which can be evicted; on-demand list prices ranged from $3.99 (Lambda) to $12.29 (Azure) per GPU-hour, and the parent's Cost lab holds the dated table.

Why the busy share decides it. Owned and reserved GPUs cost the same every hour, busy or not, so their cost per useful hour is the hourly cost divided by the share of hours that do useful work. On-demand and spot are paid only while used, but on-demand rarely offers a thousand interconnected GPUs at once, and each spot eviction costs the work since the last checkpoint plus the restart (section 5), which the flat line below does not include.

Paid every hour or paid when used: the busy share sets the price of useful work

derived from the defaults above and list prices read 2026-10-16; the default 70% busy share is the planner's utilisation input. At 70%: owned $2.00 and Capacity Block $8.53 per useful GPU-hour (recomputed in src/scripts/recompute.py). Per token: the default run (Llama 3.1 405B, 15.6T tokens, H100 at 40% MFU, 85.5% goodput) gets 500,258 training tokens per paid GPU-hour (585,137 at 100% goodput), so at 100% busy a million training tokens cost $2.80 owned, $11.93 on a Capacity Block, $13.75 on demand, and $4.54 on spot before evictions. Serving cost per token is owned by {{LLM serving in production|n:3f35c17b0d0d818aa406da1f7a784c62}}.

What moves the answer most: the purchase price, the depreciation life (Amazon shortened a subset of its servers from six to five years in 2025, citing "the increased pace of technology development, particularly in the area of artificial intelligence and machine learning"), and the rental price you can actually negotiate: Epoch AI estimates effective 2024 market rents of $1.50 to $2.50 per H100-hour (independent), which makes renting competitive with owning. The calculation leaves out a building with the power (a 16,384-GPU fleet at 1.275 kW per GPU is about 21 MW before cooling), lead times of months, cash up front, the risk that the next generation makes the old one worth less per FLOP before it is paid off, and, for renting, capacity you may not get when you need it. Large buyers negotiate prices that are not published on either side.

Capacity: how many GPUs, for how long

  1. The run. GPUs to meet a deadline, with goodput falling as the job grows (more GPUs, more failures per hour). With the planner's defaults (Llama 3.1 405B, 15.6T tokens, H100, 40% MFU, Llama 3's failure rate of 0.474 interruptions per 1,000 GPU-days, 60 s checkpoint stall, 10 min restart, 90 days) the answer is 14,440 GPUs at 85.5% goodput.
  2. The fleet. The year's demand: the main runs plus everything else (ablations, smaller models, fine-tunes, evaluation: an assumption, defaulted to half again), divided by 8,760 hours and a scheduling efficiency (85%, Meta's level). With one main run a year the deadline sets the fleet, and the year's hours fill only 37% of it: a lab sized for its biggest run has spare hours the rest of the year, which is why labs share clusters between pretraining and everything else.
  3. Own or rent. The fleet for a year costs $177M owned against $755M on Capacity Blocks; owning beats on-demand list prices once more than 20% of the hours are used, on these assumptions.
