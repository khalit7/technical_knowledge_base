7. Alerting on hangs and stragglers

The defaults detect a hang late and a straggler never. A collective that never completes ends at the process-group timeout: 10 minutes for NCCL and 30 minutes for gloo in torch 2.14.1 ({{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}}, section 3, from ProcessGroupNCCL.hpp and Backend.hpp), and every GPU of the job is held all that time. A slow node never times out at all: the job just runs slower. So alert on the training job's own progress, and use the device counters to find the cause. The rules that ran (src/scripts/prom/oc_rules.yml):

# a hang: no step logged for 20 s by a run that has not ended (scale 20 s to a few step times)
- alert: TrainingStalled
  expr: (time() - max by (system, run) (oc_train_last_step_timestamp_seconds)) > 20
        and on (system, run) oc_train_ended == 0
  for: 10s
# a straggler or slow node: under 60% of this run's own best rate in the last 3 minutes
- alert: TrainingSlowdown
  expr: oc:train_steps_per_second < 0.6 * max_over_time(oc:train_steps_per_second[3m])
        and on (system, run) oc_train_ended == 0
  for: 10s
# the node behind it (on GPUs: clock event reasons, Xid, NVLink errors)
- alert: PodThrottled
  expr: oc:pod_throttled_ratio > 0.5
  for: 10s
# quota held and nothing trains
- alert: GpusHeldNoProgress
  expr: sum(oc_kueue_usage{resource="example.com/gpu"}) > 0
        and on () (sum(oc:train_steps_per_second) or vector(0)) == 0
  for: 20s

The progress alerts fired in 33 and 39 s; the defaults would have waited 10 minutes, or for ever

measured, from the ALERTS series at 5-second resolution. Hang (rank 3 frozen at step 150): TrainingStalled fired 33 s after the freeze, GpusHeldNoProgress 53 s, and the 45-second process-group timeout ended the job 83 s after it; NCCL's default would be 600 s, gloo's 1,800 s. Slow node (the pod holding ranks 2 and 3 capped at 0.2 CPU at step 150): TrainingSlowdown fired 39 s after the cap; PodThrottled 69 s, because its first rule read the wrong cgroup and was fixed 51 s after the cap (below). Time to page is at most threshold + for + scrape interval + evaluation interval: 20 + 10 + 5 + 5 s here, 33 s measured.

The cost of the threshold. GpusHeldNoProgress went pending at the start of every job here: quota admitted, no step yet (start-up took about 7 s), and only its 20-second for: kept it from firing. A real job takes minutes to start (image pull, data, compile, checkpoint load), so a progress alert needs a grace period after start ("no step for N x the job's own median step time, after the first step"), or it pages at every launch; and a stall threshold shorter than the longest legitimate pause (a checkpoint save, an evaluation) pages on healthy jobs. Set both from the job's own step and checkpoint times.

Stragglers. After the pod holding ranks 2 and 3 was capped, the healthy pod's CPU fell to the same 0.17 cores, because synchronous training makes everyone wait for the slowest: utilisation could not name the slow node; the throttling counter of its cgroup could. The first version of the rule read the container cgroup and returned 0 while the pod was throttled 70% of its periods: the cap was on the pod cgroup, and throttling is counted where the limit is hit. Check which cgroup (or, on GPUs, which clock event reason) carries the limit before trusting a zero. On GPUs the equivalents are the clock event reasons, power and temperature per GPU, and per-rank timing of the same work: NVIDIA's NVRx straggler detector scores each rank's section and kernel times against the best rank ({{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}}, section 8, replays it), and ByteDance's MegaScale reports finding about 0.5% straggler machines with CUDA-event timers ({{MegaScale sec. 5.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15627v1}}, operator).

8. Scheduling efficiency: GPUs idle with work waiting

Before a job can waste a GPU-hour, the cluster has to hand it out. Scheduling efficiency is allocated GPU-hours over capacity; what is left is idle (nobody asked) or fragmented (somebody asked, but the free capacity was the wrong shape). The parent's {{Scheduling lab and Cost lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} measured backfill and gang-scheduling losses; this page measured the two kinds of fragmentation a dashboard has to catch.

GPUs free and a job waits: in Kueue the memory quota ran out, in Slurm the free GPUs were split across nodes

measured. Kueue: a ClusterQueue with 12 GPUs, 4 CPUs and 3 GiB; three jobs of 4 GPUs, 2 CPUs and 1,800 MiB. Memory admits one at a time: couldn't assign flavors to pod set node: insufficient unused quota for memory in flavor default-flavor, 528Mi more needed. GPU quota used: 4 of 12, the whole time; admission waits 0, 39 and 76 s (Kueue events), the third-submitted job admitted before the second. Slurm: a 2-node job holds 2 of 4 GPUs on each of two nodes, a 1-node job fills the third; the next 1-node 4-GPU job waits with reason Resources for 66 s while 4 GPUs are free. Allocated over total reads 67%.

That 67% is exactly what community Slurm exporters report as "GPU utilization": "allocation ratio (alloc/total) per GPU type" (vpenso/prometheus-slurm-exporter README), never device utilisation. What to put on the dashboard: allocated over capacity per GPU type and partition; pending jobs by reason and their age (Kueue's kueue_pending_workloads and kueue_admission_wait_time_seconds, Slurm's pending reasons); the largest free block (GPUs free on one node, nodes free in one rack), because that decides whether the next big job can start; and an alert for work waiting while capacity is free (PendingWithFreeGpus fired for about a minute in the Kueue test, SlurmPendingWithFreeGpus for about a minute in the Slurm test). The fix is in the scheduler: job shapes that tile the nodes (whole nodes for multi-node jobs), every quota resource sized to the job shape (or the cheapest resource decides how many GPUs you use), topology-aware placement, backfill with honest time limits ({{Slurm|n:3c65c17b0d0d81889c32cd050bbbadd3}}, {{Kubernetes for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce}}). At scale, Meta's RSC clusters averaged 83% and 85% allocation over 11 months, and Google reports scheduling goodput above 95% for TPU jobs of all sizes thanks to preemption settings (both operator).

9. Chargeback and quotas

A shared cluster is a budget split between teams; without a bill, the team that queues most wins. Showback and chargeback are computed from the scheduler's accounting, and the main design choice is to charge for allocated GPU-hours, not used ones: allocation is what the others could not have, the scheduler knows it exactly, and it gives teams a reason to release GPUs, fix hangs and size jobs well. Charging for SM activity would reward a team for shrinking its hang detection instead of its allocation; show utilisation next to the bill as a quality signal.

Slurm. Each partition has TRESBillingWeights; a job's billing is the weighted sum of what it was allocated, used for fairshare and limits and reported by sreport. The local cluster bills a GPU at 1.0 and a CPU at 0.25. Its accounting for the Slurm test (sacct -X -P -o JobID,User,Account,...,ElapsedRaw,AllocTRES):

Two Slurm details explain the table: billing "is stored as an integer and truncated if needed" ({{Slurm TRES|https://slurm.schedmd.com/tres.html}}), so bob's 4 GPUs and 1 CPU, which should be 4.25, show billing=4 (use weights that give whole numbers for your job shapes, or bill from AllocTRES directly); and on Kubernetes nothing computes the bill by default: it is the Workload's admitted resources times its Admitted-to-Finished time, which you export yourself.

Accounting commands on Slurm, Kubernetes and Ray

PriorityFlags=MAX_TRES_GRES sums billable GPUs but takes the maximum of the other resources, so a job cannot pay twice for CPUs and memory that come with its GPUs. The monthly query: sreport -t hours -T gres/gpu cluster AccountUtilizationByUser start=2026-09-01 end=2026-10-01 (composed from the documented options; sreport rolls usage up hourly, so it shows nothing for the last few minutes, as {{Slurm|n:3c65c17b0d0d81889c32cd050bbbadd3}} found). Kubernetes: quotas are Kueue ClusterQueues (nominal quota per flavor), budgets between teams are cohorts with borrowing and lending limits, and the bill is the Workload's admitted resources times its Admitted-to-Finished time, which no tool computes by default: export it ({{Kubernetes for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce}} covers cohorts and fair sharing). Ray has no accounting of its own beyond its state API; on KubeRay the Kubernetes path applies.
