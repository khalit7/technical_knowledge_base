2. GPU counters: what each one counts

"Is the GPU busy?" has at least five answers. The driver keeps one coarse duty-cycle counter (GPU utilisation, field 203); DCGM reads finer profiling counters from the chip's performance monitors: SM activity (1002), occupancy (1003), tensor activity (1004), DRAM activity (1005), NVLink and PCIe bytes, plus power, temperature, clock event reasons and Xid errors. Fig. 1 applies the first three definitions to one H100, situation by situation.

  1. One H100: 132 SMs, three meters. Each block on the floor is one streaming multiprocessor (SM); its height will be the warps resident on it, out of the 64 an SM can hold. On the right, three DCGM meters over one sample window: GPU utilisation (203, the number nvidia-smi shows), SM activity (1002) and tensor activity (1004).
  2. Healthy matmuls: every SM busy. Large BF16 matrix multiplies on all 132 SMs, fed from device memory (the plate behind, 35% DRAM activity). GPU util 100%, SM activity 1.00, tensor activity 0.75. Only about 12 of 64 warp slots are used per SM, so occupancy (0.19) is low, and that is fine.
  3. Too few blocks: 26 of 132 SMs. A kernel launched with 26 thread blocks on a 132-SM GPU runs for the whole one-second sample. A kernel was executing the whole time, so the readings are GPU util 100%, SM activity about 0.2: 26/132 = 0.20 (DCGM's documentation gives the same example with N/5 blocks), and tensor activity 0.14. The opposite case, all SMs busy for a fifth of the window, also reads 0.2 SM activity but 20% utilisation: SM activity alone cannot tell them apart; the pair can.
  4. A hung collective: 8 SMs polling. A peer froze inside an all-reduce; the communication kernel stays resident on a few SMs (8 here), polling for data that never arrives, and nothing moves on NVLink. GPU util still reads 100%; SM activity 0.06, tensor activity 0.00. One SM would be enough: the definition asks only whether a kernel was executing.
  5. Memory-bound: every SM full, tensor pipes idle. An optimiser or elementwise phase: 48 of 64 warps on every SM (occupancy 0.75) and the memory interface busy 90% of the time. GPU util 100%, SM activity 1.00, tensor activity 0.00: a full SM is not a working tensor pipe.
  6. Four situations, one GPU utilisation. The three meters of each situation side by side: GPU utilisation reads 100% in all four, and only SM and tensor activity separate them. That is why a GPU dashboard needs the profiling counters, and why the job's own log (section 4) still decides whether it trained.

Three facts that change what you deploy
The default exporter does not ship SM activity. Its two lines are commented out in default-counters.csv (lines 105 and 106 at 4.8.4); enable them (and occupancy), and run the exporter with SYS_ADMIN, which profiling fields need.
The profiling fields were renamed. In DCGM 4.6.1, 1002 is DCGM_FI_PROF_SM_UTIL_RATIO and 1004 DCGM_FI_PROF_TENSOR_UTIL_RATIO; the old names stay as aliases (dcgm_fields.h lines 3821 to 3825), so dashboards may need both.
They are sampled, and multiplexed. DCGM samples at 1 Hz by default (100 ms minimum); counters that cannot be read together are sampled in turns, and "collection at higher frequencies will result in zeroes returned". dcgm-exporter asks for a new value every 30 s by default (--collect-interval 30000). A 2-second stall between steps is invisible at that resolution; the training log sees it.

Seven situations, and what to check next

How to read them together: SM activity says how much of the chip has work; occupancy how full each busy SM is; tensor activity how much matmul work is done; DRAM activity whether the phase is memory-bound. NVIDIA's own rule of thumb for SM activity: "A value of 0.8 or greater is necessary, but not sufficient, for effective use of the GPU. A value less than 0.5 likely indicates ineffective GPU usage." 3. The gap at fleet scale

Three different numbers are all called GPU utilisation: the share of GPUs allocated to jobs, the share of time any kernel ran, and the share of SMs with work. Published fleets show how far apart they sit.

Predict: in the Shanghai AI Lab's two LLM-development clusters (4,704 A100s, March to August 2023), median GPU utilisation (the duty cycle) was 97% and 99%. What was the median DCGM SM activity on the same clusters?
"The median SM activity in both clusters is approximately 40%" ({{Hu et al., NSDI 2024|https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07648v2}}, operator). The duty cycle sees that a kernel is running; it cannot see that most SMs are idle or waiting inside it.

Never compare two fleets' "utilisation" without asking which of the three it is: Meta's 83% is allocation, Philly's 52% a duty cycle, PAI's 0.042 SM usage per task instance. Acme is the one study that reports two kinds on the same clusters, which is why it is the one to quote. A high allocation number is a good sign for the scheduler and says nothing about the jobs: Philly's GPUs were mostly allocated and their duty cycle averaged 52%.
