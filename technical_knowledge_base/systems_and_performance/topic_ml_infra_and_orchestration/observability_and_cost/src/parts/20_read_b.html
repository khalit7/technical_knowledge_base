2. GPU counters: what each one counts

"Is the GPU busy?" has at least five answers. The driver keeps one coarse duty-cycle counter (GPU utilisation, field 203); DCGM reads finer profiling counters from the chip's performance monitors: SM activity (1002), occupancy (1003), tensor activity (1004), DRAM activity (1005), NVLink and PCIe bytes, plus power, temperature, clock event reasons and Xid errors. The seven situations below apply their definitions; the fields themselves, quoted, are in the closed block.

Three facts that change what you deploy
The default exporter does not ship SM activity. Its two lines are commented out in default-counters.csv (lines 105 and 106 at 4.8.4); enable them (and occupancy), and run the exporter with SYS_ADMIN, which profiling fields need.
The profiling fields were renamed. In DCGM 4.6.1, 1002 is DCGM_FI_PROF_SM_UTIL_RATIO and 1004 DCGM_FI_PROF_TENSOR_UTIL_RATIO; the old names stay as aliases (dcgm_fields.h lines 3821 to 3825), so dashboards may need both.
They are sampled, and multiplexed. DCGM samples at 1 Hz by default (100 ms minimum); counters that cannot be read together are sampled in turns, and "collection at higher frequencies will result in zeroes returned". dcgm-exporter asks for a new value every 30 s by default (--collect-interval 30000). A 2-second stall between steps is invisible at that resolution; the training log sees it.
Predict: a kernel is launched with 26 thread blocks on a 132-SM GPU and runs for the whole one-second sample. What do GPU utilisation and SM activity read?
A kernel was executing the whole time, so the duty cycle reads 100%. Only 26 of 132 SMs have a resident warp, so SM activity is 26/132 = 0.20 (DCGM's documentation gives the same example with N/5 blocks). The opposite case, all SMs busy for a fifth of the window, also reads 0.2 SM activity but 20% utilisation: SM activity alone cannot tell them apart; the pair can. It is the "Too few blocks" situation below.

The point is which counters can tell two situations apart, not the second decimal.

How to read them together: SM activity says how much of the chip has work; occupancy how full each busy SM is; tensor activity how much matmul work is done; DRAM activity whether the phase is memory-bound. NVIDIA's own rule of thumb for SM activity: "A value of 0.8 or greater is necessary, but not sufficient, for effective use of the GPU. A value less than 0.5 likely indicates ineffective GPU usage." 3. The gap at fleet scale

Three different numbers are all called GPU utilisation: the share of GPUs allocated to jobs, the share of time any kernel ran, and the share of SMs with work. Published fleets show how far apart they sit.

Predict: in the Shanghai AI Lab's two LLM-development clusters (4,704 A100s, March to August 2023), median GPU utilisation (the duty cycle) was 97% and 99%. What was the median DCGM SM activity on the same clusters?
"The median SM activity in both clusters is approximately 40%" ({{Hu et al., NSDI 2024|https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07648v2}}, operator). The duty cycle sees that a kernel is running; it cannot see that most SMs are idle or waiting inside it.

Never compare two fleets' "utilisation" without asking which of the three it is: Meta's 83% is allocation, Philly's 52% a duty cycle, PAI's 0.042 SM usage per task instance. Acme is the one study that reports two kinds on the same clusters, which is why it is the one to quote. A high allocation number is a good sign for the scheduler and says nothing about the jobs: Philly's GPUs were mostly allocated and their duty cycle averaged 52%.
