5. Gang scheduling: all of a job or none of it

The problem. A distributed training job is useless until every rank is running: rank 0 waits at the rendezvous for the others (the {{Launch lab|#t-launch}} shows it), and every GPU it holds meanwhile does nothing. A scheduler that places a job's pieces one by one can give a job part of what it needs. With two or more such jobs in a full cluster, each holds part and waits for the rest, which only the others could free: a deadlock. Gang scheduling starts all of a job's pieces together or none of them.

Who has it. In Slurm a job's allocation is one object: it gets all its nodes at once or stays pending, so gangs come free. Kubernetes' default scheduler places pods, one scheduling cycle per pod, and knows nothing of the job they belong to. Upstream is adding a native path (KEP-4671, "Gang Scheduling using Workload Object": alpha in v1.35, its GenericWorkload feature gate beta in v1.37), but the gate is off by default: the v1.37.0 kube-scheduler --help here lists GenericWorkload=true|false (BETA - default=false). Until then gangs come from an add-on: Kueue (admits a whole workload against quota, then lets the pods be created), Volcano (its own scheduler, with a PodGroup and minMember), or the coscheduling plugin of scheduler-plugins. In Ray, a placement group reserves all its bundles atomically (section 7).

The deadlock, then the fix, on real Kubernetes measured

Three JobSets, sc-a, sc-b and sc-c, each the running example (2 pods, 2 ranks per pod) with each pod asking for a whole worker's 4 fake GPUs. Three workers, so the cluster holds exactly one and a half jobs. Without Kueue, each pod was created behind a scheduling gate and the gates were removed in the order a0, b0, c0, a1, b1, c1, one second apart: the order in which the default scheduler would see them if three job controllers created pods at the same moment. With Kueue, the same three JobSets were created together with a queue label and nothing else.

Without gang scheduling the default scheduler did exactly its job, one pod at a time: a0, b0 and c0 each found a free worker and were bound; a1, b1 and c1 found none. Every job held half of what it needed. Four minutes later nothing had moved:

Only an admitted workload is unsuspended, so its pods are created only when all of them are covered. Note what this checks: quota, not which node each pod will land on. 6. Quotas and borrowing

The problem. Two teams each own half of a cluster. If team B is idle, should team A be allowed to use B's half? Yes, or the cluster sits half empty; but when B comes back, B must get its half quickly. Quota is a team's guaranteed amount; borrowing lets a team use another's unused quota; reclaiming takes it back by preempting the borrower.

In Kueue. A ClusterQueue holds a team's nominalQuota per resource; ClusterQueues in the same cohort lend unused quota to each other (up to an optional borrowingLimit); preemption.reclaimWithinCohort lets a queue preempt workloads of cohort members that are borrowing. Namespaces submit to a LocalQueue that points at a ClusterQueue. In Slurm the same ideas are QOS and association limits (GrpTRES) plus partition preemption; Slurm has no lending between limits, so idle capacity is used through a low-priority preemptible partition or QOS instead.

Team A borrows, team B reclaims measured

ClusterQueues sc-cq-a and sc-cq-b, 6 fake GPUs of quota each, in cohort sc-cohort, both with reclaimWithinCohort: Any. Team A submits three single-pod jobs of 4 GPUs (12 in all: its 6 plus 6 borrowed from B); 15 s later team B submits one job of 4 GPUs.

What happened. Kueue admitted all three of team A's jobs within 4 s, the second and third by borrowing (Flavors considered: main: default-flavor(Fit;borrow=1)). Eight seconds after team B's job was created its pod was running: Kueue evicted team A's newest borrowing workload (EvictedDueToPreempted ... due to reclamation within the cohort), waited for its pod to stop (WaitingForPreemptedWorkloads), then admitted team B (QuotaReserved ... wait time since queued was 6s). When team B's job finished, team A's evicted job was readmitted by borrowing again, 54 s after it was queued. Borrowing kept every GPU busy, and reclaim gave B its share within seconds; the price was one evicted job, which a training job pays in work since its last checkpoint.

7. Placement and topology

The problem. Which machines a job gets matters as much as how many. The gradient all-reduce of every step crosses the network between nodes; a job spread across distant switches pays more for each collective than one packed under one switch, and a job split across many partly used nodes leaves fragments nobody else can use. Topology-aware placement keeps a job's nodes close; packing fills nodes before opening new ones; spreading does the opposite (for fault tolerance, or memory bandwidth).

Ray placement groups on the lab's Ray cluster measured

Two worker nodes with 2 CPUs and 2 fake GPUs each. A placement group reserves a list of bundles (here each bundle is 1 CPU + 1 fake GPU, what one Ray Train worker asks for) with a strategy; Ray reserves all bundles together or none (its two-phase commit), and actors or tasks are then placed into the bundles.
