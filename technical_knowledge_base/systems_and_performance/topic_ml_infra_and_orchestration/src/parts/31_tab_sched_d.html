The pattern: Slurm was built for batch jobs that hold machines for a time limit, so queueing, backfill, fairshare and gangs are one integrated plugin chain. Kubernetes was built for services that run forever, so its scheduler places pods and everything job-shaped is an add-on; Kueue is the add-on that turns it into a batch system without replacing the scheduler. Ray schedules inside one application and leaves multi-user policy to whatever runs Ray.

JobArrayTaskLimit: an array's %N throttle (here %2) is holding the other tasks; nothing is wrong. Ray

ray status lists pending and infeasible resource demands; a placement group that can never be satisfied stays PENDING forever, with the autoscaler's No available node types can fulfill resource request (section 7) as the only clue. ray list placement-groups shows each group's state.

Asking for far more time than the job needs
The backfill plan uses your limit. A 2-hour job that asks for 24 hours cannot fit in a 3-hour hole before a reservation and waits behind everything. Ask for the expected time plus a margin, and checkpoint.
Reading (Priority) as "something is broken"
It means a job ahead of yours is waiting. Look at that job (squeue --start, sprio); your job moves when it does, or when backfill finds a hole that fits your limit.
Free GPUs that nothing can use
GPUs come with CPUs and memory. A job that takes all CPUs or memory of a node strands its remaining GPUs (section 1's first attempt). Request CPUs and memory per GPU (--cpus-per-gpu, --mem-per-gpu, or tasks with --gpus-per-task) and check AllocTRES.
Running multi-pod training on plain Kubernetes
Without gang admission, partial jobs hold GPUs while waiting at the rendezvous (section 5). Use Kueue, Volcano or the coscheduling plugin, and set waitForPodsReady so a job that cannot fully start gives its pods back.
Quota without capacity
Kueue admits against quota, not nodes. If the ClusterQueue's quota is larger than the healthy capacity (a drained node, fragmented GPUs), admitted workloads end up with Pending pods. Keep quota equal to what nodes can deliver, or use TAS, which checks node capacity too.
Borrowing without preemption
If reclaimWithinCohort is Never, a team that lent its quota waits until the borrower's jobs end, which for training can be days. Allow reclaim, and make borrowers' jobs checkpoint and resume.

10. Interview questions

What is backfill, and why does it need time limits?

Backfill starts a lower-priority job ahead of its turn when it fits in idle resources and will finish (by its time limit) before the reserved start of higher-priority jobs, or uses resources they will not need. The scheduler cannot know real runtimes, so it plans with limits; over-asked limits make jobs look too long to fit in any hole.

EASY or conservative backfill?

EASY reserves only for the first waiting job: more jobs can jump, so usually lower mean wait, but later jobs' predicted starts can slip. Conservative reserves for every waiting job: predictable starts, slightly less packing. Slurm is conservative within bf_max_job_test and bf_window; its reservations block whole nodes.

A 2-GPU job has been pending for hours on a cluster with free GPUs. What do you check?

The reason (squeue): Priority means it is behind a waiting job, Resources that it does not fit now. Its time limit against the hole before the next reservation (squeue --start for the jobs ahead). Whether the free GPUs come with free CPUs and memory (scontrol show node: AllocTRES). Limits on its account or QOS (reasons such as AssocGrpGRES). Its fairshare (sprio, sshare).

Why does distributed training need gang scheduling, and how do you get it on Kubernetes?

All ranks must run at once; a partial allocation holds GPUs that do nothing, and several partial jobs can deadlock the cluster (section 5 reproduced it). On Kubernetes: Kueue (admit the whole workload against quota, with waitForPodsReady), Volcano (PodGroup minMember), the coscheduling plugin, or, from v1.35 onward, the upstream Workload API behind the GenericWorkload gate.

Design the scheduling for a 1,000-GPU lab with three teams.

Gang allocation of whole nodes for training; a quota per team equal to its budget, with borrowing of idle quota and reclaim by preemption (Kueue cohorts, or Slurm accounts with a preemptible scavenger QOS); fairshare with a half-life of days so heavy weeks are paid back; time limits enforced and backfill on, with a short-job partition for debugging and evaluation; topology-aware placement for jobs above one switch's worth of nodes; every preemptible job checkpointing and requeueing automatically; dashboards of queue wait per team and of allocated-but-idle GPUs (the {{Cost lab|#t-cost}}).

How is fairshare computed in Slurm?

Usage (billed resources times seconds) decays with a half-life; Fair Tree compares, level by level in the account tree, shares over usage (S/U), ranks users depth first, and gives each user rank / number of users; priority adds weighted factors (fairshare, age, size, partition, QOS). A user in a team that used a lot ranks below every user of a team that used less.
