9. Goodput: how often to checkpoint, given how your scheduler recovers

Goodput is "the time spent computing useful new steps over the elapsed time of the training job" (Gemini's definition, {{Gemini 1.0, section 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11805}}). Throughput counts every step computed, including the ones redone after a failure; goodput counts each step once. Three things eat into it: time spent writing checkpoints (C each time), work redone since the last checkpoint after each failure, and the recovery time R (detect, reschedule, start, reload). Checkpoint often and you pay C often; rarely, and each failure costs more rework. Young's classic answer for the interval of work between checkpoints is τ ≈ √(2CM), with M the job's mean time between failures; Daly refined it to √(2C(M+R)) + C when R is not small. The {{reliability page|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22}} derives both and simulates them; here the new input is R, the thing this lab measured, and the point is how much the recovery path matters next to the checkpoint interval.

Reading the calculator. At Llama 3's failure rate a 16,384-GPU job fails every 3.1 hours. With a 1-minute blocking checkpoint and 10 minutes to recover, the best interval is about 19 minutes of work and goodput is about 85% calculation. Cutting recovery from 10 minutes to 1 raises it to about 89%, more than the whole difference between a 10-minute and a 40-minute interval (84% and 83% against the optimum of 85%). Near the optimum the curve is flat; recovery time is not. That is why Gemini kept model replicas in memory and spare cubes, why DeepSeek checkpoints "typically every 5 minutes" asynchronously (see the {{Reading tab|#t-read}}), and why the measured differences in section 2 (seconds for an in-job restart, 2.5 minutes for a Slurm requeue, a person for a silent Kubernetes node) matter more at 16,000 GPUs than at 4.

10. Common mistakes and interview questions

Shipping a job spec with no retries
failurePolicy.maxRestarts: 0 (JobSet), FailureConfig(max_failures=0) (Ray Train) and plain torchrun (default --max-restarts=0) all turn one crashed rank into a dead run: measured on all three in section 2. Slurm never retries a job that failed by itself; resubmit it from a wrapper or let torchrun restart inside the allocation.
Trusting a restart path you have never exercised
Our 2-node torchrun --max-restarts=2 restarts failed with Gloo connectFullMesh failed with ... timed out connecting: SO_ERROR: Connection refused until TORCH_DISABLE_SHARE_RDZV_TCP_STORE=1 was set. Kill a rank in a test job before the real run needs the restart.
Reading torchrun's "Root Cause" as the culprit in a hang
It names the first process that exited with an error, which in a hang is a healthy rank that timed out (section 5). The {{Launch lab|#t-launch}} saw the same on Kubernetes: each pod's summary named a waiting rank (rank 0 on pod 0, rank 2 on pod 1) while the stalled rank 3 appeared only as exitcode: -15 (SIGTERM), killed by its own agent. Find the rank everyone waited for, and dump its stack.
Assuming a restart resumes from the newest checkpoint on disk
With Ray Train's own checkpoint API it may not. The {{Ray|n:3fc5c17b0d0d811cba13f3aeccf8724b}} child page crashed rank 3 at step 120 with checkpoints every 50 steps: in 3 of 3 runs the new worker group restored step 50, although every worker had reported step 100 and its files were already in storage. Ray Train v2 registers a checkpoint only when the controller holds that report from every worker (report_handler.py in Ray 2.59.0: the method at line 40 says "Wait for all workers to report training results", and line 63, if not all(self._training_report_queues): return, skips any poll in which one worker's report is missing); the crashed rank's report never left its process. 70 steps were redone instead of 20, and the step-100 directory stayed behind as an orphan that num_to_keep did not delete. Log which checkpoint a restart loaded, and compare it with the newest on disk.
Leaving the timeouts at their defaults without knowing them
Slurm notices a dead node after SlurmdTimeout (300 s default), a hung NCCL collective errors after 10 minutes (PyTorch 2.14), Kubernetes evicts pods from an unreachable node after 300 s. Each is idle time on every GPU of the job, per failure. Choose them; keep them above your longest legitimate pause (checkpoint save, evaluation).
Assuming Kubernetes recovers from a dead node by itself
A pod on a node whose kubelet is gone stays Terminating; a Job waiting for it never fails over (measured: stuck for over 7 minutes until the node.kubernetes.io/out-of-service taint). Automate the taint with a node-health controller.
A health check that only exits non-zero
Slurm ignores the exit code of HealthCheckProgram; the script must run scontrol update ... state=drain itself (section 7).
Hunting a straggler by the longest all-reduce
The rank with the longest wait is a victim; the slow rank waits least (12 ms against 63 ms, section 6).
Hosting the elastic rendezvous on any node
With the c10d backend one agent hosts the store; lose that node and every agent dies with RendezvousConnectionError (section 8).

Interview questions (short answers; expand)

A 2,048-GPU job at Llama 3's failure rate, checkpoints take 30 s to write. How often does it fail and how often should it checkpoint?
MTBF scales as 1/N: 3.09 h x 16,384 / 2,048 = about 24.7 h. Young: τ = √(2 x 30 s x 89,100 s) = about 2,310 s, roughly every 39 minutes of work; checkpoint overhead C/τ is about 1.3% and expected rework τ/2M about 1.3% calculation. Then say what would change it: async checkpointing shrinks C, slow recovery adds R/M per failure.
A node dies mid-run. What happens on Slurm, on Kubernetes with JobSet, on Ray Train?
Peers error at once or at the collective timeout. Slurm notices after SlurmdTimeout, sets the node DOWN, requeues a --requeue job (held about 2 minutes) onto healthy nodes. Kubernetes marks the node NotReady after 50 s; with JobSet maxRestarts above 0 the JobSet recreates its pods, but a pod on a dead kubelet blocks that until the node is tainted out of service. Ray marks the node dead after its health checks (30 s here) and Ray Train restarts the worker group if max_failures allows, but only once enough resources exist: without a spare it waits.
A job is stuck at step 40,000: no error, GPUs at 0% except one. What do you do?
It is a hang: everyone waits in a collective for one rank. Dump stacks of all ranks (py-spy, or the NCCL flight recorder dump), find the rank not in the collective, check its node (Xid errors in the kernel log, ECC, NVLink, the NIC), drain it, restart from the last checkpoint. Afterwards, shorten the collective timeout or add a watchdog so this costs minutes, not hours.
Your resumed run's loss differs from the original in the 7th digit. Bug?
Probably not, if it is a last-bit difference with no jump: the reduction order changed (here DDP rebuilds its buckets after a process's first step; on GPUs, nondeterministic kernels). A jump at the resume step is a bug: optimiser state, LR schedule step, data position or RNG state was not restored.
Design failure handling for a 1,000-GPU training platform.
Prevent: burn-in and periodic health checks that drain or taint bad nodes automatically. Detect: per-job heartbeats, collective timeouts of minutes not tens of minutes, flight recorder, per-rank step-time telemetry for stragglers. Recover: job-level retries with a fresh rendezvous, spare nodes so a restart never waits, asynchronous checkpoints sized by τ ≈ √(2CM) (at 1,000 GPUs and Llama 3's rate, M is about 51 h), checkpoint load path tested. Measure goodput as the platform's headline number, and break each failure's cost into detection, waiting, start-up and rework.
Go deeper (optional): the child page {{Fault tolerance at scale: failures, checkpoint/restart and elastic training|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}} (its Failure taxonomies tab puts Llama 3's table beside Meta's 2026 32K-GPU table and others, Recovery designs runs this lab's goodput model on more designs, Straggler replay measures detection); Ray Train's restore behaviour on {{Ray: tasks, actors, the object store and Ray Train|n:3fc5c17b0d0d811cba13f3aeccf8724b}} (Train failure replay); Kubernetes failure policies on {{Kubernetes for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce}}.
