What "GPU utilisation 100%" does and does not mean

nvidia-smi's utilisation, and DCGM_FI_DEV_GPU_UTIL that dashboards plot from it, is defined as the "Percent of time over the past sample period during which one or more kernels was executing on the GPU" (nvidia-smi documentation). One kernel on one of the chip's SMs (streaming multiprocessors, its compute units: 132 on an H100 SXM, {{GPU architecture|n:3c65c17b0d0d810682aae39bd614ec64}}) counts the same as a kernel filling all of them. DCGM's profiling metrics look inside: SM activity is "the fraction of time at least one warp was active on a multiprocessor, averaged over all multiprocessors", and its documentation's own example is that a kernel using a fifth of the SMs for the whole interval and a kernel using all of them for a fifth of the interval both read 0.2 (DCGM feature overview, profiling metrics, read 2026-10-16).

Predict: a 64-GPU job's dashboard shows every GPU at 100% utilisation for the last 20 minutes, but the training log has not printed a step in that time. What is the most likely state?
A collective kernel waiting for a peer that never arrives is still a kernel executing, so the definition reads 100% while nothing is computed (case C). Check SM activity and tensor activity (both near zero), the step counter, and the hung rank's stack (the {{Launch lab|#t-launch}} shows how: py-spy, the flight recorder). The job then holds every GPU until the collective times out: 10 minutes by default for NCCL in torch 2.14.

Dashboards that tell the truth about cost

Three sources, and each answers a different question. The scheduler's accounting says who held what and for how long (the bill). The GPU's counters say what the hardware did. The training log says what the model got. Only the three together say what you paid for useful work.

Common mistakes

Reporting GPU utilisation as efficiency
nvidia-smi's number is a duty cycle of "any kernel running". A training job can read 100% while hung, or while one SM in 132 works. Report MFU (from the log) and SM and tensor activity (from DCGM) instead.
Pricing a run at ideal GPU-hours
6ND divided by peak times MFU is the floor. Goodput (failures, restarts, checkpoint stalls) and idle reservation multiply it: the calculator's Llama 3 405B defaults add about on top of the ideal hours.
Comparing an on-demand price with a cluster price
A single 8-GPU node on demand and a reserved InfiniBand cluster are different products: Lambda's cluster price per GPU is above its single-node on-demand price. Price the thing a multi-node run needs.
Buying a reservation and not watching the queue
A reservation is paid whether jobs run or not, so idle, fragmentation and deadlocks become direct cost. Measure scheduling efficiency (allocated over capacity) every week, and look for jobs that hold GPUs with no steps logged.
Long collective timeouts on big jobs
A hang holds every GPU of the job until the timeout fires. On 1,024 GPUs a 30-minute default is 512 GPU-hours per hang. Shorter timeouts and a watchdog on the step counter cut it, at the risk of killing a slow but healthy first step.
Treating short test jobs as representative
The 200-step job here spent most of its allocation starting up. Startup is fixed, so its share shrinks with job length; measure efficiency on runs as long as the real ones, or subtract startup explicitly.

Interview questions

How would you estimate the cost of training a 70B model on 15T tokens?
6 x 70e9 x 15e12 = 6.3e24 FLOPs. At 40% MFU on H100 (989.5 TFLOP/s dense BF16) that is about 4.4M GPU-hours ideal. Then add goodput (failures, restarts, checkpoints: 85 to 95% at scale) and idle reservation, choose the price kind (reserved for a run of weeks), and say which inputs dominate the uncertainty: MFU and price. Use the calculator above to check.
Your 512-GPU cluster shows 95% allocated but training throughput is down 30% this month. Where do you look?
Allocation is not work. Per job: step time and tokens per second against last month; SM and tensor activity per GPU (a straggler or a throttled node shows as one low GPU); jobs with GPUs held and no steps (hangs, deadlocks at rendezvous); failure and restart counts (goodput); checkpoint stall time (storage got slower?). Then the queue: did fragmentation rise (more odd-sized jobs)?
Reserved or on-demand for a 3-week pretraining run on 1,024 GPUs?
Reserved capacity (capacity blocks, cluster contracts): on-demand rarely guarantees 1,024 interconnected GPUs at once, and a run needs all of them together. The trade-off is that a reservation is paid through every failure, hang and idle gap, so goodput work pays off directly. Spot only for work that checkpoints often and restarts cheaply (evaluation, small fine-tunes, data processing).
What does "GPU utilisation 100%" mean?
That at least one kernel was executing during the sample period, nothing more. It says nothing about how many SMs worked or whether the work was useful: a hung all-reduce reads 100%. Use SM activity, tensor activity and MFU.
How many GPUs do you need to train 8B parameters on 15T tokens in 30 days?
7.2e23 FLOPs; at 40% MFU on H100 about 506K GPU-hours ideal; over 720 hours that is about 700 GPUs at 100% goodput, around 780 at 90%. Check it with the deadline field of the calculator and the hardware topic's planner.
