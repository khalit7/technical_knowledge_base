Interview questions

"Slurm or Kubernetes for a new 1,000-GPU training cluster?"

Ask who uses it and what else runs on it. Mostly researchers running long multi-node jobs, with a small ops team: Slurm (gang allocation, fairshare, backfill and topology placement built in, little to operate). A company that already runs Kubernetes and wants training, inference and pipelines on one pool: Kubernetes plus Kueue (or KAI) and JobSet or TrainJob. Wanting both is common enough that SchedMD (Slinky) and CoreWeave (SUNK) sell Slurm running on Kubernetes. Ray goes inside either for Python-level orchestration.

"Why is the default Kubernetes scheduler not enough for distributed training?"

It places one pod at a time and has no queue or quota between teams. A 16-pod job can get 10 pods placed and wait forever for the rest while holding the GPUs (two such jobs deadlock). You add all-or-nothing admission and queues: Kueue keeps the default scheduler and admits whole jobs against quotas; Volcano and KAI replace the scheduler with gang scheduling built in. The Scheduling lab shows the deadlock and its fix.

"A team's manifests use PyTorchJob. What do you tell them?"

Training Operator v1 is in maintenance on its release-1.9 branch, and Kueue calls its integration legacy and deprecated. New work should use Kubeflow Trainer v2's TrainJob (run as a JobSet) or a JobSet directly. Note Trainer v2 still calls itself alpha, and Kueue 0.20 also stopped serving its own v1beta1 API, so check both upgrades together.

"How do you decide whether an infrastructure dependency is safe to adopt?"

Owner's notice first (README banners, shutdown pages), then the archived flag, then the date of the last release, and only then commit activity. Then who owns it and how it makes money: the 2024 to 2026 record has a tracker shut down three months after its acquisition (Neptune), an open-source object store archived in favour of a commercial edition (MinIO), and a vendor platform with no release since March 2025 (Determined). Prefer projects under a foundation (Kubernetes, Kueue, Ray in the PyTorch Foundation, Airflow) and keep data exportable.

"Where does Ray fit if you already have Slurm or Kubernetes?"

Ray is not a cluster scheduler between teams; it schedules tasks and actors inside one Ray cluster that Slurm or Kubernetes (KubeRay) gave it. Use it when the job itself is many cooperating Python processes (RL with generation, reward and training workers; data pipelines feeding GPUs); plain DDP pretraining does not need it.
