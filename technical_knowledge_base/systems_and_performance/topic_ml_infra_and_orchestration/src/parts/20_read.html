ML infrastructure is everything between "I have a training script" and "it ran on 1,000 GPUs, survived the failures, and I know what it cost": the machines, the software that hands them out, the way the processes start and find each other, where the data and checkpoints live, and the meters on all of it. This page teaches it from zero, at overview depth, for someone who already knows how to train a model. Reading time about 28 minutes (measured from the words outside widgets at 200 a minute; the folded answers and code add about 8).

The whole subject on one screen: the life of one training job

1. SubmitYou write a job spec: how many machines and GPUs, for how long, which container or environment, which command.a Slurm batch script, a Kubernetes YAML, a Ray script
▼
2. Wait in a queueA scheduler decides when the job gets machines, given everyone else's jobs, priorities and quotas.FIFO, backfill, fairshare, preemption, gang scheduling
▼
3. Launch and meetOne process per GPU starts on every machine; they find each other (rendezvous) and open a communication group.torchrun, srun, a pod per node; MASTER_ADDR, the c10d store, NCCL
▼
4. TrainSteps run; data streams in from storage; every so often all ranks write a checkpoint; metrics flow to dashboards.parallel file systems, object storage, DCGM, Prometheus
▼
5. Fail and resumeA GPU or link dies; the job stops, a bad node is taken out, the job restarts from the last checkpoint.health checks, drain and cordon, restart policies, elastic training
▼
6. AccountGPU-hours paid against useful work done: queueing, failures, checkpoints and slow steps all show up here.goodput, MFU, utilisation, cost per run

The running example. one small PyTorch training job: a character-level GPT (2 layers, 2 heads, width 64, 112,577 parameters) on TinyShakespeare, trained with DDP over gloo (the CPU stand-in for NCCL) as 4 ranks on 2 "nodes" of 2, for 200 deterministic steps, checkpointing every 50 steps (1.4 MB each) and resuming exactly from the last one. It is packaged once as a container image and submitted the way each system expects: sbatch on a Slurm cluster in containers, a JobSet admitted by Kueue on a kind Kubernetes cluster, and Ray Train on a Ray cluster in containers (definitions in the root's src/data/). Every lab tab submits that one job, so its queueing, launch, failures, checkpoints and cost can be compared across the three systems. Everything ran on an Apple M1 Pro: the clusters are real Slurm and Kubernetes software in containers, so what the systems do (which job runs where and when, what a failure does, the logs, the environment variables) is real, while how fast is laptop speed, not data-centre speed.

Numbers are labelled: measured means produced here in October 2026, published a figure an operator reports about its own cluster or run (a paper or engineering blog), vendor a claim a company makes about a product it sells, independent a figure from someone without a stake, derived computed from other numbers with the formula shown, simulated produced by a small model of the mechanism, and unconfirmed a claim no primary source confirms.

Four questions run through the page, one per goal: how do I build with it (write the job spec, launch script and health check), how does it work inside (what the scheduler and launcher actually do), how do I judge and debug it (stuck pending, hung at init, slow, failing), and how do I explain a design in an interview.
