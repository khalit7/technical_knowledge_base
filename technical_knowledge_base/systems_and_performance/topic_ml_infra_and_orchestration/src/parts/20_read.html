0. The map

The idea. A synchronous training job is one program spread over hundreds or thousands of GPUs that moves only when every piece is present. So the infrastructure around it does four things: give the whole job its machines at once, start and connect one process per GPU, restart all of them from a checkpoint when any one fails, and meter what the GPU-hours bought. Slurm, Kubernetes and Ray split those duties differently; the duties stay the same.

Fig. 1 is the page: the life of one training job on this topic's own Slurm lab cluster, each part lit where it acts, with the section here and the child page whose own figure teaches it. Kubernetes and Ray do the same duties under other names, given in each step.

  1. The map: one job, one cluster. alice submits to a controller, which hands out the GPUs of three nodes; the job reads and writes a shared volume; meters count what it used and a tracker records what it made. Drawn: the lab's Slurm cluster (a controller and nodes c1 to c3 with four fake GPUs each, containers on an M1 Pro) running the running example, 2 nodes of 2 ranks; the meter and tracker boxes are illustrative. Each step lights one part and links the child page that owns it.
  2. The cluster: GPUs in nodes, nodes on networks. A typical node holds 8 GPUs joined by NVLink (the lab's nodes have four fake ones); nodes are joined by a scale-out fabric, and storage and the controller sit on the network, not in the node. Per direction an H100's NVLink carries about 450 GB/s against 50 GB/s for one 400 Gb/s card (section 1).   3. Submit and wait: a scheduler queues the job. The job file asks for nodes, GPUs and a time limit; the controller puts it in a queue with everyone else's and decides who goes next by priority, backfill and fairshare (section 2). Slurm: sbatch to slurmctld. Kubernetes: a JobSet in a Kueue queue. Ray: a job on a running Ray cluster. Owners: {{Slurm from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81889c32cd050bbbadd3}}, whose {{hero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81889c32cd050bbbadd3#stg-hero}} follows one job through this same lab cluster from its log and whose {{backfill figure|n:3c65c17b0d0d81889c32cd050bbbadd3#stg-bf}} steps the main loop and the backfill loop; {{Kubernetes for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce}}, whose {{control loop|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce#stg-loop}} follows one Job object. Tool: {{Scheduling lab|#t-sched}}.
  4. Get machines: all or nothing. The scheduler starts the job only when every GPU it asked for is free together (gang scheduling): here 2 nodes with gpu:2 each, while c3 stays free. Slurm gives one allocation from slurmctld; Kubernetes needs Kueue to admit a JobSet whole; Ray reserves a placement group. Without it, Kubernetes placed half of each of three jobs and held 12 of 12 GPUs with no step run (section 2). Owner: {{Kubernetes for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce}}, whose {{all-or-none figure|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce#stg-gang}} replays that deadlock and its fix; {{Ray|n:3fc5c17b0d0d811cba13f3aeccf8724b}}'s {{Fig. 4|n:3fc5c17b0d0d811cba13f3aeccf8724b#stg-train}} shows the placement group behind Ray Train.
  5. Start and meet: one process per GPU. A launcher starts the processes; they find each other (rendezvous) and build the communicator the gradients travel on. Slurm: srun then torchrun, meeting at rank 0's store on c1:29500. Kubernetes: a pod per node, pod 0's DNS name. Ray: Ray Train sets it all. Submit to the first finished step, medians of 5: 7.5 s on Slurm, 5.8 s on Kubernetes, 10.0 s on Ray measured (section 4). Owner: {{Launching distributed training|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81b3a8a5c58224bff9fa}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81b3a8a5c58224bff9fa#stg-hero}} times every link of this launch and {{Fig. 3|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81b3a8a5c58224bff9fa#stg-hang}} finds the one stuck rank in a hang. Tool: {{Launch lab|#t-launch}}.
  6. Train: read data, all-reduce gradients. Steps run; data streams in from shared storage and every step all-reduces the gradients across both nodes. The same code gave the same global loss bit for bit on all three systems (step 200: 2.2638769149780273, section 3). PyTorch's side (DDP, FSDP2, c10d) is {{Distributed PyTorch|n:3c65c17b0d0d815e85aff8decd8f35ab}}.
  7. Checkpoint: the state, written out. Every so often every rank writes its part of a checkpoint at once; here rank 0 writes 1,386,049 bytes every 50 steps. At 70B parameters a checkpoint is about 0.98 TB, about 16 GB/s of writes for one minute (section 6). Owners: {{Storage and data movement|n:3fc5c17b0d0d818a94ced3fc7c3331fe}}, whose {{Fig. 4|n:3fc5c17b0d0d818a94ced3fc7c3331fe#stg-burst}} writes one checkpoint four ways and {{Fig. 1|n:3fc5c17b0d0d818a94ced3fc7c3331fe#stg-pfs}} stripes a file over four servers; {{Cluster anatomy|n:3fc5c17b0d0d816a871df56477fc83b2}}'s {{Fig. 4|n:3fc5c17b0d0d816a871df56477fc83b2#stg-sto}} draws the storage tiers; {{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}}'s {{Fig. 1|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b#stg-ck}} saves and restores with four strategies. Tool: {{Storage lab|#t-io}}.
  8. Fail and resume: routine at scale. A GPU, link or host dies; every rank stops at the next collective; the bad node is taken out and the job restarts from the last checkpoint. Measured here: c2 killed, Slurm set it DOWN at 32 s, requeued the job, restarted it on c1 and c3 at 162 s and it resumed from step 100 at 166 s, redoing 20 steps measured. Kubernetes: JobSet restart, cordon and taint. Ray: Ray Train restores the worker group (section 5). Owner: {{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}}, whose {{Fig. 2|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b#stg-el}} loses a node, shrinks and grows back; {{Ray|n:3fc5c17b0d0d811cba13f3aeccf8724b}}'s {{Fig. 3|n:3fc5c17b0d0d811cba13f3aeccf8724b#stg-loss}} follows six fates when a node is lost. Tool: {{Failure lab|#t-fail}}.
  9. Account: what the hours bought. Meters set paid GPU-hours against useful work: idle allocations, restarts, checkpoint stalls and slow steps all appear as losses, and they multiply: 90% goodput at 40% MFU uses 36% of what was paid for derived (section 7). Owner: {{Observability and cost|n:3c65c17b0d0d818ebf79d1af4532f3ee}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d818ebf79d1af4532f3ee#stg-sm}} reads one H100 with three meters, {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d818ebf79d1af4532f3ee#stg-gp}} splits the allocated seconds and {{Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d818ebf79d1af4532f3ee#stg-cb}} charges them to accounts. Tool: {{Cost lab|#t-cost}}.
  10. Record: the platform around every job. Around every step sits the platform that records which code, data and run made which model: an experiment tracker, a workflow engine, a model registry (section 8). Owner: {{ML platforms and workflows|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b9943cf6c72600f96b}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b9943cf6c72600f96b#stg-stack}} follows a champion model's chain layer by layer and {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b9943cf6c72600f96b#stg-flow}} runs a workflow on its datastore. New terms on this page (16)
node, cluster
one server (typically 8 GPUs); many nodes joined by networks, shared storage and control machines
rank, world size
one training process, normally one per GPU; how many there are
GPU-hour
one GPU for one hour: the unit of cost and of quota
scheduler, allocation
the program that queues jobs and hands out machines; the machines one job holds
gang scheduling
start every process of a job together or none of them
backfill
start a later, smaller job in idle nodes if it ends before the waiting big job could start
fairshare
priority that falls for teams that used more than their share recently
preemption
evicting a running job for a more important one
pod, Kueue, JobSet
Kubernetes' unit of containers; its job-queueing add-on; its "group of pods as one job" object
placement group
Ray's reservation of several bundles of resources at once
launcher
starts one process per GPU and tells each its rank (torchrun, srun, Ray Train)
rendezvous, TCPStore
how the processes meet; the small key-value server they meet at
process group
the communicator collectives run on (gloo on CPUs, NCCL on NVIDIA GPUs)
MTBF
mean time between failures, of a part or of a whole job
drain, cordon
Slurm's and Kubernetes' ways of keeping new work off a node
goodput, MFU
the share of paid time that made progress; the share of the chip's peak that progress used

The running example. one small PyTorch training job: a character-level GPT (2 layers, 2 heads, width 64, 112,577 parameters) on TinyShakespeare, trained with DDP over gloo (the CPU stand-in for NCCL) as 4 ranks on 2 "nodes" of 2, for 200 deterministic steps, checkpointing every 50 steps (1.4 MB each) and resuming exactly from the last one. It is packaged once as a container image and submitted the way each system expects: sbatch on a Slurm cluster in containers, a JobSet admitted by Kueue on a kind Kubernetes cluster, and Ray Train on a Ray cluster in containers (definitions in the root's src/data/). Every lab tab submits that one job, so its queueing, launch, failures, checkpoints and cost compare across the three systems. The clusters are real Slurm, Kubernetes and Ray software in containers on an Apple M1 Pro: what the systems do (which job runs where and when, what a failure does, the logs, the environment) is real; how fast is laptop speed, not data-centre speed.
