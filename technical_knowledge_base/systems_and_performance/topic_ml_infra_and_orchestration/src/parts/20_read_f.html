9. Choosing

The scheduler matters less than people argue: every large operator extends whatever it chose with health checks, fast restarts and storage sized for checkpoints (section 3's dated evidence). Choose by what else the cluster must do and who will use it.

Whatever the choice, the same five things decide whether 1,000 GPUs do useful work: gang admission (no partial jobs), health checks with automatic draining, checkpoints that are sharded and asynchronous, storage sized for the checkpoint burst, and meters for goodput and MFU, not just utilisation.

Depth: four mistakes that cross the children
Trusting the default timeouts, or setting them too short
At 30 minutes (gloo) or 10 minutes (NCCL) a hang burns that much of every GPU before anything fails; but a timeout shorter than your slowest legitimate wait (a first-step compile, a large synchronous checkpoint) kills healthy jobs. Set it on purpose, and add a watchdog on step progress. Know the two NCCL messages apart (torch 2.14.1, {{ProcessGroupNCCL.cpp|https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.14.1/torch/csrc/distributed/c10d/ProcessGroupNCCL.cpp#L1954}}): Watchdog caught collective operation timeout: ... ran for ... milliseconds before timing out. means a collective exceeded its timeout (a peer is missing or stuck); ProcessGroupNCCL's watchdog got stuck for ... seconds without making progress in monitoring enqueued collectives. means the watchdog thread itself hung (often a stuck CUDA or NCCL call), after 480 s by default (TORCH_NCCL_HEARTBEAT_TIMEOUT_SEC).
A checkpoint that restores the model but not the run
Without the optimiser state, the learning-rate scheduler step, the data loader's position and the random-number generators, a resumed run sees different data and its loss jumps. Save all of them, and test a restore before you need one. Measured in the {{Failure lab|#t-fail}}: seven resumed 4-rank runs on three schedulers all continued the same trajectory, matching the uninterrupted loss at 8 of 10 logged steps and differing at two by one float32 rounding step, because DDP rebuilds its gradient buckets after a process's first step; with 2 ranks, or with the rebuild switched off, resume was bit-exact.
Checkpoints only on node-local disk, or gathered on rank 0
Local disk dies with the node it was meant to protect against; gathering everything on rank 0 is slow and can run that rank out of memory. Stage locally if you like, but upload, and shard the write across ranks.
Elastic restarts that silently change the experiment
Continuing on fewer workers changes the global batch size (or the steps per epoch) unless the code compensates; torchrun also reassigns ranks between restarts, so code that assumed a fixed rank-to-data mapping reads the wrong shards.

The other classic mistakes are taught where they happen: a time limit that blocks backfill ({{Slurm from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81889c32cd050bbbadd3}}, section 6), multi-pod training on plain Kubernetes (section 2 here and {{Kubernetes for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9417de52108cfbce}}, section 7), a rendezvous address that works on one node only ({{Launching distributed training|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81b3a8a5c58224bff9fa}}, section 9), a restart onto the same broken node ({{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}}, section 3), and reading 100% utilisation as efficiency (section 7 here).

Depth: interview rehearsal across the topic
Design the training platform for a 1,000-GPU lab: scheduler, storage, failure handling, cost controls.

Shape: 125 nodes of 8 GPUs, NVLink inside, one 400 Gb/s NIC per GPU on a rail-optimised fabric, a separate front-end network. Scheduler: Slurm with fairshare and QOS if most work is large training by researchers; Kubernetes with Kueue (team ClusterQueues in a cohort, borrowing, preemption) and JobSet if the lab also serves models and runs pipelines; either way gang admission and topology-aware placement, and a low-priority preemptible queue that soaks up idle GPUs. Failures: at Llama 3's rate a 1,000-GPU job stops about every 51 hours; with a 1-minute blocking checkpoint, Young gives about 78 minutes between checkpoints and about 3% loss with 10-minute restarts derived. Add asynchronous checkpoints, preflight and periodic health checks with automatic drain, a few spare nodes, and automatic requeue. Storage: a parallel file system sized for the checkpoint burst (a 70B model is about 1 TB, so about 16 GB/s to write it in a minute), object storage for datasets and old checkpoints, local NVMe for staging. Cost: per-team quotas, dashboards of allocated against idle GPUs, goodput and MFU per job, alerts on step time and on GPUs allocated but idle.

Slurm or Kubernetes for training?

Slurm gives batch semantics (queue, backfill, fairshare, all-or-nothing allocations) for free and is simplest for big synchronous jobs on a fixed cluster. Kubernetes gives one platform for everything and an ecosystem, but needs Kueue or Volcano and JobSet to behave like a batch system. The deciding question is what else must run on the cluster and who operates it. Both are used at frontier scale (section 3).

Why does distributed training need gang scheduling?

Synchronous training cannot start until all ranks exist; a partly started job holds GPUs and does nothing. Two partly started jobs can each wait forever for GPUs the other holds: a deadlock (section 2's figure). All-or-nothing admission prevents both.

How often should a 16,000-GPU run checkpoint?

Estimate the job's MTBF (Llama 3: about 3.1 hours on 16,384 GPUs) and the blocking write time C; Young's √(2CM) gives about 19 minutes for C = 1 minute. Then make C smaller (asynchronous, in-memory copies) because the loss falls with √C, and make restarts faster because each one costs the restart time on top. The exact interval and where Young breaks are {{Power, cooling, reliability|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22}}, section 6.

Goodput against MFU?

Goodput is the share of paid time spent making progress (lost to queues, launches, failures, checkpoints); MFU is how much of the chip's peak the progress uses while it runs. Cost per useful FLOP is the product of both: 90% goodput at 40% MFU uses 36% of what you paid for (section 7).
