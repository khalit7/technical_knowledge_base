2. Kill it: the same failures on Slurm, Kubernetes and Ray

Who can notice a failure, and how. Three layers watch a training job, each with its own clock:

  • The other ranks. They talk to the failed rank over TCP (gloo here, NCCL on GPUs). If its process died, the kernel closes its sockets and the peers get an error at once ("Connection reset by peer"). If it is alive but silent (frozen, or its machine cut off), nothing errors until the collective timeout: how long a rank waits inside an all-reduce before giving up (30 s in these runs; PyTorch's default is 30 minutes for gloo and 10 minutes for NCCL in 2.14).
  • The launcher on each machine (torchrun's agent, a Ray Train worker, the container runtime) sees its own child processes exit and their exit codes immediately.
  • The scheduler. It learns a machine is gone only when heartbeats stop: Slurm's controller waits SlurmdTimeout (30 s in this cluster, 300 s by default), Kubernetes marks a node NotReady after node-monitor-grace-period (50 s by default in v1.37), and Ray's GCS declares a node dead after its health checks fail (30 s measured).

What happens next is policy, and the three systems ship different defaults. The matrix below holds every run; click a cell to replay it step by step from its own logs.

What the twelve runs show.

  • A crash is seen in well under a second everywhere; what differs is the decision. Slurm marks the job FAILED and does nothing more: its --requeue covers node failure and preemption, not a program that exited with an error. A JobSet fails unless failurePolicy.maxRestarts is above 0, and Ray Train unless FailureConfig(max_failures) is; the running example sets both to 0 (as is common in templates), so all three systems stopped. With retries allowed, JobSet recreated every pod and resumed in 10.3 s, Ray Train started a new worker group and resumed in 10.2 s.
  • Retries inside a Slurm job are the launcher's job: torchrun --max-restarts=2. With PyTorch 2.14.1's defaults on two machines the restart failed twice in a row, in two separate jobs (Gloo connectFullMesh failed ... Connection refused): the restarted ranks dialled addresses from the previous attempt. Setting TORCH_DISABLE_SHARE_RDZV_TCP_STORE=1 gives each restart a fresh store, and the same crash then resumed in 3.4 s. The {{Launching distributed training|n:3fc5c17b0d0d81b3a8a5c58224bff9fa}} child page isolates the trap in the source.
  • A crash that happens every time eats the retry budget: the poison-step run crashed at step 120 on all three attempts, redoing steps 100 to 120 each time, then failed. Retries cure transient faults, not bugs or a bad batch.
  • Losing a machine takes a heartbeat timeout to notice, then each system diverges. Slurm set the node DOWN after 32.1 s, requeued the job automatically, held it for about two minutes (a requeued job gets a new eligible time) and restarted it on the spare node c3: resumed after 166 s with no person involved. Ray declared the node dead after 30 s and then waited: its worker group needs four slots and only two existed, so it resumed only when the node came back. Kubernetes with a silent node was the worst case: the surviving pod failed on the gloo timeout, the JobSet asked for a restart, and the Job then waited for the pod on the dead node to finish terminating, which a frozen kubelet never confirms. The default 300 s eviction did not help; it took an operator's out-of-service taint (section 3) at 445 s.
  • Every resumed run restarted from step 100 and redid 20 steps: checkpoints every 50 steps, failure after step 120. The cost of a failure is the work since the last checkpoint plus the time to detect and restart; section 3 checks that accounting on three runs, and {{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}} turns it into goodput.
Predict: a node dies on Slurm, the job was submitted with --requeue, a spare node is idle. How long before the job runs again, here with SlurmdTimeout=30?
About 2.5 minutes (162 s to start, 166 s to the first resumed step). The ranks on the healthy node failed within a second, but Slurm only acted when the node missed its heartbeats (32 s), and a requeued job is not eligible again until about 2 minutes later (the Scheduling lab measured the same 121 s hold after preemption). With the default SlurmdTimeout=300 the first part alone would be 5 minutes.
