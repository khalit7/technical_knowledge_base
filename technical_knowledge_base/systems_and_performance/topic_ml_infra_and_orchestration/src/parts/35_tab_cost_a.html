Cost lab: what the time and money go on

A training run is paid for in GPU-hours: GPUs held, times hours held, whether they compute or not. This lab follows one paid GPU-hour down to the useful arithmetic it buys, measured on the topic's three small clusters (where the GPUs are fake and only counted), then priced at real scale from published numbers. The question it keeps returning to: of every hour you pay for, how much trains the model, and where does the rest go?

In one screen
  • Five losses stand between a paid GPU-hour and useful work: the GPU is not given to any job (idle, fragmentation), it is given but the job is not training yet or any more (startup, teardown, hangs), training is redone or paused (failures, checkpoints), and the steps that do run use only part of the chip (MFU). The waterfall puts them in one picture.
  • Measured here: a 200-step job spent only of its Kubernetes allocation in training steps, and at 2,000 steps; backfill raised a Slurm queue's GPU use from to ; a gang deadlock held 12 of 12 GPUs for with zero steps; a lost node cost the GPU-seconds of a clean run.
  • Prices differ more by contract than by provider: the same H100 lists at to per GPU-hour on 2026-10-16 (table), and reserving is paid whether the GPUs work or not.
  • "GPU utilisation 100%" only means a kernel was running, not that the GPU was busy or the job was progressing (drill).

The words, from zero

GPU-hour
One GPU held for one hour. A cloud bill, a reservation and a scheduler's accounting (sacct's AllocTRES times Elapsed) all count this, not work done. 1,024 GPUs for a day is 24,576 GPU-hours.
Allocated vs used
The scheduler allocates GPUs to a job from its start to its end. Whether a kernel ran on them is a separate question that only the GPU's own counters answer.
Scheduling efficiency
Allocated GPU-hours divided by the GPU-hours the cluster could have given out over the same period. What is left over is idle (no job wanted it) or fragmentation (jobs wanted GPUs but the free ones were in the wrong shape: 4 free GPUs spread over 4 nodes cannot run a job that needs 4 on one node).
Goodput
The share of wall-clock time that becomes kept training progress, after failures, restarts and checkpoint pauses. {{Fault tolerance at scale|n:3fc5c17b0d0d815786d1c1dddb311a9b}} accounts for it and {{Power, cooling, reliability|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22}} derives its optimum checkpoint interval; this tab only prices it.
MFU and HFU
Model FLOPs Utilisation: the FLOPs the model needs per step (about 6 per parameter per token) divided by what the GPUs could do at peak in that time. Hardware FLOPs Utilisation also counts recomputed work, so it is never below MFU. Both are explained, with published values and a calculator, on {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}} (Topic: hardware); here MFU is the last loss in the chain.

One allocation: where the seconds went measured

The running example (a 112,577-parameter GPT, 2 nodes x 2 ranks, 4 fake GPUs) was given 4 GPUs by each system's own accounting: Slurm's sacct Start to End, Kueue's Workload Admitted to Finished. Its rank 0 log says what happened inside that window: when the process started, when step 1 ended, each checkpoint, the last step. Pick a run; the bar is the allocation drawn to scale, coloured by what it was spent on.

Three things to read off it. Startup is a fixed cost: a few seconds of container start, imports, rendezvous and process-group init, whether the job then trains for 6 seconds or 6 days. The end is not free either: Kubernetes keeps the allocation until the Job controller sees both pods finish and Kueue marks the Workload finished, about after the last step here. Checkpoints show up as a storage choice: the same 1.39 MB checkpoint took per write on Slurm's Docker volume and on kind's host folder, the 36x the {{Storage lab|#t-io}} measured.

Slurm also records CPU time: TotalCPU of CPU-seconds allocated, a "CPU efficiency" of (what seff prints), while only of the allocation was training steps. Imports and spin-waiting burn CPU too: busy is not the same as useful, which is the whole lesson of the GPU utilisation drill below.
