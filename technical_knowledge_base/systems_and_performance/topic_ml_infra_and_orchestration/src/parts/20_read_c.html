4. Launching a distributed job and finding peers

The scheduler hands over nodes; the training script needs one process per GPU, each knowing who it is and how to reach the others. Four numbers say who a process is: its rank (0 to W−1), the world size W, its local rank (its index on its node, which picks its GPU) and its node's index. A launcher starts the processes and hands them those numbers as environment variables (RANK, WORLD_SIZE, LOCAL_RANK): torchrun runs once per node and starts and restarts its workers; Slurm's srun starts tasks on every node and sets SLURM_PROCID and the node list; a Kubernetes indexed Job or JobSet gives each pod an index and, through a headless service, a stable DNS name; Ray Train starts worker actors and sets everything itself.

Rendezvous is how they meet: everyone is told one address, MASTER_ADDR:MASTER_PORT, where PyTorch's TCPStore (the "c10d store") collects each rank's details, and nobody proceeds until all W have arrived. Then init_process_group builds the process group that collectives run on: gloo on CPUs (this page's running example), NCCL on NVIDIA GPUs, which shares a unique id through the same store and then builds its own rings and trees. A launch that goes wrong almost always hangs rather than crashes, because the ranks that arrived wait for the ones that did not; how long they wait is set by timeouts, not by the fault.

Where the seconds went, stage by stage (medians of five runs)

By stage (medians, which need not add up to the median total): Slurm 0.7 s from sbatch to the batch script, 1.3 s for torchrun to import, 1.1 s of c10d rendezvous (the dynamic backend polls once a second), 2.5 s to spawn the workers and import torch, 0.01 s for init_process_group, 2.0 s for data, model and the DDP wrapper; Kubernetes 0.9 s for Kueue admission, scheduling and container start, 1.9 s of torchrun imports, 0.03 s of static rendezvous, 1.1 s of worker start, 1.3 s of data, model and DDP; Ray 1.6 s for the driver to import Ray, then 4.4 s for Ray Train's controller, placement group and worker processes, 0.9 s until the worker actors were ready, 0.3 s for the process group and 2.0 s for data, model and DDP. Pulling the image is not in these numbers; on a real cluster a multi-gigabyte image pulled to every node is often the longest stage, which is why images are cached on nodes.
