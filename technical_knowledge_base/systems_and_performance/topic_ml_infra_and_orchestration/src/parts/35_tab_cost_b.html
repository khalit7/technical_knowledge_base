A queue: scheduling efficiency on the three systems measured

One job's allocation is the inner loss. The outer one is the cluster: how many of its GPU-seconds were given to any job at all. These are the {{Scheduling lab|#t-sched}}'s recorded runs, read through the cost lens (that lab explains the policies; here only the accounting).

Read as money, the three systems fail in three different ways. Slurm without backfill leaves GPUs idle behind a big job at the head of the queue: the same eight jobs used of 8 GPUs' time under FIFO and with backfill, and the mean wait fell from to . Kubernetes without gang scheduling can allocate every GPU and run nothing: half of each of three jobs held all 12 GPUs for ( GPU-seconds), every one of them idle at a rendezvous, until the lab gave up. Kueue's all-or-nothing admission removed the deadlock but not every loss: with 8-GPU jobs and a 12-GPU quota, 4 GPUs stayed free the whole time because no waiting job fit in them (fragmentation: at most of the quota can ever be busy). Ray refuses rather than wastes: a 4-bundle STRICT_PACK group stays PENDING forever on nodes of 2 fake GPUs each, holding nothing, and a second gang waits with one GPU free rather than taking a partial set.

Predict: a 1,000-GPU cluster's scheduler reports 92% of GPU-hours allocated over a month. Your finance team asks whether the lab is using its GPUs well. What do you answer?
The scheduler only knows what it gave out. The Kubernetes run above was 100% allocated and 0% useful for four minutes; the Slurm job above was 100% allocated and about 40% in training steps. You need the GPU's own counters and the training logs (tokens per second, MFU) to say more, and 100% allocation is not the goal either: a queue with no slack makes every new job wait.

Failures priced measured

The {{Failure lab|#t-fail}} broke the running example on Slurm on purpose: it explains what each failure does and how runs survive it. Here each run is reduced to the one number a bill sees, the GPU-seconds held (4 fake GPUs times the time each job held them, from sacct), against the clean run. The first five runs train the same 200 steps to the same final loss, except the hang, which never finishes; the three straggler runs train 100 steps and are compared with each other.

Select a bar for its story.

The pattern scales. A crash costs the work since the last checkpoint plus the restart, so the checkpoint interval is a cost knob ({{Power, cooling, reliability|n:3f05c17b0d0d8143851cfc3ae14c3c22}}, section 6, finds its optimum). A lost node costs detection plus requeue delay: here Slurm noticed after (SlurmdTimeout=30) and then waited before starting the requeued job (a requeued job's earliest start is pushed back by the credential lifetime, the {{Scheduling lab|#t-sched}} found in job_mgr.c). A hang is the worst kind: it holds every GPU until a timeout fires ( here, because the lab set a short one; PyTorch's default process-group timeout is 30 minutes, NCCL's 10) and produces nothing. And one slow rank makes all ranks slow: synchronous training runs at the pace of the slowest, so a straggler multiplies cost by the number of GPUs waiting for it.
