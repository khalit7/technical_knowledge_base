8. Container networking for distributed jobs

Rendezvous needs one thing from the network: every process can open a TCP connection to every other process, by a name that means the same machine everywhere. Containers make that harder, because a container usually gets its own network namespace (its own interfaces and addresses; the namespace mechanism itself is on {{Containers and isolation|n:3f05c17b0d0d8156a841e4bf28f11293}}).

9. Common mistakes and interview questions

Setting MASTER_ADDR=localhost (or a short host name) in a multi-node script
Every node then meets itself. Each node's torchrun hosts its own store and waits for a partner that is waiting somewhere else; nothing errors until the join timeout. Use the first host of the allocation on Slurm (scontrol show hostnames $SLURM_JOB_NODELIST | head -n1) and pod 0's DNS name through the headless Service on Kubernetes.
Opening only port 29500 in the firewall
With torchrun's c10d backend the workers use a second, random port on node 0 (section 5), and gloo and NCCL open more ports between every pair of ranks. Allow all TCP traffic between the job's nodes on the training network, or pin the interface (GLOO_SOCKET_IFNAME, NCCL_SOCKET_IFNAME).
Hard-coding WORLD_SIZE instead of reading it from the launcher
When the job is resized (fewer nodes, a different --ntasks), the processes wait for ranks that will never exist. Read it from the launcher (WORLD_SIZE under torchrun, SLURM_NTASKS under srun).
Reading the 511 healthy logs instead of looking for the missing one
When most ranks wait at the rendezvous or the first collective, the cause is the rank that is not there: its node, its pod status, its own log. Count processes or pods against the world size first.
Raising every timeout "to be safe"
A 30-minute process-group timeout means a stuck job wastes 30 minutes of the whole cluster before anything restarts it. Keep the start-up timeouts close to how long start-up really takes (this lab's numbers give the scale), and keep the collective timeout above the longest legitimate pause (a checkpoint, an evaluation) but not far above it.
Forgetting that the rendezvous id must be unique per job
Two jobs that reuse one --rdzv-id and one endpoint join the same rendezvous. Use the scheduler's job id ($SLURM_JOB_ID), as the script here does.

Interview questions

What does torchrun do that a plain srun python train.py does not?
It runs the rendezvous between nodes (so ranks are assigned and the meeting address is published, without the script computing them), sets the PyTorch environment variables for each worker, monitors the workers and, with --max-restarts, tears them all down and restarts the group after a failure, re-running the rendezvous. With plain srun you map SLURM_PROCID to RANK yourself and Slurm's own options handle failures.
Walk me through what happens between init_process_group being called and the first all-reduce.
The process connects to the TCPStore at MASTER_ADDR:MASTER_PORT (retrying until the timeout if nobody listens yet), the backend registers this rank's address in the store and reads everyone else's, then connects to the other ranks (gloo: a full mesh of TCP connections; NCCL: a bootstrap ring, then topology detection and rings and trees over NVLink and the network). DDP then broadcasts rank 0's parameters to everyone, and the first backward pass all-reduces gradients bucket by bucket.
A 512-GPU job is stuck at init. What do you check, in order?
(1) Did every rank start: count processes or pods against the world size, and look at the missing one's node and scheduler log. (2) Can every node reach MASTER_ADDR:MASTER_PORT, and does the name resolve the same everywhere. (3) Do all ranks agree on world size and rendezvous id. (4) If all arrived and it hangs in init_process_group or the first collective: NCCL_DEBUG=INFO (interface choice, transport errors), py-spy dump on a few ranks, the flight recorder to find the rank out of step, then that node's health. Drain the bad node and resubmit.
Static or dynamic (c10d) rendezvous: when would you choose each?
Static when the scheduler already fixes node ranks and the address of node 0 (a Kubernetes Indexed Job with a headless Service, an MPI-style launch): no negotiation, no extra round. Dynamic when nodes may arrive in any order, be replaced, or vary in number (elastic training with --nnodes=min:max, spot instances): the agents negotiate ranks each round and can re-run the rendezvous after a failure.
Why do launch timeouts matter for cost?
Every minute a broken launch waits, every GPU in the allocation is paid for and idle. A missing node with default settings costs 10 minutes of the whole job before torchrun gives up (the join timeout); a hang inside a collective costs the process-group timeout (10 minutes for NCCL by default, 30 for gloo). On 1,024 GPUs a 10-minute wait is about 170 GPU-hours. Detect early (health checks before launch), and set timeouts from measured start-up times.
How does a pod in a JobSet know where rank 0 is before rank 0 exists?
The name is deterministic: JobSet names pods <jobset>-<replicatedJob>-<job index>-<pod index>, sets each pod's hostname and subdomain, and creates a headless Service named after the JobSet that publishes pods even before they are ready. So mi-train-node-0-0.mi-train can be written into the spec, and resolves to pod 0's address once it is scheduled.
