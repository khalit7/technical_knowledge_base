What a GPU-hour costs published

Three kinds of price, and they are not interchangeable. On-demand list: pay per hour, stop any time, no promise of capacity. Reserved (commitments, capacity blocks, cluster contracts): a lower or fixed rate for a term, paid whether the GPUs work or not, which is why every loss in this lab becomes real money on a reservation. Spot: spare capacity at a discount that the provider can take back with short notice, usable only by jobs that checkpoint and restart well. All prices per GPU-hour, US East, read on 2026-10-16 from each provider's own pages or price data; an 8-GPU instance price is divided by 8.

  • Azure's reservation figures are the price of the whole term; the table divides by the hours in it (8,760 a year) and by 8 GPUs. Large customers negotiate prices that are not published; treat these as ceilings.

Run-cost calculator derived

From a model and a token budget to days and dollars, with every assumption visible. The compute side (6 FLOPs per parameter per token, peak FLOPs, MFU) is the {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}} page's, with the same peak values, so the two pages agree; its Run and serving planner tab sizes a cluster for a deadline. What this calculator adds is the infrastructure: failures, checkpoints, restarts, idle reservation, and the price kind.

Formulas: training FLOPs = 6 × parameters × tokens; ideal GPU-hours = FLOPs / (peak × MFU) / 3,600; job MTBF = 1 / (interruption rate per GPU × GPUs), so a job twice as big fails twice as often; goodput = 1 − stall/interval − (interval/2 + restart)/MTBF (first-order: on average half an interval is redone per failure); wall days = ideal GPU-hours / GPUs / goodput / 24; paid GPU-hours = GPUs × wall hours / (1 − idle share). Capacity planning solves for the GPU count that meets the deadline, with goodput recomputed at each count. Checked against src/cost/recompute.py.
