Interview questions

What is in an 8-GPU training node, and which network does each card serve?
8 GPUs joined by NVSwitch (scale-up); two CPU sockets (two NUMA nodes) with about 2 TB of DRAM; one 400 Gb/s NIC per GPU for the compute fabric, each under the same PCIe switch as its GPU; two dual-port cards for storage and in-band management; a BMC on 1 GbE for out-of-band control; local NVMe as a scratch tier (DGX H100: 8 x 3.84 TB in RAID 0); about 10 kW.
Why keep the compute and storage networks separate? When would you merge them?
Separate, a checkpoint burst cannot slow the gradient all-reduce and each can be sized on its own (NVIDIA: compute non-blocking, storage near 4:3 at the node). Merged is cheaper: one card, one set of switches. DeepSeek's Fire-Flyer 2 merged them and kept the traffic apart with InfiniBand service levels and virtual lanes; Meta puts storage on its front-end Ethernet.
What is a scale-up domain, and what does an NVL72 change for the scheduler?
The GPUs joined by NVLink-class links (8 in an HGX server, 72 in an NVL72 rack). With NVL72 the node (a tray of 4) is no longer the domain, so the scheduler must place a job's tensor- or expert-parallel groups within one rack: Slurm's topology/block, Kueue's Topology Aware Scheduling. The rack also becomes the unit of power (about 120 kW, liquid) and of some failures.
How many 64-port switches does a non-blocking, rail-optimised fabric for 1,024 GPUs (128 nodes) need?
It fits in two layers (642/2 = 2,048 ports). Each leaf has 32 ports down and 32 up; 8 rails x ceil(128/32) = 32 leaves; their 1,024 uplinks need 1,024/64 = 16 spines: 48 switches, 1,024 node cables and 1,024 leaf-spine cables. The same arithmetic gives NVIDIA's published 32 leaves and 16 spines for 127 nodes. The {{Sizing calculator|#t-casize}} does it for any size.
Why would anyone oversubscribe the top layer of a training network?
Because most jobs are smaller than a pod and the top layer is expensive. Alibaba reports about 96.3% of its training jobs use under 1K GPUs (see the interconnects page). Meta joins 3,072-GPU pods at 1:7 and has the scheduler keep jobs inside a pod; data parallelism across pods still works because its traffic overlaps with compute.
The same job is fast on Monday and slow on Tuesday. What cluster-level causes do you check?
Placement (spread across racks or pods: compare scontrol show job node lists), other jobs sharing an oversubscribed layer, a NIC or link that came up at lower speed or a NCCL fallback to sockets (grep the NCCL INFO transport line), a node with a failing GPU or NVLink (a straggler), storage contention from someone else's checkpoint, and rank binding on the wrong NUMA node after an image change.
Design question: the hardware for a 1,000-GPU training lab.

Shape. 128 HGX nodes (H100 or B200), 1,024 GPUs: two-layer rail-optimised fat tree, InfiniBand or RoCE, 400 Gb/s per GPU, non-blocking: 32 leaves and 16 spines of 64 ports. A separate storage fabric sized at about 160 GB/s read and 80 GB/s write (NVIDIA's standard tier for 4 SUs), so a 4 GB-per-GPU checkpoint drains in about 51 s, and checkpoints go to host memory or local NVMe first. A front-end Ethernet for logins, the scheduler and registries; an out-of-band network to every BMC and PDU.

Power. 128 x 10.2 kW = 1.31 MW of server power for H100 nodes (1.83 MW for B200), about 1.7 MW at a PUE of 1.3 derived; at 40 kW per rack, 3 H100 nodes per rack, 43 racks.

Operations. Topology-aware scheduling (Slurm topology/tree or Kueue TAS), a few spare nodes, health checks before a node takes jobs, and NCCL transport checks in the first job of every image. Trade-offs to say out loud: RoCE against InfiniBand (cost and vendors against tuning), one storage network or two, NVL72 racks (bigger scale-up domain, liquid cooling, rack-level failures) against 8-GPU servers.

A colleague says "the cluster has 400 Gb/s networking, so the all-reduce gets 400 Gb/s". What do you ask?
Per GPU or per node (Fire-Flyer 2 has 200 Gb/s per node, 25 Gb/s per GPU)? Per direction or both? Through how many switch layers, and is any of them oversubscribed? Is NCCL actually using RDMA, and the NIC next to each GPU? And how far apart did the scheduler place the ranks?
