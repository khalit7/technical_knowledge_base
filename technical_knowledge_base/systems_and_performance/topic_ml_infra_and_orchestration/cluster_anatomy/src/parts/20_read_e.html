4. From rack to cluster: the levels and why placement matters

Rack. A steel frame with shared power feeds and, often, a switch at the top (top-of-rack, ToR). How many GPUs go in one is decided mostly by watts and cooling (section 6): Meta's Llama 3 cluster puts 16 GPUs (two servers) behind each rack switch ({{Llama 3, section 3.3.1|https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21783v3}}) published; NVIDIA's B200 SuperPOD puts "two DGX B200 systems per rack" ({{SuperPOD components|https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-superpod/reference-architecture-scalable-infrastructure-b200/latest/dgx-superpod-components.html}}) vendor; an NVL72 is one rack of 72.

Pod, or scalable unit. The block designed once and copied: racks whose GPUs reach each other at full NIC speed. NVIDIA's scalable unit (SU) is 32 DGX nodes with 8 leaf switches, one per rail ({{SuperPOD network fabrics|https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx-superpod/reference-architecture-scalable-infrastructure-b200/latest/network-fabrics.html}}); in its tables one DGX system is removed "to accommodate for UFM connectivity", which is why 4 SUs hold 127 nodes and 1,016 GPUs, not 1,024. Meta's pod (also called an AI zone) is 192 racks, 3,072 GPUs, with "full bisection bandwidth" inside (Llama 3 report). How leaves and spines are wired and what rail-optimised means is section 6 of the {{interconnects page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}}.

Cluster. Pods joined by a top layer of switches, usually oversubscribed. Meta's 8 pods are joined at 1:7; the "cross-AI Zone connectivity is oversubscribed by design", and "the job scheduler tries to minimize cross-zone traffic" ({{Meta engineering, Aug 5 2024|https://engineering.fb.com/2024/08/05/data-center-engineering/roce-network-distributed-ai-training-at-scale/}}) published.

How big can each level be? Set by switch ports. With switches of k ports, a two-layer fat tree reaches k2/2 network cards and a three-layer one k3/4 ({{Al-Fares et al. 2008|https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1402958.1402967}}): 2,048 and 65,536 with 64-port switches derived. The arithmetic reproduces published designs: DeepSeek's Fire-Flyer 2 used 40-port QM8700 switches, and 402/2 = 800 is exactly its "800-port Fat-Tree" "configured with 20 spine switches and 40 leaf switches" authors; NVIDIA's 4-SU SuperPOD has 32 leaves and 16 spines, which the {{Sizing calculator|#t-casize}} gets from the same formula.

Bandwidth per hop, one direction: NVLink 4 450 GB/s, PCIe 5.0 x16 about 63, NIC and leaf-spine links 50, pod-to-core 50 / oversubscription when every GPU crosses at once derived. Small-message latency: NVLink 3.33 µs, InfiniBand 2.8 µs within a leaf, 3.7 µs across leaves ({{Zhao et al. 2025, Table 5|https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09343}}) authors; none is published for the PXN and cross-pod routes. Time for 1 GB = latency + 1 GB / slowest hop. PXN is NCCL moving data over NVLink to the GPU whose NIC sits on the destination's rail. How collectives combine such messages is the interconnects page's Collective simulator.

The same 16-GPU job, placed two ways: equal on an idle cluster, seven times slower spread on a busy one

Predict first: on an idle cluster, is the spread job slower than the packed one? Then step both modes. A cluster shaped like Llama 3's, drawn small: 2 pods of 4 racks, 2 servers of 8 GPUs per rack (the real pods have 192 racks). One ring all-reduce of 1 GB (gradients of about 0.5B parameters in bf16). Switches crossed per ring edge: 0 inside a server (NVLink), 1 inside a rack, 3 between racks of one pod, 5 between pods. Time is the bandwidth term only, 2(N-1)/N x 1 GB / slowest edge derived: latency per hop and congestion are left out, so real gaps are larger.

On an idle cluster both placements take the same 37.5 ms in this model, because each GPU's own 50 GB/s card is the limit either way. When the cluster is busy, the spread job's two cross-pod edges share the 1:7 top layer with every other job, and the ring runs at the pace of its slowest edge: 262.5 ms derived. It also crosses 36 switches per trip round the ring against 2, so every step pays more latency, and it depends on 8 racks and 2 pods being healthy instead of one rack. That is why schedulers pack: Slurm's topology/tree plugin and --switches option, Kueue's Topology Aware Scheduling, and DeepSeek's HAI platform, which allows "cross-zone computing tasks" to be "limited to one at most" (Fire-Flyer 2, section III-B) authors. Scattered placement costs little on an idle network and a lot on a busy one, so the same job is fast one day and slow the next. Packing has its own cost: waiting for 2 whole nodes in one rack takes longer than taking any 16 free GPUs, the trade-off the parent's {{Scheduling lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} measures.
