Why it matters to an ML engineer. Power decides how many GPUs share a rack, which decides how many share a rack switch or an NVLink domain, which decides what "packed" can mean for your job. At site level, megawatts, not money, are usually what limits how many GPUs a cluster can have (the power page works the numbers), which is why a 24,576-GPU cluster at 10.2 kW per 8 GPUs needs about 31 MW for its servers alone, before network, storage and cooling derived (assuming DGX H100 power for Meta's Grand Teton servers, whose rating is not given in the sources here).

What each one teaches.

  • Meta RSC, 2022: a two-level Clos "that has no oversubscription" for up to 16,000 A100s; the conservative design, every GPU equal ({{Meta, Jan 24 2022|https://ai.meta.com/blog/ai-rsc/}}) published.
  • Meta's two 24K clusters, 2024: the same GPU count twice, one cluster on RoCE (Arista 7800 switches) and one on InfiniBand (Quantum-2), both of which "interconnect 400 Gbps endpoints", with storage behind a FUSE API on Tectonic and a parallel NFS from Hammerspace ({{Meta, Mar 12 2024|https://engineering.fb.com/2024/03/12/data-center-engineering/building-metas-genai-infrastructure/}}) published. Llama 3 405B then trained on the RoCE one.
  • Llama 3: oversubscription at the top (1:7) plus a scheduler that keeps jobs inside pods; sections 4 and 5.
  • Fire-Flyer 2: the cheap end. PCIe A100s with one 200 Gb/s card per 8 GPUs: 3.125 GB/s of network per GPU against 50 for a DGX H100, 16 times less derived, storage on the same fabric, two zones instead of a third switch layer. The abstract claims "reducing costs by half and energy consumption by 40%", while section III-C says "60% of the GPU cost and energy consumption" at about 83% of DGX-A100 GEMM performance authors: the paper's two figures differ, and its storage-node count does too (nearly 200 in III-B, 180 in VI-B2).
  • xAI Colossus: 100,000 Hopper GPUs on Ethernet (Spectrum-X), built "in just 122 days"; NVIDIA claims "95% data throughput" against "only 60%" for standard Ethernet ({{NVIDIA, Oct 28 2024|https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/spectrum-x-ethernet-networking-xai-colossus}}) vendor: a supplier's claim about its own product, with no independent measurement found.
  • AWS Project Rainier: "nearly half a million Trainium2 chips" for Anthropic, over several US data centres: a scale-up domain of 64 chips (UltraServers of 4 servers x 16 on NeuronLink) and EFA between them ({{Amazon, Oct 29 2025|https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/aws/aws-project-rainier-ai-trainium-chips-compute-cluster}}) published. TPUs and Trainium in depth: {{TPUs and other accelerators|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187}}.
  • AI2 Jupiter: 1,024 H100s with WEKA storage; a cluster the size most labs actually run ({{OLMo 2, section 6|https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00656}}) published.
