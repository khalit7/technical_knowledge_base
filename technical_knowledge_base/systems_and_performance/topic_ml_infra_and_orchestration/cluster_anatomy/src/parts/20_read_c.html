2. The scale-up domain: how many GPUs share NVLink

The scale-up domain (NVIDIA: NVLink domain) is the set of GPUs joined by memory-speed links, so any of them can read another's memory at hundreds of GB/s. Outside it, GPUs talk through network cards at about a ninth of that (450 against 50 GB/s each way for an H100 and a 400 Gb/s card derived). Why tensor and expert parallelism must stay inside the domain is on the {{interconnects page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81808563fd41fb163af0}}; here the question is what the domain's size does to a cluster.

Predict. A model needs tensor parallelism of 16. On 8-GPU H100 servers, what carries half of each tensor-parallel all-reduce, and how much slower is it than NVLink?
A group of 16 spans two servers, so the all-reduce crosses the 400 Gb/s NICs: 50 GB/s against 450 GB/s each way, 9 times slower derived, on the critical path of every layer. That is why Llama 3 used tensor parallelism of exactly 8, and why rack-scale domains exist: on an NVL72 a group of 16 (or 64) stays on NVLink. What tensor parallelism sends per layer is section 5 of {{Distributed training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}.
