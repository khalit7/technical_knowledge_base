7. Every timeout on the way, in one table

Each layer of section 1 has its own clock, and they were set by different teams for different reasons, from 15 seconds to 30 minutes. Knowing them turns "it hung for ten minutes" into a diagnosis: the length of the silence names the layer. The bars are on a log scale; a dot marks the time at which the error actually appeared in a run on this page or its parent.

Defaults from the linked source lines (torch 2.14.1, Ray 2.59.0, NCCL v2.30.7-1). Dots: measured on Apple M1 Pro, Docker Desktop VM, shared machine; deterministic up to a second or two.

Three lessons fall out. The defaults are long and uneven: a c10d job with a missing node fails in 10 minutes, the same job on the static backend in 15, an NCCL collective in 10 and a gloo one in 30, and Ray Train never fails at all. Set them on purpose, each a little longer than the slowest legitimate wait at that layer: --rdzv-conf join_timeout=... (image pulls and node boot), init_process_group(timeout=...) (the longest gap between collectives, often a checkpoint save or an evaluation on rank 0 alone), the scheduler's own limit above both. A shorter timeout is not free: it trades waiting for false alarms, and every false alarm is a restart (section 3) costing a rendezvous and a cold start on every node.

8. Debugging a hang: find the one rank that is different

A hang in a collective job is almost always one rank doing something else while all the others wait for it in a collective. The waiting ranks look identical and tell you nothing; the work is finding the odd one and seeing what it is doing. Two questions sort every case: did it hang before the first step or during training? (before: a link of section 1; during: a collective mismatch, a dead or stuck rank, or the network) and are all ranks stuck in the same place? The {{Hang triage|#t-ldhang}} tab has five recorded hangs of the running example's loop with every rank's stack, to practise on.
