6. Placement controls: affinity, taints, spread, priority and preemption

Training pods only on GPU nodes, everything else kept off them, a job's pods close together, important jobs able to take GPUs from unimportant ones: four controls do it.

  • nodeSelector and node affinity: node labels the pod requires (requiredDuringSchedulingIgnoredDuringExecution, a Filter) or prefers (preferred..., a Score). Kueue's flavors (section 8) add one for you.
  • Taints and tolerations: a node's taint repels every pod that does not tolerate it. GPU pools are usually tainted (for example nvidia.com/gpu=present:NoSchedule) so only training pods and the device plugin DaemonSet land there. NoExecute also evicts running pods; the node lifecycle controller adds node.kubernetes.io/unreachable this way when a node goes silent (the root's {{Failure lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} measures it).
  • topologySpreadConstraints: keep the count of matching pods per topology domain (nodes sharing a label value, such as a zone or this cluster's block mi.example.com/block) within maxSkew.
  • Pod affinity and anti-affinity: place a pod near (or away from) pods with given labels in a domain. JobSet's exclusive placement (section 9) is built from these.

A spread rule meets a tainted node. Three 1-GPU pods with maxSkew: 1 over the two blocks (b1 = worker and worker2, b2 = worker3), while worker3 carries a taint the pods do not tolerate. Predict: does b2 still count as a domain they must spread into?

src/k8s/spread-pod.sh; taint km.example.com/reserved=inference:NoSchedule on worker3 during the runs, removed after.

With the default, the tainted block still counts as a domain with zero pods, so after one pod in b1 the second would make the skew 2 and is refused: two pods Pending with 2 node(s) didn't match pod topology spread constraints, 2 node(s) had untolerated taint(s). With nodeTaintsPolicy: Honor the tainted node is left out of the count and all three run in b1. The same trap exists for nodeAffinityPolicy, whose default is Honor.

Priority and preemption. A PriorityClass maps a name to an integer. Higher-priority pods go first in the queue, and when nothing fits, the default PostFilter plugin looks for a node where evicting lower-priority pods makes room (preemption). The victims get the DisruptionTarget condition (reason PreemptionByScheduler), SIGTERM and their grace period; the preemptor waits with status.nominatedNodeName set. preemptionPolicy: Never keeps the queue priority but never evicts.

Three low-priority jobs fill the cluster; two high-priority jobs arrive measured

Cost and when it matters. The scheduler preempts pods, not jobs: evicting one pod of a 64-pod training job kills all 64 (section 2), and it will happily preempt a pod 3 minutes from a checkpoint. That is why training clusters move preemption up a level: Kueue preempts whole workloads by priority or to reclaim quota (the parent's {{Scheduling lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} shows the reclaim), and the native PodGroup has a disruptionMode (single or all) for exactly this. A PodDisruptionBudget limits voluntary evictions (drains), but not preemption by priority beyond a best effort.
