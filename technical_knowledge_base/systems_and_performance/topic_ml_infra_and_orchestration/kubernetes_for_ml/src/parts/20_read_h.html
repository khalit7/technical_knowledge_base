7. Gang scheduling: every pod or none

The scheduler places one pod per cycle and knows nothing of the job it belongs to. Two jobs that each need the whole cluster can each get half, and wait forever for the other half: a partial-placement deadlock. The parent's {{Scheduling lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b5925bfd1d8665dd4b}} produced exactly that on this cluster and fixed it with Kueue. A gang is a set of pods that must start together; gang scheduling binds all of them or none. Kubernetes v1.37 has it built in, behind a gate: a PodGroup object with gang.minCount, and a GangScheduling plugin that holds a group's pods at PreEnqueue until minCount exist and at Permit until minCount are placed. This page switched the gate on for one run, on the parent's deadlock input:

The parent's deadlock input, without and with a PodGroup measured

  1. Six pods, all gated. Three jobs a, b and c of two pods; each pod holds a whole worker's 4 fake GPUs, 12 in all, so 1.5 jobs fit. A scheduling gate holds every pod until the run releases it.
  2. a0, b0 and c0 are released and bound. Released one second apart, each first pod finds a free worker: a0 on worker2, b0 on worker, c0 on worker3.
  3. a1, b1 and c1 are released: Pending. No worker has 4 GPUs free. The scheduler sees three more pods it cannot place; it has no idea they belong to jobs that are already half running.
  4. 28 s after the last release: deadlock. Three Running pods wait for peers that cannot be placed: every job half placed, none able to run. This would last until a timeout or a person.
  1. Six pods, gated, in three PodGroups. The same pods; each pair now names a PodGroup with gang.minCount 2.
  2. a0, b0 and c0 are released: held at PreEnqueue. None even enters the scheduling queue: waiting for minCount pods from a gang to appear in scheduling queue. No GPU is taken.
  3. a1 is released: gang a is bound whole (4.4 s). Both pods are placed, held at Permit until the second is reserved, then released together: Quorum is met for a gang. Allowing other pods from a gang waiting on permit. a0 on worker, a1 on worker2.
  4. b1 and c1 are released: gangs b and c are refused whole. Only worker3 is free: minCount (2) cannot be satisfied: 1 scheduled, 0 remaining. Neither pod of either gang is bound, so no GPU is held by half a job.
  5. Gang a finishes; gang b is bound whole (39.3 s). a's pods ended at 34.8 and 35.3 s; the next gang takes both freed workers.
  6. Gang b finishes; gang c is bound whole (85.3 s). b's pods ended at 69.4 and 69.9 s. The gangs ran one after another as the GPUs freed up, and no job ever sat half placed.

Three jobs of two pods, each pod holding a whole worker's 4 fake GPUs (12 in all, so 1.5 jobs fit), released one second apart in the order a0, b0, c0, a1, b1, c1. Pods sleep 30 s in the gang run (600 s in the other, which was stopped after 28 s). Snapshots in src/runs/e11nogang.snap, e11gang.snap; scheduler log in e11gang.schedlog.txt.

Which one to use. Native gangs fix the deadlock but have no notion of quota or queues: a big job still waits behind whatever arrives first, and two teams still race. Kueue gives queues and quotas and works with the default scheduler today (and its admission can lag behind real placement: an admitted workload can still find a node broken, which waitForPodsReady, default timeout 30 minutes in v0.20 per apis/config/v1beta2/defaults.go line 48, handles by evicting and requeueing it). Volcano and KAI replace the scheduler and give both, at the cost of running a second scheduler you must trust with every GPU pod.

Beyond Kueue and the native PodGroup, three batch schedulers replace kube-scheduler for GPU pods and do gangs, queues and fair share inside it: Volcano (CNCF), KAI Scheduler (NVIDIA, from Run:ai) and YuniKorn; the older coscheduling plugin is a second scheduler build with a PodGroup CRD (what OpenAI used on its 7,500-node cluster); LeaderWorkerSet gangs each serving group (section 11).

Kueue's webhook is on the path of every pod

Restarting the API server for this run restarted Kueue's controller too, and until it served again every pod creation in the cluster failed (failed calling webhook "mpod.kb.io" ... connection refused): Kueue's pod webhook has failurePolicy: Fail for every namespace except kube-system and kueue-system, so Kueue is on the critical path of all pods, queued or not. Run it with several replicas and watch its readiness.
