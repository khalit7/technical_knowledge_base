Divergence hunter

Every switch is one porting difference, trained alone for 300 steps from the PyTorch twin's initial weights on the same batches (src/runs/pj_hunt.py). Seven of them were trained by the root's Training lab and are reused here (marked "root"); the rest were trained for this page with the same harness, which first reproduced the root's correct-port curve exactly (largest difference ). Turn switches on to overlay how far each run's training loss is from PyTorch's, then press play to watch the curves separate step by step. The table under the chart gives each run's evidence ladder: the first rung that fails tells you where to look.

What the runs say
  • Forward-pass differences show at step 0. The epsilon, GELU, bfloat16 and mask switches all fail the logits rung; one batch of logits from copied weights catches every one of them before any training.
  • Optimiser differences pass three rungs. Weight decay, b2, clip position and the schedule's off-by-one leave logits and gradients identical and only appear at the one-step rung or in the curves.
  • Two differences are harmless. PyTorch's clipping formula and Flax's fast variance stay within the correct port's own noise for all 300 steps; the ladder passes them, which is the point of having thresholds.
  • A wrong mask can look like success. Without the causal mask the training loss collapses (the model reads the answer), validation with a proper mask is against .
