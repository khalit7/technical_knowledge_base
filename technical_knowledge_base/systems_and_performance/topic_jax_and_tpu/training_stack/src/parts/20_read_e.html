4. optax, transformation by transformation

An optax GradientTransformation is a named tuple of two pure functions: init(params) -> state and update(grads, state, params) -> (updates, new_state) (). optax.chain(t1, t2, ...) feeds each one's output into the next and keeps a tuple of their states (). Every optimiser you know is a chain: optax.adamw is scale_by_adam, then add_decayed_weights, then scale_by_learning_rate (). Because the state is just more arrays, it is jitted, sharded and checkpointed like the model. The algorithms themselves (why Adam, AdamW, Lion, Muon, which schedule) are {{Optimisers and learning-rate schedulers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817080bbc90954681d09}}'s; this section is how optax builds and runs them.

The running example uses chain(clip_by_global_norm(1.0), adamw(lr_schedule, b1=0.9, b2=0.95, eps=1e-8, weight_decay=0.1, mask=decay_mask)). A script recomputes every stage by hand on the real gradients of the first 12 steps and checks it against tx.update: the largest difference was 7.3e-11 measured, float rounding in the bias correction. The four things PyTorch does by mutation, done as transformations

  • Changing a hyperparameter mid-run: inject_hyperparams. An optax optimiser closes over its hyperparameters, so they are constants in the compiled step; optax.inject_hyperparams(optax.adamw)(learning_rate=1e-3, ...) moves them into the state as traced arrays you can overwrite (). Changing it four times traced the step once; rebuilding optax.adamw(lr) with a new Python float traced it once per value measured. A schedule needs neither: it is evaluated inside the step from the step count.
  • Parameter groups: masks and optax.partition. A mask (a pytree of booleans, or a function returning one) switches one transformation per leaf (adamw(mask=...) decays only masked leaves); optax.partition sends each leaf to a different optimiser by label (; optax.multi_transform is now its backwards-compatible alias, ). Freezing with set_to_zero() also saves memory: it keeps no moments.
  • Gradient accumulation: optax.MultiSteps(tx, every_k_schedule=4). For three calls it adds the gradient to an accumulator and returns zero updates; on the fourth it passes the mean to tx (). Cost: one more parameter-sized accumulator. Trap: the step count and every schedule now advance once per four calls, so count real updates (state.gradient_step).
  • Skipping bad steps: optax.apply_if_finite(tx, max_consecutive_errors=3). If any gradient is NaN or infinite it returns zero updates and leaves the inner state untouched (); after max_consecutive_errors bad steps in a row it applies the update anyway, so persistent NaNs still surface. It is the JAX counterpart of the skip that PyTorch's GradScaler performs on overflow.

Schedules are functions of the step

A schedule is any function from the step count to a number, evaluated inside the compiled step. The old page's warmup_cosine_decay_schedule(0.0, 3e-4, warmup_steps=2000, decay_steps=100000, end_value=3e-5), evaluated by optax, gives 3.00e-4 at step 2,000, 1.650e-4 at step 51,000 (halfway through the cosine: (3e-4 + 3e-5)/2 derived) and 3.00e-5 at 100,000: decay_steps includes the warmup (). Warmup-stable-decay (WSD) is join_schedules of three linear or constant pieces.

Lion and Muon in optax

Both are in optax (Lion in core, Muon in optax.contrib, where optax keeps newer optimisers alongside schedule-free, Prodigy and SOAP-style ones); how they work is on the optimisers page. One optax-specific trap matters in practice:

optax's Muon applies to every 2-D parameter by default
By default optax.contrib.muon labels every two-dimensional leaf "muon" and everything else "adam" (). In the running example that includes the token and position embeddings and the output head, which Muon's authors keep on AdamW. Pass muon_weight_dimension_numbers returning None for them.
Lion and Muon measured here: Lion's sign step, Muon's Newton-Schulz on a real gradient, and 300 steps with four optimisers

Lion (Chen et al., 2023) keeps one moment and steps by its sign: every coordinate moves by exactly the learning rate. On one step at lr 1e-4, 99.92% of the updates had magnitude 1e-4 and the rest were exact zeros (zero gradients) measured. Its state is one tree (826,369 numbers, half of AdamW's), its default weight decay is 1e-3 (), and its authors recommend a learning rate 3 to 10 times smaller and decay 3 to 10 times larger than AdamW's (arXiv 2302.06675, section 5).

Muon (Jordan, 2024) applies momentum, then replaces each weight matrix's update by an approximately orthogonal one (the same singular vectors, singular values pushed towards 1) through five iterations of a quintic Newton-Schulz map with optax's coefficients (3.4445, −4.7750, 2.0315) (, ). On the running example's real first-layer MLP gradient ([128, 512]) the singular values spanned a ratio of 1.2e8; after normalisation and five iterations 122 of the 128 ended between 0.5 and 1.20, close to the 0.5 to 1.5 band the optax docstring describes, while the smallest stayed near zero (5.7e-6): a direction with essentially no gradient is not amplified in five iterations.
