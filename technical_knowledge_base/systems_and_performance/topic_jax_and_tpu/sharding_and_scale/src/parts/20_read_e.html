Collective cost models, checked against the compiler

The model. A collective's time has a latency part, paid per step of the algorithm whatever the size (call it α, about 1 µs per ICI hop: "This is typically around 1us", {{scaling book, part 3|https://jax-ml.github.io/scaling-book/sharding/}}, 2025-02-04), and a bandwidth part, bytes over link bandwidth W. The algorithm decides how many latency steps there are and how many links share the bytes:

AlgorithmAll-gather of V bytes over n devicesAll-reduceWhere it is used
Ring (1-D, both directions, wraparound)max(α·n/2, V / Wbidi)twice the all-gather (reduce-scatter, then all-gather)One axis of a TPU torus; NCCL rings inside a server
2-D torus (X × Y, gather over both axes)max(α·(X+Y)/2, V / (2 Wbidi))twice thatA collective over two mesh axes of a slice; the 2-D all-reduce of {{Ying et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06992}} on TPU v3 pods
Tree (double binary tree)not used for gathersabout 2·log2(n)·α + 2V/WNCCL across many nodes: "latency scales linearly with the number of GPUs" for rings, trees give "full bandwidth and logarithmic latency" ({{NVIDIA, 2019-02-04|https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/massively-scale-deep-learning-training-nccl-2-4/}})

The ring and torus rows are the scaling book's: a bidirectional ring all-gather takes "Ttotal = V / Wici", which "doesn't depend on X!", and gathering over several axes "increases the available bandwidth by a factor of Naxes"; "an AllReduce is twice as expensive as an AllGather"; an all-to-all on a 1-D ring is "1/4 the cost of an AllGather". The tree row is the usual α-β estimate, derived. The calculator puts them side by side; the crossover is the point of it: for small messages latency dominates and a ring over many devices loses, for large ones bandwidth dominates and the ring's latency is irrelevant.

The useful test of a cost model is whether it predicts the compiled program. Two-line predictions for each layout (src/runs/ss_formulas.py; P is the parameter bytes, L the layers, A one [batch per data group, sequence, d_model] activation):

Bytes are logical bytes per train step (see the first table). On TPU or GPU two terms change: FSDP's gradient all-reduce becomes a reduce-scatter of the same logical size, and tensor parallelism's backward has 2LA instead of 4LA. The one miss, tensor parallel over 8 devices on the 4-head running example, is the heads problem from the previous section; with 8 heads the prediction is exact.

Choosing a mesh for a model and a TPU slice

The question. Given a model, a global batch and a slice (say 256 v5e chips or 512 v5p chips), how many ways to split for each strategy, and which physical torus axes should carry which? Three constraints decide it.

  1. Memory. Parameters, gradients and optimizer state (16 bytes per parameter for float32 weights, float32 gradients and two float32 Adam moments, 4 + 4 + 8; bfloat16 weights and gradients plus a float32 master copy also come to 16) divided by the FSDP and tensor degrees, plus activations divided by the batch-splitting and tensor degrees, must fit in HBM (16 GB per v5e chip, 95 GiB per v5p chip, Google Cloud TPU docs, last updated 2026-10-13).
  2. Communication hidden under compute. The scaling book's conditions ({{part 5, How to parallelize a transformer for training|https://jax-ml.github.io/scaling-book/training/}}): data parallelism and FSDP stay compute-bound while the batch per chip exceeds C/Wici, which for v5p is "2,550" tokens (C = 4.6e14 FLOP/s, Wici = 1.8e11 B/s bidirectional); spreading FSDP over all three axes of a v5p pod lowers it to about 850. Tensor parallelism "becomes communication bound when Y > MY · F / 2550", "for most models ... between 8 and 16-way". Mixing the two, the best FSDP degree is Xopt = √(B/F · MX/MY · N), which lets the batch per chip fall to about 100.
  3. Topology. A logical mesh axis should sit on physical axes whose neighbours are one ICI hop apart, and its size must be a product of physical axis sizes. The root's {{TPU and Pallas lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81ecbc04eea6aa45ea1a}} (tab "TPU and Pallas lab") ran JAX's own create_device_mesh on mock slices: on a v5p 4x4x4 cube, a (16, 4) mesh puts the 4 on one physical axis (1 hop) and the 16 on the other two (2 hops between logical neighbours), while (8, 8) is refused outright with unless you allow splitting physical axes.

Across slices the network is the data-centre network (DCN), about 6.25 GB/s per chip in the book's v5p numbers, so the batch per slice must exceed C/Wdcn ≈ 73,440 tokens; the standard answer is FSDP and tensor parallelism inside the slice and pure data parallelism (or HSDP) across slices. MaxText encodes exactly this: 12 mesh axes (), each with an ici_*_parallelism and a dcn_*_parallelism degree, defaults ici_fsdp_parallelism: -1 # recommended ICI axis to be auto-sharded and dcn_data_parallelism: -1, and comments marking dcn_tensor_parallelism "never recommended" (). Model code never names a mesh axis: it names logical axes ('embed', 'heads', 'activation_batch_attn'), and logical_axis_rules () map them to mesh axes, so one config change re-lays out the whole model.
