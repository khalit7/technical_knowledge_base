8. Pipeline parallelism, written by hand

A pipeline gives each group of devices a run of consecutive layers (a stage) and streams microbatches through them; stages idle at the start and the end, the bubble. The theory and the GPU schedules (1F1B, interleaved, zero bubble, DualPipe) are {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}'s section 6 and the {{Distributed PyTorch|n:3c65c17b0d0d815e85aff8decd8f35ab}} page's pipeline schedules; this section is how JAX does it.

No propagator invents a pipeline: it is a loop over time. The GSPMD paper reduced it to sharding ({{section 3.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04663}}): keep a buffer with one slot per stage, run every stage's layer at once with vmap, and shift the buffer by one each iteration; shard the stage dimension and "GSPMD will turn the buffer shifting into cross-device communication via CollectivePermute". MaxText's pipeline is this design: a jax.vmap over stages () and shard_map rotations with ppermute over the stage axis (). The version run here writes the per-device loop directly in shard_map, which is easier to read, and lets jax.grad differentiate the whole loop: the backward pass is the forward schedule run in reverse (GPipe's order: all forwards, then all backwards).

Three details carry the design. The stage index comes from jax.lax.axis_index("stage"), so one program serves every stage: whether a device reads a new microbatch or the buffer is a jnp.where, not Python control flow. rot sends stage j's output to j+1 and the last stage's back to stage 0: for circular schedules that is the path to the next repeat (stage 0 keeps it in circ until its turn). And the result is handed to every stage with a psum over stage of the last stage's outputs, an all-reduce you could avoid by computing the loss on the last stage only.

Circular schedules: why repeats shrink the bubble. With R repeats each stage holds R non-consecutive blocks of layers (stage 0 of 2: layers 0 and 2; stage 1: layers 1 and 3) and every microbatch goes round the ring R times. A tick now costs 1/R of a GPipe tick, and the S−1 bubble ticks are 1/R as long: on the running example, 2 stages with 4 microbatches, the bubble falls from 1/5 (GPipe, ticks of 2 layers) to 1/9 (circular, ticks of 1 layer). The price is R times as many transfers (16 collective-permutes against 8) and a ring back from the last stage to the first.

9. Expert parallelism: two all-to-alls you write yourself


In a mixture-of-experts layer each token goes to a few of many expert MLPs (the form is {{Distributed Training|n:3c65c17b0d0d8103a916c4824abbd82e}}'s section 7). Put one expert per device and tokens must travel to their experts and back: two all-to-alls per layer. Routing is data-dependent, so propagation finds that pattern only sometimes; here it is written with shard_map.


The setup (src/runs/ss_moe.py): the 4,096 tokens of the running example's batch 0, taken after block 0 and the next LayerNorm ([4,096, 128]), 512 on each of 8 devices; a router [128, 8] and 8 expert MLPs (128 → 256 → 128, exact GELU), one per device. The router and experts are new seeded weights, illustrative, not trained: the routing is lopsided (expert 6 is wanted by 120 to 150 of each device's 512 tokens, expert 1 by 21 to 35), which makes capacity visible. Each device routes its own tokens (Switch Transformer's groups); capacity C = ⌈capacity factor × 512 × k / 8⌉ tokens per expert per device; tokens over capacity are dropped (the layer outputs 0 for them, the residual stream carries them). Top-1 is Switch's routing ({{Switch Transformers paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81a8b541c9babbf40c94}}), top-2 GShard's and Mixtral's ({{Mixtral paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eba72af0ac91ddc6b3}}).
