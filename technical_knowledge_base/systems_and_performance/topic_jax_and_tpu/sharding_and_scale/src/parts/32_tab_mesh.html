Pick a model, a TPU slice and a split; see what each chip holds and how long a step's compute and communication take, from published chip specs and the scaling book's formulas.

Everything on this tab is a derived calculation, not a measurement: chip numbers are Google's (Cloud TPU docs, last updated 2026-10-13, as collected by the root's TPU and Pallas lab) and the per-link ICI bandwidth is the scaling book's table. What was measured is the structure of the byte formulas: on 8 simulated devices they reproduce the compiled programs within 0.4% (table at the bottom; the Reading tab's {{Collective costs|#t-read}} explains them). The arithmetic is mirrored in src/runs/ss_mesh.py and checked against it.

    Formulas used

    N
    parameters, counted from the shapes (reproduces 826,368; 25,417,728; Llama 3.1 8B's 8,030,261,248 and 70B's 70,553,706,496 from their config.json values)
    state
    16 N / (X Y) bytes per device (float32 weights, gradients, two Adam moments)
    activations
    L · t · 2d with full remat (bfloat16 layer inputs), or L · t · 34d / Y without (Korthikanti et al. 2022, with sequence parallelism and no stored score matrix); t = tokens per device group = B / (D X)
    compute
    6 N B / (devices · peak bfloat16 FLOP/s): a lower bound at 100% of peak
    FSDP
    3 collectives (gather weights forward and backward, reduce-scatter gradients) of 2N/Y bytes each, each V / (MX W)
    DP
    all-reduce of the 2N/(XY) gradient bytes: 2V / (MD W)
    TP
    4 all-reduces per layer (2 forward, 2 backward) of a bfloat16 activation t · d · 2 bytes: 4L · 2V / (MY W)
    W
    bidirectional bandwidth of one ICI axis = 2 × one-way link (scaling book); M = number of physical axes the mesh axis spans
