Design: shard Llama 3 70B for pre-training on a full TPU v5p pod. What mesh, and why?

State first: 70.55B parameters at 16 bytes each is 1.13 TB of weights, gradients and Adam state (derived), so no form of pure data parallelism fits in 95 GiB chips; the state must be sharded. The scaling book takes 8,960 chips (16x20x28) and a 4M-token batch (1,024 sequences of 4,096), that is 468 tokens per chip: below the 2,550 that pure FSDP needs on v5p and above the about 113 that mixed FSDP plus tensor parallelism needs (α²/(MXMYF) with F = 28,672, MXMY = 2). The best FSDP degree is √(B/F · MX/MY · N) ≈ 1,619 with MX/MY = 2, and tensor parallelism must stay below F/2,550 ≈ 11. The book's answer: "roughly 2048-way FSDP and 4-way tensor parallelism", with tensor parallelism on the axis with the fewest hops; its takeaway box phrases the same budget as "data parallelism (1024-way), sequence parallelism (2-way), and tensor parallelism (4-way)". At 40% MFU it estimates "about 44 days" ({{scaling book, part 6|https://jax-ml.github.io/scaling-book/applied-training/}}). In JAX: a mesh ('fsdp', 'tensor') made with jax.make_mesh over the slice, parameters sharded by logical rules, activations constrained at block boundaries, jax.checkpoint per layer, and orbax for sharded checkpoints. Mention what you would check first: the compiled HLO for unplanned all-gathers, and memory_analysis() against HBM.

Your FSDP step is far slower than data parallel at the same batch. What do you look for?

All-gathers of activations rather than weights: attribute the collectives in compile().as_text() to source lines and look for [batch, sequence, hidden]-shaped gathers (in the root lab's unconstrained FSDP case, 33.5 MB of MLP-hidden all-gathers in the forward and the same again in the backward). Fix with with_sharding_constraint on the residual stream (or Explicit mode). Then check the per-chip batch against C/Wici, whether weights are re-gathered in the backward pass, and whether the gradient reduction is a reduce-scatter (on TPU it should be).

Why is pipeline parallelism less common on TPU than on GPU clusters?

Inside a slice, ICI connects every chip on a torus at high bandwidth, so FSDP and tensor parallelism reach thousands of chips; the bubble ((S−1)/(MR+S−1)) and the memory for microbatches in flight are costs you only pay when the network forces you to. GPU clusters hit slow inter-node links at 8 GPUs, which is where pipelining pays. The scaling book says the same in its pipelining section.
