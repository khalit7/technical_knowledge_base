Training lab: the training stack, and porting from PyTorch

JAX gives you arrays and transformations; the libraries around it give you layers (Flax), optimisers (optax), checkpoints (orbax) and data loading (grain). This lab takes each one from zero for someone who knows PyTorch, runs it on the page's running example, and checks the JAX version against the PyTorch twin number by number.

The running example is the tiny character-level transformer of the Reading tab: 826,368 parameters, 4 layers, width 128, trained on TinyShakespeare, written in PyTorch, in Flax NNX and in plain JAX with optax, all three starting from the same weights and reading the same batches (setup in src/data/README.md). Everything below ran on an Apple M1 Pro CPU with jax 0.11.2, flax 0.12.10, optax 0.2.8, orbax-checkpoint 0.12.7, grain 0.2.18, equinox 0.13.8, dm-haiku 0.0.17 and torch 2.14.1. Losses, differences and file sizes are deterministic; times are rough (shared machine, 4 threads, low priority) and say nothing about accelerators.

Layers
torch.nn.Module → Flax NNX (or Linen, Equinox; Haiku is legacy)
Optimiser
torch.optim.AdamW + LR scheduler → optax (a chain of pure functions)
Checkpoints
torch.save, torch.distributed.checkpoint → orbax (async, sharded)
Data
DataLoader + Sampler → grain (deterministic, resumable)
