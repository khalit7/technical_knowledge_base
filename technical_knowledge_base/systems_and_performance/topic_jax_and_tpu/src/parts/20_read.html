0. The map: one train step's life

The core idea
JAX runs your Python function once on placeholders to record it as a whole program, then transforms that program (differentiate, batch, split across devices) and hands it to the XLA compiler in one piece; TPUs were designed around exactly that compiled model, and the price is that your functions must be pure.

Fig. 1 follows one call of the running example's jitted train step (below) down the stack, one station per layer. Each layer passes the next a program, and each has a child page; read down the list for the order this page takes.

  1. Five layers, each passing a program down. Your code hands down a pure function of (params, batch); the transformations hand down a jaxpr: JAX's typed program; the compiler and the partitioner hand down one executable per device, with collectives; and those run as kernels on chips, joined by ICI. Drawn: one station per layer, left to right; the rows, cubes and boxes stand for the counts in the steps, not one for one illustrative layout.
  2. Your model and loop. Flax NNX modules, the optax optimiser, orbax checkpoints and grain data (in PyTorch: nn.Module, torch.optim, DCP, DataLoader). JAX itself has none of them (section 4); together they hand down a pure function, so the running example's 826,368 parameters and their Adam state, 9.9 MB, go in as arguments and come back as results. Owner: {{The JAX training stack|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ea0ace9a2ca8677ec}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d817ea0ace9a2ca8677ec#stg-hero}} follows one training step through the four libraries; the port: {{PyTorch to JAX|n:3fa5c17b0d0d81c5abb9fedab418578d}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3fa5c17b0d0d81c5abb9fedab418578d#stg-hero}} gives one step's state its two homes. Lab here: {{Training lab|#t-train}}.
  3. Traced to a jaxpr. jit, grad and vmap call the function once with tracers (a shape and a dtype, no values) and record a jaxpr: 3,411 equations for the whole step, forward, backward, clipping and AdamW measured. grad and vmap are interpreters that rewrite one jaxpr into another (in PyTorch: torch.compile, loss.backward(), torch.func.vmap). Owner: {{JAX core|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b7a0d1faba6cef5561}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b7a0d1faba6cef5561#stg-hero}} runs one sin through three interpreters, {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b7a0d1faba6cef5561#stg-jit}} traces once, compiles once and runs many times, {{Fig. 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b7a0d1faba6cef5561#stg-ad}} differentiates an MLP one plate per equation and {{Fig. 4|n:3c65c17b0d0d81b7a0d1faba6cef5561#stg-vmap}} nests vmaps. Lab: {{Transforms lab|#t-xf}}. Section 2.
  4. Lowered and fused by XLA. The jaxpr becomes StableHLO (5,318 operations), and XLA's passes leave 816 kernels, 783 of them fusions measured, compiled once in 3.6 s and then cached (in PyTorch: TorchInductor, but always the whole step). Owner: {{XLA and compilation|n:3fa5c17b0d0d8135adcedefac9e1bb48}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3fa5c17b0d0d8135adcedefac9e1bb48#stg-hero}} takes one attention sub-block down through the forms and {{Fig. 2|n:3fa5c17b0d0d8135adcedefac9e1bb48#stg-fuse}} sets the same chain unfused against fused. Lab: {{Compiler lab|#t-xla}}. Section 3.
  5. Sharded over a mesh. A Mesh and a PartitionSpec per array; the partitioner splits the work into one program per device and inserts the collectives. On 8 simulated devices, data parallel compiles to 1 all-reduce of 3.3 MB, and FSDP leaves 0.4 MB of weights on each device (section 5's table) measured. In PyTorch: DDP, FSDP2, DTensor, chosen as wrappers. Owner: {{Sharding and scale|n:3c65c17b0d0d8125b084e6720cc66b3d}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d8125b084e6720cc66b3d#stg-hero}} puts two annotations on 8 devices and finds one collective, and {{Fig. 5|n:3c65c17b0d0d8125b084e6720cc66b3d#stg-smap}} writes the per-device program with shard_map. Lab: {{Sharding lab|#t-shard}}. Section 5.
  6. Run on the chip. On each TPU chip, the MXU (a 128 × 128 systolic array) does the matmuls and the VPU the elementwise work, on tiles copied into VMEM, a software-managed scratchpad, from HBM (819 GB/s on v5e); ICI links join it to its neighbours (in PyTorch's usual world: CUDA GPUs). Owner: {{TPU programming and performance|n:3c65c17b0d0d8129b0a5fd82e44ecf6e}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d8129b0a5fd82e44ecf6e#stg-chip}} draws one chip as your program meets it and {{Fig. 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d8129b0a5fd82e44ecf6e#stg-mxu}} puts a weight on the MXU. Lab: {{TPU and Pallas lab|#t-tpu}}. Section 6.
  7. Pallas, where XLA is not enough. A hand-written kernel runs on a grid, and a BlockSpec per input says which block each grid step copies into VMEM: the lab's tiled matmul on the running example's MLP shape copies 512 × 128 blocks of A on an (8, 1, 4) grid, 32 steps measured in interpret mode; the first 8 are drawn (in PyTorch: Triton). Owner: {{Pallas kernels|n:3fa5c17b0d0d817699fcf65d085402e7}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3fa5c17b0d0d817699fcf65d085402e7#stg-hero}} follows one grid step on a TPU core and {{Fig. 3|n:3fa5c17b0d0d817699fcf65d085402e7#stg-fl}} the causal flash forward. Section 6.
  8. The codebases on top, and PyTorch beside. MaxText, Tunix and levanter assemble every layer for LLMs, with rules in a config that map each weight's named dimensions onto the mesh. Beside them, the PyTorch twin's eager step sends 1,302 operators from Python on every call, where JAX runs 816 kernels and Python ran once (section 2). Owner: {{JAX LLM codebases|n:3fa5c17b0d0d81f8bd5ad523e9f2dde0}}, whose {{Fig. 1|n:3fa5c17b0d0d81f8bd5ad523e9f2dde0#stg-hero}} sets five boxes against four codebases and {{Fig. 3|n:3fa5c17b0d0d81f8bd5ad523e9f2dde0#stg-shard}} maps logical names to chips. Tools and their status: the {{Tool map|#t-map}}. Sections 7 and 8.

A map, not a mechanism: each step lights the layer one child page owns and links that child's own figure. Read it as the life of one train step: the highlighted labels are what one layer hands the next. Numbers are the running example's, measured by this page's labs on a CPU; the chip's are section 6's.

You need PyTorch as a user (the page maps every idea to it; its layers are on {{Topic: pytorch-ecosystem|n:3c65c17b0d0d8150aa65ca44b6ed2fd5}}); rooflines and MFU help in section 6 ({{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}).

New terms, each used again below:

tracer
a stand-in argument carrying only a shape and dtype (an abstract value, such as f32[8,128]), recording what is done to it
jaxpr
the recorded program: typed, side-effect free, a list of primitives (dot_general, add, tanh)
pure function
its result depends only on its arguments, and it changes nothing outside itself
pytree
any nesting of lists, tuples, dicts and registered classes with arrays at the leaves
key
random-number state as an explicit array value
XLA, HLO
Google's compiler for array programs, and its internal program form
Mesh, PartitionSpec
devices as a named grid, and which mesh axis splits each dimension of an array
collective
communication among devices: all-reduce, all-gather, reduce-scatter, all-to-all, permute
MXU, VMEM, ICI
the TPU's matrix unit, its software-managed on-chip memory, its chip-to-chip links
Pallas
JAX's kernel language, the counterpart of Triton
MFU
model FLOPs utilisation: useful FLOPs ÷ (step time × peak FLOP/s)

The running example. Every lab works on the same model, so their numbers compare. One tiny decoder-only transformer language model over characters: the 65 distinct characters of TinyShakespeare, 4 layers, width 128, 4 attention heads, MLP width 512, context 128 characters, 826,368 parameters. It is written three times, in PyTorch, in Flax NNX and in plain JAX with optax (src/data/), and all three read the same batches of 32 sequences. Started from the same weights, their logits agree to within 8.3e-07 and their training losses over 500 steps to within 2.9e-05 measured (setup's check_equiv.py), so any later difference between them is a finding, not noise. Everything ran on an Apple M1 Pro CPU; for sharding, one flag makes JAX treat the CPU as 8 separate devices, so meshes, shardings and collectives compile and run for real (the programs and memory figures are real; the speeds are not those of accelerators).
