1. A TensorCore, as JAX sees it

The {{Reading tab|#t-read}} defined the parts (MXU, VPU, VMEM, HBM, ICI) and gave each generation's peak and ridge point. Here is the one level down that a JAX or Pallas programmer touches. A TensorCore is one sequential processor with no threads: a scalar unit runs the control flow and computes addresses (from its own small SMEM), and issues wide vector instructions; the VPU works on vector registers of 8 × 128 32-bit values (128 lanes, 8 sublanes); the MXUs multiply; DMA engines copy tiles between HBM and VMEM in the background while the core computes. In PyTorch-on-GPU terms: VMEM plays the role of shared memory, but it is tens of megabytes rather than a few hundred kilobytes, and nothing caches HBM for you, so the compiler (or your Pallas kernel) decides every copy.

Where the sources disagree (checked 2026-10-15)
    Predict: a kernel's working set is 40 MiB of tiles. On which generations does it fit in one core's VMEM?
    v5e and v6e have 128 MiB per core; v5p and TPU7x have 64 MiB, v4 16 MiB (tpu_info.py). So 40 MiB fits v5p and 7x too, but Pallas double-buffers every input and output by default (two copies, so the next tile loads while this one is used), so 40 MiB of tiles needs 80 MiB: only v5e and v6e. And the compiler applies a default per-kernel ("scoped") VMEM limit; a kernel that needs more asks with vmem_limit_bytes (section 7).

    2. The MXU, and why shapes should be multiples of 128

    An MXU is a systolic array: a square of multiply-accumulate cells holding one tile of the weight matrix, through which rows of activations pulse, each cell multiplying, adding and passing its result to the next cell, so each number read from VMEM is used 128 (or 256) times. It holds a whole k × n tile at a time, of side 128 (v4, v5e, v5p) or 256 (v6e, TPU7x), and the scaling book's rule follows: "Weight matrices need to be padded to at least size 128 (256 on TPU v6e) in both dimensions to fill up the MXU." A weight of 65 columns occupies a 128-wide tile and leaves the rest of the cells multiplying zeros. The cycle-by-cycle picture of the array is on the {{Hardware topic's TPU page|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187}}; here is what it means for the shapes in your model.

    Two lessons from the running example, both visible above. Its output head (D = 128 to a vocabulary of 65) fills 51% of a 128-wide MXU and 13% of a 256-wide one; its attention, with a head dimension of 32, fills 25% and 6%. This is why production models use vocabularies padded to a multiple of 128 and head dimensions of 128 (MaxText's Llama 2 7B config: head_dim: 128, vocab_size: 32000 = 250 × 128), and why a model tuned on a v5e can lose efficiency on a v6e: every 128 × 128 projection of this model is 25% of a 256 × 256 array.
