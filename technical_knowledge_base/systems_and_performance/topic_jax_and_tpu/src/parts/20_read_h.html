8. JAX against PyTorch, honestly

Neither is faster in general: both end in compiled kernels on the same hardware, and speed comes from the program, the shapes and the sharding more than the framework. They differ in what they make easy. Each row says who wins and the evidence; where the evidence is an opinion, it says so.

9. Choosing

Choose JAX when

  • you will train on TPUs (it is their native language, and the price per chip-hour is the reason to be there);
  • you want one program that a compiler shards across hundreds of chips, with sharding kept out of the model code (MaxText's logical axes);
  • your research composes transformations: per-example gradients, Hessian-vector products, ensembles by vmap over stacked parameters;
  • bit-reproducible runs matter (explicit keys, levanter's preemption-proof determinism).

Stay with PyTorch when

  • you train and serve on NVIDIA GPUs and need the newest kernels the week they appear;
  • you start from Hugging Face checkpoints and PyTorch-first tools (vLLM, SGLang, TRL);
  • your team debugs by stepping through eager code, and the model changes shape a lot (variable-length everything);
  • the codebase already exists: port only when moving to TPUs, and then consider PyTorch/XLA or torchax before rewriting.

A practical path (the old page's, kept because it works): develop and test on CPU, where JAX traces identically; check sharding on simulated CPU devices as this page does; smoke-test on a free single TPU chip; train on a slice; profile before buying more.
