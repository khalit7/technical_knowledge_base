The core idea
A JAX transformation runs your Python once on tracers (stand-ins that carry only a shape and a dtype), and every array operation becomes a primitive that asks the current interpreter what it means: run it (no transformation), record it into a jaxpr (jit), differentiate it (grad, jvp) or batch it (vmap); the interpreters stack, so the transformations compose. Everything else on this page (dtype rules, the jit cache, control flow, custom rules, pytrees, keys) is a consequence of that design.

One sin in your code, four meanings. sin_p.bind(x) hands the operation to whichever interpreter is current; under jit(vmap(jvp(f))) three of them run in turn, each binding its output on the next.   2. jvp's trace receives sin first, because jvp is the innermost transformation. On the per-example float32[3] it runs the JVP rule of sin: the primal needs sin(x), the tangent needs cos(x) times t. It binds sin, cos and mul on its parent, with the parent set as the current trace.

  3. vmap's trace batches sin: the batching rule of an elementwise op applies it to the whole batch, so float32[3] becomes float32[2,3], batch dim unchanged. It binds that on the jaxpr trace, which records an equation: sin of the batched array. Nothing is computed.

  4. cos takes the same path: batched to float32[2,3], recorded as a second equation.

  5. mul, the tangent t * cos(x): both operands carry batch dim 0, so one mul on the batched arrays, recorded as the third equation.

Environment and what transfers
Every run: jax , jaxlib , Python , CPU only (JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu), on an Apple M1 Pro shared with other long jobs (1-minute load average 20 to 45 during these runs). Programs, jaxprs, counts, FLOPs from XLA's cost analysis and numerical differences are deterministic and hold on any machine; the few timings are labelled and rough. Scripts are in this page's src/runs/; the jit-cache drills, side-effect and key-reuse results in sections 3 and 10 were recorded by the root's Transforms lab (src/xf/ of the root) and are copied into src/inputs/ with their provenance.
