The whole of JAX in four sentences
A jax.Array is an immutable, typed, device-resident buffer. A transformation calls your Python function on tracers, stand-ins that carry an abstract value (shape and dtype); every array operation lands on a primitive, and the primitive asks the current trace (an interpreter) what to do with it. One interpreter records a jaxpr (jit), one computes derivatives (grad, jvp), one adds a batch dimension (vmap), and they stack. Everything else on this page (dtypes, control flow, custom rules, pytrees, keys) is a consequence of that design.

How to read this page. Sections 1 to 3 are the data model (arrays, tracers, jaxprs). Sections 4 to 7 are the transformations, each with its cost. Sections 8 to 10 are the pieces you use daily (pytrees, keys) and the extension point (primitives). Four lab tabs give the long visuals room: the Jaxpr explorer (every equation of six real programs), the Autodiff tape (the running example's MLP differentiated step by step, before and after transposition), Control flow (a chooser and every loop form measured), and Custom derivatives (gradients that go NaN and the rules that fix them). Environment for every run: jax , jaxlib , Python , CPU only (JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu), on an Apple M1 Pro shared with other long jobs (1-minute load average 20 to 45 during these runs). Programs, jaxprs, counts, FLOPs from XLA's cost analysis and numerical differences are deterministic and hold on any machine; the few timings are labelled and rough. The running example is the root's tiny character transformer (4 layers, d_model 128, 826,368 parameters, TinyShakespeare), defined in the root's src/data/README.md; scripts for this page are in src/runs/.
