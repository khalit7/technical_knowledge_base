MaxText 3: sharding by logical axis names

The problem. In Megatron-LM, tensor parallelism lives in the model: a ColumnParallelLinear knows it is split and calls the all-reduce. Change the parallelism and you change model code. MaxText's model code never names a device or a mesh axis.

How it works. Every parameter is created with logical axis names, one per dimension: the MLP input kernel of Llama 2 is ("embed", "layers", "mlp"), the query kernel ("embed_attn", "layers", "q_heads", "kv"). Activations get names too, through maybe_shard_with_logical calls inside the layers, which become with_sharding_constraint. The mesh has 12 named axes (): diloco, data, stage, fsdp, fsdp_transpose, context, context_usp_ulysses, context_autoregressive, tensor, tensor_sequence, expert, autoregressive, with sizes from the ici_*_parallelism and dcn_*_parallelism flags (inside a slice and across slices; most are 1). The 80 logical axis rules () map names to mesh axes, and logical_to_spec () applies them: "A dimension takes the first matching rule whose mesh axes no other dimension uses yet" (). Mesh axes of size 1 are then dropped, which is why the long rule lists collapse to a short PartitionSpec. Flax Linen's logical_to_mesh_axes does the same; the sibling page {{Sharding and scale|n:3c65c17b0d0d8125b084e6720cc66b3d}} explains meshes, PartitionSpec and the collectives the compiler then inserts.

The animation answers a question the YAML raises: why does embed have two rules, one with fsdp_transpose and one without ()? Because mlp's rule comes first () and takes fsdp_transpose; the first embed rule then collides and is skipped, and the second applies. One table serves every weight, and two-dimensional FSDP (fsdp on one dimension, fsdp_transpose on the other) falls out of rule order.

runs/lc_shard.py builds the abstract model with create_nnx_abstract_model (): shapes and shardings only, no memory, so a 6.7B-parameter model costs under a second on a laptop. The mesh is MaxText's get_mesh_from_config on 8 simulated CPU devices. Bytes are float32 (weight_dtype); per-device bytes count every parameter's local shard.

What it shows. Llama 2 7B comes out at 6,738,415,616 parameters in 12 arrays (the 32 layers stacked), 26.95 GB in float32 and 3.37 GB per device under FSDP over 8. Adding tensor parallelism splits mlp, q_heads and vocab over tensor and keeps embed on fsdp. Under FSDP 2 × tensor 4 the norm scales are split only two ways, over fsdp, and copied over tensor, because llama2-7b.yml replaces base.yml's ['norm', ['tensor']] with ['norm', 'fsdp'] (); that adds 399,360 bytes per device. With scan_layers=false the same model is 291 arrays and the specs lose the layers dimension.

When it fails. The running example's vocabulary of 65 cannot be split four ways. Under ici_tensor_parallelism=4 MaxText still builds the abstract model, but the token embedding and output kernel get P('tensor', 'fsdp') and P('fsdp', 'tensor'), and asking for their per-device shape raises IndivisibleError: ... implies that array axis 1 is partitioned 4 times, but the dimension size is 65. base.yml's comment on vocab_size says it: "powers of 2 for sharding". Real tokenizers' vocabularies are padded to a multiple of 128 or more for this reason (and for the MXU).

How to check. Build the abstract model and print the shardings before compiling anything (debug_sharding=true makes setup_train_loop call print_shardings_params); at start-up MaxText also calls assert_params_sufficiently_sharded with sharding_tolerance (2% by default) of parameters allowed to be unsharded. Pipeline parallelism uses the stage axis and its bubble formula is in base.yml (), measured on {{Sharding and scale|n:3c65c17b0d0d8125b084e6720cc66b3d}} (tab "Pipeline").
