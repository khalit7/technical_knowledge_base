3. How a config becomes a sharding: logical axis names and rules

In Megatron-LM tensor parallelism lives in the model: a ColumnParallelLinear knows it is split and calls the all-reduce, so changing the parallelism means changing model code. MaxText's model never names a device. Every parameter is created with one logical axis name per dimension: Llama 2's MLP input kernel is ("embed", "layers", "mlp"), the query kernel ("embed_attn", "layers", "q_heads", "kv"); activations get names through maybe_shard_with_logical, which becomes with_sharding_constraint. Three config entries then decide where everything lives:

  • The mesh: 12 named axes (), diloco, data, stage, fsdp, fsdp_transpose, context, context_usp_ulysses, context_autoregressive, tensor, tensor_sequence, expert, autoregressive, sized by ici_*_parallelism (inside a slice) and dcn_*_parallelism (across slices); most are 1.
  • The 80 logical axis rules (), applied by logical_to_spec (): "A dimension takes the first matching rule whose mesh axes no other dimension uses yet" ().
  • Size-1 axes are dropped, which is why long rule lists collapse to a short PartitionSpec. Flax Linen's logical_to_mesh_axes does the same; what a PartitionSpec does and which collectives the compiler inserts is on {{Sharding and scale|n:3c65c17b0d0d8125b084e6720cc66b3d}}.

Rule order explains a puzzle in the YAML: embed has two rules, one with fsdp_transpose and one without (). mlp's rule comes first () and takes fsdp_transpose; the first embed rule collides and is skipped, the second applies. One table serves every weight, and two-dimensional FSDP falls out of rule order.

What MaxText's own code produced measured (runs/lc_shard.py, abstract model, 8 simulated devices). Llama 2 7B: 6,738,415,616 parameters in 12 arrays (32 layers stacked), 26.95 GB in float32 and 3.37 GB per device under FSDP over 8. Adding tensor parallelism splits mlp, q_heads and vocab over tensor and keeps embed on fsdp. Under fsdp 2 × tensor 4 the norm scales are split only two ways and copied over tensor, because llama2-7b.yml replaces base.yml's ['norm', ['tensor']] with ['norm', 'fsdp'] (): an extra 399,360 bytes per device. With scan_layers=false the same model is 291 arrays and the specs lose the layers dimension.

When it fails. The running example's vocabulary of 65 cannot be split four ways. Under ici_tensor_parallelism=4 MaxText still builds the abstract model, with P('tensor', 'fsdp') and P('fsdp', 'tensor') on the embedding and output kernel, and asking for their per-device shape raises IndivisibleError: ... implies that array axis 1 is partitioned 4 times, but the dimension size is 65. base.yml's comment on vocab_size says "powers of 2 for sharding"; real vocabularies are padded to a multiple of 128 or more (which also suits the MXU).

How to check. Print the shardings of the abstract model before compiling anything (debug_sharding=true makes setup_train_loop call print_shardings_params); at start-up assert_params_sufficiently_sharded allows sharding_tolerance (2% by default) of parameters unsharded. Pipeline parallelism uses the stage axis; its bubble formula is in base.yml () and measured on {{Sharding and scale|n:3c65c17b0d0d8125b084e6720cc66b3d}}.
