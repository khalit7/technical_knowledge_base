Grid and BlockSpec

Pick a kernel from this page, then step through its grid: for every step, which block of every operand it uses, whether Pallas copies that block (only when its block index changed since the previous step), and whether the kernel body runs at all. The index maps are the kernels' own, evaluated in src/runs/pk_grid.py; the body conditions are their pl.when tests. For two kernels you can switch the index map to see the same grid before and after clamping, and for two you can replay the order the TPU interpreter actually used.

Try: Flash attention forward, "naive map", then "clamped map": the same 16 steps and the same 10 computed tiles, but K and V are copied 16 times against 9. Then LayerNorm backward: dscale and dbias are copied once and written back once, the reason its axis must be "arbitrary". Then the paged decode: watch the page index jump around the pool, and step past a sequence's end.

Each operand is drawn to its own scale (the arrays range from 8 × 32 to 16,384 × 128), with its block grid; an operand that is a single tall column of more than 8 blocks is drawn wrapped, its blocks numbered in order; three-dimensional operands are drawn as the two dimensions a block covers (flash: one head; paged: the pool as 40 pages of 16 tokens). Bytes count bf16 for activations and weights and float32 for statistics and fp32 outputs, the dtypes a TPU kernel would use. "Minimum" is every element read or written once. The recorded orders come from pltpu.InterpretParams(grid_point_recorder=...) on the real kernels; with "parallel" axes the interpreter shuffles them on purpose, even on one core, and a copy count in that order is computed here from the recorded order, per core.
