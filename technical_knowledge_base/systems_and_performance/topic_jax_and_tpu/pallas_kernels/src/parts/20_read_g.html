Design a fused grouped matmul for mixture-of-experts on a TPU: grid, BlockSpecs, semantics and test plan

Inputs: tokens sorted by expert (m × k), expert weights (E × k × n), group sizes. Precompute on the host or in jnp a metadata list mapping each grid index to (group, row tile), with tiles that straddle a group boundary listed once per group; pass it by scalar prefetch. Grid (tiles, n blocks, k blocks); lhs map (row tile, k), rhs map (group, k, n block), out map (row tile, n block); semantics ("parallel", "parallel", "arbitrary") with the k loop accumulating in a float32 scratch; mask rows outside the group on straddling tiles. Backward: a transposed variant for dW (per group) and the same kernel for dX, under custom_vjp. Tests: per-group jnp matmuls as the reference, uneven and empty groups, bf16 tolerances, the TPU interpreter with two cores, lowering for TPU; then tile sizes swept on the chip. This is megablox's design (ran here: error 2.7e-7).

How do you test a TPU kernel without a TPU, flag by flag?

A jnp reference; interpret=True for values and gradients (assert_allclose with dtype-based tolerances); interpret=pltpu.InterpretParams(detect_races=True, num_cores_or_threads=2) for ordering, DMA and out-of-bounds bugs; jax.export(..., platforms=["tpu"]) to catch illegal block shapes and unsupported ops. Then the chip, for speed and VMEM.

Why does the production flash kernel store its running max and sum 128 values wide?

VMEM and vector registers are laid out in 8 × 128 tiles; an array whose last dimension is 1 occupies a full 128-lane row anyway, and keeping the statistic replicated across lanes makes broadcasting it against a 128-wide score tile free. The same padding is why a 65-wide vocabulary wastes 49% of the lanes (section 3).
