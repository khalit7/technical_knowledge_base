Tiles and padding

How a TPU stores an array, and what an awkward shape costs. The rules come from XLA's docs/tiled_layout.md at the pinned commit 91888df6 published: 8 x 128 tiles, 2 x 128 or 4 x 128 when the second-to-last dimension is at most 2 or 4, and for 16-bit and 8-bit types a second level that packs 2 or 4 rows into each 32-bit word. Everything on this tab is calculated from those rules for a row-major array (XLA may choose another dimension order to avoid padding). Reading section 4 explains why it matters.

A matmul on the MXU: how much of the array does useful work

The weight [k, n] is cut into MXU-sized tiles (128 x 128 on v4, v5e, v5p; 256 x 256 on v6e and TPU7x); the grey part of each picture is padding the array multiplies as zeros. Activation rows stream through in groups of 8 (sublanes), so m is padded to a multiple of 8. Utilisation = m k n ÷ (pad8(m) padS(k) padS(n)), an upper bound that ignores the time to load each weight tile and assumes the compiler does not repack shapes.
