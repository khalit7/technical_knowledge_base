1. The chip, as your program meets it

The picture. A GPU is about a hundred small processors (SMs) running thousands of threads; you write kernels and launch them. A TPU chip is one or two big sequential processors, called TensorCores, and you write no kernels at all: XLA compiles your whole jitted step into one program that runs on each TensorCore, with every memory copy scheduled in advance. Five parts of a TensorCore and four connections off the chip are all a JAX programmer needs.

How many JAX devices is a chip? On v4 and v5p the two TensorCores of a chip are fused into one JAX device by default, called megacore (supports_megacore is true only for these two, jax/_src/tpu_info.py:103); on TPU7x each TensorCore is its own device, so a 4-chip host shows 8 entries in jax.devices(); v5e and v6e have one core per chip. This matters the moment you write a mesh shape: a TPU7x 2x2x1 slice is 8 devices, not 4.

Why this shapes your code. Three consequences follow, and each has a section: matmuls are fast only when the MXU is fed enough reused data (rooflines) in tiles of the right size (padding); everything else runs on a unit roughly 30 times slower, so a step full of small elementwise ops is slow whatever the MXUs can do (profiling shows it); and every byte lives in one of HBM, VMEM or a register, placed by the compiler, so memory is planned, not allocated (memory).

Go deeper. One TensorCore drawn per generation, and a matmul streamed tile by tile through a 128 and a 256 MXU, are on the root page's {{TPU and Pallas lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81ecbc04eea6aa45ea1a}}; the systolic array cycle by cycle on {{TPUs and other accelerators|n:3c65c17b0d0d8151bbc2e0ceea196187}}.

2. Five generations, published numbers

Google runs two tracks: e for efficiency (v5e, v6e: one TensorCore, 2D torus, pods of 256 chips, cheapest per FLOP) and p or unlettered for performance (v4, v5p, TPU7x: two TensorCores, 3D torus, pods of thousands). Predict: at on-demand list prices, which generation gives the most bf16 peak FLOP/s per dollar?
v6e: 918 TFLOP/s for $2.70 an hour is 340 TFLOP/s per dollar-hour, against 192 for TPU7x ($12.00), 164 for v5e ($1.20), 109 for v5p ($4.20) and 85 for v4 ($3.22) calculated from published prices. Peak per dollar is not cost per token, though: v6e's ridge point of 560 means it needs over twice v5e's batch per chip to reach that peak, its 32 GB holds less than v5p's 95 GiB, and its 2D pod stops at 256 chips.
