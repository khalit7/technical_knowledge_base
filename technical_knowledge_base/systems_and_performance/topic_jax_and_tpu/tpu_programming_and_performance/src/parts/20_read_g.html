The practical loop, which this whole topic follows: develop and test on CPU (everything traces and compiles the same way, and 8 simulated devices give you real meshes and collectives), then take the program to a TPU only to measure speed and memory, profiling before scaling.

Common mistakes, with the real messages

Testing matmul precision on a CPU
Every precision setting is accepted and ignored on CPU (the docstring says so: "Has no impact on CPU backends"), so a test that passes at 1e-5 on your laptop can fail on a TPU, where DEFAULT runs one bf16 pass. Measured here: identical results for nine settings; only TF32 is refused: 
 Fix: set precision="highest" in numerical tests, or emulate the passes as section 5 does.


A mesh axis that is not a product of whole physical axes
jax.make_mesh places each logical axis on whole torus axes; on a 4x4x4 v5p cube a model axis of 8 has nowhere to go: 
 Fix: use sizes that are products of the slice's sides (4, 16, 64 on a cube), or build the device array yourself (only v5e lets make_mesh split a physical axis, sharding_impls.py, per the root's TPU lab).


Counting chips when JAX counts TensorCores
On TPU7x every TensorCore is a JAX device, so one 4-chip host is 8 devices and a mesh written for chips fails: 
 Read jax.device_count(), never the slice name; and remember v4-8 is 4 chips.


Expecting decode to use the MXUs
A decode step reads every weight to produce one token per sequence. Below the ridge point (240 sequences per v5e chip, 560 per v6e) the step time is the weight bytes over HBM bandwidth, whatever the peak; attention over the KV cache is memory-bound at any batch. Measure tokens per second against the decode bound, not against peak FLOP/s.


Small or odd dimensions
A head size of 64 fills half a 128-wide MXU and a quarter of a 256-wide one; a last dimension of 65 is stored as 128. Nothing errors; the step is just slower and bigger. Check the shapes against section 4 before choosing a model for a TPU.


float32 activations "because PyTorch was float32"
On a TPU the matmuls round to bf16 anyway (DEFAULT precision), but float32 activations double the bytes of every memory-bound operation and of the residuals kept for the backward pass. MaxText computes in bfloat16 and keeps float32 master weights (dtype: "bfloat16", weight_dtype: "float32").


Comparing MFU numbers without asking how attention was counted
The same run reads 40.4% or 45.5% MFU depending on whether causal attention is counted as half (section 9). Ask for the FLOP formula, or compare tokens per second per chip on the same model and sequence length.


Planning a single slice the size of the pod
v5p's pod is 8,960 chips, but Cloud schedules at most 6,144 in one slice; beyond that you are on Multislice, with DCN between slices (section 7).







Interview questions


What is the critical batch size of a TPU, and why does it differ between v5e and v6e?
The number of tokens per chip above which a bf16 weight matmul becomes compute-bound: peak FLOP/s ÷ HBM bandwidth, because the intensity of [B, D] x [D, F] is about B. v5e: 240; v6e: 560, because Trillium's peak grew 4.7 times and its bandwidth 2 times. Int8 weights with bf16 compute halve it.


Your JAX model matches PyTorch to 1e-6 on CPU and to 1e-2 on TPU. What happened?
TPU DEFAULT precision computes float32 matmuls as one bf16 pass; the CPU ignores precision. Emulated on the running example, one pass moves logits by up to 0.096. Use jax.default_matmul_precision("highest") (6 passes) in the comparison test, and decide separately what precision training needs.


Why do production models use a head size of 128 and widths in multiples of 128 or 256?
The MXU is a 128 x 128 (or 256 x 256 on v6e and TPU7x) systolic array and HBM arrays are stored in 8 x 128 tiles; smaller or odd sizes are padded, wasting MXU cycles and bytes. Head size 64 is half a 128 MXU.


What does rematerialisation cost and buy, and how do you choose a policy?
It recomputes forward values in the backward pass instead of storing them. On the running example full remat cut residuals 17-fold for 28% more FLOPs; saving only matmul outputs cost 5% for a 2.7-fold cut. Choose the cheapest policy that fits HBM at the batch you need for the roofline; on TPU you can also offload named residuals to host memory over PCIe.


Why does multislice training put only data parallelism on DCN?
DCN gives a v5p chip 6.25e9 B/s against six ICI links of 9e10 B/s each way. Data parallelism needs one gradient reduction per step, which overlaps with the backward pass; FSDP and tensor parallelism need collectives inside every layer. MaxText's defaults encode it: dcn_data_parallelism: -1, dcn_tensor_parallelism: 1 # never recommended.


A run reports 50% MFU on TPU and another 43% on GPU. Which is better?
Unknown until you know how each counted FLOPs (causal attention halved or not, embeddings included or not) and which peak it divided by (dense bf16, not sparse or fp8). Compare tokens per second per chip on the same model and sequence length, then cost per token at your prices.


Design: you must pre-train a 70B dense model on about 2 trillion tokens within 30 days. Choose a TPU generation and slice, and say how you would shard it.
Compute: about 6 x 70.6e9 x 2e12 = 8.5e23 FLOPs, plus attention. At 45% MFU a v5p chip delivers 0.45 x 459e12 = 2.1e14 FLOP/s, so 30 days needs about 1,580 v5p chips; a v6e chip 4.1e14 FLOP/s, about 790 chips, but v6e pods stop at 256, so that is four slices over DCN. State: 70.6B x 16 bytes = 1.13 TB, 12 v5p chips' worth or 36 v6e chips' worth, fine at either size once sharded. Choice: one v5p slice of 2,048 chips (8x16x16, inside the 6,144-chip limit), FSDP over most of the slice with a small tensor-parallel axis on one torus axis (sizes that are products of physical axes), remat policy chosen to fit 95 GiB at a per-chip batch above the ridge (166 tokens), bf16 compute and float32 master weights; check the step against the roofline and the MFU calculator, then compare the price against v6e multislice at your negotiated rates.
