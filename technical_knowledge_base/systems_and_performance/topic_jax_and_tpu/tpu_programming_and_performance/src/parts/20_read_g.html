The practical loop this whole topic follows: develop and test on CPU (everything traces and compiles the same way, and 8 simulated devices give you real meshes and collectives), then take the program to a TPU only to measure speed and memory, profiling before scaling.


Interview rehearsal: two design questions (optional)


You must pre-train a 70B dense model on about 2 trillion tokens within 30 days. Choose a TPU generation and slice, and say how you would shard it. Compute: about 6 x 70.6e9 x 2e12 = 8.5e23 FLOPs, plus attention. At 45% MFU a v5p chip delivers 0.45 x 459e12 = 2.1e14 FLOP/s, so 30 days needs about 1,580 v5p chips; a v6e chip 4.1e14 FLOP/s, about 790 chips, but v6e pods stop at 256, so that is four slices over DCN. State: 70.6B x 16 bytes = 1.13 TB, 12 v5p chips' worth or 36 v6e chips' worth, fine at either size once sharded. Choice: one v5p slice of 2,048 chips (8x16x16, inside the 6,144-chip limit), FSDP over most of the slice with a small tensor-parallel axis on one torus axis (sizes that are products of physical axes), remat policy chosen to fit 95 GiB at a per-chip batch above the ridge (166 tokens), bf16 compute and float32 master weights; check the step against the roofline and the MFU calculator, then compare the price against v6e multislice at your negotiated rates.


A run reports 50% MFU on TPU and another 43% on GPU. Which is better? Unknown until you know how each counted FLOPs (causal attention halved or not, embeddings included or not) and which peak it divided by (dense bf16, not sparse or fp8). Compare tokens per second per chip on the same model and sequence length, then cost per token at your prices.

  • VMEM "tens of MB (128 MB on newer chips)": per TensorCore 16 MiB (v4), 128 (v5e, v6e), 64 (v5p, TPU7x), not monotonic by generation (tpu_info.py).
    • 

  • "v7 Ironwood, GA Apr 2026": Cloud's TPU7x page (2026-10-13) calls it "the latest TPU available on Google Cloud" and gives no GA date; unconfirmed. ">4x v6e per chip" is not a bf16 ratio: 2,307 ÷ 918 = 2.5x in bf16, 5.0x comparing TPU7x fp8 with v6e bf16.
    • 

  • v4 "2021": deployed in 2020, announced 2021 (the root page).
    • 

  • Colab "a single v5e chip", Kaggle "free v5e-8, roughly 20 h/month", TRC "about 30 days, v2-8/v3-8 up to v4/v5e": none is stated by a primary source (see free access above).
    • 

  • "MXU does one 128x128 matmul per cycle-ish": an MXU is 256 x 256 on v6e and TPU7x, and the published peaks do not reconcile with side2 multiply-adds per cycle at any published clock for those two (root's TPU lab); kept as unconfirmed.
