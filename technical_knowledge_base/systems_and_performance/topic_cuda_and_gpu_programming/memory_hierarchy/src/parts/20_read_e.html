8. Your kernel on a roofline: from bytes and FLOPs to a verdict

The problem. After all the rules above you still need one number: is this kernel close to what the GPU can do, and which limit is it against? The roofline answers it (the model itself, ridge points per chip and MFU are on {{Topic: hardware|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}} and {{Performance math|n:3c65c17b0d0d81faad1ef390b0e54d08}}); here is the programmer's recipe for one kernel.

  1. Count the bytes the kernel must move if every input is read once and every output written once (not what it actually moves: that is what you are judging).
  2. Count the FLOPs (an FMA is 2).
  3. Time it honestly (warm-up, many repetitions, synchronise: {{Profiling, benchmarking and correctness|n:3c65c17b0d0d813da895ffee55b0457c}}).
  4. Divide: achieved bandwidth = bytes / time, achieved FLOP/s = FLOPs / time, arithmetic intensity = FLOPs / bytes.
  5. Compare with the roof at that intensity: min(peak FLOP/s, intensity × peak bandwidth). Near the slanted part: memory-bound, so move fewer bytes (fuse, use smaller types). Near the flat part: compute-bound, so use tensor cores or do less math. Far below both: neither; the access pattern or latency hiding is broken, and sections 1 to 6 are where to look.

To see a real roof rather than a datasheet one, this page measured it: a kernel that reads one float4 per thread, does F steps of 8 independent FMAs, and writes one float, for F from 1 to 1,024, so its intensity runs from 1.35 to 820 FLOP per byte.

measured on Apple M1 Pro GPU: dots are the sweep (median of 3 runs; whiskers span the runs), dashed lines are the roofs it implies: 156 GB/s (median bandwidth of the sweep's points up to F = 16) and 4,992 GFLOP/s (its best point), a ridge at 32 FLOP per byte derived: 4,992 / 156.3. Both agree with the independent measurements in {{Topic: hardware|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}} (about 5.0 TFLOP/s FMA, 143 to 165 GB/s streaming) and sit at 94% of the 5,308 GFLOP/s that 16 cores × 128 ALUs × 2 × 1.296 GHz gives ({{metal-benchmarks|https://github.com/philipturner/metal-benchmarks}}). The single coloured points are kernels from the parent's {{Kernel lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c39f34d5e070d783c1}} placed by the recipe above, with their minimum bytes.

The two softmaxes do the same math on the same data; the eager one sits at a quarter of the bandwidth roof because, counted against the minimum bytes, it moves the tensor several times (five kernels, each a full pass). The naive matmul has an intensity of 341 FLOP per byte, ten times the ridge, yet runs at a fraction of the compute roof: by the recipe it is "far below both", and indeed its threads re-read A and B from memory for every output (no tiles, section 3 of the parent). Tiles and registers (the 4 × 4 per thread kernel) and then the matrix instructions in MLX's library close the gap.
