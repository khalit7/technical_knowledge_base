8. FP8 and FP4: the low-precision paths

What the formats are (E4M3, E5M2, E2M1, the MX and NVFP4 block formats) and how scaling granularity trades range for accuracy is on {{Quantization and Precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}; how much each generation's tensor cores gain from them is on {{Topic: hardware|n:3c65c17b0d0d8118beeefaed56da6f8e}} and {{numerical computing|n:3f05c17b0d0d81d6af0be4a9a2edba8d}}. Here: which instruction does the arithmetic, and how the libraries take the scale factors (FP8 and FP4 have so little range that every tensor, row or block carries a multiplier).

{{ScaledBlas.cpp|https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.14.1/aten/src/ATen/native/cuda/ScaledBlas.cpp}} and the CUDA 13.4.2 header vendor. PyTorch notes in the same file that "cuBLAS only started supporting row-wise scaling in version 12.9, and only for compute capability 9.0+. In other cases we use CUTLASS."

Why the block recipe exists: DeepSeek-V3 trained in FP8 on H800s with activations scaled per 1 × 128 tile and weights per 128 × 128 block, and found that Hopper's FP8 tensor-core accumulation keeps only around 14 bits, enough for nearly 2% relative error at K = 4,096. Their fix copies the tensor-core partial sums to FP32 registers every 128 elements of K and accumulates there. That is a mainloop change no library offered at the time, which is why it was a custom kernel ({{DeepGEMM|https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepGEMM}}) before it was a cuBLAS scale mode. Blackwell's block-scaled instructions move the per-block scaling into the tensor core itself. If you write your own FP8 kernel, test its error against an FP32 reference at the real K.

DeepSeek-V3's words (the quotes behind the paragraph above)

Activations are scaled "per token per 128 channels"; "the accumulation precision of FP8 GEMM on NVIDIA H800 GPUs is limited to retaining around 14 bits"; with K = 4,096 that gave "a maximum relative error of nearly 2%" in their test. Their fix: every NC = 128 elements of K, copy the partial sums to FP32 registers ("promotion to CUDA Cores") ({{DeepSeek-V3 report, section 3.3|https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v2}}) independent. The report itself is summarised on the {{DeepSeek-V3 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}}.

How to check what ran

  • Kernel names in Nsight Systems or torch.profiler: sm90_xmma_gemm_... (cuBLAS on Hopper), cutlass::... (CUTLASS inside a library), triton_... (Inductor), splitKreduce_kernel (cuBLAS chose split-K).
  • What a binary contains: cuobjdump -lelf (which architectures), cuobjdump -sass and a grep for HMMA, HGMMA, UTCHMMA, UTMALDG, as in this page's {{Instruction atlas|#t-atlas}}.
  • Which SDPA backend: force one with torch.nn.attention.sdpa_kernel and compare times and kernel names against the default.
Two mistakes that cost time (awkward shapes, timing the first call)

Shapes that defeat the heuristic. Dimensions not divisible by 8 (16 bytes of FP16/BF16) block the vectorised and TMA paths; a tile count just past a multiple of the SM count wastes a wave. Pad vocabulary and hidden sizes to multiples of 64 or 128; compare against a padded shape before blaming the library. On the M1, MLX's library GEMM loses 4.9% when N goes from 4,096 to 4,095 (median of 3 runs) measured on M1 Pro; the effect on NVIDIA GPUs, where alignment decides whether TMA and 16-byte vector paths are usable, is not measured here not run here. A decode-shaped GEMM (M = 8 rows) is a different problem altogether: it reads the 32 MiB weight matrix for almost no arithmetic, and the M1 library call streamed it at only 67.5 GB/s against the 146 to 163 GB/s that plain copies reach on the same GPU ({{GPU memory hierarchy|n:3c65c17b0d0d814a8edef7bd21c303d3}}).

Timing the first call. cuDNN runtime-fusion plans and Triton or CuTe DSL kernels compile on first use; cuBLAS loads modules lazily. Warm up, then time (the method is on {{Profiling and correctness|n:3c65c17b0d0d813da895ffee55b0457c}}).
