# Profiling, benchmarking and correctness: Nsight, honest timing, numerical checks

Notion: https://app.notion.com/p/3c65c17b0d0d813da895ffee55b0457c (child of Topic: cuda-and-gpu-programming)

Interactive page: `index.html`, built from `src/` (`sh src/build.sh`; see `src/README.md`). The four questions of performance work, taught from zero: why a stopwatch around GPU code lies (one matmul timed four ways, animated to scale, measured on the M1 Pro GPU through PyTorch MPS); honest benchmarking (warm-up in fresh processes, hot against cold cache, the spread of 300 timings in three runs, clocks, `triton.testing.do_bench` read in the Triton 3.8.0 source and ported); where the time goes (Nsight Systems from its docs, a real torch.profiler trace of a training step on CPU and MPS); why a kernel is slow (Nsight Compute 2026.3.1 reading the six sample reports NVIDIA ships, walked before and after; replay costs; metric anatomy; question-to-metric table checked against four chips); and whether the answer is right (formats and assert_close defaults, a fp16 MPS matmul against float64, accumulation error against K, summation order and split-K in ulps, float atomics measured on the M1 GPU, determinism settings, FP8 scaling, compute-sanitizer's four tools, a testing checklist), then the workflow, mistakes and interview questions. Tabs: Reading, Report reader, Numerics lab, Further reading. Rebuilds the old written page "Profiling and tools" (saved in `src/live.md`, mapped in `src/coverage.json`; corrections: ncu's clock default, do_bench's flush argument, the CUDA version); no child pages, databases or video. No NVIDIA GPU was available: NVIDIA evidence is real tool output from the CUDA 13.4.2 image and NVIDIA's own reports and docs; measurements are the M1 Pro's, labelled.
