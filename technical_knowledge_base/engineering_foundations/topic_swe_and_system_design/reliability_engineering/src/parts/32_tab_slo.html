Error budget and alerts

Pick an SLO and a traffic level for the chat assistant, then play an incident against the SRE workbook's recommended multiwindow, multi-burn-rate alerts (page at 14.4x over 1 h and 5 min, or 6x over 6 h and 30 min; ticket at 1x over 3 days and 6 h). See how much budget it spends, and whether and when each alert fires. Every number follows from the formulas shown; the incidents are illustrative.

Try: the 5-minute spike of 10% errors (no alert: it spends about 1% of the budget, not worth waking anyone), then the 30-minute full outage (pages after one minute: a full outage spends the budget 1,000 times too fast), then the slow leak (never pages, opens a ticket after about 1.3 days, yet spends a large share of the month). Move the SLO from 99.9% to 99.99% and watch the same incident become a budget emergency.

budget = (1 − SLO) × requests in the window
burn rate = error ratio / (1 − SLO)
share spent = burn rate × duration / window
window
28 days, the workbook's "four-week rolling window" (its alert thresholds are quoted for 30 days; over 28 days the same thresholds spend 7% more budget, which this tab shows rather than hides)
alert
fires when the error ratio averaged over the long window and over the short window both exceed threshold × (1 − SLO) (SRE workbook, ch. 5)

Detection is computed minute by minute on the error-ratio timeline; the line under the cards compares the time to the first page with the workbook's formula ((1 − SLO) / error ratio) × window × burn rate, which assumes no errors before the incident. Requests are spread evenly over the day (real traffic has a daily cycle, so a night-time incident spends less budget). Checked by src/recompute.py.
