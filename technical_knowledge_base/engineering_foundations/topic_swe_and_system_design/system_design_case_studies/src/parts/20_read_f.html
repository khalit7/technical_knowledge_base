Design 5An LLM gateway for a company: routing, quotas, fallbacks, cost

Every team now calls large language models (LLMs), each with its own API keys, retry code and surprise bill. An LLM gateway is one internal service every model call goes through. Its mechanisms (authentication, routing, token-based limits, budgets, fallbacks) are taught in {{Gateways and routing|n:3f15c17b0d0d81e4a964c129a3e520ae}}, caching and the degradation ladder in {{ML system design|n:3c65c17b0d0d8117b057dc2586836ec8}} (section 7, and its Cost-quality lab tab); this section runs the interview: what to ask, what the numbers say, what to build first.

Requirements
Functional
  • One OpenAI-compatible API for every model, so existing SDKs work.
  • Per-team keys, budgets, rate limits, usage and cost.
  • Routing to vendors and self-hosted models, with automatic fallback.
  • Personal data removed before it leaves the company; an audit log of every call.
Non-functional
  • 10,000 employees, 40% using AI daily, 30 requests each, plus 400,000 automated requests a day (illustrative).
  • Adds under 50 ms before the first token; streams pass through unbuffered.
  • More available than the providers behind it; spend correct to the cent per team per month.

What the numbers say. Throughput is not the problem: 18 requests a second at peak fits on one small server (run at least two). Uber's real gateway reported "16 million queries per month, with a peak QPS of 25" for "close to 30 customer teams" ({{Wang and Bansal, Uber, 2024-07-11|https://www.uber.com/blog/genai-gateway/}}), the same order as ours (an independent comparison, not a reproduction). What is big is money: about $4,200 a day, $1.5 million a year at the illustrative Sonnet 5.5 price of $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens (from {{ML system design, section 6|n:3c65c17b0d0d8117b057dc2586836ec8}}), where a 10% routing mistake is a salary. Also about 180 streams open at once (Little's law with 10-second replies), which sets connection limits and timeouts, and 5.5 GB of request logs a day holding whatever employees typed, a security asset to protect.

The design, built step by step: from keys in every service to one front door

The decision that matters: what to build first, in order

  1. Virtual keys and usage events. The gateway holds the real provider keys; teams get gateway keys; every call emits a usage event to a queue and a warehouse. This alone answers "who spends what" and is the precondition for everything else.
  2. Budgets and limits in tokens. A request's cost is unknown until it finishes, so reserve the worst case (input tokens plus max_tokens) before the call and refund the difference after, with atomic counters as in design 2 ({{Gateways and routing, sections 5 and 6|n:3f15c17b0d0d81e4a964c129a3e520ae}}, including what LiteLLM really does under concurrency).
  3. Fallbacks with one retry layer. An ordered route per alias (vendor A, vendor B, a self-hosted model), a timeout per hop, a cooldown for a failing route. Retry in the gateway only, or retries multiply (the 243× case in {{Reliability engineering, Retries|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8152b8dececf34196174}}); what a fallback costs in time is measured in {{Gateways and routing, sections 3 and 4|n:3f15c17b0d0d81e4a964c129a3e520ae}}.
  4. Data protection. Redact personal data before external calls according to each team's policy; route data that may not leave to self-hosted models. Policy beyond redaction belongs to {{Guardrails|n:3c65c17b0d0d819bbfa5dffa9246c297}}.
  5. Cost cuts, only after measuring: prompt (prefix) caching, tier routing of easy requests to a cheaper model, batch APIs for pipelines that can wait ({{ML system design, sections 4, 6 and 7|n:3c65c17b0d0d8117b057dc2586836ec8}}).

Model aliases (fast, smart) let the platform team change the model behind them; teams that need stability pin a dated version. Buy or adopt first (LiteLLM, Envoy AI Gateway, Kong, Portkey) unless data rules force you to build.

What real gateways do: Uber, Wealthsimple, LiteLLM's knobs

Uber (2024): "A pivotal design decision was to mirror the HTTP/JSON interface of the OpenAI API". The gateway generates "audit logs for comprehensive cost attribution", and its "centralized nature ... streamlines the management of usage and budgeting across various teams". A "PII redactor that anonymizes sensitive information within requests before forwarding them to third-party vendors" restores the entities in the response (Wang and Bansal, above).

Wealthsimple: its gateway redacted personal data too, "enhanced by a PII removal model we developed in-house" ({{Wealthsimple engineering, date unconfirmed (2023 or 2024)|https://engineering.wealthsimple.com/get-to-know-our-llm-gateway-and-how-it-provides-a-secure-and-reliable-space-to-use-generative-ai}}).

LiteLLM: the proxy takes per-team tpm_limit, rpm_limit, max_parallel_requests and a max_budget with a budget_duration ({{LiteLLM docs|https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/users}}), and names the fallback settings fallbacks, num_retries, allowed_fails, cooldown_time, request_timeout ({{LiteLLM reliability docs|https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/reliability}}).

Trade-offs rejected, failure modes, and what interviewers listen for
  • A shared client library instead of a service. No extra hop, but every language needs a copy, keys still live in every service, and budgets cannot be enforced across processes. Rejected: the extra few milliseconds are nothing against seconds of generation.
  • Logging full prompts and responses forever. Useful for evaluation, dangerous for privacy. Chosen instead: metadata always; content sampled, redacted, with a retention limit and access controls.
  • Exact-match response cache for everything. Cheap, but personal answers can leak between users. Only per team and per opted-in feature; semantic caching only with a measured false-hit rate ({{ML system design, section 7|n:3c65c17b0d0d8117b057dc2586836ec8}}).
  • Building it. Build when your policy needs outgrow the off-the-shelf gateways.

What interviewers listen for
  • You noticed throughput is small and moved the discussion to cost, keys, data rules and reliability.
  • Limits and budgets are counted in tokens, reserved before and settled after.
  • One retry layer, ordered fallbacks, and how you would test the fallback.
  • Usage events off the request path, attributed per team and feature.
  • A clear build order, and a buy-versus-build opinion.
