The child pages of this topic, in reading order

Ten pages, each the depth behind part of this page, taught from zero on the same chat product. This order suits a beginner: each page uses only what the pages before it taught. About 8 h in all (times as each page states them).

1. {{Building a backend API|n:3c65c17b0d0d8190ade6c34e8f653e79}}

Your first real service: HTTP byte by byte, a FastAPI service line by line, validation, errors, pagination, idempotency keys, authentication, streaming, versioning and OpenAPI, every exchange captured from a running service. About 45 min. When: first; the depth behind Step 1.

2. {{Distributed systems fundamentals|n:3c65c17b0d0d8115bc45c31d915b4d33}}

What changes when data lives on more than one machine: the network, replication, partitioning, consistency models, CAP and PACELC, quorums, clocks, consensus (Raft), leases and fencing tokens, with Postgres replication measured and Jepsen's findings. About 45 min. When: after Step 2.

3. {{Queues, streams and async work|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81aebf91df927f215b6e}}

Taking work off the request path: queues against logs, Kafka from zero, delivery semantics and what "exactly-once" really means, idempotent consumers, dead-letter queues, backpressure, the transactional outbox, sagas, Python job queues. About 50 min. When: after Step 3.

4. {{Reliability engineering|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8152b8dececf34196174}}

Staying up when dependencies fail: timeouts, retries and retry budgets (a retry storm measured), circuit breakers, bulkheads, load shedding, rate limits, safe deploys, SLOs and error budgets, incidents and postmortems. About 45 min. When: after Step 4.

5. {{Observability|n:3ef5c17b0d0d811e8490c54dd42c2b9b}}

Seeing inside a running service: structured logs, metrics and percentiles (Prometheus), distributed traces, OpenTelemetry, profiling, dashboards, alerting on SLO burn rates, on-call, and what the tools cost. About 45 min. When: after Reliability engineering.

6. {{Capacity planning and performance|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81458291d55d7443700c}}

How fast and how much: latency and percentiles, the queueing maths of "fine at 60%, down at 90%", load balancing, caching and stampedes, connection pools, profiling, load testing, and planning for the peak (measured peak factors). About 50 min. When: with Step 6's estimate.

7. {{ML system design: LLM and ML services|n:3c65c17b0d0d8117b057dc2586836ec8}}

Model services in production: the LLM gateway, routing and fallbacks, token rate limits, autoscaling GPUs, caching layers, rollouts, failure ladders, observing an LLM service, classic ML serving, and the ML system design interview. About 50 min. When: after Step 5.

8. {{Testing and quality|n:3c65c17b0d0d81548848e2ebdd2f63ee}}

Knowing your code works while you change it, with real pytest code: unit and property-based tests, tests against a real database, contract tests for model output, data and model tests, CI. About 45 min. When: once you ship code others depend on.

9. {{Code design|n:3ef5c17b0d0d817b99a0ff9701ff30cd}}

Python that others can read, change and trust: one real handler refactored in ten measured steps, abstractions that pay, code review, library design. About 40 min. When: after Testing and quality.

10. {{System design case studies|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81c3b211d75127ef5ded}}

The capstone and interview practice: seven complete designs (URL shortener, rate limiter, chat, news feed, LLM gateway, RAG service, job scheduler) run through Step 6's method, each technique linked to the page that teaches it. About 60 min. When: last, as practice.

A map from outside: Andrew Ng's five pillars. In August 2026 Andrew Ng published an AI Engineering Skills Map for software engineering fundamentals ({{Andrew Ng on X, 2026-08-28|https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/2093388974194872781}}, about 10 min). His argument: even when a coding agent writes every line, you need the fundamentals to steer it, because without them the agent makes "bad tradeoffs in latency, availability, consistency, reliability, maintainability, simplicity, and/or cost", and "the developer didn't know such tradeoffs even existed and therefore did not steer the agent". He names five skills; here is where this knowledge base teaches each. A path from beginner to pro

Read in this order. Each stage assumes the one before; do not start with the big books.

Stage 1: the vocabulary and the shape (first two weeks)

The System Design Primer

Donne Martin's free GitHub guide (about 373k stars on 2026-10-04). Read the sections on performance against scalability, latency against throughput, availability against consistency, load balancers, caches, databases and asynchronism. About 2 h for those sections. When: right after this page.

System Design Interview: An Insider's Guide, Volume 1

Alex Xu, 2nd edition, 2020, 276 pages (Open Library). Its first chapter scales a system from zero to millions of users, the same journey as this page; then the four-step interview framework and a dozen worked designs (rate limiter, key-value store, chat system). About 8 h. When: weeks 1 to 2.

Notes on Distributed Systems for Young Bloods

Jeff Hodges, 2013: the mindset of distributed systems in one essay. About 20 min. When: any time in stage 1.

Stage 2: failure and operations, from people who run large systems (months 1 to 3)

Amazon Builders' Library: five essays

Timeouts, retries and backoff with jitter (Brooker), Making retries safe with idempotent APIs (Featonby), Caching challenges and strategies (Brinkley and Chhabra), Avoiding insurmountable queue backlogs and Using load shedding to avoid overload (Yanacek). About 25 min each, 2 h in all. When: after Steps 2 to 4.

Exponential Backoff and Jitter

Marc Brooker, AWS Architecture Blog, 2015: the simulation behind "full jitter". About 15 min. When: with Step 4.

Stripe: idempotency and rate limiters

Brandur Leach (2017) on idempotency keys; Paul Tarjan (2017) on token buckets and load shedders in a real API. About 15 min each. When: with Steps 4 and 5.

Google SRE book, five chapters

Free online. Chapter 3 (Embracing Risk: error budgets), 4 (Service Level Objectives), 6 (Monitoring Distributed Systems: the four golden signals), 21 (Handling Overload) and 22 (Addressing Cascading Failures). About 3 h for the five. When: months 1 to 3, before you are on call.

Machine Learning Systems Design

Chip Huyen's free booklet: the ML-specific design and interview framing. About 1 h 30 min. When: before an ML system design interview.

Stage 3: depth (months 3 to 9)

Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2nd edition

Martin Kleppmann and Chris Riccomini, O'Reilly, March 2026 (author's site). The anchor book: storage, replication, partitioning, transactions, consistency and stream processing, explained from first principles. Read it after stage 1 gave you the vocabulary. About 15 to 20 h. When: months 3 to 6, a chapter a week.

AI Engineering

Chip Huyen, O'Reilly, 2025. For this topic, chapter 9 (Inference Optimization) and chapter 10 (AI Engineering Architecture and User Feedback) carry Step 5 into practice (table of contents). About 3 h for chapters 9 and 10; about 12 h for the book. When: after Step 5, alongside DDIA.

System Design Interview, Volume 2

Alex Xu and Sahn Lam, March 2022, 434 pages: harder worked designs (proximity service, payment system, distributed message queue). About 12 h. When: when preparing for interviews, after DDIA's first half.

Three short primary sources

Jepsen's consistency models map (about 20 min); Abadi's PACELC paper (IEEE Computer 2012, about 30 min); Bronson et al., Metastable Failures in Distributed Systems (HotOS 2021, about 30 min). About 1 h 20 min. When: with DDIA's consistency chapters and after Step 4.

Stage 4: the layer underneath (as needed)

Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces

Arpaci-Dusseau and Arpaci-Dusseau, free online, organised around virtualization, concurrency and persistence: processes, memory, threads, disks and file systems. Its paging chapters are the idea PagedAttention borrowed for the KV cache. About 20 h for the book; read the parts you need. When: when a bug or bottleneck sits below your code.

Orca and vLLM (PagedAttention)

The two papers behind continuous batching and paged KV caches (OSDI 2022, SOSP 2023). About 1 h each. When: after Topic: inference-and-serving.

The Site Reliability Workbook

Google's practical companion to the SRE book: implementing SLOs, alerting on them, managing load. About 2 h for the SLO chapters. When: when you own a service's SLO.
