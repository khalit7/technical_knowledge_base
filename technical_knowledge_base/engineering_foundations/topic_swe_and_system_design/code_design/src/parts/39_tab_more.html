Books and long reads

{{A Philosophy of Software Design|https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/book.php}}

John Ousterhout, 2nd edition 2021. Deep modules, information hiding, complexity as the enemy: the best short book on abstraction. About 4 h 45 min.

{{Ousterhout and Martin: APOSD vs Clean Code|https://github.com/johnousterhout/aposd-vs-clean-code}}

Their written debate (2024 to 2025) on method length, comments and test-driven development. About 1 h.

{{Refactoring, 2nd edition|https://refactoring.com/}}

Martin Fowler, 2018: the catalogue of safe, named refactorings (examples in JavaScript). Read the first four chapters, then use the catalogue. About 4 h for the opening chapters.

{{Characterization testing|https://michaelfeathers.silvrback.com/characterization-testing}}

Michael Feathers, 2016, on pinning what code does before changing it; his book Working Effectively with Legacy Code (2004) is the long version. 10 min (post).

{{Parnas, On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules|https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/361598.361623}}

Communications of the ACM, 1972: where information hiding comes from. About 30 min.

Short reads and talks

{{Google Engineering Practices: Code Review|https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/}}

Reviewer and author guides; calibrates what to flag and what to let go. About 1 h.

{{The Wrong Abstraction|https://sandimetz.com/blog/2016/1/20/the-wrong-abstraction}}

Sandi Metz, 2016. 5 min.

{{Functional Core, Imperative Shell|https://www.destroyallsoftware.com/screencasts/catalog/functional-core-imperative-shell}}

Gary Bernhardt's free screencast, 2012; then his talk {{Boundaries|https://www.destroyallsoftware.com/talks/boundaries}}. About 45 min for both.

{{Hexagonal architecture|https://alistair.cockburn.us/hexagonal-architecture/}}

Alistair Cockburn's ports and adapters article. 20 min.

{{PEP 8|https://peps.python.org/pep-0008/}} and {{PEP 387|https://peps.python.org/pep-0387/}}

Python's style guide, and its backwards compatibility and deprecation policy. 40 min and 10 min.

{{Semantic Versioning 2.0.0|https://semver.org/}}

The versioning contract, with its FAQ. 10 min.

{{METR: AI and experienced open-source developers|https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/}}

The randomised trial behind "19% slower", and the {{2026 update|https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/}}. 20 min.
