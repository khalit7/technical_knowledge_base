8. The design interview, run with this page

An ML or LLM design interview keeps the parent topic's method and its timing for 45 minutes (scope 3 to 10 minutes, high-level design 10 to 15, deep dive 10 to 25, wrap-up 3 to 5, after Alex Xu) and swaps the middle for this page's sections in order: frame (1), data and skew (2), evaluation (3), serving and budget (4), monitoring (5), and for an LLM product the design ladder and cost (6, 7). What interviewers reward is quantified trade-offs, not component names. Full worked designs from requirements to bottlenecks are on {{System design case studies|n:3ef5c17b0d0d81c3b211d75127ef5ded}}; its Design 5, an LLM gateway for a company, is the one to practise with after this page. Frontier-lab interviews go deeper into the engine (KV cache sizing, prefill and decode on separate GPUs, speculative decoding): {{Topic: inference-and-serving|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c08b3bc95ff45c7b13}}.

Rehearsal: the method for an LLM product, step by step (optional)
  1. Requirements (5 min). Users and tenants; traffic; what a good answer is; latency as TTFT and TPOT targets; cost per request or per user; data rules (which vendors may see what); realtime or batch. State assumptions aloud.
  2. Metrics (3 min). Offline: task metrics on an evaluation set. Online: resolution rate, thumbs, escalations. System: TTFT and TPOT percentiles, availability, cost per request. Guard metrics: safety violations, wrong-answer rate.
  3. Estimate (5 min). Tokens per second at peak, GPUs or API dollars, concurrent streams (Little's law), as in section 6's worked budget.
  4. Model and data (5 min). API or self-hosted; which tier; prompt, retrieval or fine-tune, justified by the estimate.
  5. High-level design (10 min). The hero figure and section 7's model layer, pruned to what the requirements need.
  6. Deep dives (10 to 15 min). The two the numbers flag: usually cost (routing and caching) and capacity (autoscaling and the degradation ladder), or quality (rollout and monitoring).
  7. Wrap-up. What breaks at ten times the traffic, and what you would build next.
Mistakes that are not obvious from the sections (depth)
  • Huge max_tokens by default. Where limits reserve max_tokens (OpenAI), it burns rate limit that is never used; everywhere it allows runaway cost. Set it per feature.
  • A fallback that has never carried full traffic. Its quota, prompt format, tool-call format and cold cache are untested until the outage. Test failover on a schedule (the checklist is on {{Gateways and routing|n:3f15c17b0d0d81e4a964c129a3e520ae}}, section 3).
  • An evaluation set drawn only from what the current model escalated or answered. It inherits the feedback loop of section 5; keep a random slice.
