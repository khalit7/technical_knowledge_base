Best resources

{{Kafka 4.3 design document|https://kafka.apache.org/43/design/design/}}

Delivery semantics, transactions and share groups from the source; clearer than most books. About 1 h.

{{Yanacek, Avoiding insurmountable queue backlogs|https://builder.aws.com/content/3EuRcgkTP1MI0c7zM8W6HL3WIqA/avoiding-insurmountable-queue-backlogs}}

Amazon's playbook for backlogs: age metrics, LIFO, sidelining, shuffle sharding. About 30 min.

{{Morling, Reliable microservices data exchange with the outbox pattern|https://debezium.io/blog/2019/02/19/reliable-microservices-data-exchange-with-the-outbox-pattern/}}

The outbox with Debezium, by its lead. About 25 min.

{{microservices.io patterns: outbox, idempotent consumer, saga|https://microservices.io/patterns/data/transactional-outbox.html}}

Short canonical pattern pages by Chris Richardson. About 30 min for the set.

{{Jepsen, Bufstream 0.1.0|https://jepsen.io/analyses/bufstream-0.1.0}}

How Kafka-compatible transactions fail in practice; read the Kafka sections. About 45 min.

{{Garcia-Molina and Salem, Sagas (1987)|https://www.cs.cornell.edu/andru/cs711/2002fa/reading/sagas.pdf}}

The original saga paper, short and readable. About 40 min.

{{Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2nd ed.|https://martin.kleppmann.com/}}

Kleppmann and Riccomini, O'Reilly, 2026: the chapters on stream processing and on transactions. About 3 h for those chapters.

{{Celery: tasks guide (v5.6.3)|https://github.com/celery/celery/blob/v5.6.3/docs/userguide/tasks.rst}}

Read the sections on acks, retries and idempotence before running Celery in production. About 30 min.

{{Temporal: understanding durable execution|https://docs.temporal.io/temporal}}

Workflows, activities, determinism and replay. About 30 min.
