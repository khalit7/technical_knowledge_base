A Postgres replica gotcha worth knowing
A long query on a standby can conflict with changes the primary has made (for example, rows it vacuumed away). By default the standby waits up to max_standby_streaming_delay, "The default is 30 seconds", and then cancels the query; hot_standby_feedback ("The default value is off") lets the standby ask the primary to keep those rows, at the cost of bloat on the primary ({{PostgreSQL 18 docs|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/runtime-config-replication.html}}). Analytics on a read replica is the classic way to meet this.

What testing found: Jepsen

Jepsen (Kyle Kingsbury and collaborators) runs real databases on real clusters, injects partitions, crashes, pauses and clock skew, records every operation's history, and checks it against the promised consistency model, exactly the histories of the Consistency lab at scale. As Jepsen puts it, "we can prove the presence of bugs, but not their absence" ({{NATS 2.12.1 report|https://jepsen.io/analyses/nats-2.12.1}}). The analyses most relevant to this page, oldest first:
