Start here (the old page's best resources, checked)

Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2nd edition

Kleppmann and Riccomini, O'Reilly, March 2026. The anchor book: its middle part (replication, sharding, transactions, what goes wrong in distributed systems, consistency and consensus) is this page at full length. About 15 to 20 h for the whole book; 6 h for those chapters.

Jepsen: consistency models

The clickable map from eventual to strict serializable, each model with its definition, examples and availability. About 20 min.

Notes on Distributed Systems for Young Bloods

Jeff Hodges, 2013. The mindset in one essay. About 20 min.

The System Design Primer

Breadth-first interview coverage of everything above. About 2 h for the core sections.

Kleppmann: Distributed Systems lecture notes (Cambridge)

Free notes: models, clocks, broadcast, replication, quorums, Raft, consistency, CRDTs. A rigorous, compact course. About 6 h, more with the exercises.

The Secret Lives of Data: Raft

A narrated animation of Raft elections and replication, a gentle complement to the Raft lab. About 10 min.

Primary sources cited on this page

Ongaro and Ousterhout: In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm

USENIX ATC 2014 (extended version). Figure 2 is the whole algorithm on one page. About 1.5 h.

Kleppmann: How to do distributed locking

2016. Leases, GC pauses, fencing tokens, and why Redlock depends on timing. About 25 min.

Jepsen: etcd 3.4.3

2020. A model Jepsen report: what was strict serializable, why locks are unsafe, and the fencing fix. About 40 min.

Gilbert and Lynch: Brewer's Conjecture and the Feasibility of Consistent, Available, Partition-Tolerant Web Services

2002. The CAP proof; the definitions are the point. About 45 min.

Brewer: Spanner, TrueTime and the CAP Theorem

2017. CAP from its author, with Google's incident data. About 20 min.

Abadi: Consistency Tradeoffs in Modern Distributed Database System Design

IEEE Computer, 2012. PACELC and the classification table. About 20 min.

DeCandia et al.: Dynamo and Elhemali et al.: Amazon DynamoDB

SOSP 2007 (leaderless, consistent hashing, vector clocks, sloppy quorums) and USENIX ATC 2022 (what DynamoDB actually became: Multi-Paxos per partition). Read them as a pair. About 1 h each.

Corbett et al.: Spanner

OSDI 2012. Paxos groups, TrueTime, commit wait, 2PC over Paxos. About 1.5 h.

Lamport: Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events in a Distributed System

CACM 1978. Happened-before and logical clocks. About 40 min.

Bailis and Kingsbury: The Network is Reliable

ACM Queue, 2014. A catalogue of real partitions and what they did (not re-read for this page; the site refuses automated fetches). About 30 min.

Jepsen: all analyses

Every report since 2013; read the one for the database you run. 30 to 60 min each.
