Alert lab: six ways to alert on an SLO

The SRE workbook builds its recommended alert in six steps (Reading, section 10). Here all six rules watch the same incident, minute by minute (10-second steps), so you can see when each pages, how much budget is gone by then, how long it keeps firing afterwards, and whether it pages at all. The rules and thresholds are the workbook's ({{SRE workbook, ch. 5|https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/}}); the incident shapes are illustrative, chosen to match the cases the workbook discusses. Traffic is constant and there are no errors outside the incident, as in the workbook's own formulas.

Try: the total outage (rule 1 pages in seconds, rule 3 never does, rule 2 keeps paging for 36 hours); then flapping errors (rule 3's timer keeps resetting); then the 35x burn (rule 4 stays silent while the budget empties); then the blip (only rule 1 wakes anyone, for 0.2% of the budget). Switch the SLO to 99% and watch the slow leak stop mattering; set it to 99.99% and watch everything become significant.

Top: the error ratio (log scale) with the budget line at 1 − SLO. Lanes: when each rule's page (red) or ticket (amber) condition is true. A real system would also need Alertmanager to merge overlapping notifications.

All incidents at once: precision and recall

Each cell says whether the rule paged for that incident. Green: it paged for a significant incident, or stayed quiet for an insignificant one. Red: a miss or a false page. Precision is the share of pages that were for significant incidents; recall is the share of significant incidents that paged (tickets do not count as pages).
