On-call basics, in Google's numbers (2 min)

On-call means being reachable to respond to pages for a period (a shift, often a week), on a rotation shared by the team ({{SRE book, ch. 11, Being On-Call|https://sre.google/sre-book/being-on-call/}}):

  • Response time: typically "5 minutes for user-facing or otherwise highly time-critical services, and 30 minutes for less time-sensitive systems".
  • Two people: "Many teams have both a primary and a secondary on-call rotation"; the secondary may be "a fall-through for the pages the primary on-call misses", or handle non-urgent work while the primary handles pages.
  • Load: one incident (root cause, fix, postmortem) takes about 6 hours, so "the maximum number of incidents per day is 2 per 12-hour on-call shift", and the median should be zero.
  • Team size: no more than 25% of an engineer's time on call, which with a primary and a secondary gives "the minimum number of engineers needed for on-call duty from a single-site team is eight"; "a reasonable minimum size of each team is six" for a team split across two sites.

What makes it bearable: a handoff note at every shift change; page statistics reviewed with management (Google reviews "incidents per shift" quarterly); every page followed by a fix to the alert or the system; and "Adequate compensation needs to be considered for out-of-hours support" (Google gives time off or cash, capped). Incident roles and blameless postmortems are on {{Reliability engineering|n:3ef5c17b0d0d8152b8dececf34196174}}.

If you are the first engineer with a pager you will not have eight people, but the principles scale down: page only on a few user-facing SLO burn alerts (availability and latency of the main endpoint), route everything else to tickets, put a runbook link on every page, set up an external prober (a black-box check from outside your cloud, so it still works when your monitoring does not), and keep a log of every page with what you did.

Self-host or buy? Self-hosting (Prometheus, Grafana, Loki, Tempo on object storage) is cheap per gigabyte and costs engineers' time: upgrades, capacity, and the monitoring of the monitoring. SaaS is fast to start and charges per unit, and bills grow quietly with traffic, labels and log verbosity; teams that buy usually also run the OpenTelemetry Collector to sample, drop and redact before data leaves, which is where most of the savings are. A sensible start for a small team: OpenTelemetry in the code, one SaaS or one managed Grafana stack, SLO alerts on two or three symptoms, and a monthly look at the bill by unit.

What pros run in production: a checklist (1 min)
  1. OpenTelemetry everywhere: SDK plus auto-instrumentation in every service; a Collector beside each node (agent) and a gateway pool that batches, redacts and tail-samples before the backends.
  2. RED metrics per service and per dependency as histograms with buckets at the SLO thresholds (or native histograms), low-cardinality labels only, recording rules for anything on a dashboard or in an alert.
  3. JSON logs to standard output with trace and request IDs, INFO in production, WARNING and above indexed, the rest archived; secrets never passed to the logger.
  4. Traces tail-sampled, exemplars linking latency graphs to traces, context propagated through HTTP, gRPC and queue messages.
  5. SLOs as code, with multiwindow, multi-burn-rate rules generated by tools such as {{Sloth|https://github.com/slok/sloth}} ("Prometheus SLO ... generator") or {{Pyrra|https://github.com/pyrra-dev/pyrra}} (the dashboard in section 15), unit-tested with promtool.
  6. Pages only on symptoms (SLO burn, plus a black-box prober from outside), a runbook link on every alert, grouping and inhibition, a weekly review of every page.
  7. Continuous profiling where CPU is money; monitoring outside the failure domain, so the alerting path does not depend on the cluster, DNS or network it watches.
  8. For LLM services, time to first token, tokens per second, KV cache, cost and quality on top: {{ML system design|n:3c65c17b0d0d8117b057dc2586836ec8}}, section 12.
Mistakes the sections above do not already show (1 min)

Broken traces nobody noticed. Context is lost at a header with unexpected case (this page's own bug), a thread pool, a queue, or a proxy that strips headers. Check that one request's trace crosses every hop.

Logs without trace IDs. Then a slow trace cannot lead to its log lines, nor a log line to its trace. Add trace_id and span_id in the log formatter, once.

Monitoring that shares the failure. If the monitoring runs on the cluster, DNS or service directory that failed, it fails with it (Roblox, 2021). Keep the alerting path and a prober outside.

Paging too often. Aim for fewer than two incidents per 12-hour shift; delete alerts nobody acts on.
