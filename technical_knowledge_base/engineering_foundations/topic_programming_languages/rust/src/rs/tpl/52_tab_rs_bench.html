Service benchmark: axum vs FastAPI, measured

The same service twice: tokserve (axum 0.8.9 on tokio 1.53.2, release build) and its FastAPI twin (FastAPI 0.142.2, uvicorn 0.54.0 with uvloop and httptools, pydantic 2.13.5, CPython 3.14.8). Same routes, same JSON, same load generator (oha 1.16.0, 2 threads), same machine (Apple M1 Pro, 10 cores, in normal use). Three rounds of 3 s per load, configurations in alternating order; bars show the median, whiskers the lowest and highest round. Method and raw runs: src/rs/bench/.

Experiment B: blocking the runtime, measured

tokserve with 2 async workers; 6 connections keep posting a 3.9 MB log to /count_log (about 10 ms of counting each) while /health is probed at a steady 100 requests per second, open loop (latency counted from the scheduled send time, so a stall cannot hide). Once with the counting inline on the async worker (--inline), once with spawn_blocking.

Caveats
  • Laptop in normal use: load average during the runs (10 cores). The load generator shares the machine; numbers are lower and noisier than on a quiet server.
  • Closed-loop loads (32 or 8 connections, as fast as possible) measure capacity; their latency excludes queueing that a real arrival process would see. The "@1000/s" and "@20/s" loads are open loop with oha's latency correction.
  • oha with 2 threads can be the bottleneck for axum's fastest loads; the /health and /count gaps are lower bounds.
  • axum "1 worker" still runs /count_log on tokio's blocking pool, so it used more than one core there; FastAPI's count runs on a thread but the GIL serialises it within a worker.
  • Memory: resident set of the server process and its children (uvicorn's supervisor plus workers), sampled every 0.25 s.
