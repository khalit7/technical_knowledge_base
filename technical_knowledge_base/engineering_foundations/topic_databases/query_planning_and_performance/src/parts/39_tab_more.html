Best resources
{{Using EXPLAIN|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/using-explain.html}} and {{Statistics Used by the Planner|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/planner-stats.html}}

PostgreSQL documentation, chapter 14: the clearest primary source on plans, costs, row estimates and extended statistics, with worked examples. About 1 hour.

{{Use The Index, Luke|https://use-the-index-luke.com/}}

Markus Winand's free book on indexing and SQL performance across databases: composite order, sargability, pagination, ORMs. About 4 hours.

{{The Internals of PostgreSQL, chapter 3: Query Processing|https://www.interdb.jp/pg/pgsql03.html}}

Hironobu Suzuki's walk through parser, planner and executor, with the cost formulas worked by hand. About 1.5 hours.

{{CMU 15-445, Query Planning and Optimization|https://15445.courses.cs.cmu.edu/fall2024/schedule.html}}

Andy Pavlo's database systems course, lecture 15 (Fall 2024): slides, notes and video on how optimizers search and estimate. About 1.5 hours.

{{How Good Are Query Optimizers, Really?|https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol9/p204-leis.pdf}}

Leis et al., PVLDB 2015: the Join Order Benchmark, and the evidence that cardinality estimates, not cost models, decide plan quality. About 1 hour.

{{pg_stat_statements|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/pgstatstatements.html}} and {{auto_explain|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/auto-explain.html}}

The two extensions behind section 7: every column and setting. About 30 minutes.

{{explain.dalibo.com|https://explain.dalibo.com}}, {{explain.depesz.com|https://explain.depesz.com}}, {{pgMustard's EXPLAIN glossary|https://pgmustard.com/docs/explain}}

Paste a plan to see it as a tree with the expensive nodes highlighted; a reference for every field EXPLAIN prints. About 15 minutes to learn.

{{Bao|https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03814}}, {{Neo|https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03711}}, {{Balsa|https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01441}}

The learned optimizers of section 12. Read Bao first: it steers the existing planner, the approach that has proved practical. About 45 minutes each.

{{Training a 4B model to produce 81% faster query plans than Postgres|https://rohanbansal.com/qorl}}

Rohan Bansal, 2026-09-16: QORL, with a careful measurement harness worth copying (warm-up until page counts settle). Read its results table, not its title. About 1 hour.

Measurements: PostgreSQL 16.2 (pgserver wheel binaries, with pg_stat_statements, auto_explain and pageinspect built from the 16.2 source and pg_hint_plan REL16_1_6_1), DuckDB not used on this page, psycopg 3.3.6, on an Apple M1 Pro (16 GB), 2026-10-04. The data is the parent page's generator (100,000 users, 1,000,000 chats, 10,000,000 messages) plus one table of 4,000,000 billing events. Scripts and raw outputs: src/lab/ and src/inputs/ in the repository.
