{{Cache lab|#t-lab}}

Papers and primary sources

{{Scaling Memcache at Facebook (NSDI 2013)|https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/nsdi13/nsdi13-final170_update.pdf}}

Look-aside caching at billions of requests a second: leases, stale sets, mcsqueal invalidation from the commit log, pools, Gutter, cold clusters, remote markers. The reference production paper. (The old page's link, nsdi13-nishtala.pdf, now returns 404.) About 1 hour.

{{Redis key eviction|https://redis.io/docs/latest/develop/reference/eviction/}}

The policies including LRM, the sampled approximations, LFU tuning, the INFO fields to watch. About 15 minutes.

{{Web Caching and Zipf-like Distributions (Breslau et al., INFOCOM 1999)|https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~cao/papers/zipf-implications.html}}

Why web requests follow a Zipf-like law and what that implies for hit ratios. About 40 minutes.

{{ARC (Megiddo and Modha, FAST 2003)|https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/fast03/tech/full_papers/megiddo/megiddo.pdf}}

A self-tuning balance of recency and frequency using ghost lists of recently evicted keys. About 45 minutes.

{{TinyLFU (Einziger, Friedman and Manes)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.00727}}

Frequency-based admission with a compact sketch; the policy in Caffeine. With {{Caffeine's efficiency notes|https://github.com/ben-manes/caffeine/wiki/Efficiency}}. About 1 hour.

{{Cost-Aware WWW Proxy Caching Algorithms (Cao and Irani, 1997)|https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/usits97/full_papers/cao/cao.pdf}}

GreedyDual-Size: eviction that weighs what a miss costs. About 40 minutes.

{{Optimal Probabilistic Cache Stampede Prevention (VLDB 2015)|https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~avattani/papers/cache_stampede.pdf}}

XFetch, the early-refresh rule. About 45 minutes.

{{RFC 9111, HTTP Caching|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9111}} and {{MDN HTTP caching|https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Guides/Caching}}

The header semantics; MDN is the readable version. About 30 minutes for MDN.

{{Postgres: REFRESH MATERIALIZED VIEW|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-refreshmaterializedview.html}} and {{pg_prewarm|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/pgprewarm.html}}

The two Postgres tools of section 8. About 10 minutes.

{{vLLM automatic prefix caching|https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/design/prefix_caching/}} and {{SGLang RadixAttention|https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2024-01-17-sglang/}}

The two production designs for reusing KV across requests. About 20 minutes each.

{{GPTCache|https://github.com/zilliztech/GPTCache}}

The reference open-source semantic cache; its README names the parts (embedding, vector store, similarity evaluator, eviction) and warns of false positives. About 25 minutes.

{{Portkey: semantic caching thresholds|https://portkey.ai/blog/semantic-caching-thresholds/}}

AWS's threshold sweep and practical tuning advice. About 15 minutes.

{{vCache (arXiv 2502.03771)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03771}}

{{LiteLLM caching|https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/caching}} and {{Redis LangCache|https://redis.io/blog/spring-release-2025/}}

Semantic caching as a proxy option and as a managed service. About 15 minutes each.
