12. Semantic caching: the only cache that can be wrong

The idea

An ordinary cache matches keys byte for byte, which almost never fires on natural language: "What is your refund policy?" and "How do refunds work?" are the same question with different bytes. A semantic cache turns each incoming prompt into an embedding (a vector of a few hundred numbers produced by a small model, such that texts with similar meaning get vectors pointing in similar directions), finds the most similar stored prompt, and returns that prompt's stored answer if the cosine similarity (the cosine of the angle between the two vectors: 1 means the same direction) is above a threshold. Otherwise it calls the model and stores the new pair. GPTCache made the design popular ({{repository|https://github.com/zilliztech/GPTCache}}); LiteLLM ships it as a proxy option ({{docs|https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/caching}}); Redis sells it as a service (LangCache, {{Redis, spring 2025 release|https://redis.io/blog/spring-release-2025/}}).

Every other cache on this page is exact, so a hit is provably the right answer. A semantic hit is a guess that two different questions deserve the same answer, and the threshold trades money saved against wrong answers served. That makes it a model-quality change disguised as an infrastructure change.

Measured: hit rate against wrong answers as the threshold moves

Two real labelled datasets and two small open embedding models, run on the laptop's CPU measured:

  • Quora Question Pairs (the GLUE validation split: pairs of real user questions, labelled duplicates by people; {{dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/nyu-mll/glue}}). The non-duplicates are hard on purpose: pairs on the same topic with different answers, exactly the near misses a cache must refuse.
  • PAWS ( pairs built by swapping or copying words, so almost every pair shares nearly all its words; are paraphrases; {{dataset|https://huggingface.co/datasets/google-research-datasets/paws}}).
  • Models: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (22.7 million parameters, ms to embed one query on two CPU threads) and BAAI bge-small-en-v1.5 (33.4 million, ms).

For each pair the cache holds the first question and the second arrives as a query: it is served from cache if their similarity is at least the threshold, and that is right only for a duplicate. The true-hit rate is the share of duplicates served; the false-hit rate is the share of non-duplicates served.

A real cache looks up the nearest of many entries

The pair view gives each query one candidate. A real cache compares the query against everything stored. With the first questions of 10,000 Quora pairs stored and each second question looked up against all of them (MiniLM, threshold 0.90), of queries were served from cache: by their own labelled duplicate (right), by their own labelled non-duplicate (wrong), and by a different stored question with no label. Read by eye, most of those high-similarity "other" matches are genuinely the same question asked twice on Quora (the dataset repeats popular questions), so the labelled numbers understate the hit rate a little, and the wrong-answer share remains.

What published measurements say

AWS's test on chatbot queries (Claude 3 Haiku answers, Titan embeddings), as reported by Portkey ({{Portkey, semantic caching thresholds|https://portkey.ai/blog/semantic-caching-thresholds/}}): threshold 0.99, 23.5% hits at 92.1% accuracy; 0.95, 56.0% at 92.6%; 0.90, 74.5% at 92.3%; 0.80, 87.6% at 91.8%; 0.75, 90.3% at 91.2%. On that traffic accuracy barely moved, the opposite of the hard pairs above: the curve is a property of your traffic and your model, which is the whole lesson. Portkey's advice is to start between 0.90 and 0.95, build a validation set with "queries that look similar but should produce different answers", and treat a false-positive rate above roughly 3 to 5% as the limit of the embedding model, not of the threshold. Research systems learn the threshold instead of fixing it: vCache learns one per cached prompt and reports "up to 12.5× higher cache hit and 26× lower error rates" than a static threshold ({{arXiv 2502.03771|https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03771}}).

Correction to the old page
The old page attributed "start near 0.95" to Portkey; its current post says "Start with a threshold between 0.90 and 0.95". The AWS figures it quoted are confirmed in Portkey's post (a secondary report of AWS's test).

How to run one safely, if you must

It works best on high-volume, low-variance, factual traffic (FAQ-style support questions, documentation lookups, classification prompts) and badly on open-ended generation, agent steps, or anything where users expect a fresh answer.

  1. Try the exact caches first: prompt caching (section 11), an exact-match cache on the normalised request (model, full prompt, temperature, tools), caching retrieval results and embeddings of repeated documents.
  2. Scope the key: tenant, user where answers are personal, model and prompt version, and the retrieved context. "What is my balance?" from two users is the same text with different answers. Never cache answers that depend on live state (stock, balances, order status) or on time ("current pricing").
  3. Verify before serving: require extracted entities, numbers and dates to match exactly; or re-rank the top few candidates with a cross-encoder or a small judge model instead of trusting one cosine score.
  4. Measure your own curve: a few thousand real queries with a no-cache baseline, replayed through the cache, judged; plus a deliberate slice of near-miss pairs from your domain, which the natural distribution under-represents.
  5. In production: log the similarity of every hit, audit a sample of hits, alert on the false-hit rate as a service level objective, and keep a kill switch. When the knowledge behind the answers changes, version the cache namespace (a reindex invalidates everything at once) or tag entries with the documents they came from.
