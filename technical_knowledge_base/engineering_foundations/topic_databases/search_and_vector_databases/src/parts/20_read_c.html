4. Ranking with BM25

The index finds the matches; it does not order them. Three intuitions, each fixing the flaw of the one before: term frequency (a text using the word more is more about it, but "the" would win and ten mentions are not ten times better); inverse document frequency (a word in few documents tells more: "start" in 1 of 6 questions beats "invest" in 3 of 6; tf times idf is TF-IDF); and saturation with length normalisation (repeats level off, and a long text that mentions a word once counts less than a short one). BM25 puts all three in one formula and is the default ranking in Lucene, Elasticsearch (since 5.0, {{docs|https://www.elastic.co/docs/reference/elasticsearch/index-settings/similarity}}) and OpenSearch. Work it on the six questions first: move k1 and b.

BM25 on the six questions. Query "How do I start investing in shares?", lexemes from Postgres. Predict before you drag: with b at 0, does the long "step by step guide" question rise or fall?

At b = 0 the long question rises, because its length no longer counts against it; at k1 = 0 every match of a term scores just its idf. "start" (in one question) carries the largest idf, which is why "How can I get started in stock market?" ranks high on one word.

score(D, Q) = Σt in Q idf(t) · tf(t,D)·(k1+1) / ( tf(t,D) + k1·(1 − b + b·|D| / avgdl) )
idf(t) = ln( 1 + (N − n(t) + 0.5) / (n(t) + 0.5) )
tf(t,D)
times term t occurs in document D
|D|, avgdl
D's length in terms, and the collection's average
N, n(t)
documents in all, and how many contain t
k1
how fast repeats saturate (Lucene default 1.2); 0 ignores tf
b
how much length matters (default 0.75); 0 ignores length, 1 fully normalises
Postgres's ts_rank is not BM25
Postgres ships ts_rank (term frequency, optionally weighted by document part) and ts_rank_cd (cover density: how close the matched terms sit), and "the ranking functions do not use any global information" ({{PostgreSQL docs, 12.3.3|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/textsearch-controls.html}}): no idf, so "market" counts as much as "kohinoor". On 1,000 Quora test questions, ranking the OR-matches by ts_rank scored nDCG@10 @@q_fts_or@@; BM25 over exactly the same Postgres lexemes scored @@q_bm25@@ measured. Same tokens, same matches, different order. For real BM25 inside Postgres use an extension (ParadeDB's pg_search, section 6) or compute it yourself. nDCG@10 is 1 when the labelled relevant results fill the top in the ideal order, discounted further down.

The BM25 knobs swept on real data, and why the tokeniser matters more than k1 and b, are on {{The retrieval pipeline|n:3c65c17b0d0d819899fde889e1e8b782}} (its section 2); this page keeps BM25 as Postgres and Lucene configure it.

Provenance, and Lucene's small departures from the textbook formula

BM25 ("Best Match 25") comes from the Okapi system at City University London, first run at {{TREC-3 in 1994|https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec3/papers/city.ps.gz}}; theory in {{Robertson and Zaragoza 2009|https://doi.org/10.1561/1500000019}}. Lucene's {{BM25Similarity|https://github.com/apache/lucene/blob/main/lucene/core/src/java/org/apache/lucene/search/similarities/BM25Similarity.java}} uses this idf and these defaults. Since Lucene 8.0 it drops the (k1+1) factor from the numerator, which multiplies every score by the same constant and so "doesn't affect ordering" ({{CHANGES.txt, LUCENE-8563|https://github.com/apache/lucene/blob/main/lucene/CHANGES.txt}}); this page keeps the textbook form. Lucene also stores each document's length in one byte, so its lengths are slightly rounded.

5. Phrases, prefixes and typos

Phrases work because postings keep positions: phraseto_tsquery('english', 'share market') becomes ("share" immediately followed by "market") and matched @@phrase_hits@@ questions, against @@and_hits@@ that merely contain both words measured. websearch_to_tsquery takes what users type: "share market" -india or stocks becomes . Prefixes: to_tsquery('english', 'invest:*') matches every lexeme starting with invest, Postgres's search-as-you-type, supported by GIN.

Typos are not fixed by stemming: "kohinor diamnd" matched @@typo_fts_hits@@ questions through full-text measured. Word similarity: the share of trigrams in the best-matching stretch of the question; this run kept results at 0.5 or more (the default threshold for <% is 0.6, {{docs|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/pgtrgm.html}}). A common production pattern: try full-text first, fall back to trigrams when it returns nothing. Search engines use edit distance instead (Levenshtein: single-letter insertions, deletions or substitutions): Lucene's fuzzy queries allow up to 2 edits ({{FuzzyQuery: maxEdits is capped at MAXIMUM_SUPPORTED_DISTANCE, 2|https://lucene.apache.org/core/10_5_0/core/org/apache/lucene/search/FuzzyQuery.html}}); Meilisearch allows 1 typo in words of 5 or more letters and 2 from 9 letters by default ({{Meilisearch docs|https://www.meilisearch.com/docs/learn/relevancy/typo_tolerance_settings}}).
