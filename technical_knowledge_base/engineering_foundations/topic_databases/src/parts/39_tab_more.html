A reading path, from beginner to professional

In order. Each step says when it pays to read it.

1. {{PostgreSQL tutorial|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/tutorial.html}}

The official tutorial, PostgreSQL 18 (the current release; 19 is in beta): getting started, the SQL language, then views, foreign keys, transactions and window functions. Read first, with a local Postgres open. About 2 hours.

2. {{Use The Index, Luke|https://use-the-index-luke.com/}}

Markus Winand's free web book on indexing across Postgres, MySQL, Oracle, SQL Server and Db2: how an index works, the WHERE clause, joins, sorting, why a query does a full scan. After section 4, before your first slow-query hunt. About 4 hours.

3. {{Using EXPLAIN|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/using-explain.html}}

PostgreSQL 18 manual, section 14.1: reading plans, costs, EXPLAIN ANALYZE, and how the planner's estimates are computed. The day a query is slow, alongside the {{Query plans|#t-plan}} tab. About 45 minutes.

4. {{Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2nd edition|https://dataintensive.net/}}

Martin Kleppmann and Chris Riccomini, O'Reilly, March 2026 ({{author's site|https://martin.kleppmann.com/}}). The standard book on how data systems work and fail: data models, storage engines, replication, sharding, transactions, streams. After this page, a chapter at a time. About 15 hours.

5. {{CMU 15-445/645 Database Systems|https://15445.courses.cs.cmu.edu/}}

Carnegie Mellon's introductory course, Fall 2026 taught by Jignesh Patel (25 lectures from the relational model to recovery and distributed databases; this semester's videos are on CMU's Panopto). Andy Pavlo's recorded Fall 2025 lectures are public on {{YouTube|https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLSE8ODhjZXjYMAgsGH-GtY5rJYZ6zjsd5}}. When you want to know how an engine is built; the projects build one. About 30 hours of lectures.

6. {{Database Internals|https://www.databass.dev/}}

Alex Petrov, O'Reilly, 2019. Part I is storage engines (B-trees, files, the log, LSM-trees); Part II distributed systems (failure detection, consensus). After DDIA, for the level below it. About 9 hours 30 minutes.

7. {{Jepsen analyses|https://jepsen.io/analyses}}

Kyle Kingsbury's tests of what databases actually do under network faults and crashes, against what their documentation claims, with a {{map of consistency models|https://jepsen.io/consistency}}. Recent: {{Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL 17.4|https://jepsen.io/analyses/amazon-rds-for-postgresql-17.4}} (2025). Before you choose or operate a system: read its analysis. About 1 hour per analysis.

8. {{The Log: what every software engineer should know about real-time data's unifying abstraction|https://web.archive.org/web/20260612105408/https://engineering.linkedin.com/distributed-systems/log-what-every-software-engineer-should-know-about-real-time-datas-unifying}}

Jay Kreps, LinkedIn Engineering, December 2013. Why one append-only log sits under replication, change streams and stream processing: section 4's WAL, generalised. The original LinkedIn address returned "not found" on 2026-10-04, so this is the Internet Archive's copy from June 2026. After section 4 and DDIA's chapter on streams. About 1 hour.

Papers and primary sources cited on this page

{{A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks|https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/362384.362685}}

E. F. Codd, Communications of the ACM, 1970: the relational model. About 1 hour.

{{A Critique of ANSI SQL Isolation Levels|https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0701157}}

Berenson, Bernstein, Gray, Melton, O'Neil and O'Neil, SIGMOD 1995: why the standard's isolation levels are ambiguous; snapshot isolation and write skew. About 1 hour.

{{Transaction isolation in PostgreSQL|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/transaction-iso.html}}

The manual's chapter behind section 3's table and fixes. About 40 minutes.

{{HNSW|https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09320}}

Malkov and Yashunin: the graph index behind most vector search. About 45 minutes.

{{Learned Postgres query plans|https://rohanbansal.com/qorl}}

Rohan Bansal, 2026-09-16: the section 8 result, with its measurement protocol. About 30 minutes.

{{Just Use Postgres for Everything|https://www.amazingcto.com/postgres-for-everything/}}

Stephan Schmidt: the short argument for the Postgres default, with the extensions that replace other stores. An opinion piece. About 10 minutes.
