B-tree and LSM lab

Insert the same keys into a small B-tree and a small LSM-tree and watch what each one writes. Pages here hold a handful of keys instead of hundreds, so a few hundred inserts show what millions do on a real server. The rules are the real ones: a B-tree splits a full page in two (or keeps the left page 90% full when appending at the right edge, as Postgres does), a checkpoint writes each dirty page once, and the first change to a page after a checkpoint logs a full-page image; an LSM-tree fills a memtable, flushes it as a sorted run, and merges runs by leveled or tiered compaction. Sizes are in key slots, not bytes. illustrative The measured versions of the same counters, on a real Postgres and a real RocksDB, are in the Reading tab's sections 3, 5 and 7.

Try: insert 1,000 ascending keys, note the B-tree's page writes and fill, press Reset, then insert 1,000 random keys: fill drops to about 70% and page writes multiply, which is the measured UUID v4 story of section 5. Then switch the LSM to tiered: fewer slots written, more runs to probe. Use Overwrite to see an LSM keep stale versions until compaction.

Write amplification counts every slot written to storage per key inserted: for the B-tree, page writes at checkpoints plus full-page images in the log plus one log record per insert; for the LSM-tree, one log record per insert plus every slot written by flushes and compactions. Space amplification is slots on disk per live key. Read cost is pages read (B-tree height) or sorted runs searched for one lookup; with bloom filters a run is searched only when its filter says "maybe" (false-positive rate 0.82% at 10 bits per key, formula in section 6).
