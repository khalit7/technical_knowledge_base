A reading path, from beginner to professional

1. {{PostgreSQL manual, chapter 13: Concurrency Control|https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/mvcc.html}}

The official account of MVCC, the isolation levels, explicit locking and serializable's rules, with examples. Read first, sections 13.1 to 13.4. About 1 hour.

2. {{Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2nd edition|https://dataintensive.net/}}

Martin Kleppmann and Chris Riccomini, O'Reilly, 2026. The transactions chapter is the clearest book treatment of every anomaly on this page, including the on-call doctors. About 2 hours for the chapter.

3. {{Hermitage|https://github.com/ept/hermitage}}

Kleppmann's test suite (2014 onward): every anomaly as a two-session script, run against Postgres, MySQL, Oracle, SQL Server, CockroachDB and others. The model for this page's recordings. About 30 minutes.

4. {{PostgreSQL wiki: SSI|https://wiki.postgresql.org/wiki/SSI}}

Worked serializable examples, including the deposit report of section 3. About 30 minutes.

5. {{Implementing Stripe-like Idempotency Keys in Postgres|https://brandur.org/idempotency-keys}}

Brandur Leach, 2017: atomic phases, recovery points and serializable transactions in a real API design. About 40 minutes.

6. {{Zero-downtime Postgres migrations: the hard parts|https://gocardless.com/blog/zero-downtime-postgres-migrations-the-hard-parts/}}

GoCardless engineering blog: the lock queue incident of section 6 and lock_timeout. About 15 minutes.

7. {{MySQL 8.4 manual: InnoDB locking and transaction model|https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/innodb-locking-transaction-model.html}}

Record, gap and next-key locks, consistent reads, the four levels as InnoDB implements them. About 1 hour.

8. {{Jepsen: PostgreSQL 12.3|https://jepsen.io/analyses/postgresql-12.3}} and {{Jepsen: MySQL 8.0.34|https://jepsen.io/analyses/mysql-8.0.34}}

2020-06-12 and 2023-12-19: how professionals test isolation claims, and what they found. About 45 minutes each.

Papers

{{A Critique of ANSI SQL Isolation Levels|https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0701157}}

Berenson, Bernstein, Gray, Melton, O'Neil and O'Neil, SIGMOD 1995: lost update, read skew, write skew and snapshot isolation named. About 1 hour.

{{Weak Consistency: A Generalized Theory and Optimistic Implementations|https://pmg.csail.mit.edu/papers/adya-phd.pdf}}

Atul Adya's MIT thesis, 1999: isolation levels as forbidden dependency cycles (G0, G1, G2). Skim chapters 3 and 4, about 1.5 hours.

{{A Read-Only Transaction Anomaly Under Snapshot Isolation|https://www.cs.umb.edu/~poneil/ROAnom.pdf}}

Fekete, O'Neil and O'Neil, SIGMOD Record 2004: the anomaly of section 3, card 8. About 30 minutes.

{{Serializable Isolation for Snapshot Databases|https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1376616.1376690}}

Cahill, Röhm and Fekete, SIGMOD 2008: the dangerous-structure test that SSI is built on. About 1 hour.

{{Serializable Snapshot Isolation in PostgreSQL|https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4179}}

Ports and Grittner, VLDB 2012: how Postgres implemented SSI, its read-only optimisations and its measured cost. About 1 hour.
