# Practice drills (t-drill): ideas built and rejected

Question the tab answers: "can I now do it myself, without looking?" The reader is rusty and wants four things (read papers, derive and debug, interviews, research), so the tab is graded practice, not more explanation: every problem points back to the Reading section or data tab that teaches it.

## Built

## Sources of problem ideas (wording is our own)
- Chip Huyen, Introduction to Machine Learning Interviews, chapter 5 (5.1.1 vectors, 5.1.2 matrices, 5.1.4 calculus and convex optimisation, 5.2.1.2 probability questions, 5.2.2 stats): eigenvalues against trace and determinant, Jacobian shapes, convexity of the logistic loss, Hessian test of critical points, Jensen, constrained optimisation, Bayes diagnosis, birthday collisions, independence against zero covariance, coin fairness.
- Mathematics for Machine Learning (Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong): inner products, rank, eigendecomposition, SVD, Eckart-Young, MLE and MAP.
- Stanford CS229: problem set 1 (summer 2019) problem 1(a), the logistic Hessian is PSD (checked in the PDF); main notes (least squares as MLE, logistic regression, Hoeffding); probability review.
- Kaplan et al. 2020, section 2.1 and Table 1 (read from the PDF): 24 d_model^2 non-context FLOPs per token per layer, 2 n_ctx d_attn context FLOPs, "d_model > n_ctx/12", C = 6N per token, backward about twice forward.
- Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995 (mammography numbers 1%, 80%, 9.6%); Saito and Rehmsmeier 2015; Miller 2024 (error bars, paired differences); Chen and Guestrin 2016 (XGBoost leaf weight); Kingma and Ba 2015 (Adam); Cohen et al. 2021 (edge of stability); Dauphin et al. 2014 (saddles); van den Oord et al. 2018 and Poole et al. 2019 (InfoNCE); Bishop PRML 1.2.4, 1.6.1, 10.1.2; Williams 1992; Mohamed et al. 2020; Karpathy 2019 (loss at init); The Pile (bits per byte).
- The old Topic: math page: the z = (2, 1, 0) worked example and the Bernoulli curvature triple (recomputed; both correct), and the flagged questions (independent against dependent variables, entropy, Bernoulli and classification, cost-function gradients, second-order differentiation), each now a problem.

## Rejected
- A free-text "explain it" box graded by keywords: unreliable in a sandbox with no model, and it would reward keyword stuffing; the self-check list does the job honestly.
- Spaced repetition scheduling (due dates per problem): needs reliable persistent storage across devices, which localStorage is not; "Hide solved" plus "Pick one for me" covers most of the value.
- Randomised numbers per attempt: every number must be recomputed offline and visible in the solution steps; generating them at run time would ship unverified numbers and need a runtime TeX engine for the steps.
- A separate animation per derivation: one animation (the softmax CE gradient, the derivation used most) is enough here; the Gradient lab owns live gradients.

## What the methodology lacked for this tab
Section 2 scores visuals that reproduce a published figure. A practice tab's equivalent is "every answer recomputed and every step number checked against the page text", which `recompute.py` part 2 does mechanically; worth adding as a rule for any quiz-like tab.
