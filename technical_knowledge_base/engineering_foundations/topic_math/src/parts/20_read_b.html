The step itself is gradient descent, \(W \leftarrow W - \eta\,\nabla_W L\). On this model the new scores are \(z - \eta \lVert x \rVert^2 (p - y)\) with \(\lVert x \rVert^2 = 5\), so at \(\eta = 0.1\) each score moves by half its gradient and sat's probability rises from 0.090 to 0.170. One backward pass did two matrix products (\((p - y)\,x^\top\) for the weights, \(W^\top (p - y)\) for the input) against the forward pass's one: the factor of two behind "training costs about 6 FLOPs per parameter per token", derived on {{Matrix calculus and backprop, section 8|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93#mc-s8}}.
