Almost all the mathematics an ML engineer needs shows up in one training step of one model. This page follows a single prediction of a tiny language model through that step, and introduces each area of maths at the moment the step needs it, never before.

The running example: a three-word language model

The model knows three words (its vocabulary): cat, dog and sat. It has just read "the cat" and must guess the next word. The right answer is sat. A real language model does exactly this, with a vocabulary of about 100,000 pieces of words (tokens) instead of three, and with thousands of numbers per word instead of two.

  • "The cat" has been turned into a list of two numbers, \(x = (2, 1)\). How a real model gets this list is the job of its earlier layers; here we simply fix it.
  • The model has one table of six numbers, \(W\), with one row per word: cat \((1, 0)\), dog \((0, 1)\), sat \((1, -2)\). These six numbers are what training changes.
  • With these numbers the model scores the words \((2, 1, 0)\), turns the scores into probabilities \((0.665, 0.245, 0.090)\), and so bets on cat. It gave the right word only 9%. It is wrong, and the rest of the page is about what happens next.

The seven steps, one lesson each

Vectors and matrices: turn the input into scoresA word is a list of numbers (a vector); a layer is a table of numbers (a matrix). Multiplying them takes one dot product per row, and each dot product measures how well the input matches that word. Probability: turn scores into a bet, and score the betSoftmax turns any scores into probabilities. The loss is minus the log of the probability given to what really happened; choose a distribution and the loss follows. Information: what the loss number meansMinus log probability is surprise, measured in bits or nats. The training loss is an average surprise (cross-entropy); it can never fall below the data's own unpredictability (entropy). Derivatives: which way to change each numberA derivative says how much the loss moves when one number moves. The chain rule multiplies local slopes; for softmax plus cross-entropy the result collapses to "prediction minus target". Optimisation: how far to stepStep against the gradient, scaled by a learning rate. Curvature sets the largest safe step: above 2 divided by the largest curvature, the steps overshoot and grow. Statistics: was the improvement real?One sentence improving proves little. Average many, and the error bar shrinks like one over the square root of how many; compare before and after on the same sentences. Reading an equation in a paperName every symbol, find every shape, read the operations inside out, plug in tiny numbers, then ask why each piece is there.
The one idea to keep
Training is a loop of four moves: score (linear algebra), bet and pay (probability and information), find the downhill direction (derivatives) and step (optimisation), followed by one question asked from outside the loop, did it really get better (statistics). When a paper or a training run confuses you, ask which of the five moves it is about.
