Glossary

Every term and symbol introduced on this tab, in the order the training step meets them. Each name links to the place it is explained.

Terms

Vocabulary: the set of tokens a language model can output (3 words here, about 100,000 in real models).
Vector: an ordered list of numbers; its dimension is how many.
Matrix: a table of numbers; its shape is rows by columns.
Dot product: multiply two vectors entry by entry and add; large when they point the same way.
Matrix-vector product: one dot product per row of the matrix.
Weight matrix: the numbers a layer learns.
Logits: raw scores before softmax.
Length (norm): \(\lVert x \rVert = \sqrt{\sum_i x_i^2}\).
Cosine similarity: the dot product divided by both lengths; 1 aligned, 0 at right angles.
Transpose: rows and columns swapped, \(W^\top\).
Batch: several inputs processed together as one matrix.
Outer product: a column times a row, giving a rank-1 matrix.
Rank: how many independent directions a matrix really uses.
Singular value decomposition (SVD): any matrix as a sum of rank-1 layers, largest first.
LoRA: fine-tuning by learning only a low-rank change \(BA\).
Probability distribution: non-negative numbers, one per outcome, adding up to 1.
Categorical distribution: one draw among several outcomes.
Softmax: exponentiate the scores, then divide by their total.
Exponential: \(e^z\), always positive; \(e \approx 2.718\).
Temperature: divide the scores by \(T\) before softmax; low sharpens, high flattens.
Likelihood: the probability the model gave to what actually happened.
Independent (i.i.d.): knowing one tells nothing about another; identically distributed means drawn from the same distribution.
Negative log-likelihood (NLL): \(-\ln p_y\), the loss.
Maximum-likelihood estimation (MLE): choose the weights that make the data most probable.
MAP estimation: MLE plus a prior; "loss plus regulariser".
Proper scoring rule: a loss whose expected value is lowest at the true probabilities.
Bernoulli: a yes-or-no outcome; its NLL is binary cross-entropy.
Gaussian: the bell curve; its NLL is squared error.
Surprise: \(-\log p\) of an outcome.
Bit and nat: units of surprise with \(\log_2\) and \(\ln\); 1 nat = 1.443 bits.
Entropy: average surprise; the shortest average code length.
Cross-entropy: average surprise when the data comes from \(q\) and you bet \(p\); the training loss.
KL divergence: cross-entropy minus entropy; the removable part of the loss; not symmetric.
One-hot: a vector with 1 for the right answer and 0 elsewhere.
Perplexity: \(e^{\text{loss}}\); an effective number of choices; tokenizer-dependent.
Bits per byte: total surprise in bits divided by text length in bytes; tokenizer-free.
Derivative: a slope, change in output per tiny change in input.
Partial derivative: the slope along one input with the others fixed.
Gradient: all partial derivatives together; points uphill.
Chain rule: the slope through a chain of steps is the product of their slopes.
Backpropagation: the chain rule run from the loss backwards through every layer.
Jacobian: the table of every output's slope with respect to every input.
Vector-Jacobian product: what each layer computes in backprop, without building its Jacobian.
Gradient descent: \(W \leftarrow W - \eta \nabla_W L\).
Learning rate: the step size \(\eta\).
Curvature or second derivative: how fast the slope changes.
Hessian: the matrix of second derivatives.
Eigenvalues: here, the curvatures along the Hessian's principal directions.
Condition number: largest over smallest curvature; how lopsided the bowl is.
Newton's method: divide the gradient by the curvature, \(-H^{-1}g\).
Adam: a per-weight step size from a moving average of squared gradients.
Convex: bowl-shaped everywhere; any minimum is the global one.
Mean, standard deviation, variance: the average, the typical distance from it, and that distance squared.
Standard error: the uncertainty of a mean, \(s/\sqrt{n}\).
Central limit theorem: averages of many independent measurements are approximately normal.
Paired comparison: compare two models example by example so shared difficulty cancels.
Minibatch gradient: the average gradient over \(B\) examples; noise shrinks like \(1/\sqrt{B}\).
InfoNCE: cross-entropy over one positive and \(N - 1\) negatives; contrastive learning's loss.
Mutual information: how much knowing one variable tells you about another.
Score (statistics): the slope of the log-likelihood.
Fisher information: the variance of the score; the expected curvature of the NLL.

Symbols

\(x\), \(W\), \(z\): the input vector, the weight matrix, the scores \(z = Wx\).
\(W_{ij}\), \(w_i\): the entry in row \(i\), column \(j\); row \(i\).
\(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\): add up over \(j\) from 1 to \(d\).
\(\lVert x \rVert\): the length of \(x\).
\(\top\): transpose.
\(p\), \(p_i\), \(y\): the model's probabilities; the probability of word \(i\); the right answer (an index, or a one-hot vector).
\(e\), \(\ln\), \(\log_2\): Euler's number; the natural log; the base-2 log.
\(\propto\): proportional to.
\(L\): the loss.
\(P(Y \mid X)\): the probability of \(Y\) given \(X\).
\(H(p)\), \(H(q, p)\), \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p)\): entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence.
\(\partial\), \(\nabla\): partial derivative; gradient (\(\nabla_W L\): gradient of \(L\) with respect to \(W\)).
\(\sigma(z)\): the sigmoid \(1/(1 + e^{-z})\). (Elsewhere \(\sigma\) can mean a standard deviation; context decides.)
\(\eta\), \(\leftarrow\): the learning rate; "replace with".
\(\lambda\), \(\lambda_{\max}\), \(\kappa\): a curvature (eigenvalue), the largest one, the condition number.
\(H\), \(g\): the Hessian and the gradient (in section 3, \(H\) is entropy: papers reuse letters).
\(\bar{d}\), \(s\), \(n\), \(\mathrm{SE}\): a mean, a standard deviation, a sample size, a standard error.
\(Q\), \(K\), \(V\), \(d_k\): attention's queries, keys, values and key length.
