A reading path, from rusty to research

In order. Each step says when to read it; stop when your goal is met. The first two steps rebuild intuition, the next four rebuild technique, the last two take you to research depth.

1. 3Blue1Brown, Essence of Linear Algebra

16 short animated videos: vectors, matrices as maps, determinants, eigenvectors. When: first, before any book, if section 1 felt shaky. About 3 h.

2. 3Blue1Brown, Essence of Calculus

12 videos: derivatives as slopes, the chain rule, Taylor series. When: right after step 1, before section 4's derivation feels routine. About 3 h 10 min.

3. Mathematics for Machine Learning (Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong)

Cambridge University Press 2020, free PDF. Chapters 2 to 7 (linear algebra, analytic geometry, matrix decompositions, vector calculus, probability and distributions, continuous optimisation) cover this page in ML notation. When: the backbone text once the videos are done; read the chapter matching whichever section of this page you want deeper. About 20 to 30 h for chapters 2 to 7.

4. The Matrix Calculus You Need For Deep Learning (Parr and Howard, 2018)

Jacobians, the vector chain rule and layout conventions, assuming only school calculus. When: before deriving your first backward pass by hand. About 1 h 30 min to 2 h 30 min, with a pencil.

5. Visual Information Theory (Chris Olah, 14 October 2015)

Entropy, cross-entropy, KL and mutual information as pictures of codes. When: right after section 3 of this page. About 45 min to 1 h.

6. Stanford CS229 lecture notes (Ma and Ng)

Losses derived as maximum likelihood (generalised linear models), plus the short linear algebra review (Kolter, 26 pages) and probability review (Maleki and Do, 12 pages). When: the reviews as a checklist before interviews; the notes' first chapters to see section 2 done in general. About 1 h for the probability review, 2 to 3 h for the linear algebra review, about 5 h for the first chapters of the notes.

7. Gilbert Strang, MIT 18.06 Linear Algebra and 18.065 Matrix Methods in Data Analysis, Signal Processing, and Machine Learning

18.06: 34 recorded lectures, the classic course. 18.065 (2018, with the book Linear Algebra and Learning from Data, 2019): lectures 1 to 9 on least squares, the SVD, Eckart-Young and norms, and 21 to 27 on gradient descent, momentum, SGD and backprop. When: when you want the SVD and low-rank methods (LoRA, compression) properly, or a rigorous full course. About 12 h for the 18.065 lectures named; about 25 h for all of 18.06.

8. Convex Optimization (Boyd and Vandenberghe, 2004)

Free PDF. Chapters 2 to 5: convex sets, convex functions, convex problems, duality (Lagrange multipliers done properly). For a gentler start, the same authors' free Introduction to Applied Linear Algebra (2018). When: research on constrained objectives (KL-constrained updates, regularisation as a constraint); optional for most engineering work. About 10 h for chapters 2, 3 and 5.1 to 5.5; 20 to 30 h for all four.

9. Deep Learning: Foundations and Concepts (Bishop and Bishop, 2024) or Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning (Bishop, 2006)

Both free to read online. The 2024 book's chapters 2 (probabilities) and 7 (gradient descent) cover sections 2, 5 and 6 at research depth; PRML chapters 1 and 2 are the classic treatment of probability for ML. When: when you start reading papers that assume Bayesian reasoning or new losses. About 4 to 6 h for the two 2024 chapters; 8 to 10 h for PRML chapters 1 and 2.

10. Information Theory, Inference, and Learning Algorithms (MacKay, 2003)

Free PDF. Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 8 (introduction; probability, entropy and inference; the source coding theorem; dependent random variables). When: after Olah, for research on estimators, bounds and compression. About 8 to 12 h.

Reference: The Matrix Cookbook (Petersen and Pedersen, version of 15 November 2012)

72 pages of matrix identities and derivatives. When: look things up while deriving; do not read it through. About 30 min to learn your way around.

Primary sources cited on the Reading tab

  • Vaswani et al. 2017, Attention Is All You Need: equation 1 and footnote 4 (the \(\sqrt{d_k}\) scaling). About 1 h.
  • Hu et al. 2021, LoRA: low intrinsic rank, \(B\) initialised to zero, rank 1 often enough on GPT-3. About 1 h.
  • Kaplan et al. 2020, Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models, section 2.1: about 6N operations per training token. About 10 min for the section.
  • Chen et al. 2016, Training Deep Nets with Sublinear Memory Cost: activation checkpointing. About 40 min.
  • Kingma and Ba 2014, Adam: the second-moment estimate and its relation to the diagonal Fisher (section 5 of the paper). About 45 min.
  • Cohen et al. 2021, Gradient Descent on Neural Networks Typically Occurs at the Edge of Stability. About 1 h.
  • Dauphin et al. 2014, Identifying and attacking the saddle point problem. About 45 min.
  • van den Oord et al. 2018, Contrastive Predictive Coding: the InfoNCE bound \(I \ge \log N - L\). About 45 min.
  • Radford et al. 2021, CLIP: batch of 32,768 pairs, learned temperature. About 1 h for the method sections.
  • Gao et al. 2020, The Pile: bits per byte and 0.29335 GPT-2 tokens per byte. About 15 min for the evaluation section.
  • Martens 2020, New insights and perspectives on the natural gradient method: when the Fisher equals the generalised Gauss-Newton matrix. About 2 h; research depth.
