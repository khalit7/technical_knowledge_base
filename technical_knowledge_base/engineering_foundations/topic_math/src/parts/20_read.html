The idea in one screen

One training step of one model uses almost all of the mathematics of machine learning, each area for one move: linear algebra scores the input, probability turns the scores into a bet and prices it, information theory says what the price means, calculus finds the downhill direction, optimisation decides how far to step, and statistics asks, from outside the loop, whether the model really got better. Floating point sits under every move, and variational inference is where the areas meet.

The running example every child page uses

A language model with a three-word vocabulary, cat, dog and sat, has read "the cat"; the right next word is sat. The context is the vector \(x = (2, 1)\) and the model is one weight matrix \(W\) with one row per word: cat \((1, 0)\), dog \((0, 1)\), sat \((1, -2)\). It scores the words \(z = Wx = (2, 1, 0)\), turns the scores into the bet \(p = (0.665, 0.245, 0.090)\), and so bets on cat and gives the truth only 9%. A real language model does the same with a vocabulary of about 100,000 tokens and thousands of numbers per word. Every child page works its examples on this model (or on the two-layer version in the {{Gradient lab|#t-grad}}), so a number met on one page can be checked on another.

Fig. 1 plays one training step of it, forward along the front row and backward along the row behind it, and pins each child page's concept where it acts. Every number is the tiny model's own, computed on this page; each step links to the section that owns it.

  1. Input and weights (linear algebra). The context "the cat" is the vector \(x = (2, 1)\); the whole model is one weight matrix \(W\), the glass plate, with one row per word. {{Linear algebra, section 1|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d89c26e1dab2fcb701#rd-vec}}
  2. Score (linear algebra). \(z = Wx = (2, 1, 0)\): one dot product per row, large when a word's row points where \(x\) points. {{Linear algebra, section 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d89c26e1dab2fcb701#rd-mul}}
  3. Bet (probability). Softmax turns the scores into the bet \(p = (0.665, 0.245, 0.090)\): most on cat, 9% on sat, the truth. {{Probability, sections 6 and 7|n:3c65c17b0d0d815f9a47d613409b5a0c#rd-s6}}
  4. Pay (information theory). The loss is the surprise at the truth, \(L = -\ln 0.090 = 2.408\) nats \(= 3.473\) bits, above the uniform guess's \(\ln 3 = 1.099\) (the flat plate). {{Information theory, sections 1 and 3|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c6baf3f778a62e14e5#it-s1}}
  5. Gradient on the scores (calculus). The backward pass starts at the loss and runs along the back row: \(\nabla_z L = p - y = (0.665, 0.245, -0.910)\), prediction minus target. {{Calculus and optimisation, section 2|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eb8b49ed6a0f897f3b#rd-loss}}
  6. Gradient on the weights (matrix calculus). \(\nabla_W L = (p - y)\,x^\top\), the bar field behind the plate: shaped like \(W\), of rank 1, fed by the gradient arriving and the input the forward pass kept. {{Matrix calculus and backprop, section 5|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93#mc-s5}}
  7. Step (optimisation). \(W \leftarrow W - 0.1\,\nabla_W L\), then the forward pass again: sat's probability rises from 0.090 to 0.170 and the loss falls to 1.773. The learning rate stays under the local limit \(2/\lambda_{\max} = 2/1.855 = 1.08\). {{Calculus and optimisation, section 4|n:3c65c17b0d0d81eb8b49ed6a0f897f3b#rd-gd}}
  8. Was it real? (statistics). The training sentence's loss fell by construction; only the average change on held-out sentences, with its error bar, says the model improved. With a spread of 0.5 nats per sentence and 100 sentences, a 0.1-nat gain has a 95% error bar of \(\pm 1.96 \times 0.5/\sqrt{100} = \pm 0.098\): it only just clears zero. {{Statistics, sections 5 and 7|n:3f05c17b0d0d810f896cf688ce9aed1b#rd-s5}}
  9. Under every box (numerical computing). Every number above is a float: \(e^{z}\) passes the largest float32 at \(z = 88.72\), so a real softmax subtracts the largest score first. Where all the areas meet, in a model with a hidden cause, is the ELBO. {{Numerical computing, section 7|n:3f05c17b0d0d81d6af0be4a9a2edba8d#rd-soft}}; {{Variational inference, section 3|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530#vi-s3}}

You need: nothing; this is the entry page, and each child page says what it assumes.

New terms

Logits: raw scores, one per word, before softmax turns them into probabilities.
Loss (negative log-likelihood): \(-\ln p_y\), minus the log of the probability given to what happened.
Nat and bit: units of the loss with \(\ln\) and \(\log_2\); 1 nat = 1.443 bits. Training code prints nats.
Gradient: one slope of the loss per number in the model, the same shape as what it is the gradient of.
Curvature: how fast the slope changes; with many weights, the Hessian matrix.
Standard error: the uncertainty of an average, \(s/\sqrt{n}\).
