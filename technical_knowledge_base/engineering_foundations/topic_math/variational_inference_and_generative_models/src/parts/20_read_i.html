12. Diffusion: the forward process and its closed form

The question. A diffusion model's latents are the data with more and more noise added. What exactly is added, and why can we jump straight to any noise level?

Take a data point \(x_0\) (an image; here a 2-D point). The forward process adds a little Gaussian noise \(T\) times (Ho et al. 2020 eq. 2; {{Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015|https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03585}} introduced the idea):

\[ q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t;\ \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\ \beta_t I\big), \qquad t = 1, \dots, T. \]
\(\beta_t\)
the noise variance added at step \(t\), from a fixed schedule; Ho et al. use \(T = 1000\) and \(\beta_t\) rising linearly from \(10^{-4}\) to 0.02.
\(\sqrt{1-\beta_t}\)
a slight shrink of the previous value, so the total variance stays bounded instead of growing.
\(\alpha_t,\ \bar\alpha_t\)
shorthand: \(\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t\), and \(\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^t \alpha_s\), the signal fraction left after \(t\) steps (the bar is a product, not an average).

Read it as "\(x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}\, x_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\varepsilon_t\)" with fresh noise \(\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) each step: in the VAE language of section 7, this is a reparameterised encoder with no trainable parameters.

The closed form, derived by induction (Ho et al. eq. 4)

  1. Claim: \(x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\, \bar\varepsilon\) for a standard Gaussian \(\bar\varepsilon\). True at \(t = 1\), since \(\bar\alpha_1 = \alpha_1\).
  2. Assume it for \(t - 1\) and substitute: \[x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t-1}}\, \bar\varepsilon'\big) + \sqrt{1 - \alpha_t}\, \varepsilon_t\].
  3. The signal part is \(\sqrt{\alpha_t \bar\alpha_{t-1}}\, x_0 = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0\).
  4. The two noise parts are independent zero-mean Gaussians; independent variances add: \(\alpha_t(1 - \bar\alpha_{t-1}) + (1 - \alpha_t) = 1 - \alpha_t \bar\alpha_{t-1} = 1 - \bar\alpha_t\). A Gaussian with that variance is \(\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\,\bar\varepsilon\). Done:
\[ q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_t;\ \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0,\ (1 - \bar\alpha_t) I\big). \]

Any noise level in one jump: training never runs the chain, it draws \(t\) and noise and computes \(x_t\) in one line. If the data has unit variance, so does every \(x_t\) (\(\bar\alpha_t + 1 - \bar\alpha_t = 1\)): the "variance-preserving" process.

Worked, and checked by running the chain

The root's example ({{Topic: math, Notation decoder|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cdb851c0835b25a538}}, "Diffusion training loss"): \(x_0 = (0.5, -0.2)\), \(\varepsilon = (1, -0.5)\), \(t = 200\) on the linear schedule. \(\bar\alpha_{200} = \prod_{s \le 200}(1 - \beta_s) = 0.6590\), so \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha} = 0.8118\), \(\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha} = 0.5839\), and \(x_{200} = 0.8118\,x_0 + 0.5839\,\varepsilon = (0.9898, -0.4543)\).

Monte Carlo: 200,000 copies of \(x_0\) pushed through all 200 single steps, fresh noise each step. Mean \((0.4049, -0.1627)\) against \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha}\,x_0 = (0.4059, -0.1624)\) (standard error 0.0013); variance 0.342 against \(1 - \bar\alpha = 0.341\). After all 1,000 steps: variance 1.001 and 1.007 against \(1 - 4.0 \times 10^{-5}\); the data is gone.

Two schedules. Left: \(\bar\alpha_t\), the signal fraction left, for the linear schedule (Ho et al.) and the cosine schedule (Nichol and Dhariwal 2021, eq. 17: \(\bar\alpha_t = f(t)/f(0)\), \(f(t) = \cos^2\!\big(\frac{t/T + 0.008}{1.008}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\big)\), \(\beta_t\) clipped at 0.999). Right: 400 two-moons points at the chosen step, \(x_t\) drawn from the closed form.

Why a second schedule. On the linear schedule \(\bar\alpha_{500} = 0.079\): by the halfway point only 8% of the signal variance is left, and "the latents in the last quarter of the linear schedule are almost purely noise" ({{Nichol and Dhariwal 2021, section 3.2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09672}}), so those steps teach little. The cosine schedule keeps \(\bar\alpha_{500} = 0.494\) and drops roughly linearly in the middle. A useful single number per step is the signal-to-noise ratio \(\text{SNR}(t) = \bar\alpha_t/(1-\bar\alpha_t)\), usually as its log \(\lambda_t\): linear runs from \(\lambda = 9.2\) to \(-10.1\), cosine from 10.1 to \(-12.9\) at \(t = 999\) and \(-19.8\) at \(t = 1000\), where the 0.999 clip leaves \(\bar\alpha = 2.4\times 10^{-9}\). {{Kingma et al. 2021 (Variational Diffusion Models)|https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00630}} show that in continuous time the ELBO depends on the schedule only through its two end values of SNR; schedules change which noise levels training spends its effort on.
