9. Common mistakes

Each one opens; the first five are about the ELBO itself, the rest about the diffusion and flow derivations of section 7.

Using the wrong direction of KL for variational inference
VI minimises \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p(z\mid x))\), the average under \(q\). The other direction would need samples from the posterior. The direction is why VI underestimates spread (section 4) and picks one mode (section 3, model B).
Reading the ELBO as the log-likelihood
It is a lower bound with an unknown gap. On the MNIST VAE the encoder's ELBO was 8.2 nats below the true \(\log p(x)\) (section 4). Compare models with an importance-weighted estimate at stated \(K\) (or exact quadrature when the latent is tiny), on the same data and binarisation.
Mixing sum and mean between the two loss terms
Mean over pixels for reconstruction and sum over latent dimensions for the KL is a hidden beta of 784 on MNIST. Sum both over dimensions, then average over the batch (D1).
The KL formula with \(\log\sigma\) where it wants \(\log\sigma^2\)
\(\tfrac12(\mu^2 + \sigma^2 - 1 - \log\sigma^2)\): if the network outputs \(\log\sigma\), the last term is \(2\log\sigma\) and \(\sigma^2 = e^{2\log\sigma}\). Getting it wrong still trains, to the wrong objective. Check against a Monte Carlo estimate, as D1 does.
Diagnosing posterior collapse from the total loss
Look at the KL per latent dimension: a dimension with KL near 0 for every input is unused (the MNIST \(\beta\) sweep on {{Autoencoders and VAEs|n:3c65c17b0d0d814da93bdb159b7c1e11}}, section 5: 8 of 16 at \(\beta = 8\)). The total loss can look fine.
\(\alpha\) means \(\bar\alpha\) in some papers
DDPM: \(\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t\), \(\bar\alpha_t\) the product. DDIM: \(\alpha_t\) is the product. VDM and others use \(\alpha_t, \sigma_t\) for the signal and noise scales. Read the definitions first.
Time running the other way
Diffusion and Stable Diffusion 3 put data at \(t = 0\); Lipman et al.'s flow matching puts noise at \(t = 0\). The velocity's sign flips with it.
Two guidance scales with the same name
The paper's \(w\) and the libraries' \(s = 1 + w\) (D7). "Guidance 7.5" is \(w = 6.5\).
Dividing by \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\) near the end of the cosine schedule
With \(\beta_t\) clipped at 0.999 the cosine schedule ends at \(\bar\alpha_{1000} = 2.4\times10^{-9}\), and the predicted \(x_0 = (x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha}\,\hat\varepsilon)/\sqrt{\bar\alpha}\) multiplies the network's error by about 20,000. On the two-moons toy of {{Diffusion from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9bf0d94e57ce9164}} (section 5), 20-step DDIM on that schedule put 1.2% of 600 samples on the data until the predicted \(x_0\) was clipped to the data range (\([-3, 3]\)) and the noise re-derived from the clipped value; with that fix, 95.3%. Image samplers clip to \([-1, 1]\) for the same reason.
Treating \(L_{\text{simple}}\) as the ELBO
It drops the per-step weights (D4): the true bound weighs \(t = 1\) about 80 times more than \(t = 200\). Likelihood numbers need the weighted bound.
Mixing up the two "scores"
Section 5's score is \(\nabla_\phi \log q_\phi(z)\), a gradient with respect to parameters, averaging to zero. D5's score is \(\nabla_x \log p(x)\), a gradient with respect to the data. The link to noise prediction is \(s = -\varepsilon/\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\), with that minus sign.
