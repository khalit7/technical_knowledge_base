The idea in one screen

When a model explains data \(x\) through a hidden cause \(z\), the log-likelihood \(\log p(x)\) needs an integral over every \(z\) that nobody can compute; so pick a simple distribution \(q(z)\) and maximise the ELBO, \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x,z) - \log q(z)]\), which is always below \(\log p(x)\) by exactly the gap \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \,\Vert\, p(z\mid x))\), so raising it trains the model and fits \(q\) to the posterior at once.

The bound is the evidence minus the gap: one model, one \(q\), every number exact

Model A of section 1 (prior \(\mathcal{N}(0,1)\), \(x = z\) + noise of sd 0.5, observed \(x = 1.5\)). Left: the prior (dashed), the true posterior \(\mathcal{N}(1.2,\ 0.2)\) (shaded) and a deliberately poor \(q = \mathcal{N}(0.5,\ 0.5^2)\). Right: \(\log p(x) = -1.9305\) splits into ELBO \(-3.1689\) plus gap 1.2384, the KL from \(q\) to the posterior. Move \(q\) onto the posterior and the gap is 0 (try it in section 3).

You need: Bayes' rule and Gaussians ({{Probability for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d815f9a47d613409b5a0c}}, sections 1, 2 and 5; Monte Carlo and the law of large numbers in section 4) and KL divergence with its forward and reverse directions ({{Information theory for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81c6baf3f778a62e14e5}}, sections 3 and 4). Latent variable \(z\)
the hidden cause; never observed.
Prior, decoder
\(p(z)\), and the likelihood \(p_\theta(x\mid z)\) with trainable \(\theta\).
Evidence
the marginal likelihood \(p_\theta(x) = \int p(z)\,p_\theta(x\mid z)\,dz\).
Posterior
\(p_\theta(z\mid x)\), which causes explain this \(x\).
Variational family
the simple distributions \(q_\phi\) we search; \(\phi\) are the variational parameters.
ELBO
evidence lower bound, \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log p(x,z) - \log q(z)]\).
Mean field
a \(q\) that factorises over latents.
Amortised inference
one network outputs \(q(z\mid x)\) for every \(x\).
Pathwise, score function
the two ways to differentiate an average under \(q_\phi\) (section 5).

1. Latent-variable models, and the integral you cannot do

A latent-variable model tells a two-step story: draw a hidden value \(z\) (the latent variable) from a simple prior \(p(z)\), then draw the data from the likelihood or decoder \(p_\theta(x\mid z)\), with \(\theta\) the parameters we train (in a VAE, a network's weights). We only ever see \(x\), so the probability of an observation is the marginal likelihood or evidence, the joint added up over every \(z\):

\[ p_\theta(x) = \int p(z)\, p_\theta(x \mid z)\, dz . \]

Maximum likelihood ({{Probability for ML, section 7|n:3c65c17b0d0d815f9a47d613409b5a0c}}) needs \(\sum_i \log p_\theta(x_i)\) and its gradient, so it needs this integral for every example.

Model A, the running example (sections 1 to 5)

Prior \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\); decoder \(x \mid z \sim \mathcal{N}(z, s^2)\) with \(s = 0.5\); we observe \(x = 1.5\). Here the integral has a closed form: \(x\) is a sum of two independent Gaussians (variances 1 and 0.25), so \(x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1.25)\) and

\[ \log p(x{=}1.5) = -\tfrac12 \log(2\pi \cdot 1.25) - \frac{1.5^2}{2 \cdot 1.25} = -1.0305 - 0.9000 = -1.9305, \]

that is \(p(x) = 0.1451\). Every number in sections 2 to 5 is measured against this \(-1.9305\) (src/recompute.py recomputes each one).

Why it is usually impossible. Model A is solvable only because a Gaussian pushed through a straight line stays Gaussian. Make the mean a network's output, \(x \mid z \sim \text{Bernoulli}(\text{decoder}_\theta(z))\), and nothing cancels: Kingma and Welling name exactly this case, intractable "in cases of moderately complicated likelihood functions \(p_\theta(x \mid z)\), e.g. a neural network with a nonlinear hidden layer" ({{Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes, section 2.1|https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114}}). A grid of 100 points per axis costs \(10^4\) decoder calls for a 2-D latent and \(10^{32}\) for a 16-D one. Naive Monte Carlo (draw \(z\) from the prior, average \(p(x\mid z)\)) is unbiased, but almost every prior sample explains a given image terribly, so the average is dominated by rare lucky draws.

Where latents appear. A Gaussian mixture (\(z\) is the cluster; the integral is a sum of \(K\) terms, easy, and its posterior is EM's responsibilities); a VAE (\(z\) a continuous vector of 2 to a few hundred numbers, decoder a network: intractable); a diffusion model (the latents are 1,000 progressively noisier copies of the image, \(x_1, \dots, x_T\), each as large as the image: intractable); also topic models, probabilistic PCA and Bayesian neural networks (where \(z\) is the weights). Contrast: a next-token model has no latent; \(p(\text{word}\mid\text{context})\) is a softmax, one exact sum over the vocabulary, which is why language models train on plain cross-entropy and need nothing on this page.
