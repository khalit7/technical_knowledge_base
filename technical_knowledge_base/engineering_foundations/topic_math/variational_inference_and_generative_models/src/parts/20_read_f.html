6. Where the ELBO reappears: VAEs and diffusion

The same bound, two choices of \(q\): learned and one-step (VAE), fixed and many-step (diffusion)

Top: a VAE learns both arrows; its ELBO is reconstruction minus a KL to the prior. Bottom: diffusion fixes the forward arrows to Gaussian noise and learns only the reverse chain; its ELBO is a sum of KLs between Gaussians, one per step, plus two end terms.

The VAE ({{Kingma and Welling 2013|https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114}}; {{Rezende, Mohamed and Wierstra 2014|https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4082}}) is sections 3 to 5 with networks: an encoder outputs a diagonal Gaussian \(q_\phi(z\mid x)\) (amortised, section 4), a decoder outputs \(p_\theta(x\mid z)\), the prior is \(\mathcal{N}(0, I)\), and the loss is minus the "reconstruction minus KL" ELBO of section 3 (Kingma and Welling eq. 3 and 7) with one reparameterised sample per image (section 5; their section 2.3). The KL has a closed form, \(\frac12 \sum_{j} (\mu_j^2 + \sigma_j^2 - 1 - \log \sigma_j^2)\) (their Appendix B, derived in section 7; 1.750 nats for \(\mu = (1, -0.5)\), \(\sigma = (0.5, 2)\)). Two consequences are pure ELBO maths: only \(\beta = 1\) on the KL is a bound on \(\log p(x)\) (a \(\beta\)-VAE trains a rate-distortion objective, {{Alemi et al. 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00464}}), and the KL is minimised, to 0, by \(q(z\mid x) = p(z)\), so a decoder that reconstructs well without \(z\) gives nothing a reason to move away: posterior collapse. The model itself (architectures, the units of the loss, the blur, collapse and its fixes, VQ-VAE, the latent scaling factor of latent diffusion) is taught on {{Autoencoders and VAEs|n:3c65c17b0d0d814da93bdb159b7c1e11}}; this page's own MNIST VAE, its training curves, latent map and a \(\beta\) sweep, is measured in section 8.

Diffusion is a latent-variable model with latents \(x_{1:T}\), each a noisier copy of the data, whose \(q(x_{1:T}\mid x_0)\) is fixed Gaussian noise rather than learned, so there is no amortisation gap to train away. Its ELBO (Ho et al. eq. 3 and 5) telescopes into \(L_T + \sum_{t>1} L_{t-1} + L_0\), each \(L_{t-1}\) a KL between two Gaussians with a closed form; writing the true reverse step's mean in terms of the added noise turns each KL into a weighted squared error on the noise, and dropping the weights gives Ho et al.'s one-line loss \(L_{\text{simple}} = \mathbb{E}\,\|\varepsilon - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2\). That drop is a choice: the true bound weighs \(t = 1\) about 80 times more than \(t = 200\), so \(L_{\text{simple}}\) is a reweighted ELBO, better samples for slightly worse likelihood. The eight steps, the score view, DDIM, guidance and flow matching are derived in section 7; the models, schedules, samplers and guidance in practice are {{Diffusion from zero|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cf9bf0d94e57ce9164}}, {{Flow matching and rectified flow|n:3fc5c17b0d0d8136a1efc73aaa446ceb}} and {{Latent diffusion and diffusion transformers|n:3fc5c17b0d0d814fbcb5e0c8df5289f3}}.
