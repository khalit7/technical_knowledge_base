11. Normalising flows, in one section

The question. Section 4's model B showed a Gaussian \(q\) cannot fit a two-humped posterior. How do you build a distribution that is flexible but still has a density you can evaluate exactly?

Idea: start from a simple random variable \(z\) and push it through an invertible function \(x = f(z)\). Where \(f\) stretches space, probability thins out; where it squeezes, it piles up. The change-of-variables formula keeps the books.

  1. One dimension, \(f\) increasing: the probability in a small interval is preserved, \(p_x(x)\,|dx| = p_z(z)\,|dz|\).
  2. So \(p_x(x) = p_z(z) \left|\frac{dz}{dx}\right| = p_z\big(f^{-1}(x)\big)\, \left|\frac{df}{dz}\right|^{-1}\).
  3. In \(D\) dimensions the stretch factor of a small volume is the absolute determinant of the Jacobian matrix \(\partial f/\partial z\) ({{Matrix calculus and backprop, section 2|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93}}; the determinant is the volume scaling of a linear map, {{Linear algebra for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d89c26e1dab2fcb701}}). In logs:
\[ \log p_x(x) = \log p_z(z) - \log\left|\det \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right|, \qquad z = f^{-1}(x). \]
Two checks

\(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\), \(x = 2z + 1\): at \(x = 3\), \(z = 1\), \(df/dz = 2\), so \(p_x(3) = \mathcal{N}(1; 0, 1)/2 = 0.2420/2 = 0.121\); the log-determinant is \(\log 2 = 0.693\). \(x = e^{z}\) (the log-normal): at \(x = 2\), \(z = \ln 2\), \(df/dz = e^z = 2\), so \(p_x(2) = \mathcal{N}(0.693)/2 = 0.1569\); counting four million samples falling in \([1.99, 2.01]\) gives 0.158.

A normalising flow composes \(K\) such maps, \(z_K = f_K \circ \dots \circ f_1(z_0)\), and the log-densities add up (Rezende and Mohamed {{"Variational Inference with Normalizing Flows", eq. 6 to 7|https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05770}}): \(\log q_K(z_K) = \log q_0(z_0) - \sum_{k=1}^K \log\left|\det \frac{\partial f_k}{\partial z_{k-1}}\right|\). The whole design problem is making that determinant cheap: a general \(D \times D\) determinant costs \(O(D^3)\).

  • Coupling layers (RealNVP, {{Dinh, Sohl-Dickstein and Bengio 2016|https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08803}}): split \(z\) into halves \((a, b)\); keep \(a\); set \(b' = b \odot e^{s(a)} + t(a)\) with any networks \(s, t\). The Jacobian is triangular, so its determinant is the product of the diagonal, \(\exp(\sum s(a))\): one sum. Inverting needs no inverse of \(s\) or \(t\).
  • Autoregressive flows (IAF, {{Kingma et al. 2016|https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04934}}; MAF): each coordinate depends only on earlier ones, again a triangular Jacobian. IAF used as the VAE's \(q\) is what fixes model B's problem at scale: the approximate posterior can bend and split.
  • Continuous flows: let \(K \to \infty\) with tiny steps, \(dz/dt = v(z, t)\). The log-density then changes at the rate \(-\operatorname{tr}(\partial v/\partial z)\) (the instantaneous change of variables, {{Chen et al. 2018, Neural ODEs|https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366}}). Flow matching (section 18) trains exactly such a \(v\), without ever computing the trace during training.
What it is used for in ML
Richer VAE posteriors (IAF), exact-likelihood generative models (RealNVP, Glow), and, as continuous flows, the probability-flow view of diffusion (section 15) and flow matching (section 18).
