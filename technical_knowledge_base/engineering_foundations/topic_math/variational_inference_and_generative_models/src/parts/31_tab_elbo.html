Move the approximation \(q(z) = \mathcal{N}(m, \sigma^2)\) by hand and watch the identity \(\log p(x) = \text{ELBO} + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p(z\mid x))\) hold at every setting: the ELBO rises exactly as the gap shrinks. Then let an optimiser do it, with either gradient estimator of Reading section 6.

Let an optimiser fit \(q\)

Gradient ascent on \((m, \log\sigma)\) from the current \(q\), seeded so a run repeats exactly. The pathwise estimator uses the slope of \(\log p(x, z)\) at each sample and the closed-form entropy of \(q\); the score-function estimator uses only the values of \(\log p(x,z) - \log q(z)\), times the score of \(q\). Both are unbiased; compare how wobbly the ELBO trace is.

Things to try
  • Model A: put \(m\) at the posterior mean and \(\sigma\) at its sd (the defaults' posterior is \(\mathcal{N}(1.2, 0.447^2)\)): the gap reads 0 and the ELBO equals \(\log p(x) = -1.9305\).
  • Model B with \(x = 2\): no setting closes the gap; the best is about 0.71 nats, near \(\ln 2\). Lower \(x\) towards 0 and the two humps merge into one; the gap shrinks.
  • Run the score-function estimator with 1 sample per step and step size 0.03, then the pathwise one: same destination, very different paths.
