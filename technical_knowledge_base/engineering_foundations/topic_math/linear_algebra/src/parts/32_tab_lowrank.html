Low-rank lab: truncate a real GPT-2 weight matrix

Pick one weight matrix from layer 5 of GPT-2 small and keep only its \(k\) largest singular directions (the Eckart-Young truncation of Reading section 12). See what you save in parameters, what you lose in the matrix, what you lose on the real inputs that reach that matrix, and what happens to the model's next-token loss. Below, the same question for all 48 matrices at once, and the spectra of two real LoRA updates.

Spectrum: real against random

Singular values, largest first, on a log scale. Grey: a Gaussian random matrix of the same shape scaled to the same Frobenius norm. Shaded: the directions truncation drops.

Error as k grows

Relative error of the matrix itself (Frobenius, exact from the spectrum) and of its output on 2,048 real token inputs, \(\lVert X(W - W_k)\rVert_F / \lVert XW \rVert_F\). The two differ because real inputs do not point equally in every direction.

All 48 attention and MLP matrices truncated at once

Each matrix keeps the stated fraction of its full rank (768); the attention input matrix is truncated as its three 768 by 768 blocks, Q, K and V, separately. "Parameters in low-rank form" counts \(k(m + n)\) per matrix, capped at \(mn\): below rank 384 for a square matrix, a truncated SVD is not even smaller than the original. A uniform guess over the 50,257-token vocabulary scores \(\ln 50257 = 10.8\) nats.
