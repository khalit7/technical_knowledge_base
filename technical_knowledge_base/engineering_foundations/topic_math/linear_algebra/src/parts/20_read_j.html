13. Embeddings and attention, read as linear algebra

Attention itself, \(\operatorname{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\), with every shape and why the scores are scaled, is taught on {{The transformer from zero|n:3fd5c17b0d0d8174980ddde4a20c7b3c}}. Three facts from this page's side, measured on GPT-2 small:

  • An embedding lookup is a matrix product. Token \(i\) as a one-hot vector \(e_i\) (all zeros except a 1 in position \(i\)) times the table \(E\) (\(V \times d\)) picks out row \(i\). Libraries index instead, because multiplying by zeros is wasteful, but the gradient is that of a matrix product: only the rows that were used get updated. GPT-2 small's table is \(50{,}257 \times 768\), reused as the output matrix (section 5's softmax bottleneck).
  • Real embeddings are not centred. Two random GPT-2 token embeddings have cosine similarity on average, not 0: the vectors share a common direction (the mean embedding's length is of the average embedding's length). So cosine similarities like cat to dog (0.55) must be read against that baseline, and many retrieval systems subtract the mean or whiten embeddings before comparing them; a PCA of them must centre first.
  • The query-key product is a low-rank bilinear form. The score between hidden states \(h_i\) and \(h_j\) is \(q_i \cdot k_j = h_i^\top (W_Q W_K^\top) h_j\). The \(768 \times 768\) matrix \(W_Q W_K^\top\) is a product through a 64-wide middle (\(d_k = 64\) in GPT-2 small), so its rank is at most 64. Measured on layer 5, head 0: exactly nonzero singular values out of 768 (the 65th is \(3 \times 10^{-14}\), rounding). Each head compares tokens through a 64-dimensional "view" of the 768-dimensional residual stream; twelve heads give twelve such views. The same holds for \(W_V W_O\), the head's output map (Elhage et al., {{A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits, 2021|https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html}}, call these the QK and OV circuits). The \(768 \times 2304\) matrix c_attn holds \(W_Q, W_K, W_V\) side by side, each twelve \(768 \times 64\) blocks, one per head.
Attention piece by piece in this page's terms, with the root's numbers

For a sequence of \(T\) token vectors stacked as the rows of \(H\) (\(T \times d\)), one head computes \(Q = H W_Q\), \(K = H W_K\), \(V = H W_V\) (projections, section 2); \(QK^\top\) is \(T \times T\), entry \((i, j)\) the dot product of query \(i\) with key \(j\) (row times column, section 3); dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) keeps the scores' spread near 1 when the entries of \(q\) and \(k\) have variance 1 (the variance of a sum of \(d_k\) products is \(d_k\)); softmax along each row turns scores into positive weights summing to 1; multiplying by \(V\) makes each output row a weighted average of value vectors, a point inside their convex hull: the column picture of section 3, with the weights as coefficients.

Treat the tiny model's input \(x = (2, 1)\) as a query and its three word rows as keys and values (\(d_k = 2\)): scores \(Wx/\sqrt{2} = (1.414, 0.707, 0)\), weights \((0.576, 0.284, 0.140)\), output

\[0.576\,(1, 0) + 0.284\,(0, 1) + 0.140\,(1, -2) = (0.716, 0.004)\]

14. Which tool when

  • Solving \(Ax = b\): factorise and solve (torch.linalg.solve, cholesky_solve); never form \(A^{-1}\), which is slower and less accurate, and fails badly when \(\kappa\) is large.
  • Least squares: QR by default (lstsq); normal equations only when well conditioned; SVD when rank-deficient.
  • Top few singular directions of a big matrix: power iteration or a randomized SVD (torch.svd_lowrank), not a full SVD.
  • Eigen or SVD: eigen for symmetric matrices; SVD for anything about stretch, size or rank.
  • Rank of a floating-point matrix: never literally; count singular values above a tolerance (NumPy's matrix_rank uses \(\sigma_{\max} \cdot \max(m, n) \cdot \varepsilon\)) or look at how fast the spectrum falls.

Depth: mistakes and interview rehearsal

Common mistakes, each with the section that shows it

Treating eigenvalues as stretches. For a non-symmetric matrix they differ from the singular values (3 and 5 against 6.708 and 2.236 for \(A\)), and the eigenvectors need not be orthogonal (section 10).

Forgetting to centre before PCA. \(\frac{1}{n}X^\top X\) is the covariance only if every column of \(X\) has mean zero. GPT-2's embeddings, with a mean cosine near 0.27 between random tokens, are a real case (section 13).

Mixing layouts and conventions. \(Wx\) with \(W\) as (out, in), \(XW^\top\) in PyTorch, \(XW\) with GPT-2's Conv1D (in, out). Each is right; mixing two in one derivation puts transposes in the wrong places. Write the shape beside every symbol. (Derivative layouts are a separate convention, owned by {{Matrix calculus and backprop|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93}}.)

Reading "low rank" as "small", or "small" as "unimportant". A rank-1 matrix can have a huge norm and a full-rank matrix can be tiny: rank counts directions, norms measure size. And small singular values of a pretrained weight still matter: dropping the bottom 10% of every GPT-2 matrix raises the loss from 3.17 to 3.91 (section 12).

Inverting a matrix to solve a system, or forming \(X^\top X\). 77% coefficient error against 0.0018% for QR in the float32 run of section 11.

Silent broadcasting. \((n, 1) - (n,)\) gives an \(n \times n\) table, not \(n\) differences (section 4). Assert shapes before every reduction.

Assuming \(AB = BA\). Order matters (\(PQ \ne QP\) in section 3), and \((AB)^\top = B^\top A^\top\), \((AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}\): both reverse the order.
