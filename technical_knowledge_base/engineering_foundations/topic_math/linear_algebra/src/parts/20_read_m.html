18. Common mistakes, and the questions interviews ask

Mistakes

Treating eigenvalues as stretches
For a non-symmetric matrix they differ from the singular values (3 and 5 against 6.708 and 2.236 for \(A\)), and the eigenvectors need not be orthogonal. Stretch, norms and conditioning are singular-value questions.
Forgetting to centre before PCA
\(\frac{1}{n}X^\top X\) is the covariance only if every column of \(X\) has mean zero; otherwise the first "principal component" mostly points at the mean. GPT-2's embeddings, with a mean cosine near 0.27 between random tokens, are a real case.
Mixing layouts and conventions
\(Wx\) with \(W\) as (out, in), \(XW^\top\) in PyTorch, \(XW\) with GPT-2's Conv1D (in, out). Each is right; mixing two in one derivation puts transposes in the wrong places. Write the shape beside every symbol. (Derivative layouts are a separate convention, owned by {{Matrix calculus and backprop|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93}}.)
Reading "low rank" as "small", or "small" as "unimportant"
A rank-1 matrix can have a huge norm and a full-rank matrix can be tiny: rank counts directions, norms measure size. And small singular values of a pretrained weight still matter: dropping the bottom 10% of every GPT-2 matrix raises the loss from 3.17 to 3.91 (section 14).
Inverting a matrix to solve a system
Computing \(A^{-1}b\) is slower and less accurate than a factorise-and-solve, and fails badly when \(\kappa\) is large. Likewise, forming \(X^\top X\) squares \(\kappa\): 77% coefficient error against 0.0018% for QR in the float32 run of section 13.
Taking the rank of a floating-point matrix literally
In floating point almost every matrix is full rank. Ask how many singular values exceed a tolerance (NumPy's matrix_rank uses \(\sigma_{\max} \cdot \max(m, n) \cdot \varepsilon\)), or look at how fast the spectrum falls.
Silent broadcasting
\((n, 1) - (n,)\) gives an \(n \times n\) table, not \(n\) differences: perfect predictions scored as mean squared error 1.333 in section 4. Assert shapes before every reduction.
Assuming \(AB = BA\)
Order matters (\(PQ \ne QP\) in section 3), and \((AB)^\top = B^\top A^\top\), \((AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}\): both reverse the order.
