No. With about ten thousand positions, most 7-byte contexts occur once, so the "next byte" is always certain in the sample. On the whole book the same estimate falls more slowly (0.81 bits at \(N = 8\)) and would keep falling with longer contexts. Shannon's 1951 lower bound of about 0.6 bits per letter is the sanity check: a count-based estimate below it on a short sample is the estimator, not the text. The obvious estimator, count, divide by \(N\) and compute \(-\sum \hat p \log \hat p\) (the plug-in or maximum-likelihood estimator), is biased downwards: outcomes not yet seen count as impossible, and the seen ones look more concentrated than they are. For \(m\) possible outcomes and \(N\) samples,

\[ \mathbb{E}\big[\hat H_{\text{plug-in}}\big] - H \approx -\frac{m - 1}{2N} \text{ nats} \]

({{Paninski 2003, eq. 4.6|https://www.stat.columbia.edu/~liam/research/pubs/info_est-nc.pdf}}, completing Miller's 1955 result; valid when \(N\) is large compared with \(m\).) The Miller-Madow correction adds \((\hat m - 1)/(2N)\) back, with \(\hat m\) the number of outcomes actually seen; the same bias as a case of estimator bias and variance in general, with an exact table on the tiny model's three words, is section 2 of {{Statistics for ML|n:3f05c17b0d0d810f896cf688ce9aed1b}}.

Mutual information inherits the bias, with a twist. In \(I = H(X) + H(Y) - H(X, Y)\) the joint has many more cells (\(m_X m_Y\)) than the marginals, so it is underestimated the most, and the plug-in mutual information comes out too high, by about \(\frac{(m_X - 1)(m_Y - 1)}{2N}\) nats (the Miller-Madow terms combined; derived here, not quoted). With 100 samples, two variables that have nothing to do with each other show 0.48 nats (0.69 bits) of "shared information": reading a plug-in mutual information without its bias is a classic way to find structure in noise.

High dimensions: bounds instead of counts. For images, sentences or embeddings every sample is unique, so methods switch to variational bounds learned by a network, InfoNCE (section 9) and others that trade bias against variance ({{Poole et al. 2019|https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06922}}: "the existing variational lower bounds degrade when the MI is large, exhibiting either high bias or high variance"), all under the \(O(\ln N)\) limit of section 9.

A checklist for any reported entropy or mutual information
  1. How many samples per possible outcome (or per cell of the joint)? Below a few, plug-in numbers are mostly bias.
  2. Was a bias correction or a shuffled-data baseline used? Shuffle \(Y\) against \(X\) and re-estimate: the shuffled value is the noise floor of "independent".
  3. For continuous data, how was it binned? The answer changes with the bins.
  4. For a variational bound: what was \(N\) (the batch size)? Is the reported value near \(\ln N\)?
  5. In bits or nats?
