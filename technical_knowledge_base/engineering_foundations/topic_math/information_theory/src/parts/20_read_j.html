9. InfoNCE: a classification loss that bounds mutual information, up to \(\ln N\)

Read the tiny model as a contrastive scorer: cat is the positive (the candidate that truly goes with the anchor), dog and sat the negatives, scores 2, 1, 0. Picking the positive with a softmax costs \(-\ln 0.665 = 0.408\) nats, and that loss certifies \(I \ge \ln 3 - 0.408 = 0.691\) nats of mutual information between anchor and positive. With sat as the positive the loss is 2.408 and the bound is negative: it certifies nothing.

\[ L_{\text{NCE}} = -\,\mathbb{E}\left[\log \frac{e^{f(x, y^+)}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} e^{f(x, y_j)}}\right], \qquad I(X; Y) \ge \ln N - L_{\text{NCE}} \]

The InfoNCE loss is an \(N\)-way classification whose classes are the candidates in the batch (one positive, \(N - 1\) negatives, usually the other batch items), scored by a learned critic \(f(x, y)\), often a scaled cosine similarity ({{van den Oord et al. 2018, §2.3|https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748}}; proved exactly by Poole et al. 2019, details below). A loss is never negative, so the bound can never exceed \(\ln N\): with 4 candidates even a perfect critic certifies at most \(\ln 4 = 1.386\) nats. Poole et al. (§2.3, with \(K\) for \(N\)): "\(I_{\text{NCE}}\) is upper bounded by \(\log K\), meaning that this bound will be loose when \(I(X; Y) > \log K\)". Predict before you choose a correlation: with a perfect critic and 16 candidates, what does InfoNCE report for a true 5.41 nats?

A small batch does not just add noise; it caps what the loss can certify

estimate ln N − Lln N, the ceiling (dashed)true I

Two correlated Gaussians, \(x \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\), \(y = \rho x + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\varepsilon\), whose mutual information is known, \(I = -\tfrac12 \ln(1 - \rho^2)\). The optimal critic \(f = \log p(y \mid x) - \log p(y)\); for each \(N\), 2,048 anchors in batches of \(N\) (two batches at \(N = 1{,}024\)); seeded random numbers (mulberry32, seed 7) identical here and in src/recompute.py.

With weak dependence (\(\rho = 0.5\)) every batch size gets close to the truth, from below. With strong dependence (5.41 nats) the estimate rides the \(\ln N\) ceiling until \(N\) is in the hundreds: 2.73 nats at \(N = 16\), 5.20 at 1,024. The ceiling is not a quirk of InfoNCE: {{McAllester and Stratos (2020)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04251}} prove that "any distribution-free high-confidence lower bound on mutual information estimated from N samples cannot be larger than O(ln N)". CLIP trains with 32,768 image-text pairs per batch, so each direction is a 32,768-way classification with a ceiling of \(\ln 32{,}768 = 10.4\) nats (15 bits) ({{Radford et al. 2021|https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020}}). Reading any reported "mutual information" estimate: check the batch size first; a value near \(\ln N\) is the ceiling talking. InfoNCE as a training method (SimCLR, MoCo, SimCSE, temperature, collapse) is on {{Contrastive and self-supervised learning|n:3c65c17b0d0d811db6a7d02d0ee7215c}}; the {{CLIP page|n:3c65c17b0d0d8194a85dfc5f3bf3f799}} trains a toy one.

The exact bound, the best critic, CLIP's temperature, and a worked batch of four

The CPC derivation used an approximation; {{Poole et al. 2019 (eq. 10 and footnote 2)|https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06922}} prove the bound exactly and show the best critic is the log density ratio \(\log \frac{p(y \mid x)}{p(y)}\) plus any function of \(x\) alone (it cancels inside the softmax). CLIP's loss is symmetric (image to text and text to image), and its temperature, which divides the similarities before the softmax, is learned as a log-parameterised scalar initialised to 0.07 and clipped so the logits are never scaled by more than 100 (Radford et al. 2021, §2.3 and §2.5).

\(N = 4\), positive scored 2.0, three negatives 0.0: the positive's probability is \(e^2/(e^2 + 3) = 7.389 / 10.389 = 0.711\), the loss \(-\ln 0.711 = 0.341\) nats, and \(I \ge \ln 4 - 0.341 = 1.386 - 0.341 = 1.046\) nats (1.51 bits).
