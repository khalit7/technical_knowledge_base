10. KL penalties: RL fine-tuning, DPO and the ELBO

Take the tiny model as a policy: reference \(\pi_{\text{ref}}\) = (cat 0.665, dog 0.245, sat 0.090), reward 1 for sat and 0 otherwise illustrative. Fine-tuning with a reward model \(r(x, y)\) usually maximises reward minus a KL to the reference:

\[ \max_{\pi}\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D},\, y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}\big[r(x, y)\big] \;-\; \beta\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi(\cdot \mid x) \,\Vert\, \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\big) \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \pi^*(y) = \frac{1}{Z}\, \pi_{\text{ref}}(y)\, e^{r(y)/\beta} \]

The optimum reweights the reference by ereward/β; cat and dog keep their ratio at every β

Exact optimum on the tiny policy for each \(\beta\) (src/recompute.py). At \(\beta = 1\): \(Z = 0.665 + 0.245 + 0.090\,e = 1.155\), \(\pi^* = (0.576, 0.212, 0.212)\); expected reward rises from 0.090 to 0.212 at a KL of 0.068 nats, and the objective \(0.212 - 0.068 = 0.144\) equals \(\beta \ln Z = \ln 1.155\).

This is equation 3 of {{the DPO paper|https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290}}; InstructGPT puts the same term into the reward per sample as \(r_\theta(x, y) - \beta \log \frac{\pi^{\text{RL}}(y \mid x)}{\pi^{\text{SFT}}(y \mid x)}\) ({{Ouyang et al. 2022, eq. 2|https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155}}), whose average over \(y \sim \pi\) is exactly \(\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\). The ideal fine-tuned model is the reference reweighted by \(e^{\text{reward}/\beta}\): small \(\beta\) lets rewards dominate and concentrate the policy on the best response, large \(\beta\) barely moves it, and nothing gets probability the reference does not give, because the KL is reverse (section 4). DPO runs the closed form backwards: solve it for the reward, \(r(x, y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x)\) (DPO eq. 5), substitute into a preference model where only reward differences matter, and \(Z(x)\) cancels, leaving a loss on the policy alone ({{DPO page|n:3c65c17b0d0d818bb1d8cafd30e20f9e}}; the family is compared on {{Alignment: SFT, RLHF, DPO Family, RLVR|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d5bed7e608d4061c7a}} and the RL algorithms on {{RL for LLMs|n:3c65c17b0d0d818c9bcff7177325fe56}}).

What a KL budget guarantees: Pinsker's inequality, \(\mathrm{TV}(\pi, \pi_{\text{ref}}) \le \sqrt{D_{\mathrm{KL}}/2}\) (KL in nats), bounds the total variation, the largest possible change in the probability of any event. So a KL penalty becomes a guarantee about behaviour: no event's probability can move by more than \(\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}/2}\). On the tiny policy at \(\beta = 1\), sat moved by 0.122, within \(\sqrt{0.068/2} = 0.18\).

Deriving the closed-form optimum (DPO, Appendix A.1, one prompt)
  1. Write the objective as one average over \(y \sim \pi\): \(\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\big[r(y) - \beta \log \frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)}\big]\).
  2. Divide by \(-\beta\) to minimise: \(\min_\pi \mathbb{E}_\pi\big[\log \frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y)\, e^{r(y)/\beta}}\big]\).
  3. Normalise the denominator: \(Z = \sum_y \pi_{\text{ref}}(y) e^{r(y)/\beta}\), \(\pi^*(y) = \pi_{\text{ref}}(y)\, e^{r(y)/\beta} / Z\); the objective is \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi \Vert \pi^*) - \log Z\).
  4. \(Z\) does not depend on \(\pi\), and the KL is 0 only at \(\pi = \pi^*\) (Gibbs, section 3); the best achievable objective is \(\beta \log Z\).

Estimating the KL from sampled tokens

The KL over all possible responses cannot be summed, so it is estimated from samples \(y \sim \pi\) with \(r = \pi_{\text{ref}}(y)/\pi(y)\). John Schulman's note ({{"Approximating KL Divergence", 7 March 2020|http://joschu.net/blog/kl-approx.html}}) compares \(k_1 = -\log r\) (unbiased, but negative for some samples, high variance), \(k_2 = \tfrac12 (\log r)^2\) (always positive, low variance, biased) and \(k_3 = (r - 1) - \log r\) (unbiased and always positive, because \(\log r \le r - 1\)). GRPO uses \(k_3\) (DeepSeekMath, eq. 4); the {{DeepSeekMath page|n:3c65c17b0d0d817f9fc5cb9a9fbcbee5}} explains why differentiating \(k_3\) inside the loss does not give the gradient of the KL it estimates.

The three estimators on the tiny policy
At \(\beta = 1\), true \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi^* \Vert \pi_{\text{ref}}) = 0.0681\) nats. A cat or dog sample has \(r = 1.155\): \(k_1 = -0.144\) (negative), \(k_2 = 0.010\), \(k_3 = 0.011\). A sat sample has \(r = 0.425\): \(k_1 = 0.856\), \(k_2 = 0.367\), \(k_3 = 0.281\). Averaged over \(\pi^*\): \(\mathbb{E}[k_1] = \mathbb{E}[k_3] = 0.0681\) exactly, \(\mathbb{E}[k_2] = 0.0858\) (biased upwards here).

The ELBO is the same identity, with a reverse KL

\[ \log p(x) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p(x, z) - \log q(z)]}_{\text{ELBO}} + D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(z) \,\Vert\, p(z \mid x)\big) \]

For a latent-variable model with an encoder \(q_\phi(z \mid x)\), the log-evidence splits into the evidence lower bound plus a reverse KL to the true posterior; since the KL is \(\ge 0\) the first term is a lower bound, and raising it by changing \(q\) lowers that KL by exactly the same amount (Kingma and Welling 2013, eq. 3, write it as a reconstruction term minus \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(z \mid x) \Vert p(z))\)). The Gaussian KL term in closed form (0.818 nats for \(\mu = 1, \sigma = 0.5\)), VAEs and diffusion as a chain of KL terms are derived on {{Variational inference and generative-model maths|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}}.

The ELBO identity in three lines
  1. For any \(z\): \(\log p(x) = \log p(x, z) - \log p(z \mid x)\); add and subtract \(\log q(z)\).
  2. Average over \(z \sim q\); the left does not depend on \(z\), and the two brackets become the ELBO and \(D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q(z) \Vert p(z \mid x))\).
  3. Splitting \(\log p(x, z) = \log p(z) + \log p(x \mid z)\) gives \(\mathrm{ELBO} = -D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(q(z \mid x) \Vert p(z)\big) + \mathbb{E}_{q}\big[\log p(x \mid z)\big]\) ({{Kingma and Welling 2013, eq. 3|https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114}}); with \(q = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) and \(p(z) = \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\) the KL per dimension is \(\tfrac12(\sigma^2 + \mu^2 - 1 - \ln \sigma^2)\) (their Appendix B, which writes its negative).
