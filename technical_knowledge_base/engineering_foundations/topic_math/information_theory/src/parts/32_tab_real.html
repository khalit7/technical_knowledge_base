Real text, measured

Four small open language models and five general-purpose compressors on the same three public-domain texts. The question each view answers: does perplexity rank models the way their compression does? Reading section 10 explains the units; section 11 the compressors.

What was run
Texts (in src/inputs/): Darwin, On the Origin of Species, chapter III, first 8 paragraphs (Project Gutenberg #1228; 10,376 bytes, 10,371 characters); Hugo, Les Misérables, tome 1, first 15 paragraphs (Project Gutenberg #17489; 5,953 bytes, 5,773 characters); SQLite src/util.c lines 1300 to 1560 (commit 4d577df of 24 September 2026; SQLite is in the public domain; 6,680 bytes). Paragraphs unwrapped to one line each; em-dashes replaced by two hyphens (a house rule of this knowledge base) before measuring.
Models: openai-community/gpt2 (124M, 50,257-token vocabulary), facebook/opt-125m (the same GPT-2 byte-level BPE, 50,265 entries with its special tokens), HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M (49,152), Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B (151,665). transformers 5.18.0, torch 2.14.1, float32 on CPU. Each model's beginning-of-text token is prepended, so every byte of the text is predicted; windows of 1,024 tokens with a stride of 512, so every scored token has at least 512 tokens of context once the text is long enough (src/data/score.py).
Compressors: Apple gzip 487.0.1 (-9), bzip2 1.0.8 (-9), xz 5.8.2 (-9e), zstd 1.5.7 (-19), brotli 1.1.0 (-q 11), whole file in one call, headers included (src/data/comp.py).

What reproduces, and what does not

  • Perplexity is \(e^{\text{nats per token}}\) and bits per byte is total bits over bytes, recomputed from the stored totals for every row (src/recompute.py asserts both): by construction.
  • GPT-2's tokenizer gives 0.204 tokens per byte on Darwin, 0.363 on Hugo and 0.499 on the C code, against the 0.29335 the Pile paper measured over the whole Pile ({{Gao et al. 2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00027}}): the Pile mixes English prose with code, mathematics and other languages, so its average sits between them. Consistent, not a reproduction.
  • No published bits-per-byte figure exists for these exact passages; the model numbers are measurements, single runs, deterministic on CPU. Short texts favour the models over the compressors (section 11 counts the cost of the weights).
  • Any of these texts may be in a model's training data (Project Gutenberg books and SQLite are widely copied), which would lower its bits per byte. That is a reason to compare models with each other only on the same text, and not to read these numbers as general quality.
