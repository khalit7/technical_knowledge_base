6. Softmax as a distribution, and temperature

Softmax turns any real scores into a categorical distribution, \(p_i = e^{z_i} / \sum_k e^{z_k}\): exponentiate (everything positive), divide by the total (everything sums to 1). Two properties do most of the work:

  • Only differences of scores matter. Adding the same constant to every \(z_i\) cancels in the ratio: \(z = (2, 1, 0)\) and \(z = (1000, 999, 998)\) are the same distribution. Ratios are exponentials of gaps: \(p_{\text{cat}}/p_{\text{dog}} = e^{2 - 1} = 2.718\). This invariance is what makes the stable log-sum-exp possible (section 12).
  • Temperature rescales the gaps. \(p^{(T)}_i \propto e^{z_i / T} \propto p_i^{1/T}\). At \(T = 0.5\) the tiny model's bet becomes \((0.867, 0.117, 0.016)\), the same as squaring \(p\) and renormalising; at \(T = 2\), \((0.506, 0.307, 0.186)\). As \(T \to 0\) all mass goes to the top score (greedy decoding); as \(T \to \infty\) the bet becomes uniform. Dividing by \(T \gt 0\) never changes which word is top.

Predict, then drag: at \(T = 2\), is sat's probability closer to 0.09 or to 0.2? And what is the entropy at \(T = 10\), next to its ceiling \(\ln 3 = 1.099\)?

One knob between "always the top word" and "any word at random"

The tiny model's scores \(z = (2, 1, 0)\) divided by \(T\), then softmax. \(T = 1\) is the model's own bet, \((0.665, 0.245, 0.090)\), entropy 0.832 nats. Values recomputed in src/recompute.py.

Temperature appears in three different jobs. Decoding: sampling at \(T \lt 1\) concentrates on likely tokens; top-k and top-p go further and cut the tail, so the sampled text is no longer a sample from the model (the sampler stack, its order and min-p, with a live lab on a real model, are on {{Sampling and Decoding|n:3c65c17b0d0d8115bafcda29a2c7086e}}). Calibration: one \(T\) fitted after training fixes over- or underconfidence without touching accuracy (section 8). Distillation: soft targets at a raised temperature expose the teacher's ranking of the wrong answers ({{Hinton, Vinyals and Dean, 2015|https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531}}).

Top-k and top-p on the tiny model (depth; decoding is owned by Sampling and Decoding)

Every decoding knob replaces \(p\) by another distribution and samples from that. Top-k: keep the \(k\) most probable, renormalise; \(k = 1\) is greedy. Top-p (nucleus): keep the smallest set of most probable items whose total reaches a threshold (0.9 here), renormalise. At top-p 0.9: cat (0.665) is not enough, cat plus dog (0.910) is, so sat is removed and the bet becomes \((0.731, 0.269, 0)\). None of these changes the model. Sampling from a truncated distribution means the text's probability under the model is no longer what you sampled from, which matters when the samples train another model (RL, distillation).
