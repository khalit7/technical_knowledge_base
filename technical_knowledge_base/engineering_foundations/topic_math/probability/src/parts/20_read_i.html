12. Full Bayesian inference: keep every answer, weighted

The question. MLE and MAP each return one parameter value. After 3 heads in 3 flips, how sure should we be about the coin, and what should we bet on the next flip?

The posterior and the predictive distribution

\[ p(\theta \mid D) = \frac{p(D \mid \theta)\, p(\theta)}{p(D)}, \qquad p(y^* \mid D) = \int p(y^* \mid \theta)\, p(\theta \mid D)\, d\theta. \]
\(p(\theta \mid D)\)
the posterior: how plausible each parameter value is after the data.
\(p(D) = \int p(D \mid \theta)p(\theta)\,d\theta\)
the evidence or marginal likelihood: the probability the model as a whole gave the data, averaged over its prior. It compares models.
\(y^*\)
a new outcome to predict.

In plain words: instead of betting with one parameter value, bet with all of them, each weighted by how plausible it is. Uncertainty about \(\theta\) becomes honest uncertainty about \(y^*\).

A conjugate example, derived: the Beta-Bernoulli coin

A prior is conjugate to a likelihood when the posterior has the same form as the prior. Take the prior \(\text{Beta}(a, b) \propto p^{a - 1}(1 - p)^{b - 1}\) and \(h\) heads, \(t\) tails:

\[ p(p \mid D) \propto \underbrace{p^{h}(1 - p)^{t}}_{\text{likelihood}} \cdot \underbrace{p^{a - 1}(1 - p)^{b - 1}}_{\text{prior}} = p^{a + h - 1}(1 - p)^{b + t - 1} \;\Longrightarrow\; p \mid D \sim \text{Beta}(a + h,\, b + t). \]

Updating is adding counts. The predictive probability of heads is the posterior mean, \(\int_0^1 p\,\text{Beta}(p; a', b')\,dp = a'/(a' + b')\). With the mild prior Beta(2, 2) and 3 heads:

  • MLE: \(3/3 = 1\).
  • MAP: the mode of Beta(5, 2), \((5 - 1)/(5 + 2 - 2) = 4/5 = 0.8\).
  • Bayesian predictive: the mean of Beta(5, 2), \(5/7 = 0.714\), with posterior standard deviation \(\sqrt{5 \times 2 / (7^2 \times 8)} = 0.160\).

With the flat prior Beta(1, 1), the predictive is \((h + 1)/(n + 2)\), Laplace's rule of succession (0.8 after 3 heads in 3).

Watch it: one coin, flip by flip

Twelve flips, H H H T H H T H T H H T. Before (MLE alone): one number, the running frequency; after three heads it says \(p = 1\), so it bets everything against tails, and the tails on flip 4 costs it an infinite log loss. After (Bayesian, prior Beta(2, 2)): the whole posterior curve, with the MLE, the MAP and the posterior mean marked and the 95% credible interval shaded. The counter adds up each method's log loss on predicting the next flip before seeing it.

Credible intervals are the 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles of the Beta posterior, computed in the page by numerical integration (Simpson's rule, 4,000 panels) and checked against src/recompute.py. MLE's first prediction, before any data, is taken as 0.5. The flips are illustrative.

The numbers
After flip 4 (the first tails) the Bayesian predictive had been \(5/7\) for heads, so the tails cost \(-\ln(2/7) = 1.253\) nats, a large but finite surprise. The Bayesian sum over all 12 predictions is 8.518 nats, and it equals exactly \(-\ln p(D)\), minus the log evidence of the 12 flips under the Beta(2, 2) prior: by the chain rule (section 5), the evidence is the product of the sequential predictive probabilities. After 12 flips the MLE (0.667), MAP (0.643) and mean (0.625) are close: the prior's 2 imaginary flips are being swamped.

When the integral is intractable

For a neural network the posterior over millions of weights has no closed form, so it is approximated:

  • Variational inference: fit a simple distribution (say a Gaussian per weight) to the posterior by maximising the ELBO; on {{Variational inference and generative-model maths|n:3f05c17b0d0d81aa9857eb5fa1bac530}}.
  • Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC): a random walk whose visits are distributed like the posterior (Metropolis-Hastings, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo); averages over its samples estimate the predictive integral (section 14).
  • Laplace approximation: a Gaussian at the MAP with covariance the inverse Hessian of the loss (often approximated by the Fisher or K-FAC).
  • Deep ensembles: train several networks from different random initialisations and average their predictions, a cheap and strong practical approximation ({{Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017|https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474}}).
What it is used for in ML
Uncertainty for small data and for decisions (active learning, Bayesian optimisation of hyperparameters, Thompson sampling in {{Bandits and exploration|n:3ee5c17b0d0d81acba54d70ac51c601c}}, which draws from exactly this Beta posterior per arm); model comparison by evidence; the conceptual basis of VAEs and diffusion (a posterior over latent variables, approximated).
