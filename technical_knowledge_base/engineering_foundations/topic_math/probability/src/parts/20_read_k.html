Common mistakes

Reading a classifier's precision off its accuracy, ignoring the base rate

A 95%-sensitive, 5%-false-alarm filter on a 1% class is right only 16% of the time it fires (section 2). Always ask how common the positive class is.

Mixing up the two meanings of "independent"

"The dependent variable" is a role in an experiment; "these variables are dependent" is a claim about a joint distribution. Learning needs input and target to be dependent in the second sense (section 2).

Treating zero correlation as independence

\(Y = X^2\) with symmetric \(X\) has zero correlation and total dependence (section 4).

Reading a density as a probability

Densities can exceed 1 and Gaussian NLLs can be negative; only areas are probabilities (section 3).

Confusing likelihood with the probability of the parameters

\(p(D \mid \theta)\) as a function of \(\theta\) does not integrate to 1 (\(p^3\) has area 1/4); the posterior needs the prior (sections 10 and 12).

Treating the loss as a free design choice

MSE on a binary label is a Gaussian model of a yes-or-no outcome, with vanishing gradients through a sigmoid (section 8).

Reading a top-1 confidence as calibrated after post-training or fine-tuning

Pretraining by maximum likelihood tends to give calibrated next-token probabilities; post-training and some fine-tuning can break it. Measure it (ECE beside NLL) and fix it with temperature scaling (section 9).

Believing MAP is "the Bayesian answer"

MAP is a point estimate that moves under reparameterisation (the coin's 0.8 becomes 0.714 in logit space); the posterior and the predictive are the Bayesian answer (sections 11 and 12).

Assuming i.i.d. when it fails

Near-duplicate items, many items from one document, or test data leaked into training make examples dependent, so the likelihood is not a clean product and every error bar is too narrow (handled on {{Statistics for ML|n:3f05c17b0d0d810f896cf688ce9aed1b}}).

Forgetting that decoding changes the distribution

Text sampled at top-p 0.9 or temperature 0.7 is not a sample from the model; probabilities computed under the model do not describe it (section 13).
