Calibration, real models

Three small open models predict the next token of the same text. For every position, we record the model's top-1 token, its probability (the confidence), and whether it was the right token. Grouping positions by confidence into 15 bins and comparing each bin's accuracy with its average confidence gives the reliability diagram of the Reading's {{section 9|#t-read}}; the expected calibration error (ECE) is the size-weighted average gap. The temperature slider divides every logit by \(T\) before the softmax, the one-number fix of temperature scaling.

Try: GPT-2 at \(T = 1\) sits on the diagonal; drag \(T\) to 0.6 and every bar falls below it (overconfident: ECE 0.250 against 0.015), to 1.6 and above it (underconfident). Switch to the instruct model: its bars at \(T = 1\) sit a little below the diagonal, and the best \(T\) by NLL is 1.05. Accuracy never moves: dividing by \(T\) keeps the top token.

The NLL is a proper score, so the \(T\) that minimises it is the temperature-scaling fit (Guo et al. fit it on a validation set; here it is fitted and shown on the same tokens, so it is an in-sample illustration). ECE's minimum need not coincide with the NLL's.

How this was computed

  • Text: the test split of WikiText-2 (raw), Salesforce/wikitext, wikitext-2-raw-v1, concatenated; the first 20,480 tokens under each model's own tokenizer, scored in windows of 512 tokens (each window starts without earlier context, so its first tokens are hard).
  • Models: openai-community/gpt2 (124M), Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B (base) and Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, in float32 with transformers 5.18.0 and torch 2.14.1 on CPU; no chat template for the instruct model, so it is scored on text unlike its post-training data.
  • Measures: top-1 confidence binned into 15 equal-width bins (Guo et al., 2017, eq. 3); NLL is the average \(-\ln p(\text{true token})\) at each \(T\) from 0.50 to 2.00 in steps of 0.05. Script src/inputs/calib.py, output src/inputs/calibration.json; src/recompute.py recomputes every ECE from the stored bins.
  • Caveats: one text sample, 20,480 tokens, three small models; Qwen's training data may include Wikipedia (GPT-2's removed it). Read the numbers as the measurement made concrete, not as a comparison of the models.
