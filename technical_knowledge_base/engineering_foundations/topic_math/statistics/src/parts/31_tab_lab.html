CI and test lab: does a 95% interval cover 95%, and how much power does it give?

Choose a population, a sample size and interval methods. Every number is from 5,000 simulated experiments per setting, each with its own sample, its own intervals and (for the two bootstrap methods) 999 resamples simulated (seeded; src/sims.py). Monte Carlo error is about \(\pm 0.6\) points on a coverage near 95% (two standard errors of a proportion over 5,000 runs). The four methods are those of the Reading tab: z (1.96 with \(s\)), t (Student's multiplier), percentile bootstrap and BCa bootstrap (sections 5 and 6).

Try: the lognormal at n = 5, then 200: every method improves, none reaches 95%. Then t3 (heavy symmetric tails): t is fine, BCa is worst. Then the power curve: at n = 5 the bootstrap rejects a true null far too often, which looks like extra power but is a false-positive rate of 17% or more.

Power: how often the interval rejects a value shifted by h standard deviations

At h = 0 the "power" is the false-positive rate, 1 minus coverage (should be 5%). Shifts are in units of the population's standard deviation; the test is "reject \(\mu_0\) if it lies outside the interval", the duality of section 7.
