Double descent lab: bias, variance and the interpolation threshold

The experiment of Reading section 11, with every setting. Inputs are \(D = 100\) independent standard Gaussian features; the target is \(y = x^\top\beta + \varepsilon\) with noise standard deviation \(\sigma = 0.2\) and \(\lVert\beta\rVert = 1\). The model sees only the first \(p\) features and is fitted on \(n = 40\) examples by least squares (the minimum-norm solution when \(p \ge 40\)) or by ridge regression with penalty \(\lambda\). For each \(p\) from 1 to 100 it was refitted on the same 1,000 independent training sets; the test error of a fit is exact for Gaussian inputs, \(\lVert\hat w - \beta_{1..p}\rVert^2 + \lVert\beta_{p+1..D}\rVert^2 + \sigma^2\), and its average splits into bias² and variance as in section 11 (src/sims.py, part dd). With \(\lambda = 0\) the circles are Belkin, Hsu and Xu's Theorem 1 (arXiv 1903.07571, SIAM J. Math. Data Science 2020), computed exactly: our averages reproduce it independently (largest gap 3.2% at least five features from the threshold; the theorem's expectation is infinite at \(p = 39, 40, 41\)). Selecting features in order of decreasing \(\beta_j^2\), as the two decaying signals do, is the paper's "prescient" selection model (its Figure 2).

Try: signal "spread evenly" with \(\lambda = 0\): the best error is at \(p = 100\), past the threshold. Switch to "first features matter most": the classic sweet spot below the threshold wins. Then raise \(\lambda\) and watch the peak flatten (Nakkiran et al., arXiv 2003.01897).

Why the peak: the smallest singular value of the training matrix

Median over the 1,000 training sets of the smallest singular value of the \(40 \times p\) matrix of training inputs. Least squares divides the noise by these singular values, so when one is near zero (at \(p = n\), a square random matrix) the fit amplifies noise without limit. This is one of the three factors identified by Schaeffer et al., "Double Descent Demystified" (arXiv 2303.14151). Ridge adds \(\lambda\) to the squared singular values, which caps the amplification.
