Source of the interactive HTML on the Notion page "Statistics for ML: estimation, bias and variance, bootstrap, hypothesis tests" (child of Topic: math).

Build: `sh build.sh` writes `../index.html`: parts `01_head`, `10_header`, `20_read_a` to `20_read_j` and `20_read_z` (Reading), `31_tab_lab`, `32_tab_dd`, `39_tab_more`, then every `*.js` in name order in its own `<script>` (`20_js_data.js` first), `99_js_tabs.js` last. LaTeX in `\( \)` and `\[ \]` becomes MathML through `html_utils/tex2mathml.mjs` (530 formulas).

Data, in order:
1. `sims.py` (run from `src/` with `uv run --no-project --with numpy==2.5.3 --with scipy==1.18.1 python sims.py`, threads capped at 2; about 3 minutes) writes seeded simulations to `inputs/sims_<part>.json`: `grid` (coverage, misses, width and power of z, t, percentile and BCa intervals; 6 populations x 7 sample sizes x 5,000 experiments x 999 resamples), `anim` (100 experiments for the Reading animation), `clt` (sampling distribution of the mean, 20,000 per cell), `boot` (one sample, its bootstrap and the truth), `entropy` (exact multinomial enumeration), `dd` (weak-features double descent, 1,000 training sets per p, four ridge strengths), `conc` (exact binomial tails), `peek` (optional stopping, 200,000 runs), `cv` (naive 10-fold CV interval coverage, 4,000 datasets).
2. `recompute.py` (same command) recomputes every number written on the page, recounts the animation's coverage, checks the double-descent run against Belkin, Hsu and Xu's Theorem 1, and writes `expected.json` (stdout in `recompute.out`).
3. `mk_data.py` writes `parts/20_js_data.js` (window.SD, 121 KB) from the inputs and `expected.json`.
4. From the repo root, `node technical_knowledge_base/engineering_foundations/topic_math/statistics/src/check_page.mjs` checks about 140 numbers written in the text, then at 390 px dark and 920 px light with reduced motion clicks every control (CLT populations and steps, every animation scenario and multiplier to step 100 with exact counts, bootstrap samples, every bounds n, ridge toggle, every lab population, n and method, every double-descent signal, ridge and p extreme) and compares the JavaScript's numbers with `expected.json`. Last run 2026-10-05: 500 checks, 0 fails. `sh html_utils/checkpage.sh` on the folder: fail=0, emdash 0, errbox 1, clipped 0.

Inputs (`inputs/`): the `sims_*.json` files; `belkin_hsu_xu_2020_theorem1.txt` (pdftotext excerpt of Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, arXiv 1903.07571v2); `asa_2016_pvalue_press_release_excerpt.txt` (the six principles, from the ASA's release PDF); `pytorch_v2.14.1_variance_excerpts.txt` (BatchNorm biased and unbiased variance, `torch.std` correction default). Coverage: `coverage.json` maps the 40 facts handed over by the probability page (`../../probability/src/handoff_statistics.md`) and the root's section 6.

Shape: Part B of `html_utils/methods/topic_pages.md`, following the root and probability pages' teaching pattern for a rusty reader (each numbered section: the question, the words, the formula with every symbol, plain words, numbers, what it is used for in ML, and a "Go deeper" link to the matching section of Eval statistics). Departure: the running example is the tiny model's sampled loss rather than a real model, because a known truth (entropy 0.832 nats) is what lets coverage, bias and power be measured; real model comparisons live on Eval statistics. Real things: seeded simulations computed in Python and reproduced, a published closed form (Theorem 1) reproduced independently, library source quoted at pinned versions.
