12. NaN hunting: where non-finite numbers come from and how to find the first one

The question. Step 41,200 logs loss: nan. Every weight is now NaN. Which operation started it?

NaN never appears from nowhere: some operation received an input outside its domain or produced a result outside the format's range. Once it exists it spreads through every operation that touches it, through the backward pass into the gradients, through the optimiser into the weights, and the run is over. The usual sources:

OperationResultTypical ML cause
\(e^{x}\), \(x \gt 88.72\) (fp32) or \(\gt 11.09\) (fp16)\(\infty\)naive softmax or sigmoid, an exponentiated log-variance
any result above 65,504 in fp16\(\infty\)attention scores, sums of squares, large activations, too large a loss scale
\(\infty / \infty\), \(\infty - \infty\), \(0 \times \infty\)NaNnormalising by an overflowed sum; Adam's \(m / \sqrt{v}\) once both overflow
\(0 / 0\)NaNa norm or standard deviation of a zero or constant vector without \(\epsilon\)
\(\ln 0\), \(x / 0\)\(-\infty\), \(\pm\infty\)log of an underflowed probability; division by an underflowed count
\(\ln x\), \(\sqrt{x}\) for \(x \lt 0\)NaNa variance that cancelled to a negative number (section 6)
\(\arccos x\), \(|x| \gt 1\)NaNa cosine similarity that rounds to \(1.0000001\) (measured: torch.acos(torch.tensor(1.0000001)) is NaN; clamp first)
gradient of \(\sqrt{x}\) at 0\(\infty\)an L2 norm of a zero vector inside the loss (measured: the gradient is inf)

A real hunt, on the running example

The naive softmax cross-entropy on logits \((1000, 999, 0)\), target sat, in PyTorch 2.14.1 (measured, src/inputs/nan_demo.py): the forward pass returns loss = inf, not NaN (\(p_{\text{sat}} = 1/\infty = 0\), \(-\ln 0 = \infty\)), and the backward pass returns gradients [nan, nan, nan]. Wrapping the same code in torch.autograd.detect_anomaly() stops at the culprit: Function 'DivBackward0' returned nan values in its 0th output, the division by the overflowed sum. The fused F.cross_entropy on the same logits returns 1000.3132 with gradient [0.7311, 0.2689, -1.0].

A procedure

  1. Check where it first appears. Log the loss and the global gradient norm every step; a gradient norm that jumps by orders of magnitude a few steps before the NaN points to an instability (learning rate, a layer's logits growing), not a single bad operation. With GradScaler, a few skipped steps are normal; skipped steps every iteration are not.
  2. Find the first non-finite activation. Register a forward hook on every module that checks torch.isfinite(output).all() and records the module name; the first one to fail is the culprit, everything after it is collateral.
  3. Find the first non-finite gradient. torch.autograd.detect_anomaly() (or set_detect_anomaly(True)) does "two things": it records forward tracebacks so the backward error can name "the forward operation that created the failing backward function", and with check_nan=True "any backward computation that generates 'nan' value will raise an error". Its own warning: "This mode should be enabled only for debugging" ({{anomaly_mode.py, v2.14.1|https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.14.1/torch/autograd/anomaly_mode.py}}).
  4. Replay the batch. Save the batch and the weights at the failing step; rerun in float32, then in float64. If float64 is finite, it is a precision or range problem (section 4); if not, it is the data or the maths (a log of 0, a division by a zero norm).
  5. Fix the cause, not the symptom. Replace the naive formula with its stable form (sections 9 and 10), keep the risky operation in float32, lower the learning rate or clip gradients for an instability. torch.nan_to_num on a loss hides the evidence; skipping a step on a non-finite loss is acceptable only as a guard while you look.
What it is used for in ML
Every divergence post-mortem. Two published instabilities had exactly this shape: PaLM added an auxiliary "z loss \(= 10^{-4} \cdot \log^2 Z\) to encourage the softmax normalizer \(\log(Z)\) to be close to 0, which we found increases the stability of training" ({{Chowdhery et al. 2022|https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311}}, section 5); ViT-22B saw attention logits "grow to over 50000 in magnitude" without normalisation, near float16's 65,504, and fixed it by applying LayerNorm to queries and keys ({{Dehghani et al. 2023|https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05442}}, appendix B).

13. Where research is now

The question. Which numerical questions are still open in 2026, and which papers are worth reading next?
  • Fewer bits for training. FP8 training at 671B parameters with fine-grained scaling (DeepSeek-V3, December 2024; the {{DeepSeek-V3 paper page|n:3c65c17b0d0d815fb8dac9ba1e35ab81}} animates it in its Quantise and accumulate tab) and NVFP4 pretraining of a 12B model on 10 trillion tokens, "the longest publicly documented training run in 4-bit precision to date", using random Hadamard transforms, two-dimensional block scaling, stochastic rounding of gradients and a few layers kept in higher precision ({{NVIDIA, Sep 2025|https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149}}). Every ingredient is this page's: range (scales), precision (rounding), and accumulation. Depth and measurements: {{Quantization and precision|n:3c65c17b0d0d817b8f41ff66fba4cd0e}}.
  • 16-bit-only training. Can the float32 master copy go? Zamirai et al. show nearest rounding "cancels small updates" in bf16 and that stochastic rounding and Kahan summation on the weight update recover "0.1% lower to 0.2% higher validation accuracy compared to 32-bit training" across seven applications ({{2020|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06192}}).
  • Determinism as a feature. Batch-invariant kernels make inference reproducible at a speed cost ({{Thinking Machines 2025|https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/}}); on-policy reinforcement learning needs the sampler's and the trainer's log-probabilities to match bit for bit.
  • Accurate kernels, one pass. Online softmax ({{Milakov and Gimelshein 2018|https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02867}}) inside FlashAttention, with error analysis of shifted log-sum-exp and softmax ({{Blanchard, Higham and Higham 2021|https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/draa038}}).
  • Stability by architecture. QK normalisation, z-loss and softmax capping keep logits in range so that low precision survives (section 12).

14. Common mistakes

Comparing floats with ==
\(0.1 + 0.2 \ne 0.3\) in every binary format. Compare with a tolerance scaled to the dtype: torch.testing.assert_close or math.isclose(a, b, rel_tol=...), with the relative tolerance a few times \(\varepsilon\) of the dtype.
Writing torch.log(torch.softmax(z)) or Sigmoid then BCELoss
Probabilities round to 0 or 1 before the log sees them. Use log_softmax, cross_entropy on logits, and binary_cross_entropy_with_logits (sections 9 and 10).
Keeping a running sum, a metric or an optimiser state in bf16
A bf16 running sum of ones stops at 256; a bf16 sum of a thousand numbers in \([0, 1)\) is 50% low (measured, section 7). Accumulate in float32; keep master weights and Adam's moments in float32.
Believing machine epsilon is the smallest positive number
\(\varepsilon\) is the gap above 1 (float32 \(1.19 \times 10^{-7}\)); the smallest positive float32 is \(1.4 \times 10^{-45}\) and the smallest normal one \(1.18 \times 10^{-38}\). And libraries' eps is \(2u\), twice the unit roundoff in textbooks.
Using fp16 without a loss scaler, or bf16 with one "to be safe"
fp16 without scaling silently zeroes gradients (8.2% on SmolLM2, measured). bf16 does not need scaling; a scaler is harmless but adds skipped steps if misconfigured.
Clipping gradients before unscaling them
With GradScaler the gradients are \(S\) times too large until unscaled; clip after scaler.unscale_(optimizer).
Expecting bit-identical results across GPUs, batch sizes or thread counts
The order of additions changes (section 8). torch.sum of the same tensor gives different results at 4 and 8 CPU threads (measured). Compare with tolerances; for exact reproducibility fix hardware, versions, batch shapes and use deterministic algorithms.
Computing a variance as \(\overline{x^2} - \bar x^2\)
It can be negative (\(-8\) on three float32 numbers near 10,000, measured). Use two passes or Welford.
Storing logits or targets in bf16 and expecting their small differences to survive
999 and 1000 are the same bfloat16 number; so are 256 and 257. Keep logits, losses and anything compared by small differences in float32.
Trusting an FP8 cast's overflow behaviour without checking
PyTorch saturates \(1000 \to 448\) in E4M3; ml_dtypes returns NaN (measured, section 4).
Letting the compiler reassociate
Fast-math flags (-ffast-math, some JIT settings) let the compiler assume associativity and delete Kahan's correction term as algebraically zero (section 7).
