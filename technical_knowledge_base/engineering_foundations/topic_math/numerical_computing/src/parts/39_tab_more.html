Further reading

Reading times for knowledge base pages are each page's own stated time where it gives one, otherwise estimated from the length of its Reading tab (about 230 words a minute). Times for outside resources are for the part recommended, checked on 5 October 2026. Every link opens in a new tab.

The tabs on this page

{{Float explorer|#t-float}}

Any number in float64, float32, TF32, bfloat16, float16, FP8 E5M2 and E4M3: bits you can flip, stored value, rounding error, gap, neighbours, and what adding a small \(y\) does.

{{Summation lab|#t-sum}}

Up to 100,000 numbers summed in 16 or 32 bits by four algorithms, with error against \(N\), five kinds of data and 20 shuffled orders.

Books and classic papers

{{Goldberg 1991, "What every computer scientist should know about floating-point arithmetic"|https://doi.org/10.1145/103162.103163}}

The classic tutorial: rounding error, guard digits, cancellation, the IEEE standard, signed zero, denormals. Read sections 1 and 2. About 60 min.

{{Higham 2002, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, 2nd ed.|https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9780898718027}}

The reference. Chapter 1 (principles: cancellation, conditioning, stability), chapter 2 (floating point, the standard model), chapter 4 (summation: recursive, pairwise, compensated). About 3 hours for the three chapters.

{{Blanchard, Higham and Higham 2021, "Accurately computing the log-sum-exp and softmax functions"|https://doi.org/10.1093/imanum/draa038}}

Rounding-error analysis of the shifted formulas of section 9. About 40 min.

{{Kahan 1965, "Further remarks on reducing truncation errors"|https://doi.org/10.1145/363707.363723}}

One page: compensated summation. About 5 min.

{{Welford 1962, "Note on a method for calculating corrected sums of squares and products"|https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1962.10490022}}

The one-pass variance of section 6. About 5 min.

{{IEEE 754-2019|https://doi.org/10.1109/IEEESTD.2019.8766229}}

The standard itself (paywalled); the formats, rounding modes and special values of sections 2 and 3. Reference.

Formats and mixed precision

{{Micikevicius et al. 2017, "Mixed Precision Training"|https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.03740}}

Master weights, loss scaling, FP32 accumulation; section 3 is the argument reproduced on SmolLM2 in section 11. About 25 min.

{{Micikevicius et al. 2022, "FP8 Formats for Deep Learning"|https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05433}}

E4M3 and E5M2, table 1, and which tensors get which. About 20 min.

{{OCP 8-bit Floating Point Specification (OFP8) rev. 1.0, June 2023|https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-8-bit-floating-point-specification-ofp8-revision-1-0-2023-06-20-pdf}}

Tables 1 to 3: encodings, special values, saturating and non-saturating conversion. About 15 min.

{{Wang and Kanwar 2019, "BFloat16: The secret to high performance on Cloud TPUs"|https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/bfloat16-the-secret-to-high-performance-on-cloud-tpus}}

Why bf16 keeps float32's exponent. About 10 min.

{{Kalamkar et al. 2019, "A Study of BFLOAT16 for Deep Learning Training"|https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12322}}

bf16 across image, speech, language and recommendation models. About 25 min.

{{NVIDIA 2025, "Pretraining Large Language Models with NVFP4"|https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149}}

4-bit pretraining at 12B and 10T tokens: Hadamard transforms, 2D scaling, stochastic rounding. About 40 min.

{{Zamirai et al. 2020, "Revisiting BFloat16 Training"|https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06192}}

Stochastic rounding and Kahan summation instead of float32 master weights. About 30 min.

Non-associativity, atomics, batch invariance; the 80 unique completions. About 30 min.

{{Milakov and Gimelshein 2018, "Online normalizer calculation for softmax"|https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02867}}

The one-pass softmax. About 15 min.

{{PyTorch notes: Numerical accuracy (v2.14.1)|https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.14.1/docs/source/notes/numerical_accuracy.md}}

Non-associativity, extremal values, TF32, reduced-precision reductions, denormal flushing on AMD. About 15 min.

{{PyTorch notes: Reproducibility (v2.14.1)|https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.14.1/docs/source/notes/randomness.md}}

Seeds, cuDNN, deterministic algorithms, DataLoader workers. About 10 min.
