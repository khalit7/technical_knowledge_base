3. Critical points and the Hessian: minimum, maximum or saddle

Where the gradient is zero the ground is flat in every direction: a critical point. A bowl, an upside-down bowl, a saddle and a flat-floored trough all share that flatness; only the second derivatives tell them apart.

Four flat points, four shapes: each panel's arrows are its Hessian's eigenvectors (blue: positive eigenvalue, the surface curves up; orange: negative, it curves down; grey: zero, drawn short), with lengths proportional to the eigenvalue.

The Hessian is symmetric when the second derivatives are continuous (Schwarz's theorem; \(f = x^2 y\) has both mixed derivatives equal to \(2x\)), so it has real eigenvalues \(\lambda_i\) and perpendicular eigenvectors \(u_i\) (the spectral theorem; {{Linear algebra for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d89c26e1dab2fcb701}}, sections 10 and 11). Along an eigenvector the Taylor model at a critical point is a plain parabola, \(L(\theta^* + t\,u_i) \approx L(\theta^*) + \tfrac12 \lambda_i\, t^2\): each eigenvalue is the curvature along its direction, and the second-derivative test follows.

The fraction of negative eigenvalues is the critical point's index (section 11 uses it). For softmax cross-entropy, differentiating \(p - e_t\) once more gives

\[ \nabla_z^2 L = \operatorname{diag}(p) - p\,p^\top \]

The label has disappeared: the curvature does not depend on the target. For one sigmoid output it is the number \(p(1 - p)\) (the root's {{Gradient lab|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cdb851c0835b25a538}}, panel 4, shows why it equals the Fisher information). For logistic regression the chain rule through \(z_i = w^\top x_i\) gives \(\nabla_w^2 L = \frac1n X^\top S X\) with \(S = \operatorname{diag}(p_i(1-p_i))\).

The numbers
Tiny model, in the scores: at \(p = (0.665, 0.245, 0.090)\) the eigenvalues of \(\operatorname{diag}(p) - pp^\top\) are 0, 0.119 and 0.371 (the zero is the direction \((1, 1, 1)\): shifting every score equally changes nothing). In the weights each is multiplied by \(\lVert x \rVert^2 = 5\), so \(\lambda_{\max} = 1.855\). Real logistic regression (569 breast-cancer patients, 30 features plus an intercept, section 6): at the start the Hessian's eigenvalues run from 0.0018 to 3.32, a condition number of 1,855; at the optimum from 0.0018 to 0.150, a condition number of 85.9.
