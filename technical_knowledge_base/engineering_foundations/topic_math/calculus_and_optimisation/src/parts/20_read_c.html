2. The gradient of every standard loss: prediction minus target

Backprop starts from \(\partial L / \partial z\), the gradient of the loss in the logit \(z\) (the raw score before any squashing). For every standard pairing of final activation and loss it comes out as prediction minus target, \(\hat y - y\), \(p - y\), \(p - e_t\) or \(e^z - y\); the result, the table of pairings and what it means for training (why squared error on a sigmoid goes silent when the model is most wrong) are owned by {{Loss functions|n:3c65c17b0d0d8161a72bc8c572f37d55}}, and why each loss is a negative log-likelihood by {{Probability for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d815f9a47d613409b5a0c}}, section 7; this section owns the derivation steps (closed below). The step that makes them all short: in the binary case the slope of the loss in \(p\), \(\frac{p - y}{p(1 - p)}\), blows up as \(p\) saturates, and the slope of \(p\) in \(z\) is \(p(1 - p)\): they cancel exactly.

Two more consequences. Stability: frameworks fuse the softmax into the loss (CrossEntropyLoss and BCEWithLogitsLoss take logits) and compute \(\ln \sum_j e^{z_j} = m + \ln \sum_j e^{z_j - m}\) with \(m = \max_j z_j\); for logits \((1000, 1001)\), \(e^{1001}\) overflows even in float64, while the shifted form gives 1001.313. Cost: the \(K \times K\) softmax Jacobian is never built; \(p - e_t\) costs \(K\) operations, with \(K\) the vocabulary for a language model. From here the gradient flows back through the network: {{Matrix calculus and backprop|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93}} (sections 4, 5 and 7, including the old page's worked logistic unit, \(z = 0.1\), \(p = 0.525\), loss 0.644, one step to \(p = 0.595\)).

Derivations and worked numbers for each loss

Mean squared error and the factor of a half. \(L = \tfrac12 (\hat y - y)^2\) with \(\hat y = z\) gives \(\partial L/\partial z = \hat y - y\). The half is a convention. Without it the gradient is \(2(\hat y - y)\); averaged over a batch, \(L = \frac1n \sum_i (\hat y_i - y_i)^2\) gives each example \(\frac{2}{n}(\hat y_i - y_i)\) (the old page's "\(2/n\)", which the root's Gradient lab corrected by stating both conventions). PyTorch's MSELoss has no half; the constant only rescales the learning rate. Example: \(z = 1.5\), \(y = 1\): with the half, \(L = 0.125\) and gradient 0.5; without it, \(L = 0.25\) and gradient 1.0.

Linear regression. With \(\hat y = Xw\) and the batch-mean loss without the half, \(\nabla_w L = \frac{2}{n} X^\top (Xw - y)\); setting it to zero gives the normal equations \(X^\top X w = X^\top y\): at the optimum the residual is perpendicular to every column of \(X\). Worked example (old page, recomputed): points \((1, 1), (2, 3), (3, 4)\) with an intercept column give \(X^\top X = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 6 \\ 6 & 14 \end{pmatrix}\), \(X^\top y = (8, 19)\), determinant 6, so \(w = (-1/3, 3/2)\): the line \(\hat y = -0.333 + 1.5x\). The residuals \((0.167, -0.333, 0.167)\) sum to 0 and \(1(0.167) + 2(-0.333) + 3(0.167) = 0\). Solving it stably (QR rather than the normal equations): {{Linear algebra for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d89c26e1dab2fcb701}}, section 7.

Binary cross-entropy. \(L = -[y \ln p + (1 - y) \ln (1 - p)]\), \(p = \sigma(z)\): \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial z} = \frac{p - y}{p(1 - p)} \cdot p(1 - p) = p - y\). Computed stably on the logit as \(\max(z, 0) - yz + \ln(1 + e^{-|z|})\), which never exponentiates a large positive number. Examples (old page, recomputed): \(z = -6\), \(y = 1\): \(p = 0.0025\), loss 6.0025, gradient \(-0.9975\), nearly the largest push possible. \(z = 2\), \(y = 1\): \(p = 0.8808\), loss 0.1269, gradient \(-0.1192\).

Softmax cross-entropy. Write \(L = -z_t + \ln \sum_j e^{z_j}\): the slope of \(-z_t\) in \(z_k\) is \(-\mathbb{1}[k = t]\), and the slope of the log-sum-exp is \(p_k\), so \(\partial L/\partial z_k = p_k - \mathbb{1}[k = t]\). Going through the softmax Jacobian \(\partial p_k / \partial z_j = p_k(\delta_{kj} - p_j)\) (as a matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(p) - pp^\top\)) gives the same answer. With soft targets \(q\) (label smoothing, distillation) \(\nabla_z L = p - q\) because the \(q_k\) sum to 1. Old page example: \(z = (2, 1, 0.1)\), class 1 true: \(e^z = (7.389, 2.718, 1.105)\), sum 11.2125, which rounds to 11.213 (the old page printed 11.212), so \(p = (0.659, 0.242, 0.099)\), loss 0.417, gradient \((-0.341, 0.242, 0.099)\). The components sum to 0, because adding a constant to every logit leaves the softmax unchanged. The tiny model, true word sat: \(p - e_{\text{sat}} = (0.665, 0.245, -0.910)\); with soft target \(q = (0.9, 0.05, 0.05)\): \(p - q = (-0.235, 0.195, 0.040)\).

Poisson. Rate \(\mu = e^z\), loss \(e^z - yz + \ln y!\), gradient \(e^z - y\); with \(y = 3\) and \(z = \ln 2\), the loss is 1.712 and the gradient \(-1\).
