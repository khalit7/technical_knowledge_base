19. Common mistakes and misconceptions

Treating \(g = 0\) as a minimum
In high dimensions a stationary point is far more likely to be a saddle (section 17). Check the curvature: the smallest Hessian eigenvalue, estimated by power iteration on \(H - cI\) or by Lanczos.
"Blame the data" for a learning-rate blow-up
A loss that oscillates and then grows right after the learning rate was raised is the \(2/\lambda_{\max}\) limit (section 8). In full-batch training the network may instead sit at the edge of stability, with a loss that wobbles but falls (section 17).
Quoting \((\kappa - 1)/(\kappa + 1)\) as gradient descent's rate at any step
It holds only at the best fixed step \(2/(\lambda_{\max} + \lambda_{\min})\); at the safe step \(1/\lambda_{\max}\) the rate is \(1 - 1/\kappa\), and anything above \(2/\lambda_{\max}\) diverges on a quadratic.
Running Newton with an indefinite Hessian or from far away
Undamped, it steps towards maxima and saddles, and from a flat region it jumps far (the \(w = 8\) start in section 10 diverges). Use a line search, a trust region, \(H + \lambda I\), or \(|H|\).
"Adam is a second-order method"
It divides by the square root of a moving average of squared gradients, a diagonal quantity related to the Fisher, not the Hessian, and not inverted (section 10).
MSE for classification, or squashing twice
On a sigmoid output MSE's gradient vanishes exactly when the model is confidently wrong (203 times smaller than cross-entropy's at \(z = -6\)). Passing probabilities to BCEWithLogitsLoss or CrossEntropyLoss, which expect logits, squashes twice and shrinks gradients. Computing log(softmax(z)) in two steps underflows; use the fused log-softmax.
Forgetting which MSE convention is in use
\(\tfrac12(\hat y - y)^2\) has gradient \(\hat y - y\); \((\hat y - y)^2\) has \(2(\hat y - y)\); the batch mean divides by \(n\). Each changes the effective learning rate by a constant (section 5).
Assuming the whole network is convex because cross-entropy is
Cross-entropy is convex in the logits; the loss in earlier layers' weights is not (the permutation argument, section 13).
Using L-BFGS with minibatches
Gradient differences then measure sampling noise as much as curvature, and the estimate degrades; L-BFGS is for full-batch, deterministic objectives (section 11).
Expecting a constant SGD step to converge
It stalls at a noise floor proportional to the step (section 16); decay the step, or grow the batch.
Mixing up the lambdas
On this page \(\lambda\) is an eigenvalue (sections 6 to 9), a Lagrange multiplier (section 14) and an L2 penalty (sections 11 and 12). The KKT view links the last two: a penalty strength is a multiplier.
Reading sharpness as a fixed property of the problem
In a network the curvature moves with training and is driven to \(2/\eta\) by the learning rate itself (section 17).
