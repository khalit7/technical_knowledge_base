The idea in one screen

A gradient is a local straight-line model of the loss; a Hessian is a local bowl-shaped model; every optimiser is a rule for how far to trust that model.

Two models of the same real loss at the same point, and where each one sends you
The logistic-regression loss of one weight on the breast-cancer data (grey; the same function as the Newton animation of section 5), at \(w_0 = 1.5\): slope \(-0.106\), curvature 0.104. Gradient descent trusts the tangent line (dashed) only under a step penalty; with \(\eta = 1/\beta\) it lands at 1.920, safe and short. Newton trusts the touching parabola (blue) and jumps to its bottom, 2.518. The optimum is 3.551. Far from it the parabola can be badly wrong (section 5).

Write the loss near where you stand as a Taylor model, \(L(\theta + \delta) \approx L(\theta) + g^\top \delta + \tfrac12 \delta^\top H \delta\). Every section reads something off it: which way is down (\(g\)), how sharply it curves (\(H\)), how big a step is safe (\(2/\lambda_{\max}\)), where the bottom is (\(-H^{-1}g\)), and when the bottom you find is the only one (convexity). Constraints (Lagrange, KKT) and minibatch noise change the model; deep networks show where it stops being enough.

You need: eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix and what positive definite means ({{Linear algebra for ML|n:3c65c17b0d0d81d89c26e1dab2fcb701}}, sections 8 and 9). Elsewhere: how gradients are computed through a network (Jacobians, vector-Jacobian products, Hessian-vector products) is {{Matrix calculus and backprop|n:3f05c17b0d0d817e9892e01088179c93}}; SGD, Adam, Muon and schedules in practice are {{Optimisers and learning-rate schedulers|n:3c65c17b0d0d817080bbc90954681d09}}; floating point and stable softmax are {{Numerical computing for ML|n:3f05c17b0d0d81d6af0be4a9a2edba8d}}. This page is the mathematics underneath them.

Taylor model: value plus slope term plus curvature term, valid near a point.
Hessian \(H\): the matrix of second derivatives; its eigenvalues are curvatures.
Condition number \(\kappa\): \(\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}\), how stretched the bowl is.
\(\beta\)-smooth: curvature never above \(\beta\) in any direction.
Convex: chords lie above the graph, so any flat point is the global minimum.
Saddle: a flat point that curves up in some directions and down in others.
Sharpness: the top Hessian eigenvalue during training.
Lagrange multiplier, KKT: the price of a constraint, and the conditions an optimum under constraints satisfies.
