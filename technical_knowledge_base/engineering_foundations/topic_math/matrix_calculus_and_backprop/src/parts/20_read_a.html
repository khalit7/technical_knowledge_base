In one screen

Training needs one number per parameter: how much the loss changes when that parameter moves. Matrix calculus is the bookkeeping that says what shape those numbers come in and how to get them from local pieces; backpropagation is that bookkeeping run backwards through the computation, one cheap product at a time. This page rebuilds it from the slope of a curve to the autograd engine inside PyTorch. About 45 minutes in all: sections 1 to 9 are the main line (about 30 minutes); sections 10 to 13 and the research notes are depth to come back to.

The running example
The same tiny next-token model as the {{root page|n:3c65c17b0d0d81cdb851c0835b25a538}}: the context "the cat" is the input \(x = (2, 1)\), the weight matrix \(W\) has one row per word, cat \((1, 0)\), dog \((0, 1)\), sat \((1, -2)\), the scores are \(z = Wx = (2, 1, 0)\), softmax turns them into probabilities \(p = (0.665, 0.245, 0.090)\), and the right next word is sat, so the loss is \(L = -\ln 0.090 = 2.408\). Every formula below is tried on these numbers first.
