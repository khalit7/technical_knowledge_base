5. Backprop through a two-layer network, by hand

Put a ReLU layer in front of the scores: \(a = W_1 x\), \(h = \operatorname{ReLU}(a)\), \(z = W_2 h\), cross-entropy on sat. This is the root page's Gradient lab network, \(W_1\) (\(3 \times 2\)) with rows \((0.5, 0)\), \((0, 1)\), \((1, -3)\) and \(W_2\) (\(3 \times 3\)) with rows \((2, 0, 1)\), \((0, 1, 0)\), \((-1, 1, 0)\) illustrative, chosen so the scores come out \((2, 1, 0)\) again and one hidden unit is off. Every step below is one of the rules in section 6, and the hero figure at the top is this network.

One forward pass, one backward pass, every gradient: watch unit 3's row of W̄1

The numbers, checked against PyTorch 2.14.1 autograd (largest difference 4.4e-16, src/two_layer.py)
Forward: \(a = (1, 1, -1)\), \(h = (1, 1, 0)\), \(z = (2, 1, 0)\), \(p = (0.665, 0.245, 0.090)\), \(L = 2.408\). Backward: \(\bar z = p - y = (0.665, 0.245, -0.910)\); \(\bar W_2 = \bar z\,h^\top\) has a zero third column; \(\bar h = W_2^\top \bar z = (2.240, -0.665, 0.665)\); \(\bar a = \bar h \odot [a > 0] = (2.240, -0.665, 0)\); \(\bar W_1 = \bar a\,x^\top\) has rows \((4.481, 2.240)\), \((-1.330, -0.665)\), \((0, 0)\); \(\bar x = W_1^\top \bar a = (1.120, -0.665)\). One step with learning rate 0.1 on both weights raises \(p_{\text{sat}}\) from 0.090 to 0.483 and lowers the loss from 2.408 to 0.728.

Three moves did all of it: a matrix product sends \(\bar y\) back through \(W^\top\) and gives its weight \(\bar y\,x^\top\); an elementwise function multiplies by its slope (a ReLU's is 0 or 1); and the loss starts the pass at \(p - y\). Unit 3 was off, so its row of \(\bar W_1\) and its column of \(\bar W_2\) are zero: a unit that is off for every example never learns (a dead ReLU). The matrix-product work was 15 multiply-adds forward and 30 backward, twice, which is section 8's rule on the smallest possible case.

\(x = (1, 2)\), \(w = (0.5, -0.25)\), \(b = 0.1\), label 1. Forward: \(z = w \cdot x + b = 0.1\), \(p = \sigma(0.1) = 0.525\), loss \(-\ln 0.525 = 0.644\). Backward: \(\bar z = p - 1 = -0.475\); the linear rule gives \(\bar w = \bar z\,x = (-0.475, -0.950)\), \(\bar b = -0.475\) and \(\bar x = \bar z\,w = (-0.2375, 0.1188)\). One step with learning rate 0.1: \(w = (0.5475, -0.155)\), \(b = 0.1475\), new \(z = 0.385\), \(p = 0.595\): the right answer became more likely.
