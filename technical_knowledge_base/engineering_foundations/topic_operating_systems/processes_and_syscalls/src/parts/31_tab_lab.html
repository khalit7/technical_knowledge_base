Two small simulators of the kernel's process rules, to run the calls of sections 3 to 7 yourself. The first follows descriptors through pipe, fork, dup2, close and exec; the second follows the process tree through fork, exit and wait, with Linux's rules and OSTEP's, and is checked against OSTEP's own homework simulator. Both are models: they apply the rules this page states, they do not run a kernel. Where a preset matches a real run, the measured result is quoted.

1. Descriptor tables, step by step

Write your own script

One operation per line, processes by number: proc 1 sh, tty 1, pipe 1 or pipe 1 O_CLOEXEC, open 1 log O_WRONLY|O_CREAT|O_TRUNC (add |O_CLOEXEC), fork 1 2 name, dup2 2 4 1 (pid, old, new), close 2 3 4, exec 2 python, write 2 1 text\n, read 3 0, exit 2. Then pick "your own script" above.

2. The process tree: fork, exit, wait, and who adopts orphans

Actions, comma-separated, in OSTEP's fork.py notation: a+b means a forks b, b- means b exits. Added for Linux mode: a* means a calls wait and reaps its exited children. Process a is PID 1.

Process tree
What happened

The rules. Linux: an exiting process becomes a zombie (Z) until its parent waits; its direct children move to the nearest subreaper ancestor, or to PID 1; grandchildren stay where they are; a zombie whose parent is PID 1 stays until PID 1 waits, unless PID 1 reaps (tini does). fork.py default: every descendant of the exiting process moves directly under the root. fork.py -R: the exiting process's children move to its parent. fork.py -L: only leaves may exit. The real reparenting run in section 5 of the Reading tab shows Linux's rule.

b's zombie waits for a; when c exits it has been adopted by a, so it is a's zombie too; d was adopted by a when c exited, so its zombie is a's as well. Three zombies under PID 1, which never waits: exactly the training-script-as-PID-1 problem. Tick "PID 1 reaps" and run again.
