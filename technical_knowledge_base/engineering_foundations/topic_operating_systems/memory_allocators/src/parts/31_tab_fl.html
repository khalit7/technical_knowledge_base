Free-list lab: OSTEP's malloc simulator, animated

A heap of a few hundred bytes, one free list, and the three classic ways to search it. This is OSTEP's chapter 17 homework simulator, malloc.py, ported line for line: the same options produce the same random operations from the same seed and the same printed answers . Each step draws the heap to scale (allocated chunks coloured by pointer, free chunks white) and the free list in its order. Turn on Compare to run the same operations twice, with coalescing off and on: the before/after of the mechanism that keeps free space usable.

Try: preset Q5 (1,000 random operations) with Compare on: without coalescing the free list grows to dozens of slivers and later large requests fail although plenty of bytes are free in total; with it the list stays short. Then Q7 and switch the policy between BEST, WORST and FIRST.

The printed output, as malloc.py -c prints it, up to this step


External fragmentation here is 1 minus (largest free chunk / total free bytes): 0 when all free space is one chunk, near 1 when it is many slivers. "Searched" is how many list elements the search looked at, OSTEP's measure of search cost.
