3. Inside glibc's malloc: chunks, bins, the heap's top, and two thresholds

ch. 17 (real allocators), beyond OSTEP

glibc's allocator (ptmalloc, descended from Doug Lea's dlmalloc) is what malloc means on Debian, Ubuntu and most Linux machines a training job runs on. Its source is malloc/malloc.c plus arena.c; line numbers below are {{glibc 2.36|https://sourceware.org/git/?p=glibc.git;a=blob;f=malloc/malloc.c;hb=refs/tags/glibc-2.36}}, the version in the lab image, and glibc's {{MallocInternals|https://sourceware.org/glibc/wiki/MallocInternals}} page is the best tour.

A chunk (struct malloc_chunk, line 1148) starts with the previous chunk's size (used only while that one is free) and its own size, whose three low bits are flags because sizes are multiples of 16: P (PREV_INUSE, line 1366: do not merge with the chunk before me), M (IS_MMAPPED, line 1373: I came from my own mmap), A (NON_MAIN_ARENA, line 1382: I belong to a thread arena, section 4). A free chunk keeps its list links fd and bk in its first user bytes.

malloc(n) tries the cheapest place first, and asks the kernel last

Simplified order of glibc 2.36 for a request that fits each row's range: the tcache check is in __libc_malloc, the rest in _int_malloc (line 3765), which also consolidates fastbins before a large request and searches bigger bins through a bitmap before using the top. Freeing merges a chunk with free neighbours and, if it touches the top, grows the top; tcache and fastbins keep chunks whole for speed.

The cost of each place, measured. A malloc plus free of the same small size, repeated, is a tcache push and pop: 11.1 ns here. A thousand mallocs followed by a thousand frees overflow the 7-entry tcache and go through the bins, still cheap for 256 bytes (28.9 ns a pair) but 899.1 ns for 4 KiB. That jump is the trim threshold, below.

Where the bytes come from: brk, mmap, and the dynamic threshold

The main heap grows by moving the "program break" with brk ({{brk(2)|https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/brk.2.html}}): cheap, but only the top can ever be given back. A request at or above M_MMAP_THRESHOLD gets its own mmap instead, unmapped the moment it is freed. The threshold starts at 128 KiB (DEFAULT_MMAP_THRESHOLD_MIN, line 961) and is dynamic: freeing an mmapped chunk larger than it raises the threshold to that size and the trim threshold to twice that (lines 3366 to 3371), up to 32 MiB on 64-bit (4 * 1024 * 1024 * sizeof(long), line 972), on the guess ({{mallopt(3)|https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man3/mallopt.3.html}}) that the program will allocate that size again and should not pay an mmap, a munmap and fresh page faults each time.

Predict: malloc 1 MiB, touch it, free it, then malloc 1 MiB again and free it. Which system calls does the second malloc make?
The first free raised the threshold to about 1 MiB, so the second request is below it and comes from the heap (one brk), and freeing it gives nothing back: the trim threshold is now about 2 MiB. Step through both traces below, the default and with the threshold pinned.

The trim threshold, and the 899 ns. When the top chunk grows past M_TRIM_THRESHOLD (128 KiB to start, line 919), free shrinks the heap with brk (systrim, line 2971). In the batch benchmark, a thousand 4 KiB blocks freed together merge into a 4 MiB top, glibc trims it, and the next round grows the heap again and takes a page fault on every new page: 899.1 ns a pair. With MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_=67108864 the heap is kept and the same loop costs 28.4 ns. The price moves elsewhere: the setting also pins the mmap threshold, so 1 MiB blocks now take an mmap and munmap every time (4,723.3 ns a pair, against 2,142.4 by default). Every knob here trades memory held against system calls and page faults.

malloc_trim. malloc_trim(0) ({{malloc_trim(3)|https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man3/malloc_trim.3.html}}) does more than its name says: it consolidates every arena and then calls madvise(MADV_DONTNEED) on every whole free page inside free chunks anywhere in the heap, not just at the top (mtrim, lines 5035 to 5089). The kernel drops those pages from RSS while the addresses stay valid. Section 5 measures what that recovers, and what it cannot.

The knobs, and how to look inside a running process (reference)

Each tunable can be set three ways: mallopt() in C; an environment variable with a trailing underscore (MALLOC_ARENA_MAX, MALLOC_MMAP_THRESHOLD_, MALLOC_TRIM_THRESHOLD_, MALLOC_TOP_PAD_); or GLIBC_TUNABLES=glibc.malloc.trim_threshold=...:glibc.malloc.arena_max=2 ({{glibc manual, memory allocation tunables|https://www.gnu.org/software/libc/manual/html_node/Memory-Allocation-Tunables.html}}). To look inside: malloc_stats() prints per-arena totals, malloc_info(0, stdout) prints XML with every bin, mallinfo2() returns the totals as a struct ({{mallinfo(3)|https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man3/mallinfo.3.html}}). From Python, ctypes.CDLL("libc.so.6").malloc_stats() works in any process; gdb -p <pid> -batch -ex 'call (void)malloc_stats()' works in one you did not write.
