PyTorch's CUDA caching allocator, simulated from its source

not run here: no GPU A model of c10/cuda/CUDACachingAllocator.cpp at v2.14.1, written in Python from the source (src/sim/cache_ref.py, every rule with its line numbers) and ported to this page, which is checked against it step by step (). Two lanes run the same requests under two configurations: each bar is the device memory, drawn to scale; segments (what the allocator got from cudaMalloc, or mapped pages of an expandable segment) are laid side by side, live tensors filled, cached free blocks hatched.

Try: the batch-size scenario with the default on the left and expandable_segments:True on the right: the left lane fails with hundreds of MiB "reserved by PyTorch but unallocated", the right one finishes. Then set the right lane to max_split_size_mb:200, the older fix, and watch it return whole segments to the driver (cudaFree) to make room.

Model simplifications (the real allocator does more): one device and one stream (no cross-stream reuse, CUDA events or record_stream), no CUDA graph pools, no garbage_collection_threshold; cudaMalloc succeeds exactly when reserved plus the request fits the capacity (the driver's own 2 MiB granularity is ignored); an expandable segment reserves address space equal to the capacity rounded up to its page (2 MiB small pool, 20 MiB large pool), and releasing it unmaps the whole pages inside free blocks. Scenario sizes and capacities are illustrative; the rules are the source's.
