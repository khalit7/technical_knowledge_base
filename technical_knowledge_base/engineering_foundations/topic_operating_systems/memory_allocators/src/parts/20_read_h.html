The problem. Asking the CUDA driver for memory is slow, and caching "allows fast memory deallocation without device synchronizations" ({{CUDA semantics, memory management|https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/cuda.html#memory-management}}), so PyTorch keeps every freed block of a training step and reuses it: glibc's bins again. The visible consequence, in the docs' words: "the unused memory managed by the allocator will still show as if used in nvidia-smi".

The rules (the comment at the top of the file, lines 93 to 122, and the code): every request is rounded up to a multiple of 512 bytes; requests up to 1 MiB come from 2 MiB segments (the small pool), requests of 1 to 10 MiB from 20 MiB segments, larger ones from a segment of their own size rounded to 2 MiB; a request takes the best fit among cached free blocks and splits it; freeing never calls the driver, the block returns to its pool and merges with free neighbours in its segment; and when cudaMalloc fails, the allocator releases cached segments that hold nothing live and retries, then raises. A segment with even one live block cannot be returned.

Each rule with its source lines (reference)
  • Rounding. Every request is rounded up to a multiple of 512 bytes (round_size, lines 3060 to 3072; kMinBlockSize 512). With roundup_power2_divisions:N large requests are instead rounded to one of N steps between powers of two (lines 3036 to 3058), which makes sizes repeat and blocks reusable.
  • Two pools. Requests up to 1 MiB (kSmallSize) go to the small pool, packed into 2 MiB segments (kSmallBuffer); larger ones to the large pool (get_pool, lines 3640 to 3667).
  • Segment size. When nothing cached fits, a new segment comes from cudaMalloc: 2 MiB for small requests, 20 MiB for requests from 1 to 10 MiB (so several share one), and the request rounded up to 2 MiB above that (get_allocation_size, lines 3695 to 3703).
  • Best fit. A pool's free blocks are sorted by stream, size, segment age and address; a request takes the smallest one that fits (get_free_block, lines 3705 to 3762).
  • Splitting. If the chosen block is bigger, the front is handed out and the rest stays cached, in the small pool if at least 512 bytes remain, in the large pool only if more than 1 MiB remains (should_split, lines 3675 to 3693).
  • Freeing never calls the driver: the block goes back to its pool and merges with free neighbours in the same segment (free_block and try_merge_blocks, lines 3502 to 3612). A block is tied to the stream it was allocated on; using it on another stream needs tensor.record_stream(s), or it may be reused too early.
  • Out of memory: the retry chain. If cudaMalloc fails, the allocator frees cached blocks and tries again: first just enough oversize blocks (only when max_split_size_mb is set), then every cached segment that is not split, then raises (malloc, lines 1720 to 1792). A segment with even one live block cannot be returned.

The failure. Segments are sized by the first requests that needed them. When later requests are a little bigger (batch 9 after batch 8, a longer sequence), they do not fit the free holes, new segments are made, and the slivers left in old segments are stranded beside something live, until cudaMalloc fails with hundreds of megabytes free in total. The message says so (lines 1927 to 1953):

torch.OutOfMemoryError: CUDA out of memory. Tried to allocate <segment size>. GPU 0 has a total capacity of <total> of which <free> is free. ... Of the allocated memory <a> is allocated by PyTorch, and <r> is reserved by PyTorch but unallocated. If reserved but unallocated memory is large try setting PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True to avoid fragmentation.

"Tried to allocate" reports the segment size the allocator asked the driver for (alloc_size), so a 3 MiB tensor can fail as "Tried to allocate 20.00 MiB". "Reserved but unallocated" is the fragmentation: cached memory no request fit.

The fix: expandable segments. The file's Note [Expandable Segments] (lines 311 to 401) tells the batch-size story and the cure: "Instead of making one segment per allocation, it tries to make one segment (per stream) that grows as necessary". It reserves a range of GPU virtual addresses as large as the device memory (cuMemAddressReserve) and maps physical memory into it in pages, 2 MiB for the small pool and 20 MiB for the large one, with cuMemCreate and cuMemMap, so new memory lands right after the old and free space at the end of the segment joins with what is mapped next: virtual memory (the {{virtual memory page|n:3f05c17b0d0d81a48892cea154ce950b}}), applied by a user-space allocator to the GPU. The limits the note lists: slightly slower first allocations, and no CUDA IPC of such memory (sharing GPU tensors between processes).

What the model leaves out

One device and one stream (no cross-stream reuse, CUDA events or record_stream), no CUDA graph pools, no garbage_collection_threshold; cudaMalloc succeeds exactly when reserved plus the request fits the capacity (the driver's own 2 MiB granularity is ignored); an expandable segment reserves address space equal to the capacity rounded up to its page (2 MiB small pool, 20 MiB large pool), and releasing it unmaps the whole pages inside free blocks. Scenario sizes and capacities are illustrative; the rules are the source's.

What to look at. torch.cuda.memory_allocated() (live tensors) against torch.cuda.memory_reserved() (what the cache holds); torch.cuda.memory_stats()["inactive_split_bytes.all.current"], the free space stranded in split segments, the fragmentation number; torch.cuda.memory_summary() for all of it in a table. nvidia-smi shows what the cache reserved plus the CUDA context, never what the model needs. torch.cuda.empty_cache() returns cached, unused segments to the driver: useful when another process needs the GPU, useless against fragmentation, and slower afterwards because the cache must refill.

Two more interactions. Every process has its own cache: two processes on one GPU (a trainer and an evaluator) each hold reserved memory the other cannot use. And CUDA graphs capture fixed addresses, so captured work allocates from a private pool kept until the graph is destroyed (the Note at lines 126 to 156).
