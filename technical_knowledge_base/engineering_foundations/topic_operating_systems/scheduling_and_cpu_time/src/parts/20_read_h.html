9. cgroup CPU control: cpu.weight, cpu.max and throttling

{{cgroup v2: CPU interface files|https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html#cpu-interface-files}}; {{CFS bandwidth control doc|https://docs.kernel.org/scheduler/sched-bwc.html}}

A container's cgroup has two independent CPU knobs. cpu.weight is relative: it matters only when CPUs are contended, and splits them between groups the way nice splits them between threads. cpu.max is absolute: a cap even on an idle machine. (cpuset.cpus is affinity, section 7; the rest of cgroups is {{Containers and isolation|n:3f05c17b0d0d8156a841e4bf28f11293}}'s.)

cpu.weight: shares of a contended CPU

cpu.weight runs from 1 to 10,000, default 100, and becomes the group's CFS weight as weight × 1024 / 100: a default group weighs one nice-0 thread in its parent's queue. The trap is the translation. Docker's --cpu-shares and Kubernetes' requests.cpu speak cgroup v1 "shares" (default 1024), which the runtime converts to a weight: runc 1.1's linear map, 1 + (shares - 2) × 9,999 / 262,142, sends the default 1024 shares to weight 39, not 100; the newer quadratic map in the opencontainers/cgroups library sends 1024 to 100 (sources in the closed block).

Measured with this VM's Docker 20.10 (the old map): busy containers with --cpu-shares 1024 and 512 on one CPU got weights and split it (predicted 39 / 59 = 66.1%). Then a one-thread training step (eight 512 × 512 matmuls) at weight 100 beside a neighbour:

A "default" neighbour at weight 39 still takes a third of your CPU

A neighbour started with Docker's "default" --cpu-shares 1024 got weight 39 and still took of the shared CPU, slowing the step from to ms (the weights predict × 139 / 100 = ms); a neighbour at --cpu-shares 128 (weight 5) cost ms (predicted ). Weight protects you only in proportion to everyone else's, and only when you are actually competing for the same CPUs.

cpu.max: the quota, and why it stalls

cpu.max holds "$MAX $PERIOD": $MAX microseconds of CPU per $PERIOD (default 100,000 us); --cpus 2 writes 200000 100000. CFS bandwidth control enforces it in three steps:

  1. Each group has a global pool, refilled to the quota at every period by a timer.
  2. Each CPU running the group's threads borrows from the pool in slices of sched_cfs_bandwidth_slice_us = 5,000 us and charges the time its threads run.
  3. When a CPU's slice runs out and the pool is empty, throttle_cfs_rq() removes the whole group from that CPU's run queue: every thread of the container stops, runnable or not, until the next refill.

So the quota is spent at the speed of however many threads are running. Five threads on a 1-CPU quota burn 100 ms of CPU in 20 ms of wall time and then all stop for 80 ms. Predict what one thread, two and five threads look like on the same quota, then step through:

Threads beyond the quota do not wait politely: they all stop together

Measured: inside a container with a CPU limit, N threads spin for 1 s, and each records every gap of more than 300 us in its own progress (time it was not running). One lane per thread, dark where it ran, light where it was stopped; 100 ms per step. measured

With as many threads as the quota allows, nothing stops. With five, every thread stops for ms in each 100 ms period (predicted: 100 - 100 / 5 = 80 ms). The CPU used is the same in every run: throttling costs not work but latency, which a training step, a request or a GPU waiting for its next batch feels. A 10 ms period caps the stall near 8 ms (measured up to ms) for ten times as many throttling events. throttled_usec sums over every CPU the group was on, so it can exceed the run's wall time.

Fixes. Size pools and workers to the quota (section 11); in Kubernetes, set requests without limits so pods share idle CPU by weight, use a shorter period (the kubelet's CPUCFSQuotaPeriod feature gate, {{helpers_linux.go line 73|https://github.com/kubernetes/kubernetes/blob/v1.34.0/pkg/kubelet/cm/helpers_linux.go#L73}}), or give the pod exclusive cores with the static CPU manager policy (section 7).

cat /sys/fs/cgroup/cpu.max /sys/fs/cgroup/cpu.weight /sys/fs/cgroup/cpu.stat # inside the container

docker run --cpus 2 --cpu-shares 512 --cpuset-cpus 0-3 ...; systemd-run -p CPUQuota=200% -p CPUWeight=50 ...

Weights and bandwidth control in the source, limits and burst (depth)

Weights. The kernel conversion is in {{core.c line 8286|https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v5.10/kernel/sched/core.c#L8286}}. Kubernetes turns a request into shares as milli-CPUs × 1024 / 1000 ({{pkg/kubelet/cm/helpers_linux.go line 88, v1.34.0|https://github.com/kubernetes/kubernetes/blob/v1.34.0/pkg/kubelet/cm/helpers_linux.go#L88}}). The linear map is runc's {{libcontainer/cgroups/utils.go line 423, runc v1.1.2|https://github.com/opencontainers/runc/blob/v1.1.2/libcontainer/cgroups/utils.go#L423}}; the quadratic one is in opencontainers/cgroups from v0.0.3 ({{utils.go line 425|https://github.com/opencontainers/cgroups/blob/v0.0.3/utils.go#L425}}), and runc v1.3.6 and v1.4.0 depend on versions that have it, per their go.mod.

The refill is __refill_cfs_bandwidth_runtime ({{fair.c line 4601|https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v5.10/kernel/sched/fair.c#L4601}}) called from do_sched_cfs_period_timer (line 4942); the 5 ms borrowing slice is set at line 129 (the same value read here); charging is __account_cfs_rq_runtime (line 4653); the throttle itself throttle_cfs_rq() (line 4741). Periods may be 1 ms to 1 s and quotas at least 1 ms ({{core.c lines 7891 to 7919|https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v5.10/kernel/sched/core.c#L7891}}). Kernels from 5.14 add cpu.max.burst, which lets unused quota carry over to absorb short bursts ({{sched-bwc doc|https://docs.kernel.org/scheduler/sched-bwc.html}}) not on this kernel.
