Interleaving lab: you are the scheduler

A concurrency bug needs a particular order of steps, which is why it passes tests and fails in production. Here you choose the order. Each line below is one indivisible step; press a thread's button to run its next step, and watch the shared memory, the kernel's wait queues and the threads' states. The lab stops when two threads are in the critical section, when a consumer reads an empty slot, or when every thread that is not done is asleep with nobody left to wake it (the lost wake-up, or a hang).

How this is checked. The page explores every interleaving in your browser (a depth-first search over states, counting the distinct schedules that lead to each ending). src/recompute.py does the same search with an independent Python implementation of the same programs (src/inputs/lab_programs.json); the counts above say whether the two agree.
