Start here if networks are new to you

Julia Evans, Networking! ACK!

A short illustrated zine: packets, TCP, ports, the tools. About 30 min.Read first; it makes the toolbox in section 1 feel natural.

Julia Evans, How DNS Works

Resolvers, caching, TTLs and the failures of section 3, drawn. About 30 min.Read with section 3. Her post on {{a DNS resolver in 80 lines of Go|https://jvns.ca/blog/2022/02/01/a-dns-resolver-in-80-lines-of-go/}} shows the referral walk in code.

Ilya Grigorik, High Performance Browser Networking

The clearest explanation of why latency, TCP and TLS decide performance; free online (2013, so before HTTP/3). Chapters 1 to 4, about 3 h.Read after this page, starting with {{Building Blocks of TCP|https://hpbn.co/building-blocks-of-tcp/}} and {{Transport Layer Security|https://hpbn.co/transport-layer-security-tls/}}.

The Illustrated TLS 1.3 Connection

Every byte of a real TLS 1.3 handshake, annotated; a {{QUIC version|https://quic.xargs.org/}} exists too. About 45 min.Read after section 5 when you want the bytes inside the records this page shows from outside.

Beej's Guide to Network Programming

Sockets from the programmer's side: connect, bind, listen, send, recv. About 2 h for the first chapters.Read when you write your own server or debug one at the socket level.

MDN, HTTP

The reference you will actually open for methods, status codes and headers, plus a guide to {{Server-Sent Events|https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Server-sent_events/Using_server-sent_events}}. About 1 h for the core pages.Keep open while building APIs.

The specifications that matter

{{RFC 9110: HTTP Semantics|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110}} (2022)

What methods, status codes and headers mean in every HTTP version. Sections 9 and 15, about 1 h.Read when you argue about retries or status codes.

{{RFC 9293: TCP|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293}} (2022)

The consolidated TCP specification. Sections 3.1 to 3.5, about 40 min.Read with section 4 if you want the handshake and states exactly.

{{RFC 9846: TLS 1.3|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9846}} (July 2026)

TLS 1.3 respecified, replacing RFC 8446. Section 2 (overview), about 30 min.Read with section 5. Cloudflare's {{detailed look at TLS 1.3|https://blog.cloudflare.com/rfc-8446-aka-tls-1-3/}} (2018) explains the design choices.

{{RFC 9000: QUIC|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9000}} (2021)

The transport under HTTP/3. Sections 1 to 3, about 40 min.Read after Cloudflare's {{HTTP/3: past, present and future|https://blog.cloudflare.com/http3-the-past-present-and-future/}} (2019) and {{The road to QUIC|https://blog.cloudflare.com/the-road-to-quic/}} (2018), which explain why it exists.

{{RFC 1034: DNS concepts|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1034}} (1987)

The original design, still accurate. Sections 2 to 5, about 1 h.Read when a DNS problem does not match what you expected.

{{WHATWG HTML, section 9.2: Server-sent events|https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/server-sent-events.html}}

The whole SSE format and parsing rules. About 20 min.Read before writing an SSE parser or proxy.

{{RFC 6455: WebSocket|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6455}} (2011)

Handshake, framing, closing. Sections 1, 5 and 7, about 1 h.Read when building a realtime or voice channel.

{{gRPC core concepts|https://grpc.io/docs/what-is-grpc/core-concepts/}} and {{deadlines|https://grpc.io/docs/guides/deadlines/}}

Service definitions, the four call types, deadlines and cancellation. About 40 min.Read with section 8; add the {{protobuf encoding guide|https://protobuf.dev/programming-guides/encoding/}} (30 min) for the wire format.

Auth

{{OAuth 2.1 draft|https://oauth.net/2.1/}} (draft 16, 3 September 2026)

The consolidated OAuth: PKCE always, no implicit or password grants. About 2 h 30 min in full.Read the flows you use after Parecki's guide.

{{Aaron Parecki, OAuth 2 Simplified|https://aaronparecki.com/oauth-2-simplified/}} and {{oauth.com|https://www.oauth.com/}}

The clearest flow-by-flow explanations, by one of the editors of the OAuth 2.1 draft. 30 min, then about 1 h.Read first for anything OAuth.

{{RFC 8725: JWT Best Current Practices|https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8725}} (2020)

The pitfalls list: algorithms, audiences, key handling. About 40 min.Read before accepting JWTs in a service.

{{Stripe: idempotency|https://stripe.com/blog/idempotency}} (2017)

Retries made safe with idempotency keys, from the API that popularised them. 15 min.Read before retrying POSTs.
